# Hostile-Referee Audit: “Nondualistic Interactionism” (unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/)

## 1. TL;DR — Verdict: REVISE-HARD

The article gets Griffin mostly right, but it frames its two critics dishonestly and leaves its sourcing partly unverified, so it must be revised before it can stand as the Map’s reference treatment of its “nearest non-dualist rival.”

- **What is broken.**

- The “Two published responses” paragraph treats Chalmers (2003) as a response to Griffin. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/) It is a one-line taxonomic footnote listing Griffin among type-F monists. [consc](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)
- It presents Kim (1999) as a response to Griffin’s 1998 book. Kim was actually replying to Griffin’s 1999 *Process Studies* paper, and Kim says he had not yet read the book. [religion-online](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/)
- It omits Kim’s most damaging claim: that panexperientialism is inconsistent with causal closure in the sense closure is meant. [religion-online](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/) That claim directly undercuts the article’s section headed “Closure Kept, Completeness Denied.”
- About ten of the roughly twenty pinpoint-paged Griffin quotations could not be confirmed at source. One (p. 214, freedom vs. conservation) looks suspiciously like a reworking of a verified p. 51 sentence about mind–body interaction.
- **What survives.**

- The core exposition of Griffin is accurate: the numerical vs. ontological theses, organizational duality, dominant occasions, and the chapter-10 relabelling as “panexperientialist physicalism.”
- The article explicitly declines to claim a refutation. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/)
- Its own reading of Chalmers is honestly marked as the Map’s own. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/)
- **The calibration problem.**

- The Tenet 2 test faults Griffin for supplying “no mechanism” and for discounting conservation. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/) The Map’s own outcome-selection interface faces an unresolved Born-rule dilemma by its own admission, so this is calibration asymmetry and tenet leakage.
- Griffin’s same-kind prehensive interaction does better than the Map on the pairing problem, and the article concedes only “half” of that. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/)
- The “nearest rival” framing contradicts two neighbouring pages, which name Russellian monism and process monism as the closest or strongest rival.

### 2.1 Dimension 1 — Fidelity to Griffin

**(i) Numerical distinctness.** The article’s gloss is accurate and better than the brief assumed.

- Griffin defines the mind as an enduring individual “numerically distinct from the highest-level enduring individuals (the neurons) constituting the brain as such.” [newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html) This is confirmed in the UC Press/newdualism text, though the page could not be confirmed.
- Griffin holds that the dominant occasions are members of the same compound individual whose other members are the neurons. In his words, “every actual occasion has a determinate spatiotemporal location relative to all others,” and this applies to “the ‘dominant occasions’ belonging to a human mind.” [cdlib](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/view)[religion-online](https://www.religion-online.org/article/materialist-and-panexperientialist-physician-a-critique-of-jaegwon-kims-supervenience-and-mind/)
- So the mind is numerically distinct from each neuron, but it is not outside the society or nexus. The article’s “a further actual entity presiding over them rather than a soul-substance apart from them” captures this. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/)
- What the article does not draw out is the consequence. Because Griffin’s two relata share a nexus and a location, Kim’s pairing problem barely gets a grip on Griffin. This is the article’s largest concession failure (§2.4).

**(ii) Hard-core common sense and the world-knot.** This is represented correctly, but too briefly.

- Griffin’s method is Whiteheadian: hard-core notions are those “inevitably presuppose[d] in practice.” [religion-online](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/) His formal principle is that we are “guilty of self-contradiction if our theory denies these notions” (ch. 3–4, pp. 16–21, verified). [cdlib](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/unsnarling-the-world-knot-consciousness-freedom-and-the-mind-body-problem.html)[newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)
- His diagnosis is that dualism and materialism share one root, the “Cartesian intuition about matter” (ch. 6, p. 46, verified). [cdlib +2](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/unsnarling-the-world-knot-consciousness-freedom-and-the-mind-body-problem.html)
- The article reduces the argument against dualism to two charges: interaction of unlike things, and continuity (p. 51, verified). [cdlib +2](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/unsnarling-the-world-knot-consciousness-freedom-and-the-mind-body-problem.html) It omits Griffin’s claim that materialism’s distinctive problems are downstream of the same Cartesian premise. [cdlib](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/unsnarling-the-world-knot-consciousness-freedom-and-the-mind-body-problem.html)[newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html) That claim is what makes the view a third option rather than a dualism-lite.
- The article’s analogy to the Map’s self-stultification reasoning is fair, and Griffin’s text supports it.

**(iii) “Closure kept, completeness denied.”**

- **Where the brief’s premise is wrong.** Griffin does not simply reject closure as materialist dogma.

- The p. 237 sentence is verified verbatim: “The closure of the system of physical causes to influence from nonphysical causes does not, accordingly, exclude human minds from the universal causal nexus.” [cdlib](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/view)[newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html) Griffin keeps a closure principle by redefining “physical” so that every actuality, minds included, has a physical pole.
- The “pipe dream” sentence denying physics’ predictive and explanatory completeness is verified verbatim. [newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html) It is followed by “Contemporary physical theory is not even remotely close to such a capacity.” [cdlib](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/view)
- So “closure kept, completeness denied” is a fair compression of Griffin’s text, not a Map invention. The label is the Map’s; the structure is Griffin’s.
- **Where the article fails.** It presents the move as if it were coherent, and it suppresses the cited critic’s rebuttal. Kim (1999), the article’s own source, writes three things:

- Closure “guarantees the explanatory sufficiency of physics.” [religion-online](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/)
- Denying closure means “theoretical physics is *in principle incompletable*.” [religion-online](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/)
- Panexperientialism “is inconsistent with each of the three doctrines (understood in their proper intended sense),” and closure is one of the three. [religion-online](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/)
- Kim’s position is therefore that closure without completeness is not closure. The article quotes Kim’s label complaint and his composition demand, then drops the one Kim argument aimed squarely at the section heading. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/) That is selective citation.

**(iv) Classification.** This is handled correctly.

- The article reports Griffin’s chapter-10 relabelling. Verified: ch. 10 is titled “Supervenience and Panexperientialist Physicalism” and concludes “I have proposed a fourth option: a nonreductive, panexperientialist physicalism.” [cdlib +2](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/unsnarling-the-world-knot-consciousness-freedom-and-the-mind-body-problem.html)
- It notes the clash with Kim’s “misleading” verdict. [religion-online](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/)
- It misses the irony the brief identifies. The Map classifies as its “nearest *non-dualist*” rival a view whose author calls it physicalism, which Kim calls not physicalism, and which Chalmers’s structural criterion would put with type-D dualism. The view’s taxonomic instability is itself evidence that the Map’s “kind axis” is doing verbal, not substantive, work. The article half-admits this (“The two disagree about what ‘dualism’ names”). [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/)

**(v) Vacuous actuality and aggregational vs. compound societies.**

- The aggregational/compound distinction is rendered accurately. “Aggregational societies devoid of a dominant member” is verified verbatim, in a sentence about “sticks and stones.” [newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html) The law-of-large-numbers point appears in Griffin’s own p. 52 discussion of freedom. [cdlib](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/unsnarling-the-world-knot-consciousness-freedom-and-the-mind-body-problem.html)[newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)
- The target never names Whitehead’s rejection of “vacuous actuality.” It leaves that to process-philosophy, which states it correctly. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/process-philosophy/)

**(vi) Freedom, concrescence and subjective aim.**

- The treatment is thin but not inaccurate. “Partly self-determined in each moment” matches Griffin’s “self-determination in the moment” (ch. 5, p. 40, verified). [cdlib](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/unsnarling-the-world-knot-consciousness-freedom-and-the-mind-body-problem.html)[newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)
- The article does not register that Griffin calls the freedom chapter (ch. 9) “the most important chapter of the book” (Introduction, p. 2, verified). [cdlib](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/unsnarling-the-world-knot-consciousness-freedom-and-the-mind-body-problem.html)[newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html) It treats freedom as a footnote to Tenet 4, when it is Griffin’s central motivation.

**Overall.** The article’s Griffin is a real Griffin, lightly steelmanned toward the Map’s vocabulary of “difference-making point” and “completeness.” The distortion lies not in Griffin’s portrait but in how his critics are framed.

### 2.3 Dimension 3 — Missing counterarguments and literature

These omissions are ranked by damage.

1. **Basile (2010), “Materialist vs. Panexperientialist Physicalism: Where Do We Stand?,” *Process Studies* 39(2): 264–284, DOI 10.5840/process201039224.** This is the single most relevant missing source. Its abstract says it argues that, “contrary to what has been argued by David Ray Griffin in his important book, Unsnarling the World-Knot—even Whitehead’s version of the doctrine fails to provide a satisfactory solution” to the composition problem. The article’s “Map’s strongest objection, the composition problem” therefore has a published Whiteheadian-sympathetic precedent, which the article ignores. That makes the Kim demand look like the Map’s lone discovery (coherence inflation).
2. **Griffin’s own replies.**

- Griffin, “Panexperientialist Physicalism and the Mind-Body Problem,” *JCS* 4(3), 1997: 248–268. [springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12136-025-00656-0) It states the organizational-duality thesis in its shortest form. [newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Griffin-JCS1997.pdf)
- Griffin, “Materialist and Panexperientialist Physicalism,” *Process Studies* 28(1–2), 1999. This is the actual target of Kim’s reply. [religion-online](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/)
- Citing Kim without the text he answered is unacceptable.
3. **The combination literature.**

- Chalmers, “The Combination Problem for Panpsychism,” in Brüntrup & Jaskolla (eds.), *Panpsychism: Contemporary Perspectives* (OUP 2016), pp. 179–214. It separates “the subject combination problem, the quality combination problem, and the structural combination problem.”
- Sam Coleman, “The Real Combination Problem: Panpsychism, Micro-Subjects, and Emergence,” *Erkenntnis* 79(1): 19–44 (2014), DOI 10.1007/s10670-013-9431-x. Coleman argues that subjects cannot combine and recommends neutral monism.
- Goff (2017), Roelofs (2019), and Mørch’s 2023 Cambridge Element.
- Griffin’s dominant occasion is non-constitutive. The new subject is not composed of micro-subjects, which emergent-panpsychist responses to Coleman exploit. The article should say whether Griffin is constitutive or emergent. It is closer to Chalmers’s “emergent panpsychism,” and that matters to whether Kim’s composition demand even applies. [consc](https://consc.net/papers/combination.pdf)
4. **Process-internal critiques of the dominant occasion.** Missing are Lewis Ford and Joseph Bracken on regnant nexūs, Hartshorne’s own reservations, Weekes & Weber (eds.), *Process Approaches to Consciousness* (SUNY 2009), and McHenry’s review of *Unsnarling* (*Faith and Philosophy* 16(2), 1999). [asburyseminary](https://place.asburyseminary.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi) The worry that a regnant occasion is a disguised Cartesian ego is the obvious objection from the Map’s own side, and it is absent.
5. **The quantum-process literature.** Missing are Epperson (2004), Shimony, Stapp’s Whiteheadian turn, and Chalmers & McQueen, “Consciousness and the Collapse of the Wave Function,” in Shan Gao (ed.), *Consciousness and Quantum Mechanics* (OUP 2022), pp. 11–63. That chapter combines integrated information theory with continuous spontaneous localization. The article’s assertion that “a Whiteheadian could graft a quantum locus” is made without engaging the literature that tries exactly this. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/)
6. **Closure formulations.** Missing are Papineau (2001); Barbara Montero, “Varieties of Causal Closure,” in Walter & Heckmann (eds.), *Physicalism and Mental Causation* (Imprint Academic 2003), pp. 173–187; Gibb (2015); and Lowe (2000/2008). The whole “closure kept, completeness denied” section needs Montero’s closure/completeness distinction. [newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/B.Montero/Varieties-of-Causal-Closure.htm) On her account, completeness alone leaves distinct mental causes “not definitively barred” (p. 174). Without that distinction the article cannot say whether Griffin’s move is coherent.
7. **Russellian monism (Alter & Nagasawa 2015).** Chalmers places Griffin in this family. [consc](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html) The article puts Russellian monism in the table but never tests it against the tenets.
8. **Active inference (Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria 2025, *Neurosci. Biobehav. Rev.* 176:106296).** [ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/nebior/abstract/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106296~a-beautiful-loop-an-active-inference-theory-of-consciousness) This is low priority here. The article does not make selfhood or minimal-experience claims, so the omission is not a defect of this page. ### 2.4 Dimension 4 — Calibration asymmetry and the “nearest rival” framing

- **Tenet 2 test (tenet leakage and calibration asymmetry).**

- The article faults Griffin on two counts. His influence is “neither minimal nor quantum-located,” and “no mechanism is given,” with only an Elsasser footnote. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/)
- The Map’s own outcome-selection mechanism is, by the site’s admission across its Born-rule pages, impaled on a dilemma. If selection preserves Born statistics, its aggregate efficacy is invisible. If it does not, it predicts deviations that have not been observed. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-state-inheritance-in-ai/)[unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/free-will/)
- Demanding a mechanism from Griffin while the Map’s is an acknowledged open debt is calibration asymmetry.
- “Fails Tenet 2” is also not a finding about Griffin. Tenet 2 is a Map stipulation, so Griffin “fails” it the way any non-quantum interactionism does, by definition. The article’s own closing concession, “differ in risk tolerance rather than in logical compatibility,” is correct, [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/) and it shows the Tenet 2 section is a constraint, not a test. Under the constrain-vs-establish gate it should be relabelled.
- **Conservation.** Griffin’s p. 51 argument, that we are less sure of universal conservation than of mind–body influence, is presented as a defect against the Map’s “keeps the law.” [cdlib +2](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/unsnarling-the-world-knot-consciousness-freedom-and-the-mind-body-problem.html) The Map keeps the law only by pushing all efficacy into Born-preserving selection, which is the move that generates its own dilemma. Neither side has paid this debt, so presenting Griffin’s discount as a strictly worse position is coherence inflation.
- **Pairing problem.** The article concedes only that “the ‘how can unlike act on unlike’ half” lapses. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/) On Griffin’s view the relata share a kind, a spatiotemporal location and a causal mode (prehension). Kim’s pairing question asks what links *this* mind to *this* body, and Griffin answers it by membership in one compound individual. The Map’s own interactionist-dualism page states that for its substance-leaning reading “the pairing problem is a live unpaid debt.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/interactionist-dualism/) Griffin therefore fares better than the Map on pairing, and the article buries this.
- **Parsimony.** The article’s Tenet 5 paragraph is symmetric (“the Map cannot call panexperientialism unparsimonious”), which is good. On the site’s own bounded-Occam terms that is no decisive argument, but it is a point where Griffin is not worse.
- **Composition demand used both ways.** The article admits that “the Map’s own account… faces a structurally related question.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/) This is confession without correction: the admission is made, but the composition demand still headlines as “the Map’s strongest objection.”
- **Register repair as speculation.** The article finds that P-D2 (“Bidirectional Interaction selects dualism”) is falsified by Griffin, who is inside the selection. It then only “speculates” a repair, and the register entry still reads “selects dualism.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/) This is the paradigm case of confession without correction. A page has shown a register entry false, and the register is unchanged.
- **“Nearest rival” framing (constitutional-attractor effect).** The Map currently names three different closest or strongest rivals:

- bi-aspectual-ontology names russellian-monism “the closest monist competitor”; [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology/)

The tests chosen here (irreducibility, completeness denial, two relata) are the Map’s own selection criteria. Griffin is “nearest” because he passes the Map’s filters, so his nearness is an artefact of the filters, not independent evidence. That is evidential-independence failure in the framing, though the article’s tenet-by-tenet results do not depend on it.
- **Epistemic-to-metaphysical slide.** This is mild. “Tenet 4: compatible… a natural single-world ally” converts compatibility into alliance. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/) Griffin never addresses Many-Worlds. That is the constrain-vs-establish gate again: compatibility is not support.

## 5. Site-Wide Methodology Improvements

1. **The citation pipeline under-verifies book quotations.** The site’s changelog shows careful grep-matching for PMC and Crossref sources. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/) Pinpoint-paged quotations from a book with a freely available UC e-book (publishing.cdlib.org) went in with about half not checkable against page markers. Book quotations should be held to the same raw-text grep standard as journal abstracts, with the page marker captured in the research note.
2. **Critic-framing check.** Add a gate to the deep-review checklist: for every “X responds to Y” claim, record X’s actual target text and date. The Kim mis-scoping is the kind of error a model reconstructing a debate from memory produces, and a reviewing model of the same family tends to accept it because the attribution is plausible.
3. **Claude-on-Claude review blind spot.** The article was generated and reviewed by Claude-family systems (claude-fable-5-1, claude-opus-5-5). [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/) Same-family review catches internal-consistency issues well; the changelog’s hedge-density and anchoring audits show this. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/) It catches omissions of a cited author’s own counter-argument (Kim on closure) poorly, because both writer and reviewer share the same compressed picture of the source. Add an omission audit: “List the cited author’s three strongest claims against the view; are they all present?”
4. **Register amendment must be mandatory when a page falsifies an entry.** Make “page shows register entry false” trigger a blocking positions-evolve task rather than a “speculates” sentence. Otherwise confession without correction becomes structural.
5. **One canonical “nearest rival” ledger.** Maintain a single register entry naming the Map’s closest rivals by axis: closest monist, closest interactionist non-dualist, strongest physicalist. Have every page cite it, so “nearest/strongest rival” cannot be asserted independently on three pages.
6. **Benchmark carry-through.** Whenever a page applies a mechanism demand to a rival, automatically insert a link to the Map’s own mechanism debt (Born dilemma, pairing debt). This is a lint rule against calibration asymmetry.
