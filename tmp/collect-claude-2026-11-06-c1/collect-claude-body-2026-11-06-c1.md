# Critical Audit: “Substance Dualism vs Property Dualism” (unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)

**Headline verdict: REVISE-HARD overall. The article’s central “the Map accepts either view” stance gets DELETE as a headline claim.** It contains a factual error about Descartes, misattributes an argument to Zimmerman, misplaces Russellian monism inside property dualism, and leaves out Kim’s exclusion argument. It answers a version of illusionism that Frankish explicitly disowns. It fails the Laukkonen–Friston–Chandaria blocking gate. Its “either view” neutrality is contradicted by the site’s own background-commitments page, which says the operational framework “runs substance-dualist”. ## 1. TL;DR

- **Verdict: REVISE-HARD (article); DELETE (the “accepts either” headline).** The Cartesian section says Descartes answered Elisabeth by pointing to the pineal gland. He did not: his 1643 replies appealed to three “primitive notions” and a scholastic analogy with heaviness. The contemporary substance-dualist roster is two names deep. The property-dualism taxonomy files Russellian monism and panpsychism under dualism, against the standard type-F classification and against those authors’ own self-understanding. Chalmers and Jackson are stale: SEP §3.2 (Robinson & Weir 2025) records that Jackson (2003) abandoned property dualism for materialism and that Chalmers has expressed a preference for substance over property dualism (Chalmers 2016, p. 24). The article reports neither.
- **The objections are not stated at full strength.** Kim’s exclusion argument, Papineau’s causal argument, phenomenal-concept strategies, the debunking argument and Pitts’s Noether-based conservation analysis are all absent. The pairing reply stops one step short of Kim’s own rejoinder. The “regress” reply to illusionism attacks a position Frankish 2016 explicitly rejects (§1.1). [github](https://keithfrankish.github.io/articles/Frankish_2016_Illusionism_as_a_theory_of_consciousness_eprint.pdf) The witness-consciousness reply relies on minimal phenomenal experience reports that the active-inference “Beautiful Loop” theory claims to model, and the article never engages that theory. That fails the blocking gate.
- **“Either view” is tenet-leakage-concealing layering, not honest neutrality.** Epiphenomenalist property dualism is excluded by Tenet 3, so “either” already means “interactionist property dualism or substance dualism”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) The site’s background-commitments page (modified 2026-11-05) says Tenets 1, 3 and 4 share a persisting-subject posit and that the operational framework runs substance-dualist. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/background-commitments/) The No-Many-Worlds tenet therefore cannot be fully carried by property dualism as the site states it. The site’s haecceity and self pages keep “neutrality” only by redefining “substance” as an unchanging soul. The bi-aspectual-ontology page calls a third, substance-free picture “foundational”, and this article does not link to it. ### 2.1 Accuracy of the Cartesian account

**Error: the pineal gland as Descartes’s answer to Elisabeth.** The article says Descartes “answered unsatisfactorily, pointing to the pineal gland.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) In the 1643 exchange, Descartes’s replies of 21 May and 28 June set out three primitive notions (soul, body, and their union). [earlymoderntexts](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/descartes1643_1.pdf)[stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/emotions-17th18th/LD2Descartes.html) He used the scholastic notion of heaviness as an analogy for how the soul moves the body, and said that matters of the union are known “very clearly by the senses” but only obscurely by the understanding. Elisabeth’s 1 July reply pressed that this explained nothing. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/JANEOB)[lisacshapiro](https://lisacshapiro.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/shapiro-published.pdf) Shapiro’s edition notes that Descartes “appeals to the Scholastic notion of a real quality,” specifically heaviness. [dokumen](https://dokumen.pub/the-correspondence-between-princess-elisabeth-of-bohemia-and-rene-descartes-9780226204444.html) The pineal gland belongs to the *Treatise on Man* and *Passions of the Soul*, not to the answer Elisabeth received. The Map’s own `history-of-interaction-problem` page (search copy) separates the pineal-gland proposal from the Elisabeth exchange and dates her letter correctly to May 1643. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/history-of-the-interaction-problem/) That inter-page inconsistency should be fixed in this article. “Elizabeth” should also be “Elisabeth”, the spelling of the standard edition and of the Map’s own history page. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/history-of-the-interaction-problem/)[dokumen](https://dokumen.pub/the-correspondence-between-princess-elisabeth-of-bohemia-and-rene-descartes-9780226204444.html)

**Missing: the real distinction, the substance notion, the *Principles*, animals.** The article reduces Descartes to the extended/unextended and divisible/indivisible contrast. It omits:

- the real-distinction argument (the clear-and-distinct-conceivability route of Meditation VI), which is the historical ancestor of the zombie argument the article later invokes;
- the *Principles* definition of substance as what needs nothing else to exist, with God the only substance in the strict sense; [qom](https://pfk.qom.ac.ir/article_2728_ac6215627aec096d65c16be0c33671d1.pdf)
- the scholastic background (substance as the bearer of accidents, the Aristotelian form/matter framework Descartes was reacting against);
- the beast-machine doctrine, which is directly relevant to the article’s later claims about consciousness tracking brains.

A comparison article that never says what “substance” means cannot adjudicate a substance-vs-property question. That is the core defect, and §2.5 shows it is not cosmetic: the site elsewhere escapes substance commitments by equivocating on exactly this term.

**Leibniz is compressed anachronistically.** The article has Leibniz arguing that mental pushing “would inject energy, but physics says energy is conserved.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) Leibniz’s objection concerned the conservation of directional quantity of motion against Descartes’s scalar quantity. The energy-conservation version is largely a later reconstruction. Pitts describes the objection as recurring “off and on since Leibniz’s day” but especially since the late 19th century. [closertotruth](https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/pitts-s-interactionist-dualism-energy-conservation-and-mental-causation) The article projects 19th-century energy talk back onto a 17th-century momentum argument.

### 2.2 Accuracy and currency of contemporary substance dualism

**Roster.** The article names only Lowe and Zimmerman, as “naturalistic substance dualism”, and mentions Hasker only in the property-dualism section. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) It omits Swinburne (*Mind, Brain, and Free Will*, 2013; *Are We Bodies or Souls?*, 2019), Taliaferro, Meixner, Plantinga, Moreland, Farris, Howard Robinson’s *From the Knowledge Argument to Mental Substance* (CUP 2016), the Loose/Menuge/Moreland *Blackwell Companion to Substance Dualism* (2018), and all 2023–2026 work. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Robinson) That includes Weir’s 2024 “Property Dualism Implies Substance Dualism” and Ralph Weir’s *The Mind-Body Problem and Metaphysics: An Argument from Consciousness to Mental Substance* (Routledge, 2023). SEP “Dualism” names “Weir 2023” alongside Zimmerman 2010, Lycan 2013 and Robinson 2016 as challenges to the idea that property dualism is preferable to substance dualism. For a page whose title is this exact contrast, the omission of the property-to-substance literature (SEP §4.6; Zimmerman 2010; Lycan 2013; Robinson 2016; Weir 2023) is disqualifying. [stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2025/entries/dualism/)[stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dualism/)

**Varieties.** The article never distinguishes Cartesian, Thomistic/hylomorphic, emergent (Hasker), non-Cartesian (Lowe) and compound/Strawsonian-person views as varieties of substance dualism. The SEP entry the article cites as its taxonomy source has a dedicated section, 3.6, on Hylomorphic Dualism. [stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2025/entries/dualism/)[stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dualism/) The article never mentions hylomorphism, so it does not actually follow the taxonomy it claims to follow.

**Lowe misrepresented by juxtaposition.** The “Contemporary Substance Dualism” section lists “Quantum mechanics” as the first resource “modern substance dualists” have. It then names Lowe as the second resource without separating the two. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) Lowe’s own model is not quantum. His 1999 abstract places conscious states as emergent effects of prior physical causes, in a universe where every physical event appears to have a wholly physical sufficient cause. [philpapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/LOWSAA) The SEP Mental Causation entry notes that Lowe and Gibb advance models on which the mental neither adds nor redistributes energy. [stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mental-causation/) Presenting the Map’s Minimal Quantum Interaction tenet as a generic resource of contemporary substance dualism is tenet leakage. Most named contemporary substance dualists do not use it.

**Combination-problem advantage overstated.** The claim that substance dualism “sidesteps the combination problem” ignores that substance dualism has its own unity and individuation problems. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)[unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) SEP §5.2 discusses the “Unity of the Mind” as a problem for both bundle and substance dualism. [stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dualism/) Emergent substance dualism (Hasker) must explain how a unified simple subject arises from a composite brain, which is a combination problem in reverse. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/history-of-the-interaction-problem/) The Map’s own `consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-individuation` page (search copy) concedes that the substance-leaning reading must ground individuation in “primitive identity, haecceity, or some principle we have not yet articulated.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-individuation/)

### 2.3 Accuracy and currency of property dualism

**Russellian monism is miscategorised.** Putting Russellian monism under “Varieties of Property Dualism” and then diagnosing it as “disguised property dualism” is question-begging. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)[unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) Pautz, the article’s own source, treats Russellian monism as a “non-standard form of physicalism” that “tries to pave a middle way between dualism and standard materialism”. He presents Chalmers’s case for it as a Hegelian synthesis of the two. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/PAUCRM) The taxonomic boundary with neutral monism and panpsychism is not drawn at all. The phrase “panpsychist property dualism” conflates two camps whose leading defenders (Goff; Galen Strawson’s realistic monism) reject dualism by name.

**The Pautz quote is unverified.** The article quotes Pautz: “Though called a physicalist view, it seems to have many of the vices of dualism.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)[unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) The PhilArchive text of the 15 December 2015 draft reads, in the relevant sentence, “calling it a physicalist view, but it seems to have many of the vices of dualism.” [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/PAUCRM)[philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/PAUCRM) The article’s opening words do not appear in that text. The quotation is either drawn from an unlocated version or reconstructed. Pautz labels the draft “very rough” and invites comments, which makes it a weak anchor for a verbatim quote. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/PAUCRM) The 2026-05-27 deep review calls the same source “Pautz (2019)”, an internal dating inconsistency. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/reviews/deep-review-2026-05-27-substance-property-dualism/)

**Pautz co-opted.** Pautz’s conclusion is that “there is no strong argument for” Russellian monism, not that it is false. His opening premise is that dualism faces the “complexity problem” of being “decidedly uneconomical”. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/PAUCRM) His parity result says Russellian monism shares dualism’s vices, which concedes that dualism has them. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/russellian-monism/) Using him as support for “accepting the structural commitment openly” recruits a parity argument as if it favoured one side.

**Chalmers is stale.** “Chalmers’s early position” is the article’s only nod to change. It does not say what Chalmers holds now. SEP §3.2 reports that Chalmers has expressed a preference for substance dualism over property dualism (Chalmers 2016, p. 24). Chalmers and McQueen (2022, OUP) developed a consciousness-collapse model combining integrated information theory with continuous spontaneous localization. That is a Chalmersian quantum-interactionist research programme, and they say simple versions are “falsified by the quantum Zeno effect.” [philpapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/CHACAT-24)[arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.02314) It falsifies the article’s claim that “Chalmersan psychophysical laws” are a non-quantum framework “unaffected” by decoherence. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) The type-D/type-E/type-F taxonomy of “Consciousness and its Place in Nature” (2003), which is the obvious organising frame, is absent.

**Jackson is stale.** “Where They Agree” lists the knowledge argument as pointing “beyond physics”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) It never says that Jackson abandoned it. In “Mind and Illusion” (in O’Hear, ed., *Minds and Persons*, CUP 2003, pp. 251–272) he wrote that he had “capitulated” and now sees the task as finding where anti-physicalist intuition arguments “go wrong”. SEP §3.2 records that Jackson “has abandoned property dualism in favour of materialism.” [stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dualism/) A comparison page that leans on Mary’s Room without that sentence fails the currency standard.

**Searle is handled correctly, but out of place.** The Further Reading entry calls biological naturalism a “non-dualist rival”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) That matches Searle’s own “Why I Am Not a Property Dualist” (JCS 9(12):57–64, 2002). [philpapers +2](https://philpapers.org/rec/SEAWIA) But Searle’s argument there, that property dualism collapses into a dilemma between epiphenomenalism and overdetermination, is the strongest version of the pressure the article attributes to Zimmerman. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_dualism) The body never states it.

**Missing figures.** Kripke, Nagel, Broad, O’Connor’s emergentism, Nida-Rümelin, Stoljar and Robinson’s SEP epiphenomenalism entry are all absent. Epiphenomenalism is treated as a “temptation”, not as a variety with its own defenders. That variety, as Jackson’s 1982 “Epiphenomenal Qualia” defended it, is the historical source of the knowledge argument the article invokes. [Wikipedia +2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Cameron_Jackson)

**“Epiphenomenalism is not a natural implication of property dualism.”** This understates the problem. The standard route to epiphenomenalism is property dualism plus causal closure plus a ban on systematic overdetermination. That is Kim’s exclusion argument, and Searle’s 2002 dilemma. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_dualism) The article says closure is “an additional commitment ... that the Map rejects”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) That reframes a widely held inductive premise (Papineau) as an optional extra and never engages the argument for it.

**The illusionism replies.** The “regress problem” assumes that “seemings are themselves phenomenal states.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) Frankish 2016 §1.1 states that the posited introspective representations “are not themselves phenomenally conscious ones,” and calls the alternative “self-defeating.” §1.2 defines quasi-phenomenal properties as non-phenomenal physical properties that introspection misrepresents. §3.2 answers the “appearance is the reality” objection directly. A footnote cites Pereboom 2011 for why no infinite regress follows. [github](https://keithfrankish.github.io/articles/Frankish_2016_Illusionism_as_a_theory_of_consciousness_eprint.pdf) The article’s reply begs the question against the view it names and engages none of these moves.

“Introspection survives debunking” misdescribes the illusionist inference. The argument is not “introspection is fallible, therefore its object is illusory”. It is that a complete functional explanation of problem-intuitions, independent of phenomenal properties, undercuts their evidential value. [github](https://keithfrankish.github.io/articles/Frankish_2016_Illusionism_as_a_theory_of_consciousness_eprint.pdf) That is Chalmers’s 2018 debunking structure.

The “contemplative challenge” treats practitioner reports of contentless awareness as evidence against representationalism. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) Reports are exactly the explananda the meta-problem targets. Citing reports as independent evidence against a theory of reports is an evidential-independence failure.

[ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/nebior/abstract/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106296~a-beautiful-loop-an-active-inference-theory-of-consciousness) The paper’s abstract calls the theory “deeply revealing about altered states, meditation”, and a journal highlight says it explains “meditation, psychedelics, and minimal states”. The article treats contentless-awareness reports as embarrassing for physicalist or representational accounts. It never addresses the leading physicalist model built specifically to explain those reports. Predictive processing appears nowhere in the article.

### 2.5 Coherence of “either view” with the tenets

[unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) The accepted set is interactionist property dualism plus substance dualism, and the site’s commitment to Born-rule-preserving selection constrains the interactionist property dualist further. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/interactionist-dualism/)

**Tenet 4 cannot be carried by property dualism as the site states it.** The live background-commitments page says the indexical objection to many-worlds “needs a determinate ‘I’ whose branch-assignment is a real further fact”. It says Tenets 1 (the substance-leaning sub-reading), 3 and 4 “all draw on this one root” (Posit One). It says the operational framework “runs substance-dualist”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/background-commitments/)[unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/background-commitments/) The article itself concedes that property dualism “must explain why *this* brain’s phenomenal properties are *mine*”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) Since No Many Worlds is a stated tenet, the tenet package is not neutral. Only the wording of Tenet 1 is. The article’s line that “the distinction matters less than it might seem for the Map’s core commitments” contradicts its parent page. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)

**“Preference” vs “load-bearing”.** The article calls the substance lean “a philosophical preference, not a doctrinal requirement”. Its own next section is headed “Where the Substance-Leaning Becomes Load-Bearing”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)[unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) A commitment that the agency cluster and Tenet 4 cannot do without is a requirement of the framework, whatever layer it is filed under. The tenet-level/posit-level layering is the mechanism by which leakage is concealed. Calling it a reconciliation does not make it one.

**Survival and personal identity.** The article says property dualism makes survival “harder”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) The `personal-identity` page (search copy) says the Map makes survival “a coherent possibility” provided indexical identity is preserved. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/) The `self-and-self-consciousness` page gives the Map’s position as a “non-physical subject” that “persists through qualitative change”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/) The `haecceity` page says the irreducible element is “not a soul-substance” but a process. 1. **Claude-on-Claude circular review.** This article has had at least six deep reviews.
