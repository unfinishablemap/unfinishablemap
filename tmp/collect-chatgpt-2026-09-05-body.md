# External referee report: moral-status register

## Overall verdict

**Major revision is warranted.** The page is unusually candid about its liabilities: it labels both entries framework-internal, gives them no independent empirical support, calls the no-self reply “contested, not tested,” concedes that the Feinberg argument locates rather than settles the dispute with biocentrism, admits that P-MS2’s enhancement direction is an orphan, and acknowledges that the status/stakes distinction merely relocates the utility-monster problem. Those are genuine strengths. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/moral-status/)

But the candour has not propagated into the positions’ calibration. The central problems are:

1. **P-MS1 is not derived from phenomenal value realism.** It requires several additional normative bridge principles, one of which the cited parent article explicitly calls a “further commitment rather than a corollary.”
2. **P-MS2 blocks one invalid argument for scalar status but does not positively establish threshold status.**
3. **The marginal-cases treatment contains a direct unresolved contradiction about permanently comatose humans.**
4. **The no-self response may preserve the badness of an experiential moment, but it does not yet establish that a diachronic entity has moral status or can be wronged.**
5. **Feinberg is substantively misused.** Attfield is compressed in a way that omits a distinction closely resembling the Map’s own status/stakes distinction.
6. **“Developed” is too high a maturity band for both entries.** P-MS2 is explicitly orphaned; P-MS1’s crucial welfare-to-status and moment-to-subject transitions remain unworked.

I audited the live version marked as last modified on **27 August 2026**. Repository history confirms that P-MS2 was introduced on 24 August and that the momentary-locus/no-self revision was added on 27 August. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/moral-status/)

## 1. Are the confidence bands earned?

### Summary assessment

| Entry | Current calibration | Referee assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P-MS1** | moderate · D · moderate centrality · developed · indirect · framework-internal | A single band should not cover this conjunction. Generic sentientism might merit **moderate philosophical plausibility**, but phenomenal exclusivism plus necessity, sufficiency, threshold capacity and momentary bearership merits **low overall credence** until separated and argued. Maturity should be **programme**. |
| **P-MS2** | low · D · low centrality · developed · none · framework-internal | **Low credence is appropriate.** But D/none are the wrong categories for a predominantly normative position; they should be **n/a/n/a**. Maturity should be **programme**. Low global centrality is defensible, but it is not peripheral relative to P-MS1 because the page makes it answer a named defeater. |

The Map’s calibration schema expressly says that **external-evidence grade and empirical discriminability are n/a for normative positions**. “D” means no independent empirical support for a first-order claim about the world; “none” means empirically equivalent rival descriptions. Neither is the right description of a normative claim about what grounds standing. “Developed” requires a worked argument, whereas “programme” is for a commitment without one. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/methodology-and-calibration/)

### P-MS1

P-MS1 packages at least six claims:

- valenced experience exists as a genuinely phenomenal property;
- possessing its capacity is sufficient for moral status;
- possessing it is necessary for moral status;
- functional sentience without phenomenality is insufficient;
- standing capacity, rather than present experience, is the relevant condition;
- a momentary experiential locus is an adequate status-bearer.

The page says its credence is “capped by P-VS3.” That supplies only an **upper bound**, not positive support for all the added conjuncts. P-VS3 says that intrinsic value resides in phenomenal experience. It does not establish the exclusivity of sentience as a ground of moral status, the sufficiency of valence for wrongability, or the adequacy of momentary loci. Indeed, the value register itself recognises the distinction between P-VS3 and P-MS1. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/value-in-selection/)

The best calibration would therefore be to **split P-MS1**. The metaphysical phenomenal-reading component can be D/indirect; the normative necessity and sufficiency components should be n/a/n/a. The distinctive conjunction should not inherit “moderate” merely because its weakest upstream premise is moderate.

“Developed” is especially hard to defend. The parent argument says its second step—beneficiaryhood requires experience—“carries the weight” and cannot be established by defining beneficiaryhood phenomenally. More decisively, the same article states that the converse from consciousness grounding value to consciousness sufficing for moral status **is not delivered by the argument** and is instead an additional commitment. [The Unfinishable Map+1](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

The no-self article likewise describes the construction of diachronic harm or welfare from momentary loci as the “least-developed” part of the reply. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value/)

**Recommended calibration:** generic sentientism, moderate; phenomenal exclusivism, low; full P-MS1 conjunction, low; maturity, programme.

### P-MS2

The low credence band is honest and justified. P-MS2 depends partly on P-VS4, which itself has low credence and acknowledges that only its aesthetic dimension has a worked dissociation argument; its eudaimonic, autonomy, epistemic and relational dimensions remain largely table-level commitments. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/value-in-selection/)

But the other bands are not internally consistent:

- **External evidence D** should be **n/a**, because threshold versus scalar status is normative.
- **Empirical discriminability none** should be **n/a**, not “none.”
- **Developed** conflicts with the page’s admission that the enhancement/upward direction is argued in no article and remains an orphan.
- **Low centrality** is plausible across the whole Map, but P-MS2 is presented as answering P-MS1’s marginal-cases defeater. It cannot simultaneously be P-MS1’s answer to a live objection and merely an expendable clarification with no bearing on P-MS1’s support.

The page’s own methodological rule says that disclosure should produce a scoping or calibration change rather than merely coexist with the unchanged downstream claim. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/methodology-and-calibration/)

**Recommended calibration:** low · n/a · low globally/moderate within the moral-status cluster · programme · n/a · framework-internal.

## 2. The derivation from value realism is invalid as stated

Let P-VS3 be represented approximately as:

> **V:** All intrinsic value is instantiated in phenomenal experience.

P-MS1 asserts:

> **S:** An entity has moral status if and only if it possesses the capacity for valenced experience.

**V does not entail S.**

At least five bridge premises are missing:

1. **Status bridge:** an entity has moral status exactly when there are non-derivative moral reasons concerning it for its own sake.
2. **Welfare exclusivity:** all such reasons must derive from the entity’s welfare or intrinsically valuable experience.
3. **Beneficiary bridge:** an entity is a welfare beneficiary exactly when it can undergo valenced experience.
4. **Bearer identity:** the entity whose experience has value is the same entity that bears moral status.
5. **Grounds exclusivity:** life, agency, autonomy, relationships, membership, rational nature and other proposed grounds cannot independently confer status.

These are substantive normative and metaphysical claims. None follows merely from locating intrinsic value in phenomenal states.

The literature distinguishes at least two notions that the page moves between:

- utilitarian **moral considerability**, in which welfare interests enter a calculation;
- stronger non-utilitarian status or wrongability, involving reasons owed to the entity that can constrain aggregate promotion.

A being’s pain supplying a reason for action does not by itself establish rights, stringent protections, fairness claims, or every other implication ordinarily associated with being wronged. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/grounds-moral-status/)

There is also a category shift from **value-bearer** to **status-bearer**. P-VS3 treats experiential qualities or tokens as intrinsically valuable. P-MS1 attributes status to a system or subject with a standing capacity. Moving from “this pain is bad” to “this organism, person, stream or momentary locus can be wronged” requires an account of which entity inherits reasons from the valuable state.

The target page itself implicitly recognises the non-entailment when it says that P-VS3 would survive a widening of the moral circle beyond sentience. If P-MS1 were genuinely derived from P-VS3, such a widening would require rejecting at least one derivational bridge. The page should therefore say:

> P-VS3 motivates a welfare-based route to sentientism; P-MS1 adds the further normative commitments that welfare is the exclusive ground of standing and that capacity for valenced experience is necessary and sufficient for welfare subjecthood.

That would agree with the parent article’s description of sufficiency as a “further commitment,” rather than contradicting it. [The Unfinishable Map+1](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/moral-status/)

## 3. Biocentrism: fairly introduced, inadequately answered

### What the page gets right

The register correctly identifies biocentrism as a more direct rival to sentientist necessity than ecocentrism. It also avoids caricaturing biocentrism as mystical: organisms exhibit forms of self-maintenance, development and biological flourishing that can intelligibly be described as goods of their own. The page commendably admits that invoking Feinberg only locates the central disagreement rather than resolving it. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/sentientism/)

The HIV example is also handled more carefully than a simple reductio. The surrounding sentientism article acknowledges that Taylor has priority principles for dangerous organisms and that the example does not demonstrate that biocentrism is incoherent. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/sentientism/)

### Where the representation is incomplete

#### Taylor

The central description of Taylor—living beings as teleological centres possessing goods of their own and inherent worth—is substantially accurate. Taylor does deny that consciousness is necessary for a good of one’s own. [Rintintin+1](https://rintintin.colorado.edu/~vancecd/phil308/Taylor.pdf)

Two corrections are needed.

First, the register says that the biocentrist “concedes that status attaches to individuals.” Taylor’s 1981 essay also attributes goods of their own and inherent worth to **species populations and communities of life**. He is therefore not cleanly captured by an exclusively individualist foil. [Rintintin+1](https://rintintin.colorado.edu/~vancecd/phil308/Taylor.pdf)

Second, Taylor does not simply infer moral standing from a descriptive fact about biological functioning. He treats respect for nature as an **ultimate moral attitude**, not as something derived from a higher norm. The Map may reject that normative starting point, but it should not present Taylor as merely failing to notice the transition from biological description to normativity. [Rintintin](https://rintintin.colorado.edu/~vancecd/phil308/Taylor.pdf)

#### Attfield

Attfield is the most important missed opportunity. His 1987 article expressly distinguishes:

- all living beings having moral standing;
- equal moral considerability or inherent worth;
- unequal **moral significance** and unequal weight of their goods.

That structure is strikingly similar to the Map’s distinction between threshold status and graded stakes. Attfield therefore cannot be treated merely as a weaker or hierarchical biocentrist who concedes the Map’s central architecture. He offers a biocentric implementation of that very architecture. [Philosophica+1](https://www.philosophica.ugent.be/article/id/82495/download/pdf/)

He also formulates the argument explicitly:

- all living beings have goods of their own;
- whatever has a good of its own has moral standing.

Attfield admits that the second premise cannot be demonstrated, but defends it through the centrality of beneficence and susceptibility to benefit or harm. The Map needs to rebut that bridge rather than simply restate its rival phenomenal bridge. [Philosophica](https://www.philosophica.ugent.be/article/id/82495/download/pdf/)

#### Varner

The Varner reference is real and broadly relevant. But the short description misses his hybrid structure. Varner grants interests to all organisms while giving priority to organisms with conscious desires and further priority to certain characteristically human interests. He is therefore useful precisely because he shows that accepting biological interests does not require flat biotic egalitarianism. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/49452)

### Is the answer adequate?

No. The eventual answer is:

> normative teleology requires an experiencer.

But that is the proposition under dispute. The biocentrist argues that being an internally organised, self-maintaining entity with a good of its own can ground beneficiaryhood. The Map answers by reserving “normative” beneficiaryhood for perspectives. Without an independent argument, this is **dialectical restatement**, not rebuttal.

A stronger answer would need to explain one of the following:

- why benefit and harm are conceptually phenomenal rather than merely biological;
- why duties for an entity’s own sake require phenomenally accessible interests;
- why Attfield’s beneficence bridge fails;
- or why a living entity’s good can generate agent-relative or indirect reasons but never status-conferring reasons.

At present, biocentrism is fairly announced but not defeated. Accordingly, the necessity half of P-MS1 should not receive moderate credence on the existing treatment.

## 4. Marginal cases: the most serious internal inconsistency

The standard marginal-cases argument is presented fairly **against sophisticated-cognition thresholds**. Rationality, language or autobiographical memory would exclude infants and many cognitively disabled humans while admitting some non-human animals. That is a genuine problem for species-based cognitive thresholds.

But the article mishandles the transition from that argument to sentientism.

### Three populations are conflated

- Human infants are generally capable of valenced experience.
- Most severely cognitively impaired humans remain sentient.
- Permanently unconscious or irreversibly comatose humans may lack both occurrent sentience and the capacity to recover it.

Only the third is a direct marginal case for P-MS1. The others are marginal relative to rationality or agency criteria, not relative to valenced experience.

The claim that these are humans “whose protection nobody is willing to withdraw” is also too sweeping. There is no comparable consensus regarding humans permanently incapable of consciousness, and philosophical debates explicitly question their intrinsic status. [The Unfinishable Map+1](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/sentientism/)

### Direct contradiction

P-MS2 states that:

- removing the capacity for valenced experience removes moral status;
- yet the Map has declined to let a comatose human’s status fall with capacity.

Both cannot be true without a further distinction. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/moral-status/)

Possible repairs include:

1. **Standing-capacity interpretation:** a temporarily unconscious person retains a dispositional capacity for valence. This does not cover irreversible coma.
2. **Historical-capacity interpretation:** once a subject has had valenced experience, status persists. This makes present capacity unnecessary and falsifies P-MS1 as worded.
3. **Relational or trajectory grounds:** status is retained because of history, relationships or prior projects. This introduces additional grounds of status and again falsifies the necessity claim.
4. **Derivative-duty interpretation:** the permanently unconscious individual lacks intrinsic status, but there remain strong duties concerning their body, prior wishes, relatives and social practices. This preserves P-MS1 but requires the Map to accept the uncomfortable conclusion openly.

The sentientism article invokes trajectory, history and standing relationships to protect marginal humans. But standard moral-status analysis distinguishes such relationship-generated reasons from reasons constitutive of an entity’s status. They may justify treating someone *as if* they retain full status without showing that they actually retain it on the proposed intrinsic criterion. [The Unfinishable Map+1](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/sentientism/)

The page currently oscillates between options 3 and 4 without choosing.

## 5. P-MS2 does not establish threshold status

P-MS2 makes one correct logical point:

> From “P is sufficient for Q,” it does not follow that more P produces more Q.

That defeats a bad argument from sufficiency to scalarity. It does **not** show that Q is threshold rather than scalar. At most, it shows that P-MS1 alone leaves the question open.

Several possibilities remain:

- possession of any valence capacity confers minimal status, while richer capacities confer higher status;
- valence confers one tier and agency, autonomy or self-consciousness confer further tiers;
- intensity or temporal integration of valence affects the strength or kinds of status-conferring reasons;
- minimal status is threshold but full moral status is separately grounded;
- “capacity” itself is scalar, multidimensional or vague.

Threshold accounts in the literature expressly leave open whether other properties confer lesser or higher degrees of status. They also require an explanation of why differences in degree or exercise of the grounding capacity do not matter, rather than merely observing that sufficiency does not imply monotonicity. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/grounds-moral-status/)

The site therefore needs an **affirmative equality argument**. Candidates include:

- equal wrongness of inflicting equal suffering;
- equal consideration of equal interests;
- a non-aggregation or separateness-of-persons argument;
- a rights threshold triggered by having a point of view;
- a contractual or fitting-attitude explanation of why degree is irrelevant after entry.

None is presently developed.

### Status versus stakes

The distinction can be coherent. Two beings may have equal status while one loses more through death, has richer interests or can undergo more intense suffering. Standard discussions similarly warn that stronger reasons to save one being need not imply higher status. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/grounds-moral-status/)

But the Map has not shown that its distinction is **action-guiding rather than verbal**. If all choices involving rescue, resource distribution, killing and suffering are ultimately weighted by graded stakes, then declaring status equal may change no verdict. P-MS2 itself lists practical indistinguishability as a defeater and concedes that enhanced intensity could recreate an unbounded utility monster. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/moral-status/)

The page needs at least one case where:

- a scalar-status theory permits an act;
- threshold status forbids it;
- and the difference cannot be recreated merely by assigning different stakes.

Without such a case, P-MS2 is a terminological allocation of scalarity from one column to another.

## 6. The no-self objection

### Representation

This is the strongest part of the objection handling. The associated article distinguishes:

- elimination of a substantial or persisting self;
- elimination of any momentary first-personal locus;
- a phenomenal moment’s badness;
- diachronic welfare, narrative value and prudential concern.

It does not simply assume that Buddhism or Parfit denies experience. It grants the reductionist structure for argument’s sake and draws on Zahavi, Strawson and Albahari only for the thin subject-pole structure, not as supporters of dualism. [The Unfinishable Map+1](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value/)

It also explicitly concedes that the residual dispute may be close to terminological and that constructing diachronic benefit from momentary loci remains underdeveloped. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value/)

### Adequacy of the reply

The reply secures less than P-MS1 requires.

The key move is:

> felt badness is undergone; whatever is undergone is for whoever undergoes it.

Against a no-ownership theorist, however, that risks circularity. The opponent can accept that a painful event occurs while denying that “undergoing” introduces an additional owner or metaphysical subject. The no-self article itself recognises that the reductionist may regard “bad for a momentary locus” as merely “this feeling-moment is bad” with an owner smuggled into the grammar. [The Unfinishable Map+1](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value/)

Even granting the momentary locus, the argument establishes at most:

> this experiential moment has subject-relative badness.

It does not yet establish:

- which continuing entity has status;
- which entity is harmed by deprivation or death;
- how preferences extending across time belong to one beneficiary;
- whether a momentary locus can possess rights;
- or what it means to wrong one locus now for the sake of another locus later.

The associated article expressly concedes that diachronic harm requires separate treatment. Yet the target page uses the momentary-locus answer to preserve the full moral-status derivation without lowering its credence or maturity. That is overreach. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value/)

The proposed defeater—a verified valenced experience genuinely “for no one”—is also poorly operationalised. The page itself explains why any report necessarily arrives through a later reporter. A condition acknowledged to be almost structurally inaccessible should not be presented as if it provides ordinary empirical exposure. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value/)

The legitimate conclusion is narrower:

> No-self arguments do not obviously eliminate token phenomenal badness.

The illegitimate additional conclusion is:

> therefore a momentary locus possesses the kind of moral status required for wronging, rights and diachronic welfare.

## 8. Tenet-protective bracketing

The page is not crudely tenet-protective. It openly records framework dependence and names conditions under which P-MS1 would fall. That is preferable to presenting sentientism as independently established.

Nevertheless, the argument is protected in several subtler ways.

### 1. Tenet 1 is made to do more than irreducibility supplies

Dualism may support the claim that phenomenal properties are irreducible. It does not establish:

- that moral standing is exhausted by phenomenal value;
- that valence is necessary and sufficient;
- that the boundary is sharp rather than vague;
- that capacity is threshold rather than scalar;
- or that a momentary locus is a rights-bearer.

The page says the phenomenal reading is “licensed” by Tenet 1 and that it converts hard-case uncertainty into ignorance of a “real boundary.” But irreducibility alone does not entail a binary moral boundary. A real phenomenal property can have vague instantiation conditions, multidimensional realisation or gradable capacities.

### 2. Functionalism is disadvantaged by description

The sentientism article contrasts a real phenomenal boundary with functional thresholds that are partly stipulated. Yet a functionalist can hold that there is a determinate causal or organisational natural kind while accepting epistemic uncertainty and semantic vagueness at its edges. The existence of borderline classification does not show that the property is merely conventional.

### 3. Illusionism is acknowledged but fenced off

The articles candidly state that no in-framework refutation of illusionism is supplied and that Dualism simply denies it. That is honest. But it means Tenet 1 functions as a **premise fixing the answer**, not as evidence for P-MS1. The calibration should reflect the resulting conditionality more strongly. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/sentientism/)

### 4. No-self exposure is largely non-operational

A “report of valenced experience for no one” is close to inaccessible by the page’s own account. Meanwhile, the live countermodel—that felt badness need not add a metaphysical owner—is classified as nearly terminological. This makes the view difficult to move without first accepting its preferred subject vocabulary.

### 5. Cascade isolation preserves more than the argument has earned

The no-self article explicitly says it was constructed so that the value chain does not fall with the Map’s persisting-self commitments. That isolation is defensible for **token phenomenal badness**. It is not yet defensible for the stronger claims about moral status, wronging and diachronic welfare carried by P-MS1. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value/)

### 6. Disclosure substitutes for recalibration

The page discloses that P-MS2’s upward argument is absent and that the no-self reply is untested, yet leaves “developed” and P-MS1’s moderate credence unchanged. This is precisely the failure mode the Map’s P-M5 discipline warns against: admissions that do not narrow or recalibrate downstream claims. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/methodology-and-calibration/)

## 9. Integration with surrounding articles

### Direct contradiction with `consciousness-value-connection`

The moral-status page says P-MS1 is **derived** from P-VS3 through the consciousness-value connection. The cited parent article says the converse—from consciousness grounding value to consciousness sufficing for moral status—**is not delivered by that argument** and is a further commitment. This is not a nuance; it is a direct contradiction over inferential status. [The Unfinishable Map+1](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/moral-status/)

### P-MS2 remains genuinely orphaned

The moral-status register admits that the enhancement/upward direction is argued nowhere. The natural home, `ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism`, cites P-MS1 for equal standing but contains no P-MS2 entry and does not defend the threshold conclusion. Its current positions list still includes P-MS1 but not P-MS2. [The Unfinishable Map+1](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/moral-status/)

### Dependence on weak P-VS4 support

P-MS2 routes graded stakes through P-VS4. But P-VS4 is low-credence and admits that only aesthetic value has a worked separability argument. The dimensions most relevant to enhancement—agency, understanding and eudaimonic value—are among the under-argued dimensions. P-MS2 therefore treats a weak upstream taxonomy as if it already supplied an adequate stakes metric. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/value-in-selection/)

### Inconsistent handling of coma in `sentientism`

`sentientism` says a typical adult mammal may have more at stake than a permanently comatose human and invokes trajectory, history and relationships to bound the consequence. P-MS2 simultaneously says loss of valence capacity removes status and that the Map refuses status loss for the comatose human. The articles need one shared account of temporary unconsciousness, irreversible unconsciousness, prior capacity and relational reasons. [The Unfinishable Map+1](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/sentientism/)

### Drift in `ethics-under-dualism`

That article initially says subjective experience or consciousness grounds moral status and later states that moral patienthood extends to all conscious beings and only conscious beings. It subsequently narrows the criterion to felt valence. An affectively neutral conscious being therefore moves in and out of the moral circle depending on the paragraph. P-MS1 requires the narrower formulation throughout. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/ethics-under-dualism/)

### Environmental article attacks the wrong strongest rival

`phenomenal-normativity-environmental-ethics` is listed as a place where P-MS1 is argued, but it devotes its central opposition to ecocentrism and ecological wholes. It does not engage Taylor’s individual organisms, Attfield’s standing/significance distinction or Varner’s biological interests. That is especially problematic because the register says biocentrism, not ecocentrism, is the live rival. [The Unfinishable Map+1](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/moral-status/)

### `phenomenal-value-realism` supplies a premise, not the status argument

That article argues for phenomenal value and plural dimensions of experiential value. It does not independently argue the necessity or sufficiency of valence for moral status. It should be listed as **“premise supplied by”**, not as a location in which P-MS1 itself is argued. [The Unfinishable Map](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenal-value-realism/)

## Concrete potential improvements

### Changes to `positions/moral-status`

1. **Split P-MS1 into separate register entries:**

- P-MS1a: valenced experience suffices for minimal moral considerability;
- P-MS1b: valenced experience is necessary—no non-sentient entity has intrinsic standing;
- P-MS1c: the criterion is phenomenal rather than functional;
- P-MS1d: a momentary experiential locus can bear the relevant reasons;
- P-MS1e: standing capacity, rather than occurrent valence, is the relevant temporal condition.
2. **Replace “derives” with “adds as a further normative commitment”** unless the missing bridge principles are explicitly defended.
3. **State the full bridge argument formally**, including why welfare is the exclusive ground of standing and why the bearer of valuable experience is the bearer who can be wronged.
4. **Recalibrate P-MS1:** normative components n/a/n/a; phenomenal-metaphysical component D/indirect; overall conjunction low credence; maturity programme.
5. **Split P-MS2** into:

- a threshold claim about minimal standing;
- a denial that degree of valence capacity raises status;
- a pluralist claim that enhancement raises stakes;
- an equal-consideration principle governing interests.
6. **Recalibrate P-MS2** to low · n/a · programme · n/a. Do not call it developed while its upward argument is explicitly orphaned.
7. **Resolve the coma contradiction.** Separate temporary coma, irreversible coma, retained dispositional capacity, historical capacity, prior wishes and relationship-generated duties. State plainly whether an irreversibly non-sentient human retains intrinsic moral status.

### Methodological changes

1. **Enforce normative-axis validation:** a register linter should reject D/none for purely normative positions and require either n/a or decomposition of mixed claims.
2. **Add a conjunction rule:** no compound position may inherit an upstream premise’s credence without separately calibrating each added conjunct.
3. **Add a maturity gate:** any position described as orphaned, untested, “least-developed,” or lacking its central bridge argument should automatically be capped at programme.
4. **Introduce contradiction checks** for inferential-status language such as “derived,” “entailed,” “motivated” and “further commitment,” and for verdict conflicts such as capacity loss both removing and not removing status.
5. **Require pinpoint citation ledgers:** for every historical source, record the exact proposition supported, edition, chapter or page, and whether the source endorses the Map’s bridge or merely supplies vocabulary.
6. **Require strongest-rival coverage:** a necessity claim should engage at least one contemporary defender of the excluded class and one contemporary pluralist account, not only canonical twentieth-century sources.
7. **Apply an action-difference test to conceptual distinctions:** status/stakes, standing/treatment and token-value/subject-status distinctions should each be accompanied by cases showing what practical or inferential work the distinction performs.
8. **Trigger dependency re-review automatically:** when a no-self article or moral-status entry changes, every page that calls the claim derived, developed or settled should lose its stability status until the dependency is rechecked.
