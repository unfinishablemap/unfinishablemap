# Pre-Publication Referee Report: Mine-ness and the Ownership Void

This manuscript (*Mine-ness and the Ownership Void*, hosted at `https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/mine-ness/`, last modified November 19, 2026) is unsuitable for publication in a top-tier philosophy journal and warrants rejection rather than minor revision. Its central thesis—that clinical dissociations expose a separable phenomenal feature of "mine-ness" (*for-me-ness* / *Jemeinigkeit*) whose introspective opacity reveals an "Ownership Void" aligned with non-reductive dualism—rests on a superseded 2000s-era double-dissociation taxonomy, an unresolved terminological equivocation, selective appropriation of naturalistic sources, and severe neglect of the 2020–2025 philosophical and neurocognitive literature.   

A scope caveat must be stated up front: direct retrieval of the live target page (`https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/mine-ness/`) failed during review due to an access error. This report therefore evaluates the manuscript from its published catalog summary, indexed verbatim excerpts and reference records of `concepts/mine-ness.md`, cross-referenced companion articles on `unfinishablemap.org` (specifically on depersonalization, thought insertion, and self-consciousness), and the site's live changelog (`https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/`). The retrieved changelog logs two actions referencing `mine-ness`: converting `"the owner of experience"` at L86 into a link to `pudgalavada`, and noting that the article contains no text addressing trauma or the dissociative subtype of PTSD. While no substantive empirical revisions to `mine-ness` appear in the retrieved changelog entries, because the live page carries a November 19, 2026 modification date and could not be fetched directly, any localized phrasing changes made on that date cannot be independently verified and are flagged accordingly below.   

## Empirical Accuracy Against the 2020s Literature

The manuscript anchors its empirical case in a clean clinical double dissociation drawn from Shaun Gallagher (2000, 2012): schizophrenic thought insertion allegedly disrupts the sense of agency while preserving ownership, whereas depersonalization disorder (DPD) allegedly exhibits *"the reverse dissociation... where ownership can fade while ordinary agency remains intact"* (L48), reinforced by somatoparaphrenic limb-disavowal (L116) and meditative selflessness. Rather than functioning as clean natural experiments that toggle a primitive ownership tag independently of agency and content, every clinical domain the manuscript cites has been substantially reinterpreted by 2020–2025 empirical and computational research as an entanglement of predictive, interoceptive, affective, and hyperreflexive processes.   

### Depersonalization Disorder: Disrupted Agency and Selective Bodily Alteration

The manuscript's claim at L48 that depersonalization removes felt ownership *"while ordinary agency remains intact"* is contradicted by contemporary theoretical and empirical work, including authors the manuscript itself relies upon. Within an active-inference framework, Ciaunica, Seth, Limanowski, Hesp, and Friston (2022) argue that DPD arises from aberrant somatosensory attenuation and precision weighting that disrupt *both* the sense of self and the sense of agency and control over perceptions and actions; clinical testimony frequently features patients reporting *"I 'know' I'm in control, but I'm not 'feeling' in control"* and feeling reduced to *"automatic pilot"*. Testing implicit and explicit agency via intentional binding in a non-clinical sample with high versus low depersonalization experiences, Ciaunica, Ayache, Haggard, Nakul, Bonnet, and Auvray (2024) found no overall group difference on standard agency indices, but observed significant temporal-perception distortions for short intervals without self-initiated motion and a trend toward a more time-sensitive explicit sense of agency driven by the *Alienation from Surroundings* (derealization) facet rather than anomalous body awareness ("mineness")—indicating that agency is intertwined with spatiotemporal world-presence rather than cleanly dissociated from bodily ownership.   

More damagingly, the manuscript misrepresents two of its own central references on this exact issue. Deane, Miller, and Wilkinson (2020)—cited in the manuscript for modeling the self as a `"temporally deep generative model"`—explicitly conceptualize DPD as a **sustained loss of allostatic self-efficacy and inferred agentive control**, in which the generative model stops positing the self as an endogenous controller of self-evidencing outcomes. Even Alexandre Billon (2023), the manuscript's primary clinical ally for mineness-loss, lists **deagentivation** (*"intentions-in-action lacking their normal mineness, making the actions they guide feel alien"*) as a constitutive manifestation of depersonalization.   

Nor does empirical evidence support treating DPD as the uniform subtraction of a single global "mine-ness" tag across all mental states. In a controlled sequential-matching study comparing non-clinical groups with high versus low depersonalization traits, Woźniak, McEllin, Hohwy, and Ciaunica (2023) found that both groups exhibited the standard self-prioritization pattern for their own name and for arbitrarily self-associated geometric shapes, differing *solely* in their processing of bodily stimuli (avatar faces). This domain-specific bodily profile converges with the four-factor structure of the Cambridge Depersonalisation Scale (Anomalous Body Experience, Emotional Numbing, Anomalous Subjective Recall, and Alienation from Surroundings), with interoceptive predictive-coding models tying DPD to attenuated Heartbeat Evoked Potential (HEP) modulation during interoceptive attention (Gatus, Jamieson, & Stevenson, 2022), and with neurobiological research on trauma-linked depersonalization in the dissociative subtype of PTSD—a clinical literature that the changelog confirms is entirely absent from `mine-ness.md`.   

### Thought Insertion: An Abandoned Binary Model Backed by a Constructed Quote

In `§Thought Insertion` (L80–82), indexed excerpts of `concepts/mine-ness.md` present Gallagher's agency-lost/ownership-preserved account as `"the actual phenomenology—mine but not authored—shows the features are separable"`, supported by a constructed, unsourced patient quotation (`"The patient says: 'this thought occurs in my mind but I did not produce it'"`). Even if late-2026 edits hedged that local phrasing, the manuscript's published double-dissociation architecture remains structurally dependent on Gallagher (2000, 2012).   

The 2020–2025 philosophy of psychiatry and clinical literature has eroded this binary model from multiple directions. López-Silva (2025; online 2024) undermines its baseline premise about ordinary cognition, pointing out that everyday thinking lacks a uniform phenomenal "sense of agency" or "emotion of authorship" (echoing Strawson and Proust), and arguing that inserted thoughts must be understood within broader disruptions of reality-experience, emotional coloring, and intersubjective social phenomenology. Moreover, as Billon (2013) and Feyaerts, Nelson, and Sass (2024) emphasize, intrusive thoughts in obsessive-compulsive disorder arrive unbidden and without felt agency yet are not disowned, showing that loss of felt agency is insufficient for thought insertion. Empirically, a 20-year prospective study by López-Silva, Harrow, Jobe, Tufano, Harrow, and Rosen (2024; online 2022) tracks thought insertion alongside thought withdrawal, thought broadcasting, and auditory verbal hallucinations, proposing that shifting psychotic symptom constellations reflect pseudo-coherent realities shaped by interacting top-down priors and bottom-up anomalies rather than an isolated modular deficit in mental authorship.   

Contemporary philosophical analyses converge against the manuscript's binary framing. Mathieson (2023) rejects the standard agency-absent/subjectivity-intact model, noting clinical evidence that many patients reject textbook "inserted into my head" formulations and cannot separate content-alienation from mode of presentation. Patel (2023) challenges the shared "realism" assumption that thought-insertion patients actually experience a token thought matching their report, defending an anti-realist simulationist alternative. Lane (2020) directly critiques the Zahavian minimal-self hypothesis (specifically Henriksen, Parnas, & Zahavi, 2019), arguing that scant empirical evidence supports the necessity of minimal selfhood for consciousness and that phenomenological conceptual analysis allows excessive interpretive latitude. Most tellingly, even leading architects of phenomenological psychopathology have revised the simple diminished-self model: Feyaerts and Sass (2024) and Sass and Feyaerts (2024) demonstrate that schizophrenia-spectrum self-disorders equally feature **heightened, exaggerated self-presence** (quasi-solipsism and ontological paranoia) unified by **hyperreflexivity**—in which normally tacit, transparent processes become explicit, alienated objects of attention—which Feyaerts, Nelson, and Sass (2024) analyze as disorders of phenomenal transparency rather than the mere subtraction of an ownership feature.   

### Bodily Ownership: Interoceptive Restoration in Somatoparaphrenia

At L116, the manuscript cites `"somatoparaphrenic limb-disavowal"` alongside depersonalization as among `"the most vivid disruptions"` of mine-ness. Yet it omits the pivotal 2020 clinical-intervention study on disturbed sense of limb ownership (DSO) following right-hemisphere stroke. Jenkinson et al. (2020) evaluated 26 acute right-hemisphere stroke patients with DSO (from 42 recruited across London and Verona, including 16 in an initial feasibility study) in a 2 × 2 repeated-measures protocol contrasting slow, C-tactile (CT)-optimal affective touch (3 cm/s) against fast, CT-suboptimal neutral touch (18 cm/s), delivered by self or experimenter. Experimenter-administered CT-optimal affective touch produced a **statistically significant increase in felt limb ownership**, while voxel-based lesion-symptom mapping linked failure to regain ownership specifically to lesions of the **right insula** and, more substantially, the **right corpus callosum**. Felt limb ownership is thus causally modulated by peripheral spinothalamic CT-afferent interoceptive signals integrated within specific right-hemispheric cortical-callosal networks, rather than reflecting a mysterious non-physical ownership tag.

## Challenged Results, Replication Fragilities, and Citation Failures

Auditing the empirical and bibliographic foundations reveals both unacknowledged methodological crises across the experimental paradigms that underwrite ownership/agency claims and concrete citation defects within the manuscript itself.

First, the manuscript treats **Gallagher's (2000, 2012) agency/ownership double dissociation** as settled clinical consensus, even though both halves have been extensively challenged over the past decade by philosophical and clinical research (Billon, 2013, 2023; Lane, 2020; Ciaunica et al., 2022, 2024; Mathieson, 2023; López-Silva, 2025).   

Second, the three flagship experimental paradigms supporting empirical claims about separable bodily ownership, interoceptive self-awareness, and implicit agency have all faced serious validity and replication challenges in the 2020s, none of which the manuscript registers. In bodily ownership research, Lush, Botan, Scott, Seth, Ward, and Dienes (2020) demonstrated across large samples (n = 156, 404, and 353) that trait hypnotizability ("phenomenological control") substantially predicts rubber hand illusion (RHI) reports, while Lush, Seth, and Dienes (2021) showed in two preregistered studies (n = 140 and n = 45) that hypothesis awareness confounds asynchronous control conditions even on indirect measures like proprioceptive drift. Although Ehrsson, Fotopoulou, Radziun, Longo, and Tsakiris (2022) countered that reanalyzing Lush et al.'s data using standard synchronous-minus-asynchronous subtraction yields no significant correlation with suggestibility (p = 0.219, n = 353), that suggestibility explains roughly 9% of variance across both conditions, and that multisensory integration still induces the RHI in the least suggestible quartile, Lush and Seth (2022) replied that R² = 0.09 represents a medium-sized effect (with ownership ratings increasing by b = 0.76 Likert units per SWASH unit, t = 5.79, p < 0.001, Spearman r = 0.27) and highlighted order effects in which mean ownership agreement emerged only after exposure to the asynchronous control task. Relatedly, Carey, Crucianelli, Preston, and Fotopoulou (2020) found no significant difference in whole-body illusion embodiment between slow affective (3 cm/s) and fast non-affective (18 cm/s) touch across two experiments (p = 0.375, r = 0.14; p = 0.791, r = 0.04), demonstrating that interoceptive-touch effects are paradigm-sensitive rather than uniform.   

Parallel methodological vulnerabilities affect interoception, dissociative neuroimaging, and implicit agency. In interoception research, Desmedt et al. (2020) demonstrated that standard Heartbeat Counting Task (HCT) scores are heavily confounded by time estimation (β = 0.39, p < 0.001) and prior beliefs about resting heart rate (r = 0.27, p = 0.004 under original instructions vs. r = 0.02, p = 0.85 under adapted instructions), reinforcing Desmedt et al.'s (2018) finding that mean HCT accuracy drops by half (from M = 0.61 to M = 0.30) once estimation strategies are discouraged. In clinical neuroimaging, Modesti, Rapisarda, Capriotti, and Del Casale's (2022) systematic review of functional neuroimaging in dissociative disorders cautioned that most included studies suffer from small samples (under 45 patients, including DPD fMRI samples as small as n = 9 patients), heterogeneous diagnostic criteria, medication confounds, and high risk of bias, while Gatus et al. (2022) emphasized the unresolved causal gaps and unreliable clinical treatment outcomes of classical fronto-limbic affective-blunting models of DPD. Finally, even the intentional binding paradigm—standardly treated as an implicit behavioral measure of sense of agency—has been challenged by Gutzeit, Weller, Kürten, and Huestegge (2023), who showed that canonical intentional binding effects can be reproduced as procedural confounds driven by overt-action presence and temporal predictability rather than genuine intentional agency.   

Third, documented records of `concepts/mine-ness.md` reveal four concrete citation and attribution failures inside the manuscript itself:

1. **Constructed clinical quotation (L82):** The manuscript places `"this thought occurs in my mind but I did not produce it"` in quotation marks attributed to `"The patient"` without any underlying clinical case report. Presenting a fabricated composite gloss as verbatim patient testimony fails basic standards of empirical scholarship.
2. **Encyclopedia paraphrase quoted as primary text:** Textual records of `concepts/mine-ness.md` as of November 18, 2026 document a *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* sentence presented in quotation marks and misattributed as Dan Zahavi's verbatim wording. Because the live page could not be retrieved and lists a November 19, 2026 modification date, this audit cannot verify whether that defect was repaired on November 19; every quotation in the manuscript requires primary-source verification prior to any resubmission.
3. **Tendentious comparative-philosophy linking (L86, verified live in changelog):** The manuscript converts the phrase `"the owner of experience"` into a direct link to `pudgalavada`. Yet the site's own analysis of Pudgalavāda notes that Vasubandhu dissolves that framing and contemporary scholarship denies that the *pudgala* functions as an "owner". Subsuming a contested Buddhist non-self position under an "owner of experience" label distorts the source tradition.
4. **Selective co-optation of physicalist allies:** The manuscript recruits Billon (2023) to support separable mineness while suppressing both his *deagentivation* analysis and—as the site's own companion page on depersonalization explicitly concedes—his conclusion that mineness must be explained in **purely neurophysiological terms**. It repeats the identical omission with Deane, Miller, and Wilkinson (2020), citing their `"temporally deep generative model"` while omitting their explicit thesis that active inference dissolves any `"essential subject to appropriate these states as 'me' and 'mine'"`.

## Protective Framings and Motivated Bracketing

Beyond empirical obsolescence, the manuscript deploys four systematic immunizing framings that soften, bracket, or invert adverse evidence to insulate `unfinishablemap.org`'s five non-reductive philosophical commitments (Dualism, Minimal Quantum Interaction, Bidirectional Interaction, No Many Worlds, and Limits on Occam's Razor).   

### Terminological Equivocation Between Universal For-Me-Ness and Detachable Mineness

At L48, the manuscript defines mine-ness as an `"implicit tag"` that varies independently and can vanish in pathology, while immediately identifying it with Dan Zahavi's *for-me-ness* (`"Phenomenologists call this for-me-ness (Dan Zahavi)"`) and Martin Heidegger's *Jemeinigkeit* (§9). This conflates incompatible concepts. Zahavi and Kriegel (2016) and Henriksen, Parnas, and Zahavi (2019) define *for-me-ness* as a **universal, constitutive, non-detachable** structural feature of all conscious experience (*"what-it-is-like-ness is properly speaking what-it-is-like-for-me-ness"*). Conversely, Billon (2023) defines *mineness* as a **contingent, separable, non-universal** phenomenal property that can lapse in DPD, while Guillot (2017) disaggregates *for-me-ness* (the experience being given to a subject), *me-ness* (the subject appearing to herself), and *mineness* (the subject appearing as the owner of the experience).   

This equivocation insulates the manuscript's metaphysical commitments. When arguing that ownership is an independent phenomenal feature separate from content, the manuscript invokes *detachable mineness* in DPD. When arguing for an unrepeatable persisting subject—needed to anchor the site's indexical argument against Many-Worlds and to deny that genuinely anonymous experiences exist—it pivots to Zahavi's universal, `"completely irrelational"` *for-me-ness*. That slip generates an open contradiction with the site's own companion page on thought insertion (which adopts Guillot's distinction, holding *for-me-ness* universal and *mineness* lapsable), and leaves the universalist kind-claim empirically untestable because its global falsifier (*"phenomenal life continuing without any residue of for-me-ness"*) cannot in principle be reported by any patient.   

### Bracketing Metzinger's Ontology While Appropriating His Opacity Mechanism

Indexed records confirm that `mine-ness.md` *"brackets Metzinger's physicalist framework while accepting his structural transparency point"*—endorsing Thomas Metzinger's (2003) Phenomenal Self-Model (PSM) analysis of transparency and autoepistemic closure while rejecting his physicalist ontology. This is an illicit separation. On Metzinger's account, introspection cannot reveal the mechanism of ownership *precisely because* the PSM is a transparent subpersonal neural model whose construction is hidden from attentional access. Once autoepistemic closure explains introspective opacity, inferring an irreducible, non-physical "Ownership Void" commits an epistemic-to-metaphysical slide—mistaking a representational blind spot of biological self-modeling for an ontologically primitive subject.   

Worse still, the manuscript ignores Metzinger's 2020–2024 empirical research program on **Minimal Phenomenal Experience (MPE)** (Metzinger, 2020; Gamma & Metzinger, 2021; Metzinger, 2024). Metzinger (2020) characterizes MPE as pure awareness devoid of spatial, temporal, or subject-object relations, Gamma and Metzinger (2021) operationalize a 12-factor psychometric profile of such meditative states (the MPE-92M questionnaire), and subsequent computational-phenomenology work drawing on Metzinger (2024) documents reports of a **"zero-person perspective"** in which *"the previous 'I' as experiencer, chooser, thinker did not exist"*. This empirical record directly undercuts the manuscript's portrayal of contemplative selflessness as merely approaching the same primitive ownership boundary with positive affective valence.   

### Asymmetric Parity: Equating Post-Hoc Accommodation with Mechanistic Prediction

In `§Relation to Site Perspective → Dualism` (L136), earlier indexed text of `mine-ness.md` asserted that Dualism `"finds direct support"` in the separability of mine-ness, before retreating to a softened `"compatible with, not evidenced by"` framing aligned with the site's companion depersonalization page. Yet even the softened compatibility framing commits a fundamental philosophy-of-science error by portraying the clinical evidence as a neutral standoff. Predictive-processing and active-inference theories (Deane et al., 2020; Ciaunica et al., 2022; Gatus et al., 2022; Gerrans, 2020) specify concrete computational and neurophysiological variables—precision weighting, somatosensory attenuation, HEP modulation, and right-insula CT-afferent integration—that yield **testable, falsifiable predictions**, including selective bodily-versus-abstract self-prioritization shifts in high-DP cohorts (Woźniak et al., 2023) and limb-ownership restoration via slow affective touch (Jenkinson et al., 2020). A non-reductive dualist "interface" or "primitive ownership" posit generates zero distinctive empirical predictions and merely *accommodates* clinical findings after the fact. Predictive power strictly outranks post-hoc accommodation.   

### Immunizing Stratagems Against Localism and Parsimony

When engaging Franz Knappik's (2025; online 2022) deflationary challenge—which argues that the clinical heterogeneity of DPD is better explained by independent breakdowns of modality-specific self-relations (bodily, autonoetic episodic, affective, perceptual) with no need for a global mineness feature—the manuscript neutralizes the refutation by fiat, asserting that even if Knappik is right, *"instead of one ownership mystery, there are many"* (a *"plural for-me-ness"* or family of separable phenomenal features). Converting the empirical failure of one's unitary explanandum into a proliferation of smaller mysteries is a textbook immunizing stratagem. Similarly, invoking an anti-parsimony premise (`Occam's Razor Has Limits`) to dismiss reductive explanations as *"leaving something out"* begs the central question under dispute: whether any irreducible non-physical ownership relation exists to be left out.   

## Untested Counterarguments and Competing Frameworks

A publishable philosophical treatment of mine-ness in the mid-2020s must directly test its thesis against the three primary naturalistic frameworks that challenge phenomenal ownership realism. The manuscript fails to confront any of them at full strength.

### Deflationary "No-Mine-Ness" and Anti-Acquaintance Critiques

The manuscript's most damaging philosophical omission is **Adam Bradley (2024)** (*Australasian Journal of Philosophy*), which explicitly dismantles the "Pathology Argument" from depersonalization and thought insertion. Bradley shows that proponents of pathology-based ownership arguments conflate two distinct claims: *Self-Acquaintance* (that a subject exists and is directly acquainted with itself via a phenomenal feature F) and *Experiential Basis (Self-Ascription)* (that ordinary experiences possess some feature F—such as interoceptive, affective, or metacognitive cues—that prompts normal self-ascription). Drawing an analogy to cerebral achromatopsia—which proves normal vision has an experiential basis for color judgment without establishing direct acquaintance with primitive objective color properties—Bradley demonstrates that DPD and thought insertion support *Experiential Basis* without providing abductive justification for *Self-Acquaintance* or a substantial subject.   

Equally glaring is the omission of **Tom McClelland (2023)**, *"Four Impediments to the Case for Mineness"*, which identifies four formidable barriers—epistemological identification, representational content, functional role, and the possibility of malfunction—that any realist defense of mineness must overcome. Crucially, McClelland's chapter appears in the *exact same Oxford University Press volume* (*Self-Experience: Essays on Inner Awareness*, edited by García-Carpintero & Guillot, 2023) from which the manuscript cites Billon's Chapter 14; the manuscript completely ignores the volume's entire skeptical Part I (including Salje & Geddes, McClelland, Howell, Soldati, Wu, and Orbán & Wong). The manuscript likewise omits **Shao-Pu Kang (2025)**, who refutes Guillot's epistemic argument for pre-reflective mineness ("typicalism") by showing mineness fails to explain immediate first-person justification, as well as direct internal critiques of Zahavi by **Lilian Alweiss (2022)**—who argues that Zahavi misreads Husserl and that transparent pre-reflective awareness requires no abiding sense of ownership—and **Daniel Wehinger (2024)**, who critiques the transcendental argument for universal mineness.   

### Predictive-Processing and Interoceptive Self-Model Accounts

Beyond bracketing Metzinger (2003) and selectively quoting Deane et al. (2020), the manuscript leaves the 2020s predictive-processing consensus untested. **Gerrans (2020)** models pain asymbolia as localized depersonalization for pain—a loss of "mineness" accounted for entirely by interoceptive active inference and affective predictive modeling without positing a non-physical subject. **Ciaunica et al. (2022)** and **Gatus et al. (2022)** unify depersonalization, derealization, altered agency, early somatosensory prediction-error potentials (P45), and blunted Heartbeat Evoked Potentials within hierarchical Bayesian precision weighting, with Gatus et al. detailing how transient DP/DR can be experimentally induced in healthy individuals via virtual-reality sensory-expectancy mismatches. The manuscript provides no principled reason to prefer a non-reductive dualist void over mechanistic models capable of predicting, measuring, and experimentally reproducing the phenomenology.   

### Strong Illusionism and Attention Schema Theory

Recorded counterargument coverage in the manuscript reduces eliminative physicalism to a brief *"bedrock disagreement"* parried by pointing back to DPD phenomenology. That reply fails completely against 2020s illusionism. **François Kammerer (2021, 2022, 2025)** directly targets appeals to phenomenological obviousness: Kammerer (2021) offers a theory of phenomenal introspection explaining why conscious experience seems robustly real and why illusionism feels counterintuitive (the "illusion meta-problem"), while Kammerer (2022) argues that Moorean appeals to the obviousness of felt experience are dialectically ineffective because they cannot justify their crucial premise without begging the question against illusionism.   

Even more directly fatal to the manuscript's "Ownership Void" is **Michael Graziano's Attention Schema Theory (AST)** (Graziano, 2020, 2022; Graziano, Guterstam, Bio, & Wilterson, 2020). Graziano proposes that the brain constructs a simplified, detail-poor internal control model of selective attention (an *attention schema*) that represents attention inaccurately as *"a capacity to take vivid mental possession of items"*—a *"ghostly, nonphysical, mental essence"* stripped of its underlying neural mechanics. AST therefore **predicts** the exact introspective signature the manuscript interprets as a metaphysical "Ownership Void": introspection encounters an apparent non-physical possession of experience with zero access to its physical construction *precisely because* an efficient control schema omits mechanistic implementation detail. Without engaging and refuting AST and Kammerer-style illusionism, the manuscript's central inference from introspective opacity to non-reductive ownership collapses.   

## Disqualifying Weaknesses and Submission Verdict

1. **False "Reverse Dissociation" Claim in Depersonalization (L48):** The manuscript claims depersonalization strips away ownership while sparing ordinary agency, contradicting 2020s active-inference accounts of impaired allostatic control and aberrant somatosensory attenuation as well as experimental evidence linking explicit agency time-sensitivity in high-DP individuals to derealization rather than bodily mineness (omits **Ciaunica, A., Seth, A., Limanowski, J., Hesp, C., & Friston, K. J., 2022**, *"I overthink—Therefore I am not: An active inference account of altered sense of self and agency in depersonalisation disorder,"* *Consciousness and Cognition*, 101, 103320, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2022.103320; omits **Ciaunica, A., Ayache, J., Haggard, P., Nakul, E., Bonnet, E., & Auvray, M., 2024**, *"Explicit and implicit sense of agency in depersonalisation experiences,"* *Scientific Reports*, 14(1), 15396, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-65862-z; and mishandles **Deane, G., Miller, M., & Wilkinson, S., 2020**, *"Losing ourselves: Active inference, depersonalization, and meditation,"* *Frontiers in Psychology*, 11, 539726, https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.539726).
2. **Refuted Global-Subtraction Model of Depersonalization:** Framing DPD as the subtraction of a single global phenomenal "mine-ness" tag—and deflecting Knappik's localism via an ad hoc multiplication of "voids"—conflicts with experimental evidence showing depersonalization selectively alters self-prioritization of bodily stimuli (avatar faces) while leaving abstract self-prioritization (own name and geometric shapes) unchanged (omits **Woźniak, M., McEllin, L., Hohwy, J., & Ciaunica, A., 2023**, *"Depersonalization affects self-prioritization of bodily, but not abstract self-related information,"* *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance*, 49(11), 1447–1459, https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0001153; and mishandles **Knappik, F., 2025 [online 2022]**, *"No need for mineness: Depersonalization/Derealization Disorder and mental state types,"* *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences*, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11097-022-09872-0).
