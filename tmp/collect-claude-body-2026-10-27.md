# Hostile-Referee Audit: “Depersonalisation and the Separability of For-Me-Ness” (unfinishablemap.org/concepts/depersonalisation/)

Verdict: **REVISE-HARD**. The article’s top-line significance verdict, that the datum is null for dualism, is correct and well disciplined. But the “architecture-tier” claim it does defend rests on empirical premises the clinical literature contradicts: content held fixed, cognition intact, substrate undamaged. Its “Map’s reading”, a separable for-me-ness feature shared with Billon, is inconsistent with the Zahavian constitutive minimal self the Map commits to in its self article. And at least five sibling pages still cite depersonalisation as evidence or in a form the article has already withdrawn.

## 1. TL;DR and Headline Verdict

- **Verdict: REVISE-HARD (with sub-verdicts).** The significance-tier result (null) should be retained. The “Map’s reading” (separable for-me-ness) should be flagged as perpetually contested. The discrimination-problem reconstruction (“same first-order content… content stays fixed”) should be deleted because its premise is empirically false for much of the syndrome.
- **Citations:** I found no confirmed fabricated quotation. There is one stance misapplication: Billon 2024 is recruited as neurophysiological-reductionist when the paper concerns the metaphysics of external reality. [wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/meta.12666) There is one overreach: Gerrans’s “cognition and perception are otherwise intact” is presented as “a formulation every reading accepts”. Four quotations could not be located in the primary text and must be marked unverified, including the Hunter et al. 2004 “in the region of 1-2%” line attributed to the abstract.
- **Integration:** The article is better disciplined than its siblings, which is exactly the problem. Its null verdict has not propagated. The pages self-and-self-consciousness, dualist-perception, phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity, phenomenology-of-recursive-self-awareness and voids/self-opacity still carry either evidential claims or the “agency intact” inversion. The changelog itself records that inversion as an error. This is confession-without-correction at site scale.

### 2.1 The clinical datum: accuracy against the literature

**What the article gets right:**

- The DSM-5 code 300.6 (F48.1) and ICD-11 6B66 are correct. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/depersonalisation/)
- Criterion B (intact reality testing) is stated correctly. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/depersonalisation/)
- The 26–74% lifetime transient prevalence is confirmed in the Hunter, Sierra & David 2004 abstract (PubMed 15022041, [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15022041/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15022041/)). [psychiatrypodcast +2](https://www.psychiatrypodcast.com/psychiatry-psychotherapy-podcast/episode-188-depersonalization-and-derealization)
- The four-factor CDS structure is confirmed: Anomalous Body Experience, Emotional Numbing, Anomalous Subjective Recall, Alienation from Surroundings (Sierra et al. 2005, n=138 patients, 73.3% of variance; [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16164776/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16164776/)). [nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16164776/)
- The fronto-limbic suppression model is confirmed (Sierra & David 2011, [https://philpapers.org/rec/SIEDAS](https://philpapers.org/rec/SIEDAS)). [philpapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/SIEDAS)

**Where it fails:**

1. **“Perception and cognition are otherwise intact … the patient sees, remembers, reasons and acts normally.”** This is overstated. Knappik’s symptom catalogue, built from the CDS and Sierra 2009, lists several symptom classes as part of the syndrome ([https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11097-022-09872-0](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11097-022-09872-0)):

- perceptual distortions (teleopsia, microscopia, loss of stereoscopic depth, altered auditory distance);
- observer-perspective episodic recall; [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/BILWII)
- lifeless or absent imagery; [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/BILWII)
- time disintegration;
- déjà/jamais vu;
- cognitive deficits: Guralnik, Schmeidler & Simeon 2000 (Am J Psychiatry 157(1):103–109; 15 patients against 15 matched controls) found that patients “showed a distinct cognitive profile”, including deficits in visual perception and visual-spatial reasoning, and short-term memory and attention deficits are also reported (Guralnik et al. 2007).

The article cites Knappik for his localist thesis but suppresses his symptom list, which falsifies its own lead characterisation. That is cherry-picking. Gerrans’s sentence is an assumption of his own model, stated in his abstract ([https://philpapers.org/rec/GERDDA-4](https://philpapers.org/rec/GERDDA-4)). [philpapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/GERDDA-4) Calling it “a formulation every reading accepts” is false: the localist reading rejects it.
2. **“Without damage to the substrate that delivers the content.”** This is equivocal and tenet-laden. Sierra & David report “increased prefrontal activation as well as reduced activation in insula/limbic-related areas” to aversive stimuli. [philpapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/SIEDAS) The substrate is functionally altered; that is the mechanism. “Substrate that delivers the content” is interface vocabulary placed inside a sentence advertised as the theory-neutral architecture tier. This is tenet leakage.
3. **Outdated nosology.**

- The article cites DSM-5 (2013), not DSM-5-TR (2022).
- It admits both manuals are “as transcribed by secondary sources”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/depersonalisation/) That is confession-without-correction for a page billed as the Map’s home for its “most-cited clinical datum”.
- It omits Criteria C–E: distress or impairment, the substance/medical exclusion, and not better explained by another disorder. The substance exclusion is where cannabis, ketamine and hallucinogen-induced depersonalisation sit, and none of these appear.
- It omits anxiety and panic as major proximal triggers; Yang et al. 2023 report the disorder in 3.3–20.2% of anxiety-disorder patients, a comorbidity rate rather than a trigger rate. It also omits the cognitive-behavioural maintenance model of Hunter, Phillips, Chalder, Sierra & David 2003 (Behaviour Research and Therapy 41(12):1451–1467), on which transient symptoms are “catastrophically misinterpreted as signs of deteriorating or damaged mental health”, producing anxiety that worsens them. Both are central to how clinicians understand the disorder.
- It is silent on the dissociative-versus-anxiety-spectrum classification debate, although Medford et al. (Advances in Psychiatric Treatment) report that the Hunter 2003 model holds that “depersonalisation is best conceptualised as related to anxiety disorders rather than to dissociative conditions.” ICD-10 placed depersonalisation under “other neurotic disorders” (F48.1); DSM and ICD-11 place it among the dissociative disorders. That debate bears directly on whether the subtraction is “affective”.
4. **Prevalence not updated.** The article uses Hunter 2004’s “1–2%”. The same group’s update, Yang, Millman, David & Hunter 2023 (J Trauma Dissociation 24(1):8–41; [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35699456/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35699456/)), reviewed 23 papers. It reports rates of “0% to 1.9% amongst the general population, 5-20% in outpatients and 17.5-41.9% in inpatients”, about 50% in depression and 25–53.8% in people who experienced interpersonal abuse, and concludes the general-population prevalence “is around 1%”. The 26–74% transient figure also needs a heterogeneity caveat: definitions and timeframes vary widely across the surveys. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depersonalization)
5. **Insight versus reality testing.** The article treats “insight is retained” and “reality testing remains intact” as one fact. They are distinct constructs. The “as if” quality the article foregrounds is itself a phenomenological convention, not diagnostic text, as the article half-concedes.

**Net:** The datum is characterised competently on prevalence, factor structure and neural correlates. It is characterised inaccurately on exactly the point the Map’s reading needs: the claim that the first-order content stays fixed while only for-me-ness varies.

### 2.3 The four readings: exhaustiveness, fairness, and the preferred reading

**What the article states.** It announces “four families” with four headed sections:

1. “The Affective Reading” (Sierra/Berrios, Sierra/David, Medford, with Gerrans as the “predictive-coding descendant”);
2. “Billon: The Lack of Subjective Character”;
3. “The Active-Inference Self-Model” (Seth et al., Ciaunica et al. 2022, Deane et al., with Ciaunica et al. 2021 transparency appended);
4. “Universalism and Localism” (Zahavi & Kriegel, Henriksen & Parnas versus Knappik). [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/depersonalisation/)

The opening paragraph lists five candidates instead: for-me-ness, emotional colouring, “a form of awareness”, transparency, and local self-relations. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/depersonalisation/)

**Taxonomy defects:**

- The count is unstable. Section 4 lumps two readings that the article itself says attack “from opposite sides”, so there are really five.
- Transparency (Ciaunica 2021) is a phenomenological reading, yet it is filed under active inference.
- “A form of awareness”, Billon’s term for the sense of ownership as distinct from mineness, is listed as a candidate and then never developed. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/BILWII)
- A reader cannot tell which thesis the “Map’s reading” is being compared against.

**Omitted readings:**

- **Metzinger’s self-model theory (SMT).** It appears only as a Further Reading link (“The transparent self-model that cracks”), [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/depersonalisation/) although SMT is the canonical representationalist account of depersonalisation as transparency loss.
- **Representationalist and deflationary readings**, on which mineness is representational content, not an extra phenomenal datum. Guillot 2017 (“I Me Mine”, [https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5362670/](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5362670/)) separates for-me-ness, me-ness and mineness and shows “no conceptual implication from for-me-ness to me-ness or mineness”. [nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5362670/) This bears directly on the article, because Guillot co-edits the very volume containing Billon 2023. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/BILWII) Her distinction dissolves the article’s straddle (below) and is absent.
- **Higher-order and global-workspace readings:** a metacognitive tagging failure.
- **Illusionist and no-self readings:** Letheby’s self-fictionalism is engaged on the self page [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/) but not here.
- **The Sass/Parnas ipseity-disturbance reading**, invoked on the self page. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/) Sass & Parnas 2003 (Schizophrenia Bulletin 29(3):427–444) define it as “fundamentally a self-disorder or ipseity disturbance”, marked by hyperreflexivity and diminished self-affection.
- **The anxiety-hypervigilance cognitive-behavioural model** (Hunter et al. 2003).
- **Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria 2025**, “A beautiful loop” (Neurosci Biobehav Rev 176:106296; [https://www.ovid.com/journals/nebior/abstract/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106296~a-beautiful-loop-an-active-inference-theory-of-consciousness](https://www.ovid.com/journals/nebior/abstract/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106296~a-beautiful-loop-an-active-inference-theory-of-consciousness)), is absent. Its precision “hyper-model”, which it applies explicitly to “meditation, psychedelics, and minimal states”, is the most current active-inference account of the very selfless-state contrast the article borrows from Deane et al. [ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/nebior/abstract/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106296~a-beautiful-loop-an-active-inference-theory-of-consciousness)

**The standing predictive-processing (PP) check passes only partially.** Seth, Ciaunica 2022 and Deane are engaged as rivals and not residualised; I record that as a fact, not as praise. But Laukkonen et al. and Seth’s later “controlled hallucination”/”beast machine” self-model are missing.

**Is the preferred reading coherent with the Map?** No. The article says for-me-ness is “a single phenomenal feature” that depersonalisation subtracts, “the Map’s reading, shared with Billon”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/depersonalisation/) The Map’s own self article ([https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/)) adopts the opposite thesis:

- The minimal self “is the very form conscious experience takes, not an added feature.”
- “The Map adopts Dan Zahavi’s phenomenological minimal self.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/)

Zahavi & Kriegel deny exactly what Billon asserts, namely a detachable self quale. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/ZAHFWI) The article tries to reconcile the two: the kind-claim “sides with the universalist about the reporting position and with Billon … about the felt feature”. ## 5. Site-Wide Methodology Improvements

1. **Propagation gate.** When a concept page changes a datum’s status (here, depersonalisation → null significance; the agency inversion withdrawn), the pipeline must grep every slug citing that datum and block publication until each is reconciled. The changelog shows the agency inversion was fixed in two pages [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/) and survived in at least three. That is a mechanical propagation failure, not a judgment failure.
2. **Changelog completeness.** The target article’s 2026-10-20 modification has no entry in a changelog last modified 2026-10-15. Every published modification needs a log line, or the audit trail the site relies on is broken.
3. **Primary-source floor for clinical criteria.** “As transcribed by secondary sources” should be a blocking flag, not a disclosed caveat, on any page billed as a datum’s home. The DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 CDDR texts are the minimum.
4. **Quotation ledger with tri-state tags.** Every quotation in references should carry one tag: [raw-verified], [abstract-verified], [secondhand], or [unlocated]. The site’s research workflow already grep-verifies summariser quotations (changelog 2026-10-15). [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/) That practice is not enforced on concept pages, where four unlocated quotations shipped.
5. **Empirical-premise audit before philosophical reconstruction.** The AI-assisted drafting step reconstructs arguments (here, the discrimination-problem exhibit) on premises like “content stays fixed” without checking them against symptom catalogues the page itself cites. A reviewer pass should list every empirical premise and its source sentence.
6. **Stance-scope check.** For each recruited author, the reviewer should state the paper’s actual target thesis in one line. That would have caught Billon 2024, whose target is external-world metaphysics, not mineness. [wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/meta.12666)
7. **Cross-page consistency register.** The Zahavi-versus-Billon conflict between self-and-self-consciousness and depersonalisation is the kind of contradiction a positions register (as used in the quantum-interface domain) catches. Extend registered, confidence-banded positions to the self/mineness domain, with dependencies, so that a concept page cannot adopt a reading the register rejects.
8. **Standing rival list.** Make the PP/active-inference rival set a required checklist item for any self or phenomenology page: Seth et al. 2012; Gerrans 2019; Deane et al. 2020; Ciaunica et al. 2021/2022; Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria 2025; Metzinger; Letheby. Also include the representationalist alternatives (Guillot 2017).
9. **Benchmark parity.** Apply the apex standard (explicit failure modes, falsifiers, preprint and peer-review flags, “research agenda, not result”) to concept pages that apex syntheses consume. Otherwise calibration asymmetry lets weaker concept-tier claims launder into apex coherence.
