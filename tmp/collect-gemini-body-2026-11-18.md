The manuscript under review attempts to delimit the lower boundaries of phenomenal consciousness by positing an apophatic architecture of the mind, arguing that minimal phenomenal experience (MPE) constitutes an inherently unchartable epistemological "void." The author defines these domains as "the dark spaces on our map where the terrain is not merely uncharted but may be unchartable". If mind is more than one more physical phenomenon, the space of minds may be as vast as physical reality itself"—the manuscript seeks to insulate the baseline of subjective experience from standard cognitive-neuroscientific operationalization.   

While mapping the limits of introspective and cognitive access is a recognized pursuit in the analytic philosophy of mind, this manuscript is catastrophically unready for publication in a top-tier peer-reviewed journal. Instead of engaging rigorously with the contemporary science of consciousness, the author selectively misreads the empirical literature, commits severe category errors regarding meditative states, relies on unfalsifiable circular axioms to dismiss deflationary accounts, and deliberately ignores the leading neuro-computational paradigms of the 2020s that successfully formalize the exact phenomena the text declares ineffable. The following sections provide an exhaustive pre-publication audit across four strictly defined dimensions, culminating in a formal enumeration of the manuscript's fatal weaknesses.

## Dimension A: Empirical Accuracy Against the 2020s Literature

The primary structural conceit of the manuscript is its classification of minimal phenomenal experience as a region of cognitive closure, grouping it among "The Unexplorable — darkness that cannot be illuminated by minds like ours". To project empirical legitimacy onto this highly speculative apophatic project, the text attempts to co-opt the recent burgeoning literature on Minimal Phenomenal Experience (MPE). The manuscript quotes Thomas Metzinger verbatim, claiming to integrate "sixteen phenomenological case-studies" and "six semantic constraints" derived from the existing literature, alongside specific Metzingerian nomenclature such as "pure awareness," "wakefulness," and the "zero-person perspective". It further categorizes minimal awareness as an "asymptote toward zero".   

This framing constitutes a profound misrepresentation of the empirical reality established by the Minimal Phenomenal Experience Project throughout the 2020s. The manuscript leverages MPE as a theoretical "floor" of consciousness that is permanently shielded from introspective access and scientific operationalization. However, the contemporary literature definitively proves that MPE is not a "void"; rather, it is a highly structured, distinctly reportable, and systematically chartable phenomenal state.

In Gamma and Metzinger’s (2021) landmark psychometric study, the researchers deployed the Minimal Phenomenal Experience questionnaire (MPE-92M) to a massive cohort of advanced meditators. If minimal consciousness were truly an unchartable void defined by an epistemic darkness, subjective reports would logically yield random noise, null findings, or uniform negative responses. Instead, the factor analysis of the 1,403 usable MPE-92M responses yielded a robust, finely grained 12-factor model that explains 44% of the total variance of the experience. These factors successfully parse the precise internal architecture of the "pure awareness" state.   

| Factor Number | MPE-92M Factor Designation | Phenomenological Implication for Minimal Consciousness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Factor 2 | Peace, Bliss and Silence | Demonstrates that the minimal baseline retains distinct affective valences, contradicting the premise of an empty, inaccessible void. |
| Factor 3 | Self-Knowledge, Autonomous Cognizance | Indicates that autonomous cognizance persists even when typical egoic structures are dissolved, allowing for introspective reporting. |
| Factor 4 | Wakeful Presence | Validates that tonic alertness remains a core, reportable feature of the baseline state. |
| Factor 6 | Luminosity | Operationalizes the epistemic openness and "stand-alone quality" of awareness as a measurable phenomenological variable. |
| Factor 8 | Emptiness and Non-egoic Self-awareness | Specifically isolates the uncontracted, objectless state, proving that "contentless" awareness is highly structured and empirically measurable. |
| Factor 12 | Witness Consciousness | Captures the global phenomenology of a non-conceptual "knowing" function that survives the cessation of specific sensory contents. |

By arbitrarily defining the minimal state as "unchartable," the manuscript ignores that Metzinger himself published *The Elephant and the Blind: The Experience of Pure Consciousness* (2024), an exhaustive 648-page synthesis of over 500 experiential reports spanning 57 countries. This major work dedicates entire chapters to meticulously cataloging the phenomenological nuances of the state, including "Silence," "Clarity," "Density," "Suchness," and "Transparency, Translucency, and Virtuality". The manuscript’s failure to engage with this highly detailed 12-factor psychometric structure constitutes a gross dereliction of scholarly duty. The terrain of minimal consciousness is heavily populated by specific features that have been successfully mapped by the very researchers the manuscript selectively quotes.   

Furthermore, the manuscript completely ignores the integration of these phenomenological findings with active inference and the free-energy principle. Computational phenomenologists have recently mapped these exact MPE states mathematically. Sandved-Smith et al. (2024) have modeled the "zero-person perspective" and the sense of "timelessness" as a specific, mathematically determinate region of state space incurring minimal free energy. The computational counterpart to pure awareness involves a flattening of the temporal planning horizon (zero temporal depth) and the modeling of meta-awareness as the likelihood precision of second-order observations of model parameters. Thus, the "zero-person perspective" that the manuscript treats as a mystical void is actually a predictable artifact of a system operating at zero temporal depth within a predictive processing architecture.   

This empirical distortion is compounded by the manuscript's handling of sleep states. The author attempts to locate another manifestation of the void in dreamless sleep, suggesting a seamless continuity between minimal meditative states and the supposed darkness of non-REM sleep. Yet, the 2020s literature demonstrates that dreamless sleep is not an experiential void either. Alcaraz-Sánchez (2021, 2024) utilized rigorous micro-phenomenological interviews to reveal that conscious dreamless sleep is characterized by objectless awareness that contains specific, reportable dimensions such as "Richness of the Content, Bodily-Awareness, Passage of Time, Attentional Focus, and Self-Revelation". Similarly, Windt's (2024) work on immersive spatiotemporal hallucinations (ISTH) carefully distinguishes between the minimal phenomenological selfhood present in sleep and total unconsciousness. By forcing the highly structured empirical data of objectless awareness into a predetermined "void" framework, the manuscript actively suppresses the rich phenomenological variation documented in the current sleep and meditation literature.   

## Dimension B: Results Relied Upon That Have Been Misapplied or Commit Category Errors

A fatal failure of logical inference in the manuscript involves its treatment of meditative cessation as an evidentiary bracket for the minimal consciousness void. In an attempt to triangulate the absolute floor of experience, the manuscript relies on recent neurophenomenological studies of advanced Buddhist meditation, specifically citing the work of Ruben Laukkonen. The manuscript quotes Laukkonen (2023) verbatim regarding the "cessation of feeling and perception" and characterizes this state as a "gap" or "cut" in the stream of consciousness. The text attempts to utilize this phenomenon as a "phenomenological bracket" to validate its claims regarding the unchartable depths of minimal consciousness.   

This represents a profound and highly destructive category error that fundamentally misreads Laukkonen et al. (2023). In their paper *Cessations of consciousness in meditation: Advancing a scientific understanding of nirodha samāpatti*, Laukkonen and colleagues explicitly and repeatedly distinguish between minimal phenomenal experiences (such as pure awareness) and actual *cessation* events (*nirodha*). A cessation event is not a highly attenuated form of minimal consciousness; it is the *complete, absolute, and total absence of consciousness*.   

The manuscript attempts to weave Laukkonen's "gap" into a continuum of phenomenal depth, utilizing it to describe the baseline architecture of the conscious mind. However, Laukkonen et al. clearly state that cessation marks a "non-phenomenological interval beyond ordinary experience, where reportable contents disappear". During cessation, all perceptions, feelings, space, time, and awareness completely cease. It is entirely distinct from the "luminosity" or "wakefulness" characteristic of Metzinger's MPE. Practitioners undergoing *nirodha samāpatti* are completely impervious to external stimulation and cannot be awakened, presenting a stiffened body posture and a total absence of time experience. By conflating *nothingness* (the absence of consciousness in cessation) with *no-thingness* (the objectless presence of pure awareness in MPE), the manuscript attempts to build a theory of minimal consciousness upon a phenomenon that is strictly defined by the neuroscience literature as a period of profound unconsciousness.   

To use a gap in consciousness as evidence for the intrinsic "unchartability" of minimal consciousness is an egregious logical leap. It is akin to using the blank screen of a powered-off television as evidence that the broadcast signal itself is fundamentally mysterious and unchartable. The "gap" is a temporal discontinuity of the biological system, not a structural property of subjective experience. Laukkonen et al. emphasize that what makes cessation scientifically fascinating is not that it is a "void" of experience, but rather the brain's ability to maintain a stable, latent organizing structure *despite* the total absence of phenomenal experience, leading to a sudden sense of clarity and a phenomenological "reboot" upon emergence (frequently measured by a median 3.6-second delay between the cessation-related eye blink artifact and a subsequent button press). The manuscript’s utilization of Laukkonen's "cessation of feeling and perception" to define the bounds of a phenomenal void demonstrates a severe, disqualifying misunderstanding of the target literature.   

This misunderstanding of phenomenological thresholds is further exposed by the manuscript's failure to engage with psychophysical threshold measurements. The manuscript treats the boundary of consciousness as a sudden drop-off into an unobservable abyss. However, Overgaard and Sandberg (2021) have extensively defended the Perceptual Awareness Scale (PAS), demonstrating that subjective awareness scales predictably and measurably. PAS calibrates introspective reports on a four-point continuum: No experience, Brief glimpse, Almost clear experience, and Clear experience. Overgaard (2021) demonstrates that the transition from unconscious processing to conscious awareness is gradual, highly structured, and tightly correlated with specific neural activation ranges (e.g., occipital activation at 250ms followed by medial orbitofrontal activation at 540ms). The so-called "voids" of introspection that the manuscript fixates upon are readily explained as weak perceptual signals failing to fully cross the threshold of access consciousness, rather than existing as fundamental, metaphysical limits on the knowability of the mind.   

## Dimension C: Framings That Hold Only if the Site's Own Commitments Are Assumed

A manuscript submitted to a top-tier philosophy journal must be capable of defending its central thesis without relying entirely on circular, axiomatic commitments. The text under review fails this standard spectacularly by explicitly relying on "Tenet-Generated Voids — explicitly framework-internal by construction". The author argues that minimal consciousness cannot be accessed or understood because of an inherent "enrichment problem"—the assertion that the very act of introspecting upon a minimal state structurally enriches and destroys the minimality of that state, thereby rendering the baseline permanently occluded.   

This "enrichment problem" is deployed throughout the text as an unfalsifiable shield against all deflationary, materialist, and reductive critiques. The manuscript brazenly states that "physicalist illusionism denies the surplus rather than acknowledging it as a void". By framing illusionism as merely "denying the surplus," the author begs the question against the entire illusionist paradigm. Illusionism, as rigorously articulated by Kammerer (2021, 2022) and Frankish (2021), does not simply stubbornly "deny" a self-evident surplus of experience. Rather, it provides a sophisticated, evolutionary, and meta-theoretical account of *why* human cognitive systems are predisposed to falsely model their own internal states as possessing irreducible phenomenal properties.   

For Kammerer (2021), the "illusion meta-problem" requires researchers to explain the specific computational and psychological mechanisms that generate our unshakeable intuition of phenomenal certainty. If strong illusionism is correct, the "enrichment problem" is not a profound metaphysical boundary (a "void" shielding a dualistic surplus), but rather a predictable artifact of the cognitive system's inability to introspectively access its own sub-personal computational mechanics (the "distortion thesis"). The manuscript dismisses this purely as a "bedrock disagreement", effectively stating that it will not engage with materialism because it has already axiomatically assumed that consciousness is fundamental. A philosophical argument cannot merely stipulate its most contested premise. Stating that "If mind is more than one more physical phenomenon, the space of minds may be as vast as physical reality itself" is a conditional that demands empirical and logical defense, not a premise that can be used to unilaterally declare cognitive constraints as sacrosanct.   

The text goes further into speculative theology, asserting the existence of an "Ineffable Encounter Void — Why consciousness encounters meaning and sacred significance it cannot articulate or evaluate from within" and a "Normative Void — Moral truths beyond human cognitive reach". This framing is entirely unacceptable in contemporary analytic philosophy of mind. It pathologizes basic cognitive limits by elevating them to the status of apophatic mysticism. To claim that cognitive occlusion is purposefully designed "whether by simulation architects or reality itself" shifts the discourse violently from the philosophy of mind into untestable, conspiratorial metaphysics. If the "enrichment problem" makes minimal consciousness impossible to verify by definition, and if all competing physicalist explanations are dismissed *a priori* as denying the reality of this unverifiable void, the manuscript is logically vacuous. It operates as a closed loop of self-affirming axioms immune to empirical or philosophical scrutiny.   

## Dimension D: Untested Counterarguments and Competing Frameworks

The most glaring omission in the manuscript is its total failure to engage with the leading contemporary frameworks that provide structural, physicalist, and cognitive-access explanations for the very phenomena the author declares "unchartable." The manuscript attributes the introspective opacity of minimal consciousness to an ontological void. Yet, the literature of the 2020s offers vastly superior, parsimonious explanations that the author completely ignores.

### The Access-Phenomenal Bottleneck vs. The "Conceptual Metabolism Void"

The manuscript frequently invokes a "Conceptual Metabolism Void — How understanding occludes the naive standpoint" and "The Language-Thought Boundary — What cannot be said" to argue that subjective experience possesses a surplus that inherently eludes articulation. In the context of minimal awareness, the author suggests a structural, almost mystical barrier to knowing the experience. However, the author entirely ignores the extensive literature on cognitive access limits, most notably Ned Block's (2023) continued, rigorous defense of the distinction between phenomenal consciousness and access consciousness.   

Block’s recent monograph, *The Border Between Seeing and Thinking* (2023), and the associated literature on "phenomenal overflow," argue that our subjective experience (phenomenal consciousness) is highly rich, but our working memory and reporting mechanisms (access consciousness) are strictly capacity-limited. Block responds to critiques of the "Fragment View" by defending a "Fragmentary Rich View," acknowledging that while subjects may only have conscious access to fragments of an array, those fragments still support a rich phenomenology that outstrips cognitive access. When a subject reports an inability to articulate a state, or when introspection seems to fail during minimal awareness, this is not evidence of a mystical "Conceptual Metabolism Void." It is the result of a well-documented bandwidth bottleneck between posterior cortical representational areas and prefrontal global access workspaces. The manuscript’s failure to engage with the access vs. phenomenal distinction means it systematically conflates *the system's inability to cognitively access and report a state* with *the state itself lacking structure or existing as an ontological void*.   

### The Ignorance of Integrated Information Theory (IIT) 4.0

The manuscript dismissively notes that it scopes Integrated Information Theory (IIT) merely as "its own contested claim". In a paper explicitly attempting to define the lower bounds (the physical and phenomenal minimum) of consciousness, ignoring the formal mathematical framework of IIT 4.0 is professional negligence.   

Published in late 2023 by Albantakis, Tononi, and colleagues, IIT 4.0 formulates the properties of phenomenal existence in rigorous physical and operational terms, updating the theory significantly from its 3.0 iteration. IIT 4.0 proceeds from five phenomenal axioms (Existence, Intrinsicality, Information, Integration, Exclusion) and translates them directly into operational physical postulates.   

| IIT 4.0 Axiom | Physical Postulate / Operationalization | Refutation of the "Void" Thesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Existence** | A substrate must have cause–effect power (taking and making a difference). | Consciousness cannot be a "void" or an absence; it requires definitive causal friction. |
| **Intrinsicality** | Cause-effect power must be intrinsic. It increases with expansion without noise, and decreases with dilution. | The "Unobservable Self" void is refuted; the system exists for itself mathematically, not via mysterious occlusion. |
| **Information** | The system selects a specific cause-effect state, maximizing intrinsic information (informativeness over chance). | The "Affective Void" or "Valence Void" are specific geometric shapes of the cause-effect structure, not unchartable dark spaces. |
| **Integration** | The system cannot be partitioned into independent parts without loss of intrinsic information (measured by system integrated information, Φ). | The "enrichment problem" is merely a shift in the system's integration status, trackable mathematically. |
| **Exclusion & Composition** | The maximal substrate (complex) fully unfolds its cause-effect power over all subsets (mechanisms). | Every phenomenal property, no matter how minimal, is completely accounted for by the physical properties of the unfolded Φ-structure. |

Under IIT 4.0, an experience is strictly identical to the cause–effect structure unfolded from a maximal substrate in its current state. For IIT 4.0, there is no "void." Every phenomenal experience, no matter how minimal or devoid of standard sensory content, corresponds exactly and completely to a specific, mathematically determinate cause-effect structure. The degree of irreducibility (Φ\PhiΦ) specifies the quantity of consciousness, and the highly specific shape of the cause-effect structure specifies the exact quality of the experience. By simply waving away IIT as a "contested claim," the manuscript dodges the most comprehensive, mathematically formalized theory of minimal consciousness currently available. If IIT 4.0 is even partially correct, the apophatic cartography proposed by the manuscript is fundamentally obsolete. The terrain is not unchartable; we simply require sufficient computational capacity to unfold the cause-effect structure of the substrate. The manuscript's insistence on an "Ineffable Encounter Void" collapses entirely when confronted with a theory that posits a direct, testable explanatory identity between phenomenology and operationalized physics.   

## Conclusion

The manuscript attempts to legitimize "Apophatic Cartography — Formalising the method with evidential standards". Apophaticism—knowing through negation—is a theological methodology (the *via negativa*). While it holds historical relevance in the philosophy of religion, attempting to inject it into the contemporary science of consciousness represents a massive methodological regression. The manuscript attempts to formalize ignorance, branding the absence of current empirical data or the presence of standard cognitive bottlenecks as profound, irreducible ontological features of the universe.   

By categorizing empirical limits as "voids" and attributing "sacred significance" to them, the author abandons the foundational premise of naturalistic philosophy. The text attempts to construct a dualist bastion immune to the advancements of psychometrics (Gamma & Metzinger, 2021), neuro-computational modeling (Sandved-Smith, 2024), and formalized physicalist identities (Albantakis et al., 2023). It misreads the neuroscience of cessation (Laukkonen et al., 2023) to falsely prop up its premises, and it uses circular, question-begging devices like the "enrichment problem" to dismiss materialist counter-theories (Kammerer, 2021) without engaging their actual arguments. Consequently, the manuscript reads less like a peer-reviewed philosophy article and more like an esoteric, internally referential manifesto.

1. **Category Error Regarding Meditative Cessation:** The manuscript fundamentally misreads the neurophenomenology of meditation by quoting Laukkonen’s "cessation of feeling and perception" to establish the bounds of a phenomenal void. The literature explicitly defines *nirodha samāpatti* (cessation) as the total absence of consciousness (a strictly non-phenomenal interval), not a minimal phenomenal state, rendering the manuscript's primary empirical bracket logically invalid.

3. **Failure to Engage Cognitive Access Limits (Phenomenal Overflow):** The manuscript invokes an introspective "Conceptual Metabolism Void" and a "Language-Thought Boundary" to argue for an ontological surplus to consciousness that cannot be articulated. 5. **Question-Begging Dismissal of Illusionism:** The manuscript relies on the unverified, axiomatically constructed "enrichment problem" to dismiss deflationary accounts, explicitly stating that physicalist illusionism merely "denies the surplus rather than acknowledging it as a void." **Verdict on Academic-Submission Readiness:**
Due to a systematic mischaracterization of the primary empirical literature, profound category errors regarding meditative cessation, reliance on unfalsifiable circular axioms, and a deliberate refusal to engage with leading physicalist and computational paradigms, this manuscript is categorically unfit for publication and requires outright rejection.
