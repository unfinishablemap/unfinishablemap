# Referee Report: The Empirical and Theoretical Collapse of Non-Reductive Depersonalisation Accounts

## Introduction and Exegesis of the Manuscript's Philosophical Project

The manuscript under review attempts an ambitious but ultimately untenable project: to position the clinical phenomenon of depersonalisation as a foundational, irrefutable datum for a non-reductive theory of consciousness. By interpreting depersonalisation as a psychopathological state in which the normally transparent self-model becomes epistemically opaque, the text argues that specific phenomenal layers—namely, "mine-ness" (or perspectival ownership) and "agency" (the sense of initiation)—can be systematically decoupled and clinically isolated. The manuscript marshals this alleged decoupling to claim that physicalist, functionalist, and reductive accounts of mind are structurally incapable of explaining the subjective residue of experience. It argues that this clinical subtraction reveals the architectural necessity of a non-physical, irreducibly subjective tier of experience that is ontologically distinct from the cognitive and affective machinery of the brain.

However, a rigorous audit of the manuscript’s empirical claims, counterargument coverage, and logical inferences against the 2020–2025 cognitive neuroscience and philosophy of mind literature reveals systemic, unrecoverable flaws. The manuscript relies on theoretical gerrymandering to bracket competing physicalist frameworks—most notably, interoceptive active inference and predictive processing—while simultaneously misrepresenting the contemporary empirical consensus on the phenomenology of dissociative states. By treating depersonalisation as a clean, modular subtraction of a monolithic "for-me-ness" tier, the text ignores robust experimental evidence demonstrating that depersonalisation involves complex, hierarchical disruptions of implicit agency, bodily self-prioritization, and allostatic precision-weighting.

Furthermore, the manuscript exhibits a troubling tendency to immunize its claims against falsification by defining the boundaries of the "significance question" in a manner that explicitly excludes modern computational and predictive models of mind. It attempts to build a metaphysical argument out of a phenomenological heuristic, conflating structural deficits in predictive coding with an encounter with an epistemic, dualistic void. The ensuing analysis will demonstrate that the manuscript's non-reductive bridging premises collapse under theoretical stress-testing, rendering the argument unfit for publication in a top-tier philosophy journal.

## The Methodological Failure of the "One-Way Separation" Thesis

A central load-bearing pillar of the manuscript's architectural argument is the claim that depersonalisation provides clinical evidence for the separability of ownership and agency. The manuscript's own revision history indicates a strategic retreat on this front. Recognizing that modern active-inference accounts describe a profoundly disturbed sense of control in depersonalisation, the manuscript abandons a previously held "double dissociation" model in favor of a narrower, highly specific "one-way separation". It now argues that, under a "Gallagher-style reading," depersonalisation disrupts "mine-ness" while leaving the specific sense of *initiation* intact. This maneuver attempts to keep the phenomenal layers of the self conceptually distinct, thereby preserving the manuscript's claim that a non-physical tier of subjective ownership can be cleanly stripped away from the functional machinery of action. This theoretical patch is empirically untenable and theoretically obsolete.

### The Collapse of Intact Initiation

The manuscript’s reliance on a Gallagher-style phenomenological reading implies that while the subject feels detached from their body or the experiential world, they still experience a veridical, uncorrupted sense of authorship over their self-initiated actions. The text asserts that actions "feel mine to initiate but the experiential field as a whole feels alien". This assertion directly contradicts highly cited empirical research published within the current decade that measures the sub-personal architecture of agency.

Recent experimental paradigms have moved beyond subjective clinical self-reporting to measure the *implicit* mechanisms of agency, revealing that the initiation of action in depersonalisation is, in fact, profoundly disturbed. Ciaunica, Ayache, Haggard, et al. (2024) conducted rigorous investigations into both the explicit and implicit sense of agency in individuals experiencing depersonalisation traits. Utilizing the intentional binding paradigm—a gold-standard implicit measure of the sense of agency based on the perceived temporal compression between a voluntary action and its sensory outcome—the data revealed significant spatiotemporal disruptions. Specifically, individuals with high occurrences of depersonalisation exhibited a highly time-sensitive explicit sense of agency coupled with severe temporal distortions for short intervals in the absence of self-initiated motion.

This documented discrepancy between implicit sensorimotor binding and explicit judgments of control falsifies the manuscript's premise. Agency, even in its most minimal form of initiation and outcome-binding, is not "intact" or "spared" in depersonalisation. Instead, the predictive mechanisms that tether motor initiation to sensory consequences are fundamentally degraded. When the manuscript claims that the sense of initiation is spared, it is relying on a superficial, explicit narrative gloss that actively ignores the sub-personal collapse of action-binding mechanisms. The "one-way separation" is an artifact of outdated phenomenological interviewing, not a reflection of the cognitive architecture of the self.

### The Disintegration of Phenomenal Modularity

By resting its architectural claims on a "one-way separation," the manuscript forces a false, modular binary onto the phenomenology of the self. The cognitive neuroscience of the 2020s models agency and ownership not as distinct, separable blocks that can be cleanly toggled on and off by psychopathology, but as continuous, co-emergent properties of a hierarchical generative model.

| Phenomenological Component | Manuscript's Conceptualization | 2020-2025 Empirical Consensus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ownership ("Mine-ness")** | A monolithic, non-reductive phenomenal tier that can be cleanly subtracted from the experiential field. | A multidimensional predictive construct; abstract self-representation may remain intact while bodily self-prioritization is degraded. |
| **Agency ("Initiation")** | Spared entirely during depersonalisation; operates independently of the disrupted experiential field to form a "one-way separation". | Implicit sensorimotor binding is severely disrupted; profound spatiotemporal discrepancies exist between implicit and explicit agency. |
| **Epistemic Status** | Depersonalisation serves as a "natural experiment" exposing a transparent, non-physical self-model. | Depersonalisation is an active computational failure of predictive processing and interoceptive precision-weighting. |

The attempt to salvage the argument by narrowing the definition of agency to mere "initiation" fails precisely because the initiation of a motor command in an active inference framework cannot be phenomenologically isolated from the predicted sensory consequences of that command. Active inference posits that action *is* the minimization of sensory prediction errors. If the experiential field feels alien (disrupted ownership), the predicted sensory consequences of initiation are fundamentally compromised. This means the sense of initiation itself is operating under a state of high uncertainty and unattenuated prediction error. Therefore, the architectural claim that the non-reductive tier of "mine-ness" separates cleanly from the physicalist mechanisms of agency represents a gross misunderstanding of how sensorimotor contingencies construct self-awareness.

## The Systematic Evasion of Interoceptive Active Inference

The most egregious theoretical weakness of the manuscript is its systematic bracketing and evasion of the dominant 2020–2025 paradigm for explaining altered states of consciousness: interoceptive active inference and predictive processing. The manuscript attempts to construct a non-reductive bridging premise by demonstrating that physicalist accounts leave an "explanatory gap" regarding the subjective feel of depersonalisation. To do this, it must caricature physicalist models as overly simplistic.

### The Marginalization of the "Affective Reading"

The manuscript categorizes physicalist explanations of depersonalisation under the heading of "The Affective Reading." In doing so, it primarily targets outdated neurobiological models, extensively citing Sierra and Berrios's (1998) corticolimbic inhibition model and Medford's (2012) account of reduced emotional responsivity. By focusing on these older frameworks, the manuscript sets up a straw man, arguing that the "Affective Reading" merely explains the loss of emotional coloring ("hypoemotionality") but fails entirely to account for the irreducible "for-me-ness" of the experience.

When the manuscript finally acknowledges the predictive-processing descendant of this model—specifically citing Gerrans (2019)—it performs a textbook act of theoretical gerrymandering. The manuscript states that Gerrans's account "grants separability—of affect from content—under a different description, and closes the significance question by construction". It further claims that this active inference model still leaves "mine-ness" unaddressed, retreating to the assertion that the physicalist model only explains the "unpredicted absence of feeling". This is a profound misrepresentation of the predictive processing literature. Gerrans, Seth, and other active inference theorists do not "close the significance question by construction"; rather, they provide a mathematically and neurobiologically rigorous reduction of "mine-ness" that the manuscript simply refuses to engage with on its own terms.

### The Reduction of "Mine-ness" to Interoceptive Precision

In the contemporary active inference framework, "mine-ness" (or subjective presence) is not a non-physical tier of experience that hovers above cognitive architecture. It is identical to the successful integration of interoceptive prediction errors. Gerrans (2020) explicitly defines pain asymbolia as a form of "depersonalization for pain," where nociceptive signals are not attributed to the self due to a specific failure in interoceptive active inference.

According to this model, the brain maintains a hierarchical multidimensional self-model. The feeling of "mine-ness" is the result of the mind modeling and predicting fluctuations of affect by attributing them to a continuing self, a process critically mediated by the anterior insula cortex. When an organism experiences depersonalisation, the mechanism at fault is the failure to appropriately deploy precision weighting over interoceptive signals. Because the interoceptive signals are not weighted with sufficient precision, they are not integrated into the higher-level generative self-model. The resulting phenomenology is precisely the "alien" or "unreal" sensation described by patients.

The manuscript willfully ignores this literature because integrating the active inference framework would collapse its non-reductive, dualistic scaffolding into sophisticated functionalism. By defining "mine-ness" as an irreducible subjective property prior to analysis, the manuscript insulates itself from the massive body of work by Seth (2021), Ciaunica et al. (2022), and Gerrans (2020). These theorists demonstrate that the phenomenal signature of mineness is perfectly explainable as the allostatic regulation of interoceptive prediction errors.

### The Failure of the Non-Reductive Bridging Premise

The manuscript notes that the route from the clinical separability of phenomenal layers to a non-physical conclusion runs through a "bridging premise" that essentially amounts to the dualist thesis itself. By the text's own admission, this inference risks assuming what it sets out to establish. To counteract this epistemic vulnerability, the manuscript must prove that physicalist accounts *cannot* capture the clinical datum of depersonalisation without leaving an unexplained phenomenal residue.

However, because the manuscript entirely omits the mechanics of sensory attenuation and precision weighting, its critique of physicalism rings hollow. Ciaunica et al. (2022) provide a robust active inference account of depersonalisation, showing how a disruption in sensory attenuation and aberrant self-focus triggers a split in the sense of agentive control. When prediction errors are persistently unattenuated, the organism is forced into a state of chronic uncertainty, failing to regulate internal states across multiple domains. This computational failure produces the exact phenomenology of "watching oneself from the outside" without requiring the subtraction of an ontologically distinct "self" tier. The manuscript’s failure to test its non-reductive threshold theory against these formidable, functionally complete competing frameworks is a disqualifying omission.

## The Fracturing of the Minimal Self: A Critique of Monolithic "For-Me-Ness"

The manuscript operates on the unexamined assumption that "for-me-ness" (often equated in the phenomenological literature with the minimal self or ipseity) is a singular, monolithic property of consciousness that is cleanly subtracted during depersonalisation. The text utilizes this alleged subtraction to argue that the normal, intact self-model possesses a specific, unified metaphysical weight that physicalism cannot account for. This interpretation of the minimal self as an indivisible unit of subjectivity is fundamentally out of step with the 2020–2025 phenomenological and cognitive science consensus.

### Perspectival Ownership is Never Lost

The manuscript uses depersonalisation as the archetype for the loss of "mine-ness." However, extensive philosophical and empirical work demonstrates that the minimal self is not fully eradicated even in the most severe psychopathological or dissociative states. Lane (2020) provides a definitive critique of the "minimal self hypothesis" in *Consciousness and Cognition*, arguing that while patients may report an "identity void" or a lack of warmth and intimacy in their thoughts, the sheer epistemic asymmetry of experience remains.

Even when a patient reports that a thought is inserted or that an action is not theirs, it is still undeniably *their* experience of an inserted thought or *their* feeling of alienation. The pre-reflective "perspectival ownership" is strictly preserved; what is altered is the cognitive integration of that experience. The manuscript's claim that depersonalisation represents a literal subtraction of "mine-ness" conflates the loss of *personal* ownership (the narrative or affective endorsement of a state) with the loss of *perspectival* ownership (the basic locus of experience). By treating "for-me-ness" as a monolithic tier that vanishes entirely, the manuscript builds a metaphysical argument on a severe phenomenological error.

### The Fragmentation of Self-Prioritization

Further undermining the manuscript's monolithic treatment of the self is recent experimental data on self-prioritization in depersonalisation. If the minimal self is a unified, non-reductive tier that is subtracted during depersonalisation, one would expect a universal degradation of all cognitive processes that rely on self-association. This is empirically false.

Wozniak, McEllin, Hohwy, and Ciaunica (2023) demonstrated that depersonalisation does not uniformly degrade self-related information processing. In their studies, individuals experiencing depersonalisation traits showed altered self-prioritization for *bodily* information, failing to show the normal cognitive facilitation when processing stimuli associated with their own physical body. However, crucially, their processing of *abstract* self-related information remained entirely intact, showing standard self-prioritization effects.

| Processing Domain | Phenomenal Representation | Impact of Depersonalisation (Wozniak et al., 2023) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bodily Self-Prioritization** | Somatic mapping, interoceptive integration, physical boundaries. | **Degraded:** Subjects fail to prioritize somatic/bodily self-associations. |
| **Abstract Self-Prioritization** | Narrative identity, abstract conceptual tagging, semantic self-association. | **Intact:** Subjects show normal prioritization for abstract self-stimuli. |

The empirical reality that abstract self-processing survives while bodily self-processing degrades perfectly aligns with the hierarchical, distributed nature of the predictive brain (as posited by physicalist active inference models) and directly contradicts the manuscript's non-reductive, unitary tier model. Depersonalisation is not the loss of a monolithic "self"; it is the computational decoupling of specific, lower-level bodily prediction systems from higher-level abstract narrative models. The manuscript's failure to address this modular fragmentation of selfhood renders its architectural deductions regarding a singular subjective tier void.

## Epistemic Circularity, Transparency, and the Misappropriation of "Fitness-Beats-Truth"

In a bid to fortify its non-reductive stance, the manuscript takes a radical epistemological turn, arguing that the intact self-model is "transparent" (hiding its representational nature) and that this opacity is self-concealing and self-protecting. Drawing heavily on Alston's theories of epistemic circularity and Hoffman, Singh, and Prakash's (2015) "Fitness-Beats-Truth" theorem, the manuscript suggests that evolutionary forces tune perceptual interfaces for survival rather than objective reality. From this, the text extrapolates that the collapse of this interface (as seen in depersonalisation) reveals an unbridgeable metaphysical void that physicalist science is epistemically barred from accessing.

### The Misinterpretation of Transparency Failure

The application of Thomas Metzinger’s phenomenal transparency concept here is highly selective and ultimately logically flawed. The manuscript claims that when transparency fails, as in depersonalisation or psychotic breaks, "what happens when the self-model cracks reveals what the intact system protects against". It portrays this clinical disruption as a confrontation with an epistemic void, suggesting that the brain is actively hiding the non-physical nature of the self to prevent "destabilisation anxiety".

However, modern computational phenomenology provides a far more parsimonious, physicalist explanation for transparency and its failure, which the manuscript entirely ignores. Within the active inference framework, an experience is phenomenally transparent when the generative model successfully minimizes prediction errors without requiring the organism to shift precision to the generative model itself. When this process fails—due to intractable, unattenuated prediction errors or a loss of interoceptive precision—the organism becomes hyper-aware of the representational mechanics. This is not an encounter with a dualistic "void"; it is simply the phenomenology of an overly rigid, inflexible prior expectation failing to update in the face of anomalous sensory evidence.

Fernandez (2024) and Ciaunica and Safron (2022) provide extensive analyses of how active inference accounts for the loss of self-as-object in various states. They demonstrate that depersonalisation is an *uncontrolled, rigid non-flexible (dis)integration* of habitual self-models, leaving the subject feeling "stuck" due to a failure in precision-weighting. The system is not protecting a metaphysical secret; it is suffering from a mathematically definable computational bottleneck. By treating a functional breakdown as an ontological revelation, the manuscript engages in profound mystification.

### The Functionalist Resolution of Epistemic Vertigo

Furthermore, the manuscript's use of the "Fitness-Beats-Truth" theorem to attack functionalism is logically inverted. The active inference paradigm fully accommodates the fact that brains optimize for fitness (defined mathematically as the minimization of variational free energy or surprise, which keeps the organism within its viable bounds). The fact that perception acts as a user interface tailored to the organism's survival (its Markov blanket) does not necessitate a retreat to non-reductive metaphysics.

The "epistemic vertigo" the manuscript describes as proof of a non-reductive gap is adequately and elegantly modeled as a profound failure of allostatic self-efficacy—the inability of the organism to predict its own regulatory capacities, resulting in overwhelming, unresolved prediction errors. By prematurely dismissing functionalist and computational architectures, the manuscript ignores robust 2020-2025 literature demonstrating that predictive cognitive architectures can non-trivially ground phenomenal experience, epistemic boundaries, and selfhood without invoking irreducibility. The manuscript's framing is entirely bracketed by the site's preconceived dualist commitments, preventing any genuine engagement with the explanatory power of modern computational theories.

## Phenomenological Conflation: The Misuse of the Cotard Heuristic

Finally, the manuscript attempts to build its metaphysical case on a highly questionable heuristic link between depersonalisation and the Cotard delusion (the nihilistic delusion wherein a patient believes they are dead or do not exist). The text relies extensively on Billon's (2016, 2023) assertion that depersonalisation is the "'as if' form of the Cotard syndrome," utilizing this rhetorical comparison to argue that depersonalisation represents a fundamental existential subtraction of the self, differing from Cotard only in the preservation of cognitive insight.

This framing, while perhaps rhetorically convenient for establishing a continuum of "self-loss," is profoundly challenged by contemporary neuropsychiatric literature. Davies and Coltheart (2022) explicitly investigate the relationship between the Cotard delusion and depersonalisation. Their findings propose that "in at least some cases, the idea 'I am dead' comes to mind in response to symptoms of depersonalisation or derealisation".

This temporal and causal distinction is catastrophic for the manuscript's architectural argument. If the Cotard delusion is an *abductive response* (a secondary, top-down cognitive explanation) to the primary, bottom-up aberrant prediction errors of depersonalisation, then Cotard and depersonalisation do not represent parallel, clean subtractions of a non-physical "for-me-ness" tier with varying levels of insight. Instead, they represent sequential stages of hierarchical belief updating in a damaged Bayesian inference system.

By treating Billon’s phenomenological heuristic as an architectural truth, the manuscript commits a mereological fallacy. It assigns systemic metaphysical significance to what is empirically demonstrable as a localized failure of interoceptive and exteroceptive integration, subsequently misinterpreted by higher-order cognitive faculties. The "as if" nature of depersonalisation (retaining insight) versus the delusional nature of Cotard (losing insight) maps perfectly onto the hierarchy of precision-weighting in predictive processing. The delusion arises when high precision is afforded to the aberrant interoceptive priors over contradictory exteroceptive evidence. This fully functionalist explanation entirely circumvents the need for the manuscript's non-reductive ontological claims, yet the manuscript leaves this abductive framework untested and unmentioned.

## Specific Weaknesses and Verdict

The manuscript under review exhibits severe structural, empirical, and theoretical flaws. It relies on a gerrymandered reading of the literature to support preconceived non-reductive commitments, systematically failing to test its hypotheses against the dominant computational paradigms of the current decade. The specific weaknesses justifying rejection are as follows:

1. **Misrepresentation of the Sense of Agency:** The manuscript claims that the sense of agency (initiation) is spared in depersonalisation, utilizing an ad-hoc "one-way separation" to justify its phenomenal architecture. This contradicts recent empirical data demonstrating profound temporal distortions and severe discrepancies between implicit sensorimotor binding and explicit agency in non-clinical depersonalisation traits (Ciaunica et al., 2024, *Scientific Reports*).
2. **Evasion of Interoceptive Active Inference Models:** The manuscript systematically ignores the dominant 2020–2025 paradigm for depersonalisation, actively bracketing models that successfully reduce the feeling of "mine-ness" to the allostatic regulation of interoceptive prediction errors within the anterior insula (Gerrans, 2020, *Frontiers in Psychology*).
3. **Monolithic Fallacy Regarding Minimal Selfhood:** The manuscript treats "for-me-ness" as a singular tier that is cleanly subtracted during depersonalisation. This ignores robust experimental evidence that depersonalisation fragments self-prioritization, degrading bodily information processing while leaving abstract, narrative self-representation fully intact (Wozniak et al., 2023, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance*).
4. **Phenomenological Conflation with Cotard Delusion:** The manuscript inappropriately utilizes Billon's heuristic linking depersonalisation to Cotard syndrome as evidence for architectural subtraction, ignoring contemporary abductive accounts which demonstrate that the Cotard delusion is a secondary cognitive inference responding to anomalous depersonalisation symptoms, not a parallel structural subtraction (Davies & Coltheart, 2022, *Cognitive Neuropsychiatry*).
5. **Misapplication of Phenomenal Transparency:** The manuscript invokes transparency failure and epistemic circularity to assert the existence of a non-reductive metaphysical void, failing to engage with active inference accounts that explain transparency breakdown purely computationally as the manifestation of rigidly inflexible prior expectations failing to update in the face of sensory evidence (Fernandez, 2024, *Frontiers in Psychology*).

**Verdict:** The manuscript's pervasive reliance on gerrymandered phenomenology, its documented empirical omissions regarding implicit agency and interoceptive self-prioritization, and its systematic evasion of the contemporary active inference literature render it fundamentally unsuitable for academic publication.
