# Referee Report: "Consciousness, Evolution, and the Limits of Biology"

The present manuscript, evaluated for publication in a top-tier philosophy of mind and biology journal (e.g., *Mind*, *Synthese*, or *Philosophical Studies*), attempts to construct a non-reductive, interactionist framework for the evolutionary function and biological emergence of consciousness. By attempting to synthesise quantum interface models, evolutionary biology, and the phenomenology of agency, the text makes highly ambitious claims regarding the causal efficacy of phenomenal experience and its discontinuous emergence in biological history. However, a rigorous audit of the manuscript’s empirical claims, deployment of citations, engagement with counterarguments, and logical inferences reveals fatal structural, evidentiary, and conceptual defects.

The manuscript suffers from profound empirical obsolescence, systematically ignoring definitive falsifications in quantum physics and cognitive neuroscience published between 2020 and 2025. Furthermore, the text relies on circular epistemological framings that hold only if the manuscript’s own highly idiosyncratic dualist commitments are assumed *a priori*, effectively begging the question against standard physicalist and illusionist accounts. Finally, the manuscript demonstrates a complete failure to engage with the most formidable contemporary frameworks in the philosophy of animal sentience and the metaphysics of sensory experience. This report dissects these failures across four specific dimensions: empirical accuracy against 2020s literature, reliance on retracted or challenged results, question-begging conceptual framings, and untested competing frameworks.

## Dimension A: Empirical Falsification of the Quantum Interface Mechanism

A central pillar of the manuscript’s argument for interactionist dualism is the assertion that consciousness exerts genuine causal influence on biological systems via a quantum-mechanical interface. To bridge the explanatory gap between non-physical phenomenality and physical behaviour, the text explicitly grounds its mechanism in quantum biology and gravity-related wave function collapse. The manuscript asserts that this mechanism operates via “the same selective logic that produced quantum magnetoreception in birds and quantum tunnelling in enzymes”. It further claims that “Minimal Quantum Interaction explains why biology's tools miss consciousness's causal contribution”.

This reliance on macroscopic quantum coherence in the warm, wet environment of the brain constitutes a severe empirical failure, relying on theoretical physics that has been decisively constrained, if not outright falsified, in the contemporary experimental literature. The text deploys Roger Penrose’s gravitational objective reduction (and the associated Diósi–Penrose model) as the theoretical baseline for this quantum interface. The Diósi-Penrose (DP) model suggests that a spatial quantum superposition generates a superposition of different space-times, which nature suppresses, causing wave function collapse at a rate proportional to the gravitational self-energy of the difference between the two mass distributions. The manuscript leverages this to suggest that gravity-related collapse provides a natural, non-ad-hoc locus for conscious intervention in neural superpositions.

However, the manuscript entirely ignores the definitive 2021 empirical falsification of the parameter-free Diósi–Penrose model. In a landmark underground experiment at the Gran Sasso laboratory, Donadi et al. (2021), published in *Nature Physics* (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-020-1008-4), measured the spontaneous radiation emission predicted by the continuous spatial diffusion inherent in gravity-related wave function collapse. Using a high-purity germanium detector in an ultra-low background environment over 62 days, the researchers found no evidence of the predicted spontaneous X-ray emission. This null result set a lower bound on the effective spatial resolution of the mass density ($R_0 \gtrsim 0.54 \times 10^{-10}$ m) that is roughly three orders of magnitude larger than previous bounds.

This result completely rules out the natural, parameter-free version of the Diósi–Penrose model upon which non-ad-hoc theories of gravitational quantum mind rely. While it is theoretically possible to rescue gravity-related collapse models by introducing arbitrary free parameters to avoid the radiation constraints, doing so destroys the very mathematical elegance and physical necessity that the manuscript attempts to appropriate. By anchoring its theory of mental causation to an empirically falsified baseline collapse mechanism without acknowledging the 2021 Gran Sasso results, the manuscript builds its evolutionary argument on a thoroughly debunked physical foundation.

Furthermore, the manuscript’s analogy to “quantum magnetoreception in birds and quantum tunnelling in enzymes” betrays a profound misunderstanding of the decoherence timescales involved in macroscopic neural activity. The avian compass relies on radical pair mechanisms in specific proteins (cryptochromes) that are highly isolated and operate on extraordinarily brief timescales before thermal decoherence destroys the entanglement. To extrapolate from these highly protected, nanoscale quantum effects to macroscopic, behaviour-steering neural superpositions capable of being "selected" by consciousness demonstrates a reckless disregard for the established physics of environmental decoherence in biological tissue. The text fails to provide any mathematical apparatus or decoherence timescale calculations to justify how a superposed neural state could survive long enough to be influenced by phenomenal consciousness in the brain. Without addressing the Donadi et al. (2021) falsification, the manuscript’s entire interactionist interface collapses into mere speculative fiction, rendering the subsequent evolutionary arguments scientifically void.

## Dimension B: Obsolescence in Animal Consciousness and Threshold Dynamics

The manuscript consistently frames the emergence of consciousness in evolutionary history as a discrete, threshold-crossing event. The text argues for a “genuine discontinuity” in biological history, asserting that the evolution of consciousness must “carve nature at its joints”. In its taxonomy of emergence, the manuscript claims that “Threshold emergence: Consciousness appeared suddenly when certain organisational thresholds were crossed... Below threshold: nothing. Above: everything”. It further dismisses gradualist accounts by insisting that they fail to answer the hard problem at intermediate scales, forcing a binary view where organisms either possess full phenomenal experience or are entirely devoid of it.

This binary threshold framing is completely disconnected from the contemporary consensus in the philosophy of animal minds and cognitive ethology. The 2020–2025 literature is overwhelmingly dominated by arguments for moral-status gradualism and the recognition of multidimensional, scalar sentience. The most devastating omission in the manuscript is its failure to engage with the multidimensional framework of animal consciousness established by Birch, Schnell, and Clayton (2020), published in *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2020.07.007). Birch et al. forcefully argue that consciousness should not be treated as a monolithic, binary threshold that a species either crosses or fails to cross. Instead, they propose a multidimensional framework that maps specific consciousness profiles across diverse taxa, thoroughly dismantling the manuscript's archaic "lights on / lights off" dichotomy.

To illustrate the severity of the manuscript's obsolescence, one must observe how the contemporary literature systematically evaluates animal sentience, which contradicts the manuscript's discrete threshold logic:

| Dimension of Consciousness (Birch et al., 2020) | Definition within the 2020s Literature | Manuscript's Obsolete Binary Counterpart |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Perceptual Richness (p-richness)** | The level of detail, bandwidth, and multidimensionality of sensory input processing, varying continuously across species based on neural complexity. | Assumes sensory processing either achieves total phenomenal rendering or remains purely mechanical ("zombie" processing). |
| **Evaluative Richness (e-richness)** | The capacity for valenced affective states (pain, pleasure) which scales with neuroanatomical architecture and interoceptive mapping. | Treats valence as an indivisible, irreducible phenomenal currency that either exists absolutely or not at all. |
| **Synchronic Integration** | The degree to which distinct sensory streams are unified into a single perceptual moment, testable via cross-modal binding tasks. | Requires a totalizing global workspace or quantum coherence threshold for any unification to occur. |
| **Diachronic Integration** | The capacity to integrate experiences across time (episodic memory, trace conditioning). | Ignored; time-consciousness is treated as a downstream feature of an already-established binary conscious agent. |
| **Self-consciousness** | The varying gradations of bodily self-recognition, source monitoring, and meta-cognition. | Conflates basic basal sentience with the high-level capacity for meta-representational self-modelling. |

The manuscript’s insistence on "genuine discontinuity" forces it into highly ad hoc, post-facto ethical and biological manoeuvres. By refusing to adopt a scalar view of sentience—where a cephalopod or insect might possess a biologically measurable fraction of the phenomenological complexity of a primate due to massive variations in these five dimensions—the manuscript fails to account for the actual evolutionary record. Evolution rarely, if ever, produces absolute functional discontinuities; traits emerge via gradual accumulation, continuous variation, and exaptation. By demanding an absolute threshold where “Below threshold: nothing; Above: everything,” the manuscript imposes a pre-Darwinian essentialism onto the continuum of biological evolution.

Furthermore, the text briefly mentions Ginsburg and Jablonka's Unlimited Associative Learning (UAL) framework as proposing a "specific threshold". However, it fundamentally misreads the 2020s literature surrounding UAL. As detailed in the recent literature, UAL is increasingly interpreted not as an absolute binary switch that turns the "lights on," but as an evolutionary marker indicating a high degree of integration and evaluative richness that is built upon older, more basal forms of learning and sentience. The manuscript's failure to test its dualist threshold theory against the formidable competing multidimensional frameworks (e.g., Birch et al., 2020) constitutes a disqualifying omission for a philosophy of biology paper.

## Dimension C: Failure to Acknowledge the Adversarial Falsification of Cognitive Architectures

The manuscript relies heavily on cognitive organisational thresholds—specifically pointing to Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT) and Integrated Information Theory (IIT)—as empirical analogues or competitors to its own quantum dualist threshold. The text cites these theories to ground its argument about physical systems achieving the "right architecture" for consciousness to emerge: “Neural systems with the right architecture—perhaps involving quantum coherence, global broadcasting, or integrated information—provide interfaces; other systems don't”. However, the manuscript treats these theories as static, abstract philosophical models rather than active empirical research programs that have recently undergone devastating experimental falsification.

The manuscript fundamentally ignores the 2025 results of the Cogitate Consortium, a landmark adversarial collaboration led by Lucia Melloni and colleagues. Published in *Nature* (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1), the study "Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness" subjected both GNWT and IIT to direct, pre-registered neuroimaging tests. The experimental setup was unprecedented in consciousness science: proponents of both theories agreed in advance on specific predictions and what would count as falsifying evidence, utilizing a massive dataset comprising functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), magnetoencephalography (MEG), and intracranial electroencephalography (iEEG) across 256 participants.

The Cogitate Consortium data severely challenged the core biological implementations of both theories. IIT’s central prediction—that conscious perception depends on sustained, continuous neural synchronization specifically in the posterior cortical 'hot zone' for the entire duration of a conscious experience—was not supported by the data. The posterior regions failed to maintain the predicted sustained synchronization across time. Simultaneously, GNWT’s prediction regarding the prefrontal cortex (PFC)—that conscious access requires a sustained "ignition" and a distinct offset response in the PFC—was also challenged. The prefrontal signals observed via iEEG were primarily transient and onset-responsive, failing to demonstrate the late amplification, global broadcasting, and offset-tracking required by the global workspace hypothesis.

By continuing to cite these threshold architectures as viable empirical paradigms for the biological emergence of consciousness, the manuscript operates in a pre-2025 empirical vacuum. The author utilises IIT and GNWT to gesture toward the idea that specific neural architectures might provide the requisite interface for interactionist dualism. Yet, because the manuscript entirely misses the Melloni et al. (2025) falsification of these very architectural predictions, its neurobiological grounding is rendered obsolete. A contemporary philosophical account of the biological function of consciousness cannot merely mention GNWT or IIT as theoretical possibilities; it must actively grapple with the collapse of their empirical predictions under adversarial testing. The manuscript’s failure to do so indicates a superficial, outdated engagement with the cognitive neuroscience of consciousness, rendering its assertions regarding "architectural interfaces" entirely speculative.

## Dimension D: Structural Circularity in the Zombie-Selection Argument

The most egregious logical flaw in the manuscript lies in its central evolutionary argument regarding the "Zombie Intuition." The text attempts to weaponise the concept of the philosophical zombie (a creature physically identical to a conscious organism but lacking inner experience) against evolutionary materialism. The manuscript explicitly claims that under physicalism, “natural selection … cannot distinguish the conscious version from the zombie. Both survive equally. Both reproduce equally”. The argument suggests that if consciousness has no physical effects (epiphenomenalism) or is merely identical to functional states (materialism), evolution could not have specifically selected for *phenomenal* experience, rendering the existence of consciousness a biological mystery that only dualism can solve.

However, this framing holds only if the manuscript selectively assumes the premises of the very materialist frameworks it seeks to attack, while simultaneously suspending its own foundational tenets. The manuscript is built upon a framework of interactionist dualism, specifically "Bidirectional Causation," which posits that consciousness genuinely authors action by selecting among superposed quantum neural trajectories. If consciousness literally “chooses” which superposed neural firing patterns become actual, then consciousness is doing objective physical work in the universe.

This creates a fatal internal contradiction. Under the manuscript’s own interactionist commitments, a "zombie" that lacks consciousness would *not* be behaviourally or physically identical to the conscious organism. Because the zombie lacks the non-physical consciousness required to bias quantum outcomes and select optimal neural trajectories, its behaviour would diverge radically from the conscious organism. The zombie would be subject to random quantum decoherence or deterministic physical laws, leading to different motor outputs, diminished adaptive flexibility, and ultimately, drastically lower fitness. Therefore, natural selection *can* distinguish between the conscious organism and the zombie, because the conscious organism would exhibit physically measurable, behaviourally adaptive deviations driven directly by its quantum-biasing consciousness.

By asserting that "both survive equally, both reproduce equally," the text begs the question against materialism by illicitly importing an epiphenomenalist premise (that consciousness does nothing physical) into its critique, while simultaneously hiding its own interactionist premise (that consciousness does something physical). The author stages the zombie-evolution problem as broadly forceful against physicalism, but under the manuscript's own rules, the conscious organism and any behaviourally matched zombie are *not* physical duplicates. The classic zombie argument depends entirely on the premise of exact physical and behavioural duplication—a premise the manuscript's interactionist dualism explicitly denies.

This structural circularity renders the entire "Evolutionary Case for Mental Causation" section logically incoherent. The author cannot claim that evolution is blind to consciousness (to attack physicalism) while concurrently arguing that evolution selected consciousness precisely because it biases physical action (to defend dualism). The text fails to navigate the classic interactionist/screening-off dilemma, utilizing physicalist causal closure when convenient for a *reductio ad absurdum*, and discarding it when establishing its own theory. This demonstrates a profound lack of philosophical rigor.

## Dimension E: Strawman Dismissals of Illusionist and Qualitative Physicalism

The manuscript’s engagement with opposing philosophical frameworks—specifically Illusionism and Representationalism—is superficial and highly dismissive, failing to test its dualist assumptions against the strongest contemporary counterarguments in the 2020–2025 literature. The text attempts to dismiss physicalist and illusionist epistemology by demanding a “missing metarepresentational mechanism” and asserting that illusionism assumes the very phenomenal properties it seeks to deny, claiming that a "seeming" of consciousness requires a conscious observer to experience the seeming.

This critique targets a weak strawman version of illusionism and completely ignores the sophisticated, biologically grounded developments in the contemporary literature. The manuscript fails to engage with François Kammerer's (2021) seminal work in *Synthese*, "The illusion of conscious experience" (https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-019-02138-1), and Keith Frankish's recent elaborations on weak illusionism. Kammerer systematically demonstrates how introspective misrepresentation is not an evolutionary accident or a logical paradox requiring a "homunculus," but a highly adaptive, computationally efficient cognitive architecture. Illusionism argues that our introspective mechanisms evolved to track complex, multidimensional physical states of the brain, but represent them to the organism in a simplified, unified, and qualitatively "thick" format for the sake of efficient behavioural control and global broadcasting.

The manuscript naively assumes that for an illusion of phenomenality to exist, there must be a phenomenal experience *of* the illusion. Kammerer and Frankish bypass this by providing strictly functional, non-phenomenal accounts of how cognitive systems can possess dispositions to judge, react, and report that they have phenomenal properties, without any actual non-physical phenomenal properties existing. By claiming that misrepresentation-under-selection requires its own evolutionary account of "genuine" consciousness, the manuscript simply begs the question. It assumes the irreducible reality of the very target property that illusionism successfully naturalises as an adaptive cognitive shortcut. The manuscript’s failure to grapple with the evolutionary utility of introspective opacity—the idea that it is biologically cheaper, faster, and more adaptive for a brain to model its own massively parallel physical states as irreducible "qualia"—leaves its critique of illusionism entirely hollow.

Furthermore, the manuscript’s framing of sensory experience relies on a naive dismissal of the metaphysics of perception, setting up a false dichotomy between reductive representationalism and its own quantum dualism. It ignores formidable contemporary alternatives, such as David Papineau's (2021) *The Metaphysics of Sensory Experience*, published by Oxford University Press (https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198862390.001.0001). Papineau defends a qualitative, non-representational view of sensory experience that remains entirely physicalist.

Papineau demonstrates that one can reject the idea that conscious states are essentially representational relations to the external world, and instead accept that conscious sensory properties are intrinsic, qualitative states ("all paint," in his terminology), without abandoning the physical causal closure of the brain or resorting to quantum dualism. Sensory experiences, under this view, are intrinsic physical properties of the brain that happen to be correlated with external objects, much like marks on a page have intrinsic physical shapes while also carrying representational weight. The manuscript behaves as if the only alternative to a failed reductive representationalism is its own brand of interactionist quantum dualism. By failing to test its framework against Papineau's qualitative physicalism, the manuscript creates a theoretical vacuum that artificially inflates the appeal of its dualist conclusions. A rigorous manuscript must defeat the strongest versions of physicalism—not just the most vulnerable representationalist models.

## Conclusion and List of Weaknesses

The manuscript’s attempt to construct an evolutionary biology of consciousness based on quantum interactionist dualism is fundamentally and structurally flawed. It rests on falsified quantum physics, obsolete binary biology, and logically circular philosophical argumentation. The systematic evasion of contemporary empirical tests (such as the Cogitate Consortium's adversarial testing) and robust counter-frameworks (such as scalar animal sentience and illusionist epistemology) renders the arguments invalid.
