# Referee Report: Audit of "Dualism Cartography" and Associated Metaphysical Framework

## 1. Introduction and Scope of the Dialectical Audit

The manuscript under review, centered on the apex article "Dualism Cartography" and its underlying conceptual scaffolding, advances a highly systematic defense of interactionist substance dualism. The framework attempts to synthesize contemporary neuroscience, quantum foundations, and the philosophy of mind into a cohesive, non-reductive architecture. By structuring its arguments around a set of explicitly stated foundational tenets—including bare dualism, a hypothesized "Minimal Quantum Interaction," and the explicit rejection of the Many-Worlds interpretation—the text attempts to circumvent traditional materialist objections through an elaborate "mechanism cost" methodology. This approach purportedly charts positions on the mind-body problem by exposing each theoretical cell to a fixed battery of mechanism questions to calculate its explanatory debts. The central article begins by noting that a "recurring confusion runs through the dualism debate: the question 'is dualism...'", promising to resolve foundational ambiguities by mapping the exact costs of various ontological commitments.   

However, subjecting the substantive philosophical content, logical inferences, and empirical scaffolding of this submission to rigorous academic scrutiny reveals fatal structural and methodological defects. The manuscript systematically engages in metaphysical bracketing, whereby heavy ontological commitments are smuggled into the premises to dismiss competing physicalist paradigms by definitional fiat rather than argument. Furthermore, it demonstrates a profound mishandling of both the empirical neuroscience literature and the contemporary philosophy of causation. The text relies on gerrymandered assimilations of neurobiological phenomena, distorts the metaphysical architectures it borrows, and willfully ignores modern philosophical literature that directly undermines its foundational load-bearing inferences.

This report audits the submission across four strictly defined dimensions:

1. Empirical accuracy against 2020s consciousness science and philosophy of mind.
2. Reliance on results or theoretical maneuverings that have been challenged, retracted, or conceptually superseded.
3. Framings that quietly hold a contested commitment fixed instead of arguing for it.
4. Competing frameworks and lethal objections left entirely untested.

The subsequent sections will systematically dismantle the manuscript's architectural foundations, scrutinize its mishandling of key philosophical figures, verify its empirical claims against the 2020–2025 literature, and expose its failure to engage with the bleeding edge of the causation and consciousness debates.

## 2. Metaphysical Overreach in the Causal Exclusion Problem

A central pillar of the audited project is its attempt to rescue mental causation from Jaegwon Kim’s devastating causal exclusion argument. Kim famously argued that four principles are jointly inconsistent: the reality of mental causation, the causal closure of the physical domain, the irreducibility of the mental, and the ban on systematic causal overdetermination. If every physical event has a sufficient physical cause (the principle of the causal closure of the physical), there is no causal work left for an immaterial mind to do without invoking absurd, systematic overdetermination. Non-reductive physicalists and dualists alike are thus forced into a trilemma: accept epiphenomenalism, embrace reductionism, or swallow the bitter pill of ubiquitous, coincidental overdetermination.   

### 2.1 The Misappropriation of Trumping Preemption

To bypass this nomological constraint, the manuscript invokes the concept of "trumping preemption," originally articulated by Jonathan Schaffer (2000), as a structural metaphysical template for mental causation. Schaffer introduced trumping as a fourth species of redundant causation, distinct from early preemption, late preemption, and symmetric overdetermination. In Schaffer’s originating thought experiment, the characters Merlin and Morgana both cast spells to turn a prince into a frog; the laws of magic dictate that the higher-ranking spell (Merlin's) takes effect, thus "trumping" the lower-ranking spell, even though both processes run to completion and are causally sufficient. The manuscript attempts to wield this architecture to posit that a non-physical experiential state can serve as the "authoritative" cause of a physical effect, while its corresponding physical neural correlate remains merely "sufficient" in a counterfactual sense.   

This represents a catastrophic misunderstanding of how causal authority functions within a rigorous metaphysical framework. The translation of magical laws conferring authority to neurobiological and psychophysical laws is entirely fraught. As later critiques of Schaffer's work have persuasively argued, Schafferian trumping ultimately reduces to either early preemption with a hidden cutting point or, more damagingly for the dualist, symmetric overdetermination with causally redundant properties. If the author’s ultimate fallback defense against the exclusion problem is to simply bite the bullet and stipulate that systematic overdetermination is philosophically acceptable when masked as "trumping," then the entire extensive detour through the preemption literature is dialectically inert. The manuscript utilizes the complex architecture of trumping merely as window dressing; when pressed on the metaphysical viability of that architecture, the text abandons it entirely in favor of brute-force overdetermination justified by definitional fiat.   

### 2.2 The Omission of Contemporary Interventionist Semantics

More damningly, the manuscript’s central theoretical problem—the perceived need to meticulously separate causal sufficiency from causal authority using the complex machinery of trumping preemption—relies entirely on an antiquated reading of the exclusion argument. The text ignores vital recent literature demonstrating that strict causal sufficiency is a flawed and entirely unnecessary requirement for formulating or solving the exclusion problem.

Specifically, the manuscript wholly omits Bram Vaassen's (2021) critical work, "Causal Exclusion without Causal Sufficiency," published in *Synthese*. Vaassen demonstrates that traditional exclusion arguments rely on a highly problematic notion of causal sufficiency—one that requires causes to be spatially enormous and maximally specific to guarantee their effects. Vaassen proves that valid, modern exclusion arguments can and should be formulated using directed acyclic graphs and counterfactual dependence without relying on sufficiency at all. By omitting this foundational work, the manuscript wastes thousands of words attempting to bypass a concept of "sufficiency" that the contemporary philosophy of causation has already discarded.   

Furthermore, the manuscript attempts to appropriate the interventionist theory of causation, heavily associated with James Woodward, without respecting its strict criteria. Woodward (2021) has extensively defended the view that true causal claims must satisfy conditions of "proportionality" and "conditional irrelevance" to be explanatory. Conditional irrelevance is what allows upper-level explanations to be autonomous from microscopic details, a condition used widely in the emergence literature to abstract from microscopic dynamics. The manuscript's interactionist dualism fails to satisfy these conditions, as it posits downward causal efficacy from a non-physical mind onto specific quantum indeterminacies in the brain—a maneuver that fundamentally violates the conditional independence required by a coherent interventionist framework. Interventionism must either require interventions on a composite object to hold the behavior of its parts fixed or allow such interventions to vary the behavior of those parts; the manuscript attempts to gerrymander a third option where the non-physical mind intervenes without respecting the structural equations of the physical system it influences.   

## 3. The Distortion of Delegatory Dualism and the Unearned Quantum Assimilation

The load-bearing pillar of the manuscript’s positive proposal is its reliance on Bradford Saad’s concept of delegatory dualism, primarily articulated in his 2025 paper "A Dualist Theory of Experience" in *Philosophical Studies*. The manuscript explicitly claims that its architecture is "structurally close to Saad’s delegatory dualism read minimally". However, a forensic examination of this claim reveals that the manuscript fundamentally misrepresents and structurally violates Saad's explicit ontological commitments.   

### 3.1 The Hijacking of Observational Closure

Saad’s delegatory framework is designed to meet a stringent set of constraints for constructing a dualist theory: it must construe experiences as causes of physical effects, ensure that experiences do not cause observable violations of the causal closure of the physical domain, avoid overdetermination, specify a set of psychophysical laws, and ensure that functional duplication preserves phenomenology. To achieve this, the framework operates via two fundamental laws: a Subset Law, which assigns experiences to physical states with matching causal profiles, and a Delegatory Law, which dictates that when a match occurs, the experience preempts the physical state’s causal contribution and becomes the authoritative cause.   

Crucially, Saad’s framework replaces the strict, universal causal closure of the physical domain with "Observational Closure," a weaker constraint permitting unobservable violations of physical closure while maintaining strict empirical adequacy. Saad rigorously defines Observational Closure as a structural thesis concerning the formal mapping of causal profiles. It is explicitly engineered to remain neutral regarding the lower-level physical mechanisms that might underwrite it. In Saad's framework, the delegation of causal authority from a physical state to an experiential state requires no specific quantum-mechanical interpretation to function logically; it relies purely on sui generis psychophysical laws.   

### 3.2 Metaphysical Bracketing and the Born-Rule Forcing

The manuscript shatters Saad's careful physical bracketing by violently forcing a "Minimal Quantum Interaction" requirement onto the delegatory framework. The text posits a "delegation-selection bridge," arguing that Saad's default causal profile "just is the Born-rule distribution," thereby attempting to make delegation the structure and quantum selection the mechanism of a single causal event.   

This aggressive co-optation of Saad to legitimize highly speculative quantum biology constitutes a severe misreading of Saad’s methodology. It relies on framings that are merely bracketed by the author's own prior dualist commitments. As the manuscript's own version history ironically concedes, this forced quantum assimilation is "a foundational move the programme has not earned". By treating Minimal Quantum Interaction as an established biophysical reality rather than a highly contested frontier, the manuscript surgically strips rigorous causal models of their requisite ontological neutrality, forcibly welding them to incompatible, speculative quantum-collapse mechanisms.   

The text routinely elevates speculative physics to the status of established neurobiological mechanisms, ignoring the immense theoretical and empirical hurdles facing quantum models of consciousness. For instance, the orchestration of objective reduction (Orch OR) and other quantum-mind hypotheses face devastating decoherence timescale objections; the warm, wet, and noisy environment of the brain forces quantum superposition states to collapse orders of magnitude too quickly to influence neural firing in the manner the manuscript envisions. The manuscript holds the contested commitment of macroscopic quantum coherence fixed, rather than engaging with the fatal biophysical critiques it faces.

## 4. Empirical Mishandling of the Information-Theoretic Bottleneck

Beyond its metaphysical overreach, the manuscript attempts to ground its interactionist dualism in contemporary empirical neuroscience, specifically focusing on the bandwidth constraints of human cognition. The text heavily leverages Jieyu Zheng and Markus Meister's 2024/2025 research, which quantifies a massive asymmetry in human information processing: sensory systems deliver approximately 1 billion bits per second (109 bits/s) to the brain, while behavioral output is bottlenecked at a vanishingly small ~10 bits per second.   

### 4.1 The Zheng-Meister Baseline and the Misappropriation of the Filter Theory

Zheng and Meister synthesized existing measurements across diverse domains—from choice reaction experiments and memory sports to reading rates—using Shannon information theory to conclude that human conscious throughput is bounded at ~10 bits/s. They proposed a distinction between an "outer brain" for high-dimensional parallel sensory and motor processing, and an "inner brain" for serialized, low-dimensional conscious thought.   

The manuscript seizes upon this "108-fold reduction in information rate" to argue for a dualistic "Filter" theory. It claims that this massive data reduction proves the brain acts as a literal interface that prepares options for a non-physical consciousness to select among. The text confidently asserts that this bottleneck "strongly supports Dualism—the bottleneck is inexplicable if consciousness is merely neural computation, since the neural hardware has vastly more capacity". Furthermore, the manuscript states that "the structural argument is not 'the hierarchy proves the filter theory' but 'the hierarchy locates the disagreement at the mechanism-cost layer'", effectively arguing that the physicalist must pay a massive explanatory cost for this bottleneck that the dualist gets for free.   

### 4.2 The Sauerbrei-Pruszynski Rebuttal and the Evasion of Motor Bandwidth

However, this conclusion relies on a deliberate misrepresentation of the contemporary scientific debate surrounding these exact figures. In 2025, Britton Sauerbrei and J. Andrew Pruszynski published a direct critique of the Zheng & Meister claims in *Nature Neuroscience*, titled "The brain works at more than 10 bits per second". Sauerbrei and Pruszynski demonstrate that unconscious motor control processes, which guide complex movement in unpredictable environments, significantly exceed the 10 bits/s limit. They calculate that the vestibulo-ocular reflex and other continuous motor adjustments require vastly more bandwidth than Zheng and Meister's methodology accounts for, establishing that the 10 bits/s figure is a lower bound restricted only to *conscious* declarative reporting, not a systemic limit on the brain's physical or behavioral output.   

The manuscript attempts to assimilate the Sauerbrei & Pruszynski (2025) critique by claiming it strengthens the dualist position. The text argues that because Sauerbrei and Pruszynski accept the ~10 bits/s ceiling for conscious cognition but show that unconscious motor control exceeds it, they have successfully located the constraint at "conscious access rather than in the computational substrate".   

### 4.3 The Suppression of Shannon Channel Coding and Cognitive Reserve

This is a textbook example of holding a contested commitment fixed instead of arguing for it. The manuscript ignores the actual physicalist explanation for this bandwidth gap provided in the literature. As outlined in the surrounding scientific discourse, the 109-to-10 bits/s gap between sensory input and behavioral output is hypothesized to function as "error-correcting redundancy in the sense of Shannon's channel coding theorem". In this robust physicalist framework, progressive neuronal loss maps to symbol erasure in a redundant code. The critical damage threshold, below which cognitive function is preserved and above which it collapses, is mathematically defined, providing a quantitative, information-theoretic foundation for cognitive reserve against dementia pathology.   

Rather than testing its dualist interface theory against this robust, mathematically grounded physicalist alternative, the manuscript simply declares the dualist reading victorious. It conflates the bandwidth of conscious accessibility with the ontological reality of an immaterial mind, completely failing to address how an error-correcting redundant physical network perfectly mimics the "bottleneck" it mistakenly attributes to a soul. By suppressing the Shannon channel coding explanation, the manuscript fabricates an explanatory void that it then conveniently fills with quantum dualism.

## 5. False Dichotomies and the Evasion of Russellian Monism

The most glaring omission in the manuscript’s "Dualism Cartography" is its complete failure to engage with the dominant non-reductive physicalist framework of the 2020s: Russellian Monism. The manuscript dedicates itself to exposing each theoretical cell to a fixed battery of mechanism questions to calculate "mechanism cost". It claims that materialism incurs impossible explanatory debts regarding phenomenal consciousness, thereby justifying the extreme ontological costs of interactionist dualism and quantum-collapse interfaces.   

### 5.1 The Structure and Dynamics Argument (Alter 2021)

By framing the debate strictly as a binary between reductive materialism and interactionist dualism, the text leaves the most lethal competing framework entirely untested. The contemporary case against standard materialism is best articulated by Torin Alter's "Structure and Dynamics Argument against Materialism" (2016, 2021). Alter’s argument, foundational to modern Russellian monism, demonstrates that physical theory only describes the world in terms of its spatiotemporal structure and dynamics. Physics gives us the causal roles, the spatiotemporal states, and the equations governing their evolution (the structure), but remains entirely silent on the intrinsic nature of the entities that instantiate those roles. As Bertrand Russell noted, "as to what it is that changes, and what it changes from and to—as to this, physics is silent".   

Russellian Monism steps into this epistemic gap. It posits that phenomenal properties (or proto-phenomenal properties, known as quiddities) serve as the intrinsic, categorical grounds for the dispositional properties described by fundamental physics. Under this framework, consciousness is not a non-physical substance intervening from the outside (which violates causal closure and incurs the massive mechanism costs of dualism), but rather the intrinsic nature of the physical itself.   

### 5.2 The Untested Mechanism Costs of Non-Reductive Physicalism

If Russellian Monism is true, it solves the hard problem of consciousness and the causal exclusion problem simultaneously. Because phenomenal properties *are* the intrinsic realizers of physical roles, they are causally efficacious in virtue of grounding those physical roles. There is no overdetermination, no need for the antiquated machinery of "trumping preemption," no need to violate the causal closure of the physical, and absolutely no need to postulate highly speculative "Minimal Quantum Interactions" to serve as a biological interface bridge.

The manuscript’s "Dualism Cartography" is fundamentally invalid because it fails to plot Russellian Monism on its grid. By ignoring the Structure and Dynamics argument, the manuscript artificially inflates the relative plausibility of its own interactionist dualism. It forces the reader to choose between eliminative materialism and quantum dualism, deliberately suppressing the literature that demonstrates how non-reductive accounts can thrive without the fatal biophysical costs of interactionism. While Russellian monism faces its own challenges—most notably the combination problem regarding how microsubjects aggregate to constitute macrosubjects—a cartography that fails to map the primary continent of its opponents is not a philosophical map; it is a gerrymandered ideological tool.   

## 6. Citation Integrity, Context Stripping, and Methodological Failures

A hostile audit of the manuscript’s scholarly apparatus reveals a distressing pattern of misattribution, context-stripping, and empirical failure. The text frequently appropriates the language of philosophers and scientists to support a dualist metaphysics that the original authors explicitly rejected or heavily bracketed.

### 6.1 Contextual Mismatches in Anti-Physicalist Evidence

The manuscript routinely isolates quotes to generate a false sense of congruence with its own tenets. For example, it extracts quotes from Graham ("I am located therefore I am") and Mathews ("mind is… primordial; human consciousness is just a single mode") to serve as metaphysical anti-physicalist evidence congruent with its first tenet. However, as the project's own version history acknowledges regarding this section, this is an interpretive equivocation; the source material "is framed as ethics, Law, and relationality rather than as a thesis about consciousness". Transplanting ethical, ecological, and relational frameworks into a hard metaphysical ontology of interactionist substance dualism is academically dishonest. Furthermore, the manuscript mislabels Searle's distinction between intrinsic and derived intentionality, initially claiming that "all intentionality is conscious or derivable from conscious states", a gloss that conflates the Connection Principle with entirely separate doctrines.   

### 6.2 Granqvist and the Falsification of Sufficiency

In its attempt to validate its architectural arguments against materialist production theories, the manuscript leans on empirical studies that actively contradict its claims. In its discussion of the "God Helmet" and the argument from mechanism, the text relies on Granqvist et al. (2005) to discuss the sliding necessity-to-sufficiency anatomy of brain stimulation. However, the manuscript fails to accurately report the findings. The Granqvist study actually found "no evidence for any effects of the magnetic fields," meaning the sufficiency premise it attempts to leverage was never established in the literature. Relying on a study that failed to find an effect to support a complex metaphysical argument about causal sufficiency is a catastrophic methodological failure.   

### 6.3 Misattribution in the Category Theory Literature

The manuscript demonstrates a failure to maintain accurate academic metadata, raising severe concerns about the rigor of its literature review. In its attempt to formalize the interface void, it mistakenly credits the 2019 article in *Entropy* (21(12):1234), titled "Mathematics and the Brain: A Category Theoretical Approach to Go Beyond the Neural Correlates of Consciousness," to "Chang & Tsuchiya". A rudimentary check of the 2019 publication record proves the actual authors are Georg Northoff, Naotsugu Tsuchiya, and Hayato Saigo. Misattributing foundational category theory papers while attempting to build a highly technical mathematical formalization of consciousness demonstrates a lack of basic scholarly diligence.   

## 7. Five Specific Weaknesses Justifying Rejection

Per the remit of this audit, the following five specific, critical weaknesses—each grounded in the 2020-2025 peer-reviewed literature—render the manuscript unfit for academic publication.

### 7.1 Mishandling of Saad (2025) via Unwarranted Quantum Assimilation

The manuscript fatally misrepresents the ontological commitments of Bradford Saad’s delegatory dualism. The text asserts that its framework is "structurally close to Saad’s delegatory dualism read minimally" but then forces a microphysical wave-function collapse mechanism onto it. Saad (2025, *Philosophical Studies*) rigorously defines "Observational Closure" as permitting unobservable physical violations, but he explicitly engineers his theory to remain completely agnostic about physical implementation to preserve its structural validity. The manuscript’s aggressive co-optation of Saad to legitimize highly speculative quantum biology (e.g., the Born rule and microtubule collapse) is a severe misreading of structural methodology that violates the exact parameters Saad established.   

### 7.2 Omission of Vaassen (2021) on Causal Sufficiency

The manuscript spends immense theoretical capital attempting to bypass the problem of causal sufficiency via the antiquated machinery of "trumping preemption". It entirely ignores Bram Vaassen (2021, *Synthese*), who decisively demonstrates that traditional exclusion arguments rely on a highly problematic notion of causal sufficiency, and that valid, modern exclusion arguments can and should be formulated strictly via counterfactual dependence without relying on sufficiency at all. By omitting this literature, the manuscript’s primary defensive architecture against causal exclusion is rendered dialectically obsolete and irrelevant to contemporary debates.   

### 7.3 Misrepresentation of the Information-Theoretic Bottleneck (Sauerbrei & Pruszynski 2025)

The manuscript leverages the ~10 bits/s behavioral throughput limit identified by Zheng & Meister (2024/2025) as definitive proof of a dualist "Filter" bottleneck, claiming it is inexplicable by neural computation. It superficially acknowledges Sauerbrei & Pruszynski (2025, *Nature Neuroscience*), who prove unconscious motor control vastly exceeds this limit, but gerrymanders this to claim it merely shifts the dualist bottleneck to conscious access. Crucially, it fails completely to test its dualist theory against the actual physicalist explanation established in the literature: that the 109-to-10 bits/s gap functions as "error-correcting redundancy in the sense of Shannon's channel coding theorem", a mechanism that provides the physical foundation for cognitive reserve without requiring an immaterial soul.   

### 7.4 Failure to Engage Russellian Monism and the Structure and Dynamics Argument (Alter 2021)

The manuscript claims to use a comprehensive "mechanism cost" methodology to evaluate the mind-body problem but entirely fails to test its interactionist dualism against the leading non-reductive physicalist framework of the decade. By ignoring Torin Alter's (2021) formulations of the Structure and Dynamics Argument against materialism, the text evades a framework (Russellian Monism) that posits quiddities to solve the causal exclusion problem without incurring any of the fatal biophysical costs of quantum interactionism. Mapping the costs of dualism without mapping the costs of its primary theoretical rival constitutes a gerrymandered taxonomy, not a rigorous cartography.   

### 7.5 Misattribution of Category Theory Literature (Northoff, Tsuchiya, Saigo 2019/2021)

The manuscript's scholarly apparatus is compromised by elementary attribution failures, notably in its attempts to construct a mathematical formalization of the consciousness interface. It mistakenly credits the critical paper in *Entropy* (21(12):1234) on the category theoretical approach to consciousness to "Chang & Tsuchiya". The actual authors of this widely cited paper, which the manuscript relies upon to push beyond the neural correlates of consciousness, are Georg Northoff, Naotsugu Tsuchiya, and Hayato Saigo. Such gross misattributions in the core mathematical citations undermine the structural integrity of the formalization attempt.   

## 8. Final Verdict

Due to severe misrepresentations of contemporary causal metaphysics, the forced assimilation of speculative quantum biology into structurally agnostic philosophical models, and a systemic failure to test its hypotheses against prevailing physicalist information theory and Russellian monism, this manuscript must be unconditionally rejected.
