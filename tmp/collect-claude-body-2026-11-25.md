# Hostile Referee Audit: “Valence” (unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/), live version of 2026-11-17

Headline verdict: **REVISE-HARD**. The page’s bibliography is accurate, and it is more candid than most of the site about where its central claim is open. But it still reads its own concessions as support for the framework. It leaves out the strongest current rival on the reasons argument (reflexive imperativism). It misreads what pain asymbolia can show. It also contradicts at least two other parts of the site on moral status and on asymbolia.

## TL;DR

- **Verdict: REVISE-HARD, with three sub-theses demoted to coherence-only.** The page now concedes that the hedonic-versus-representational choice is orthogonal to dualism and that asymbolia does not separate the rival accounts. Its “Relation to Site Perspective” section nonetheless opens by saying valence “supports the Map’s framework at multiple points.” That is coherence inflation sitting directly on top of the page’s own retractions.
- **Citations: metadata strong, stance and currency weak.** Every verbatim quotation I could reach matches its source: Russell & Barrett p. 806, Bain 2014, Martínez 2023, Barlassina & Hayward 2019, Joffily & Coricelli 2013 and Jacobson 2013 (abstract only). But one reference is an orphan and out of date (Smithies, cited as “forthcoming”, is now published and is never cited in the text). Rawlette 2016 has no publisher and is self-published. The page builds its predictive-processing rival in its own words rather than its sources’. An apex page reachable from this one still says asymbolia is “decisive against” evaluativism, which inverts Bain’s stated position.
- **Counterarguments and integration: the active-inference gate is only partly passed.** Joffily & Coricelli and Hesp et al. are engaged. Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria (2025, Neurosci. Biobehav. Rev. 176:106296), Kiverstein/Miller/Rietveld error dynamics (Synthese 2019), Van de Cruys and Seth are absent. The rival is reduced to a residual “why is it felt?” step, which is the very step the Beautiful Loop theory claims to supply. Reflexive imperativism, Klein’s care-lack reading of asymbolia, Berridge’s unconscious “liking”, Cacioppo & Berntson’s (1994) bivariate model and attitudinal theories are ignored. On moral status the page contradicts the site’s own register entry P-VS4.

## Part 2 — Dimensional analysis

### 2.1 Claims: overstated, under-argued, or over-confident

**(C1) Lead framing: “The Map argues that valence is an intrinsic phenomenal property.”** The body later says the dispute is “open”, that asymbolia “does not favour the hedonic account”, and that the Map’s dualism “would survive the hedonic account’s defeat”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/) The lead should say what the body earns: the Map *favours* the hedonic account on one consideration (reasons) that is itself contested. Rung: **compatible**. The page does not label it. This fails the constrain-vs-establish gate.

**(C2) “This generality is what makes valence fundamental … a common evaluative dimension … enabling comparison between otherwise incommensurable states.”** [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/) This is the strongest claim on the page and the least argued.

- It assumes valence is one bipolar scalar. That is exactly the claim the linked valence-as-a-natural-kind page says is where “the Map’s exposure” falls.
- It ignores Cacioppo & Berntson’s evaluative space model (1994, Psychological Bulletin 115(3):401–423), on which, in Norman et al.’s summary, the affect system “is not constrained to a bipolar processing continuum”: positivity and negativity are separable and can be co-active (mixed feelings).
- It ignores Labukt’s pluralism, which the page itself cites in the next paragraph.
- The burn/betrayal contrast assumes what is in dispute: that the two “share negative valence while differing in every other phenomenal respect”. Pluralists about hedonic tone deny exactly that.

This is a premise standing in for an argument. Rung: **compatible**, presented as established.

**(C3) “In human experience, as core affect theory describes it, some hedonic state is always present.”** The quotation is accurate. Russell & Barrett (1999, p. 806) say a person is always in some state of core affect, “even if neutral”. [frontiersin + 2](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1738304/full) But Russell & Barrett define core affect as consciously *accessible*, not always conscious. [lu](https://lup.lub.lu.se/luur/download) They also say that when mild it “can fade into the background of consciousness” (verified against the original by the delegated check). [weebly](http://emotiondevelopmentlab.weebly.com/uploads/2/5/2/0/25200250/russell_j.a.__barrett_l._f._1999.pdf) The page turns a claim about a neurophysiological state that is constantly present but only accessible into a claim about phenomenal presence. That is a small but real epistemic-to-metaphysical slide. An earlier index snapshot of the page read “Every conscious experience carries some degree of valence”, [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/) so the current wording is a partial correction, but the slide remains.

**(C4) Panksepp (1998) “suggesting valence is a foundational feature of conscious experience rather than a cognitive overlay.”** [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/) This is a fair summary of Panksepp. It is presented without any opponent, even though LeDoux (“Rethinking the Emotional Brain”, Neuron 73(4):653–676, 2012) is the standing rival: survival circuits, he writes, are not posited to have “any direct relation (causal role) in feelings”. The Map’s own minds-without-words apex discusses the Panksepp-LeDoux dispute. One side of a live dispute is reported as a finding. This is calibration asymmetry.

**(C5) The reasons argument (“The hedonic account’s advantage lies with reasons”).** This is the page’s best philosophical paragraph, and it correctly says the dispute is open. But it is under-argued in a way that matters for the verdict. It claims the hedonic account delivers the reason “with no further machinery”. Yet Barlassina & Hayward’s reflexive imperativism, which the page cites, was built specifically to deliver experience-directed reasons: an unpleasant experience commands “Get less of me!”. [philarchive + 2](https://philarchive.org/rec/BARMOM-7) Taking the painkiller satisfies that command. So the “advantage” is not unique to the hedonic account, and the page does not engage this. See §2.3.

**(C6) Valence and the hard problem: “particularly stark … the property seems so simple”.** Apparent simplicity is not evidence of irreducibility; temperature also seems simple. The linked valence-void is described as “a structurally irresolvable cognitive limit”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/) That is an unhedged modal claim about the future of inquiry. Rung: **compatible**. It should be marked as a framework reading.

**(C7) Asymbolia and causal work: “When the felt badness goes, normal avoidance goes with it.”** The page then hedges correctly (“a dissociation whose causal reading is contested”). But it never names the most important confound. On Klein’s (2015) reading, asymbolics have lost a general capacity to care about their bodily integrity. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275229078_What_Pain_Asymbolia_Really_Shows) Bain (2014) accepts this. [philarchive + 2](https://philarchive.org/rec/BAIPTD-2) The care-lack reading predicts the broad indifference to visual and verbal threats that the clinical literature records, and those threats involve no pain at all. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/BAIPTD-2)[colinklein](https://colinklein.org/papers/DeVForWeb.pdf) A common cause (lost bodily care) therefore explains both the missing unpleasantness and the missing avoidance. The page mentions “the lesion’s common-cause reading” only in passing and never states the datum that makes it compelling. The live classificatory dispute (Griffith & Kind 2024 against, Duval & Klein 2025 for “still pain”) also goes unmentioned, although concepts/pain-asymbolia carries it. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/pain-asymbolia/)[unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/pain-consciousness-and-causal-power/)

**(C8) The predictive-processing rival.** The page should be credited for conceding that the active-inference reading “predicts exactly this dissociation” and that “the dissociation no longer separates the views”. But the rival as stated is the Map’s own construction: “a precision-weighted signal of expected value: a prior over the nociceptive stream that drives avoidance-policy selection”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/) Neither Joffily & Coricelli nor Hesp et al. models nociception or asymbolia. [usc](https://dornsife.usc.edu/coricelli/) The page then rests its preference on the rival’s “undelivered step”: why the signal is *felt*. That is the generic hard problem, not something specific to valence. It confers no evidential advantage specific to valence (an evidential-independence failure). Rung: **compatible**. The page says as much (“neither side refutes the other”), and then nonetheless “takes the phenomenal horn”.

**(C9) Moral status: “valence provides the necessary condition: without felt badness, there is nothing for moral concern to be about.”** This is stated categorically. It conflicts with the site’s register entry P-VS4 (intrinsic value is plural, not hedonic valence alone). [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/reviews/optimistic-2026-08-17-ethics-value-spine/) It also conflicts with the page’s own first section, which “leaves open” whether valence-free consciousness (the philosophical Vulcan) is possible. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/) If Vulcans are possible, this sentence denies them moral status, taking Smithies’ Hedonic Sentientism side against Chalmers. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/SMIACA-16) Smithies is in the reference list but never cited at this point. See §2.4.

**(C10) “Trillions of invertebrates in agricultural systems may possess the very capacity …”** The page gives no source for the figure. The nearest source, Rethink Priorities (Rowe 2020), estimates “1 trillion to 1.2 trillion” insects farmed each year for food and feed. A plural “trillions” matches only projections, such as McKay & Shah’s (Rethink Priorities 2025) estimate of 4.8 trillion slaughtered in 2033 (90% CI 1.6–17.8 trillion). The “may” protects the modal claim but not the number.

**(C11) Relation to Site Perspective: “Valence supports the Map’s framework at multiple points.”** [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/) This is coherence inflation. The page itself says the account choice is orthogonal to dualism, that asymbolia leaves the phenomenal-concept epiphenomenalist standing, and that the selection role is held at low credence with “no independent evidence”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/) “Supports” should become “is consistent with” in every instance.

**What the page gets right (and it bears on the verdict):**

- It makes the account choice orthogonal to Tenet 1, using Aydede’s split between reductive and non-reductive representationalism.
- It concedes that asymbolia does not discriminate between the hedonic and representational accounts.
- It concedes the dissociation to the active-inference reading.
- It applies Tenet 5 symmetrically.
- It labels P-VS1 low credence, framework-internal.

These concessions are why the verdict is REVISE-HARD rather than DELETE.

### 2.3 Counterarguments: strongest objection per family

**Functionalist / affective-science (Berridge & Kringelbach, Barrett, LeDoux, common-currency neuroeconomics).**
Strongest objection: hedonic “liking” reactions can be *unconscious* and still drive behaviour, and “wanting” separates from “liking”. So the motivational role and hedonic impact of valence come apart from phenomenality in both directions. This undercuts both the page’s claim that valence is constitutively phenomenal and its use of loss of avoidance as a probe of felt badness. Common-currency neuroeconomics already models “comparison between incommensurable states” as a neural valuation function, which pre-empts the page’s argument from fundamentality. Barrett’s constructionism treats valence as interoceptive prediction, which is a direct rival, not just a natural-kind skeptic. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_constructed_emotion)
Status: **ignored.** The site has a wanting-liking page, but the valence page does not link it.

**Representationalist (Tye, Cutter & Tye, Dretske, Byrne).**
Strongest objection: transparency. Introspection presents badness as a property of the body region or the world, not of the experience. Tracking explains why felt badness co-varies with harm.
Status: **answered, but only partly.** The reasons argument and objectless moods are the right replies. The page states that a reply exists (Cutter & Tye 2014) but never says what it is: roughly, that it is rational to kill the messenger because the experience is itself a further harm. Aydede’s transparency-based case *against* representationalism, which would help the page, goes unused. [philpeople](https://philpeople.org/profiles/murat-aydede/publications)

**Evaluativist (Bain, Helm, Carruthers; critics Jacobson, Aydede & Fulkerson).**
Strongest objection: Bain argues that asymbolia is best explained by evaluativism, because only evaluative content dependent on bodily care explains both the indifference to pain and the indifference to threat. [philarchive + 2](https://philarchive.org/rec/BAIPTD-2) Bain’s “Why take painkillers?” also answers the messenger objection directly. [gla](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/137527/7/137527.pdf)
Status: **answered weakly.** The page neutralises asymbolia (“both predict”), which is fair, but it does not grant that one rival claims the explanatory advantage on the very case. Aydede & Fulkerson’s “representationalists’ headache” critique, which favours the page, is not used either.

**Imperativist (Klein 2015 book, Martínez, Barlassina & Hayward; Barlassina 2020; Martínez & Barlassina 2026).**
Strongest objection: reflexive imperativism makes unpleasantness a command directed at the experience itself. [lucabarlassina](https://www.lucabarlassina.org/uploads/7/7/1/0/77107295/barlassina_and_hayward_loopy.pdf) That gives exactly the rationality of the painkiller and of the experience-directed reason the page claims for the hedonic account. A further argument goes further still: Martínez & Barlassina’s metasemantic argument (BJPS 77(3):847–865, 2026; DOI 10.1086/726998) holds that valence’s informational profile favours imperative over evaluative content, because (un)pleasant states “are much more informative about behaviour than they are about the world”.
Status: **ignored on the decisive point.** The page cites Barlassina & Hayward only for taxonomy. This is the single largest philosophical gap on the page.

**Attitudinal / desire / adverbial (Heathwood, Brady, Aydede’s felt-evaluation adverbialism).**
Strongest objection: on attitudinal views, pleasantness is an experience being intrinsically wanted or disliked. That handles heterogeneity (C2) without any hedonic tone and without dualist commitments.
Status: **ignored.** The “three competing accounts” framing is a false trichotomy.

**Heterogeneity and measurement (Labukt, Bramble; Cacioppo’s evaluative space model).**
Strongest objection: if valence is plural, or bivariate rather than bipolar, the “single common dimension” claim (C2) and the selection-currency role both fail as stated.
Status: **handed off** to valence-as-a-natural-kind. The page’s own opening definition still assumes bipolarity, so the handoff does not cure the page.

**Pain asymbolia (Grahek; Klein 2015; Griffith & Kind 2024; Duval & Klein 2025; Gerrans 2020).**
Strongest objection: the care-lack common cause (C7), together with the live dispute over whether asymbolia is pain at all.
Status: **answered weakly.** Both are on concepts/pain-asymbolia but are not carried through to the valence page’s causal paragraph. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/pain-asymbolia/)

**Predictive processing / active inference (the blocking gate).**

- **Present:** Joffily & Coricelli 2013 and Hesp et al. 2021, accurately glossed.
- **Absent:**

- Kiverstein, Miller & Rietveld (2019, *Synthese* 196(7):2847–2869; DOI 10.1007/s11229-017-1583-9). Their abstract says valenced experiences are “hypothesised to be identical” with changes in the rate of prediction-error reduction. Experiences are “negatively valenced when overall prediction error increases”, and they read the feeling as the agent’s grip on its environment.
- Van de Cruys’s affective-value account.

## Part 5 — Site-wide methodology improvements

1. **Make the evidence ladder mandatory at the sentence level for “Relation to Site Perspective” sections.** Every tenet paragraph should carry a rung (compatible / suggestive / discriminating). The word “supports” should be banned unless the rung is suggestive or higher with independent evidence. This valence page shows that honest body text does not stop inflation in the closing section.
2. **Convert confession into binding status changes.** When a page withdraws a claim (as valence did with “asymbolia shows causal work”), the pipeline should automatically list every inbound page, apex and review that asserts the withdrawn claim, and block publication until each is fixed or annotated. Minds-without-words and the March optimistic review show that confession without correction persists across pages even when the source page is fixed.
3. **Run stance checks on both directions of use.** The site’s citation failures now concentrate on stance, not metadata. Add a field to each reference: “author’s own conclusion on the point cited”, for example “Bain: asymbolia favours evaluativism”. Check every page that cites the author against that field. This would have caught the inversion of Bain on the apex.
4. **Detect orphan and stale references.** Automatically flag reference-list entries with no in-text citation (Smithies here) and any “forthcoming” entry older than 12 months for a status check.
5. **Run a literature-currency sweep for rival families.** For each concept page, keep a list of rival families (functionalist, representationalist, evaluativist, imperativist, attitudinal, active-inference) with the most recent major paper in each. Require engagement with the newest entry. For valence that means Martínez & Barlassina 2026 and Laukkonen et al. 2025.
6. **Ban self-constructed rivals attributed to sources.** When the Map paraphrases a rival into a stronger or different form, label it “the Map’s construction of the rival”. Only views the sources actually state should be attributed to them.
7. **Make the blocking gates enforceable.** Make the active-inference gate (Laukkonen et al. 2025 plus error-dynamics accounts) a hard pre-publication check for pages on valence, learning, selfhood and altered states, as the cross-modal apex history shows it is still being skipped (“no beautiful-loop engagement added”). [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)
8. **Make cross-page consistency checks on register positions automatic.** When a concept page asserts a categorical claim that a register position (here P-VS4) contradicts, the build should fail or display a conflict banner.
9. **Treat index lag as an audit hazard.** Publish a machine-readable “last-modified by slug” feed, and keep dependency pages fetchable by slug for external auditors. This audit could not fetch the tenets page or six dependencies directly, and had to rely on index renderings for key integration findings.
10. **Address circular review (pipeline-specific; permitted here only).** The page header credits six successive model versions from one model family, [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/) and the site’s internal deep, optimistic and pessimistic reviews come from the same family. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/) The March optimistic review’s praise for a since-withdrawn asymbolia argument shows how same-family review can reward the framing it should catch. Recommendations:

- Require at least one non-same-family adversarial pass before any page moves from “revised” to “reviewed”.
- Weight external outer reviews above internal optimistic reviews when they conflict.
- Record which model family produced each review, so that repeated agreement between reviews from the same family is not counted as independent confirmation.
11. **Separate changelog categories.** Distinguish metadata repair, stance repair, propagation sweep, and substantive retreat of a thesis. Only the last should count toward a page’s “stabilised” status.
12. **Limit “framework-internal” positions.** Positions held at low credence with no independent evidence (like P-VS1) for more than a fixed number of review cycles should be automatically flagged DEMOTE-TO-COHERENCE-ONLY on every page that cites them, not only in the register.
