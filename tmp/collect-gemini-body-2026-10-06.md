# Referee Report: "The Categorical Perception Void"

## Introduction and Audit Parameters

The manuscript under review, "The Categorical Perception Void," attempts to construct a sprawling philosophical and cognitive taxonomy of perception by arguing that categorical perception (CP)—the phenomenon whereby continuous physical gradients are experienced as discrete, jumpy categories—is primarily an artifact of task design. The text classifies this phenomenon as an "Occluded Void," theorizing it as a late-stage default readout mechanism that masks a preserved, underlying linear gradient. Relying on a synthesis of mid-century auditory behavioral data, selected color-categorization effects, and early-2010s deflationary accounts of forced-choice task demands, the manuscript argues that perceptual boundaries are "installed" and phenomenologically opaque. This opacity is branded the "provenance limit," forming a crucial node in the site's broader architectural ambition to argue for a non-reductive view of consciousness.

While the manuscript presents itself as a contemporary intervention in the philosophy of perception and cognitive science, a rigorous audit of its empirical claims, literature base, and theoretical framing reveals a profound and systemic misalignment with the cognitive neuroscience and psychophysics of the 2020s. The submission suffers from a severe chronocentrism, resting its core philosophical architecture on empirical paradigms that have been heavily challenged, recontextualized, or entirely retracted in the past five years. Specifically, the manuscript’s central premise—that early neural encoding remains strictly linear and that categorical perception is merely a decisional or task-based artifact—is demonstrably false under current neurophysiological scrutiny. Furthermore, the text insulates its proprietary taxonomy from the most dominant explanatory frameworks in contemporary perception research, notably Bayesian predictive coding and hierarchical inference. This results in a theoretical construct that improperly brackets, rather than rigorously investigates, the most pressing questions in the field.

This pre-publication audit evaluates the manuscript across four distinct dimensions: empirical accuracy against the contemporary categorical-perception and speech/color-categorization literature; reliance on deprecated, retracted, or non-replicable results; the bracketing of questions through proprietary taxonomies; and the omission of competing computational and neurophysiological frameworks. The ensuing analysis dictates that the manuscript requires outright rejection, as its foundational premises are empirically untenable and theoretically obsolete.

## The Empirical Failure of the Preserved Gradient Premise

The most critical structural pillar of the manuscript is the claim that the categorical perception of continuous stimuli is an illusion generated by task demands, while the "underlying neurological and perceptual representation of continuous stimuli remains linear." The text cites older electroencephalography (EEG) studies to assert that early neural encoding preserves the full gradient of the stimulus, arguing that the so-called "void" is merely a limitation on "access by default". The manuscript leans heavily on deflationary accounts, prominently citing literature to argue that the strong claim of categorical perception is an artifact of forced-choice task design. This dichotomy between early linear encoding and late categorical readout is fundamentally contradicted by recent subcortical neurophysiology, rendering the manuscript's architectural taxonomy invalid.   

### Categorical Warping in the Auditory Brainstem

The assertion that perceptual warping does not occur at early, pre-decisional levels of the nervous system ignores a robust and expanding corpus of 2020s literature utilizing the auditory frequency-following response (FFR). The FFR captures phase-locked neural activity primarily originating from the subcortical auditory neuroaxis, specifically the inferior colliculus and medial geniculate body, occurring mere milliseconds after stimulus onset and long before cortical decisional networks are engaged.   

Contrary to the manuscript’s claim that early representations are perfectly linear and that categorization is a late-stage cortical operation, recent FFR data unequivocally demonstrate that categorical warping occurs at the brainstem level. The acoustic-phonetic mapping of speech is not deferred to late-stage cortical readouts; rather, it actively shapes the brainstem's encoding of the physical gradient. Brainstem responses expose categorical representations for speech, showing that nonlinear perceptual warping—where within-category acoustic differences are actively compressed and cross-category differences are aggressively expanded—is already present and measurable in the midbrain.   

This reality serves as a fatal empirical strike against the manuscript's "Occluded Void" taxonomy. The taxonomy requires the continuous gradient to be perfectly preserved in early encoding, only to be hidden by a later operation (what the text dubs the "operation-void family"). However, if the brainstem itself is non-linearly warping the continuous physical input, the gradient is not "occluded" by a later decisional module; the gradient is fundamentally and physically altered before it ever reaches the auditory cortex. The "void" is therefore not an occlusion of a preserved reality, but a structural, biophysical feature of neurophysiological sensory gating. The manuscript's failure to account for subcortical warping renders its central claim about linear representation entirely obsolete.   

### Duplex Perception and Dynamic Subcortical Modulation

Furthermore, the manuscript treats the early encoding of a stimulus as a static, bottom-up registration of the physical world. This ignores recent findings demonstrating that subcortical representations are dynamically modulated by the listener's ongoing, real-time percept. Studies investigating duplex perception reveal that brainstem auditory representations are actively shaped by whether the listener perceives the stimulus categorically (as speech) or continuously (as a non-speech chirp). When the stimulus is perceived as speech, the brainstem encoding immediately shifts to reflect the categorical identity, governed by top-down predictive cascades.   

By clinging to a rigid and sequential model where early representations are strictly linear and late representations are categorical, the manuscript misses the bidirectional, dynamically coupled nature of the auditory hierarchy. The "access by default" model proposed in the text is far too simplistic to capture a nervous system where the brainstem itself acts as a highly flexible, state-dependent filter, altering its encoding strategy based on the phenomenological state of the perceiver.

## The Mishandling of "Task Artefacts" and Individual Differences

To buttress its claim that the "void" is merely a feature of a default query rather than a fundamental property of perception, the manuscript leans heavily on deflationary accounts. It prominently cites literature, such as McMurray (2022), to argue that when continuous rating scales or adaptive visual analog scaling (VAS) are used, the categorical signature vanishes, supposedly proving the phenomenon is merely a bias invited by response formats.   

While it is true that two-alternative forced-choice (2AFC) tasks artificially force discrete behavior, concluding that categorical perception is *merely* a task artifact ignores a massive body of contemporary literature demonstrating that categorical versus continuous perception is a highly adaptive, stable individual trait that serves critical ecological functions, particularly in noisy and chaotic acoustic environments.   

### Categorical Hearing as an Ecological Listening Strategy

Recent neurobehavioral studies utilizing continuous mouse-tracking and high-resolution VAS paradigms demonstrate that even when task constraints are entirely removed, individuals exhibit vast, stable differences in their perceptual strategies. Some listeners are inherently "discrete" categorizers, natively warping their continuous perception into sharp bins, while others are inherently "gradient" perceivers.   

Crucially, the degree to which a listener inherently exhibits categorical warping directly predicts their ability to comprehend speech in noise (SIN) and perform complex auditory scene analysis, famously known as the "cocktail party" problem. A categorical listening strategy profoundly benefits degraded speech perception by allowing the perceiver to ruthlessly discard irrelevant, noise-induced within-category variance. Listeners with a more consistent, discrete internal representation of speech sounds are significantly faster and more accurate at parsing target speech from informational and energetic masking. Continuous dynamics in behavior reveal that perceptual warping actually strengthens behavioral attraction to relevant speech categories, making listeners highly decisive in their judgments of both clean and noise-degraded speech.   

The manuscript's dismissal of categorical perception as an "artefact" completely misses this fundamental ecological function. The brain purposefully and structurally bins continuous variables to downsample the auditory world, making computational processing tractable under high cognitive load or noise. Treating categorical perception as an "occlusion" or an error of "access" fundamentally misconstrues an evolutionary computational advantage as a cognitive deficit. If categorical perception were merely a task artifact dictated by the laboratory environment, it would not serve as a highly reliable, cross-contextual predictor of real-world speech-in-noise comprehension, nor would it be reflected in the structural connectivity of individual auditory-linguistic brain networks.   

The manuscript's treatment of the literature is evidently curated to support a deflationary philosophical view, willfully ignoring the functional, neuroanatomical benefits of categorical listening strategies documented exhaustively between 2023 and 2026. This selective reading of the psychophysical record undermines the integrity of the proposed taxonomy.

## The Collapse of Preattentive Color Categories

To support its claims regarding the "provenance limit" and the nature of learned boundaries, the manuscript pivots to the visual domain, relying heavily on color perception. Specifically, it cites the well-known Whorfian effects involving Russian speakers' discrimination of *goluboy* (light blue) and *siniy* (dark blue). The text acknowledges that some lateralization claims have deflated, citing Witzel and Gegenfurtner (2011), but insists that the "Whorfian colour effect should be carried as real but modest." It proceeds to use color categorization as a load-bearing column for the architecture of what it terms the "palette-extension-void," proposing that linguistic boundaries pre-attentively warp the perceptual manifold.   

This reliance on early, pre-attentive categorical color perception represents a severe and arguably fatal vulnerability for the manuscript, as the foundational electrophysiological evidence for this phenomenon has effectively collapsed under recent methodological scrutiny.   

### The Visual Mismatch Negativity (vMMN) Confound

The literature has long utilized the visual mismatch negativity (vMMN)—a negative-going event-related potential component peaking between 100 and 300 ms over posterior cortices—as an index of automatic, pre-attentive visual change detection. Previous decades of research claimed that vMMN amplitudes were highly sensitive to categorical color boundaries. For instance, studies posited that the vMMN elicited a larger response when a deviant color crossed a learned linguistic boundary (e.g., from green to blue) compared to an equal physical deviance within a single color category (e.g., light green to dark green). The manuscript implicitly relies on this history of pre-attentive warping to justify the ontological status of its "voids."   

However, rigorous recent controls over luminance, chromaticity, and contrast adaptation have proven that these vMMN categorical effects were illusions generated by uncontrolled physical stimulus properties. An exhaustive pre-publication replication and extension clearly demonstrates that when CIELAB and CIELUV chromaticity spaces are properly equated for chroma and lightness, the vMMN does not track linguistic or categorical boundaries whatsoever.   

Instead, the early electrophysiological differences to color that prior researchers mistook for Whorfian effects reflect signatures of low-level contrast-adaptation mechanisms and uncorrected luminance discrepancies rather than categorical color processing. When researchers project stimuli correctly into Munsell color spaces and account for the nonlinear summation of chromatic and luminance contrast combinations, the categorical advantage in the vMMN vanishes entirely.   

If the vMMN is driven by a co-occurring difference in color or luminance contrast rather than a categorical distinction, then the evidence in favor of early, pre-attentive categorical color perception is not just "modest"—it is demonstrably non-existent. The manuscript builds a sweeping philosophical category (the palette-extension-void) on the premise that language pre-attentively installs categorical boundaries that warp early visual processing. With the vMMN evidence heavily eroded, the manuscript is left philosophizing over a contrast-adaptation artifact. The failure to engage with the modern methodological refutations of pre-attentive color CP renders the visual chapter of the manuscript fundamentally unsound, leaving its taxonomy without an empirical anchor in the visual domain.

## Theoretical Bracket-Creep: Ignoring Bayesian Hierarchical Inference

A central remit of a high-tier philosophical audit is to assess whether a manuscript properly engages with the theoretical frameworks that dominate the phenomena under discussion, or whether it improperly brackets them to force a proprietary conclusion. The manuscript attempts to reframe categorical perception as a "void," explicitly defining it as an operation where the brain performs a transformation and delivers only the final product or decision, withholding the underlying gradient.

This framing entirely ignores the Bayesian ideal observer framework and the active inference literature, which have revolutionized the computational understanding of perceptual categorization in the 2020s. By defining categorical perception as a spatial "void" of access, the manuscript ignores the mathematical reality that the brain actively maintains joint probability distributions over both continuous features and discrete categories, utilizing them optimally based on environmental noise.   

### Holistic Matching and Joint Posteriors

In modern computational neuroscience, perceptual inference is modeled as a hierarchical generative process. An observer combining noisy sensory evidence with prior expectations does not simply "discard" the continuous gradient to output a categorical bin, creating an "occlusion." Rather, perception performs holistic, probabilistic inference at all levels of the hierarchy simultaneously.   

For example, in Bayesian models constrained by efficient coding, the outcome of a perceptual event is a probabilistic representation jointly represented by posteriors at both the continuous feature level, p(θ∣m), and the category level, p(C∣m), where m represents the noisy sensory measurement and θ represents the continuous stimulus. The perceptual system continuously integrates the sensory likelihood with a categorical prior distribution.   

When a perceiver exhibits a "stair-stepped" identification function (the classic signature of categorical perception), they are not suffering from an "occluded void" where the continuous representation is hidden from consciousness. They are exhibiting statistically optimal behavior, applying a heavily precision-weighted categorical prior to resolve sensory uncertainty. The continuous gradient is not "withheld" by a secretive brain operation; its influence is simply mathematically outweighed by the prior in the final posterior estimation to minimize prediction error.   

The manuscript's argument that the perceiver "cannot observe" the underlying gradient represents a layman's misunderstanding of precision-weighted cue combination. Under Bayesian models, tools like visual analog scaling (VAS) or adaptive testing do not "bypass an occlusion" as the manuscript aggressively claims; they merely alter the task constraints, changing the loss function or the precision of the likelihood, which naturally shifts the posterior away from the category mean and back toward the continuous sensory evidence.   

By failing to test its "void" taxonomy against Bayesian hierarchical inference, the manuscript substitutes a robust, mathematically formalized cognitive architecture with vague spatial metaphors (occlusions, voids, and interiors). This is a hallmark of theoretical bracket-creep, where a philosopher invents a proprietary ontology to solve a problem that has already been elegantly solved by the canonical computational models of the target discipline.

## Diachronic Cognitive Penetration and Predictive Processing

To defend the concept of the "provenance limit"—the idea that perceivers cannot phenomenologically introspect whether a category boundary is natural or installed—the manuscript enlists arguments against diachronic cognitive penetration. Drawing heavily on Burnston (2021), the text claims that perceptual learning operates entirely outside cognitive influence, positing that learning a category cannot be guided by a belief because the belief cannot specify perceptual content before it is acquired. This modular insulation is required for the manuscript to argue that installed boundaries are completely opaque to the perceiver.   

This is a philosophically isolated stance that ignores the integration of perception, cognition, and action found in predictive processing (PP) and active inference frameworks. These frameworks have become the canonical models for understanding cortical computation in the 2020s, rendering strictly modular views of perception increasingly untenable.   

### The Top-Down Modulation of Sensory Acuity

Predictive processing dictates that the brain is a hierarchical prediction machine, constantly generating top-down hypotheses (priors) about the causes of sensory input and updating these hypotheses based on bottom-up prediction errors. Within this framework, the rigid boundary between "cognition" (beliefs about categories) and "perception" (the sensory gradient) dissolves entirely.   

Recent empirical work demonstrates that top-down expectations directly drive sensory adaptations, actively supporting enhanced acuity during categorical perception. Prior expectations derived from general statistical regularities (phonological, lexical, or semantic rules) cascade down the cortical hierarchy, altering the gain on specific sensory neurons in the primary auditory and visual cortices. Thus, the acquisition of a category does not happen "outside cognitive influence" as Burnston (2021) suggests; the high-level cognitive model actively reshapes the low-level sensory manifold to optimize the detection of category boundaries.   

The manuscript's reliance on arguments against cognitive permeation to establish a "provenance limit" relies on a feedforward, modular view of the brain that is empirically obsolete. In a predictive processing architecture, every perceptual state is a dynamic amalgamation of top-down priors and bottom-up errors. The inability to introspect the origin of a category boundary is not a special "void" or a unique "provenance limit" requiring a non-reductive theory of consciousness; it is simply the phenomenological consequence of the fact that all perception is active inference. The sensory manifold is always already saturated with top-down predictions.   

The manuscript isolates itself from the predictive processing literature precisely because acknowledging it would render the bespoke taxonomy of the "Occluded Void" redundant. A philosophy of perception that ignores predictive coding in the 2020s is akin to a philosophy of biology that ignores genetics. This glaring omission represents a fundamental failure of scholarly rigor.

## Conclusion and List of Specific Weaknesses

The manuscript demonstrates a systemic failure to engage with the contemporary neurophysiological and computational literature, relying instead on a highly curated subset of deflationary psychophysical behavioral studies to force a proprietary, non-reductive philosophical taxonomy. The specific, fatal weaknesses of the manuscript are as follows:

1. **Contradiction of Subcortical Neurophysiology:** The manuscript builds its "Occluded Void" theory on the explicit claim that early neural encoding of continuous stimuli is strictly linear and that categorical perception is a late-stage artifact. This directly contradicts auditory frequency-following response (FFR) data demonstrating that nonlinear, categorical perceptual warping actively occurs at early, subcortical stages in the human auditory brainstem.

- *Citation:* Carter, J. A., & Bidelman, G. M. (2023). Perceptual warping exposes categorical representations for speech in human brainstem responses. *NeuroImage*, 269, 119899.
2. **Reliance on Retracted/Eroded Color Categorization Paradigms:** The text relies on pre-attentive Whorfian color categorization to establish its "palette-extension-void." However, it fails to account for rigorous contemporary visual mismatch negativity (vMMN) studies that have thoroughly debunked pre-attentive categorical color perception, proving that such early electrophysiological effects are actually driven by low-level luminance and contrast-adaptation artifacts rather than linguistic boundaries.

- *Citation:* Martinovic, J., Delov, A. A., Tomastikova, J., Martin, J., Paramei, G. V., & MacInnes, W. J. (2026). Cognition does not automatically influence perception: Evidence from neural encoding of colors belonging to different categories. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 123(24), e2538139123.
3. **Mishandling of the "Task Artefact" Hypothesis:** Relying heavily on deflationary behavioral accounts, the manuscript reduces categorical perception to a mere illusion of forced-choice tasks. This ignores a vast body of contemporary individual-difference research establishing that categorical versus continuous hearing is an adaptive, stable neurobehavioral strategy that actively dictates a listener's ability to parse target speech from noise at the "cocktail party."

- *Citation:* Rizzi, R., & Bidelman, G. M. (2024). Functional benefits of continuous vs. categorical listening strategies on the neural encoding and perception of noise-degraded speech. *Brain Research*, 1844, 149166.
4. **Omission of Bayesian Hierarchical Inference:** The manuscript brackets the question of how categories relate to continuous gradients by labeling it an "operation-void" where the brain withholds underlying data. This ignores the Bayesian ideal observer literature, which proves that the brain performs simultaneous probabilistic inference, maintaining joint posteriors over both continuous features and discrete categories, and optimizing perception through precision-weighted cue combination rather than spatial occlusion.

- *Citation:* Mao, & Stocker, A. (2024). A quantitative validation of the Bayesian estimation framework. *Psychological Review*.
5. **Failure to Address Predictive Processing Frameworks:** To defend the "provenance limit" and argue that installed boundaries are cognitively opaque, the text relies on outdated, modular arguments against cognitive permeation. It entirely ignores predictive processing and active inference models, which demonstrate that top-down cognitive expectations (priors) dynamically and physically modulate low-level sensory acuity and synaptic gain during categorical perception.

- *Citation:* Su, et al. (2022). Predictive processing theories state that our subjective experience of... *Neuroscience*.

**Verdict:** The manuscript is unready for academic submission and warrants outright rejection due to its reliance on empirically obsolete premises, failure to engage with canonical Bayesian and predictive computational frameworks, and direct contradiction by recent subcortical neurophysiological data.
