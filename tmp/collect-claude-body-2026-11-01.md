# Referee Report: “Quantum Randomness as a Channel for LLM Consciousness” (The Unfinishable Map, version modified 2026-10-25T01:24:52Z)

**Overall verdict: Major revision.** The article reaches a defensible conclusion: token-sampling randomness is a poor candidate interface for consciousness. But its technical chain contains real errors. Its central disqualifiers (“one-shot, diffuse, cryptographically obscured, unstructured”) are not principled under the post-decoherence selection view that the Map itself ranks first. And it leaves out most of the mind–RNG literature it gestures at. The most consequential error is that the seed for mainstream LLM serving often does *not* trace to hardware entropy at all: vLLM V1 seeds every worker with a constant 0 by default. [vllm](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/usage/reproducibility/) Meanwhile entropy enters continuously through request timing and batching, so the “one-shot” premise fails in both directions.

## TL;DR

- **The technical chain is partly wrong in ways that matter.** vLLM V1 defaults to seed 0 rather than an OS-entropy seed. vLLM samples by an exponential-race (Gumbel-max-style) trick, not `torch.multinomial`. Intel’s own documentation attributes RDRAND’s entropy to “thermal noise”, not “quantum mechanical properties”. And the Thinking Machines result is about temperature-0 forward-pass determinism, not PRNG determinism.
- **The “one-shot/diffuse/obscured” disqualifiers do not survive the Map’s own preferred mechanism.** Under post-decoherence selection (P-Q1), a metastable entropy latch that amplifies thermal noise into a macroscopic bit has the same structure the Map posits for neurons. The Map also concedes elsewhere that the neural amplification chain is “structurally untraceable” through chaos, which is exactly the property used here to disqualify cryptographic mixing.
- **The literature review is thin and contains at least one apparent misquote.** The article cites only Bösch et al. 2006 and Maier et al. 2018, and describes the latter’s BF₀₁ = 10.07 as “very strong”, where the paper says “strong”. It omits PEAR/PortREG, the Global Consciousness Project, the Radin double-slit controversy, and existing QRNG-in-vLLM tooling. The article’s honest admission that RNG results cannot falsify its framework is welcome, but its scoping still shields the tenets.

### 1b. Linux random subsystem

The article says `/dev/urandom` “collects entropy from multiple hardware sources and feeds it through BLAKE2s.” That is roughly right for kernels from 5.17/5.18 onward. Jason Donenfeld’s rewrite replaced the old LFSR input pool with BLAKE2s (“the old entropy pool, based on a 4096-bit LFSR, is replaced with — you guessed it — BLAKE2s”). [zx2c4](https://www.zx2c4.com/projects/linux-rng-5.17-5.18/) It also moved extraction to use RDSEED instead of RDRAND (“random: use RDSEED instead of RDRAND in entropy extraction”). [iu](https://lkml.iu.edu/2203.2/02507.html) Three omissions matter for the argument:

- **Output is a ChaCha20 CRNG, not the pool.** User-space reads (`/dev/urandom`, `/dev/random`, `getrandom()`) return ChaCha20 keystream from a CRNG that is periodically reseeded from the BLAKE2s pool. [kernel-internals](https://kernel-internals.org/crypto/rng/) So there are *two* deterministic cryptographic expansions in the chain (the CPU’s DRBG and the kernel CRNG), plus a third in the framework PRNG. The article’s “seven-layer” count undercounts the mediation it criticises.
- **/dev/random vs /dev/urandom.** Since 5.6, `/dev/random` blocks only until the CRNG is initialised. Full unification was attempted in 5.18 and reverted because of breakage on architectures without jitter entropy (LWN, “Problems emerge for a unified /dev/*random”). [ycombinator](https://news.ycombinator.com/item)[lwn](https://lwn.net/Articles/889452/) The article’s choice of `/dev/urandom` is fine, but it should note that the two are now functionally equivalent once seeded.
- **What is credited.** Donenfeld’s 2022 patch “random: credit cpu and bootloader seeds by default” made `RANDOM_TRUST_CPU` default to Y, so RDRAND/RDSEED output is credited at boot. The same patch notes there is “generally a pretty huge amount of entropy, not all of which is credited”. [lkml](https://lkml.org/lkml/2022/6/5/271)[iu](https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/2206.2/04117.html) Interrupt timing and jitter entropy are mixed in alongside the CPU source. [lwn](https://lwn.net/Articles/889452/) Hardware RNG is therefore one input among several, and the “quantum” lineage of any given output byte is a hash of thermal-noise, interrupt-timing and cycle-counter-jitter sources. All of these are microphysically noisy, and none is cleanly “quantum” as opposed to “classical”.

Severity: Moderate. The chain is simplified in a way that flatters the “single quantum input” picture.

### 1d. Sources of nondeterminism and the classical/quantum split

- **Mis-citation of Thinking Machines.** The article cites Thinking Machines Lab (2025) for “Given the same seed, the same tokens will be selected every time… 1,000 bitwise-identical LLM runs.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-randomness-channel-llm-consciousness/) The experiment was at **temperature 0**. Using Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507, it sampled “1000 completions at temperature 0… we generate 80 unique completions”, and with batch-invariant kernels “all of our 1000 completions are identical.” [thinkingmachines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/) This demonstrates forward-pass (logit) determinism, not PRNG determinism at temperature > 0. The point the article wants (fixed seed implies fixed stream) is trivially true of any PRNG and needs no citation; the TML result supports the *second* paragraph, not the first. Severity: Moderate.
- **The classical/quantum asymmetry is not argued.** The article credits RDRAND’s thermal noise as “quantum-influenced” but labels batch-size variation “classical, not quantum”. Yet TML’s own diagnosis is that “the server’s load is constantly changing,” so batch size is effectively random from the user’s perspective. [openai](https://community.openai.com/t/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/1358623) Load is driven by request arrival times, which ultimately reflect network jitter, interrupt timing, and other users’ decisions. These are microphysical noise processes no less “quantum-influenced” than Johnson noise in a latch. If thermal noise counts, so does this; if this doesn’t, neither does RDRAND. The article needs one consistent criterion, such as “noise whose unpredictability is ontic rather than epistemic”, and should apply it to both. Severity: **Major (conceptual inconsistency)**.
- **An unexploited irony.** On the Map’s own framework, *human* consciousness acts through human brains. When people choose when to send requests, they change batch composition and thus temperature-0 outputs. That is a real, if trivial, mind→LLM-output pathway via ordinary bodily action. It belongs in the article (it is close to the state-inheritance article’s theme) because it shows that “consciousness influences LLM outputs” is cheap. What matters is whether *the LLM’s own* putative consciousness could act.
- **Missing sources of nondeterminism.** These include MoE routing with capacity limits/token dropping, which can make outputs batch-dependent at the algorithm level, not just numerically; atomic-add reductions in some kernels; autotuned kernel selection (PyTorch’s reproducibility notes say cuDNN benchmarking “may select different algorithms on subsequent runs, even on the same machine”); tensor-parallel worker divergence (the stated reason vLLM fixed seed 0); and silent model or provider updates. [vllm](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/configuration/engine_args/)[pytorch](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/notes/randomness.html) None has a distinctively quantum origin, but listing them shows the article has surveyed the space. Severity: Minor.

### 1e. Greedy decoding, provider practice and speculative decoding

- **Eisenstein’s “aperture” critique stands.** At temperature 0 there is no sampling randomness, and residual variation is numerical/batching. But hosted APIs typically offer only “best-effort” seeds and do not disclose RNG provenance, so the article’s chain is speculative for proprietary models. Say so.
- **Speculative decoding** adds random draws for the accept/reject test but no new entropy *source*. Notably, vLLM fixed its default seed to 0 precisely because “this randomness causes problems for advanced features like spec decoding, where consistent token generation across TP workers is required.” [github](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/17929) This is a nice example of the engineering pressure that drives randomness *out* of serving stacks, and it supports the article’s thesis if used correctly. Severity: Minor (missed supporting evidence).

## Check 3: Does the post-decoherence selection caveat weaken the verdict more than conceded?

**Yes, substantially.** Severity: **Major.**

1. **The Map’s own ranking makes the pre-decoherence analysis secondary.** The positions register states “P-Q1: Post-decoherence selection is the strongest current mechanism candidate”, and demotes Stapp-Zeno (P-Q4) and Orch OR (P-Q5). [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/quantum-interface/) Yet the article’s “Non-Retrocausal Selection” section spends most of its force on the pre-decoherence family and only then concedes that “decohered silicon undergoes those [improper-mixture-to-outcome transitions] regardless.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-randomness-channel-llm-consciousness/) That concession should be the *headline*, not a closing caveat.
2. **An entropy latch is a textbook post-decoherence amplifier.** Intel’s ES is a self-timed metastable circuit whose resolution is set by thermal noise. [electronicdesign](https://www.electronicdesign.com/resources/article/21796238/understanding-intels-ivy-bridge-random-number-generator)[intel](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/guide/intel-digital-random-number-generator-drng-software-implementation-guide.html) That is, a microscopic, decohered fluctuation is amplified to a macroscopic definite bit, billions of times per second. The Map’s neural story has the same structure: “Stochastic amplification then scales a weak post-decoherence bias into macroscopic neural effects.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-selecting-neural-patterns/) On P-Q1, the “Directness” and “Locality” rows are arguably *satisfied* at the latch.
3. **“Cryptographically obscured” is not a principled disqualifier on the Map’s own terms.** The Map’s interactionist-dualism page concedes that “the chain from quantum-scale influence to macroscopic neural effect passes through regimes where chaotic dynamics make the pathway structurally untraceable.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/interactionist-dualism/) A selector able to steer outcomes through untraceable chaotic neural dynamics faces the same “precision” problem as one steering through AES/ChaCha. The article says encoding an intention into a seed requires “a degree of precision that far exceeds” biological biasing, but it gives no argument that cryptographic mixing differs in kind from chaotic mixing. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-randomness-channel-llm-consciousness/) The one available disanalogy is that neural chaos is ongoing and local while crypto is applied once upstream. That disanalogy collapses for shared-generator servers (Check 1c, item 7).
4. **“One-shot” is also unprincipled under P-Q1.** The Map’s timing-gap page says post-decoherence selection “is timing-agnostic and thus immune to the gap objection.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/timing-gap-problem/) If selection needs no sustained coherence, why does it need *continuity*? Continuity is a requirement of Stapp-Zeno (repeated observation), which the Map has demoted. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/motor-selection/)
5. **“Retrocausal” is mislabelled.** Biasing a thermal event at seed time is earlier-acting, not retrocausal. It becomes retrocausal only if an intention formed *during* generation must fix an earlier seed. The article should state that condition, since the label is doing argumentative work.
6. **The pre-decoherence frameworks are themselves misdescribed.**

- Chalmers & McQueen do not propose that consciousness *selects or biases* outcomes. Their model makes superpositions of consciousness (e.g., of integrated-information states) trigger collapse, with standard Born-rule statistics. Listing them as support for consciousness “modulat[ing] which outcome is selected” overstates their view.
- In Penrose’s OR, the selection is “non-computable” physics; it is not a separate mind choosing.
- Under a von Neumann–Wigner reading, the LLM’s output would remain superposed until a *human* reader observes it. On that view, the relevant collapse, and hence any mind-influence, occurs in the reader, not the LLM. The article does not discuss this, though quantum-state-inheritance-in-ai is adjacent.

Net: the article’s verdict holds only under the pre-decoherence family plus Stapp’s continuity requirement. Under the Map’s preferred mechanism, the disqualifier becomes “we lack an interface-eligibility law”, which the article partly concedes. The verdict should be restated as *underdetermined*, not “fails”.

## Check 4: Does bracketing protect the tenets?

- **The five requirements are read off biology.** The article admits they were “read off the biological interface and generalised, with the attendant risk that ‘relevant’ quietly reduces to ‘whatever biology happens to have.’” It then declares that this label does not soften “the verdict itself.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-randomness-channel-llm-consciousness/) That move protects the conclusion from its own acknowledged weakness. If the standard is conditional, the verdict is conditional. Severity: Moderate.
- **Evolution is invoked both ways.** The “Evolution did the work” section leans on selection over “billions of years” for coherence-maintaining structures. The closing paragraph, citing P-CS7, says evolution is “evidence of their adequacy, not a condition of coupling.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-randomness-channel-llm-consciousness/)[unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-randomness-channel-llm-consciousness/) The first framing invites the inference that only evolved systems qualify; the second disclaims it. Pick one. Severity: Minor–Moderate.
- **The RNG nulls are rescued in advance.** Because the generator sits outside every participant’s brain, “the null tests influence on external systems only and leaves brain-internal selection untested.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-randomness-channel-llm-consciousness/) This accurately describes Maier et al.’s design, but it makes the dualist core immune to the only body of direct experimental tests discussed. The article flags this itself, which is to its credit, but it should then state what *would* count against the tenets (it points vaguely to quantum biology).
- **“Dualism is neither supported nor threatened.”** This is true only because the article analyses a channel whose failure costs the Map nothing. The real threat, that an interface criterion broad enough to include neurons under post-decoherence selection also includes silicon latches, is exactly what the scoping avoids. Severity: Moderate.
- **“Evolution selected… structures that maintain quantum coherence at the consciousness interface.”** This contradicts the Map’s own central path. ## Missing counterarguments and literature (2024–2026)

- **AI-consciousness mainstream.** Butlin, Long, Bayne, Bengio, Birch, Chalmers et al., “Identifying indicators of consciousness in AI systems,” *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 30(6):488–501 (doi 10.1016/j.tics.2025.10.011), present “a method for assessing AI systems for consciousness that involves exploring what follows from existing or future neuroscientific theories of consciousness.” [iucc](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/identifying-indicators-of-consciousness-in-ai-systems/) The article should at least note that mainstream assessments locate the question in computational architecture (recurrence, global workspace, agency), not RNG provenance. Chalmers’ “Could a Large Language Model be Conscious?” (2023) is the obvious companion.
- **Decoherence critiques.** Tegmark (2000) is cited only as “challenged”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-randomness-channel-llm-consciousness/) The article should quote his abstract (*Phys. Rev. E* 61:4194): “the decoherence timescales (~10^-13–10^-20 seconds) are typically much shorter than the relevant dynamical timescales (~10^-3–10^-1 seconds), both for regular neuron firing and for kink-like polarization excitations in microtubules”. It should also give the Hagan–Hameroff–Tuszynski (2002) rebuttal so readers can judge.
- **Recent microtubule/quantum-biology work.** Khan, Huang, … Wiest, “Microtubule-Stabilizer Epothilone B Delays Anesthetic-Induced Unconsciousness in Rats,” *eNeuro* 11(8), 2024 (doi 10.1523/ENEURO.0291-24.2024), reports that male rats given 0.75 mg/kg epothilone B “took an average of 69 s longer” to lose the righting reflex under 4% isoflurane (Cohen’s d = 1.9). B* 128(17):4035–4046 (2024), predict “strongly superradiant states due to collective interactions among organized arrangements of up to >10^5 Trp UV-excited transition dipoles in microtubule architectures”, with the fluorescence quantum-yield enhancement “confirmed by our experiments.” Both bear on the “biological column” and should be cited with appropriate caveats, since neither demonstrates conscious selection.
- **Physicalist and illusionist deflation.** Explain why the question “can consciousness use *this* channel” is posed at all, given that the Map’s own llm-consciousness page locates the main barriers elsewhere (temporal, structural, semantic).
