# Pre-Publication Review Report: "Attention as Causal Bridge"

## Introduction and Metaphysical Framing

The manuscript under review, titled "Attention as Causal Bridge," presents an ambitious defense of interactionist dualism, proposing that attention functions as the physical interface through which a non-physical consciousness exerts causal influence over the brain. The author attempts to synthesize findings from neurobiology, cognitive psychology, clinical dissociations, and quantum mechanics to argue that phenomena such as willed effort, dopaminergic neuromodulatory thresholds, and behavioral bandwidth limits are indicative of a non-reductive, bidirectional causal architecture. The manuscript contends that consciousness acts as an exogenous selector among neurally generated possibilities, resolving the interaction problem by restricting non-physical intervention to a highly constrained informational bandwidth.  

While the manuscript is theoretically expansive, a forensic audit of its empirical claims, literature citations, and logical inferences reveals critical structural and methodological vulnerabilities. The analysis systematically engages in confirmation bias, extrapolating sweeping metaphysical conclusions from standard, physically reducible neurobiological constraints. Furthermore, the manuscript operates within a profound epistemic vacuum, artificially bracketing the most formidable contemporary physicalist frameworks—namely, Predictive Processing (PP), Active Inference, and Illusionism—which elegantly and exhaustively explain the article’s core phenomena without violating the causal closure of the physical domain. The resulting argument relies heavily on question-begging critiques of rival theories, mischaracterization of cited empirical data, and the treatment of highly disputed neuroscientific debates as settled doctrine. This report will systematically deconstruct the manuscript's mishandling of neurobiological data, its evasion of computational neuroscience, and its philosophical incoherence regarding information theory and physicalism.  

## Part I: The Predictive Processing Blindspot and the Architecture of Attention

The most glaring omission in the manuscript is its total failure to engage with the Predictive Processing (PP) framework and the Free Energy Principle, which have dominated computational neuroscience and philosophy of mind over the past decade. The manuscript defines attention as a unified selection interface, architecturally divided into exogenous (~100ms), instructed (~300ms), and willed (~300ms) modes, arguing that willed attention is the specific locus where a non-physical consciousness "makes its genuine contribution" by biasing neural competition.  

### Subsuming the Tri-Modal Model via Precision Weighting

The manuscript’s assertion that "willed attention" requires a non-physical selector is entirely neutralized by the predictive coding account of attention, heavily formalized by Friston, Clark, Seth, and Hohwy. In the PP framework, the brain is a hierarchical prediction machine that continuously generates top-down sensory predictions and updates its generative models based on bottom-up prediction errors. Within this architecture, attention is mathematically defined as *precision weighting*—the mechanism by which the brain estimates the reliability (inverse variance) of prediction errors and selectively increases their gain, dictating which signals are allowed to propagate upward.  

The tripartite division of attention proposed by the manuscript maps flawlessly onto fully physicalist precision-weighting algorithms without any need for dualist intervention:

| Manuscript's Attentional Mode | The Dualist Interpretation | Predictive Processing / Active Inference Counterpart |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Exogenous Attention** | Automatic bottom-up capture; consciousness merely registers the brain's selection. | High-magnitude, unpredicted sensory variance forces an immediate precision update; bottom-up prediction errors dominate the hierarchy. |
| **Instructed Attention** | Directed by external cues; consciousness follows established rules. | Contextual priors adjust the expected precision of specific sensory channels, priming the network to heavily weight incoming target features. |
| **Willed (Endogenous) Attention** | Non-physical consciousness actively sustains focus and biases neural competition. | Top-down precision modulation driven by deep hierarchical policies in Active Inference; the brain acts to fulfill higher-order predictions (minimizing expected free energy) against local distractors. |

  

By entirely ignoring Predictive Processing, the manuscript attempts to solve a problem—the causal role and unified architecture of attention—that computational neuroscience has already heavily formalized. The manuscript invokes frontal theta oscillations and frontoparietal coherence as "unique neural signatures" of willed attention. However, in PP, these exact frequency bands and coherence patterns are understood as the physical implementation of precision-weighted message passing between higher and lower cortical hierarchies. The manuscript treats the discovery of a physical mechanism for cognitive control as evidence for a non-physical controller, which is a fundamental category error. Under Active Inference, willed action is not "selected" by a non-physical consciousness; rather, the agent executes actions to fulfill prior sensory predictions, thereby minimizing variational free energy. An article making sweeping claims about attention and neural gain cannot be published in a top-tier journal while failing to even mention precision weighting.  

## Part II: Neuromodulation and the "God of the Gaps" Fallacy

To ground its "Three-Layer Architecture," the manuscript heavily relies on recent neurobiological findings regarding dopamine dynamics, specifically citing Cai et al. (2024). The manuscript notes that dopamine-deficient mice retain basic motor capacity but lose goal-directed initiative—a "can move but won't move" phenotype. The author leverages this to argue that the brain computationally generates competing motor options, dopamine sets the "salience threshold," and a non-physical consciousness ultimately selects which option crosses this threshold via willed attention.  

### The Mischaracterization of Dopaminergic Gating

This theoretical extrapolation is biologically indefensible and represents a textbook "god of the gaps" fallacy. The findings of Cai et al. establish that dopamine dynamics mediate motivational salience and reward-prediction errors. In standard computational models of the basal ganglia, action-selection competition is resolved through physical lateral inhibition and dopaminergic gating. The striatum receives converging inputs representing various motor plans; dopaminergic projections from the substantia nigra pars compacta (SNc) and ventral tegmental area (VTA) modulate the excitability of the direct (Go) and indirect (No-Go) pathways.  

When dopamine levels are manipulated, the physical threshold for action execution is altered. There is no empirical necessity, nor biological gap, left for a non-physical "chooser." The striatal microcircuitry is a fully closed physical system where the winning motor plan is determined by the integration of glutamatergic drive, local GABAergic inhibition, and dopaminergic tone. By mapping a purely neurochemical gating mechanism and arbitrarily inserting a dualist selector at the terminal node, the manuscript abandons empirical rigor. The cited study provides robust evidence for the physicalist reduction of motivation and initiative, demonstrating that "will" is intrinsically tied to monoamine concentrations. To interpret this as evidence for a non-physical consciousness requires a willful misreading of the cited neurobiology.  

Furthermore, the manuscript cites Desmurget et al. (2009) to argue for a separation of intention and execution, noting that stimulating the parietal cortex produces a conscious intention to move, whereas premotor stimulation causes movement without awareness. The manuscript claims this supports consciousness operating at the "policy/intention level." However, this finding perfectly aligns with hierarchical motor control theories (and Predictive Processing), which posit that the parietal cortex represents higher-level motor predictions (intentions/policies), while premotor and primary motor cortices handle lower-level kinematic implementations. The dissociation is a structural feature of cortical topology, not an ontological demarcation between mind and matter.  

## Part III: The Computational Reduction of Effort and Volition

A central pillar of the manuscript's argument is "The Economics of Agency," which leans heavily on the phenomenology of effort. The author argues that sustaining willed attention feels like physical work, correlating with physiological metrics such as pupil dilation and heart rate (Kahneman, 1973). To the manuscript's credit, it acknowledges the collapse of the "ego-depletion" (strength-resource) model, citing Hagger et al.'s (2016) multilab replication failure. However, the manuscript’s attempt to replace ego-depletion with opportunity-cost models (Kurzban et al., 2013; Inzlicht et al., 2021) while retaining a dualist framework reveals a profound contradiction in its handling of cited literature.  

### The Informational Nature of Felt Effort

Inzlicht and Kurzban’s opportunity-cost model fundamentally undermines the article’s thesis. The opportunity-cost framework posits that the subjective feeling of effort is not the exhaustion of a physical or spiritual resource, but an *informational signal* computed by the brain. This algorithm tracks the metabolic and evolutionary costs of maintaining a current task over pursuing alternative, potentially higher-reward actions.  

Under this model, effort is not the physical "running cost" of a non-physical pilot wrestling with neural machinery; it is a mechanistic, computational algorithm designed to shift behavior from "have-to" tasks to "want-to" tasks. By conceding the opportunity-cost model, the article inadvertently imports a fully reductive, physicalist explanation of effort. The subjective feeling of "willed attention" is unmasked as a systemic alarm bell, optimized by natural selection to prevent the organism from fixating on suboptimal behavioral policies. The manuscript simply brackets the computational implications of Inzlicht’s work to protect its prior commitment to a non-physical "willed" force.  

### Forward Models and Motor Dissociations

The manuscript also invokes Sjöberg (2024) and Naccache et al. (2005) to demonstrate that the subjective sense of effort can be dissociated from motor execution. Sjöberg’s finding that patients with supplementary motor area (SMA) resections still report a subjective sense of willed effort without voluntary motor execution is presented as evidence of an independent, non-physical willing agent.  

This entirely ignores standard forward-model theories of motor control. In physicalist sensorimotor frameworks, the brain generates an efference copy (a prediction of the sensory consequences of an action) alongside the motor command. If the motor command is issued but execution is blocked (e.g., via SMA resection), the mismatch between the predicted sensory state and the actual sensory feedback generates a massive prediction error. Phenomenologically, this prediction error translates to the profound "feeling" of effort—the system is driving the intention, but the expected feedback is absent. The manuscript fails to test its dualist hypothesis against this highly formalized, purely mechanistic alternative, treating a standard neurological dissociation as a metaphysical revelation.

## Part IV: Prefrontal Modularity vs. Ontological Dualism

The manuscript’s foundation relies heavily on establishing a strict ontological dissociation between attention and consciousness. It cites the Koch & Tsuchiya (2007) framework, peripheral phenomenal overflow (iconic memory experiments), and the adversarial COGITATE study (Ferrante et al., 2025). The author maps consciousness to a non-physical realm and willed attention to physical prefrontal cortical networks, arguing that the two work together "like a pianist and a piano".  

### Treating Disputed Framing as Settled

By strictly mapping "willed attention" to frontal networks (frontal theta, additional frontal recruitment) while keeping consciousness outside the physical brain, the article inadvertently traps itself in one of the most intense, ongoing debates in neuroscience. The manuscript treats the role of the prefrontal cortex in consciousness as a settled matter, which is empirically false. The contemporary literature, particularly between 2020 and 2025, is dominated by the debate between "frontal" theories (like Global Workspace Theory) and "sensory/posterior" theories (like Integrated Information Theory).  

Leading theorists such as Cohen, Pitts, Overgaard, and Fazekas have heavily debated whether prefrontal activation correlates with consciousness itself or merely with the *reporting* of consciousness (i.e., attention and working memory). If willed attention is physically instantiated by frontal theta oscillations and frontoparietal coherence, as the manuscript concedes, then the selection mechanism is fully localized within physical brain topology. The dissociation between attention (frontal) and consciousness (posterior/sensory) does not prove that consciousness is a non-physical pilot interacting with a physical attention-bridge. It merely provides evidence for the functional modularity of the brain—a fully expected outcome under reductive physicalism.  

The manuscript’s reliance on the COGITATE study to argue that "neither GWT nor IIT was clearly vindicated" is a vast oversimplification. The failure of a specific adversarial collaboration to definitively crown one physicalist theory over another does not default the victory to interactionist dualism. It merely highlights the complexity of localizing phenomenal correlates within highly distributed recurrent networks. The manuscript weaponizes this uncertainty, exploiting a gap in current neuroscientific consensus to insert an ontologically extravagant dualist premise.  

## Part V: The Illusionist Counter-Offensive and Petitio Principii

A pre-publication review must assess whether an article engages rigorously with the strongest forms of opposing arguments. The manuscript attempts to neutralize Graziano’s Attention Schema Theory (AST) and related illusionist frameworks through a rapid, philosophically inadequate critique. The article claims that AST "has not earned the leap from representational content to actual ontic phenomenality" and asserts that recursive self-modeling "does not generate phenomenal experience".  

### Misunderstanding the Illusionist Project

This critique commits a blatant *petitio principii* (begging the question). Illusionism (championed by Frankish, 2016, 2025) and Attention Schema Theory (Graziano, 2020-2025) explicitly and foundationally deny the existence of "ontic phenomenality" as traditionally conceived by dualists. AST posits that consciousness is a simplified, schematic model of attention constructed by the brain for the purpose of endogenous control. Just as the brain constructs a "body schema" to track and control the physical body, it constructs an "attention schema" to track and control its own data-processing resources.  

Under these frameworks, the *feeling* of a non-physical, ethereal phenomenal essence is a highly adaptive misrepresentation—a data structure computing a useful illusion. To refute AST by complaining that it fails to explain "actual ontic phenomenality" is to demand that AST accept the exact dualist premise it was specifically designed to eliminate. The manuscript treats the existence of non-physical phenomenality as an undisputed, pre-theoretical datum, entirely bypassing the rigorous illusionist arguments that explain *why* human brains are evolutionarily programmed to falsely insist they possess non-physical properties.  

| Concept | Manuscript's Dualist Claim | Illusionist / AST Counter-Claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Phenomenality** | An objectively real, non-physical property of the universe that interfaces with the brain. | A cognitive illusion; a flawed data structure the brain uses to model its own attentional states. |
| **The Explanatory Gap** | AST fails to cross the gap from representation to true conscious experience. | The gap is a conceptual error. There is nothing to cross; one only needs to explain why the brain *claims* there is a gap. |
| **Occam's Razor** | Dualism is more "honest" than AST's "just a model" simplicity. | AST eliminates an entire metaphysical category (non-physical substance) by explaining it computationally. |

  

The manuscript's dismissal of AST without engaging its central eliminativist logic insulates the author from genuine physicalist critique and renders the section academically unviable for a serious philosophy journal.

## Part VI: Information Theory, Bandwidth Limits, and the Quantum Zeno Collapse

The final leg of the manuscript's architectural claim attempts to reconcile interactionist dualism with physical conservation laws via information theory and quantum mechanics. The article cites Zheng & Meister (2025) and Sauerbrei & Pruszynski (2025) to establish that conscious behavioral throughput is severely constrained, operating at a bandwidth of approximately 10 bits per second. The manuscript utilizes this finding to argue for a "Minimal Quantum Interaction," suggesting that this incredibly low throughput represents a highly constrained causal influence that somehow bypasses the interaction problem.  

### The Physics of Information Processing

This argument betrays a fundamental misunderstanding of both information theory and the metaphysics of interactionism. First, the 10-bit per second limitation on conscious motor output (such as expert typing or competitive gaming) is entirely predicted by physicalist models. It is a biological structural bottleneck dictated by the corticospinal tract, the refractory periods of neuromuscular junctions, and the well-documented limits of working memory (e.g., Miller's Law).

Second, positing that a low bit-rate excuses a violation of the causal closure of the physical universe is mathematically and philosophically incoherent. In physics, information is physical (Landauer's principle). If a non-physical substance injects even a single bit of information into a closed physical system to alter a probabilistic neural outcome, it violates the conservation of energy and momentum. Repackaging a neurobiological bottleneck as the literal bandwidth of a non-physical soul does not circumvent the interaction problem; it merely obscures it behind borrowed information-theory terminology.

### The Self-Defeating Quantum Invocation

The manuscript's foray into quantum mechanics is equally self-defeating. The author tentatively introduces Stapp’s quantum Zeno effect, suggesting that consciousness stabilizes desired neural patterns against decoherence through rapid, repeated acts of observation. However, in the very next breath, the manuscript concedes that neural decoherence timescales in the warm, wet, macroscopic environment of the brain are vastly too fast for this stabilization to operate.  

Despite explicitly conceding the failure of the biological Zeno effect, the article retains "Minimal Quantum Interaction" and the "Rejection of Many-Worlds" as core tenets. A scholarly article cannot build a causal framework upon a physical mechanism it simultaneously admits is biologically unviable. If decoherence prevents Zeno stabilization at the synaptic level, the proposed causal bridge collapses. The author attempts to bracket this fatal flaw to protect the prior commitment to mental causation, severely undermining the paper's scientific and logical rigor. The rejection of the Many-Worlds interpretation is similarly unearned; it is presented merely as a necessity to allow for "willed selection," rather than argued for on independent quantum-mechanical grounds.

## Specific Weaknesses Validating Rejection

1. **Omission of Predictive Processing and Active Inference:** The article proposes a unified selection architecture for attention (exogenous, instructed, willed) but completely fails to test its dualist hypothesis against the Predictive Processing framework. PP explicitly formalizes attention as the precision weighting of prediction errors—a robust, mathematically complete physicalist rival that accounts for all attentional modes without invoking a non-physical selector.

- *Source mishandled/omitted:* Hohwy, J. (2020). New directions in predictive processing. *Mind & Language*, 35(2), 209-223..
2. **Misrepresentation of the Opportunity-Cost Model of Effort:** The manuscript cites the opportunity-cost model of effort to replace the debunked ego-depletion framework, yet it fails to recognize that this model fundamentally undermines its premise. By defining effort as a computational signal evaluating foregone metabolic and evolutionary alternatives, the cited literature explains volition reductively, leaving no explanatory gap for a non-physical "willed" entity.

- *Source mishandled/omitted:* Inzlicht, M., Werner, K. M., Briskin, J. L., & Roberts, B. W. (2021). Integrating models of self-regulation. *Annual Review of Psychology*, 72, 319-345..
3. **Begging the Question Against Illusionism and AST:** The article dismisses Attention Schema Theory and Illusionism by arguing they fail to explain "actual ontic phenomenality." This fundamentally misrepresents the core premise of these theories, which explicitly deny the existence of ontic phenomenality and model it as a highly adaptive cognitive illusion. Demanding an illusionist theory explain a dualist premise is a logical fallacy that artificially protects the article's core claims.

- *Source mishandled/omitted:* Frankish, K. (2025). *Illusionism as a theory of consciousness*. Journal of Consciousness Studies..
4. **Equivocation on Dopaminergic Neuromodulation:** The article cites recent findings on dopamine-deficient mice to argue that dopamine merely sets a "salience threshold" that a non-physical consciousness must then cross. This is an egregious extrapolation. The literature demonstrates that dopaminergic and basal ganglia dynamics (lateral inhibition) are physically sufficient for action selection and reward processing, providing zero empirical justification for injecting a non-physical causal variable.

- *Source mishandled/omitted:* Cai, X., Liu, C., et al. (2024). Dopamine dynamics are dispensable for movement but promote reward responses. *Nature*, 635, 406-414..
5. **Treating the Prefrontal/Consciousness Debate as Settled Doctrine:** The article relies heavily on the dissociation of attention and consciousness, linking "willed attention" strictly to frontal networks (theta/coherence) while mapping consciousness to a separate non-physical realm. In doing so, it treats the role of the prefrontal cortex as a settled foundational premise, blatantly ignoring the fierce contemporary debate between frontal and sensory theories of consciousness, thus building its dualist bridge on highly contested physicalist grounds.

- *Source mishandled/omitted:* Cohen, M. A., Pitts, M., & Overgaard, M. (2020-2025 literature on frontal vs. sensory theories / The role of the prefrontal cortex in consciousness)..

Due to pervasive question-begging, the severe mischaracterization of cited empirical neurobiology, and a systematic failure to engage with the dominant computational frameworks in contemporary cognitive science, this manuscript must be firmly rejected.
