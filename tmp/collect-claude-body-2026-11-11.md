# Hostile Referee Audit: “The Boredom Void” (Unfinishable Map, modified 2026-11-03)

The article’s quotations are almost all accurate. Its weak points are evidential and structural. It leaves out published findings that cut against faces 1, 2 and 3. Face 4 holds by definition rather than by discovery. It also links face 3 to a sibling page that claims dualist support, which contradicts the article’s own neutrality. On the evidence below, I would grade it “accept with major revisions.” No quotation is fabricated, but several inferences go further than their sources allow, and the article never engages the 2023–2025 functional-theory literature.

## TL;DR

- **The citations hold up.** Every quotation checked against a primary or full-text source matches in wording and attribution. That covers Eastwood et al. 2012, Wilson et al. 2014a, Fox et al. 2014, Westgate & Wilson 2018, Agrawal et al. 2022, Buttrick et al. 2019, Hatano et al. 2022, Chin et al. 2017, Bench & Lench 2013, Witowska et al. 2020, Burda et al. 2018, Pascal, Tolstoy, James, Williams and Slaby. The shock-study figures are right: 12/18 men and 6/24 women; at least one shock in 15 minutes; only participants who had said they would pay to avoid it. [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4330241/) The defects are framing, omission and novelty claims, not misquotation.
- **The empirical faces are weaker than the article admits.** Westgate & Wilson’s own data show people can self-report attention and meaning deficits separately, which counts against face 2. Bored people’s choices are directional, not indiscriminate, which counts against face 1. Thinking-alone is cheaply improved by thinking aids and goal instructions, and its experienced enjoyment is underestimated, which reframes face 3 as a metacognitive bias rather than an incapacity. Face 4 follows by definition from Eastwood’s stipulation. [sagepub](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691612456044) Mindfulness and self-awareness studies point in mixed directions, and none tests it directly.
- **Integration is the biggest structural risk.** Face 3 is tied to “Self-Maintained Cognitive Limits,” a page whose site-perspective section claims self-maintenance supports dualism and whose notion of “complicity” is far stronger than an avoidance feedback loop. The public changelog has no entries after 2026-10-15, so the boredom article’s creation and edits are not logged there. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/) Several linked pages could not be checked in this audit.

## Key Findings

### Verified problems (checked against primary sources or the live page)

1. **Face 2’s “unexplored margin” is partly explored by the article’s own source.** The article says whether a bored person can say which deficit made her bored is “a question Study 2 did not ask.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/) That is true only of a direct causal-attribution item. Study 2’s manipulation checks asked how personally meaningful the task was and how hard it was to concentrate, and both checks worked:

- Meaning: F(1, 214) = 58.69, d = 1.04. [erinwestgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/westgatewilson.psychreview.2018.pdf)
- Attention: F(1, 214) = 15.77, d = .54. [erinwestgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/westgatewilson.psychreview.2018.pdf)
- The attention manipulation “had no effect on reported meaning.” [erinwestgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/westgatewilson.psychreview.2018.pdf)
- In Study 1’s meta-analysis of 14 studies (N = 1,355), self-rated concentration difficulty and self-rated meaningfulness each independently predicted boredom, and they correlated only r = −.12. [apa](https://psycnet.apa.org/manuscript/2018-30815-001.pdf)[erinwestgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/westgatewilson.psychreview.2018.pdf)

So people’s self-reports do separate the two deficits. That is direct evidence against the claim that the verdict “does not carry on its face” which deficit fired. The article needs to engage it.
2. **Face 1 overstates the functional literature.** The article says functional accounts “agree on the direction without supplying a destination.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/) But Bench & Lench propose that “reference points would determine the goal that boredom motivates, as the desirable goal would be one that is different from the current situation.” [doi](http://dx.doi.org/10.3390/bs3030459) Westgate & Wilson also summarise Bench & Lench as predicting that bored people seek “mirror opposites of their previous goal.” Westgate & Wilson then report that bored people’s choices “appear neither indiscriminate nor the opposite of previous goals”: bored people prefer exciting films, meaningful activities and interesting foods. The MAC model itself predicts which kind of activity people will seek, “interesting” or “enjoyable,” depending on the cell. [erinwestgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/westgatewilson.psychreview.2018.pdf) Boredom therefore carries at least type-level directional content. The article’s own stated defeater (“evidence that bored people can reliably say which alternative would repay them would weaken it”) is already partly met at the level of type, and the article never mentions this. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/)
3. **Face 4 is analytic, not discovered.** The argument runs: Eastwood’s clause (a) defines boredom as inability to engage attention in satisfying activity, so engaged self-study negates (a), so the bout ends. [sagepub](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691612456044) That follows from the definition. Eastwood et al. present it as a proposed definition. [academia](https://www.academia.edu/21404969/Eastwood_et_al_PPS_2012) Westgate & Wilson’s Studies 3–4 show that the attentional definition is incomplete (overstimulation boredom; meaning-only boredom). [google](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup)[erinwestgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/westgatewilson.psychreview.2018.pdf) The article admits the point is “definition-relative,” which is to its credit. But “Relation to Site Perspective” still files face 4 as *unexplorable*. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/) A limit generated by stipulation should not be catalogued as a void type.
4. **Fox et al. 2014 is cited selectively.** The article reports Fox’s “somewhat enjoyable…” averages and the 14/18 curiosity reports. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/) It leaves out three points from the same commentary that weaken the shock study’s link to boredom:

- 57% of the 42 never shocked themselves, and only 17% gave themselves more than two shocks. [frontiersin](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01427/pdf)
- The shock was rated only slightly unpleasant (M = 4.12 on a 9-point scale, 5 = neutral). [frontiersin](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01427/pdf)
- Wilson et al.’s own supplement found no difference in enjoyment of thinking between people who shocked themselves and people who did not. [frontiersin](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01427/pdf)

The article’s paraphrase of the Wilson et al. 2014b reply (“all had already felt it and said they would pay to avoid it”) is consistent with the design. The reply’s actual argument is different, though. It asks why anyone would “re-experience an unpleasant stimulus just to see if it is still unpleasant,” and it adds that 32–54% of participants disobeyed the “just think” instructions. [nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4260463/)
5. **Buttrick et al. 2019 is presented as more uniform than it is.** “The results were consistent in every country” is verbatim and accurate for the between-condition difference. [nickbuttrick](https://www.nickbuttrick.com/publication/jpsp2018/) But:

- The pooled effect was d = .98, against d = 1.83 in the original Study 8, roughly half the size. [nickbuttrick](https://nickbuttrick.com/files/JPSP2018Preprint.pdf)
- Thinking-condition means did not differ significantly from the scale midpoint in Brazil, Costa Rica, Portugal or Serbia. [nickbuttrick](https://nickbuttrick.com/files/JPSP2018Preprint.pdf)
- Japan’s doing condition was not significantly above the midpoint. [nickbuttrick](https://nickbuttrick.com/files/JPSP2018Preprint.pdf)
- 54% of the thinking condition reported a forbidden activity. [nickbuttrick](https://nickbuttrick.com/files/JPSP2018Preprint.pdf)
- Buttrick replicated the thinking-versus-doing comparison (Study 8), not the shock study, and the “doing” options included TV, video games and the internet. Fox et al. argue these are not “mundane.” [nih +2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4260464/)
- The individual-difference correlates the article lists (need for cognition, openness, meditation, initial positive affect, and phone use running negatively) are reported as fully accounting for between-country variation. [nickbuttrick](https://nickbuttrick.com/files/JPSP2018Preprint.pdf) The article’s wording is acceptable, but this context is missing.
6. **Cheap fixes for face 3 are omitted.** Westgate & Wilson 2018 report two relevant results:

- Topic reminders reduced boredom with thinking across six studies (N = 1,276; d = −.20), mediated by easier concentration. [erinwestgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/westgatewilson.psychreview.2018.pdf)
- Alahmadi et al. 2017 found that simply giving people the goal of entertaining themselves produced “considerably less boredom.” [erinwestgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/westgatewilson.psychreview.2018.pdf)

These undercut the article’s framing that the limit “belongs to the untrained default” and that “training” means meditation. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/) The difficulty responds to small instructional changes. On the other side, Wilson’s own “prompted fantasy” planning conditions did not help (M = 4.97 vs 4.94). [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4330241/) The article should report both.
7. **Hatano et al. 2022 is used without its absolute levels or its boundary.** The quotations are exact, and N = 259 across six experiments is correct. [nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35901414/) But the press release reports that actual enjoyment averaged about 3–4 on a 7-point scale. [sciencedaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/07/220728091034.htm) Murayama states that participants rated thinking “not … extremely enjoyable … but simply as more enjoyable than they thought it would be.” [technologynetworks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/neuroscience/news/let-your-mind-wander-people-underestimate-how-enjoyable-waiting-is-364205) The finding is a relative underestimate around the midpoint. That reframes face 3’s “struggles to occupy itself”: the forecast error is real, but the “struggle” is modest. The article’s “self-sealing forecast” inference is flagged as the Map’s own, which is to its credit, but it has not been tested. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/) Hatano did not examine repeated exposure.
8. **Chin et al. 2017 is used in a way that cuts against the article’s other faces.** The quotation (“less consistent with accounts that exclusively associate boredom with low arousal or with situations lacking in meaning”) is exact. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/)[google](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup) The article uses it against the “simplest reading,” but the same sentence counts against an exclusively meaning-based account, and the article leans on the MAC meaning component in faces 2 and 4 and in the AI section. Chin’s measure is also a single item over 30-minute periods. [google](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup) Describing it as measuring boredom “in the moment” overstates its temporal resolution.
9. **The Burda et al. 2018 quotation is truncated in a way that matters.** “As expected the presence of the TV drastically slows down learning” continues: “but we note that [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.04355) if you run the experiment for long enough the agents do sometimes converge to getting the extrinsic reward consistently.” [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.04355) The article’s conclusion, “so a useful boredom signal needs a value term like the MAC meaning component,” is the Map’s inference, not Burda’s. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/) The machine-learning literature’s main fixes for the noisy TV are learning-progress and novelty methods, not value terms. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25438)
10. **Integration contradiction with “Self-Maintained Cognitive Limits.”** The boredom article says “nothing here requires dualism” and that “a physicalist and a dualist describe the same blank.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/) The linked page’s site-perspective section says: “Dualism gains support: if consciousness is merely what brains do, the notion of consciousness having interests in its own ignorance becomes incoherent.” That page defines self-maintenance as “complicity,” meaning “consciousness is itself implicated in the blocking—defender and defended as the same entity.” It concerns defended content such as death, cosmic insignificance and consciousness-related thoughts. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/) An avoidance loop in which a low forecast prevents disconfirming experience is ordinary operant avoidance, not complicity in that sense. Borrowing the label brings in a dualist-leaning frame the boredom article explicitly disowns.

## Details by assessment criterion

### (a) Citations and quotations

The article meets an unusually high quotation standard. The problems are about what surrounds the quotations: dropped qualifiers (Burda’s convergence clause), omitted denominators (57% never shocked), missing absolute levels (Hatano’s roughly 3–4 out of 7), and effect-size shrinkage (Buttrick’s d of .98 against 1.83). Two descriptive claims overreach: “functional accounts… without supplying a destination” and “a question Study 2 did not ask.”

### (b) The empirical claims against the 2020s literature

- **Thinking alone.** The comparative finding (doing is preferred to thinking) replicates: Buttrick across 11 countries, and Smith & Frank 2015 on Mechanical Turk (d = 1.32), which Buttrick reports. [nickbuttrick](https://nickbuttrick.com/files/JPSP2018Preprint.pdf) The aversiveness finding does not hold up as stated. Fox et al. note that across more than 4,300 participants in nine prior studies, mind-wandering was “on average mildly pleasant.” [frontiersin](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01427/pdf) Pfeifer, Geyer, Storch & Wittmann 2019 (*Psych* 1:343–352) found that after 6.5 minutes of silence alone, students were “significantly (t = −7.3, p < 0.0001) more relaxed,” with self-rated valence rising from 0.92 to 1.12 (t = −3.0, p = 0.004); they conclude that “our sample of students enjoyed their silent waiting time when instructed just to think.” Hatano shows that people underestimate the experience. [nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35901414/) Face 3 survives only as “unassisted thinking for pleasure is effortful and under-forecast.” It does not survive as “the mind struggles to occupy itself.”
- **The MAC model.** It supports face 2’s dual-cause structure, but its self-report data undermine face 2’s opacity claim (verified problem 1).
- **Functional and regulatory accounts.** Danckert & Elpidorou 2023 (*Trends in Cognitive Sciences*) propose that boredom is “an affective signal that we have deviated from an optimal (‘Goldilocks’) zone of cognitive engagement.” [philpeople](https://philpeople.org/profiles/james-danckert/publications) Trudel, Risko, Eastwood, van Tilburg, Elpidorou & Danckert 2025 (*Communications Psychology*) frame boredom as signalling “deviation from a cognitive homeostatic set point.” [jamesdanckert](https://www.jamesdanckert.com/publications) A deviation signal is informative about the state of the current engagement, not about destinations. That explains face 1’s objectlessness as the function working as designed rather than as an occlusion. The article cites none of the work Elpidorou published from 2014 onward. [google](https://scholar.google.com/citations)
- **Examination and mindfulness.** No study tests face 4 directly. The indirect evidence points both ways:

- Koval & Todman 2015 found that induced boredom lowered state mindfulness (n = 95), but a later experiment did not replicate the decrease. [academia](https://www.academia.edu/55970592/Induced_Boredom_Constrains_Mindfulness_An_Online_Demonstration)[researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383703005_Interaction_of_a_Brief_Mindfulness_Intervention_and_Induced_Boredom_on_Delay_Discounting_Behavior_and_State_Mindfulness)
- Fan, Wang, Yan & Kucukusta (*Tourism Management* 114:105385, online December 2025) collected “experience sampling data from 111 hotel employees over 10 workdays.” They found that state mindfulness reduced boredom, and that “employees perceiving their work as less meaningful may derive more benefits from state mindfulness.” That counts against the article’s claim that examination may leave meaning-deficit boredom intact, though mindfulness is not “engaged study.”
- Moynihan et al. 2015 (“Eaten up by boredom”) argue that self-awareness while bored is aversive and that people escape it by eating. [nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4381486/) That suggests attending to one’s boredom can intensify it rather than end it.

Face 4 is therefore neither supported nor refuted. It should be presented as an open empirical question, not as a void.

### (c) Competing accounts it misses or underplays

1. **Constructionist accounts.** Westgate & Wilson describe boredom as a “situated affective reaction” built from inferences about internal cues. [erinwestgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/westgatewilson.psychreview.2018.pdf) Combined with affect-as-information (Clore), that explains face 2’s outward projection without any special void: affect is routinely misattributed to whatever is salient.
2. **Regulatory and homeostatic accounts.** Elpidorou 2018; Danckert & Elpidorou 2023; Trudel et al. 2025, as above.
3. **Information-gain, learning-progress and predictive-processing accounts.** Computational work treats boredom as the switch from exploitation to exploration (Danckert 2019; Gomez-Ramirez & Costa 2017; Darling 2023; Parvizi-Wayne et al.). [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.12895) The machine-learning line of curiosity and boredom models goes back to Schmidhuber’s 1991 “A possibility for implementing curiosity and boredom in model-building neural controllers.” [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.04355)[arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.08603) The article’s AI section cites only Burda.
4. **Meaning accounts.** Van Tilburg & Igou, and Barbalet, appear only through MAC.
5. **Existential and phenomenological accounts.** Heidegger’s first two forms of boredom (being bored *by* something, and bored *with* something) are skipped, [janslaby](https://janslaby.com/static/publications/Slaby2010_Heidegger_Boredom_proofs.pdf) and Svendsen and Elpidorou’s Sartrean and Heideggerian readings are absent.
6. **A deflationary physicalist reading of all four faces together.** Every affect presents a verdict without displaying its computational grounds; pain does not show nociceptor statistics either. The article never argues that boredom’s opacity differs in kind from general affective opacity. Without that argument, the “boredom void” is a special case of the self-opacity void, not a distinct entry.

### (d) Where tenet-protective framing does the work

The article is more disciplined than most void entries. It says “nothing here requires dualism” and “the collapse of a simple story lends no support to a non-physical one.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/) Residual problems remain:

- **The Occam paragraph attacks a strawman.** “The object is dull or low stimulation feels bad” is not a live scientific rival. The real simplest rivals (MAC, opportunity cost, homeostatic signal) are the models the article itself relies on. Invoking “Occam’s Razor Has Limits” here is ceremonial.
- **The attention-as-interface gloss.** “The Map … on the attention-as-interface hypothesis reads boredom as the felt cost where attention is selected” inserts a Map-specific mechanism with no evidential support in the article. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/) It should be cut or explicitly flagged as speculative.
- **The James argument.** The article concedes that it does not distinguish interactionism from physicalism, which is honest. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/boredom-void/) But the paragraph then spends words on a claim that does no work for the site’s distinctive tenets.
- **Void classification built on definitions.** Labelling face 4 “unexplorable” and face 3 “self-maintained” turns a definitional artefact and an avoidance loop into void categories. The taxonomy then reports more voids than the evidence licenses.
- **Unfalsifiability.** Faces 1 and 2 are framed as “occlusion” in a way that absorbs counterevidence: if people can report the deficit, the “attribution” margin remains. The article names a defeater for face 1, which is good practice, but it fails to notice that the defeater is already partly satisfied.

### (e) Integration with linked Map articles

- **Self-maintained cognitive limits.** Contradictory on dualism, and a stronger “complicity” notion than face 3 can support (verified problem 10).
- **Affective-forecasting gap concept page.** That page holds that “selection needs a correct ranking, not magnitudes, so the gap bears on it only where it demonstrably reverses ranks.” It also quotes Patel & Urry (2024): “Evidence for affective forecasting errors is mixed.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/affective-forecasting-gap/) Hatano’s result, where people chose news over waiting despite equal experiences, is exactly a rank-misordering case. The boredom article’s confident “seals itself” also clashes with the concept page’s caution.
- **The “Naturally Occluded” concept page** (not linked from the boredom article) claims that “certain directions lead nowhere productive — so they register as boring rather than blocked.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/naturally-occluded/) That is a strong claim that boredom marks evolutionary occlusion. - **Circularity.** None was detected at the citation level. At the taxonomy level, the article classifies itself using a three-kinds scheme whose criteria I could not inspect.

## Recommendations

### For “The Boredom Void”

1. **Face 2.** Report Westgate & Wilson’s manipulation-check and Study 1 self-report results. Narrow the claim from “the verdict does not carry which deficit fired” to “no study has asked for a direct causal attribution, though component self-reports do track the manipulations.”
2. **Face 1.** Replace “functional accounts agree on the direction without supplying a destination” with an accurate summary: Bench & Lench’s reference-point and mirror-opposite proposal, and the evidence that bored choices favour interesting, meaningful and exciting options. Restate the void as “boredom specifies a type of target, not a token.”
3. **Face 3.** Add three things: thinking aids (d = −.20, N = 1,276) and goal instructions (Alahmadi 2017); Hatano’s absolute levels (about 3–4 out of 7); and Buttrick’s d = .98 against 1.83, with the midpoint results. Reframe the face as forecast bias plus effortful self-direction, not incapacity. Report Fox’s 57% never-shocked figure and the no-difference-in-enjoyment finding.
4. **Face 4.** Either delete the “unexplorable” classification or relabel it “definitional (Eastwood).” Add the mindfulness and self-awareness evidence on both sides: Koval & Todman 2015 and its non-replication; Fan, Wang, Yan & Kucukusta’s 2025 *Tourism Management* experience-sampling study (111 hotel employees over 10 workdays); and Moynihan et al. 2015. Recast it as an open empirical question with a stated test.
5. **Add a “Competing accounts” section.** Cover Danckert & Elpidorou 2023, Trudel et al. 2025, Elpidorou 2018, constructionist and affect-as-information accounts, learning-progress models (Schmidhuber 1991 onward), and Heidegger’s three forms. State plainly why boredom’s opacity is not just general affective opacity, or merge the entry into self-opacity.
6. **AI section.** Restore Burda’s convergence clause. Attribute “needs a value term” to the Map. Mention learning-progress fixes for the noisy-TV problem.
7. **Chin et al.** Acknowledge that the same quotation also bears against meaning-only accounts. Describe the measure accurately: a single item per 30-minute period.
8. **Remove or flag the attention-as-interface gloss** in Bidirectional Interaction, and rewrite the Occam paragraph against the real rival models.
9. **Fix metadata.** Reconcile Created and published dates. Make reference 20’s authors and date match the live page. Cite Burda at ICLR 2019. Quote Williams from the primary source.
10. **Run a literature search before keeping “neither party proposes”** for the curiosity and exploration reconciliation.

### For linked articles

1. **Self-Maintained Cognitive Limits.** Either add a “weak sense” of self-maintenance (avoidance loops without complicity) that the boredom article can cite, or the boredom article should stop using the label. Reconcile the page’s “Dualism gains support” claim with sibling voids that are explicitly framework-neutral.
2. **Affective-Forecasting Gap.** Add Hatano 2022 as a documented case where choices are misordered, with its limits: undergraduates only, waiting periods of up to about 20 minutes, relative effects.
3. **Naturally Occluded.** Qualify or source the claim that “certain directions … register as boring rather than blocked.” Link it to the boredom void and accept that page’s more modest stance.
4. **Agency-void, mirth-void, mutation-void.** Confirm they support the claims the boredom article attributes to them: the opportunity-cost face, and the “examination dissolves” shape.

### For site methodology

1. **Log every created or edited article in the public changelog.** The boredom article’s creation and its 2026-11-03 revision are invisible there. The changelog page itself was last modified 2026-10-15. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)
2. **Add an “omitted-counterevidence” check to deep review.** The quotation-grepping pipeline is visibly strong (all quotes pass). The failure mode it misses is selective reporting: dropped qualifiers, missing denominators, effect-size shrinkage, and contrary results inside the cited paper.
3. **Require a “definitional versus empirical” tag** on each void face, and forbid classifying definitional faces as Unexplorable.
4. **Require a “differs-from-general-opacity” argument** before a new void is accepted, to stop the catalogue multiplying special cases of self-opacity.
5. **Run a cross-page consistency check on tenet claims.** Flag when a page that disclaims dualist support links, for a key move, to a page that claims it.
6. **Treat novelty claims as citations.** “A reconciliation … neither party proposes” should trigger a mandatory literature search.
7. **Add a 2020s-literature sweep step** for psychology-heavy voids: functional and regulatory theory, replications, psychometrics (state versus trait; MSBS against the Boredom Proneness Scale).

## Caveats

- Several linked Map pages and the tenets page could not be fetched directly in this audit, so the integration findings are partial.
- Some primary sources were checked only through abstracts or author preprints: Eastwood, Agrawal, Hatano and Chin. Buttrick was read as an author preprint, whose wording may differ slightly from the version of record.
- The Nederkoorn et al. 2016 shock replication and the BPS psychometric critiques are not verified here; Havermans et al. 2015 has been verified against its primary PDF.
- The mindfulness evidence is mostly correlational, comes from heterogeneous samples, and does not test face 4 directly. It is cited to show the question is open, not to refute the article.
