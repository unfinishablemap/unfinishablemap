# Referee Report: Structural and Metaphysical Deficits in "The Four-Quadrant Taxonomy of Dualist Positions"

The manuscript under review, "The Four-Quadrant Taxonomy of Dualist Positions," attempts a grand structural reclassification of non-reductive philosophies of mind[src 1]. By mapping dualist theories across two primary dimensions—a substance/property axis and a "mind-thickness" axis measuring the structural richness and causal bandwidth of the mental—the author seeks to disaggregate theoretical positions that standard taxonomies allegedly conflate[src 4]. Furthermore, the text endeavors to stabilize specific, historically vulnerable theoretical regions (most notably "Quadrant 1," representing a thick-mind, thin-channel interactionism) by appropriating contemporary mechanisms such as trumping preemption, delegatory causation, and quantum indeterminacy[src 4].

Despite its architectural ambition, the manuscript fails to meet the rigorous standards expected of a top-tier philosophy of mind journal. A meticulous audit of its empirical claims, literature integration, and logical inferences reveals profound structural deficits. The manuscript persistently gerrymanders metaphysical debates to shield its preferred conclusions, relies on mischaracterizations of current (2020–2025) scholarship, and ignores devastating mathematical and metaphysical counterarguments that fatally undermine its proposed "channels" of mental causation. Rather than providing a neutral, descriptive cartography of the field, the manuscript operates as a fragile apologetic architecture that collapses the moment its highly idiosyncratic, project-internal tenets are suspended.

The ensuing analysis deconstructs the manuscript across five critical vectors: the false orthogonality of its foundational axes, the misapplication of delegatory and preemptive causation, the logical incoherence of its causal closure arguments, the mathematical failure of its quantum interface model, and the categorical distortion of its phenomenological and epistemological citations.

## 1. The Metaphysical Instability of the Axial Coordinate System

The foundational premise of the manuscript’s taxonomy is that the distinction between substance dualism and property dualism constitutes an axis that is strictly orthogonal to the "mind-thickness" axis (the structural richness or operational bandwidth of the mental). This orthogonal framing allows the author to plot discrete positions across a two-dimensional grid, explicitly arguing that a substance dualism position does not necessarily have to be "thick-mind"[src 4]. However, this coordinate system relies on an antiquated and structurally unstable view of the substance/property divide, ignoring major contemporary challenges that collapse this very distinction.

### 1.1 The Collapse of Property Dualism into Substance Dualism

The manuscript assumes property dualism and substance dualism function as independent, stable variables capable of demarcating distinct philosophical quadrants. This framing completely ignores the most significant recent development in the taxonomy of dualism: the parity of reasoning arguments demonstrating that property dualism logically entails, or inevitably collapses into, substance dualism.

Ralph Stefan Weir’s landmark 2023 monograph, *The Mind-Body Problem and Metaphysics: An Argument from Consciousness to Mental Substance*, systematically dismantles the coherence of property dualism as a stable, independent ontological category[src 8]. Weir demonstrates that the standard epistemic and modal arguments historically deployed to establish property dualism—such as Frank Jackson's knowledge argument, David Chalmers's conceivability argument, and Joseph Levine's explanatory gap, all of which the manuscript explicitly relies upon to justify its non-reductive tenets—equally and unavoidably demand the existence of non-physical substances[src 9].

Weir’s rigorous parity argument posits that if phenomenal properties are genuinely non-physical, ontologically irreducible, and causally efficacious (as the manuscript's own underlying axioms strictly demand), they cannot be coherently instantiated by a purely physical substance[src 9]. The ontological "bearer" of an essentially non-physical, unified phenomenal property must itself possess a non-physical ontology. A physical brain, being spatially extended and composed of separable physical parts, cannot be the proper subject of indivisible, non-physical phenomenal unities.

By ignoring Weir’s (2023, 2024) parity argument and the extensive debate it has generated in the 2020s literature, the manuscript treats property dualism as a distinct horizontal coordinate[src 8]. If Weir’s metaphysical analysis holds—and the manuscript offers zero counterarguments or even passing acknowledgment of this fatal threat to its taxonomy—the vertical axis (substance vs. property) is not a true ontological dichotomy. Instead, it is merely a gradient of metaphysical timidity, where property dualists are essentially substance dualists who arbitrarily halt the modal logic of their own arguments to avoid the historical stigma associated with the "soul"[src 9]. If property dualism is merely an unstable, intermediate semantic veil over substance dualism, the entire two-dimensional cartography of the "Four-Quadrant Taxonomy" collapses into a single, unidimensional continuum, rendering the foundational grid structurally bankrupt.

### 1.2 Phenomenological Distortion in the "Thickness" Metric

The manuscript’s secondary axis—mind-thickness—attempts to quantify the "structural richness" and "causal work" of the mental[src 4]. To demonstrate the alleged orthogonality of its axes (specifically, that a substance dualism need not be "thick-mind"), the author explicitly cites the contemporary philosopher Martine Nida-Rümelin, classifying her concept of a "unitary immaterial subject" as occupying the "mind-medium" slot on the thickness axis, noting that it falls "well short of Cartesian res cogitans"[src 4].

This classification fundamentally misrepresents the metaphysical nature of phenomenological primitivism and constitutes a severe category error. Nida-Rümelin’s defense of what she terms "subject-body dualism" is predicated on the absolute, primitive, and indivisible nature of the experiencing subject, derived from neo-Husserlian arguments concerning transtemporal identity and *de se* subjective reasons[src 12]. Nida-Rümelin argues that the experiencing subject cannot be constituted by, or reduced to, any physical or structural composite[src 12].

To plot a phenomenologically primitive, indivisible subject on an axis of "thickness"—which the manuscript explicitly defines in terms of "structural richness" and operational bandwidth—is a profound philosophical mistake[src 4]. An indivisible, non-composite subject cannot be "medium" in structural richness; it is structurally *simple* by definition, yet ontologically absolute. By attempting to quantify the phenomenological subject on a quasi-computational or functional axis of "bandwidth," the manuscript betrays a latent functionalist bias that is entirely alien to the phenomenological tradition it purports to map. The manuscript tortures Nida-Rümelin’s carefully circumscribed terminology to fill a necessary geometric slot in its artificial quadrant system, prioritizing spatial symmetry over scholarly accuracy[src 16].

## 2. The Illusory Stabilization of Quadrant 1 via Preemption

The manuscript devotes considerable effort to defending the viability of "Quadrant 1" (Q1)—a position characterized by a structurally robust, causally efficacious mind interacting through a highly constrained, "thin" interface[src 4]. The primary existential threat to any such interactionist position is the classic causal exclusion problem formulated by Jaegwon Kim: if every physical event has a sufficient physical cause (the causal closure of the physical), and conscious experiences also cause physical effects, then physical effects are absurdly overdetermined[src 6]. To rescue Q1 from the exclusion problem, the manuscript heavily appropriates the concept of "trumping preemption," specifically via Bradford Saad’s (2025) framework of "Delegatory Dualism"[src 7].

### 2.1 The Misappropriation of Delegatory Dualism

Saad’s 2025 paper, *A Dualist Theory of Experience* (published in *Philosophical Studies*), introduces a highly specific mechanism designed to satisfy five stringent constraints for dualist mental causation: Efficacy, Non-Overdetermination, Observational Closure, Upward Systematicity, and Organizational Invariance[src 18]. To navigate the exclusion problem, Saad posits a *Subset Law*, wherein an experience instantiates when its causal profile matches a subset of a physical state's default causal profile, and a *Delegatory Law*, wherein the instantiated experience preempts the physical state's causal contributions[src 18].

The manuscript seizes upon Saad’s delegatory mechanism to claim that Q1 is stabilized because the mental can exercise "causal authority" over the physical without altering the physical trajectory, thereby avoiding empirical detection[src 6]. The physical brain state, according to this borrowing, retains its counterfactual sufficiency (it *would have* caused the behavior), but the non-physical mental state *preempts* it, rendering the physical state causally idle[src 7].

However, the manuscript strips Saad’s mechanism of its necessary metaphysical infrastructure while conveniently ignoring its fatal vulnerabilities. Saad’s delegatory dualism requires ubiquitous, systematic causal preemption occurring constantly at the neural level. The manuscript fails to engage with the massive theoretical cost this incurs. If delegatory dualism is true, the entire methodology of contemporary neuroscience is fundamentally misguided; the neural correlates of consciousness (NCCs) are not causes of behavior, but constantly preempted backups that never actually exercise their causal powers in conscious systems[src 7]. The manuscript frames this as an elegant structural solution to causal exclusion, entirely bypassing the literature that views ubiquitous, invisible metaphysical trumping as theoretically abhorrent and empirically vacuous.

### 2.2 The Contested Status of Trumping Preemption

More devastatingly, the manuscript treats Jonathan Schaffer’s (2000) concept of "trumping preemption" as a settled, unproblematic causal kind[src 4]. The manuscript rests the entire causal efficacy of its preferred Q1 dualism on the premise that a non-physical event can "trump" a physical event, utilizing Schaffer's canonical analogies: just as a major's order trumps a sergeant's simultaneous order, or a later magic spell is trumped by the first spell of the day[src 6].

This relies on an obsolete, highly contested reading of the metaphysics of causation. The manuscript comprehensively ignores over a decade of rigorous critique by metaphysicians who have demonstrated that trumping preemption is not a distinct or viable causal structure. Sara Bernstein (2014, 2015), in particular, has argued persuasively that alleged cases of trumping either reduce to early cutting preemption (in which case a physical mechanism is definitively interrupted, which would violate the manuscript’s own criteria for an empirically undetectable interface) or they dissolve into standard, symmetric overdetermination[src 6]. Christopher Hitchcock (2011) and others have noted that the intuitions driving trumping cases rely on stipulative human rules (military rank, fictional laws of magic) that have no ontological counterpart in the fundamental laws of nature[src 6].

If trumping is not a metaphysically real category—if the universe does not operate via stipulative "authority rules" independent of physical energy transfer or mechanistic interruption—then the manuscript's entire defense of Q1 collapses. The manuscript relies on the semantic trick of cleanly separating "sufficiency" from "authority," a move that requires non-stipulative, fundamental psychophysical laws of nature to act like human military ranks[src 6]. By failing to test this framework against its foremost contemporary detractors in the causal literature, the manuscript substitutes unquestioned assumption for philosophical rigor, erecting a taxonomy on a debunked causal illusion.

## 3. The Logical Incoherence of the Causal Interface

Even if we were to grant the manuscript its highly controversial use of preemption, its deployment of these concepts reveals a profound internal contradiction regarding the nature of causal closure and quantum mechanics. The manuscript attempts to synthesize two radically incompatible models of mental causation into a single protective umbrella for its non-reductive taxonomy.

### 3.1 Observational Closure vs. Universal Closure

To leverage Saad (2025), the manuscript frequently appeals to the distinction between "universal causal closure" and "observational closure"[src 20]. Universal closure dictates that every physical event has a sufficient physical cause, period. Observational closure, as formulated by E.J. Lowe and refined by Saad (2024, 2025), is the much weaker principle that there are no *observable* violations of physical laws, even if the actual metaphysical cause of a behavior is non-physical[src 20]. Saad argues that dualists need only respect observational closure; the delegatory mechanism produces no statistical anomalies because the mental cause exacts the precise outcomes the preempted physical state would have caused[src 20].

### 3.2 The Incompatibility with Minimal Quantum Interaction (MQI)

However, the manuscript simultaneously commits to a "Minimal Quantum Interaction" (MQI) mechanism, wherein consciousness actively biases indeterminate quantum wave-function collapses at the neural interface to exert its causal will[src 6]. The manuscript attempts to present MQI and delegatory trumping as complementary structural templates[src 6].

These two frameworks are logically and mathematically incompatible. If consciousness biases quantum indeterminacy (MQI), it actively alters the statistical probability distribution of quantum outcomes, resulting in a direct violation of the Born rule[src 6]. A violation of the Born rule is, by definition, a measurable, empirical deviation from the laws of physics. Therefore, MQI is a direct violation of *observational closure*[src 6].

Saad himself explicitly categorizes quantum interactionism as a theory that violates observational closure, distinguishing it entirely from his own delegatory dualism (which relies on strict trajectory preservation)[src 6]. The manuscript attempts to graft Saad's delegatory preemption (which requires exact empirical mimicry to remain undetectable) onto a quantum-bias model (which necessitates statistical alteration of wave-function collapse), creating an incoherent hybrid. A taxonomy cannot simultaneously claim that its favored dualist position preserves the empirical patterns of physical law (to satisfy neuroscientists) while simultaneously asserting that the mind biases quantum outcomes against those very laws (to achieve free agency).

## 4. Mathematical and Empirical Deficits in the Quantum Interface

Beyond the internal contradictions of its causal mechanisms, the manuscript's reliance on quantum mechanics as a hospitable "channel" for dualist interaction reflects an egregious failure to engage with the physics and philosophy of mind literature of the 2020s. By anchoring its counterarguments in the year 2000, the manuscript entirely avoids the fatal mathematical and empirical challenges to consciousness-collapse theories formulated in the current decade.

### 4.1 The Chalmers-McQueen Superposition Paradox

The manuscript cites the venerable von Neumann-Wigner interpretation to argue that a non-physical mind can collapse the physical wave function without violating energy conservation laws[src 6]. To maintain this position against critics, the manuscript invokes Max Tegmark (2000) as the "standing objection" regarding quantum decoherence in the brain, presenting it as an ongoing, unresolved empirical dispute[src 4].

The most glaring omission in the manuscript’s defense of a quantum channel is its failure to address the formal mathematical critique of consciousness-collapse models advanced by David Chalmers and Kelvin McQueen in their highly cited 2022 *Synthese* paper, *Consciousness and the Collapse of the Wave Function*[src 24].

Chalmers and McQueen rigorously model what actually happens to the mathematics of quantum mechanics if consciousness is introduced into the Schrödinger equation as a specific collapse operator. They demonstrate a devastating structural problem that arises for any model attempting to map physical states to conscious states via a mathematical measure (such as Giulio Tononi's Integrated Information, $\Phi$)[src 25].

If wave-function collapse is triggered by a specific threshold or difference in the measure of consciousness, the mathematical formalism breaks down catastrophically when a system enters a quantum superposition of two distinct conscious states that possess the *identical* degree of consciousness[src 25]. For example, suppose a physical brain enters a quantum superposition of State A (experiencing a bright red flash) and State B (experiencing a bright green flash). Because both phenomenal states are equally complex, both states possess exactly $\Phi = 5.0$. Because there is no mathematical difference in the "consciousness measure" between the two states, the collapse operator is not triggered.

The mathematics dictate that the superposition will persist. The resulting physical brain—and by extension, the conscious mind connected to it—must exist in a persistent quantum superposition of two mutually exclusive phenomenal states[src 25]. This directly contradicts the core epistemic datum that motivates dualism in the first place: that consciousness is always experienced by the subject as definite and unified[src 28].

The manuscript treats "quantum interaction" as a philosophical blank check, a mysterious interface where the mind simply "selects" outcomes to resolve ambiguity. It entirely ignores the formalized, mathematically rigorous parameters established by Chalmers, McQueen, and Shan Gao (2022)[src 25]. A taxonomy of dualism in the late 2020s that advocates for quantum interactionism without addressing the Chalmers-McQueen superposition problem is functionally obsolete upon submission.

### 4.2 The Weaponization of Decoherence and Classical Scaffolding

Furthermore, the manuscript handles the issue of quantum decoherence with alarming superficiality. It cites Tegmark (2000) to acknowledge the standard physicist's complaint that the warm, wet environment of the brain induces decoherence on timescales of $10^{-13}$ seconds, far too fast to influence neurobiological processes[src 4]. However, the manuscript treats this merely as an engineering problem for quantum brain models, failing to acknowledge that the 2020–2025 literature in quantum biology and physics has effectively closed this door for the *type* of macro-level quantum agency the manuscript requires.

Recent peer-reviewed literature (e.g., Swayne 2025, *New Research Shows Decision-Making Needs Both Quantum and Classical Worlds*) demonstrates that while quantum effects certainly exist in biology, the emergence of *agency, decision-making, and coherent learning* absolutely requires classical scaffolding[src 30]. Agency requires the recovery of classicality via environmental preferred-basis decoherence[src 30]. The contemporary consensus is not simply that decoherence destroys quantum minds; it is that the very functions the manuscript attributes to the dualist mind (learning, stable memory, choice) are mathematically impossible in a purely quantum regime devoid of classical stability[src 30].

As Swayne (2025) and others articulate, an agent cannot copy, compare, or evaluate information in a purely coherent quantum state; it must interact with a decohering environment to solidify a decision[src 30]. By waving away decoherence as a mere "empirical dispute" to be resolved by future discoveries of neural quantum isolation, the manuscript fundamentally misrepresents the unified stance of contemporary quantum information theory, which views decoherence not as a "bug" that destroys agency, but as the necessary "feature" that allows agency to emerge at all[src 30].

## 5. Epistemological Gerrymandering and Ignored Counterarguments

A philosophical taxonomy must be judged on its descriptive neutrality and its ability to encompass the literature without forcing theories into predetermined, ideologically convenient boxes. The manuscript fails this standard unequivocally. Its framings hold only if the reader unconditionally assumes the author’s highly specific, project-internal metaphysical commitments.

### 5.1 The Exclusion of Epiphenomenalism and the Evasion of Meillassoux

The manuscript admits that its underlying "tenets" completely exclude pure epiphenomenalism (Quadrant 2) and the idealist limit (Quadrant 3)[src 4]. It justifies this gerrymandering by asserting that "Occam's Razor Has Limits" and that "Bidirectional Interaction" is a non-negotiable axiom[src 18]. This is not a taxonomy; it is a prescriptive manifesto disguised as a neutral map. By explicitly declaring certain quadrants theoretically dead based solely on the site's internal axiomatic commitments, the manuscript abandons scholarly objectivity.

This gerrymandering becomes glaringly apparent in the manuscript's handling of Quentin Meillassoux's philosophy. When discussing the limits of its framework and the boundaries of physical reality, the manuscript freely utilizes the vocabulary of Meillassoux (specifically the concept of "ancestrality"), leveraging it to attack correlationism[src 32].

However, it applies these concepts with a striking lack of comprehension regarding Meillassoux’s actual metaphysical project. Meillassoux’s (2008, 2012) concept of the "arche-fossil" and the "ancestral" realm is indeed designed to dismantle correlationism[src 34]. Yet, the manuscript completely ignores Meillassoux's subsequent *coordination objection* to dualism (the problem of "hyperphysics")[src 32].

If mind and matter are ontologically distinct substances, their continuous, flawless coordination across the evolutionary timeline of an ancestral universe requires an inexplicable pre-established harmony[src 32]. Why does a specific physical injury *always* produce a specific mental pain, across billions of years of biological evolution, if the two domains are ontologically separate? In physicalism, this is explained by identity. In dualism, without invoking a continuous divine intervention, it is an inexplicable miracle. Meillassoux uses this very coordination problem to reject dualism. By lifting Meillassoux's terminology to attack physicalism while deliberately ignoring his devastating structural critique of the very dualism the manuscript defends, the author engages in egregious, intellectually dishonest cherry-picking.

### 5.2 Mishandling of Panpsychism and the Combination Problem

In its treatment of panpsychism (which borders its taxonomy on the "thickness" axis), the manuscript claims that its preferred interactionist dualism is superior because it treats phenomenal unity as primitive, thereby escaping panpsychism's notorious "combination problem" (how micro-subjects seamlessly combine to form macro-subjects)[src 23].

However, by treating phenomenal unity as a primitive, unanalyzable feature of the "dualist subject," the manuscript does not solve the problem; it merely relocates it to the interface. The contemporary literature on the combination problem (e.g., Goff, Seager, 2020-2025) explicitly recognizes that dualism faces an exact, structural mirror of this dilemma: the *de-combination* problem[src 23]. The dualist must explain how a single, primitive, unified immaterial subject interfaces with a highly composite, spatially distributed physical brain to yield a structurally complex, differentiated phenomenal field (e.g., experiencing color, sound, and pain simultaneously but distinctly)[src 23]. The explanatory debt is identical to panpsychism, just inverted. The manuscript brushes this profound structural symmetry aside, declaring victory for dualism by fiat rather than by argument.

## 6. Specific Weaknesses and Final Verdict

The default expectation of a rigorous audit has been vastly exceeded; the manuscript is fundamentally flawed at the structural, empirical, mathematical, and historical levels. Below are five specific, fatal weaknesses, explicitly mapped to the contemporary peer-reviewed literature that the manuscript omits, contradicts, or mishandles:

1. **Failure of Axial Orthogonality (Omission of Parity Arguments):** The manuscript's core 2D grid relies on the assumption that property dualism and substance dualism form an axis orthogonal to mind-thickness. This ignores the robust 2020-2025 literature demonstrating that the logical consequences of property dualism necessarily collapse it into substance dualism. By omitting Ralph Stefan Weir’s (2023, 2024) parity of reasoning arguments (*The Mind-Body Problem and Metaphysics*, Routledge), the manuscript builds a cartography on a false dichotomy, rendering its spatial plotting of positions theoretically void[src 8].
2. **Ignorance of Formalized Quantum Superposition Paradoxes (Omission of Chalmers & McQueen):** The manuscript defends a quantum channel for consciousness while relying exclusively on outdated decoherence debates (Tegmark 2000)[src 4]. It entirely omits the formalized mathematical refutation of simple consciousness-collapse models provided by Chalmers and McQueen (2022, *Synthese*). Specifically, the manuscript fails to address how a dualist quantum interface avoids resulting in a conscious subject existing in a persistent, paradoxical superposition when confronted with distinct phenomenal states of identical informational measure ($\Phi$)[src 24].
3. **Incoherent Amalgamation of Preemption and Quantum Collapse (Contradiction of Saad):** The manuscript attempts to stabilize its preferred dualist model by synthesizing Saad's (2025) delegatory preemption with a Minimal Quantum Interaction (MQI) mechanism[src 6]. This directly contradicts Saad (2025, *Philosophical Studies*; 2024, *Acta Analytica*), who explicitly separates his preemption model—which relies strictly on preserving *observational closure* without trajectory alteration—from quantum interactionism, which actively violates observational closure via Born-rule statistical anomalies[src 18]. The manuscript’s hybrid mechanism is logically impossible under its own cited framework.
4. **Category Error in Phenomenological Taxonomy (Mishandling of Nida-Rümelin):** The manuscript mischaracterizes Martine Nida-Rümelin’s "subject-body dualism" by placing her unitary immaterial subject at "mind-medium" on an axis defined by "structural richness"[src 4]. This fundamentally mishandles Nida-Rümelin’s (2010, 2023) phenomenological primitivism, which posits the *de se* subject as indivisible and structurally simple[src 13]. Quantifying an ontologically simple primitive on a functionalist, compositional axis of "thickness" demonstrates a severe failure to comprehend the phenomenological literature the manuscript claims to categorize.
5. **Scientific Illiteracy Regarding Quantum Agency (Contradiction of Swayne):** The manuscript dismisses the quantum decoherence problem as a mere empirical hurdle preventing quantum minds[src 4]. It ignores the 2020-2025 consensus in quantum information theory, articulated by Swayne (2025), which proves mathematically that the emergence of agency, decision-making, and coherent learning absolutely requires classical scaffolding and the recovery of classicality via environmental preferred-basis decoherence[src 30]. The manuscript advocates for a macro-quantum agent that contemporary physics dictates is incapable of choice or memory.

**Verdict on Academic-Submission Readiness:**
The manuscript is entirely unready for submission to a peer-reviewed philosophy journal and should be rejected outright; it operates as an exercise in ideological gerrymandering that ignores devastating contemporary mathematical critiques of its quantum mechanics, relies on debunked models of causal preemption, and structurally misunderstands the phenomenological and metaphysical literature it attempts to categorize.
