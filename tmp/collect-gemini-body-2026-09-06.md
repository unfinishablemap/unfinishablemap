# Pre-Publication Peer Review Report: "Psychedelics and the Filter Model"

## 1. Introduction and Scope of the Audit

The manuscript under review, titled "Psychedelics and the Filter Model," undertakes a highly ambitious and fundamentally flawed defense of a non-reductive, interactionist dualist framework regarding the mind-brain relationship. Drawing explicitly upon the historical transmission theories initially formulated by William James, Henri Bergson, and Aldous Huxley, the text attempts to synthesize contemporary neuroimaging data—most prominently the suppression of the Default Mode Network (DMN) during psychedelic states—with a modern iteration of the filter theory of consciousness. The central thesis advanced by the manuscript posits that the brain operates not as the generative substrate of consciousness, but rather as an interface or a biological "reducing valve." Consequently, the manuscript interprets the neurobiological action of classic serotonergic psychedelics (such as psilocybin, LSD, and DMT) as a structural and functional loosening of this interface. This loosening, the author argues, permits a wider, preexisting, and extra-cerebral conscious awareness to manifest, which the manuscript claims accounts for the profound subjective phenomena of ego dissolution, expanded awareness, and mystical-type experiences commonly reported in clinical and naturalistic settings.   

A defining feature of the manuscript’s dialectical strategy is its reliance on the assertion that this transmission interpretation is "empirically equivalent" to physicalist production models, such as the Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics (REBUS) model developed by Carhart-Harris and Friston. Furthermore, the manuscript attempts to position its filter model as a robust alternative to sophisticated contemporary physicalist frameworks, most notably Chris Letheby’s self-fictionalism. It does so by claiming that both the physicalist and the interactionist dualist frameworks inherit the "hard problem" of consciousness equally, supposedly rendering the neuroimaging data an adjudicating stalemate.   

The present pre-publication peer review evaluates this manuscript exclusively on the basis of its empirical claims, its utilization of contemporary scholarly citations, its coverage of robust counterarguments, and the validity of its logical inferences. A rigorous pre-publication audit reveals that the manuscript is fundamentally compromised by severe epistemic overreach, a profound mischaracterization of the neurocomputational models it attempts to co-opt, and a systematic failure to engage with the precision functional mapping and spectral dynamics literature that has categorically redefined psychedelic neuroscience in the 2020s.

This review will demonstrate that the manuscript’s foundational claims do not withstand scrutiny when measured against the state-of-the-art neuroscientific literature. The text constructs a false empirical equivalence by systematically ignoring data that explicitly contradicts the transmission hypothesis, relying on retracted or methodologically fragile neurobiological assumptions, bracketing critical epistemological questions, and leaving the most potent contemporary explanatory frameworks entirely untested. Consequently, it fails to clear the necessary evidentiary, methodological, and theoretical baselines required for publication in a top-tier philosophy of mind or cognitive science journal.

## 2. The Empirical Fragility of "Filter Loosening" in the Era of Precision Functional Mapping

The manuscript’s primary empirical anchor is the foundational assertion that psychedelics decrease activity in the Default Mode Network (DMN) alongside an increase in global brain connectivity. The manuscript interprets this specific neurobiological signature as a direct "loosening" of the neural constraint that normally restricts consciousness to a narrow, biologically utilitarian band of self-referential processing. To support this, the manuscript relies almost exclusively on paradigm-defining but ultimately early-stage fMRI studies, such as the initial 2012 findings by Carhart-Harris and colleagues, to construct its theoretical model of a static biological filter being dismantled by pharmacological intervention.   

This reliance is fatally undermined by the advent of precision functional mapping (PFM) and highly dense longitudinal neuroimaging approaches that have emerged and matured in the 2020s. The manuscript entirely omits the crucial, paradigm-shifting findings of Siegel et al. (2024), who utilized massive longitudinal precision mapping to track individual-specific brain changes before, during, and after high-dose psilocybin administration, collecting approximately eighteen magnetic resonance imaging visits per participant. The Siegel et al. data decisively reframes the architecture of psychedelic action in a manner that is deeply hostile to the filter model's rigid mechanistic assumptions.   

### 2.1 The Problem of Context-Dependent "Grounding" and Filter Integrity

Siegel et al. (2024) demonstrated that while psilocybin does indeed induce a massive, acute disruption of functional connectivity (FC) across the cortex and subcortex—driven by a brain-wide desynchronization that dissolves established network boundaries and flattens the hierarchy of the brain—this state is neither static nor purely pharmacologically determined. Crucially, the researchers found that performing a basic, externally directed perceptual task (such as intentionally focusing visual attention on a crosshair) rapidly reduced the psilocybin-driven functional connectivity changes, effectively reinstating network integrity and actively diminishing the desynchronization.   

This empirical finding introduces a fatal logical contradiction for the filter theory as it is formulated in the manuscript. The text posits that the brain acts as a structural "reducing valve" (the inbound leg of the mind-brain interface) and that 5-HT2A receptor agonism physically "loosens" this valve, allowing an external or independent consciousness to manifest more broadly and with greater phenomenal intensity. If the filter is structurally and pharmacologically impaired by the biochemical presence of psilocybin, top-down cognitive tasks should not be able to instantaneously repair the valve's integrity.   

Under a physicalist production model—specifically predictive processing—this rapid resynchronization is effortlessly and elegantly explained. The external perceptual task demands high-precision sensory sampling. The cognitive system must immediately override the endogenous, relaxed priors that are driving the entropic resting-state hallucinations, shifting the brain rapidly back into a task-positive, functionally segregated configuration to process the incoming visual data. The cognitive system reallocates precision weighting to bottom-up sensory data, overriding the entropic noise of the DMN.   

In stark contrast, the transmission and filter theory possesses no logical or physiological mechanism to explain why a supposedly widened metaphysical channel to "cosmic awareness" is immediately shut the moment the subject is asked to look at a visual target. If the pharmacological agent has loosened the hardware constraints of the brain-interface, engaging in visual attention should merely add localized sensory data to the already expanded phenomenological field. It should not instantaneously reconstitute the DMN's filtering capacity and erase the expanded state. The manuscript's total failure to engage with the phenomenon of "grounding" exposes a fundamental inability of transmission models to account for the dynamic, highly contextual, and task-dependent nature of neuro-network phase transitions under psychedelics.

### 2.2 The Non-Stationarity of the Hippocampal-DMN Decoupling

A further empirical failure in the manuscript's reliance on the DMN relates to the temporal persistence of the psychedelic state and its underlying neuroplastic correlates. The text explicitly assumes a direct, real-time correlation between the suppression of the DMN (the loosening of the filter) and the persistence of the expanded conscious state, equating the physiological state of the network with the phenomenal capacity of the interface.   

However, precision functional mapping has revealed that this relationship is deeply non-stationary. Siegel et al. (2024) observed a persistent decrease in functional connectivity specifically between the anterior hippocampus and the Default Mode Network that lasted for weeks following the acute dosing session, long after the immediate pharmacological half-life of the drug had expired. If DMN suppression and its associated decoupling are the direct neurological correlates of a "loosened filter" that allows for expanded consciousness, the filter model must logically predict that subjects would experience a weeks-long continuation of ego dissolution, mystical awareness, and expanded phenomenology.   

This prediction is empirically false. The acute phenomenological state—the mystical experience, the ego dissolution, the visual hallucination—subsides entirely within a matter of hours, despite the prolonged, objectively measurable neuroplastic alterations in the hippocampal-DMN functional connectivity axis. Production models easily reconcile this temporal dissociation by interpreting the long-term decoupling as a state of heightened neuroplasticity—a "window of malleability" wherein psychological priors and narrative self-models can be cognitively updated without inducing continuous hallucinatory states. The manuscript's transmission model, operating on a rudimentary hydraulic metaphor of "valves" and "filters," cannot account for this dissociation between the neurological state of the "filter" (which remains fundamentally altered for weeks) and the phenomenological state of the subject (which rapidly returns to a rigid, baseline mundane reality).   

## 3. Methodological Blindspots: Broadband fMRI vs. Spectral Dynamics

The manuscript rests its core philosophical argument on the assumption that the neural correlates of the psychedelic state—as determined by functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI)—accurately reflect a functional "suppression" or "inhibition" of specific brain regions, most notably the DMN. This dialectical strategy relies entirely on an uncritical acceptance of the Blood Oxygen Level-Dependent (BOLD) signal as a direct, unconfounded index of neural computation. This stance has been severely and repeatedly criticized in the contemporary literature regarding the neuroscience of psychedelics, rendering the manuscript's neurobiological foundations highly suspect.   

### 3.1 The Obfuscation of Frequency-Specific Alterations

The manuscript treats the brain's activity under psychedelics as a monolithic, unidimensional variable—when "activity" goes down in the DMN, it argues that "filtering" goes down proportionately. This ignores the highly complex, frequency-specific electrophysiological realities of 5-HT2A agonism. Muthukumaraswamy et al. (2021) and subsequent methodological reviews have explicitly warned that broadband fMRI analyses of the psychedelic state are grossly inadequate and often profoundly misleading.   

Muthukumaraswamy’s application of multitaper spectral and cross-spectral analyses to resting-state fMRI data revealed that psilocybin does not simply "suppress" activity. Rather, it produces a highly selective and specific reduction in *low-frequency* spectral power (0.01 – 0.06 Hz) alongside an increase in spectral entropy. The brain does not simply "shut down" its filtering networks; rather, it undergoes a profound spatial and temporal reorganization where low-frequency dynamics are obscured by broadband analyses.   

By ignoring the spectral dimensions of neural desynchronization, the manuscript commits a philosophical fallacy of misplaced concreteness. It treats a highly complex shift in oscillatory phase-coupling as a simple, mechanical dimming of a physical filter. A mathematically and physically rigorous transmission theory must account for how specific temporal frequencies (e.g., alpha band suppression, low-frequency BOLD power drops) map onto the hypothesized metaphysical "channels" of the filter. The manuscript makes no such attempt, relying instead on a coarse-grained, outdated interpretation of fMRI heat maps to justify an expansive metaphysical leap.

### 3.2 The Threat of Neurovascular Decoupling Artifacts

An even more critical omission in the manuscript's empirical audit is the total bracketing of hemodynamic artifacts. The BOLD signal intrinsically relies on the assumption of neurovascular coupling—the premise that localized blood flow accurately and immediately tracks neural metabolic demand. However, classic psychedelics like psilocybin act directly on serotonin 5-HT2A and 5-HT1B receptors, which are not uniquely located on neural tissue. These receptors are densely expressed not only on cortical pyramidal neurons but also on the cerebrovascular endothelium and vascular smooth muscle.   

Studies utilizing arterial spin labeling (ASL) and other advanced perfusion MRI techniques have shown that psilocybin induces sharp, widespread, and potentially confounding changes in cerebral blood flow (CBF) that operate independently of neural spiking activity. What the manuscript confidently and exclusively interprets as a "suppression of the DMN"—and thus the literal opening of a metaphysical valve—may, in significant part, reflect drug-induced cerebrovascular constriction or pharmacologically altered hemodynamic response functions.   

The manuscript fails to acknowledge that its primary neurobiological evidence for "filter loosening" is highly susceptible to profound vascular artifacts. A rigorous pre-publication manuscript in the philosophy of cognitive science must, at the very minimum, address the artifactual fragility of the BOLD signal under serotonergic psychedelics before utilizing it to ground a sweeping interactionist dualist ontology. The failure to do so demonstrates a lack of engagement with the critical methodological limits of modern neuroimaging.

## 4. The Replication Crisis and Retracted Evidence in Psychedelic Science

A major requirement for a top-tier submission is a demonstration that the author is actively engaged with the epistemic health of the field they are analyzing. The manuscript treats the clinical and neurobiological literature surrounding psychedelics as a stable, settled foundation upon which to build a radical new theory of consciousness. This betrays a severe ignorance of the deep methodological crises currently tearing through psychedelic science in the mid-2020s.

The manuscript leans heavily on the purported physiological transformations and durability of psychedelic therapy as evidence of the interface fundamentally reconfiguring itself. However, the author fails to account for the fact that results the field has leaned upon have been directly challenged, heavily caveated, or outright retracted. For instance, the manuscript's assumptions regarding the clean neurobiological correlates of plasticity ignore the fact that high-profile neurobiological foundations are currently unstable, as evidenced by the retraction of a major 2024 meta-analysis regarding the effect of psychedelic drugs on brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) levels.   

Furthermore, van Elk and Fried (2023) have published extensive guidelines addressing the common and pervasive problems in psychedelic science, highlighting the small body of genuinely high-quality evidence and the immense methodological hurdles facing the field. The epistemic limits of randomization and blinding in psychedelic trials are notoriously severe; participants universally know when they have received a high-dose psychedelic versus a placebo, obliterating the blinding that protects against expectancy effects.   

Van Elk and Yaden (2022) emphasize the urgent need to avoid the extrapolation fallacy—assuming that the intense subjective experiences and acute fMRI changes observed in highly controlled, heavily curated experimental cohorts translate cleanly into universal mechanisms of consciousness. The manuscript commits this extrapolation fallacy at a metaphysical scale. It takes statistically fragile, easily confounded neuroimaging data from an unblinded, highly suggestible population and scales it up to assert a universal, non-reductive theory of the mind-brain relationship. By failing to bracket or even acknowledge the replication challenges and methodological crises defined in the 2023-2025 literature, the manuscript builds an ontological tower on a swamp of unstable data.   

## 5. The Incoherence of Co-opting Predictive Processing and the REBUS Model

Perhaps the most conceptually disastrous and logically incoherent move in the manuscript is its attempt to casually appropriate the Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics (REBUS) model, famously authored by Carhart-Harris and Friston (2019). The manuscript claims that REBUS is fully compatible with a filter theory of consciousness, arguing that where REBUS sees a predictive hierarchy relaxing, the filter theory simply "re-reads" this exact same mechanism as the brain's constraining architecture loosening so that a broader consciousness manifests.   

This demonstrates a profound and disqualifying misunderstanding of the mathematical and theoretical foundations of predictive processing, rendering the manuscript's central claim of "empirical equivalence" entirely void.

### 5.1 The Violation of the Markov Blanket

The REBUS model is not a standalone metaphor; it is entirely subordinate to and mathematically derived from the Free Energy Principle (FEP). The FEP is a rigorous formalization that asserts that any self-organizing system that resists environmental entropy must possess a Markov blanket—a statistical boundary that strictly separates the internal states of the system (the brain) from the external states (the environment), mediating interaction solely through sensory inputs and active outputs.   

Under the predictive processing framework, consciousness is intrinsically tied to the process of the internal generative model minimizing prediction errors about the hidden causes of sensory states. The system is fundamentally, mathematically closed; it inferentially guesses the nature of the outside world but it does not "transmit," "channel," or "receive" external consciousness.   

If, as the manuscript argues, the filter is "loosened" to allow an independent consciousness to enter or act upon the brain from the outside (constituting the inbound and outbound legs of interactionist dualism), the Markov blanket is mathematically breached and functionally destroyed. A system that receives raw, unmediated ontological consciousness from beyond its sensory boundary is no longer minimizing variational free energy regarding its sensory states; it is fundamentally broken.   

The following table explicitly demonstrates the strict logical incompatibility between the two models that the manuscript naively attempts to harmonize:

| Theoretical Framework | Boundary Conditions | Mechanism of Action | Ontological Status of the Content | Compatibility with Dualism |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **REBUS / Predictive Processing** | Strict Markov Blanket (Statistical insulation required by the FEP). | Precision-weighting of top-down priors vs. bottom-up prediction errors. | Internally generated by the brain's generative model to explain sensory data. | Fundamentally incompatible; requires a closed neurocomputational loop. |
| **Filter / Transmission Theory** | Permeable Interface (No strict statistical boundary can exist). | Hardware transmission constraints loosening to allow influx of consciousness. | Externally existing, preexisting in a wider, independent cosmic matrix. | Foundationally required (Interactionist dualism). |

The manuscript attempts a philosophical sleight of hand by arguing that relaxing high-level predictive constraints (REBUS) is phenomenologically identical to relaxing a transmission filter. This is a severe category mistake. Relaxing a prior in a Bayesian network simply changes the statistical gain on a prediction error within a closed system; it does not open an ontological portal to an extra-physical dimension. The manuscript cannot co-opt the prestige and neurobiological precision of the REBUS model while simultaneously violating the fundamental mathematical axioms upon which REBUS exclusively relies.   

## 6. The Epistemological Evasion of Chris Letheby’s Self-Fictionalism

The manuscript briefly recognizes Chris Letheby’s seminal work, *Philosophy of Psychedelics* (2021), characterizing it as "the strongest contemporary rival" and the "disciplined physicalist baseline the Map must clear". In his work, Letheby addresses the Comforting Delusion Objection (CDO)—the profound ethical and epistemic concern that psychedelic therapy heals patients by inducing false metaphysical beliefs (e.g., the belief in a cosmic consciousness or a transcendent soul). Letheby successfully rescues the physicalist paradigm by demonstrating that the therapeutic efficacy of psychedelics does not run through metaphysical delusion, but rather through the temporary disruption of the narrative self-model, exposing the self as a pragmatic cognitive construct rather than a fundamental ontological reality.   

The manuscript attempts to neutralize Letheby’s extensive framework by claiming that his account and the filter theory are "empirically equivalent" on the basis of altered-state data alone, arguing that both frameworks inherit the hard problem of consciousness, and therefore, neither is uniquely privileged. This argument is logically hollow and constitutes a deliberate failure to engage with the mechanistic depth of Letheby’s epistemology. By claiming equivalence, the manuscript brackets the question of epistemic justification entirely.   

### 6.1 The Metaphysical Alief Theory vs. Ontological Transmission

The manuscript completely ignores Letheby's highly specific psychological mechanism for *how* the delusion objection is circumvented: the Metaphysical Alief Theory. Letheby argues that patients do not necessarily form justified, propositional *beliefs* about cosmic consciousness; rather, they form *aliefs*—mental representations of the world that are not reflectively endorsed or believed upon sober reflection, but nonetheless heavily influence thought, emotion, and action during and after the experience.   

A physicalist model incorporating aliefs elegantly explains why a terminal cancer patient or an individual suffering from treatment-resistant depression can experience a "Joyous Cosmology" during the acute drug phase, derive immense therapeutic benefit from the emotional reset, and yet return to a strict materialist worldview post-integration, recognizing the experience as a profound but ultimately internal neurochemical event.   

The manuscript's filter theory utterly fails to account for this cognitive plasticity and epistemic nuance. If the drug truly loosens the filter, granting the subject direct, veridical access to an external, independent cosmic consciousness, then the subject's post-trip realization that it was "just a hallucination" must be viewed by the filter theorist as a tragic re-closing of the epistemic valve—a return to falsehood. Yet, therapeutic outcomes are completely independent of the subject adopting the ontological claims of the filter theory.   

By failing to engage with Letheby's vital distinction between the *process of discovery* (having a profound new mental state) and the *process of justification* (whether that state constitutes actual knowledge), the manuscript brackets the core epistemological debate of the field. The filter model claims that the content of the psychedelic experience is ontologically real, while Letheby proves that the content only needs to be psychologically efficacious. Because Letheby's physicalist model achieves the exact same therapeutic explanatory power without invoking the immense ontological extravagance of interactionist dualism, the two frameworks are categorically *not* empirically or epistemologically equivalent. The manuscript violates Occam's Razor without providing any compensating explanatory advantage.   

### 6.2 The Problem of Autobiographical Content

A devastating and untested vulnerability of the filter theory—which Letheby’s predictive processing model easily handles—is the origin of the phenomenological content during the psychedelic state. If the brain is merely an interface receiving a broadcast, and the DMN is the filter that restricts this broadcast into a narrow "narrative self", then dismantling the DMN should expose the subject to the raw, unfiltered broadcast of the external universe.

Why, then, is the psychedelic experience overwhelmingly populated by the subject’s own repressed autobiographical memories, culturally conditioned archetypes, specific semantic memories, and immediate environmental stimuli (set and setting)? Van Elk and Yaden (2022) have extensively documented the psychological mechanisms underlying psychedelics, emphasizing the extreme malleability of the experience based on prior expectations, cultural priming, and the clinical setting.   

If the brain is not a generator, where are these deeply personal, highly constructed narrative hallucinations coming from? The transmission theory is forced into the absurd, undefended position of claiming that the external, independent "cosmic consciousness" just so happens to be continuously broadcasting the exact childhood trauma of the individual whose brain-filter is currently loosened.

Letheby's predictive processing model, conversely, perfectly predicts this phenomenon: as high-level priors (the DMN and associated networks) relax, the brain frantically attempts to minimize prediction errors by constructing ad-hoc narratives using whatever lower-level memories, emotional valences, and environmental cues are available in the system. The hallucination is generated *internally*, shaped entirely by the subject's unique neurocognitive history. The manuscript completely ignores this "problem of content," offering no counterargument for how an inbound transmission filter accounts for the highly individualized, culturally bounded nature of psychedelic phenomenology.   

## 7. The False Dialectical Shield of "Empirical Equivalence"

Throughout the manuscript, the author utilizes a rhetorical maneuver characterized as "dialectical honesty"—repeatedly conceding that the neuroimaging data does not *force* the filter reading, and that sophisticated production models can accommodate the findings. The manuscript states that the data is merely "congenial" to filter theory and that the two accounts are "empirically equivalent," choosing to bracket the question of final proof.   

In rigorous academic philosophy of science, this is an unacceptable dialectical shield. Asserting that a non-falsifiable metaphysical framework (interactionist dualism) is "empirically equivalent" to a highly parameterized, mathematically grounded computational model (predictive processing/REBUS) is a false equivalence.

A physicalist production model makes specific, falsifiable predictions about neural behavior. For example, it predicts that introducing a secondary pharmacological agent to block 5-HT2A receptors (e.g., ketanserin) will immediately abort the hallucinatory state, as the hardware generating the prediction errors is blocked. It predicts that specific perceptual tasks will resynchronize the DMN. The filter theory, as presented in the manuscript, makes no such precise neurobiological predictions. It merely operates as a post-hoc interpretive overlay, passively observing the data generated by physicalists and appending the non-falsifiable claim, "This could also be a filter loosening."   

A theory that effortlessly accommodates every possible data point explains absolutely nothing. Because the filter model lacks independent, quantitative criteria for what constitutes "interface friction" or "bandwidth," it cannot be rigorously tested against the data. It is, to utilize the terminology of Herbert Feigl, a "nomological dangler"—a hypothesis that dangles from the scientific framework without doing any actual explanatory or predictive work. The manuscript's repeated admissions of empirical equivalence are not evidence of dialectical honesty; they are explicit admissions of epistemic bankruptcy. By constantly bracketing the requirement for exclusive, determinative evidence, the manuscript abdicates the primary responsibility of theoretical philosophy.   

## 8. The Intensity-Bandwidth Fallacy and the Dimensions of Consciousness

A final, fatal weakness is the manuscript's consistent equation of the subjective intensity of the psychedelic experience—its "richer, more vivid" nature—with an overall widening of the theoretical filter. The text argues that because the DMN is suppressed but consciousness is phenomenologically "expanded," the naive physicalist production model is refuted, leaving the filter theory as the superior organizing framework.   

This argument relies entirely on an obsolete, monolithic conception of consciousness that has been thoroughly dismantled in the modern literature. Seth and Bayne (2022) systematically outline the multidimensionality of consciousness, proving that consciousness is not a single volume dial or a solitary "channel" that goes "up" or "down".   

### 8.1 The Disaggregation of Phenomenal Dimensions

Consciousness comprises multiple, frequently dissociable dimensions. These include perceptual vividness, metacognitive accuracy, volitional control (agency), working memory integration, and temporal binding. During a high-dose psychedelic state, while perceptual vividness and emotional arousal may radically spike to near-maximum capacity, other critical dimensions of consciousness—such as metacognition, reality testing, volitional agency, and the ability to distinguish self from other—precipitously collapse.   

The manuscript commits what must be termed the "Intensity-Bandwidth Fallacy." It erroneously assumes that because the sensory and emotional phenomenology is intensely loud (a massive spike in one or two dimensions), the overall "bandwidth" of the filter must have widened to let more reality in. However, from a multidimensional perspective, the brain's functional capacity to actually integrate information, sustain directed thought, and parse reality is severely, toxically degraded.   

Table 2 illustrates the multidimensional shifts occurring during 5-HT2A agonism, exposing the profound inadequacy of the manuscript's monolithic "widening" metaphor:

| Dimension of Consciousness (per Seth & Bayne) | State during High-Dose Psychedelic | Interpretation under Production Models | Interpretation under Filter Models |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Perceptual Vividness** | Highly Increased (Hyper-associative visual processing) | Disinhibition of visual cortex; overriding of top-down predictive priors. | "Widened filter" allowing more transmission. |
| **Metacognitive Accuracy** | Severely Decreased (Delusional, loss of reality testing) | Collapse of higher-order thought and precision weighting mechanisms. | Unexplained. Why does an "expanded" consciousness lose basic self-awareness? |
| **Volitional Control (Agency)** | Severely Decreased (Ego dissolution, loss of motor intent) | Disruption of motor/action-oriented predictive hierarchies. | Unexplained. If the filter opens, why does the subject completely lose agency? |
| **Episodic Memory Retrieval** | Distorted / Spontaneously Hyper-associative | Spontaneous firing of hippocampal assemblies without DMN constraint. | Contradictory. Requires the external cosmic broadcast to contain the subject's internal personal memories. |

If the filter theory were correct, and the brain was merely dropping constraints on an already coherent, independent, higher-order consciousness, we should expect a *global* enhancement of all conscious dimensions. The subject should become *more* metacognitively precise, *more* volitionally capable, and *more* rationally integrated as they access this cosmic reality. Instead, the psychedelic state closely resembles the chaotic, entropic breakdown of a biological generator. The manuscript selectively highlights the dimensions that feel "expanded" (vividness, ego dissolution) to support its thesis, while completely and dishonestly bracketing the severe cognitive impairments that directly contradict the "widened access" hypothesis.

## 9. Weaknesses Justifying Rejection

1. **Failure to Account for Task-Dependent Resynchronization ("Grounding"):** The manuscript models the psychedelic state as a structural "loosening" of a neural filter, but fundamentally fails to address how externally directed attention (a simple perceptual task) can instantaneously resynchronize the supposedly impaired DMN and restore network integrity, an observation that heavily favors top-down predictive processing models over static transmission theories.
2. **Obfuscation of Spectral Dynamics and Hemodynamic Artifacts:** By relying solely on broadband fMRI and the BOLD signal to claim a generalized "suppression" of brain networks, the manuscript ignores critical findings that psilocybin induces highly specific low-frequency spectral power shifts and severe cerebrovascular perfusion alterations, masking the complex temporal dynamics that contradict a simple spatial filter.
3. **Ignorance of Retractions and the Replication Crisis:** The manuscript builds expansive metaphysical claims on the assumed durability and physiological realities of psychedelic therapy, completely ignoring the replication crisis plaguing the field, the epistemic limits of unblinded RCTs, and the recent retraction of major meta-analyses regarding psychedelic neuroplasticity mechanisms like BDNF.
4. **Logical Contradiction in Co-opting the REBUS Model:** The manuscript claims theoretical compatibility with the Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics (REBUS) model, ignoring that REBUS is strictly governed by the Free Energy Principle, which mathematically requires a closed Markov blanket—a boundary condition fundamentally violated by the manuscript's proposed inbound transmission of external consciousness.
5. **Epistemological Evasion of the Metaphysical Alief Theory:** In its attempt to counter physicalist explanations of psychedelic therapy, the manuscript brackets Chris Letheby's mechanism of "aliefs" and epistemic innocence—which perfectly explains therapeutic efficacy without requiring ontological truth—falsely claiming empirical equivalence without successfully disproving the superior parsimony of Letheby's non-delusional self-fictionalism.
6. **The "Intensity-Bandwidth" Phenomenological Fallacy:** The manuscript falsely equates heightened sensory vividness and ego dissolution with an overall "expansion" of consciousness, leaving untested the counterargument from multidimensional models of consciousness, which show that severe degradations of metacognition and volitional agency during psychedelic states strongly indicate the entropic breakdown of a generative model rather than the unconstrained transmission of a coherent awareness.

This manuscript is entirely unfit for academic submission as it relies on a superficial, obsolete reading of neuroimaging data and obfuscates severe logical contradictions in its attempt to marry interactionist dualism with contemporary predictive processing mathematics.
