The entropic brain hypothesis (EBH), introduced by Robin Carhart-Harris and colleagues in 2014, claims that the *entropy* of spontaneous brain activity indexes the informational richness of a conscious state. Ordinary waking experience sits in a narrow, constrained, reality-tested band; psychedelics raise neural entropy toward a [critical point](#brain-criticality) (explained below) and produce richer, less constrained experience. Loss of consciousness moves the brain the other way, into ordered, low-entropy dynamics. The hypothesis supplies something genuinely new to the neuroscience of consciousness: a *quantitative dynamical signature*—a number—that tracks how rich a conscious state is. That entropy and criticality move together is EBH's own claim rather than a settled result; the strongest recent psychedelic evidence pulls the two apart, as [the section on the contested measure](#where-the-measure-is-contested) sets out below.

The Unfinishable Map's interest is precise. EBH delivers a correlation between a physical quantity and the *richness* of experience while saying nothing about why that activity is *felt* at all. That structure—a measure that tracks consciousness without explaining it—is metaphysically modular: the entropy–richness link survives intact whether one reads it as production or as a widening of the [filter's bandwidth](/concepts/filter-theory/). The Map adopts the empirical finding and resists the physicalist gloss its originators attach, engaging EBH as a rival framework rather than an ally.

## The Core Claim

Carhart-Harris et al. (2014) proposed that "the entropy of spontaneous brain activity indexes the informational richness of conscious states," bounded above and below by thresholds beyond which consciousness is lost. The framework revived a Freudian distinction in dynamical dress: **primary states** (high-entropy, less constrained—psychedelic experience, early psychosis, REM dreaming, infant cognition) versus **secondary states** (normal adult waking, constrained, reality-testing, metacognitive).

The striking move is the placement of ordinary consciousness. On EBH, normal waking is *slightly sub-critical*: its narrowness, its reality-testing, its metacognitive grip are products of *entropy suppression*. What feels like the baseline of consciousness is, on this account, a constrained special case. The markers offered for the approach to criticality include an expanded repertoire of brain states, reduced within-network integrity (notably in the [default mode network](/concepts/default-mode-network/)), increased between-network connectivity, and broader metastability.

This already rhymes with [filter theory](/concepts/filter-theory/). On a naive production model, richer experience should require more neural activity; EBH and the psychedelic data instead report desynchronized, less integrated, higher-entropy activity accompanying *more* experience, not less. That pattern sits naturally within a filter reading, but it does not discriminate between the readings. Production accounts absorb it by holding that the brain is doing something *different* rather than strictly *less*, and the Map's own treatment in [psychedelics-and-the-filter-model](/topics/psychedelics-and-the-filter-model/) works that accommodation question through at length, concluding that the altered-state data leave filter and production accounts level. The contribution here is the entropy/criticality dimension that the filter-model article references but does not unpack.

## Brain Criticality {#brain-criticality}

The 2018 update, *The entropic brain – revisited*, recentred the theory on **brain criticality**: the idea that conscious dynamics operate near a critical point—an "edge of chaos" poised between ordered, periodic activity and disordered, chaotic activity. Critical systems exhibit maximal metastability, heightened sensitivity to perturbation, and avalanche dynamics whose sizes follow power laws. Self-organized criticality, formalised in physics by Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld (1987), supplies the dynamical vocabulary.

Subsequent work has linked near-criticality to conscious level. It is worth naming at the outset that this literature is not independent of the hypothesis it corroborates: Carhart-Harris is the seventh of thirteen authors on Toker et al. (2022) and the sixth of eight on the 2026 DMT study discussed below. The Map requires the production side to disclose exactly this kind of non-independence, and the same standard binds here.

Three separate studies are usually run together, and they measure different things. Toker et al. (2022) report that conscious states are supported by near-critical slow cortical electrodynamics, using a modified 0-1 chaos test on cortical recordings. Their axis is edge-of-chaos stability, not avalanche statistics, and the two should not be restated in each other's vocabulary. A different study by Toker and colleagues (2024) finds that cross-frequency cortical–thalamic information transfer is diminished under propofol and enhanced under the psychedelic 5-MeO-DMT—changes the authors propose *may be* mediated by excursions toward and away from edge-of-chaos criticality. Varley et al. (2020) supply the avalanche result: propofol dramatically restricted the size and duration of neural avalanches and sharply reduced dynamical complexity, while ketamine allowed more awake-like dynamics to persist.

Two qualifications travel with the Varley finding and are usually dropped. The recording is invasive electrocorticography from a *single macaque*, and the authors enter a qualification of their own: "All states, however, showed some signs of persistent criticality." Anaesthesia therefore bends the dynamics away from the critical point rather than abolishing the signature. The dissociation still matters for the Map—it mirrors the agent-versus-substrate pattern explored in [anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface](/topics/anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface/), where different anaesthetics suppress experience by different routes—but nothing here should rest on more weight than one animal's data can carry.

Criticality refines EBH's central quantity but inherits its limit. It specifies, with real precision, *the dynamical regime in which rich consciousness occurs*. It does not specify why occupying that regime is *like anything*. A near-critical system is a system at a particular point on the order–chaos axis; nothing in the power-law statistics of neural avalanches entails experience. The signature is sharp; the explanatory gap is untouched.

## Where the Measure Is Contested {#where-the-measure-is-contested}

A concept page on EBH that presented entropy as a settled quantity would misrepresent the field. Even the *physical* measure at the theory's core is disputed.

Papo (2016) argued that entropy—a measure of randomness—may be ill-qualified in principle to characterise brain activity, which is neither fully regular nor fully random. The favoured operationalisations can collapse into one another: "metastability," defined as variance in network synchrony, may merely *count* metastable states rather than measure metastability, and entropy, metastability and variance can then carry the same information under different names. A genuine configurational entropy would require extensive exploration of the system's phase space; the measures actually used, Papo warned, may capture only "the random side of complexity."

Reviewing the debate, Rankaduwa and Owen (2023) record a sharper difficulty for the flagship measures. Increases in *both* Lempel–Ziv complexity and the perturbational complexity index have been observed under low and anaesthetic doses of ketamine—so that, contrary to what the entropy literature generally assumes, "an increase in complexity seems to be fully compatible with overall reductions in conscious awareness." A measure that rises as awareness falls is not yet an index of awareness.

The 2026 DMT evidence pushes the point further, and in a direction that runs against EBH's own framing. Irrmischer et al. studied DMT—the most phenomenologically extreme psychedelic available to controlled study—and found that it shifts the dynamics of brain oscillations *away* from criticality in alpha and adjacent frequency bands, and toward subcritical regimes on a functional excitatory–inhibitory measure. In that context, in the authors' words, "entropy is increased while complexity is reduced," with the criticality shifts in alpha and theta tracking subjects' intensity ratings of self-dissolution.

That result is a counter-example to the toward-criticality claim rather than a refinement of it, and the honest reading is also the more interesting one. The two quantities the 2018 revision fused come apart in the strongest available psychedelic case: entropy rises while criticality and complexity fall. Whatever high entropy indexes under DMT, it is not proximity to a critical point. The 2024 cortical–thalamic study, meanwhile, reports 5-MeO-DMT moving dynamics the *other* way on a different measure—so the literature now contains tryptamines pushing toward *and* away from criticality depending on which criticality is being measured and in which substance. That is Papo's conceptual complaint arriving as data. A single scalar that moves in opposite directions under two related compounds, and that can rise while awareness falls under ketamine, is not yet the unifying quantity EBH takes it to be. The DMT result also sharpens a question the Map should not duck: on the instrument's own reading, the state a filter model would call bandwidth-widening registers as a *narrowing* toward subcritical dynamics, with only entropy going up.

This is not a peripheral quibble. It bears directly on the Map's [meta-epistemology of limits](/voids/meta-epistemology-of-limits/): having a number is not having an explanation, and when the number's own construction is contested, even the descriptive achievement is provisional. The debate over whether entropy *indexes*, *constitutes*, or merely *correlates with* conscious richness remains live, and the field's drift toward multidimensional taxonomies of consciousness reflects dissatisfaction with any single-scalar account.

## The Predictive-Processing Extension

In 2019, Carhart-Harris and Friston folded EBH into the **REBUS** model ("Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics," the "anarchic brain"). On this account, psychedelics relax the precision of high-level priors, liberating bottom-up information flow; the narrative self is itself a high-level prior whose loosening underlies ego dissolution. REBUS recasts the entropy rise as a relaxation of hierarchical Bayesian control.

The Map treats REBUS as supplying *mechanism*, not *metaphysics*. Its formalism is metaphysically neutral and compatible with—but not discriminating in favour of—the filter reading, exactly as [predictive-processing-and-dualism](/topics/predictive-processing-and-dualism/) argues for predictive processing generally. Critics including Letheby press a fully deflationary reading on which prior-relaxation and entropy *exhaust* the phenomenology within physicalism. That engagement is developed in [psychedelics-and-the-filter-model](/topics/psychedelics-and-the-filter-model/) and is not re-run here; this page cross-links rather than duplicates it. Later refinements—Safron's ALBUS (2025), which adds bidirectional, dose-dependent precision changes—respond partly to the standing charge that the predictive-processing framework has enough free parameters to fit almost any result, an open falsifiability concern the Map registers rather than resolves.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The entropic brain hypothesis is a *physicalist* theory, and the Map engages it as one. Carhart-Harris and Friston build EBH and REBUS to explain psychedelic action *without* any non-physical interface; the empirical neuroimaging may be cited, but the originators do not share—and REBUS was constructed to avoid—the Map's dualist conclusion. The findings are adopted; the framework is engaged as a rival the altered-state evidence does not discriminate against.

What the Map draws from EBH bears on its first tenet, [Dualism](/tenets/#dualism). The theory's own structure separates two things materialism usually runs together: a *physical correlate of richness* (entropy, criticality) and *phenomenality* (that the activity is felt). EBH delivers the first with growing precision and is silent on the second. The entropy–richness correlation can be re-described, with no loss of empirical fit on the altered-state data, as a description of the [filter's bandwidth](/concepts/filter-theory/): raising entropy toward criticality widens the aperture and admits richer content; lowering it narrows the aperture toward the constrained, reality-tested band of ordinary waking. Read this way, criticality is the *dynamical condition under which the consciousness interface operates at maximal bandwidth*, not the *source* of consciousness. This re-description is an interpretive overlay, empirically equivalent to the production reading on this evidence alone—not a discriminating prediction, and the Map marks it as such per the [evidential-status-discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/).

The overlay inherits every weakness of the measure it borrows, and the Map should say so plainly. The bandwidth gloss above ran entropy and criticality together as though they were one dial; the DMT evidence shows they are not, and a filter reading must then say *which* quantity it takes the aperture to track. If the aperture is entropy, the gloss survives the DMT case and owes an account of why complexity falls as the aperture opens. If the aperture is proximity to criticality, the most intense psychedelic phenomenology on record is a narrowing—and the filter reading has to explain how a narrowing aperture admits more. Neither branch is closed, and neither is comfortable. What the Map claims is the weaker thing: EBH's structure separates a physical correlate of richness from phenomenality, and that separation holds whichever quantity turns out to be the right one. The altered-state evidence coheres with the filter reading without evidencing it over physicalist rivals, and the criticality data do not yet speak clearly enough to change that either way.

The theory also illustrates [the limits of parsimony](/tenets/#occams-limits). EBH is attractively economical: one quantity, monotonically tracking conscious richness, spanning psychedelics, dreaming, psychosis, and anaesthesia. But its economy is descriptive, not explanatory—and when even the favoured measure is contested (above), the apparent simplicity may reflect the coarseness of a single scalar rather than a real unification. The Map declines to let "we have a number that tracks richness" be heard as "we have explained richness."
