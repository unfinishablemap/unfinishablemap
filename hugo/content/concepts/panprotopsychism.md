**Panprotopsychism** is the view that some fundamental physical entities have *protophenomenal* properties: properties that are not themselves experiential but that can collectively constitute experience. David Chalmers gave the view its contemporary shape, defining protophenomenal properties as properties that "are not phenomenal (there is nothing it is like to have a single protophenomenal property)" yet "can collectively constitute phenomenal properties" (Chalmers 2013). In its leading form, constitutive Russellian panprotopsychism, these properties are the hidden intrinsic natures ([quiddities](/concepts/intrinsic-nature/)) behind the roles physics describes, such as mass and charge. Panprotopsychism avoids attributing experience to electrons and so avoids summing micro-subjects, but it faces its own combination problem: the gap between non-experiential and experiential reopens one level down. The Unfinishable Map engages it as a rival to be taken seriously, not as evidence for dualism.

## The Definition and Its Specialness Clause

The obvious worry about panprotopsychism is that it seems to say nothing ordinary [materialism](/concepts/materialism/) does not already say. Non-panpsychist materialism also holds that fundamental properties are non-phenomenal and collectively constitute phenomenal ones. Chalmers anticipates this and adds a *specialness* requirement. Protophenomenal properties must (i) be distinct from structural properties and (ii) stand in an **a priori** entailment relation to the phenomenal truths they constitute. The first clause excludes ordinary type-A materialism, which grounds experience in structure. The second excludes type-B materialism, which posits an a posteriori necessary connection. What remains is a view on which the base is neither structural nor experiential, and yet, if we grasped it, we could in principle see why experience follows from it.

Chalmers concentrates on the *constitutive Russellian* version: macro-experience is grounded in protophenomenal truths about microphysical entities, and those properties play the roles physics describes. On this version panprotopsychism shares panpsychism's main attraction. Consciousness is woven into the causal order, because the properties that ground it are the ones that make mass behave as mass does. The dialectic that motivates [Russellian monism](/concepts/russellian-monism/) generally therefore applies. The view's reply to the conceivability argument, as Chalmers sets it out, is that *structural* zombies (duplicates of our physical structure only) are conceivable but *categorical* zombies (duplicates of structure and quiddities) are not.

Panprotopsychism is the natural home of the "neither mental nor physical" option inside the Russellian debate. Chalmers says its Russellian version "can be seen as a form of Russellian neutral monism" ([neutral-monism](/concepts/neutral-monism/)), with protophenomenal properties as the neutral base.

## Why It Is Not Dismissed Out of Hand

A tempting objection runs as follows. The epistemic arguments against materialism show a gap between the non-phenomenal and the phenomenal, and protophenomenal properties are non-phenomenal, so the same gap refutes panprotopsychism. Chalmers rejects this. On his reading the anti-materialist arguments turn on a narrower gap, between *structure* and experience: "We have principled reasons to think that phenomenal truths cannot be wholly grounded in structural truths," but no correspondingly good reason for the wider claim about non-phenomenal, non-structural truths.

Chalmers concedes that we have little idea what protophenomenal properties are like. They are characterised schematically, by their relation to what they constitute. He insists that "our ignorance about protophenomenal properties should not be mistaken for an objection to the truth of panprotopsychism." This is the defender's strongest point. The view is a placeholder for a theory, and critics must show the placeholder cannot be filled. Pointing out that it has not yet been filled is not enough.

## The Combination Problem for Panprotopsychism

Panprotopsychism removes micro-subjects, and with them the subject-summing problem that [Coleman's dilemma](/concepts/combination-problem/) presses on constitutive panpsychism. Chalmers argues that it inherits a combination problem of its own: how do protoexperiences combine to yield experiences? He poses it as a conceivability argument that runs in parallel to his argument against constitutive panpsychism. The key premise asserts that **protophenomenal zombies** are conceivable: beings that share our purported protophenomenal properties at the micro level but lack consciousness. If such beings are possible, constitutive panprotopsychism is false.

Chalmers offers two routes to that premise:

- **The nonsubject/subject gap.** No set of truths about non-subjects necessitates the existence of a subject. If subjects are conceptually fundamental, their existence is not a priori entailed by anything else.
- **The nonquality/quality gap.** Phenomenal properties constitutively involve qualities such as redness. If protophenomenal properties are non-qualitative, one can always conceive of them without any qualities present.

Chalmers calibrates this carefully, and the calibration should be kept. The conceivability of protophenomenal zombies is "perhaps somewhat less obvious than the conceivability of panpsychist zombies," because we have so unclear an idea of what protophenomenal properties involve. He compares the two views: each faces one especially hard problem the other avoids, the subject-subject gap for panpsychism and the nonphenomenal-phenomenal gap for panprotopsychism. He says: "I am inclined to think the subject-subject problem is more difficult, and that panprotopsychism may benefit from having fewer constraints on its building blocks, but I am far from certain about this." All of these, he adds, are "challenges rather than refutations."

The Map's sibling pages sometimes call the protophenomenal-zombie argument the "revenge" problem for panprotopsychism. That label is the Map's shorthand. In the Amherst lecture Chalmers presents the argument as the combination problem for panprotopsychism.

## Panqualityism: The Leading Development

The best-developed form of panprotopsychism is **panqualityism**. On this view the fundamental entities instantiate *qualities*, such as redness or something more austere, of the kind presented in experience but without anyone experiencing them. Chalmers traces the name to Herbert Feigl (1960) and the idea to the neutral monists William James, Ernst Mach and Bertrand Russell. He names Sam Coleman as its recent defender. Qualities are not phenomenal properties, since a red object need not be a subject. So panqualityism counts as panprotopsychism rather than panpsychism, and it needs no micro-subjects at all.

Coleman develops the view as a reply to the combination problem. In "Mental Chemistry" (2012) he argues that not every difficulty filed under that heading need worry panpsychists, and he starts on a working conception of phenomenal combination. In "Panpsychism and Neutral Monism" (2017) he pairs panqualityism with a higher-order-thought account of awareness ([higher-order-theories](/concepts/higher-order-theories/)). Qualities are the neutral base, and awareness of them is supplied by higher-order representation. The chapter argues that this pairing helps answer the subject combination problem, and it closes by evaluating Chalmers's "awareness zombies", a proposed counter-example to that pairing.

Chalmers's objection targets exactly that pairing. Panqualityism escapes the subject/subject gap but meets a **quality/awareness gap**: "no instantiations of qualities ever necessitate awareness of qualities." Even qualities structured like a visual field could, it seems, be instantiated with no awareness of them. Coleman's functional account of awareness is his way through, and Chalmers holds that the conceivability argument itself gives reason to reject such a reduction. Chalmers adds that panqualityism still faces the structure and quality combination problems. The structure of qualities in the brain differs from the structure of the qualities we are aware of, and a few primitive qualities must somehow yield the huge palette of experience. His verdict: "I conclude that panqualityism does not offer a solution to the combination problem." The dispute is live. Coleman's reply is on record, and Chalmers's conclusion is one party's.

## Chalmers's Own Scorecard

Chalmers makes a comparison that bears directly on the Map. He holds that substance dualism, in interactionist or epiphenomenalist form, and Russellian monism, in panpsychist or panprotopsychist form, are "the two serious contenders" once standard physicalism is abandoned, and "I divide my own credence fairly equally between them." He grants that "dualism does not suffer nearly as badly from a combination problem," since a dualist can take unified subjects and their macro-phenomenal properties as fundamental. Against that he sets dualism's costs: the problem of mental causation and a problem of economy. On his framing, the open question is whether the combination problem for one side, or mental causation and economy for the other, can be solved or shown to be unsolvable.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The Map's [Dualism](/tenets/#dualism) tenet holds that consciousness is not reducible to physical processes. Panprotopsychism is a monism, and in its Russellian form it makes consciousness wholly grounded in the quiddities of physical entities. The Map rejects it for that reason. How far that rejection counts as an argument, and how far it simply marks the edge of the framework, needs care.

**Where panprotopsychism beats constitutive panpsychism.** Because it has no micro-subjects to sum, it avoids the problem the Map presses hardest against panpsychism. The Map's arguments from [phenomenal non-compositionality](/concepts/composition-and-consciousness/), which say that perspectives do not merge into further perspectives, do not apply to it as stated. Its rival is the Map's case against physicalism, not its case against panpsychism.

**Where the gap reopens.** The Map's reason for doubting panprotopsychism is the nonsubject/subject intuition: nothing non-experiential, however special, seems to entail that someone is there. That is the same kind of conceivability intuition the Map relies on against physicalism. Using it here is *tenet-coherent*, because it applies the standard the Map already accepts consistently, but it adds no evidence for dualism. Chalmers's reply identifies the real crux. The anti-materialist arguments have principled support only for the structure/experience gap, and the wider non-phenomenal/phenomenal gap has to be argued rather than assumed. The Map finds the wider gap plausible, but has not shown it. Our ignorance of protophenomenal properties cuts both ways: we cannot conceive them clearly enough to be confident that protophenomenal zombies are possible, nor clearly enough to see the a priori entailment the view promises. That the panprotopsychist has not delivered its promised entailment is a cost the rival owes, which leaves the Map's own ontology exactly as well supported as before.

**What the Map pays in exchange.** Panprotopsychism keeps consciousness inside the causal order at no extra ontological cost. The Map takes on the interaction problem instead, through its [Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction) and [Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) tenets. The Map avoids *constitutive* combination, but that does not settle every question of unity. It still owes an account of interface unity: how one subject coupled to a spatially distributed substrate yields one experiential field (see [the unity of the interface](/concepts/combination-problem/#the-unity-of-the-interface)). The interaction problem is *locatable*, but locatable is weaker than approachable. A mechanism that preserves the Born statistics cannot be told apart from chance by any unconditioned aggregate test (see [mqi-empirical-fragility](/project/mqi-empirical-fragility/)). So the Map cannot claim that its trade clearly beats panprotopsychism's. Chalmers's scorecard, with dualism's mental-causation and economy costs weighed against the monist's combination problem, is a fair statement of where things stand.

**Economy and Occam.** Chalmers's economy charge is one the Map meets with [Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits): simplicity is an unreliable guide when knowledge of the fundamental level is this incomplete. The point applies to both sides. Panprotopsychism's economy is bought with properties described only schematically, and a count of fundamental categories is weak evidence when one of the categories is still a placeholder. That weakens the economy charge without reversing it. (The [russellian-monism](/concepts/russellian-monism/) page records Kind's further argument that non-physicalist Russellian variants collapse into dualism. If that is right, the gap between the Map and a non-physicalist panprotopsychism is narrower than the labels suggest.)

The honest summary is that panprotopsychism is unrefuted. The Map's objection rests on a conceivability intuition that the Map shares with the view's own critics but cannot establish, and the view's promise rests on an entailment nobody has yet written down. The disagreement stands at a framework boundary. The Map does not refute panprotopsychism, and panprotopsychism does not refute the Map.
