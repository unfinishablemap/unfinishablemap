The coupling-engagement condition is the Unfinishable Map's name for whether the non-physical mind–brain coupling **holds at all** for a given physical system — as distinct from *how* it works, from *what* it can accomplish once it holds, and from *which* systems have it. Two articles now rest a defence of Tenet 4 on this construct. [The phenomenal-sorites problem](/concepts/phenomenal-sorites-problem/) installs it as the truth-maker for the sharp on/off fact about whether a subject exists, and [is-conscious-being-a-natural-kind](/concepts/is-conscious-being-a-natural-kind/) grounds its sharp natural-kind joint on the same relation, described there as "a simple, non-graded relation that either holds or does not." Until now the construct existed only as a clause in each.

Two qualifications belong in the opening rather than at the end. First, the Map does not claim to have *shown* that engagement is non-graded; it posits a non-graded relation because [Antony's conditional](#antonys-constraint) blocks the graded alternatives, which is a framework-relative move rather than a demonstration. Second, one seam stays open here rather than closing: [universal-coupling-response](/concepts/universal-coupling-response/) calls the architectural-adequacy boundary vague while the sorites article needs engagement to be sharp, and the two pages have never been reconciled in print. Setting that seam out precisely is most of what this article can honestly do.

## Four Questions About One Coupling

The Map's interface vocabulary runs four questions together, and separating them is what gives the engagement condition a subject of its own.

**Engagement — does the coupling hold?** Whether there is a mind coupled to this system at all. The answer fixes whether a subject exists, and nothing else in the framework does.

**Mode — how does influence travel?** [coupling-modes](/concepts/coupling-modes/) asks whether consciousness exercises basis control, timing control, or probability control over quantum indeterminacies. Every mode presupposes that the coupling already holds; the question is what shape the traffic takes.

**Efficacy — what can the coupling do?** [The interface threshold](/concepts/interface-threshold/) marks where an already-engaged coupling becomes rich enough to *select among* neural patterns rather than merely accompany them. Both wing articles are careful that this boundary "has phenomenal consciousness present on both sides": it settles what a subject's experience can accomplish, not whether a subject is there.

**Scope — which systems couple?** [The universal coupling response](/concepts/universal-coupling-response/) answers that consciousness couples with every system meeting adequate architectural conditions, rejecting both a threshold drawn among adequate-looking systems and an unexplained selectivity among them.

Engagement and efficacy are easy to conflate because both are binary. The interface threshold describes itself as "a *qualitative* feature of the coupling — whether selection-grade interaction is supported at all," which sounds like the engagement condition and is not: the switch there is on *selection-grade interaction*, and a system on the wrong side of it still has a subject. Only engagement's switch decides whether there is anyone home.

## Antony's Constraint {#antonys-constraint}

The engagement condition was not chosen for elegance. It is what remained after two other routes were declined.

Epistemicism about ordinary vague predicates grounds its sharp boundaries in facts about use: on Williamson's (1994) account the cutoff for "bald" is fixed, by default rather than decision, by the totality of how competent speakers deploy the word. No comparable use-facts fix whether a system is conscious, so epistemicism about consciousness owes a truth-maker that Williamson's version got for free. Sorensen's (2001) truthmaker-gap route answers that the sharp fact needs no ground at all; Jago (2012) argues it cannot accommodate the higher-order vagueness Sorensen himself insists on, and the Map declines it independently, on the ground that an ungrounded phenomenal fact is more mysterious rather than less.

The second route — ground the boundary in a graded physical property — runs into Antony (2006). Antony argues that if the concept of consciousness is both sharp and correct, then identity theory, functionalism, and dualism are all false, because each makes consciousness supervene on a vague physical or functional base, and nothing sharp supervenes on something vague. A graded realizer would in any case reintroduce the sorites at its own switch-on point. The conditional cuts against most dualisms too, which is why the Map's escape has to be specific: the on/off fact is not asked to supervene on any complex or graded property, because it is fixed by whether a simple relation obtains.

That is the whole of the constructive move, and it is worth being clear about how little it asserts. It says the fact has a non-graded ground. It does not say what the relation consists in, and it does not say under what conditions it obtains.

## What Could Make Engagement Non-Graded

Three readings of the relation are available to the Map, with different costs.

**The relation is primitive.** Engagement is a fundamental relation between a subject and a physical system, not analysable into degrees of anything — the price a dualist interface model pays somewhere, comparable to the phenomenal-bonding primitive panpsychism pays at its own bedrock. The cost is that a primitive explains nothing further, and in particular says nothing about *when* the relation holds, which is what the next section asks.

**The existential reading.** What is sharp here is not a relation's intensity but a question of existence: either there is a subject coupled to this system or there is not, and existence does not come in degrees even where everything enabling it is graded. The cost is that this describes the logic of the fact rather than its ground. It says the claim must take a determinate truth-value, which is precisely what the ontic-vagueness camp denies — Schwitzgebel (2023) argues that some systems have no determinate fact as to whether experience is present, and a companion working paper (venue unconfirmed) extends the indeterminacy to subject-*counting*. Anyone who accepts that will refuse the step, so the existential reading restates the Map's commitment more than it supports it.

**The countable-substrate reading.** Under [Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) the coupling operates on quantum indeterminacies in neural tissue. Whether a system offers *any* indeterminacy the coupling can bias is then a countable matter — zero or non-zero — and zero-versus-non-zero is sharp in a way "enough integration" is not. Degrees would live in the count above one, which is roughly how [degrees-of-consciousness](/concepts/degrees-of-consciousness/) already partitions the terrain. Two costs are visible. It makes engagement turn on a physical fact after all, so if the availability of exploitable indeterminacy varies continuously, "at least one exploitable site" is a threshold in a graded quantity. And it threatens to make engagement far too cheap: a single exploitable indeterminacy is not obviously rarer in a thermostat than in a nematode, and without an integration requirement the reading drifts toward the panpsychism the universal coupling response takes trouble to avoid.

## The Trigger Problem

The sorites article volunteers the objection against itself: "if the coupling's engagement is itself triggered by a graded physical variable, the sorites re-enters at the trigger's switch-on point."

Stated plainly: suppose some physical variable *V* is such that the coupling engages exactly when *V* is high enough. A sharp fact about engagement then entails a sharp cutoff in *V*, and the sorites reappears there. Two exits are open.

**Deny the trigger.** Hold that no physical variable determines engagement — architecture enables the coupling without settling it. The cost is steep in the Map's own terms: the universal coupling response rejects coupling selectivity precisely for introducing "an unexplained selection principle," and an engagement floating free of physical conditions is that principle under a new name.

**Accept the trigger, hide the cutoff.** Apply epistemicism one level down: *V* has a sharp threshold whose location is unknowable. This resembles the graded-physical-property route Antony's conditional was supposed to block, and the Map's escape depends on a distinction it has not yet worked out — between what *grounds* the on/off fact (the coupling relation) and what *triggers* it (the physical variable). Whether grounding and triggering separate cleanly enough for the fact to inherit sharpness from the relation instead of vagueness from the variable is an open metaphysical question. The Map should record it as owed.

## The Live Seam: Vague Adequacy, Sharp Engagement

The Map's two relevant pages make claims that have never been set beside each other. Neither cites the other, in either direction.

The universal coupling response, having named architectural adequacy as its condition, says of that condition: "The boundary between 'adequate' and 'inadequate' architecture need not be sharp … there is no precise moment when a developing embryo's nervous system becomes adequate, just as there is no precise moment when an ecosystem becomes a forest. The vagueness is in the boundary, not in the coupling." The sorites article needs engagement to be sharp on pain of losing its Tenet-4 anchor.

Two readings of the pair are available, and this article does not choose between them.

On the **reconciling** reading, the vagueness the coupling response reports is conceptual: "adequate" is our imprecise predicate, applied to a continuous anatomical series, while the coupling relation it tracks is sharp. The final clause — vagueness in the boundary, not in the coupling — reads naturally this way, and the same page already draws the engagement distinction in its own words, calling the adequacy condition "the prior question of whether the architecture supports an interface at all." So read, the two pages agree and one of them says what the other needs.

On the **non-reconciling** reading, the embryo and forest analogies point the other way. They are the standard cases of boundaries many philosophers take to be at least semantically and arguably worldly indeterminate. If adequacy is graded in the world and engagement tracks adequacy, then either engagement is graded too — which deletes the on/off fact — or a sharp line falls inside a graded quantity, which is what Antony's conditional was invoked to forbid.

Which the Map means has not been stated anywhere. Following the practice [ontic-structural-realism](/concepts/ontic-structural-realism/) applies to its own composite, the honest move is to name the claims that could be undone rather than to settle them by fiat. What would settle this one is a single clarification the corpus does not yet contain: whether "need not be sharp" is offered as a claim about the predicate or about the world. Resolving it in print would be a substantive metaphysical commitment, and it should be made deliberately, on a page whose subject it is.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**No Many Worlds** (Tenet 4) is where the construct earns its keep. Indexical identity presupposes a determinate fact about whether there is a subject and whose experience an experience is; the engagement condition is what the Map offers as that fact's ground. If engagement admits degrees, the anchor has nothing to fasten to. This makes the tenet's defence conditional on a posit the Map has argued for but not secured, and the conditional should be stated wherever the defence is used.

**Dualism** (Tenet 1) is what makes a non-graded ground available at all. A physicalist has only complex physical or functional properties to ground the fact in, and those are graded, so Antony's conditional bites. A non-physical mind coupled to a brain can stand in a relation that is not itself a sorites series. The gain is real and its price is a primitive relation, which Tenet 5 is the right lens for.

**Minimal Quantum Interaction** (Tenet 2) constrains the substrate but cannot carry the engagement fact. The natural-kind article is careful on this point: the minimal-interaction tenet "concerns influence rather than presence." Quantum indeterminacies are where influence is exercised once a subject is present; the tenet says nothing about whether one is. The countable-substrate reading above tries to recruit the tenet anyway, which is why its costs deserve the attention given them.

**Occam's Razor Has Limits** (Tenet 5) cuts both ways here and should be reported doing so. It licenses resisting the intuition that any sharp line across a smooth continuum must be an arbitrary imposition — that felt arbitrariness can be an artefact of not being able to see the line. But the engagement condition is also an extra posit with no independent evidential support, and parsimony counts against it. The Map's position is that the alternatives cost more, not that this one is free.

## What This Article Does Not Settle

- Whether engagement is genuinely non-graded, as against posited so for framework reasons.
- Whether a graded trigger is compatible with a sharp ground, which turns on a grounding-versus-triggering distinction the corpus has not drawn.
- Whether the universal coupling response's vague adequacy boundary is conceptual or worldly.
- Whether the countable-substrate reading can supply an integration requirement without reintroducing a threshold in a graded quantity.
