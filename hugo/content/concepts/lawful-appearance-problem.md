The **lawful-appearance problem** is the question every idealism owes an answer to: if reality is wholly mental, why does it present itself, to perception and to physics, as an impersonal, lawful, mathematically structured world that looks nothing like a mind? Earlier articles on The Unfinishable Map, including [the Q3 sliding-boundary article](/topics/q3-q4-sliding-boundary-and-transparency-problem/), call it the transparency problem; the new name avoids a clash with the [transparency of experience](/concepts/phenomenal-transparency-opacity-spectrum/), a separate topic in philosophy of mind and on the Map.

Broken into its parts, the charge loses much of its idealist specificity. [Lawfulness](#lawfulness) (explained below) is owed by every metaphysics, dualism included. The mathematical [structural form](#structural-form) of physics is what any structural description shows, on a premise the Map itself holds. What belongs to idealism alone is narrower: Chalmers's [austerity problem](#austerity), the [explanatory objection](#explanatory-objection) against phenomenalist idealism, and a [constitution or bridging debt](#constitution) that idealism takes on in place of the mind–matter interface. The Map carries an [appearance debt of the same shape](#the-maps-own-appearance-debt), narrower in scope. The decomposition yields a list of who owes what. It does not show whose debt is lighter, and nothing in it is an argument for dualism.

## Berkeley's Version {#berkeley}

George Berkeley defined the laws of nature as "the set rules or established methods wherein the Mind we depend on excites in us the ideas of sense" (*Principles* §30), and he raised the difficulty against himself. The eleventh objection he answers asks "to what purpose serves that curious organization of plants, and the animal mechanism in the parts of animals", if a spirit produces every effect directly; "the like may be said of all the clockwork of nature" (§60). Why would a world of ideas be built like a machine?

His answer is teleological. The connexion between ideas is "only of a mark or sign with the thing signified": the fire I see does not cause my pain but forewarns me of it. And "the reason why ideas are formed into machines, that is, artificial and regular combinations, is the same with that for combining letters into words" (§65). Mechanism is a grammar, regular so that finite minds can read it and learn what to expect. Idealists who drop Berkeley's God must find another source for the order, and that is where the modern versions of the problem begin.

## The Four Parts and the Idealist Answers {#four-parts}

### Lawfulness {#lawfulness}

Why does appearance follow laws at all, and why these laws? Every view takes some laws as basic, and dualism adds [psychophysical laws](/concepts/psychophysical-laws/) to the physical ones. Helen Yetter-Chappell argues that "any account of laws available to materialists is equally available to the nontheistic idealist, for physical laws are—rightly—neutral as to the metaphysical nature of that which they govern" (2025, ch. 5 abstract). Bernardo Kastrup counts lawfulness among the basic facts his view must explain (2017, §2) and proposes that "the laws of nature are akin to the archetypes of universal consciousness" (§6). To the objection that a mind's laws could not be so stable, he replies that "the fallacy here, of course, is that of anthropomorphization": stability looks surprising only if universal consciousness is modelled on the reactive minds of evolved animals.

A further option may make idealist laws *less* brute than physicalist ones. Mørch's phenomenal powers view holds that phenomenal properties "produce and thereby (metaphysically) necessitate their effects in virtue of how they feel" (Mørch 2017), and Chalmers judges "the Mørch-style move of saying that cosmic experiential states ground the relevant dispositions" particularly attractive for cosmic idealism (2019). The Map's own [idealism](/concepts/idealism/) article already concedes the core point: where the idealist's structure does everything physical law does, the outcome is "a parity result, not an argument for dualism". Lawfulness is a shared item.

### Structural Form {#structural-form}

Why does the world look impersonal and mathematical, so unlike a mind? The answer open to realist idealists rests on a premise older than contemporary idealism. Russell, a neutral monist rather than an idealist, held that "Physics is mathematical, not because we know so much about the physical world, but because we know so little" (1927, p. 163). Physics records structure, so whatever fills the structure, its description will look like equations. Eddington, from the same premise, put his conclusion "crudely": "the stuff of the world is mind-stuff" (1928, p. 276). Chalmers observes that realist idealism is "highly congenial to epistemological structural realism, which says roughly that science reveals the structure of the physical world but not its intrinsic nature" (2019).

The Map holds the same premise. Its [intrinsic-nature](/concepts/intrinsic-nature/) article states that "Structural description is the method of physics, and structure all it can express." A metaphysics that accepts this cannot treat the mathematical look of physics as evidence against a mental base, since that look is fixed by the method of description rather than by what is described. The premise neutralises the form of the description only. Whether the structure the method finds is rational or teleological in content is a fact about what is described, and that residue is the austerity item below. What remains on the structural side is Newman's objection that structure alone is too thin to pick out any relations, and the Map's [treatment of Newman](/concepts/ontic-structural-realism/#newman) concedes that the objection "cuts both ways": it bears on the Map's epistemic structural realism as much as on the idealist's.

### Austerity {#austerity}

Here the charge bites on idealism specifically, and on cosmic idealism in particular (the view that physical reality is the experience of a single [cosmic mind](/concepts/cosmopsychism/)). Chalmers names it "the austerity problem": the cosmic mind "looks extremely austere, and very much unlike a mind as we normally think of it", and "there seems to be little or no rationality in this structure" (2019). As he puts it, "It is not really clear why, if there is to be a cosmic mind, it should be as austere as this."

The escape has a price. Crediting the cosmic mind with mental structure beyond what physics records raises "a sort of excess baggage problem": "we have to postulate supra-natural structure and dynamics beyond what natural science suggests", and "one might worry that they will be entirely epiphenomenal with respect to the universe we observe". Chalmers adds that some impure models let the extra structure sustain the physical dynamics, so the dilemma is a pressure rather than a proof.

Kastrup takes the first horn. His anthropomorphisation reply already denies that a cosmic mind should resemble a human one. Elsewhere, addressing why universal consciousness would dissociate into alters prone to suffering, he ties self-reflection to alter formation, so that outside its alters universal consciousness "may unfold, at least partly, along lines that we may describe as instinctual or naturalistic" (2017, §9). Read as a reply to austerity (the Map's application, not Kastrup's framing), this makes austerity something the view predicts. Whether a mind without reflection, valuing or reasoning still earns the name is where the dispute now sits, and it is open.

### The Explanatory Objection {#explanatory-objection}

The fourth part applies to anti-realist idealism, phenomenalism, on which physical truths are truths about actual and possible experiences. Chalmers ranks it the most important objection to phenomenalism: "the truths about possible experiences demand explanation, and phenomenalism gives them none" (2019). A table seen from one angle and then another, today and tomorrow, presents an order among possible experiences that calls for a ground.

Berkeley's ground was God. Yetter-Chappell keeps a ground and drops the theology: "There must be something outside of us that can sustain objects when we are not perceiving them, and account for the regularity of our perceptions. But this needn't be a god in any recognizable sense" (2025, book abstract). Her ground is a "tapestry" of experiences bound by co-consciousness. Christmann (2026) argues that the view "cannot sustain anti-subjectivism without admitting a globally unifying subject-like ground", partly because of "the absence of any adequate ground for the synchronic pattern of co-consciousness relations", and that theistic idealism comes out comparatively superior. On that argument the order debt pushes nontheistic idealism back toward Berkeley, a slide within idealism that mirrors the [slide from thick-mind dualism into idealism](/topics/q3-q4-sliding-boundary-and-transparency-problem/). It is one recent critique, and the Map has located no reply yet.

## What Idealism Takes On Instead {#constitution}

Idealism removes the dualist's interface between mind and matter, but it keeps relational debts of its own. Yetter-Chappell holds that "Idealism (per se) is not a position on the mind-body problem", being "compatible with analogues of all the major positions", and pairs her view with "a novel externalist account of psychophysical bridging laws" (2025, ch. 3 abstract). Kastrup's view, on which individual minds are dissociated alters of one consciousness, takes on the question of how a single subject is divided into many (the [de-combination problem](/topics/open-individualism-and-the-de-combination-problem/)). Chalmers judges the constitution problem for cosmic idealism "a very serious one", while holding that some routes to solving it, the identity route especially, look more promising than their micropsychist analogues. Emergent versions, he adds, have "very much the flavor of dualist views of the mind–body relation" and suffer "analogous problems of interaction" (2019).

The slide from dualism to idealism is therefore best described as a conversion: a mind–matter interface debt becomes a mind–mind constitution or bridging debt, with the austerity residue added.

## The Map's Own Appearance Debt {#the-maps-own-appearance-debt}

The Map cannot press the lawful-appearance problem without naming its own version. On the corridor reading that its [observational-closure](/concepts/observational-closure/) article uses to explain why mental causation is invisible in experiments, consciousness selects among Born-weighted outcomes, and "the selections are posited to average to exactly the Born distribution across the ensemble". That is a mental process required to look, in aggregate, exactly like physics.

The shape matches the charge against idealism: something non-physical must present as lawful physical statistics. The scope is narrower, confined to selection events at quantum indeterminacies rather than the whole of physics. And the match is posited rather than explained: the Map adopts it to respect the empirical record, and nothing in its account of conscious selection derives it. The "bending" alternative, which the Map holds open (see [the corridor-versus-bending taxonomy](/topics/born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface/)), drops the posit and accepts deviations that sufficiently precise aggregate tests could detect.

With several subjects on one event, the posit binds their composition rule: fixed weights inherit it, state-dependent weights owe a balancing law ([social choice](/concepts/psychophysical-social-choice-problem/#born-favours-fixed-weights)), and whether fixed weights may vary by context awaits [P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2)'s quantifier, worded per context but unsettled. A Zeno hold, ranked below [post-decoherence-selection](/concepts/post-decoherence-selection/), moves unconditional statistics inside the quadratic window, and "physical dephasing of the same strength in the same basis would do the same" ([Zeno](/concepts/zeno-dynamics-on-a-decohered-mixture/#unconditional)). Presenting as monitoring, the hold trades the posit for a detectability exposure: one must exclude physical dephasers of that strength.

## Rival Readings of the Decomposition {#rival-readings}

Three readings of the itemisation compete. On Kastrup's, lawful appearance is a prediction rather than a debt: dissociation explains why the laws resist our will, and the archetypes explain their stability. Chalmers presses austerity and constitution against cosmic idealism, which he rates the most promising version, but his verdict is comparative: he finds every position on the mind–body problem implausible and concludes that "Idealism is not significantly less plausible than its main competitors". On a third reading, which earlier Map articles leaned toward, the appearance debt is a cost comparable in weight to the interface debt. The Map's [dualism-cartography](/apex/dualism-cartography/) apex has since withdrawn comparative cost verdicts "for want of a scale", and this article follows it.

What follows from the neutral definition alone is the itemisation. Whether austerity is a fatal cost, a prediction, or a reason to enrich the cosmic mind depends on what one thinks a mind must be.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The Map's [Dualism](/tenets/#dualism) tenet is a commitment, and this itemisation does not supply an argument for it. Two of the problem's four parts bind the Map as well. What the Map can press legitimately is narrower: Chalmers's austerity dilemma and Christmann's argument that nontheistic idealism drifts toward a global subject. Both are internal difficulties raised by authors who take idealism seriously, and neither shows that a physical pole exists.

[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits) applies in both directions. Idealism's one-kind economy cannot settle the matter in its favour, and the Map, which declines to let simplicity count against dualism, cannot mark idealism down for the "supra-natural structure" of its impure versions on grounds of profligacy alone.

[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction) and [Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) shape the Map's own item. A selection-only channel keeps small the region where mind must present as physics, but does not remove it: wherever consciousness selects outcomes, its selections must reproduce Born statistics. The resulting position: lawfulness and structural form are shared; austerity, the explanatory objection and constitution are idealism's; the selection-statistics match is the Map's. Which list is heavier the inventory cannot say.
