The **second-order comparison** sets two accounts of the [explanatory gap](/concepts/explanatory-gap/) against each other as rival explanations of the same datum. On the dualist's account phenomenal truths resist derivation from physical truths because consciousness is something over and above the physical. On the Type-B physicalist's account, in its [phenomenal-concepts](/concepts/phenomenal-concepts-strategy/) form, the gap lies in how phenomenal concepts refer rather than in what they refer to. Both grant that the gap is real and will not close; they differ on what explains it. The comparison is second-order because its explanandum is the gap itself rather than consciousness.

The result first, since three Map pages had listed this comparison as the one reply that could lift the Map's case against Type-B above *compatible*. Run by stated criteria ([set out below](#the-criteria-and-the-scores)), the comparison splits by explanandum. Dualism leads on contrast and on reflective stability, but only against the fallacy form of the phenomenal-concepts strategy (PCS) and in company with physicalist rivals that predict the same; it leads on cost in primitives only if Chalmers's master argument succeeds. Type-B leads on the meta-problem challenge, the single criterion scored on third-person empirical data. Scope and independent motivation tie, and the master-argument criterion is open. Against Type-B the tier stays *compatible*. The framing as an inference to the best explanation is the Map's: no source read runs dualism against PCS under that name, and the criteria are drawn from texts that call the same exercise an explanation of the gap or of problem intuitions, or a debunking explanation.

## The Explananda

"The gap" names several data, and the rivals divide over which they must explain and which they may explain away. Chalmers (2007) states the target when he frames PCS as offering, if it succeeds, "the next best thing: a physical explanation of the explanatory gap". The data fall into three groups.

**A priori data.** Phenomenal truths are not a priori derivable from physical truths (E1); the knowledge and conceivability intuitions are correct (E2); and there is a justificatory asymmetry between Mary and her zombie twin, Mary's belief being justified "with something approaching Cartesian certainty" while her twin's is not (E6). Type-B and dualism grant E1 together. E2 is distinctive of Type-B, which Chalmers (2018, p. 21) says "accepts a robust understanding of our epistemic situation" on which the problem intuitions are correct, where an illusionist need not.

**Felt data.** A mind–brain identity seems to leave something unexplained (E3); the feeling attaches to phenomenal states and not to mental states generally or to other recognitional concepts (E5); and careful reflection sustains the intuition (E7).

**Reports.** People make problem judgments and reports (E4). This is the meta-problem's explanandum, and Chalmers separates it from E2: "we need only explain the fact that we have the problem intuitions; we do not also need to explain their correctness" (2018, p. 21).

E3, E4, E5 and E7 are facts about human psychology and open in principle to experiment; Papineau writes of the contrast data that "It would be useful to have further empirical investigation of this issue" (2011, p. 17). E1, E2 and E6 are a priori or first-person, and under the rule the [routing page](/concepts/type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism/#the-maps-tier-against-each-type) applies they bear on plausibility rather than on tier.

## The Lot

An inference to the best explanation ranks the candidates it has, and the Map's IBE page requires it to apply the bad-lot objection to its own abductions. Here the lot is wider than two.

**Dualism** explains the gap as ontological, taking acquaintance with consciousness as primitive. **Fallacy accounts** hold that deploying a phenomenal concept involves the experience it refers to while deploying a physical concept does not, and that we project that difference onto the referents; Papineau's antipathetic fallacy is, in Kammerer's taxonomy, "the most typical example" (2019, ms. p. 8), and Chalmers files the same move as a use-mention fallacy. **Conceptual-isolation accounts**, the recognitional, quotational and constitutional versions on the [PCS page](/concepts/phenomenal-concepts-strategy/#major-versions), hold that phenomenal concepts are sui generis and that the gap's uniqueness follows from theirs. Both families are PCS; they are scored separately because they score differently.

Three further rivals are physicalist without being PCS. **Type-B without phenomenal concepts**: Tye, who in 1999 called the gap "a cognitive illusion", by 2008 "argues that the strategy is mistaken" (publisher's abstracts; Crossref dates *Consciousness Revisited* 2008-12-12, the date used here, where the corpus elsewhere cites 2009); Fazekas (2011) offers "an alternative to the Phenomenal Concept Strategy" on which certain sensory representations "lack constituent structure". **Lack-of-understanding accounts**, Kammerer's second family, hold that we lack the concepts or truths that would make the identity intelligible; Stoljar's [ignorance hypothesis](/concepts/ignorance-hypothesis/) and McGinn's [cognitive closure](/concepts/mysterianism/) belong here, and Stoljar (2005), who named PCS, already ranked his own "missing concept strategy" above it as "a more plausible response" (abstract). **Introspective-illusion accounts**, his third, hold that introspection misrepresents our states; [illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/) is the strong form. **Psychological-bias accounts** are metaphysically neutral: Chalmers (2018, p. 33) reports Fiala, Arico and Nichols's proposal that "problem intuitions may arise from conflicting verdicts about consciousness from our fast (automatic) system and our slow (controlled) system". [Russellian monism](/concepts/russellian-monism/) also explains the gap, through physics' silence on intrinsic natures.

Kammerer's conclusion shows what the wider lot changes: "by arguing against Fallacy Accounts, we may give some indirect weight to the disjunction of these two views" (ms. p. 9), the illusion and lack-of-understanding accounts, and concludes for physicalism of one of those kinds. A dualist win over PCS is no win over physicalism.

## The Criteria and the Scores

Each criterion names who states it and whose scoring is reported; the scoring of the whole is the Map's. The four that decide the result come first.

**C2, contrast.** Chalmers states the criterion: "Contrasting these intuitions with our related intuitions about phenomena such as colour and belief can help us to determine whether a proposed mechanism explains what is distinctive about the phenomenal case" (2018, p. 20). Every party uses it. Papineau turns it on Bloom's two-module account, which "seems to overgeneralize" since "the intuition of dualism hinges on the way we think specifically about phenomenological states" (2011, p. 15). Chalmers turns it on recognitional concepts: "When we recognize a cactus, we do not have problem intuitions anything like those we have in the phenomenal case" (2018, p. 22). Sundström (2008) turns it on Papineau's own fallacy, arguing, in Papineau's report, "that it predicts dualist intuitions in cases where there are none" (2011, p. 16); Papineau answers that "Sundström's point is well-taken, but I am not sure it is conclusive" (p. 17) and offers two escape routes, while Chalmers sets the use-mention strategy aside "as one of the least promising explanations" (2018, p. 32). Dualism passes, since a real ontological gap would be felt exactly where phenomenal concepts meet physical ones. The fallacy form fails Sundström's case unless one of Papineau's escapes works. The isolation form passes by construction, since it holds phenomenal concepts unique ([Loar grants there are no other cases](/concepts/primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities/#is-there-any-other-case)), which moves the dispute to C4. Ignorance and illusion accounts pass whenever their mechanism is specific to phenomenal states.

**C3, the meta-problem challenge.** Chalmers states it: "If a theory says that mechanism M is the basis of consciousness, then it needs to explain how mechanism M plays a central role in bringing about judgments about consciousness" (2018, p. 36). Type-B identifies the basis of consciousness with the mechanism that produces judgments about it, so it meets the challenge with no further posit. Dualism meets it only where consciousness helps cause the reports, and Chalmers finds that "It is by no means straightforward to see how consciousness could play precisely the role required, either on a panpsychist or an interactionist picture" (p. 42). On the Map's [meta-problem page](/concepts/meta-problem-of-consciousness/#the-debunking-threat) the interactionist reply removes a defeater while the modal and explanatory forms of the debunking argument remain open. Chalmers aims that argument "against non-reductionist views of consciousness" (p. 44), so the coincidence charge is dualism's to answer and not Type-B's. This is the one criterion scored on uncontroversially empirical data, and it favours Type-B. The record on the reports also cuts against reading them at face value: Díaz's studies, as [the Map reports them](/topics/metaproblem-of-consciousness-under-dualism/), found problem intuitions uncommon among ordinary people and, where present, predicted by low ratings of the neuroscience rather than by cognitive style, with training and culture untested; Berent (2024) concludes from experiments that the roots of the hard problem are "partly psychological". Neither result separates dualism from Type-B, but each costs the dualist's use of the felt data more.

**C5, cost in primitives.** Chalmers's comparative passage is the strongest dualist point in the texts read and the only explicitly comparative one. Because "the dualist is already committed to positing primitive mental features", acquaintance can be primitive at no further cost, whereas "The physicalist must either explain the features, or accept a further explanatory gap" (2007). Three discounts apply. The passage holds only if the [master argument](/concepts/phenomenal-concepts-strategy/#chalmerss-master-argument) holds, since a physically explicable feature would let the physicalist explain it, and the [routing page](/concepts/type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism/#which-map-reply-reaches-which-type) already counts that argument as a separate reply, so C5 adds no independent evidence. By the [parity result](/concepts/primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities/#strong-necessities), Type-B's bridge is already epistemically primitive, so the count comes to one primitive each. And the Map's interactionism adds selection laws, so the Map's own dualism scores worse here than dualism in general. [Tenet 5](/tenets/#occams-limits) discounts the count of kinds on both sides.

**C6, reflective stability.** Kammerer argues that fallacies are not sustained by careful reflection and the intuition of distinctness is, so that "the psychological process that causes it is more similar to a case of valid reasoning than to a fallacy" (ms. p. 15); "Careful reflection sustains intuitions about experiences – not simply about how it feels to use certain concepts" (ms. p. 20). This tells against the fallacy accounts. Dualism, the ignorance accounts and the illusion accounts all predict the same stability, which is why Kammerer ends with a physicalist disjunction. The evidence is introspective, and Kammerer grants that his stronger claim, that only careful reflection produces the intuition, "may be less easy to support" (ms. p. 16). The PCS page's persistence paragraph is an informal version of this criterion and inherits the same limit.

**C1, scope.** Dualism explains the first-person data at a stroke and reaches the reports only if consciousness helps cause them. Type-B explains the reports at once and owes an account of the justificatory asymmetry that the master argument says it cannot give. A tie, pending C7.

**C4, independent motivation.** The ad hoc charge the [PCS page](/concepts/phenomenal-concepts-strategy/#the-ad-hoc-objection) presses has a mirror image: dualism's acquaintance relation and psychophysical law are also posited to explain these data. Chalmers puts the physicalist's burden precisely: some feature of phenomenal concepts may both explain the intuitions and be physically explicable, "but if so it is this feature that will be doing the explanatory work" (2018, p. 22). Symmetric.

**C7, the master argument.** Chalmers concludes that PCS "cannot reconcile ontological physicalism with the explanatory gap" (2007); Díaz-León (2010) concludes that he "has not posed any serious problem" for the strategy (publisher's abstract), and the PCS page records the exchange as open. The dilemma rests on zombie conceivability, the premise [P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1) treats as shared across the conceivability cluster.

## What Would Discriminate

Two criteria yield predictions experiment could test, and both discriminate dualism from the fallacy family only. Fallacy accounts predict felt gaps wherever a phenomenal concept faces a co-referring term that does not activate the experience, Sundström's case; dualism predicts none outside phenomenal–physical identities. Fallacy accounts predict that careful reflection weakens the intuition; dualism predicts that it does not. The isolation form, the ignorance accounts and the illusion accounts make dualism's prediction in both cases, so confirmation would move credence toward several views together.

Artificial systems do not discriminate either, by the Map's own concession. PCS-style and illusion accounts predict that any system with the right architecture produces problem reports, and Chalmers notes that a simple existing system already "mirrors at least some aspects of our phenomenal reports" (2018, n. 26). A dualist who predicted that architecture-only systems lack problem intuitions would have a discriminating claim, but the [Map's meta-problem page](/concepts/meta-problem-of-consciousness/#the-zombie-meta-problem) holds that a mindless system's reports would be statistically ordinary under the Born-preserving mechanism, and so disowns that prediction.

The one discriminator between dualism and every physicalist rival in the lot is [Tenet 3](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)'s channel, an intention-conditioned departure from Born statistics, and no such test has been run; the [IBE page](/concepts/inference-to-the-best-explanation-against-dualism/) states the disconfirmer and its status.

## The Tier

Under [P-M1](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#p-m1) a reply that removes a defeater raises no tier, and every dualist result above is of that kind or weaker. Against Type-B the comparison favours the physicalist on the one empirical criterion, favours dualism only conditionally on the master argument, and finds the remaining dualist advantages shared with physicalist rivals. The tier is *compatible*.

Against the fallacy family within Type-B the tier is also *compatible* now. If experiments confirmed the over-generation contrast and reflective stability, the result would be *suggestive* against that family without separating dualism from the ignorance and illusion accounts, the same non-separating verdict the routing page gives Type-C. *Discriminating* is reached nowhere, and only Tenet 3's outcome deviation could reach it.

Two further things would move it: a topic-neutral mechanism reproducing reports, contrast and stability together in an artificial system, which would remove dualism's lead on the felt data; and general acceptance that the replies to the master argument fail, which bears on plausibility only, the argument being a priori. Chalmers's own verdict on the field is a standoff: "neither illusionism nor realism has a truly satisfactory response to the charge of absurdity" (2018, p. 56). Two leads were not read: Bogardus (2013), who per Chalmers (2018, n. 12) argues that debunking proposals "do not succeed as defeaters for arguments for dualism", and McLaughlin (2010), the one title that names inference to the best explanation.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism).** Against Type-B, Tenet 1 stays a commitment rather than a result. The comparison shows where the dualist case is strongest, the primitive-cost asymmetry conditional on the master argument, and where it is weakest, the reports, which Type-B explains without remainder. On the gap itself the Map reads the result as a standoff.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction).** The one empirical criterion, C3, is where Tenet 3 matters: interactionism is the form of dualism that can meet the meta-problem challenge at all, because on it consciousness helps produce judgments about consciousness. The Map holds that role as available rather than actual, so the tenet turns a dualist failure on C3 into an unpaid debt; it removes a defeater and raises no tier. The mechanism debt under [P-Q10](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q10) is the price dualism pays to compete on C3.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits).** The tenet discounts the count-of-kinds strand of C5 on both sides and leaves C2, C3 and C6 untouched, since none is a simplicity criterion. Neither rival is entitled to win ties.

**The register.** The dualist advantages on C2 and C6 move credence toward dualism, the ignorance accounts and the illusion accounts together, and under [P-M2](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#p-m2)'s convergence discount they are not separate evidence. Kammerer's taxonomy also makes PCS one of three physicalist diagnoses of the shared premise rather than the mainstream statement of it.
