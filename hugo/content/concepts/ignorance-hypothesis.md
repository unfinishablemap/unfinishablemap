The **ignorance hypothesis** is Daniel Stoljar's claim that "we are ignorant of a type of non-experiential truth relevant to the nature of experience" (the wording of the publisher's abstract for the introduction to *Ignorance and Imagination*, 2006). Stoljar's "epistemic view" pairs it with a conditional: if the hypothesis is true, the conceivability and knowledge arguments against physicalism fail, and the problem of consciousness has its origin in what we do not know. By the definition in David Chalmers's taxonomy, and on Stoljar's own account, the view is type-C physicalism: a deep epistemic gap held to be closable in principle. Chalmers's type-F filing covers Stoljar's earlier view of 2001, not this one.

For The Unfinishable Map the hypothesis is the hardest opponent of its central arguments. It offers its own explanation of why the zombie, knowledge and explanatory-gap arguments seem compelling, placing our ignorance on the physical side; it therefore survives the failure of every phenomenal-concepts account and is untouched by the Map's appeal to acquaintance with consciousness. The Map's [fifth tenet](/tenets/#occams-limits) obliges it to grant half the view, that we are ignorant of something relevant. It contests the other half: that the missing truths are non-experiential and would close the gap. The replies that reach the hypothesis remove a defeater or press it dialectically, and none adds evidence, so against it the Map's case stands at *compatible*, the lowest rung of its evidential ladder.

## The Hypothesis and the Epistemic View

The hypothesis is stated in terms of *non-experiential* truths, though the book's abstract glosses the view as tracing the problem to "our ignorance of the relevant physical facts". The neutral wording lets the hypothesis be stated before settling what "physical" means.

Its target is what Stoljar calls the logical problem of experience: three theses that cannot all be true. There are experiential truths; every truth is entailed by, or supervenes on, some non-experiential truth; and not every truth is (chapter 2). The epistemic view answers with two theses:

- **E1**: "if the ignorance hypothesis is true, the logical problem is solved" (chapter 4, titled "Error from Ignorance"). David Papineau's review of the book summarises the central thesis: "we are puzzled about consciousness only because we don't know enough about the non-conscious facts".
- **E2**: the hypothesis is true. Stoljar argues it from general plausibility, from a Russellian version on which empirical inquiry acquaints us only with "relational or dispositional features" of physical objects (chapter 6), and from historical precedent: the epistemic view "is known to be correct for older philosophical problems that are structurally analogous to the logical problem" (chapter 7).

Two features separate the view from [McGinn's mysterianism](/concepts/mysterianism/), which chapter 5 compares with it. The ignorance need be neither permanent nor about categorical properties. Papineau puts it plainly: "It only adds extra hostages to argumentative fortune to claim that this ignorance is irremediable, or that it is of categorical matters." Asking for less than mysterianism, the hypothesis is harder to refute.

Stoljar also runs a collapse argument the opposite way to Chalmers's. The a posteriori and a priori entailment views, roughly Type-B and Type-A, either have "no answer to the arguments" or collapse into the epistemic view (chapters 9–10). His later name for the position is "non-standard physicalism" (Kind & Stoljar 2023). In a 2020 chapter he considers two objections to it: that "we are not ignorant of any feature that is relevant to the hard problem", and that, even if true, the view is "no contribution to that problem at all" (2020b).

## Type-C, Not Type-F

Chalmers's "Consciousness and its Place in Nature" (2003) predates the 2006 book. It files "Stoljar 2001" under type F, among views that expand "our view of the physical base to include underlying intrinsic properties". That is the view of "Two Conceptions of the Physical", which distinguishes a theory-based from an object-based conception of the physical. His 2018 discussion still cites the 2001 paper, and none of the texts checked files the 2006 hypothesis by letter.

The 2006 hypothesis is type-C on every account checked. Chalmers's own definition lets a type-C gap be closable in principle yet closed to us now, "perhaps because we do not currently grasp all the required physical truths". Stoljar adopts the label himself, recommending "type‐C Materialism" as the best response to an inconsistency he finds in Chalmers's views (2020a). McClelland (2020) records the shift: Stoljar "started out arguing for the Russellian version of IH (2001) but then went on to adopt a general formulation of IH without such a commitment". On Stoljar's reading, Chalmers would file the Nagel-inspired version Stoljar endorses the same way: "Chalmers would, I think, classify RM4 as a version of materialism—type-C materialism, in particular" (2013, n. 29). [Russellian monism](/concepts/russellian-monism/) becomes one species of the epistemic view.

Whether the hypothesis *collapses* into type-F is a separate question. Chalmers argues that unknown structural truths would leave the gap and unknown intrinsic truths would make the view type-F ([type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism](/concepts/type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism/#why-type-c-collapses)). The argument rests on a premise Stoljar disputes ([set out below](#what-does-reach-it)), so collapse into F is Chalmers's argument, not his filing.

The Map's thickness grid in [four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy](/topics/four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy/) lists the view, as "Stoljar's epistemic physicalism", in its min-mind/max-physical quadrant. It belongs there only as a flagged physicalist limit case, whose physical-side thickness is epistemic and whose mind-side is null, so that quadrant's exclusion debt and its ruling-out by Tenet 3 do not attach; Tenet 1 is the operative exclusion.

## Why It Is the Hardest Opponent

The Map treats the explanatory-gap, zombie and knowledge arguments as one premise-sharing cluster, and names the [phenomenal-concepts-strategy](/concepts/phenomenal-concepts-strategy/) as the mainstream physicalist account of the shared premise ([P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1)). The ignorance hypothesis is a second, independent account of why the arguments look compelling. It makes no claim about phenomenal concepts, so it survives the failure of every variant of that strategy.

It also finds the fault elsewhere. The phenomenal-concepts strategy locates the gap in how we think about consciousness; the ignorance hypothesis concedes our grip on consciousness and locates the gap in what we know of the physical. McClelland puts it so: the view "holds that the shortcomings lie on the physical side".

And the Map's selection step does no work against it. On the Map's account, the arguments earn irreducibility, and [Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction) then selects dualism "among the irreducibility-respecting alternatives" ([P-D2](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d2)). On the ignorance hypothesis, consciousness is physical and does whatever causal work its physical basis does, so the view is not among the alternatives Tenet 3 selects between. The whole case against it must be carried by the cluster it undercuts.

## What Does Not Answer It

Four replies the Map uses elsewhere miss the hypothesis.

**Acquaintance.** Against Type-B, the Map answers the water/H₂O analogy by holding that zombie conceivability "doesn't reflect ignorance of what consciousness is" ([zombie-master-argument](/concepts/zombie-master-argument/)). That reply concerns ignorance of the phenomenal relatum. The ignorance hypothesis grants [acquaintance](/concepts/phenomenal-acquaintance/) and places the ignorance on the other relatum.

**"All the physics."** The claim that in conceiving zombies we grasp "all the physics, none of the experience" ([philosophical-zombies](/concepts/philosophical-zombies/)) is exactly what the hypothesis denies: on it, we conceive only the physics we know. Asserting the claim begs the question.

**Persistence.** The hypothesis sets no timetable. Stoljar's ignorance holds "at least for the time being" (2020b), and the completing theory lies at a limit of inquiry "which we will perhaps never reach" (2013, p. 18). The gap's persistence is expected on this view, as on dualism.

**"Hard to see how."** Papineau, himself a Type-B physicalist, objects that non-experiential facts "would seem to be third-personal, objective, and non-perspectival", so that "It is hard to see how knowledge of the former could automatically render the absence of the latter inconceivable." The hypothesis predicts that difficulty: if the relevant truths are unknown, we should not expect to see how they close the gap. Citing the intuition as evidence against the view assumes what is in dispute. Its strongest form is the conceptual-hook argument, set out in the next section.

The obvious charge of unfalsifiability is also unavailable on the usual terms. The view is physicalism over a physics not yet known, and it says little about what that physics will contain; but the Map describes its own Tenet 2 as a "consistency claim" rather than a novel-prediction claim ([tenets](/tenets/)). Tenet 5's symmetric discipline leaves only a narrower charge: as stated, the hypothesis predicts nothing that dualism does not also predict.

## What Does Reach It

Four replies engage premises the view holds. Each removes a defeater or presses the view dialectically, and none raises the Map's evidential tier, under the Map's rule that removing a defeater never upgrades the evidence ([P-M1](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#p-m1)).

**The conceptual hook.** Chalmers (2003) argues that "epistemic implication from A to B requires some sort of conceptual hook by virtue of which the condition described in A can satisfy the conceptual requirements for the truth of B." Read on the consciousness side, the argument needs only that the concept of consciousness is not a functional concept, and claims that no non-experiential truth, known or unknown, could then supply the hook. On the Map's analysis this is where the objection is strongest, because it rests on the concept of consciousness, where the hypothesis concedes our grip, and not on what physical truths could be like, where the hypothesis claims ignorance. Stoljar's reply, as Papineau's review relays it, pairs *John is a number* with *John is not in pain*: a non-experiential truth that settles a negative experiential one. Papineau answers that this does not carry over to positive experiential truths. Amy Kind presses a related objection; in Stoljar's summary, ignorance "would not have the advertised effect" on the conceivability and knowledge arguments (Kind & Stoljar 2023). Against views on which we "underestimate the body", Chalmers (2018) holds that the problem intuitions "concern the phenomenal rather than the physical"; McClelland replies that they are hybrid, and that the hypothesis "undermines the second half of these hybrid intuitions".

**Structure and dynamics.** Chalmers's case against type-C needs no complete physics: "we simply need the claim that physical descriptions are in terms of structure and dynamics." Defending the Nagel-inspired version he endorses, Stoljar distinguishes mathematical, relational and nomic-spatiotemporal readings of "structure" and argues that the argument fails on each. On the last, "we don't know currently what those properties are", "so we are in no position to assert that no truth about consciousness is a truth about them" (2013, pp. 20–22). The Map's own statement of the premise is modest: its position "is a bet on the method-claim, not a proof of it" ([physical-completeness](/concepts/physical-completeness/)). The reply reaches the hypothesis as a contested bet.

**The precedent disanalogy.** Papineau's review discusses Stoljar's precedents, Descartes's and Broad's, and notes that the facts they lacked, about computation and quantum bonding, "are most naturally thought of in terms of causal roles". Papineau makes the point to separate Stoljar from Russell. The Map puts it to its own use: in those cases the explanandum was functional, so a conceptual hook existed. That is the disanalogy the Map draws in [the case of vitalism](/concepts/vitalism/#the-disanalogy-reply), between a placeholder that names a role and phenomenal types that do not. This reaches only the historical support for E2.

**"You're not done."** Kind's second objection, in Stoljar's summary, "concedes for the sake of argument that the epistemic view undermines arguments like the conceivability argument, but points out that there are further issues about consciousness that the view does not address". The hypothesis targets the anti-physicalist arguments and leaves the hard problem standing. The reply limits what the view achieves without showing it false.

The book has drawn further critical discussion: a 2009 symposium (Alter 2009; Bennett 2009), a review by Levine (2008) and a critical notice by Gertler (2009). Removing the hypothesis as a defeater would restore the cluster's standing against physicalism. It would not lift the tier.

## Tenet 5: What the Map Grants

The Map's fifth tenet holds that "Simplicity is not a reliable guide to truth when knowledge is incomplete", and its rationale says that "The apparent simplicity of physicalism could reflect ignorance rather than insight". That premise, incompleteness of knowledge relevant to the mind–body problem, is E2 in substance. By parity, the apparent distinctness of consciousness could reflect ignorance too. A Map that asserts the premise against physicalism's simplicity cannot call it ad hoc when Stoljar points it at the conceivability arguments. The Map therefore grants E2 as a live hypothesis and contests E1. Kind's third objection, in Stoljar's summary, is that the view treats physicalism as "a default hypothesis, and defends it at all costs". Under the tenet's symmetric discipline the Map holds that this objection binds both ways: neither framework is entitled to default status.

What follows is the Map's own analysis, drawn by neither Stoljar nor Chalmers. The disagreement is over the *content* of the ignorance. The Map also holds that current physics is incomplete in a way relevant to consciousness: [physical-completeness](/concepts/physical-completeness/) argues that "Actuality-selection is not a structural determination", so the structural completeness of current physics "does not entail ontological completeness". On the Map's view, what is missing involves consciousness itself: selection among outcomes at quantum indeterminacy. That is the fourth of Chalmers's collapse routes, a physics "appealing to consciousness itself", which ends in an irreducible consciousness and, given the Map's commitments, in type-D dualism. Stoljar's missing truths are non-experiential. Both views predict that the gap persists and that current physics is incomplete. They come apart over whether the laws of a completing theory must contain experiential variables. An outcome deviation conditioned on intention would push any completion toward the fourth route. None has been observed, and at the neural grain the test is well beyond current reach ([tenets](/tenets/)).

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism).** Tenet 1 is the commitment the hypothesis denies and the operative exclusion. The view accepts the reality of experience and denies its irreducibility, so against it Tenet 1 stands as a commitment the premise-sharing cluster must earn, while the hypothesis supplies a standing account of why that cluster might fail.

**[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction).** The hypothesis needs no gap in causal closure. A defender can absorb a future physics with novel structure, but a physics whose laws mention consciousness would take the fourth route, where the Map's interface sits.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction).** The tenet gains no purchase: on the hypothesis, consciousness does whatever causal work its physical basis does, so the selection step has nothing to exclude.

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds).** Orthogonal to the dispute.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits).** The tenet makes the hypothesis the Map's own problem: the Map grants E2, contests E1, and locates the disagreement in what we are ignorant of. Against the hypothesis the Map's standing is *compatible*. Persistence and the "hard to see how" intuition are predicted by it, the acquaintance reply misses it, and the replies that reach it remove a defeater without adding evidence. That is below the *suggestive* verdict the Map gives Type-C generally in [type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism](/concepts/type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism/), a verdict suited to a Type-C view that expects closure soon.
