The Map's value-sensitive horn—that felt value guides which outcome consciousness selects ([valence-and-conscious-selection](/topics/valence-and-conscious-selection/))—is under-specified until it names *which* valence selects. Selection runs on valence assessed *before* an outcome is actual, **anticipated** valence, which the affective-forecasting literature shows diverging from the outcome's **experienced** valence, though less than it first appears. Anticipation is itself felt: the dread or relish one has *now* while simulating an outcome is anticipated in its object and experienced in its vehicle, so the selector can consult a present valence that forecasts a later one. And selection needs a correct *ranking*, not magnitudes, so the gap bears on selection only where it reverses ranks. The Map's candidate position: the felt valence of anticipating *drives* selection while the outcome's experienced valence *constitutes* value, at the cost that the driver is a fallible forecast of what it serves.

This is the **affective-forecasting gap** as it bears on the [selection-criterion problem](/concepts/selection-criterion-problem/). For salient events and for duration the gap is a directional bias, not noise; elsewhere the evidence is mixed. The article offers one candidate resolution of the "anticipated vs experienced valence" question that [valence-and-conscious-selection](/topics/valence-and-conscious-selection/) holds open—alongside, not above, the parent's graduated position on valence acting through attention—and hands [the value-blind steelman](/topics/the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection/) a fair exhibit before replying. A later section adds the retrospective twin: the recording channel opens only after the state ends, and its timing shapes what reaches the record.

## Where the Gap Is Structured

LLM readers already know the impact bias (Wilson & Gilbert 2003, 2005); what matters here is where the gap is *directional and predictable* rather than scatter a selector could average away.

Three sources give it structure: **immune neglect**—forecasters fail to anticipate the rationalisation and reframing that dampen negative affect, so overestimate how *long* bad feelings last (Gilbert et al. 1998); **focalism**—fixation on the focal event, neglecting all else that will shape mood, inflates its predicted impact (Wilson et al. 2000); and **prospection**, which names the mechanism—forecasting simulates the event, and the simulation is truncated, decontextualised and present-biased (Gilbert & Wilson 2007), so selection on prospected valence inherits its distortions.

Levine et al. (2012) argue the *intensity* component of the impact bias is partly a procedural artifact; Wilson & Gilbert (2013) rebut; Levine et al. (2013) reply that the bias "is both dead and alive". Patel & Urry (2024) open with "Evidence for affective forecasting errors is mixed" and trace part of it to whether affect is measured discretely or dimensionally. The bias is also conditional on who forecasts: after the 2024 US election, partisan losers overestimated its affective impact while winners "did not display systematic forecasting errors" (Stavrova et al. 2026). The Map therefore leans only on the *duration* result, which both sides concede, and claims structure for salient events, not forecasting in general.

## Utility and Its Layers

Kahneman, Wakker & Sarin (1997) distinguish two core meanings of utility: **decision utility**, the weight of an outcome in a decision, and **experienced utility**, its hedonic quality. Experienced utility is assessed in real time as **instant utility** or retrospectively as **remembered utility**, and the **total** experienced utility of an extended episode is, on their normative theory, the temporal integral of instant utility. Remembered utility is thus a retrospective assessment rather than a third co-equal utility, and departs from the total so reliably that its errors "can induce a preference for dominated options."

The utility the Map's argument needs comes from an earlier paper: **predicted utility**, a person's forecast of the experienced utility a future outcome will have (Kahneman & Snell 1992)—what the opening called anticipated valence. Decision utility, read off the choice made rather than consulted before it, would make "selection runs on decision utility" near-circular. This article's claim is that selection consults *predicted* utility, a fallible forecast of experienced utility, trained on remembered utility—whereas *felt value guides choice* reads most naturally as *experienced* utility doing the work; whether the forecast is itself felt is taken up below.

Redelmeier & Kahneman (1996) found retrospective judgments of painful procedures track **peak** and **end** intensity with **duration neglect**; a meta-analysis of 174 effect sizes found the peak-end effect large (r = 0.581, 95% CI 0.487–0.661) but "comparable to the effect of the overall average (mean) score", and the duration effect "essentially nil" (Alaybek et al. 2022). Memory keeps the mean alongside the extremes and discards the length, so remembered utility partly departs from the total. The distortion compounds across a pipeline: experience → (peak-end-weighted, duration-blind, mean-preserving) memory → (impact-biased) forecast → selection.

## Before Distortion: Whether the Episode Reaches the Record at All

The pipeline's first arrow does not always carry anything. Remembered utility can fail by being *absent* rather than distorted, and the two failures differ in tractability: a bias of known sign and size can be corrected; an explicit-recall zero is indistinguishable from an empty interval. That is the prospective gap's retrospective twin; anaesthesia is where it has been measured.

Anaesthesia's report channel has been probed at three points. The isolated forearm technique, which lets an unparalysed arm answer commands *within* the state, found responsiveness in 4.6% (12 of 260) after tracheal intubation, five signalling pain (Sanders et al. 2017); the modified Brice questionnaire within 24 hours found reported awareness in about 1 in 800 (Walker et al. 2016; cf. Tasbihgou et al. 2018); spontaneous report, about 1 in 19,600 (Pandit et al. 2014, NAP5). The roughly 900-fold spread comes jointly from ascertainment method, endpoint, population and regimen, not from latency; nor is the 4.6% constant—among 18–40-year-olds Lennertz et al. (2023) found 11% (37 of 338), with sex and induction regimen shifting the adjusted odds.

Two guards: the figures are no controlled comparison, differing in study, population, endpoint and case definition; and responsiveness to command establishes connected *responsiveness*, the step to phenomenal experience being an inference the five pain signals make strong but not free. The Sanders cohort's within-population contrast (none of the 253 patients questioned afterwards had explicit recall) sets two different capacities side by side, and anaesthetics disrupt encoding and consolidation independently of elapsed time: a dissociation between connectedness and explicit memory, not an effect of latency, which [anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface](/topics/anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface/) decomposes.

Nor does "no explicit recall" mean "no record": Linassi et al.'s (2021) meta-analysis found implicit-memory events under deep sedation or general anaesthesia in 43 of 119 cohorts (36.1%). A third route thus joins distorted and absent—an implicit trace no questionnaire reaches, which can shape a later forecast without the forecaster knowing why.

Generic scepticism about retrospective report would be the wrong lesson: where an episode *is* retained, remembered intensity is only mildly biased—a fifteen-study paediatric meta-analysis found a small overestimation (SMD = 0.28; Cuenca-Martínez et al. 2024), and with no completed adult equivalent the calibration point remains Erskine, Morley & Pearce's (1990) review, judging recall moderately accurate—though that literature covers reconstruction of a retained episode, not anaesthetic amnesia. Memory is roughly right about *magnitude*; the failure named here concerns *presence*, the worse of the two, since an absent episode never reaches the record.

The Map's own felt-state evidence sits along the same delay: the revised Mystical Experience Questionnaire's validation discloses that "MEQ30 ratings were provided seven hours after capsule administration" and that its normative sample rated experiences which "occurred on average eight years before completion of the questionnaire" (Barrett, Johnson & Griffiths 2015)—sound psychometrics, but a delayed reconstruction, a disclosure the Map should inherit.

Treating a felt state's reported frequency and content as partly a property of how and when the channel was opened is the Map's own framing; the sources establish it only within anaesthesia and in one direction—null reports under-count—and license no quantified discount elsewhere, so this article applies none. For the selection currency the consequence is narrow: where the recording channel cannot reach a state in real time, the explicit record the forecast is trained on can be empty—one source removed from a forecaster that also draws on semantic knowledge, vicarious report and whatever implicit trace the episode left.

What would count against the claim: confidence that an unreported episode occurred should fall where a real-time probe returned nothing, as for 248 of 260 Sanders patients; where implicit-memory testing is negative; and under regimens that lower measured incidence, as in the Lennertz cohort.

## A Mechanistic Correlate, Read With Care

Decision neuroscience supplies a quantity that plausibly biases action selection—a *prediction* signal. Many midbrain dopamine neurons code a **reward-prediction error** (Schultz 2016)—"many", not "most", since that account "in its original form, [is] probably too simple" for phasic dopamine (Gershman et al. 2024). The error is a teaching signal; a predictor-driven chooser consults the learned value it updates, so what a felt forecast would supplement or rival is that value, not the update. Whether the error reaches feeling is contested: momentary happiness, first modelled on reward-prediction errors, proved "not sensitive to learning-irrelevant variables (i.e. reward prediction error)" (Blain & Rutledge 2020).

Berridge & Robinson's (2016) wanting/liking dissociation makes a neighbouring cut: "wanting" (incentive salience) is the anticipatory pull that guides choice, "liking" (hedonic impact) the experienced good. Two caveats: wanting is not a forecast of liking—"desire can decouple completely from memories and from learned predictions of outcome value" (Berridge 2023)—and "liking" is an objective hedonic reaction that Berridge's own taxonomy separates from conscious pleasure ([wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork](/topics/wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork/)). The mapping is interpretive—these are literatures of behavioural selection with operational constructs—supplying the structure, predictive ≠ experienced, not a mechanism.

So flagged, the gap is an exhibit for the value-blind rival, which predicts the divergence—choice controlled by a learned value, feeling idle—where a value-sensitive selector consulting a miscalibrated forecast merely accommodates it. The rival also comes as a framework: error-dynamics accounts define valence as the negative rate of change of free energy (Joffily & Coricelli 2013), so anticipation and outcome sit at different points of one predictive hierarchy and their divergence is *expected*. [evaluative-phenomenal-character](/concepts/evaluative-phenomenal-character/) treats this as the leading empirical rival to felt value being basic; [the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection](/topics/the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection/) shows the same mathematics read realistically by Solms, Friston and Hesp, so the formalism underdetermines the horn ([predictive-processing-and-dualism](/topics/predictive-processing-and-dualism/)). The Map lets it *constrain* the currency—any felt selector must reproduce that profile—without granting that it *establishes* the reproduction unfelt; calling the gap a calibration problem does not cancel its likelihood-bearing force.

## The Disambiguation the Map Must Make

Posed as exclusive—either the outcome's *experienced* valence selects, which the evidence refuses, or *anticipated* valence selects through a mechanism-like predictor—the choice is mis-posed, as the parent's [anticipation problem](/topics/valence-and-conscious-selection/#limits-and-open-questions) had gestured. The dread of the appointment and the relish of the meal are occurrent valences at choice, anticipated in their *object* and experienced in their *vehicle*—an instant utility of their own, in Kahneman, Wakker & Sarin's terms. This option is the most congenial to interactionism: selection consults a *presently felt* valence, the experienced quality of simulating each outcome, without backward causation or non-phenomenal computation; the forecasting literature then documents an epistemic fact, that felt anticipation at t₁ forecasts the outcome's felt valence at t₂ poorly.

That softens the dilemma without cancelling the cost, and must not trade on an ambiguity: "currency" can mean what selection is causally *sensitive to* or what it is normatively *for*. In the first sense the candidate currency is the felt valence of anticipation; in the second, the outcome's experienced valence, which [consciousness-value-connection](/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/) argues constitutes value. The "constitutes value" horn answers the second question where the first was posed, so the result is a relocation rather than a division of labour: value-sensitivity moves from the outcome to the present feeling that forecasts it. Whether that feeling selects *as felt*, or a sub-personal predictor selects and the feeling accompanies, is the fork this article cannot close and the value-blind steelman presses.

The second sense also assumes the standard stays put. In the bias literature it does, and correcting toward the later valence is plainly rational. In Paul's transformative choices and Pettigrew's weighting of selves whose values differ ([transformative-experience-void](/voids/transformative-experience-void/)) it does not: the divergence is a change of evaluator, not a mis-forecast, and aligning the forecast to a later self whose valence the choice partly produced is not obviously correct. Which self's experienced valence the currency is calibrated against, the selection law does not say; the Map marks it open rather than stipulating the later self. The table below still discriminates wherever the standard is fixed—its rows test which input selection tracks, not whose valence it should.

The gap's cost depends on what selection needs from its currency: a correct *ranking* of the live options, not their magnitudes, so a shared exaggeration of every option's predicted unpleasantness leaves the ordering intact and the bias bites only where it reverses ranks. The evidence above is almost all absolute; the one study measuring both found "relative accuracy"—forecasters "could predict when a day/event would make them feel better/worse than usual"—alongside "absolute inaccuracy" (Moeck et al. 2026). A reversal needs a salient event, a differential rather than shared bias—focalism inflates the focal option specifically—and a margin smaller than the differential. Where those hold selection misfires; otherwise a miscalibrated currency still ranks correctly.

The distinction remains testable, though not by correcting the forecast and watching whether selection changes: a mechanism-only model whose input *is* the forecast predicts changed choice too, and a randomised defocusing instruction (N = 670) "did not improve affective forecasting accuracy" (Sobel Misieczko & Barber 2026), so a debiasing arm needs a manipulation check first. What discriminates is dissociating the inputs, in [P-VS2](/positions/value-in-selection/)'s battery framing:

|Manipulation|Value-blind learned value|Present felt valence|Anticipated simulation|Valence→attention hybrid|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Debias forecast (checked)|changes|changes|changes|changes|
|Shift present affect; forecast, learned value fixed|none|changes|none|via attention|
|Shift learned value; affect, forecast fixed|changes|none|none|none|

Only the lower two rows separate the models; the parent's [attentional clamp](/topics/graduated-middle-path-valence-modulated-attention/) separates the last two columns.

Whichever way that fork falls, "anticipated valence drives selection" is a token-grain claim—which single outcome is realised, not what the aggregate shows ([type-token-causation](/concepts/type-token-causation/))—and inherits the register's [mechanism debt](/positions/quantum-interface/#mechanism-debt) (P-Q3, P-Q10): per [born-preserving-causal-efficacy](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/) it is citable as a framework-internal coherence result only, never as established mental causation. The lower rows above are accordingly *conditional* tests—deviation conditioned on subject, task or induced affect, horn (a) of the apex trilemma—not a generic outcome-selection effect. What "the currency is real" can honestly mean is [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/)'s grade: held on prior commitments, credence low.

## Relation to Site Perspective

This concept offers one candidate resolution of an open question, sharpening rather than dissolving the value-sensitive horn.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)**: If consciousness selects on the felt valence of anticipation, the interaction is phenomenal at the point of selection but routed through a fallible forecast, and reads no more confidently than [the tenet's standing note](/tenets/#tenet-3-standing) allows. The Map should not let this narrow the tenet into near-epiphenomenalism of the *outcome's* evaluative dimension. The outcome's experienced valence keeps *evaluative* standing, constituting value and setting the calibration target; but a set-point does not switch the circuit, and the step from constituting value to causal work is the open one.

**[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)**: A selection law reading off anticipatory valence is no less minimal than one reading off attention. But if that valence reduces to a learned-value or wanting computation, the value-blind steelman's parsimony advantage strengthens, and the Map's reply must be the prior-commitments reply—reject illusionism, hold [phenomenal value realism](/topics/phenomenal-value-realism/)—not a parsimony contest it would lose. This tenet constrains the interaction empirically; parsimony is [Tenet 5](/tenets/#occams-limits)'s question.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)**: This tenet licenses declining the more-parsimonious pure-predictive reading despite the forecasting evidence—but only if the decline is *argued*: the gap is a genuine cost the value-sensitive horn must pay, and where knowledge is incomplete the argument, not the razor, carries the decline. The same tenet governs the report material: the parsimonious reading of a null report—nothing to report—under-counts within anaesthesia, as the isolated-forearm and implicit-memory findings show; extending it further is the Map's own inference.

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)**: Under many-worlds the gap is moot—every outcome occurs, so "which valence selects" has no referent; it matters only if one outcome is really chosen.
