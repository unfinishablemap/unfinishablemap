Having information and having access to the fact that one has it come apart in clinical dissociation, at different levels in different conditions. Four levels need separating: **retention** (the information is stored), **performance-level use** (it shapes recall, recognition, priming or reaction times), **metacognitive access** (the subject can report, believe or judge that they know it) and **felt ownership** (retrieved content presents as *mine*). In dissociative identity disorder (DID) the replicated finding is a failure at the third level over intact first and second levels: identities that report no memory of another identity's experiences perform like healthy controls on objective tests of neutral material (Huntjens et al. 2003). A 2024 systematic review found the meta-analytic picture mixed (Beker et al. 2024). A 2025 corrected re-analysis, by authors of several of the primary studies, found "no reliable objective evidence of inter-identity amnesia" (Donath et al. 2025). Anosognosia shows the same split on a stroke lesion, depersonalisation shows ownership failing with content intact, and dissociative amnesia is the weakest instance ([explained below](#where-each-breaks)).

The decomposition is a discipline for reading evidence and carries no verdict on dualism. Performance without reported access is evidence about access and report. It shows neither that experience is missing in the identity reporting amnesia nor that a non-physical subject has been cut off from a store, and every level's failure has an intra-cerebral account ([listed below](#rivals)). What The Unfinishable Map takes from it is a rule: a claim that a condition "severs" or "compartmentalises" memory must name the level it means.

## The Four Levels {#four-levels}

These definitions are meant to be acceptable to every camp.

- **Retention**: information encoded earlier is still stored. It is never observed directly; it is inferred from performance.
- **Performance-level use**: the information changes behaviour on direct tests (recall, recognition) or indirect ones (priming, interference, reaction times, concealed-information responses).
- **Metacognitive access**: the subject represents their own state of knowing. Dienes and Perner's (1999) theoretical model of implicit and explicit knowledge places this at the top: "The clearest cases of explicit knowledge of a fact are representations of one's own attitude of knowing that fact." It can fail by *monitoring* (a false belief about one's memory) or by *control* (no search is started), and it is almost always measured by verbal report.
- **Felt ownership**: retrieved content presents as one's own past, the for-me-ness [mine-ness](/concepts/mine-ness/) describes. It is measured by self-report.

The pattern is an old neuropsychological generalisation. Schacter, McAndrews and Moscovitch (1988) gathered implicit/explicit dissociations across neuropsychological syndromes in a narrative review. Schacter (1990) then set patients "who are unaware of knowledge that they in fact possess" beside anosognosic patients "who are unaware of their deficit". His DICE model has a Conscious Awareness System that "normally interacts with, but can become disconnected from, modular-level processors", and he added that postulating it "does not in any sense 'solve' the awareness problem". Huntjens et al. (2006) gave DID the matching description: "a disorder characterized by meta-memory problems, holding incorrect beliefs about their own memory functioning."

## How "Autonoetic" Splits Across the Levels {#autonoetic-senses}

The Map's memory articles often describe dissociative loss as loss of *autonoetic* memory, the re-lived recollection of one's own past ([anoetic-noetic-autonoetic-consciousness](/concepts/anoetic-noetic-autonoetic-consciousness/)). The word covers four failures that fall at different levels:

- **Failure to encode for later recall**, as after sedation, is a level-1 failure. It shows that no retrievable trace formed, not what was experienced at the time.
- **Loss of autobiographical retrieval** is level 2, and wider than episodic memory: in fugue, personal facts go with events (Harrison et al. 2017).
- **Recognition** is also level 2, and can rest on familiarity rather than recollection.
- **Loss of felt ownership** is level 4.

Re-living itself is a manner of experiencing retrieved content. It is probed by reports such as remember/know judgements, so it is reached through level 3. Read level by level, DID is reported severed and objectively transferred.

### Dissociative Identity Disorder

Huntjens, Postma, Peters, Woertman and van der Hart (2003) tested 31 patients. Twenty-one reported complete one-way amnesia for the learning episode, yet "objectively, neither recall nor recognition scores of patients were different from those of normal controls." Huntjens, Verschuere and McNally (2012) found autobiographical transfer on a concealed-information task and concluded that the amnesia criterion "should be modified to emphasize its subjective nature." Marsh et al. (2021), with 12 and 14 analysed patients across two studies, found only a trend toward loss on free recall. On recognition, patients identified more of the other identity's event than instructed simulators or partially informed comparison participants. All of these are experiments.

The authors' own inference is narrow: parallels between the DID and simulator results made it "not possible to determine whether DID participants presented a true inability to access information experienced in another identity". The account they discuss, on which beliefs interfere with "initiating a search for a specific memory" or "the acknowledgment of memory ownership once a memory is retrieved", is a hypothesis about levels 3 and 4. No study has measured ownership.

The aggregate is disputed. Beker, Dorahy, Moir and Cording (2024), a systematic review with meta-analyses, found that the narrative findings "suggested a degree of inter-identity memory transfer", backed by two of four meta-analyses, while two "evidenced patterns closer to" amnesia. They flagged masked heterogeneity, a small homogeneous sample and few research teams. Donath, Van der Linde, Verschuere, Allen, McNally and Huntjens (2025), a commentary with a corrected re-analysis, name four errors:

- no healthy-comparison baseline, so an ordinary recency effect was read as amnesia;
- easily feigned tasks pooled with tasks designed to resist feigning;
- data-selection and effect-size errors;
- individual low scores read as amnesia rather than measurement error.

Corrected, the meta-analyses "find no reliable objective evidence of inter-identity amnesia". Three limits travel with this. It was read in the accepted manuscript, since the journal abstract is not indexed. Its authors include authors of several primary studies, so Beker et al.'s few-teams concern applies to the correction too. And no reply from Beker et al. appears in Crossref or OpenAlex as of October 2026.

Dimitrova et al. (2024), an experiment with 14 patients, 16 simulators and 16 controls, found no reaction-time difference between trauma words from another identity and self-relevant trauma words. They proposed renaming the phenomenon "inter-identity avoidance", a control-level reading of the same failure.

Transfer at levels 1 and 2 is therefore the better-supported reading, with its meta-analytic strength in dispute, and the partition sits in reported access. The page leaves DID's trauma-versus-sociocognitive origin to [clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence](/topics/clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence/). Huntjens et al. (2006) found that patients lacked simulators' strategic answer style, but a metamemory account fits either origin.

### Anosognosia

Fotopoulou, Pernigo, Maeda, Rudd and Kopelman (2010) found anosognosic stroke patients slowed by deficit-related sentences "despite their explicit denial of the self-relevance" of the same sentences; six of seven showed this individually. The explicit measure was a self-relevance rating, so it spans levels 3 and 4: it does not separate "I do not know I am paralysed" from "this paralysis is not mine". Moro et al. (2011) found implicit awareness in five of twelve patients. In three they found emergent awareness, raised by attempting the movement, so level 3 can return within a session. Fotopoulou et al.'s own mechanism is intra-cerebral: explicit awareness "requires the re-representation of sensorimotor information in the insular cortex". [anosognosia-and-the-reversible-self-monitoring-channel](/topics/anosognosia-and-the-reversible-self-monitoring-channel/) develops the reversals.

Anosognosia thus shows use without report on a focal destructive lesion, so a split between levels 2 and 3 does not by itself mark a lesion-free architecture.

### Dissociative Amnesia

Mangiulli, Otgaar, Jelicic and Merckelbach (2022), a systematic review of 128 published cases, found that "Most case studies failed to rule out plausible alternative explanations" such as ordinary forgetting and malingering. Level 1 is therefore contested in the data. Kikuchi et al. (2010), an fMRI case series of two patients, found raised prefrontal and lowered hippocampal activity for unrecognised acquaintances, a pattern that disappeared in the patient who recovered. The authors call it "memory repression": a retrieval-control failure at level 2, described in neural terms. Harrison et al. (2017), a case series of 53 cases, found in fugue "a severe and uniform loss of memories for both facts and events", so the loss follows self-relatedness rather than the episodic–semantic line. No study in the sources searched shows these patients using lost material on indirect tests, as DID patients do.

### Depersonalisation and Patient R.B.

Depersonalisation patients retrieve and report their memories, so attenuated ownership rests on self-report. A factor analysis of the Cambridge Depersonalisation Scale in 138 patients found an "Anomalous Subjective Recall" factor among four (Sierra et al. 2005). A five-factor solution in 394 patients had no separate recall factor (Simeon et al. 2008). Neither tested retrieval objectively; [depersonalisation](/concepts/depersonalisation/) develops the case. Klein and Nichols (2012), a single neurological case study, describe patient R.B., whose memories of scenes from his past were accurate but lacked the sense that they were his. They propose separate components for content and mineness. Level 4 can fail with levels 1 to 3 intact, outside any dissociative disorder.

## Two Defeaters for "Retained but Inaccessible" {#defeaters}

**Degraded representation.** Farah, O'Reilly and Vecera (1993), a theoretical model, showed a lesioned network producing covert without overt recognition when "damaged but not obliterated". Apparently inaccessible traces may be weak traces that only sensitive tests detect. The DID data partly answer this, since patients match healthy controls (Huntjens et al. 2003). The anosognosia data do not.

**Report is the weakest instrument.** Shanks (2017), a methodological review, notes that "Unconscious processing is inferred when above-chance performance is combined with null awareness". He shows how selecting unaware participants post hoc misleads through regression to the mean. Every design above pits a sensitive performance measure against a verbal report, and DID studies recruit patients who report amnesia. The critique tells against reading a level-3 failure as absent access rather than absent report. No study found applies it to DID transfer specifically.

## Rival Accounts of the Level-3 Failure {#rivals}

Five accounts compete, and all are intra-cerebral:

- false metabelief, a monitoring failure (Huntjens et al. 2006);
- avoidance or failure to search, a control failure (Marsh et al. 2021; Dimitrova et al. 2024);
- motivated self-presentation, the sociocognitive reading;
- disconnection from a physical awareness system (Schacter 1990);
- failed re-representation (Fotopoulou et al. 2010).

The Map's interface vocabulary of channels and disconnection maps onto the last two. It inherits their structure and adds no prediction they lack, the verdict [anosognosia-and-the-reversible-self-monitoring-channel](/topics/anosognosia-and-the-reversible-self-monitoring-channel/) reaches for anosognosia's reversals.

## The Discipline {#discipline}

The Map proposes five rules for any article that uses these conditions as evidence.

1. **Name the level.** "Severed", "compartmentalised" and "disconnected" each need one: retention, use, access or ownership. For DID the supported claim is about report.
2. **Read reported absence as evidence about access.** The measures reach report and behaviour. They do not reach experience.
3. **Name the substrate rung**: focal destructive lesion, conventional-MRI abnormality, group-level structural difference, microstructural/neurochemical/connectivity change, or physical cause. The clinical default is "no focal destructive lesion sufficient to explain the symptom". That is the defensible claim for DID, where a group-level structural difference still exists: hippocampal volumes are smaller, and the CA1 reduction correlates with self-reported amnesia (Dimitrova et al. 2023). Anosognosia sits on a focal destructive lesion.
4. **Name the source type**: experiment, case series, systematic/narrative review, theoretical model, or scale-development paper. The ownership datum in depersonalisation rests on factor analyses of a self-report scale, and the imaging of dissociative amnesia on a two-patient case series.
5. **Keep these levels apart from the four-step chain for dissociation arguments** in [evidential-status-discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/): separability, phenomenological reality, mechanistic independence, metaphysical irreducibility. Everything here establishes separability, and ownership adds first-person phenomenological support. Nothing reaches mechanistic independence beyond what production accounts supply, and nothing reaches irreducibility.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The page bears only on the bare form of the [Dualism](/tenets/#dualism) tenet, that consciousness is not reducible to physical processes, and it neither supports nor threatens that thesis. Levels 1 to 3 are functional. Their dissociations are what any physicalist theory of memory and report accommodates, and each failure above has a named intra-cerebral mechanism. Level 4 alone names a phenomenal feature. Its separability from content is compatible with Dualism without being evidence for it, as [mine-ness](/concepts/mine-ness/) argues.

What changes is how the Map may use the data. DID's partition lies in reported access, failed ownership is a hypothesis, and the objective data do not show separate stores. An interface reading of DID has to locate itself at levels 3 and 4, beside the metabelief, avoidance and re-representation accounts, and say what it predicts there that they do not. The other four tenets are not engaged.
