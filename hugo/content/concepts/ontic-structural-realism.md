Ontic structural realism (OSR) is the thesis that relational structure is what fundamentally exists — that individual objects bearing intrinsic natures are not part of the world's basic furniture. The name is James Ladyman's (1998), descending from John Worrall's claim that what survives theory change is "one of form or structure, not of content". Its interest for the Map is unwelcome: this is the position on which [Galileo's exclusion](/concepts/galilean-exclusion/) excluded nothing. No categorical residue exists anywhere — not in matter, and on the strongest reading not in mind — so the mathematical skeleton is the world itself rather than a filtered view.

Two qualifications belong here rather than in a closing caveat, since retrieval truncates. **First, the rival in that form is a composite the Map has assembled, not a position anyone defends in print.** OSR is a thesis about the ontology of physics and is nearly silent on consciousness; the philosophers who hold structuralism about *phenomenal character* decline the ontic reading. The [evidence and the concession](#constructed-rival) are below. **Second, the composite threatens the Map's route, not its conclusion.** Eliminative OSR would establish that *physical* entities lack intrinsic natures, not that *experiences* do. It removes the ontological-remainder argument in [physical completeness](/concepts/physical-completeness/), not the Dualism tenet, which rests on the [hard problem](/topics/hard-problem-of-consciousness/) and the anti-physicalist arguments rather than on matter having quiddities.

## What the Position Claims {#the-claim}

SEP surveys the family; what matters here is the fork — whether structure is all we can *know* or all there *is*.

**Epistemic structural realism** (ESR), Worrall's version, says the first: theories "tell us only about the form or structure of the unobservable world and not about its nature", in Ladyman's summary. Something underlies the structure and our theories cannot reach it — the Map's own commitment, declared in [physical completeness](/concepts/physical-completeness/). **Ontic structural realism** says the second. Ladyman introduced it as "a metaphysical position according to which... the success of science should be accounted for in terms of the representation of modal relations among phenomena, not in terms of continuity of reference to objects". In Kalevi Loorits's gloss, on Ladyman and Ross's position relata and structural elements "are merely heuristic devices with no fundamental ontological status."

**Moderate OSR** (Esfeld and Lam 2008) keeps objects as relata but denies them intrinsic identity — *worse* for the Map than the eliminative version. The tempting objection, urged by Chakravartty and others, is that there cannot be relations without relata; moderate OSR is built to absorb exactly that while keeping what the Map cares about, no quiddities. An argument aimed there refutes the version nobody needs to hold.

## Why This Rival Has to Be Built {#constructed-rival}

The composite above is not held by the people it is assembled from; the evidence is a matter of counting.

The Stanford Encyclopedia entry on *Structural Realism* is Ladyman's own, revised in May 2023 and running to roughly 170,000 characters. It contains **zero** occurrences of "consciousness", "conscious" or "experience", and exactly one of "qualia" — a report of Peter Unger's view: "Peter Unger (1979) also argues that our knowledge of the world is purely structural and that qualia are the non-structural components of reality." The canonical survey of OSR mentions qualia once, in the mouth of someone asserting the Map's premise. The mirror holds: SEP's *Russellian Monism* uses "consciousness" ninety-six times, with zero hits for "French", "Esfeld" or "relations without relata".

The structuralists about phenomenal character decline the association in as many words. Holger Lyre opens his neurophenomenal structuralism programme by disowning it: "One might be inclined to ask whether neurophenomenal structuralism is a version of structural realism. The short answer is: no." Loorits, whose paper is titled "Structural qualia: a solution to the hard problem of consciousness", classifies ontic structural realism as a radicalism he does not share: "only a relatively small minority of the proponents of scientific object structuralism believe that structure and relations are actually all there is."

So the Map constructs this opponent rather than meeting it, and constructing an opponent then defeating it yields a victory's appearance without its substance. What follows argues about the strongest position the assembly yields, and refutes none of Ladyman, French, Esfeld, Loorits or Lyre, who do not hold the composite. Whether someone has since staged the joining this article cannot close: Beni's 2026 framework, abstract-only here, is the likeliest candidate.

## What Would Actually Be Lost {#what-is-lost}

[Physical completeness](/concepts/physical-completeness/) argues that physics is structurally complete but ontologically partial: all the structure, none of the intrinsic nature, leaving a remainder physical description constitutively misses. If eliminative OSR is right there is no remainder to miss, and that argument goes — along with the first falsifier listed in [intrinsic nature](/concepts/intrinsic-nature/), a defensible structural realism dissolving quiddities altogether.

Dualism does not go with it. The Map does not require matter to have quiddities, only that consciousness not be exhausted by structure — and that second claim is attacked by phenomenal structuralism rather than by Ladyman. Loorits holds that "the apparently non-structural and monadic elements of consciousness, namely the qualia, are in fact compositional and have an internal structure", adding that consciousness "misperceives some of its own highly complex structural properties as monadic and qualitative." Lyre's parallel claim is that "the qualitative characters of sensory experiences are relational rather than intrinsic properties of the sense impressions of sentient beings." Those sentences threaten the Map's conclusion; nothing in eliminative OSR entails them.

Per the [evidential-status discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/), the remainder argument removes a defeater rather than establishing dualism, so losing it costs a defeater-removal rather than a premise — a real cost, and smaller than the opening framing suggests.

## Galileo Relocates; Structuralism Abolishes {#relocate-abolish}

[The Galilean exclusion](/concepts/galilean-exclusion/) page holds that Galileo decided rather than discovered — physics works without secondary qualities because it was built not to need them. SEP's treatment of early modern primary and secondary qualities reads Galileo as *relocating* the qualities rather than eliminating them: not in the bodies that cause them, but internal to sentient bodies. That complicates the "decided, not discovered" gloss slightly, since Galileo did assert the qualities' unreality in the object, and it sharpens the contrast this article needs, because relocation is itself a claim that a residue exists somewhere else. **Galileo relocates the residue into the sentient body; ontic structural realism abolishes it.**

So the Map's standard reply does not fit. Against the materialist who says the excluded qualities *reduce to* structure, the Map answers with the form-and-content argument of [reductionism](/concepts/reductionism/) and, on the [primary/secondary boundary](/topics/primary-secondary-quality-boundary/), the charge of simplicity through stipulation. Neither lands on a position that denies the remainder was ever a datum rather than omitting one it should have counted.

## Newman's Problem, and Why It Cuts Both Ways {#newman}

The best pressure point available is older than the position it presses. In 1928 M. H. A. Newman objected to Russell's structuralism that structure alone is too weak to say anything: "structure is not sufficient to uniquely pick out any relations in the world... only cardinality questions are open to discovery!" Given a collection of the right size some relation holds over it, so a purely structural claim is either trivially satisfied or must smuggle in a non-structural fact about which relations are real.

Turned on the ontic reading this becomes a problem about being rather than knowing: if structure is all there is and fixes only cardinality, the ontology threatens to empty out. Lyre's programme is the honest case, its abstract promising "a solution of the Newman problem of the brain in terms of spatiotemporal relations" — a non-structural input, declared rather than eliminated.

**The objection cuts both ways, and the Map has to say so.** Newman raised it against Russell's *epistemic* structuralism — the Map's own position — and later readers, Demopoulos and Friedman among them, pressed it as a collapse worry: structure-only knowledge threatens to reduce to phenomenalism. Deploying Newman against the ontic reading while the Map's commitment sits exposed to it would be an unearned asymmetry.

What the Map can claim is narrower — an asymmetry in how the two readings *answer*. The epistemic structural realist accepts Newman's conclusion about theoretical knowledge and locates the individuator in the unknown nature beneath the structure; the ontic structural realist, having denied any such nature, must find one inside structure or do without. That is a reply rather than a refutation, and leaves the epistemic reading owing an account of its own individuator that the Map does not have.

## Why the Map Prefers the Epistemic Reading {#the-choice}

The Newman asymmetry above is the strongest of the Map's grounds, a difference in available replies rather than a decisive result. Two further considerations bear on the choice.

**Acquaintance with one non-structural item.** The Map holds that consciousness is the one intrinsic nature known from the inside rather than by structural inference — the hinge of [intrinsic nature](/concepts/intrinsic-nature/). Loorits's debunking reply is serious: introspection presents redness as simple and is unreliable about complexity, so the simplicity behind the hard problem is an introspective artefact. His reply carries testable commitments — rejection of inverted qualia, holism about experience. The dispute is open inside the structuralist's framework, not closed by the Map.

**An ally the corpus has not cited.** Dan Stanciu (2021) argues for an epistemic rather than ontic framework for consciousness research specifically, on the ground that granting causal-ontological role only to relations neglects part of the empirical study of the phenomena.

One calibration point governs these. David Chalmers, whose structure-and-dynamics reasoning the Map leans on, is more generous to this rival than the Map has been: "There are respectable structuralist or dispositionalist views of physics on which physics involves just structure or dispositions all the way down", his objection being only that they "seem to yield a world devoid of substance or qualities". (The parenthesis naming Ladyman and Ross as his referent is Alter and Pereboom's gloss; the lecture names no one.) The Map should not be more confident against a position than the philosopher whose argument it borrows.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism).** The composite is incompatible with the tenet if it generalises to the mental, and the Map notes that rather than refuting it — a framework-boundary disagreement. The narrower finding is more useful: eliminative OSR *by itself* does not generalise to the mental, and phenomenal structuralism does the extending work, so that is where the Map's argument belongs.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits).** OSR is the more parsimonious ontology, and the Map's usual reply — that materialist simplicity is purchased by omitting the phenomenon — does not apply where the phenomenon is denied as a separate datum. The tenet's actual content still bites: simplicity is unreliable under incomplete knowledge, and Newman's problem is where the tidier ontology risks buying economy at the price of determinacy.

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds), and indexical identity.** A line the Map advances as its own, since no source consulted here runs it: if ontology is exhausted by structure, *which* structure-instance is mine is not a fact about the world, structure being abstract and multiply realisable where experience is particular and indexical. The tenet's refusal to let indexical facts dissolve into third-person description would then be aimed at structuralism generally, not only at many-worlds. The caution is real — indexicality may itself be structural, a position within the structure — so this is a line worth developing rather than a result.

## What Would Change This View, and What Went Unread

Three claims made here could be undone, and they sit in different states. **That the composite is unheld in print** is unresolved rather than tested, for the sourcing reasons below; Ladyman and Ross's "real patterns" machinery is where they would likeliest locate mental phenomena if pressed. **That structuralism about qualia fails** faces a live countermodel: Lyre's programme is in progress and declares its non-structural input, and Loorits's debunking of apparent monadicity is unrefuted here. A structural account of phenomenal character solving the Newman problem of the brain without privileging any relation from outside would remove the Map's central objection. **That the epistemic reading survives Newman better than the ontic** is contested: the collapse worry against Russell is live and this article does not settle it. So the Map's preference is argued but not secured: both claims doing the work — the acquaintance and Newman asymmetries — face standing replies.

The sourcing gaps sit close to the argument and are reproduced rather than omitted. **Alter (2016)**, *Noûs* 50(4), the central paper on the structure-and-dynamics argument, was not retrieved and nothing here characterises it. **Ladyman and Ross (2007)** was not retrieved: every characterisation of it above is secondhand, via Ladyman's SEP entry or Loorits's gloss. **French (2014)** was not consulted, and no position is attributed to him beyond SEP's. **Fink, Kob and Lyre (2021)** was not retrieved — the stronger formulation of phenomenal structuralism originates there and goes unquoted. **Beni (2026)**: abstract only. **Stanciu (2021)** and the SEP early-modern entry came through publisher summaries rather than raw text, which is why the Galileo passages go unquoted.
