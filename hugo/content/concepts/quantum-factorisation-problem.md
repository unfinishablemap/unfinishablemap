Quantum entanglement is a property a state has only relative to a division of the total Hilbert space into subsystems — a *tensor product structure* (TPS) — and Hilbert space does not come with one. Zanardi, Lidar and Lloyd (2004) put the point in one sentence: "the partition of a quantum system into subsystems is dictated by the set of operationally accessible interactions and measurements." Change the accessible observables and a product state becomes entangled, or an entangled state becomes a product. The **quantum factorisation problem** is the question of which factorisation is the right one, and Tegmark (2015) named it (in the literature's American spelling, the "quantum factorization problem") while arguing that it "appears intimately related to the nature of an observer."

The consequence for consciousness theories is direct. Any claim that entanglement *unifies* an experience, or *individuates* a subject, or marks the boundary between one mind and two, presupposes a factorisation in which the entanglement in question is defined. Such claims therefore owe three things, kept apart in the [three-level decomposition](#three-levels) explained below: a fine-grained factorisation, a subject boundary drawn within it, and a rule excluding the nested and overlapping candidates that any entangled whole contains. No account located as of 2026-09-25 pays all three, and no interactionist-dualist treatment of the problem as such was located at all.

This page is a constraint on the Map's own programme, not evidence for it. The exposure reaches past the Map's speculative binding pages. The Map's preferred mechanism, [post-decoherence selection](/concepts/post-decoherence-selection/), has consciousness choose among *einselected* outcomes, and einselection is defined relative to a system/environment split. That split is shared with every decoherence-based account; the Map's own debt is which split is the paired subject's, which the mechanism's statement nowhere books. The [Map's exposure](#map-exposure) section makes that case; the [inversion](#dualist-inversion) section states a candidate dualist reply and why it relocates the debt rather than discharging it.

## Entanglement Is Relative to a Factorisation

The neutral core, accepted across interpretations, is a piece of mathematics. A finite-dimensional Hilbert space of composite dimension can be written as a tensor product of smaller spaces in many inequivalent ways, and the notions of "subsystem", "product state", "reduced state" and "entanglement" are all defined with respect to one such writing. Zanardi (2001) showed that a set of operationally accessible observables "selects a preferred tensor product structure defining a partition into subsystems", so that "The notion of compoundness for quantum systems is accordingly relativized." Zanardi, Lidar and Lloyd (2004) generalised the result: the emergent multipartite structure "and the associated notion of quantum entanglement are then relative and observable-induced." Kabernik, Pollack and Singh (2020) extended it to *generalised bipartitions* fixed by the irreducible representations of a restricted observable algebra. What fixes the algebra is the open question: dynamics and locality on the mereology programme, an observer's access on the operational reading.

Nothing here is contested, and nothing here is specific to consciousness. What it removes is the option of treating entanglement as an intrinsic, observer-independent unity-maker — the option the strongest entanglement-binding claims need. If nothing observer-independent fixes the TPS — the question the mereology programme below tries to settle; even the prompting outer review granted that locality and interaction structure can privilege some divisions — then using entanglement to constitute the observer is circular.

## Three Levels of Debt {#three-levels}

The ChatGPT outer review of 2026-09-25 that prompted this page ran three demands together: which decomposition is privileged, where the subject's boundary lies, and why one subject rather than nested ones. They have different literatures and different status, and conflating them lets a result at one level pass as a solution at another.

1. **Level 1 — the fine-grained factorisation.** Which tensor factorisation of the total Hilbert space counts as *the* subsystems? This is a problem for everyone who uses quantum mechanics, physicalist Everettians included, and it is where the physics literature works.
2. **Level 2 — the subject's boundary.** Given elementary factors, which *collection* of them is the one entangled whole that is a subject: a brain, a microtubule network, or the brain together with the environment it is already decohering into? No quantum-specific solution was located.
3. **Level 3 — exclusion and nesting.** Entangled subsystems of an entangled whole are themselves candidate wholes, and so are supersystems. Why exactly one subject and not a nested hierarchy? This is the quantum face of the [combination-problem](/concepts/combination-problem/).

Level 1 is shared. Levels 2 and 3 are the Map's own debt, and they are the quantum form of the *pairing* gap the [positions register](/positions/subject-census/) already books as [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2).

## Level 1: Tegmark and Quantum Mereology {#level-1}

Tegmark's 2015 paper coined the term and reversed the usual direction of relevance: on his account "it is not so much that quantum mechanics is relevant to the brain, as the other way around." The problem, as he states it, is "why do conscious observers like us perceive the particular Hilbert space factorization corresponding to classical space (rather than Fourier space, say)", and he notes that "This fundamental problem has received almost no attention in the literature." His proposed criterion, the *cruelest cut* — factorise so as to minimise the interaction Hamiltonian between parts, then recurse — "fails catastrophically when confronted with observation": the maximally independent decomposition is the energy eigenbasis, in which "all change grinds to a halt." His quantum generalisation of integrated information fares no better: the state of an arbitrarily large system "can never contain more than about a quarter of a bit of integrated information", so "the integration principle must be supplemented by at least one additional principle." Tegmark's programme is physicalist and his positive proposals fail by his own report; the diagnostic result, that integration and independence together do not pick out the observer, is what survives. The quarter-bit bound is his own state-based measure and not a no-go on every quantum unity notion, but it tells directly against any version of entanglement binding that reads unity off the *state* rather than the dynamics.

The subsequent programme, sometimes called *quantum mereology*, tries to read a preferred TPS off the Hamiltonian. Cotler, Penington and Ranard (2019) showed that the energy spectrum generically fixes a unique description in terms of local degrees of freedom, on the condition that such a local description exists. Carroll and Singh (2021) argued that a preferred system/environment bipartition "can be defined by looking for subsystems that exhibit quasi-classical behavior", with pointer states robust under environmental monitoring, via an algorithm that (per their abstract) minimises entanglement growth together with internal spreading. Loizeau and Sels (2024) list as "The minimal ingredients to describe a quantum system … a Hamiltonian, an initial state, and a preferred tensor product structure", and propose reading the subsystem count from the spectrum. Zanardi, Dallas, Andreadakis and Lloyd (2024) offer a dynamical criterion of minimal scrambling. (Their 2019 "Mad-Dog Everettianism" essay states the Hilbert-space-fundamentalist motivation.)

Whether any of this yields a *unique* factorisation is disputed and, as of 2025–26, unresolved. Stoica (2022) argues that "whenever such a structure emerges from the Hamiltonian and the state vector alone, if it is physically relevant, it is not unique", and states that his result covers "various proposals based on decoherence". Soulas, Franzmann and Di Biagio (2025, preprint) reply that Cotler et al. has "been widely misinterpreted", that Stoica is "correct only in some weaker version", and that "a Hamiltonian and a state are enough structure to uniquely select a preferred tensor product structure." No published resolution was located. Neither side addresses minds, and the point that matters for this page holds either way: even a clean uniqueness theorem delivers local elementary factors, or a single object/environment cut chosen for classicality. It does not say which collection of factors is a subject and does not exclude nested candidates. Tegmark's cruelest cut, applied recursively, keeps cutting wherever interaction is smallest, and so decomposes any candidate subject into its least-coupled parts; the binding hypothesis needs a criterion that stops at the coupled whole, and has not supplied one. (Carroll and Singh's cross-cut criterion, read from their abstract, would isolate such a whole rather than dissolve it.) Not a refutation, but a sign the hypothesis cannot borrow Level 1 machinery without argument.

## Levels 2 and 3: Boundary and Exclusion {#levels-2-3}

The only worked rule for Levels 2 and 3 in the consciousness literature is [IIT](/concepts/integrated-information-theory/)'s exclusion postulate. IIT 4.0 (Albantakis et al. 2023) states that "the exclusion postulate requires that the substrate of consciousness must be constituted of a definite set of units, neither less nor more", and selects among overlapping candidates by maximal existence: "the one that exists is the one that exists the most." This is a genuine answer at Levels 2 and 3, the benchmark any pairing law must match or beat. But IIT's candidates are subsets of a *pre-given* set of units. The unit decomposition is an input, Level 1 again — the gap Barrett and Mediano (2019) press in arguing that Φ is not well-defined for general physical systems, and that Barrett (2014) tried to close by formulating IIT over fundamental fields. The quantum generalisation inherits the same given: Zanardi, Tomka and Campos Venuti's (2018) quantum IIT is defined on interacting networks of quantum systems, and Chalmers and McQueen (2022) describe it as covering "networks of finite-dimensional non-relativistic qudits." So the most developed consciousness-collapse model, which must say "which systems can collapse quantum wave functions", takes its subsystems and partitions from a fixed qudit network, whose factorisation is taken as given. Kremnizer and Ranchin's (2015) integrated-information-induced collapse has the same inherited status. (That Zanardi, who showed the TPS is observable-relative, built quantum IIT on a fixed qudit network is this page's observation, not his.)

Three further positions mark out the Level 3 terrain. Gambini and Pullin (2025) propose, from a panprotopsychist starting point, that entanglement *is* the subject-summing solution: "In entangled systems, the properties of individual parts disappear, giving rise to an exponential number of emergent properties and states." On their abstracts, they are the clearest published instance of the move this page audits, and the abstracts do not mention factorisation or nesting; whether the full texts do is unchecked. Ismael and Schaffer (2020) take quantum holism to its metaphysical limit: if nonseparability grounds unity, the maximally unified thing is the cosmos, and the view drifts toward priority monism — the cosmopsychist pressure [quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity](/topics/quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity/) already names. Georgiev (2021), as [entanglement-binding-hypothesis](/concepts/entanglement-binding-hypothesis/) already cites him, reads a tensor-product factorisation of the mind's state as a *split* into independent minds. That is a partial Level 3 rule, and a useful one, but it assumes a given TPS and so inherits Level 1 whole. Quantum interactive dualism, as the Map reads Stapp, answers Level 2 by stipulation: the process acts on some part of the agent's brain, and no factorisation criterion is offered. That stipulation is the Map's own inheritance.

## The Map's Exposure {#map-exposure}

**The preferred mechanism.** The factorisation debt reaches the whole interface programme, not only its speculative wing, at two levels kept apart here. The Map's preferred mechanism, registered as [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1), has consciousness select among the outcomes that survive [decoherence](/concepts/decoherence/). The menu of outcomes is fixed by einselection, and pointer states are defined relative to a system/environment split. That split is precisely what Carroll and Singh (2021) try to *derive*, and "various proposals based on decoherence" are among the views Stoica's non-uniqueness result claims to cover. The [apex statement of the programme](/apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme/) describes the einselected menu in detail and, at this page's writing, mentions neither factorisation nor tensor-product structure. That dependence is Level 1, shared with every decoherence-based account and booked for completeness, not as a liability; [many-worlds](/concepts/many-worlds/) already concedes that the selection programme relies on the einselection machinery the Map presses against Everett, and a Soulas-style uniqueness theorem would rescue the bipartition for everyone. The Map-specific debt is Level 2: a paired subject, unlike a physicalist observer, cannot inherit the split from a physics it is not identical with, so the pairing must say *which* split the einselected menu belongs to — whose pointer basis, which subsystem is the subject's — and no Level 1 result supplies that. This is the page's finding.

**The binding cluster.** [quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity](/topics/quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity/) states the Level 1 point in one sentence and routes the rest to the subject census. [entanglement-binding-hypothesis](/concepts/entanglement-binding-hypothesis/) answers Georgiev's separability objection by moving binding from coherence into the interaction Hamiltonian, so that a bound subject is a persistently coupled, non-factorisable whole. The move is legitimate, and it silently assumes Level 1 is settled: "subsystems" and "the interaction Hamiltonian between them" are defined only once a TPS is chosen. The natural way to choose one from the Hamiltonian — minimise the cross-cut interaction — is the cruelest cut, which recurses and never stops at a coupled whole. The hypothesis needs a cut that stops there, and it has not said what fixes the factorisation in which its coupling counts as binding.

**Tenet 4 parity.** The Map's indexical argument against many-worlds asks why *I* am this branch rather than another, and needs a determinate "I" to ask it. The debt is symmetric: Everettian branches are decoherence-defined and so equally factorisation-relative (Stoica 2022), and [many-worlds](/concepts/many-worlds/) already concedes the shared dependence from the Map's side. The parity weakens the indexical *argument* on both sides and leaves Tenet 4's standing where [tenets](/tenets/) locates it, in the subjecthood posit.

## The Consciousness-First Inversion {#dualist-inversion}

Two authors reverse the direction of the challenge. Tegmark holds that the properties that make something an observer should fix the factorisation. Stoica (2025) applies his non-uniqueness results to the observer itself and concludes that structure cannot be the whole story: "none of these structures emerge uniquely", and so "there exists something beyond relations, the intrinsic essence of the 'stuff in relation'." This is the closest non-physicalist engagement located. Its ontology is intrinsic-natures and Russellian-monist rather than interactionist-dualist, so the Map should treat Stoica as a convergent ally on the premise, not as a fellow dualist.

The inversion suggests a dualist candidate. The *pairing* of a non-physical subject with a physical system could be what privileges a factorisation, in the way Zanardi, Lidar and Lloyd make the TPS relative to accessible observables: the subject's accessible observables would fix its own system. The Map names this route as a candidate direction and declines to present it as a solution, for three reasons. It is circular if the same factorisation is then used to individuate the subject. It collides with the census-circularity gap already booked under [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2), since a pairing that defines the subsystem cannot be tested by subsystem-level signatures. And it is a new, unwritten posit. On this route the factorisation debt becomes the pairing-law debt, in a sharper form, rather than being paid.

Both readings of the problem are live: as a debt owed by entanglement-based unity claims, and as a reason to think observers fix structure rather than the reverse. The Map cannot help itself to the second without a pairing law.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**Tenet 1 (Dualism)** receives both-edged support. Stoica's argument that structural non-uniqueness leaves observers underdetermined by structure aligns with the Map's anti-structuralism, but it reaches only *compatible*: it is an intrinsic-natures argument that a Russellian monist can accept, and the same argument shows that entanglement-structure cannot individuate a subject either. A dualist who rejects structure-as-subject cannot also rest individuation on an entanglement structure. That is a framework-conditional implication of Tenet 1, and the Map accepts it.

**Tenet 2 (Minimal Quantum Interaction)** needs a *where*. A minimal influence exercised at the point of post-decoherence selection acts within a menu, and the menu is factorisation-relative. Until the programme says which system/environment split its einselected menu belongs to, the mechanism's location is unspecified, and that is the pairing debt again. This is a Map reconstruction of the mechanism's own commitments, and reaches no evidential tier: it concerns the Map's model, not the world.

**Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction)** is not directly touched. Stapp's stipulation that the brain is the system shows an interactionist can proceed without a factorisation criterion; it also shows that no interactionist treatment located as of 2026-09-25 has supplied one.

**Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds)** carries the symmetric parity stated above. The Map holds the tenet as a starting point that survives the loss of this argument, but the argument's standing is weakened on both sides until a factorisation is fixed.

**Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits)** gets a clean case. Hilbert-space fundamentalism — the view that Hilbert space, a Hamiltonian and a state exhaust the fundamental ontology — is the simplest structure-only ontology on offer, and the factorisation problem is where it cannot fix its own subsystems without further input, on Stoica's reading, or can do so only generically and conditionally, on Cotler's. Simplicity of the base did not settle which parts the world has. This is a documented dispute in the physics literature, not a Map reconstruction; the Map's use of it is framework-conditional.

The page's status is a booked constraint: it adds no evidential support to any tenet, records a dependency [many-worlds](/concepts/many-worlds/) had stated against Everett but the selection programme's apex statement had not booked, and gives the outer review's challenge a register address.
