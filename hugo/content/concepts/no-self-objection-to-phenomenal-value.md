If there is no self, there is no one for value to be good or bad *for*. That is the no-self objection to phenomenal value, and it targets the second link in a chain the Map relies on throughout its ethics: intrinsic value requires that something *matter*, and mattering requires a *subject* to whom it matters ([consciousness-value-connection](/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)). Two independent traditions deny that any such subject exists. Buddhist *anātman* holds that a person is nothing over and above the five aggregates, and the doctrine of momentariness (*kṣaṇikavāda*) adds that those aggregates last no longer than a moment; Derek Parfit's reductionism holds that a person's existence just consists in a brain, a body and a series of interrelated events, with no "further fact" of identity over and above them. If the mattering-recipient is a fiction, the objection concludes, either intrinsic value collapses or it must be re-grounded in something that needs no self.

The Map's reply — stated here as the Map's, on structural ground it shares with realists about the subject who are not dualists — is that the objection runs two things together. A *persisting, substantial* self, which the no-self traditions deny, is not what the value chain draws on. What the chain needs is a *momentary experiential locus*: the for-me-ness of a token experience, the fact that this pain is bad for whoever is undergoing it now. Momentariness does not remove that locus; it multiplies it. The reply is defensible but not free. It obliges the Map to say what the locus is if it is neither mechanism nor a Cartesian substance; it leaves persistence-dependent goods — prudence, narrative meaning, a life going well — resting on the persisting subject the Map asserts elsewhere, in its agency cluster and on different grounds; and it faces one rival it cannot yet refute: the claim, pressed hardest in Thomas Metzinger's minimal-phenomenal-experience programme, that experience can occur with no first-person locus at all. That claim is open, and the objection's strongest form stands or falls with it.

## The Objection Stated Neutrally {#objection}

The objection has a structure every party to the debate can accept as a statement of the dispute, whatever they think of its premises:

1. **Value requires mattering.** Intrinsic value that is good for no one and bad for no one is, in the Map's phrase, physics arranged one way rather than another.
2. **Mattering requires a subject.** For something to be good or bad *for* someone in the evaluative sense there must be a someone — a locus from which things go well or badly.
3. **There is no self.** Nothing persists beneath the stream of psychophysical events except the causally linked events themselves; the person is a convenient designation for the aggregate, and identity across time is not a further fact.
4. **Therefore** the mattering-recipient the first two premises require does not exist, and intrinsic value must either be given up or re-grounded in something that needs no self.

The first two premises are the Map's own (the [mattering-recipient route](/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/#the-mattering-recipient-route) and the realism registered as [P-VS3](/positions/value-in-selection/)); the objector grants them for the sake of argument or treats the objection as a reductio of the second. Three exits exist: deny premise 3 and defend a self; deny premise 2 and accept impersonal value; or show that "subject" in premise 2 and "self" in premise 3 are not the same thing. The Map takes the third, with a residue left over from the first, [explained below](#maps-reply).

The objection is not illusionism: both traditions affirm that experience occurs and deny only that anyone owns it. Nor is it the [combination problem](/concepts/combination-problem/), though [subjective-aim](/concepts/subjective-aim/) notes that momentariness poses a near relative of it — what holds an aim together across moments if each moment perishes.

## Two Sources of the Missing Self {#sources}

The no-self premise is not parochially Buddhist; a mainstream secular metaphysics reaches the same verdict, and the two sources cut at different grains.

### Anātman and momentariness

**What the tradition holds.** Christian Coseru's survey in the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2012) presents the not-self doctrine as one that "postulates that human beings are reducible to the physical and psychological constituents and processes which comprise them" — the five aggregates of form, feeling, apperception, volition and consciousness. In the *Milindapañha*'s chariot exchange, as Coseru summarises it, an individual "is nothing but a conventional designation that applies to the five aggregates." Momentariness sharpens this: on *kṣaṇikavāda*, "phenomena do not endure for more than a moment," so even the aggregates are successions of momentary events. The tradition answered the resulting continuity puzzle with mechanism rather than a self — the mind-stream (*citta-santāna*), the Theravāda life-continuum mind (*bhavaṅga-citta*) and the Yogācāra repository consciousness (*ālaya-vijñāna*) were developed, in Coseru's words, "precisely in order to avoid the metaphysical implications of the traditional notion of self." Vasubandhu's *Treatise on the Negation of the Person*, the ninth chapter of his *Abhidharmakośa*, is the classical refutation, aimed at Brahmanical *ātman* and at the Buddhist Personalists (*Pudgalavādins*), whose persons-in-dependence-on-the-aggregates the mainstream rejected.

**What the scholarship disputes.** Whether not-self is an ontological thesis or a soteriological instruction is contested — Coseru describes the reductive analysis as driven by an "overriding soteriological (or even therapeutic) concern" — and only the ontological reading generates premise 3. It is also disputed whether the analysis reaches consciousness itself or only the constructed person: the [emptiness challenge](/concepts/buddhism-and-dualism/#emptiness-challenge) takes the former reading, and [buddhism-and-dualism](/concepts/buddhism-and-dualism/) records that granting a minimal self conventionally while denying it ultimately is one route through that disagreement, not a consensus. Mark Siderits (1997; 2015) reads Abhidharma as a *reductionism* that retains persons as conventionally real, so that ethical discourse is re-based rather than eliminated; Javier Hidalgo (2024) argues that this Buddhist reductionism, on which "personal identity is not what matters and can also be indeterminate," survives objections Parfit's version cannot.

**What the Map proposes.** The Map observes — and this is the Map's observation, not a Buddhist argument for value realism — that *feeling* (*vedanā*) is one of the five aggregates. The analysis that dissolves the person leaves the felt tone of each moment in the inventory of what remains. That is the foothold the reply below stands on.

### Parfit's further fact

Parfit's reductionism in *Reasons and Persons* (1984, Part III) holds that personal identity consists in physical and psychological continuity, with no further fact — no separately existing entity — whose persistence identity consists in; his more famous further claim is that identity is not what matters, since what matters is Relation R, psychological connectedness and continuity with the right kind of cause. Parfit's persons are not momentary; they are temporally extended series held together by overlapping chains of connection. What he removes is the *owner* of the series. The Map rejects this account of identity on independent grounds in [parfit-reductionism](/concepts/parfit-reductionism/), because its [indexical](/concepts/haecceity/) commitments require a fact about which subject this is. That rejection is not assumed here: the reply grants Parfit's structural point for the sake of argument and asks whether the value chain survives it.

## The Map's Reply: A Locus, Not a Career {#maps-reply}

The Map's reading, marked as the Map's: premise 2 asks for a someone-to-whom, premise 3 denies a persisting someone, and the gap between those demands is where the objection fails.

Three philosophers who disagree about almost everything else mark the distinction the reply needs. Dan Zahavi's *minimal self* (2005; 2014) is the first-personal givenness of experience — its for-me-ness — identified *with* the experiential dimension rather than posited behind it, present whenever experience is; his defence of it against no-self readings (2011) turns on exactly this thinness. Galen Strawson's *thin subject* (2003; 2009; 2017) is a realist alternative to no-ownership views on which a subject exists only while there is an experience for it to be the subject of, so subjects may be short-lived and serially replaced without being unreal. Miri Albahari (2006; 2011), writing from within Buddhism, separates a constructed personal self, which she takes to be a two-tiered illusion, from an unconstructed witnessing awareness that is not. Each retains a subject-pole per experience while denying, or declining to assert, a substantial self that persists. The Map borrows the structure and not the metaphysics: Strawson is a physicalist of a panpsychist stripe, Zahavi's minimal self is a phenomenological finding rather than a dualist posit, and Albahari's further claim that witnessing awareness is unconditioned is a thesis the Map does not inherit.

Read at this grain, the value chain survives. The badness of a pain-moment is bad *for the locus of that moment* — for whoever is undergoing it, now. Nothing in the mattering-recipient route says the recipient must have a career. Momentariness does not remove the locus; it multiplies it, since every moment in the stream has its own for-whom, and on the Map's reading the Buddhist inventory itself leaves the felt tone of each moment standing. The no-self critic who insists that the value-in-experience reading "leans on there being a subject" ([wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork](/topics/wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork/)) is right, and the modest claim that article defers — that an unfelt liking reaction has no one it is good for — is cashed here: the "no one" it lacks is a locus, not a life.

The reply has three costs, and the Map pays them in the open.

**It owes a positive characterisation of the locus.** If the locus is neither the phenomenal self-model of Metzinger's [self-model-theory-of-subjectivity](/concepts/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity/) — which is mechanism — nor a Cartesian substance — which the no-self traditions rightly deny — the Map must say what it is. Its answer, developed in [self-and-self-consciousness](/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/) and [mine-ness](/concepts/mine-ness/), is that the locus is the subject-side of experience itself rather than an entity behind it: for-me-ness is the form conscious states take, and by [Tenet 1](/tenets/#dualism) that form is not captured by physical description. That is a commitment, not a discovery, and the [ownership void](/concepts/mine-ness/#the-ownership-void) records that the mechanism by which experience becomes someone's cannot be introspected.

**It leaves diachronic value resting on other ground.** Prudence, promises, desert, narrative meaning, the fear of death and the sense in which a *life* goes well all require a subject that persists. The Map does hold such a subject — [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/), [P-SC3](/positions/subject-census/) and [Posit One](/tenets/background-commitments/) register it — but on grounds drawn from [agency](/concepts/agent-causation/) and from [Tenet 4](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)'s indexical objection, not from value ([where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) traces where the commitment enters). This page deliberately does not draw on those commitments. If they fell, the base fact of mattering would survive them; what would go is the diachronic layer of value — a large loss, but a different one, fought in [parfit-reductionism](/concepts/parfit-reductionism/), [personal-identity](/topics/personal-identity/) and the [Mādhyamaka section](/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/#buddhist-no-self-and-the-madhyamaka-challenge) of the minimal-self article rather than here.

**It concedes the objection's target.** The Map asserts no *ātman*; its Dualism tenet requires irreducibility, not permanence ([buddhism-and-dualism](/concepts/buddhism-and-dualism/)). Against the Buddhist source the disagreement is therefore narrow — whether the momentary for-me-ness survives the analysis of the person — and that is where the strongest rival enters.

## Rival Readings at Full Strength {#rivals}

### Experience for no one

The radical no-self reading denies not the persisting self but the subject-pole itself: the sense that experience is *for* someone is one more constructed representation, and experience can occur with no first-person locus at all. Metzinger's minimal-phenomenal-experience (MPE) programme is its sharpest contemporary form. Taking meditators' reports of "pure awareness" as its entry point, the 2020 paper asks whether selfhood and perspectivalness are "necessary conditions for phenomenality" (p. 6) and advances the working hypothesis that "MPE is non-egoic self-modelling: as such it is atemporal, selfless, and not tied to an individual first-person perspective" (p. 36), with the further empirical hypothesis that the prototype of pure awareness "really is the content of a predictive model, namely, a Bayesian representation of tonic alertness" (p. 1). Metzinger is a naturalist whose self-model theory the Map engages as a rival, not an ally; his position here is a research programme's stated working hypothesis, not a result. Lilian Alweiss (2022) presses the same point against Zahavi from within phenomenology, arguing for self-consciousness without an "I."

If this reading is right, premise 2 fails at the base rather than at persistence, and the Map's reply collapses into the objection. The Map's response is that the MPE reports are compatible with a locus that has lost its self-*model* — its sense of being a bounded, located, agentive someone — while remaining a locus; that is how [contentless-awareness-evidence](/topics/contentless-awareness-evidence/) and [witness-consciousness](/concepts/witness-consciousness/) read the same reports. The reading is not forced by the reports, though Metzinger's own phrase "non-egoic self-modelling" shows that even his account retains a modelling relation in the state. One consequence is worth drawing out: if an MPE episode is valenced — and the contemplative literature Metzinger surveys reports peace and bliss — then on the Map's view it has a locus; if it is value-neutral, it is not a counterexample to the claim that *valued* experience has one. A verified report of a valenced experience that was genuinely for no one is what would refute this page, and none has been produced.

### Value without an owner

Buddhist reductionism and Parfit's offer a second exit: keep value, drop the owner. Pain is bad; it is simply not bad *for* a further-fact self, and person-talk is retained as conventionally true because it tracks conventionally real streams. Siderits (2016) takes up how Buddhist ethics is structured once persons are reduced; Hidalgo (2024) argues the Buddhist version does this better than Parfit's.

The Map's response turns on what "bad" means in the conventionalist's mouth. If it means *felt* badness, the conventionalist has conceded the locus: a felt badness is undergone, and to be undergone is to be for whoever undergoes it now. If it means impersonal disvalue felt by no one, it is the objection from Moore's isolation test and the fitting-attitude accounts, answered in [the parent article](/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/#objections-and-responses). The residual disagreement is whether "bad for the momentary locus" adds anything to "this feeling-moment is bad." The Map says it does not — the two are one fact under two descriptions, as P-VS3 says felt badness and badness are — and the reductionist may say the first smuggles in an owner. At the momentary grain that is close to terminological; it becomes substantive only when persistence is added, which returns the dispute to the diachronic layer set aside above.

### Mattering as essentially diachronic

The last rival grants the momentary locus and denies that it can be a *welfare* subject. To be harmed is to be made worse off than one would otherwise have been; benefit is the converse; both are comparisons across time that need a bearer on both sides, and pleasure-moments that are nobody's do not add up to anyone's life going well. The Map concedes the diachronic goods, as above, and holds that the base fact — that a pain-moment is bad for its locus — is not a comparison and needs no second time. Whether harm and benefit can be built from such moments without a persisting bearer is a question a serious treatment would take to the literature on prudential concern and time-bias, which this page does not engage; it is the least-developed part of the reply and is registered as such.

## What Follows from the Objection Alone, and What Only on the Map's Reading {#implications}

From the neutral statement, three things follow for everyone. Any theory that grounds value in mattering-to owes a statement of *which* subject it needs, and cannot answer no-self by pointing at experience alone. The two sources make different demands, so a reply that requires persistence fails against momentariness even if it answers Parfit. And the objection separates a base layer of value (a felt moment's goodness or badness for its locus) from a diachronic layer (a life going well), which any account must ground separately. None of this depends on dualism; any hedonism owes the same answers.

Only on the Map's reading do three further things follow: the momentary locus is non-physical (the reply's structure is available to Strawson's physicalism, but the Map identifies the locus with the irreducible subject-side of experience that Tenet 1 posits); diachronic value inherits the persisting subject the agency cluster asserts, so a deflation of that subject would strip the Map's ethics of its prudential and narrative layer while leaving its base intact; and MPE episodes are model-less rather than locus-less, a reading the reports permit and do not force.

What would move this page: a verified report of a valenced experience that was genuinely for no one (nothing decisive has come in; Metzinger's programme stands as a live countermodel, not an answered one); a demonstration that momentary for-me-ness is itself a constructed representation, Alweiss's line and the Mādhyamaka analysis in the minimal-self article (a standing countermodel); or an account on which even momentary badness is comparative (unresearched here). Calibrated to the weakest of these, the reply is unrefuted and contested, not tested.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** supplies the reply's distinctive content and its cost. Every philosopher the reply borrows from would decline the tenet — Strawson is a physicalist, Metzinger a naturalist, Zahavi and Albahari uncommitted to interactionism — and the Map cites them for the *structure* of a momentary subject-pole, not as allies. What the tenet adds is that the locus is not a brain-built model and not reducible to one; what it costs is a positive characterisation of a non-physical, non-persisting locus, of which the ownership void shows how little introspection can supply.

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)** rests, as the tenet itself records, on a non-deflationary persisting "I", and closed individualism (P-I1) and diachronic persistence (P-SC3) descend from the same root. This page has been built so that the value chain does *not* share that root — deliberate cascade isolation: the Map would rather its account of why suffering is bad not fall with its account of why it is *this* branch. The two commitments are compatible — the persisting subject the agency cluster asserts is, at any moment, a momentary locus too — but neither is evidence for the other.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** cuts both ways. No-self views are often commended as parsimonious, and the Map declines to let parsimony decide in either direction; the reply above is not a parsimony argument but a claim about what the value chain's second premise asks for.
