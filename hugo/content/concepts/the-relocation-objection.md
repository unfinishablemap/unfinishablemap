The **relocation objection** is the charge that a proposed explanation of consciousness moves the [hard problem](/topics/hard-problem-of-consciousness/) to a new place instead of answering it. In its neutral form it says only this: if a theory identifies phenomenal experience with some functional, representational or structural property P, and one can fully specify P while the question "why is having P like anything?" remains as open as the original question was, then the theory has changed the question's address without closing it. It descends from Joseph Levine's [explanatory gap](/concepts/explanatory-gap/) (1983) and David Chalmers's hard problem (1995); every camp recognises the pattern, whether or not it accepts that it succeeds.

The Map presses the objection against most of the scientific theories of consciousness it engages. Of the eleven pages in that group (ten theories and the unfolding argument), eight use the language of relocation, re-siting or renaming where the Map states its resistance. Those instances are gathered below as worked cases (see [Worked Cases](#worked-cases)). The objection has two limits the Map accepts. It cuts against the Map's own interface hypothesis as well (see [The Two-Edged Form](#two-edged)), and it earns a dialectical result, never an explanatory advantage for dualism (see [What the Objection Earns](#what-it-earns)).

## The Neutral Form {#neutral-form}

The objection has three steps.

1. **A theory proposes an identity or constitution claim.** Phenomenal consciousness *is* recurrent processing, or representational content, or a higher-order thought, or attended intermediate representation.
2. **The proposed base can be fully specified without mentioning feel.** Its description is functional, representational or structural. One can state the entire profile of the base state without the concept of what it is like to be in it.
3. **The original question transfers intact.** "Why does this neural state feel like anything?" becomes "why does *representing-thus* (or *re-entrant signalling*, or *being targeted by a higher-order thought*) feel like anything?" If the second question is as hard as the first, the theory has relocated the gap.

The objection makes no claim about the empirical merit of its target. A theory can be the best available account of *when* and *where* consciousness occurs and still be subject to it; what is disputed is the step from correlate to identity.

Two nearby uses of "relocation" in the Map's vocabulary should be kept apart from this one. [constitution-vs-causal-work](/concepts/constitution-vs-causal-work/) relocates a *dispute*: it shows that a disagreement between readings lives one level below the evidence both accept, and it insists that "Relocating the dispute to the bridge is a claim about *where* the disagreement lives, not a claim that the Map's side of the bridge is correct." [emergence-as-universal-hard-problem](/topics/emergence-as-universal-hard-problem/) generalises relocation to every reduction in science, a stronger thesis whose costs are discussed [below](#proves-too-much). The relocation objection proper is narrower than either: it concerns identity or constitution claims about phenomenal consciousness.

## The Map's Reading {#map-reading}

[first-order-representationalism](/concepts/first-order-representationalism/) states the Map's version in canonical form: "fixing the functional or representational facts leaves the phenomenal fact untouched." On the Map's reading, the objection works because phenomenal concepts pick out their referent directly, not through a causal or functional role. "Heat" is plausibly exhausted by a role (whatever causes these sensations and expands mercury), so once molecular motion is shown to fill the role, nothing is left over to explain. "Pain as felt" is not obviously exhausted by any role, so filling the role leaves a remainder. [Tenet 1](/tenets/#dualism) is the Map's commitment that the remainder is real.

The Map adds a diagnostic of its own. Several of the most sophisticated reductive replies, pressed on hard cases, end up specifying their base in terms of the feel it was meant to explain. The first-order representationalism page finds this in the replies to inverted qualia: each "buys its answer by individuating the represented property in terms of the phenomenal character it produces." When that happens the relocation becomes visible in the theory's own text, because a base specified by reference to feel cannot be what feel reduces to.

## Worked Cases {#worked-cases}

Each of these pages states the objection in terms of its own theory. The table records where each theory is said to re-site the question.

| Theory | What the theory fixes | Where the question re-sits |
|---|---|---|
| [Recurrent processing](/concepts/recurrent-processing-theory/) | Localised re-entrant signalling at Stage 3 | "why does re-entrant signalling feel like anything?" The Stage 2 to Stage 3 boundary is where felt quality "is declared to have arrived" |
| [AIR](/concepts/attended-intermediate-representations-theory/) | Attended, gamma-synchronised intermediate representations | The attention gate is "a re-description of the conditions under which experience shows up, not an account of why it is felt" |
| [Self-model theory](/concepts/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity/) | A transparent self-model | Transparency explains the sense of *being* someone, but "the hard problem is re-sited inside the self-model rather than removed" |
| [Higher-order theories](/concepts/higher-order-theories/) | A higher-order thought about a state with mental qualities | Rosenthal's mental-qualities reply "relocates the transition into felt experience rather than explaining it"; the page also finds the relocation going "one level up" if the higher-order seeming is itself felt |
| [First-order representationalism](/concepts/first-order-representationalism/) | Representational content | "why does representing-thus feel like anything?"; the four inversion replies show the move "in miniature" |
| [Attention schema theory](/concepts/attention-schema-theory/) | The brain's model of its own attention | Shifting the explanandum to the *appearance* of experience "renames the problem rather than solving it" |
| [NCC research](/concepts/neural-correlates-of-consciousness/) | The minimal neural correlate | Correlation is not identity; an illusionist reading of the data either leaves the seeming phenomenal, "relocating rather than eliminating consciousness", or owes an account of non-phenomenal seeming |

Two further cases mark the objection's boundary.

**[The multiple drafts model](/concepts/multiple-drafts-model/)** is the limiting case. Dennett does not propose an identity between experience and a base; he denies there is a further phenomenal fact to identify. The page records that on Dennett's terms MDM dissolves the interface question, since "there is no for-whom to which it could be relocated." So the relocation objection has no purchase there, and the Map's argument changes instrument. It targets the verificationist premise that an undetectable difference is no difference, and it concedes that the resulting standoff is "between two metaphysical defaults." Against eliminativism, the relocation objection gives way to a dispute over framework.

**[Global workspace theory](/concepts/global-workspace-theory/) and [IIT](/concepts/integrated-information-theory/)** are the two theory pages in the group that do not use relocation language (the unfolding-argument page is the third). The Map reads GWT as a theory of access consciousness that "says nothing about *phenomenal consciousness*". A theory that makes no identity claim about feel has nothing to relocate. IIT begins from axioms about experience, and the Map's disagreement with it runs through causal efficacy and falsifiability (see [the-unfolding-argument-against-causal-structure-theories-of-consciousness](/concepts/the-unfolding-argument-against-causal-structure-theories-of-consciousness/)). These exceptions show the objection's scope. It applies where a theory advertises a reduction of phenomenal character, and nowhere else.

## Strongest Rival Readings {#rival-readings}

**Identities need no explanation.** Ned Block and Robert Stalnaker (1999) and David Papineau (2002) argue that the demand behind the objection is illegitimate. Identities are not the kind of thing that gets explained. Asking why water is H₂O is misplaced; one can only justify the identity by its explanatory payoff. On this view, "why does re-entrant signalling feel like anything?" has no deeper answer to give, just as "why is heat molecular motion?" has none, and the persistence of the question reflects the psychology of our concepts rather than a gap in the world. This rival is the strongest because it grants every step of the neutral form and denies that step 3 marks a failure.

**The gap is conceptual, not ontological.** The [phenomenal-concepts-strategy](/concepts/phenomenal-concepts-strategy/), developed from Brian Loar (1990), agrees that the question survives and explains its survival: phenomenal concepts are recognitional and conceptually isolated from physical-functional concepts. The gap then belongs to our concepts, and physicalism predicts it. The objection describes the situation correctly and draws the wrong conclusion.

**The seeming is the explanandum.** [Illusionists](/concepts/illusionism/) accept that the question moves. They argue that it moves to somewhere more tractable, the [meta-problem](/concepts/meta-problem-of-consciousness/) of why we judge ourselves to have phenomenal states (Chalmers 2018 names the problem without endorsing the illusionist answer). The Map's [explanatory-gap](/concepts/explanatory-gap/) page grants that this move "gains only if the second is tractable where the first was not, which is asserted rather than shown." Illusionism has not yet delivered, and the Map has not shown that it cannot.

The Map's reply to the first two rivals is the one Chalmers and Jackson (2001) give. In the successful scientific identities, the higher-level concept was a role concept, and the identity is licensed because the lower-level story fills that role *a priori*. Phenomenal concepts do not behave that way. Block and Stalnaker reject the premise that successful reductions require this kind of a priori entailment. The dispute between the Map and these rivals lies at that premise, and neither side has closed it.

## What the Objection Implies, and What It Does Not {#disputed-implications}

The neutral form alone establishes something modest. A theory that claims to *explain* why experience is felt, and not merely to locate its correlates, has not yet done so. Identity theorists of the Block–Stalnaker kind concede this by replying that no explanation is owed.

A stronger conclusion follows only on the Map's reading: the persisting question marks a real ontological remainder. That conclusion needs the premise the rivals deny. Deploying the objection therefore does not add independent support to [Tenet 1](/tenets/#dualism). Repeated across seven theories, it shows that seven theories face the same question. It does not supply seven arguments for dualism.

### Does the Objection Prove Too Much? {#proves-too-much}

If every reduction relocates rather than resolves, then the objection counts against thermodynamics as much as against recurrent processing theory. The Map's pages divide on this. [emergence-as-universal-hard-problem](/topics/emergence-as-universal-hard-problem/) holds that "relocation rather than resolution — characterises every successful reduction," including temperature. [neural-correlates-of-consciousness](/concepts/neural-correlates-of-consciousness/) treats the heat case as the benchmark of a gap that closed, so that NCC findings becoming "increasingly intelligible" in the same way would count against the Map. On the universalist reading, the objection stops singling out consciousness and becomes a thesis about reduction in general. On the benchmark reading, it depends on phenomenal concepts differing from role concepts, which is the Chalmers–Jackson premise. The Map's deployments against the scientific theories use the second reading. The universalist page is a further and more contested proposal.

## The Two-Edged Form {#two-edged}

The objection applies to the Map's own positive view. [attention-as-interface](/concepts/attention-as-interface/) turns it explicitly against the interface hypothesis. Specifying where consciousness acts and how it might act leaves the question of why phenomenal attention exists, so that "The hard problem is relocated from 'why does neural processing produce experience?' to 'why does consciousness have this particular interface with neural processing?'" The recurrent processing page makes the same admission from the other side: "a version of it returns against the Map. Locating felt character on the non-physical side also declines to say why coupling should feel like anything; the Map relocates the mystery rather than dissolving it."

Positing a non-physical subject answers no "why is it felt?" question; it declines to derive feel from anything else. What the interface page claims instead is narrower: the hypothesis "gains value not by dissolving the hard problem but by narrowing where the explanatory gap sits." Narrowing is progress only if the narrowed question is more tractable, the test the Map applies to illusionism and must meet itself.

## What the Objection Earns {#what-it-earns}

The recurrent processing page states the limit best: "The difference claimed here is dialectical rather than explanatory." A reductive theory *advertises* a reduction and then relocates the question, so a promise has been made and not kept. The Map *assumes* irreducibility as a starting commitment and carries it openly as a debt. Neither has paid. In the recurrent processing page's words, the objection marks "a promise made and unmet, which is not the same as an explanatory advantage the Map has earned."

Three rules of use follow.

1. **Aim the objection only at identity or constitution claims.** Against a theory of correlates or of access, or one that denies any further phenomenal fact, it has no target. The GWT and multiple-drafts cases show this.
2. **Grant the empirical achievement first.** Each worked case concedes the rival's mechanism. Several also re-read the correlate as a candidate coupling site; that is Map speculation the objection does not establish.
3. **Never report the objection as a refutation.** It shows that a reduction remains owed. Showing that the reduction cannot be given would need the premise the rival readings dispute.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** is the premise the objection relies on. The objection's step 3 has ontological weight only if phenomenal concepts pick out something the functional description leaves out, and Tenet 1 is the Map's commitment that they do. The objection explains why rival theories have not displaced that commitment; it does not independently establish it.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** explains why the rivals' economy does not settle the matter. An identity theory is simpler: one kind of thing instead of two. The reply that the relocated question needs no answer turns that simplicity into a verdict. Tenet 5 holds that where knowledge of consciousness is this incomplete, parsimony is not strong enough to decide which questions are real. Tenet 5 also constrains the Map, since its warrant for the remainder is non-empirical, as the multiple drafts page concedes.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** and **[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)** enter only where the correlate is re-read as an interface. Against identity theories, Tenet 1 alone does the resisting. Once the Map proposes its own interface, the objection applies to it, as [the two-edged form](#two-edged) records. The objection does not protect the interface commitments, where the Map's empirical exposure lies.
