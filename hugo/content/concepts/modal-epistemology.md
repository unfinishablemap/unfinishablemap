Modal epistemology asks how we know what is possible, impossible and necessary. In philosophy of mind its sharpest question is whether conceiving a scenario, such as a physical duplicate of a person with no experience, gives knowledge that the scenario is metaphysically possible. The view that it can is *modal rationalism*, developed in detail by David Chalmers. At least five rival families deny that conceiving is the basic route to metaphysical possibility: counterfactual, essentialist, similarity-based empiricist, dispositional and theory-based accounts ([surveyed below](#rival-routes)).

The Unfinishable Map's zombie and Kripke-style arguments for dualism need the rationalist answer, so they rest on modal rationalism, which is one contested position among several. On the sources surveyed here, no rival account refutes the possibility of [zombies](/concepts/philosophical-zombies/) or establishes physicalism. Equally, on the Map's reading, none supports the step from conceivability to possibility on its own. Each either withholds judgement or makes the zombie verdict depend on prior knowledge of constitutive facts, of essences, of a justified theory or of actual potentialities, which is what the zombie argument was meant to supply. Rejecting modal rationalism does not mean giving up a priori modal knowledge: the counterfactual and essentialist accounts keep some form of it. What survives is the [explanatory-gap](/concepts/explanatory-gap/), Chalmers's 2007 dilemma against the phenomenal-concepts strategy, and a symmetry, since no rival delivers physicalism either ([explained below](#what-survives)).

## What Modal Epistemology Asks {#neutral-definition}

The modality at issue is *metaphysical*: what could have been the case given the natures of things, as distinct from what the laws of nature allow or what is consistent with what we know. Everyday modal knowledge (this cup could have broken) is uncontroversial. The dispute is over its source and how far it reaches.

The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* entry by Mallozzi, Vaidya and Wallner (2023) sorts accounts by the capacity they rely on, from conceiving to knowledge of essence, perception and induction, and divides them into modal rationalism, which prioritises a priori methods for knowing metaphysical modality, and modal empiricism, which pursues a posteriori accounts while often granting a priori methods some role. Its *Dependence Question* organises this article: is conceiving an independent source, or must conceivability exercises, on one answer, "be constrained by some crucial information about the (essence of) the entities involved in them"?

## The Rationalist Baseline {#modal-rationalism}

Chalmers's modal rationalism is what the Map's conceivability arguments use; [conceivability-possibility-inference](/concepts/conceivability-possibility-inference/) sets out its two-dimensional machinery. Ideal conceivability is defined by undefeatability: "S is ideally conceivable when there is a possible subject for whom S is prima facie conceivable, with justification that is undefeatable by better reasoning" (Chalmers 2002). Chalmers holds that "metaphysical modality can be explained in terms of epistemic modality plus conceptual structure, along with nonmodal truths about the actual world", and that "There is just one underlying space of possible worlds" (2014). That monism is what turns a verdict about conceiving into one about metaphysical possibility.

The Map's own reading of the zombie case, marked as the Map's: what the case supplies is sustained, prima facie, positive conceivability, in that careful reflection has found no agreed contradiction in a physical duplicate without experience. Whether that conceiving is *ideal* is a defeasible judgement, not an established result. Physicalists often grant the appearance; Papineau writes that "most contemporary physicalists will readily admit that zombies do at least appear possible, even if in reality they are not" (2007: 492). What they deny is that the appearance is evidence of possibility, and the rival epistemologies give them principled grounds for the denial.

## Rival Routes to Modal Knowledge {#rival-routes}

Each account is stated in its authors' terms; applications to zombies are labelled as the Map's wherever no source makes them.

### Counterfactual accounts: Williamson and Hill

Williamson argues that the ordinary capacity to handle counterfactuals "carries with it the cognitive capacity to handle metaphysical modality", so that "the epistemology of metaphysically modal thinking is tantamount to a special case of the epistemology of counterfactual thinking" (2007: 136, 158, as quoted in the SEP). We test a possibility by developing the supposition realistically and seeing whether a contradiction follows, and "part of the general way we develop counterfactual suppositions is to hold such constitutive facts fixed" (2007: 164, as quoted in Roca-Royes 2011). Roca-Royes presses the cost: knowing which facts are constitutive "must generalize to any constitutive fact, or we will get extensionally wrong results by over-generating possibilities" (2011: 549). Nothing here attributes to Williamson a view on zombies or dualism.

Applied to the zombie case, as the Map's inference: supposing a physical duplicate without consciousness yields a contradiction just in case the psychophysical link is among the constitutive facts held fixed. If physicalism is true it is; if dualism is true it is not. The method returns whatever one's prior view of the constitutive facts feeds it, and in the zombie case constitutive status is the point in dispute.

Christopher Hill, a Type-B materialist, aims a related account directly at the mind–body case. Necessity, for him, is truth under every subjunctive supposition: "to say that it is metaphysically necessary that p … is equivalent to saying that p would be the case no matter what else was the case" (2006). He argues that "Cartesian modal arguments for property dualism presuppose that facts about the essential natures of pain and other qualitative properties can be grasped a priori by merely conceiving of them or imagining them", and that "this presupposition fails".

### Essentialist accounts: Lowe, Hale and Kment

Essentialists locate the source of necessity in the natures of things. For Hale, facts about absolute necessity and possibility "have their source or basis, not in meanings or concepts nor in facts about alternative 'worlds', but in the natures or essences of things" (2013). Lowe's proposal "appeals instead to our capacity to grasp essences, understood in a neo-Aristotelian fashion, according to which essences are expressed by 'real definitions'" (2012), and he queries Williamson's thesis that modal truths can be explained by counterfactual ones.

Kment shows how little essential knowledge conceiving may need: "The knowledge that is required is of a negative kind and very minimal" (2021). He knows "that there is nothing in the essences of rain and Bundt-cake-hood that precludes the scenario of a Bundt cake's being made while it is raining" with only "the roughest idea of what it is to be a Bundt cake".

Applied to the zombie case, as the Map's inference: the step needs negative knowledge that nothing in the essences of the phenomenal properties, the physical properties or any law linking them precludes a physical duplicate without experience. For the Bundt cake that knowledge is uncontroversial. For zombies it is exactly what the Type-B physicalist denies, and Hill denies that conceiving gives us a priori grasp of qualitative essences. Goff's essentialist modal rationalism rescues the step on one condition: "rational coherence entails possibility when you grasp the essential nature of what you're conceiving of" (2021). Goff is a [Russellian panpsychist](/concepts/russellian-monism/) whose programme the Map treats as a rival, and his condition sits close to the strong Revelation the Map denies ([see below](#relation-to-site-perspective)).

### Similarity-based empiricism: Roca-Royes

Roca-Royes's positive account (2017, as summarised in the SEP) infers what a concrete thing could do from what actually happened to relevantly similar things, together with inductive knowledge of regularities. The SEP calls her approach modest and notes that some modest accounts are "either skeptical or silent about extraordinary or philosophical cases, such as whether the number 2 could have been the number 3 or whether there could have been philosophical zombies".

Applied to zombies, as the Map's inference: there is no actual counterpart to induce from, so the account withholds judgement. Withholding is not a verdict of impossibility. Van Inwagen's (1998) moderate modal scepticism reaches the same withholding by another route; [counterfactual-void](/voids/counterfactual-void/) treats it as a cognitive limit.

### Dispositional accounts: Vetter, and Allen's application

Vetter grounds possibility in the potentialities of actual things: "it is possible that p if and only if something has (or had, or will have) an iterated potentiality for p" (2015). Her later account bases modal knowledge on knowledge of our abilities and of the dispositions of things around us; in the SEP's words on that 2023 account, "Modal knowledge is, thus, for Vetter, distinctively empirical". Vetter stresses that it is ambitious, unlike the modest accounts; no Vetter text on zombies is cited here. Applied by the Map: a zombie world would be possible only if something actual had an iterated potentiality for it, and conceiving does not settle that.

Allen (2022) makes the application directly, for powerful qualities and pure powers alike: "the conception of properties as powerful blocks conceivability arguments unless a rather implausible form of emergence is true". The exception is phenomenal properties produced by physical ones "in virtue of a contingent form of emergence which mimics the behaviour of categorical properties"; supervenient and causal emergence, nomologically necessary on a powers view, do not qualify. She writes for physicalists, noting that her argument's success would not make powers theories inconsistent with dualism.

### Theory-based accounts: Fischer

Fischer holds that belief in an interesting modal claim, including one about what is metaphysically but not physically possible, is justified by a justified theory that implies it. Conceiving and intuiting are "downstream" from "the basic source of our justification", which is "the arguments that we give for our best theories" (2017: 25, as quoted in Biggs 2017).

Applied to zombies, as the Map's inference: zombie possibility would be justified only by a justified theory implying it, so the zombie argument cannot also serve as an independent premise for that theory without circularity, and the step is demoted from premise to consequence. Fischer's preferred version goes further. On Biggs's report it is a limited modal scepticism that turns away rationalists such as Chalmers and holds that we cannot justifiably believe or disbelieve many philosophers' modal claims, "such as that expressed by 'I could have been disembodied'". Whether it reaches consciousness, given its restriction to claims whose truthmakers are not grounded in minds, is unresolved.

### Papineau: an epistemology for strong necessities

Papineau, a Type-B physicalist, separates the two modalities. In Chalmers's summary, "Papineau suggests that epistemic and metaphysical modality have nothing to do with one another and that strong necessities are ubiquitous" (Chalmers 2014). Physicalism's support, he holds, lies elsewhere: "Most contemporary physicalists take themselves already to have conclusive evidence for this generalized claim" (Papineau 2007: 492). He finds physicalists "in the grip of an intuition of dualism that prevents them from fully believing their generalized physicalism" (2007: 492), a psychological diagnosis and no concession that dualism is true.

On the Map's reading, [strong necessities](/concepts/primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities/) are therefore not ad hoc on Papineau's epistemology or on Hale's, where necessity flows from essence, not concepts. Chalmers's reply, that there are "no clear examples of strong necessities, and the only candidates are highly tendentious" (2002), stands, as does his question of how conceivability could guide us to a modality that floats free of it. Both are a priori considerations, so they bear on plausibility rather than on evidential tier.

## What the Rivals Do to the Zombie Premise {#zombie-premise}

| Account | Effect on the zombie premise | Whose verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Modal rationalism | Delivered, if zombies are ideally conceivable | Chalmers |
| Counterfactual | Neutralised: returns the prior view of constitutive facts | Map's inference (Williamson); Hill's own |
| Essentialist | Turns on how much of phenomenal essence we grasp | Map's inference; Goff's condition |
| Similarity empiricism | Judgement withheld | SEP; Map's inference |
| Dispositional | Relocated to the metaphysics of powers | Allen's own; Map's inference (Vetter) |
| Theory-based | Demoted from premise to consequence | Map's inference |
| Papineau | Appearance explained; strong necessities ubiquitous | Papineau |

The rival routes leave zombie possibility unrefuted but take away its independence as evidence, and they leave a priori or armchair modal knowledge in place: Hill's account "allows that such claims can, in many cases, be known a priori" (2006); Hale takes some knowledge of essence to be a priori through meaning (on the SEP's account); Lowe and Kment rest modal knowledge on grasp of essence and conceptual competence; Williamson's is armchair knowledge. A physicalist can reject the conceivability route for the phenomenal case without abandoning reason as a guide to possibility.

## What Survives {#what-survives}

- **The explanatory gap.** The rivals concern metaphysical possibility, not a priori entailment, and Type-B grants the epistemic gap: "type-B materialists typically allow that there is an epistemic gap but deny that there is an ontological gap" (Chalmers 2014).
- **Chalmers's 2007 dilemma.** His master argument against the [phenomenal-concepts-strategy](/concepts/phenomenal-concepts-strategy/) needs only that zombies are conceivable, which Type-B typically grants, and assumes nothing about whether conceivability entails possibility ([type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism](/concepts/type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism/) traces its reach). No rival surveyed here touches it.
- **Symmetry.** No surveyed account delivers the psychophysical identity unless the identity is first fed in as a constitutive or essential fact. Physicalists rest it on other evidence, as Papineau says they do.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The tenet at stake is [Dualism](/tenets/#dualism), which the Map holds as a commitment, not as a result these arguments deliver. The rival epistemologies leave the tenet standing and reduce what the conceivability arguments contribute to its support.

**The cost to the Map.** The zombie argument, whose second step [zombie-master-argument](/concepts/zombie-master-argument/) states as a move from conceivability to possibility, and [Kripke's modal argument](/concepts/kripke-a-posteriori-necessity-argument/) as the Map uses it, inherit the standing of modal rationalism, and that standing is contested. On every rival route surveyed, zombie possibility no longer counts as independent evidence. [P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1) already holds that the modal arguments share the conceivability inference and fail together; that shared premise is itself the output of one modal epistemology. If modal rationalism loses to its rivals, the modal cluster's contribution falls toward nothing, and the explanatory gap and the arguments outside that cluster carry the case.

**An open debt on Revelation.** The essentialist routes need some grasp of phenomenal essence. Hill denies that conceiving supplies it a priori, Goff makes grasp of the essential nature his condition, and Chalmers's own route holds that "our most fundamental concepts are either transparent concepts or indexical concepts" (2014). The Map holds only weak Revelation, on which experience reveals the existence of a quality and part of its character, and denies the strong form ([revelation-thesis](/concepts/revelation-thesis/)). Kment's pattern suggests a way through, since partial grasp of an essence can supply negative modal knowledge in easy cases. The Map has not shown that it does so in this case. Until it does, the essentialist reading of the zombie step is a debt the Map owes, neither won nor lost.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits) does not answer these accounts.** They make no appeal to simplicity; they concern access to modal facts. The tenet binds the Map's own reasoning here: it forbids preferring modal rationalism because one space of worlds is simpler than two.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** raises one question. A consciousness with [powers](/concepts/causal-powers/) of its own does not thereby fall under Allen's exception: she treats non-physical phenomenal powers, efficacious or epiphenomenal, with her pure-powers arguments, and a world where the physical does not produce the phenomenal is "one where dualism is true", "not a relevant starting point for the conceivability argument". The exception needs contingent, brute production of the phenomenal by the physical. That bears on the Map's [pairing of powerful qualities with contingent governing laws](/topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions/), chosen partly because a zombie world is conceivable; this page leaves that question open. Neither answer would evidence the tenet. The other tenets have no bearing on the question.
