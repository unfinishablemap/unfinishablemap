The most powerful objections to a consciousness-first picture of mind no longer claim that experience is simple or given. They claim the opposite: that the self is *built*. Perception is constructed by the brain's generative models. The social self is constructed by language, culture, and mutual recognition. The narrative self is constructed by a resting brain network spinning autobiography. Even the apparent immediacy of the world is constructed—a transparent representation we mistake for unmediated contact. Each of these is true. The constructionist has won the easy half of every argument.

The Unfinishable Map concedes the construction and then draws a line that, granted its [dualist premise](/tenets/#dualism), the constructionist accounts do not cross. Across four registers—the perceptual, the social, the neural-resting, and the representational—the same move recurs: whatever is built is built *for* an experiential subject, *through* a phenomenal medium, and the building presupposes both. Construction is real. The constructor is not itself constructed, on pain of regress. This single distinction—real construction, irreducible constructor—is the spine running through perception, selfhood, and world-modelling alike. Stating it once, and watching it operate identically in four places, is the work of this synthesis.

A note on what follows. None of these four cases delivers a measurement that forces the dualist reading. Each is the Map's *interpretation* of shared data—data a sophisticated functionalist reads differently. The four domains are independent as construction phenomena, but the dualist reading applies one premise to each—that conscious content requires a subject—so their recurrence shows that premise's reach rather than four confirmations of it. By the [evidential-status discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/), such convergence raises the explanatory bar without elevating the evidence to proof.

## The Constructionist's Strongest Hand

It is worth granting the construction in full, because the dualist conclusion is only interesting if it survives the strong version of the opposing case.

In perception, [the brain genuinely architects experience](/topics/consciousness-as-perceptual-architect/). Colour constancy is not read off the wavelengths reaching the eye; the white wall *looks* white under candlelight and noon sun though the physical signal differs enormously. Perceptual learning goes further—the radiologist sees a tumour where the novice sees grey noise, the sommelier tastes minerality where the casual drinker tastes only wine. Eleanor Gibson argued this is differentiation, not enrichment: training restructures the percept itself rather than adding interpretation on top. On the constructionist's gloss, the perceptual object did not exist before the engagement that built it.

In the social register, [the self is shaped through recognition](/topics/social-construction-of-self-vs-phenomenal-self/). From Mead's symbolic interactionism through Butler's performativity, the tradition shows that identity categories—gender, role, profession—are produced through social practice, not discovered. Schachter and Singer's (1962) experiments suggested that the *same* physiological arousal could be experienced as euphoria or anger depending on social context, though replications found unexplained arousal biased toward negative feeling whatever the context (Marshall & Zimbardo 1979; Maslach 1979), and Reisenzein's (1983) review found support only for arousal intensifying emotion. Barrett's constructionist theory of emotion inherits the idea but rests on its own evidence: emotion categories are culturally learned concepts shaping how interoceptive signals are felt. The labelling is social.

In the neural-resting register, [the default mode network constructs the narrative self](/concepts/default-mode-network/). Active when the mind is not engaged externally, the DMN spins autobiographical memory, future planning, and self-referential thought—what contemplatives call "monkey mind." Killingsworth and Gilbert's 2010 experience-sampling study found people mind-wandering—thinking about something other than what they are doing—for roughly 47% of waking hours, which shows how much of waking life runs off-task but not that the off-task content is self-narrative. The narrative-self claim rests on the DMN evidence directly: damage to its regions alters self-experience, and meditation that suppresses the network loosens the sense of being a particular person. The "I" that persists through time looks, on the evidence, like an ongoing neural achievement.

And in the representational register, [the world-model conceals its own construction](/concepts/phenomenal-transparency-opacity-spectrum/). Thomas Metzinger's transparency thesis holds that the representational medium is normally invisible: you see the apple, not your visual cortex's model of it, because the constructive processes operate below introspective access. Naive realism—the irresistible sense of direct contact with reality—is precisely the signature of a construction so good it hides itself.

Four registers, one concession: in each, what seems most immediately *mine* turns out to be built. The constructionist who stops here has a coherent, well-evidenced picture. The question is whether the picture is complete.

## The Recurring Move: What Construction Presupposes

It is not. In every register, the construction presupposes something it cannot itself supply.

**Perception.** Predictive-processing accounts describe *which information* the visual system uses and *how* it transforms retinal signals into stable percepts. They explain that the generative model maintains stable priors. They do not explain why stable priors *feel like seeing a white wall*. A thermostat maintains temperature constancy—sensing, computing, adjusting—with nothing it is like to do so. The [inverted-qualia](/concepts/inverted-qualia/) thought experiment sharpens the point: two systems could implement identical constancy functions, producing the same cortical representations under the same illumination changes, yet differ in what yellow *feels like*. If that is possible—functionalists deny it—what remains invariant in experience outruns what remains invariant in the computation. The percept is constructed; that there is something it is like to undergo the construction is not delivered by the construction. As with the altered-state data below, functionalist and dualist readings both fit: the gap is located, not closed.

**The social self.** The dependence here runs one way. [Social construction requires subjects who can recognise each other](/topics/social-construction-of-self-vs-phenomenal-self/), interpret each other's behaviour, and internalise each other's perspectives—and these capacities are themselves phenomenal. On the Map's reading, Mead's "taking the attitude of the other" presupposes something it is like to take that attitude. A community of [philosophical zombies](/concepts/philosophical-zombies/) could produce identical social behaviour, use the same language, follow the same norms—yet there would be no one *for whom* the social identity is an identity, no site the construction could occupy, a verdict whose force depends on already taking phenomenal consciousness seriously. Performance without experience is mechanism without meaning. The developmental evidence points the same way: neonates show signs of pre-social self-specification—self-touch discrimination, proprioceptive awareness—weeks or months before the social interactions constructionists identify as self-generating (Rochat 2003). Self-touch discrimination fits a purely *biological* self-model too, so on its own it does not establish a phenomenal subject. Something must already be there to undergo socialisation. The social self requires the phenomenal self to get started; the phenomenal self does not require the social.

**The narrative self.** The DMN's status is the cleanest case of the asymmetry, because here both readings sit on the same neuroscience. [Production and filter models predict the same data](/concepts/default-mode-network/): loosen the network's grip through meditation, or through psychedelics—whose signature current imaging reads as cortex-wide desynchronisation, strongest in this network, rather than its simply going quiet—and self-experience changes rather than vanishes. The production model reads this as altered output from a disrupted subsystem; the [filter model](/concepts/filter-theory/) reads it as a constraint loosened, revealing consciousness less bound to personal narrative. Neither reading is forced by the data alone; [Letheby's predictive self-binding](/topics/predictive-self-binding-and-the-naturalist-challenge/) presses the point from the naturalist side, reading the loosened self-model as evidence that the substantial self was a useful fiction all along—and on altered-state data alone the filter and fiction readings are empirically equivalent. But on either reading, the DMN constructs a *narrative*, and for Dennett (1992) the self is a centre of narrative gravity, a useful abstraction rather than an extra entity, its apparent author a [product of the telling](/concepts/interpreter-module-narrative-construction-unity/), not its source. The Map grants this of the author. As [the three-layer model of selfhood](/topics/the-self-minimal-narrative-and-substantial/) makes explicit, though, the narrative self is built upon a [minimal self](/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/) it cannot itself produce: the bare for-me-ness whereby experience belongs to someone. Dennett's reply, that contents have no audience, leaves that [subject premise](/concepts/multiple-drafts-model/) a framework commitment—the subject-realism of Tenet 1—that he rejects at the source. When meditators strip away the narrative, the [witness-awareness](/concepts/witness-consciousness/) they report is, on the Map's reading, the subject to whom the dissolution appears; for Metzinger (2020) it is a representation of tonic alertness.

**The world-model.** Transparency's cracks furnish the sharpest exhibit. When a representation turns [opaque](/concepts/phenomenal-transparency-opacity-spectrum/)—through blur, depersonalisation, a lucid dream, the painter's deliberate squint—it becomes visible *as* a representation while retaining its phenomenal character. Blur feels like something; depersonalisation feels like something; lucid awareness feels like something. This suggests phenomenal character is not identical to representational status: some of it persists, altered, across the change. That persistence is consistent with dualism, though self-model theory, treating opacity as itself representational, predicts it too. Crucially, opacity has limits: it reveals *that* a representation is a representation, never *how* the representation is generated. Seeing blur does not show you your visual cortex at work. The construction becomes visible to the constructor without the constructor gaining access to the machinery—precisely the situation of a subject confronting its own representational medium from inside.

In all four cases the structure is identical. A genuine construction—percept, social identity, narrative, world-model—turns out, on the Map's reading, to be content delivered to, and through, an experiential subject who is presupposed by the delivery; the constructor stands outside the series as what the series is *for*.

The constructor is not a single undifferentiated lump, and clinical phenomenology shows it has separable phenomenal features—which sharpens rather than dilutes the argument. [Mine-ness](/concepts/mine-ness/) (the for-me character by which an experience belongs to a subject) and the sense of agency (the character by which a thought or action presents as initiated by that subject) are distinct features, though the separation is narrower than the corpus once claimed. The support that holds whichever way the clinical cases are read is Billon's (2013) observation that ordinary unbidden thoughts are owned without any sense of agency, and that patients with obsessive-compulsive disorder "will acknowledge that the intrusive thoughts are really theirs" however passive the intrusion feels. Schizophrenic [thought insertion](/concepts/thought-insertion/) supplies the same one-way separation only on Gallagher's reading—ownership preserved, agency lost—which the rival readings contest; and the reverse direction, ownership degraded while initiation stays intact, is read off depersonalisation on that same account and is less secure. One-way separability is enough for the argument here. What every reading of thought insertion shares is that the inserted thought either keeps a first-person perspective or is not conscious at all, and the depersonalised field is still reported from somewhere, though that secures a reporting position rather than an intact felt owner. Construction can scramble which feature attaches where, or degrade one of them, but it never supplies a construction that belongs to and is undergone by no one. That is the asymmetry stated at the level of the constructor's own phenomenal anatomy.

## Why the Constructor Cannot Be the Last Construction

The natural rejoinder is to construct the constructor too. Metzinger's [self-model theory](/concepts/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity/) takes exactly this route: "first-personal givenness" is itself a representational feature, the product of a transparent self-model in the brain. The phenomenal self is a construct—just a biological one rather than a social one. There is no constructor over and above the constructing; there is only the model, mistaken for a self.

This is the strongest version of the opposing case, and the Map's answer turns on a regress whose premise this constructionist rejects. Even if the *feeling* of selfhood is generated by a self-model, the self-model must be experienced *by something*, since conscious representations require a medium and a subject for whom they are present. Self-model theory answers that there is no one further to be fooled: the system misrepresents itself, and transparency explains why an unconstructed subject *appears*. On the Map's dualist reading the hard problem is thereby [re-sited](/concepts/the-relocation-objection/) inside the self-model rather than removed, which makes this a located disagreement, not a refutation. [Illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/) takes the harder line: on Frankish's (2016) formulation, introspection systematically misrepresents non-phenomenal properties of inner states as phenomenal, and the misrepresentation need not be undergone by anyone. The reply that someone must be fooled assumes the phenomenal realism in dispute, so the Map's answer here is tenet-dependent, as the minimal claim below records. The [circularity runs deep](/voids/self-opacity/)—when attention turns toward the observer it finds only more observation, never the observer itself—but the unobservability is a structural feature of being the medium through which observation occurs, not evidence of absence.

The same wall stops the move's reflective form. Włodzisław Duch's self-reflection thesis—that a system with dynamical access to its own working-memory states thereby has grounds to claim it is conscious—is the architectural cousin of higher-order theories: it tries to build the subject from a mechanism that *reflects on* internal states. It meets the regress twice over, given Tenet 1: indexing accessed states *as the system's own* is functionally trivial, but *felt* for-me-ness is presupposed by the reflective access rather than generated by it; and even granted, it delivers at most reflective self-awareness, leaving pre-reflective givenness untouched. That the thesis is substrate-independent does not rescue it; it only shows that the regress is indifferent to whether the constructing machinery is neural or computational.

The beautiful-loop theory of Laukkonen, Friston and Chandaria (2025) goes further, leaving no unconstructed element: a predictive system whose beliefs are recurrently shared throughout it comes to model its own existence, so the loop builds the reflexive structure. The Map grants that structure, read functionally. Its [reply](/topics/predictive-processing-and-dualism/#the-beautiful-loop-theory-the-strongest-contemporary-rival) is that the theory has not shown why the loop is felt, and that residue is Tenet 1: against this rival, *the constructor is not constructed* narrows to *construction does not explain phenomenality*.

This is why the constructor cannot be the last construction. Construction is an operation, and operations require an operator and a field in which they operate. Read functionally, that operator is the brain or the generative model, which every rival grants; the contested step is to an *experiencing* subject. *Constructor* here names that subject—the one for whom the building occurs, not the one who builds. You can push the operator back a level—the percept is built by the model, the model is built by the brain, the sense of self is built by the self-model—but each level you add is one more piece of *content*, and conscious content is exactly what requires a subject. The regress terminates not because we run out of patience but because the terms only make sense relative to a subject the regress was meant to dissolve. [Henrich's reflection-model argument](/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/) makes the same point against reflection theories of self-consciousness: the reflecting act must identify its target *as mine*, and recognising a state as one's own already presupposes the self-awareness it was meant to explain. Same-order self-representation (Kriegel 2009) adds no level; what it inherits, [the Map argues](/concepts/self-representational-theory-of-consciousness/), is not an unanswerable regress but an answer that costs him the representational thesis—grounding self-awareness in acquaintance rather than representation.

The minimal claim is modest and the strong claim is conditional, and it matters to keep them apart. The minimal claim—that some experiential subject is presupposed by any construction of conscious content—holds only given Tenet 1, since a constructionist who builds the conscious subject contests the regress's premise. Read formally, the claim is only the transcendental unity of apperception, which Kant grants and the Paralogisms leave standing. The strong claim—that this subject is [substantial and irreducible](/topics/the-self-minimal-narrative-and-substantial/) rather than a thin, momentary, or deflationary subject—is the Map's, and it is held as a live position rather than a demonstrated one. A Humean or Buddhist analyst can grant that experience is *for-someone* while denying that for-someone-ness implies a self-standing subject; on sustained Mādhyamaka analysis, "for-me-ness" decomposes into conditioned moments belonging to no one outside the sequence. The Map proceeds because the substantial constructor coheres with its other commitments, while conceding that phenomenology alone does not license the inference from reflexive structure to a persisting bearer. Its own [reading of Kant's Paralogisms](/topics/kants-paralogisms-and-the-maps-subject/) fixes the terms: the bare "I think" proves nothing about the thinker's substantiality, simplicity, or identity over time, so the Map *posits* its determinate subject rather than inferring it—and this synthesis stays honest only so long as felt mine-ness or felt unity is never made to stand in for that inference. [Immunity to error through misidentification](/concepts/immunity-to-error-through-misidentification/) gives the same verdict from the side of first-person reference: that "I feel pain" cannot mistake whose pain it is holds on every account of the first person, so it discriminates nothing about the bearer.

## The Threshold: Where the Constructor Becomes Indispensable

Granted Tenet 1, the four registers establish that *some* subject is presupposed. A further line of evidence, from a fifth source that sits slightly apart, suggests *where* the constructor stops being a passive presupposition and becomes a causal participant.

[Theory of mind](/concepts/theory-of-mind/) admits of levels. The lower levels—predicting behaviour, tracking what another perceives—may operate within [baseline cognition](/concepts/baseline-cognition/) without phenomenal consciousness; great apes pass many such tests. But recursive mindreading—"she thinks that I think the food is hidden"—requires holding multiple representational levels in coordinated awareness, each distinct yet bound in a single experiential grasp. This is the [metarepresentational threshold](/concepts/metacognition/): representing a mental state *as* a mental state, marked as something an agent holds that could be false. The human–ape gap is clearest here, but it is graded rather than absolute—chimpanzees weigh conflicting evidence metacognitively when revising beliefs (Schleihauf et al. 2025), short of recursive mindreading—and it does not straightforwardly track consciousness: working memory, executive function and language could each contribute independently.

The fifth source is less detached from the other four than it appears. The mindreading network—medial prefrontal cortex, temporoparietal junction, precuneus—overlaps substantially with the [default mode network](/concepts/default-mode-network/) that spins the narrative self. The same substrate that constructs the autobiographical "I" at rest is recruited to construct the model of *another* mind under recursive load. The narrative-self register and the threshold register are not two systems but one machine running self-modelling inward and other-modelling outward—which is consistent with, though not unique to, the construction/constructor distinction, since both directions deliver content *to* the same presupposed subject rather than producing it.

At the lower levels, the social world is *constructed* by behaviour-reading machinery that need not be conscious. At the metarepresentational threshold, on the Map's reading, the construction asks of the constructor what sub-personal processing does not supply: holding a nested structure as a unified content and *comparing across its levels*. The [Jourdain framing](/concepts/jourdain-hypothesis/) captures the divide—apes may read minds without knowing that minds are what they read, having states without knowing they have them. Knowing-you-have-them is the constructor becoming reflexively present to its own construction. Whether it then does causal work—recursive theory of mind underwriting moral cognition (representing an agent as aware of their reasons and able to choose otherwise) and connecting to [libertarian free will](/topics/free-will/)—turns on Tenet 3 rather than on the comparative record: no current result separates a phenomenal contribution from working memory, language, executive control and global availability. Granted that tenet, the constructor becomes a participant at the threshold rather than a screen.

This fifth case must be held with care. The quantum-coherence reading of the binding that recursive mindreading demands—gamma-band synchronisation across the mindreading network as the integration the [Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) framework would underwrite—remains a promising research direction, not established fact: neural timescales vastly exceed quantum decoherence times, and the mechanism prolonging coherence is unspecified. The threshold claim does not depend on the quantum reading. It depends only on the weaker observation that the metarepresentational gap co-varies with consciousness across levels, developmental stages, and species in a pattern the "incidental correlate" hypothesis struggles to explain.

## Synthesis: One Distinction, Four Registers, One Constructor

Set the four registers side by side and the unity becomes visible in a way no single article can show from inside itself.

| Register | What is constructed | What the construction presupposes |
|----------|--------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Perceptual | Constancy, learned percepts | A subject for whom there is something it is like to perceive |
| Social | Identity, role, emotion-categories | A phenomenal self capable of recognition and internalisation |
| Neural-resting | The narrative self | The minimal self the narrative is built upon |
| Representational | The transparent world-model | A medium-bearer to whom the model is present |

The four rows are one problem in four costumes. In each row, a constructionist account is granted its full force—the percept *is* built, the identity *is* socially shaped, the narrative *is* a neural achievement, the world-model *is* a representation—and in each row the construction turns out to be content that presupposes an experiential subject and a phenomenal medium. The constructor is the constant across the variation. It is what makes the percept *seen*, the identity *owned*, the narrative *lived*, the world-model *experienced as a world*.

Why does the distinction recur so cleanly? Because all four are instances of the same metaphysical fact under the Map's reading: construction is the brain's contribution; being-experienced is consciousness's contribution; and the two come apart exactly where the [explanatory gap](/topics/hard-problem-of-consciousness/) always opens. The brain builds the structure. Consciousness undergoes it. No amount of structural sophistication closes the gap, because structure is the very thing the construction supplies and experience is the very thing it presupposes. The constructor is the single subject whose presence the four constructions everywhere assume and nowhere produce—not a fifth construction standing alongside the other four.

This is what makes the title more than a slogan. *The self is constructed*: the narrated, socially embedded self you take yourself to be is genuinely built, and the building can be studied, disrupted, and traced to neural machinery. *The constructor is not*: given Tenet 1, the subject for whom it is built is not the output of building, because building produces content and conscious content requires a subject. To construct the constructor is to relocate it, not eliminate it.

## Evidence and Dependency

The empirical materials this synthesis rests on—colour constancy and Gibsonian perceptual learning, Barrett's work on emotion (the Schachter–Singer result only in attenuated form), the default-mode-network imaging, Metzinger's transparency data, Rochat's neonatal self-discrimination, and the comparative and developmental theory-of-mind gap—are externally evidenced and hold independently of the Map. What they do not do is discriminate the dualist reading from a sophisticated functionalist one: that each construction presupposes an irreducible experiential subject is inherited from Tenet 1, and the causal-participant and quantum-coherence glosses on the theory-of-mind threshold are inherited from Tenets 2–3 and flagged in-text as unestablished.

The clinical dissociation between mine-ness and agency is externally evidenced in one direction only—ownership without felt agency, on Billon's unbidden-thought and obsessional cases—while the thought-insertion and depersonalisation limbs are reading-dependent; the double dissociation this synthesis once cited is not something the record delivers.

The argument rests on the regress—construction is an operation requiring an operator and a field, so the constructor cannot be one more construction. This line is argued but tenet-dependent: it presupposes that conscious content requires a subject for whom it is content, and Henrich's reflection-model argument parallels it from the side of self-consciousness. It licenses, given Tenet 1, only the *minimal* claim that some subject is presupposed. The *strong* claim—a substantial, persisting subject rather than a thin or fictional one—is mutually coherent only, held as a posit rather than inferred (the Paralogisms reading says why the inference is unavailable); the naturalist rivals ([Metzinger's self-model theory](/concepts/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity/), Letheby's self-fictionalism) grant for-someone-ness while denying the bearer, and on the shared data the accounts do not separate. Convergence across four registers raises the explanatory bar; by the [evidential-status discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/) it does not become independent confirmation.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism).** The construction/constructor distinction is dualism's most general exhibit. Each register shows a structural feature—percept, social identity, narrative, world-model—describable in brain-side and culture-side terms, and a phenomenal feature—something it is like to undergo each—that the description leaves untouched. The constructor is the irreducible remainder: the subject no functional decomposition captures because decomposition produces content and the subject is what content is *for*. The recurrence of the same remainder across four domains, one premise applied four times, is the pattern dualism expects; reductive functionalism absorbs it by sub-dividing roles, and on the shared data the two readings do not separate (above).

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction).** The constructor is not a passive screen. The theory-of-mind threshold, as the Map reads it, has the constructor doing work no sub-personal process replicates; perceptual learning shows it constructing its own future perceptual conditions through sustained attentional engagement; deliberate constancy-breaking and contemplative opacity show it acting on its own representational apparatus. The Map reads these as the constructor's causal efficacy—consciousness contributing rather than merely registering. Each such reading is the Map's interpretation of phenomena fully describable in brain-side terms, not a measurement that compels downward causation; the vividness is what the cases supply, while whether it is *consciousness* doing the work remains tenet-dependent rather than empirically forced.

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds).** A constructed self is replicable; a duplicate could occupy the same social roles, tell the same story, run the same self-model. The constructor is not. *This* experiential subject—the [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/) of the one for whom the construction occurs—is not interchangeable with a qualitative twin. The perceptual ratchet's irreversibility, the indexical anchor of the minimal self, and the determinacy of "which subject am I?" all require a single experiential trajectory rather than a branching proliferation of constructor-copies.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits).** The parsimonious move is to deny the constructor: posit only the constructions and call the sense of a subject one more construction. But the simplification works by defining away the phenomenon to be explained. Granted Tenet 1, the regress shows that the dispensed-with subject is exactly what the constructions presuppose; eliminating it in the name of economy purchases simplicity by ignoring what is most in need of explanation. Simplicity is unreliable here precisely because the simpler theory is simpler *appearance*, not simpler reality—itself a consequence of the transparency that hides the construction in the first place.
