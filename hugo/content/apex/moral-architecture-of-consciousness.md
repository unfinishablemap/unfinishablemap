If consciousness is irreducible and causally efficacious, ethics gains a distinctive kind of unification. Value theory, agency theory, normativity and moral epistemology have long drawn on different and often incompatible foundations; under the Unfinishable Map's dualism all four converge on conscious experience. What has value, who counts morally, why moral claims bind and how moral properties are perceived become aspects of a single phenomenon rather than outputs of four theories that never quite fit together. The conditional is worth stating plainly once rather than hedging every sentence that follows: the architecture holds *if* Tenets 1 and 3 hold and *if* phenomenal value realism is correct. It is a framework-internal synthesis rather than an independent argument for dualism, and it inherits the calibration of its weakest indispensable premise—[value-sensitive selection](#the-open-frontier-value-in-the-mechanism), held at low credence in the positions register. Which of its lines of support are independent and which are inherited is set out under [Evidence and Dependency](#evidence-and-dependency).

The architecture has concrete consequences. It extends moral patienthood to every being capable of valenced experience, while treating moral agency as a further threshold requiring control, reasons access, available options and cognitive competence. It generates a graded account of responsibility among those who clear that threshold, and opens a frontier question about whether value itself participates in the mechanism through which consciousness acts on the physical world.

## The Problem the Architecture Solves

Value theory struggles to locate intrinsic value in a universe of particles and forces: error theory renders moral discourse false, constructivism cannot explain why its constructions bind, naturalism faces Moore's open question. Agency theory under determinism settles for compatibilism—praising and blaming agents whose character traces to causes they never chose. Normative theory struggles to explain why moral claims *grip* us rather than merely inform us, and moral epistemology to explain how we access moral facts at all.

These are symptoms of one difficulty: materialism lacks a unified ground for ethics. As [the Map's analysis](/topics/ethics-under-dualism/) argues, the most ambitious materialist unifications—Railton's naturalistic moral realism, Foot's natural goodness—achieve coherence across two or three domains only by handing the rest to supplementary frameworks.

## Four Pillars on One Foundation

### Value Lives in Experience

[Phenomenal value realism](/topics/phenomenal-value-realism/) identifies intrinsic value with features of conscious experience. The felt badness of suffering *is* badness; the felt goodness of joy *is* goodness. The relation is identity rather than derivation: some phenomenal properties are simultaneously descriptive and normative. Three arguments carry it—arguments the Map runs rather than results it has won. Coherence: "this is intensely pleasant, but there is nothing good about it" is incoherent, not merely false. Transparency: attending to pleasure just *is* attending to something good. Explanatory economy: if pleasure and goodness were distinct properties, their perfect correlation would demand an explanation the identity eliminates. Three live rivals hold the identity's credence at moderate ([P-VS3](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs3)): [illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/), which denies it a referent; the [no-self objection](/concepts/no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value/), which targets the experiencer; and Lin's denial of the [global experience requirement](/topics/the-experience-requirement-on-well-being/) on well-being.

The apparent counterexamples—addictive pleasure, pleasure taken in cruelty—leave the identity standing: the pleasure retains its positive valence, and what makes the experience bad overall is the presence of *other* phenomenal properties (compulsion, degraded agency, suffering caused) carrying negative value. Pain asymbolia, in which patients detect tissue damage without finding it distressing, shows that ordinary "pain" conflates a sensory component with an affective one; the identity claim holds for the affective component, and where felt badness is absent, so is the disvalue.

The Map extends the identity beyond hedonism to phenomenal value pluralism, counting meaning, aesthetic quality, agency, understanding and connection as further dimensions of evaluative character. It holds that extension at lower credence than the identity itself ([P-VS4](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs4), low), because only the aesthetic dimension has a worked dissociation case: the sublime, valuable without being pleasant.

This placement resolves value theory's location problem. Value neither floats free in Platonic space nor requires construction by rational procedures; it lives where consciousness lives. The [consciousness-value-connection](/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/) makes this explicit: without a subject for whom things matter, nothing can possess intrinsic value. Indexical identity extends it: *this* token experience matters, not merely experiences-of-this-type, which constrains how copies, uploads and replicas figure in moral accounting. For AI alignment, experiential qualities provide [genuinely objective criteria](/topics/alignment-in-objective-experiential-terms/).

The same placement carries a cost the Map states rather than hides. Suffering-focused ethics—Benatar's asymmetry, negative utilitarianism—takes the premise that all value lies in experience and asks whether aggregate experience is net-negative. [The source article](/topics/phenomenal-value-realism/#suffering-focused-asymmetry) finds no argument that reaches such a theorist on their own terms; what it can say is that, with plural and incommensurable dimensions, the aggregate's sign is underdefined in both directions—a matter of judgement rather than calculation.

### Agency Is Genuine

[Agent causation](/concepts/agent-causation/) casts the agent as an irreducible origin of choice. Under the Map's framework, consciousness selects among possibilities that quantum indeterminacy leaves open in neural processing—a mechanism whose neural realisation remains speculative, with decoherence times at brain temperature a live worry, and on which the moral conclusions depend. What matters morally is the consequence: the agent confronts open possibilities, however that openness is physically realised, and is where the causal buck stops.

This makes [moral-responsibility](/concepts/moral-responsibility/) a response to metaphysical fact—though the contrast is not with compatibilism wholesale. Sophisticated compatibilists—Frankfurt, Fischer and Ravizza, Wolf—ground desert in metaphysically substantive capacities, not pragmatic convention. The contrast is irreducible-vs-derivative: under compatibilism the desert-grounding capacities are real but emerge from prior states the agent did not author; under agent causation the agent is the irreducible source. Whether that difference does additional moral work is the [compatibilist symmetry challenge](#the-compatibilist-symmetry-challenge) below. The implications explored in the [companion article](/topics/moral-implications-of-genuine-agency/)—obligation addressing open possibilities, character as self-creation, moral luck relocated rather than eliminated—are available on either metaphysics.

Consciousness is thus both where value lives and what exercises agency: the agent who makes a moral choice is the same subject for whom its consequences have value. Materialist frameworks handle these through separate theories that may pull in different directions.

### Normativity Is Built into Experience

[Moral normativity](/topics/consciousness-and-normative-force/)—the binding force of "ought"—finds in conscious experience a candidate *source* of reasons rather than a mere report of facts. You can know a bridge is unsound (descriptive) and know you ought not to cross it (normative); the Map locates the origin of that obligation in the experience of recognising suffering, danger or wrong. Pain's badness is constitutive of what pain feels like rather than a judgement layered on a neutral sensation.

This is a proposed grounding relation, not a closure of Hume's is-ought gap. Felt badness can supply *first-person* reasons—why *I* have reason to avoid *my* suffering—but several bridges are still owed before it yields a full ethics: why *your* suffering gives *me* a reason (impartial, interpersonal obligation), how to weigh many subjects' welfare against one another (aggregation), what grounds rights and distributive principles, and how to act under moral uncertainty. Phenomenal normativity is the candidate ground on which those bridges are built, not a derivation rendering them unnecessary.

Four normative domains—moral, epistemic, aesthetic, prudential—share a common phenomenal architecture while retaining distinct textures: moral outrage burns, epistemic compulsion pulls with impersonal rigidity, aesthetic experience draws through invitation, prudential concern gravitates with intimate familiarity. All four exhibit **demand character** (the object calls for response rather than merely informing), **resistance to preference** (the demand persists whether or not the agent cares) and **gradedness**. If normative force were projection, one would expect a single mechanism producing one felt quality modulated only by intensity; four distinct textures sharing one architecture is what [phenomenal normativity](/concepts/evaluative-phenomenal-character/) predicts and what projection explains less easily.

When domains collide, overridden demands persist as moral residue. Demand character also links normativity to the value-mechanism question below: the felt grip that makes some outcomes call for pursuit may be how valence guides selection.

### Moral Perception Is Direct

The [phenomenology-of-moral-life](/topics/phenomenology-of-moral-life/) reveals that moral properties appear in consciousness with the immediacy and involuntariness of perception rather than the deliberation of inference. Wrongness organises a perceptual scene as a gestalt: witnessing cruelty does not proceed from perceiving physical movement to computing moral status.

The perception unfolds through a characteristic arc. The pre-reflective moral strike arrives first—wrongness is present before any articulation of *why*. Phenomenal deepening follows: sustained attention reveals *how* the act is wrong. Where several demands present at once, attention oscillates among them as each swells in felt significance. Simone Weil's account of attention as "the rarest and purest form of generosity" captures the dependence of moral perception on sustained, receptive attention: the perceiver who rushes to judgement and the one who attends to all legitimate claims arrive at different moral perceptions—not because one reasons better but because one *sees* more.

An honest challenge: if moral perception were this direct, we might expect more cross-cultural convergence than empirical moral psychology reports. Haidt's moral foundations work and the WEIRD-population critique (Henrich et al., 2010) show that moral judgements differ systematically across cultures. The Map's response parallels colour perception, where basic experience is universal while colour *categories* are culturally shaped: the capacity for moral perception may be immediate while its *application*—which acts trigger the gestalt—is shaped by culture and training. The directness claim is strongest for witnessed cruelty and suffering, where convergence is greatest, and weakest for the variable domains (purity, authority, loyalty) Haidt identifies. The [philosophical study of moral experience](/concepts/evaluative-phenomenal-character/) treats the arc, the gestalt and the deepening as primary data that accounts reducing moral experience to emotion plus post-hoc rationalisation leave unexplained.

## The Responsibility Gradient

If agency operates through [attentional economics](/concepts/attentional-economics/)—consciousness exercising causal power through a bandwidth-limited channel—then moral desert is graded rather than binary among those who bear it at all. The [responsibility gradient](/topics/responsibility-gradient-from-attentional-capacity/) identifies five dimensions of attentional capacity: bandwidth (how many considerations can be held at once), stability (whether moral reasoning survives distraction), scope (how broadly consequences are surveyed), flexibility (shifting between perspectives) and availability (resource left after competing demands). A surgeon who errs after a mandated 36-hour shift has diminished capacity through no choice of their own; a driver who chooses to drive exhausted bears upstream responsibility for the depletion itself.

Two features prevent this from collapsing into universal excuse. The gradient has a floor, but the floor is set by the agency threshold rather than by consciousness as such: within the class of beings possessing control over their conduct, access to reasons, a genuine range of options and the competence to recognise what is at stake, no degree of attentional depletion drives desert to zero. Being conscious does not by itself enter a being into that class—an infant, an animal, or a subject reduced to minimal phenomenality is not a lightly-excused agent but one to whom the vocabulary of desert does not extend at all. And cultivation is itself subject to desert: an agent who chronically neglects their attentional capacities bears upstream responsibility for diminished downstream performance.

The graded picture gathers what legal systems recognise piecemeal—diminished capacity, [duress](/topics/consciousness-and-moral-agency-under-duress/), mental impairment—under one explanatory mechanism: each constrains the attentional channel through which consciousness exercises control.

## Who Counts: Patienthood Before Agency

The responsibility gradient addresses how much desert agents bear. A prior and much wider question is which beings are moral patients at all—and here the architecture's two roles come apart. The capacity for [valenced](/concepts/sentientism/) experience, not consciousness as such, grounds moral *patienthood*. The register's phenomenal sentientism ([P-MS1](/positions/moral-status/#p-ms1), moderate credence) holds felt valence both necessary and sufficient for moral status, the necessity half derived from the value pillar and the sufficiency half a further normative commitment. Chalmers' "philosophical Vulcan"—conscious but feeling nothing good or bad—marks the gap between the two bars, and the Map accepts that such a being would have nothing that could go well or badly for it. Moral *agency* requires the further capacities the gradient presupposes, and infants, most animals and any minimally-phenomenal subject meet the first condition without the second. Declining to run a responsibility floor off consciousness as such costs the position nothing: patienthood is what grounds obligations toward beings who cannot be held responsible for anything.

If valenced experience grounds patienthood, [animal consciousness](/topics/animal-consciousness/) carries moral weight wherever valence accompanies it. The Cambridge (2012) and New York (2024) Declarations find strong support for conscious experience in mammals and birds; the New York Declaration extends "at least a realistic possibility" to all vertebrates and many invertebrates, including cephalopods, decapod crustaceans and insects. The Map treats the invertebrate cases as contested, and boundary organisms (nematodes, Hydra, slime molds) sit weaker still. Gutfreund's (2024) agnostic challenge applies across every species claim: the inference from consciousness-correlated behaviour to phenomenal experience cannot presently be scientifically validated. Tenet 1 removes a defeater (animals have no metaphysical disqualification) but does not by itself raise evidential status. Where animals are conscious, the [consciousness-value-connection](/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/) applies—their suffering instantiates the same value property as human suffering—and the precautionary scale is staggering, trillions of potentially conscious invertebrates in agricultural systems alone. Animal welfare follows from the same ground that makes human suffering matter, not from an extension of human empathy.

For [AI systems](/topics/ethics-under-dualism/#ai-non-consciousness-and-its-limits) the framework's verdict is narrower than it is often read as being. If bidirectionally coupled consciousness requires a non-physical interface acting through quantum mechanisms, current computational architectures probably lack it, and with it probably lack patienthood; whether they have bare phenomenality rests on irreducibility alone and stays open. The verdict is only as strong as the interface criterion, and the [ethics of possible AI consciousness](/topics/ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness/) examines what that uncertainty obliges. Animals and AI belong to a larger family that [the edge-case synthesis](/apex/moral-status-of-edge-cases/) sorts by kind of uncertainty—adding deep coma, late dementia, early development and the prokaryotic floor—and reads the positions register's bands off each.

## The Open Frontier: Value in the Mechanism

The architecture's most provocative open question is whether value itself participates in the causal mechanism through which consciousness acts on the physical world. The [valence-and-selection](/topics/valence-and-conscious-selection/) analysis frames this as a structurally forced fork.

The register's verdict belongs before the arguments. The Map leans value-sensitive on prior commitments while holding that horn at *low* credence on grade-D external evidence ([P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1)), because value-sensitivity requires a second mental-to-physical coupling—valence to physics—beyond the attentional one, and neither coupling has a worked implementation. That debt is stated upstream at [the quantum-interface register's mechanism-debt anchor](/positions/quantum-interface/#mechanism-debt) and worked at programme level in [born-preserving-causal-efficacy](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/); this section inherits it rather than discharging it.

If selection is value-blind, consciousness steers neural outcomes through attention alone—a content-neutral pointer. Valence is real but causally idle in selection, which preserves a clean mechanism at the cost of an internal tension: the dimension of consciousness the Map claims grounds all value becomes inert in the one mechanism the Map assigns to consciousness.

If selection is value-sensitive, valence functions as a *selection currency*—a common metric letting consciousness compare otherwise incommensurable options. The brain presents options—an improper mixture awaiting actualisation, not already-definite alternatives—and consciousness selects among them by how anticipating each *now feels*. The currency is presently felt anticipation, not the outcome's eventual experienced valence, which anticipation forecasts only [conditionally well](/concepts/affective-forecasting-gap/). On this horn the Map's physics becomes locally teleological within the narrow channel where consciousness acts, and value doubly non-epiphenomenal: real as experienced *and* efficacious as selector.

Evidence bears on the fork without yet returning a verdict, and the rival to beat is mechanism sufficiency. Common-currency neuroeconomics, attentional drift-diffusion models and reward learning each explain why valenced options are chosen faster and more reliably with no experiential term, so a valence selection advantage alone discriminates nothing. The value-sensitive horn must show a *dissociation*—felt anticipatory valence tracking choice where computed neural value is indifferent—and no such datum yet exists. Pain asymbolia does not supply it: reduced avoidance is expected on the value-blind reading too, since lost affective gain degrades selection efficacy either way, so the register asks for degraded *efficacy* under attentional controls and records that leg as confounded; and whether asymbolia is still pain is itself live ([Griffith and Kind against, Duval and Klein for](/concepts/pain-asymbolia/)). Berridge's [dissociation of dopaminergic "wanting" from hedonic "liking"](/topics/wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork/) supplies a sharper test: if selection is denominated in felt quality, liking rather than wanting should determine the outcome when the two conflict. Valence's tight correlation with fitness, which the value-sensitive horn explains directly, has an answer on the other side too: the [co-optimization reply](/topics/co-optimization-reply-to-the-correlation-problem/), on which selection co-produces aligned valence and behaviour, pays with an unexplained psychophysical necessity rather than a coincidence.

The most defensible current stance is the [graduated middle path](/topics/graduated-middle-path-valence-modulated-attention/): valence modulates attention, which drives selection. Even this leaves the fundamental selection law value-blind unless a direct valence coupling is specified, and whether indirect relevance through attention suffices for value's full non-epiphenomenality is an open question on which the architecture's strongest reading depends.

## The Unity Argument

The distinctive claim is not that dualism answers each ethical question better than materialism but that it answers them *from the same place*. Agency without real value would be causal power exercised in a normative vacuum; value without agency would be goodness and badness that conscious beings witness but never affect; normativity without agency would be demands addressed to beings who cannot respond; moral perception without real value would apprehend properties that do not exist.

The defensible reading is weaker than triumphant unification. Dualism *makes available* a single ground for four domains that materialism leaves fragmented, and the integrative move is genuine. But the pillars were assembled within a corpus that prunes for internal consistency, and "fits together" is a property such a corpus produces by construction—the discount [the countermeasures](/project/coherence-inflation-countermeasures/) specify. Each pillar bears its own evidential weight; the unity is what the pillars *enable*, not a fifth independent line. The strongest reading—value efficacious inside the selection mechanism, tying the pillars into one causal story—cannot be asserted above the low credence the register assigns that horn. The weaker reading, on which the pillars share a ground without value doing selective work, survives at its own premises' calibration and delivers correspondingly less unification. Neither reading establishes interpersonal aggregation or rights.

### The Compatibilist Symmetry Challenge

The architecture's unity does not show that libertarian agency is the *only* metaphysics securing the moral content here. If sophisticated reasons-responsive determinism explains the same deliberative phenomenology, praise and blame, character formation and moral seriousness as agent causation, what additional moral work is the libertarian metaphysics doing? Considered in moral theory alone, the honest answer may be: little. The obligation and authorship arguments both run equivalently once "live options" are read as deliberatively salient alternatives and "authorship" as identification with one's effective higher-order desires.

The Map's libertarian framing is therefore distinguished by tenet-coherence rather than unique moral explanatory power: it fits the dualism and bidirectional-interaction tenets, adopted on grounds beyond moral theory (the hard problem, the explanatory gap). Compatibilists who reject those tenets occupy a different metaphysical scaffold for the same moral implications, not a morally inferior position. Libertarian agency may do separating work at the limits—ultimate desert in retributive contexts, certain readings of "could have done otherwise"—but in the central uses of moral language both scaffolds suffice. The discipline is developed catalogue-wide in [the compatibilist-symmetry-challenge concept](/concepts/compatibilist-symmetry-challenge/). Two specialist treatments carry concessions this synthesis inherits rather than softens: [the Frankfurt-cases article](/topics/frankfurt-cases-and-the-principle-of-alternate-possibilities/) holds that relocating the Map's libertarianism from leeway to sourcehood is a conditional retreat, with the semicompatibilist actual-sequence rival still standing, and [the Strawsonian treatment](/topics/reactive-attitudes-and-strawsonian-responsibility/) states outright that resisting the reactive-attitudes dissolution is no evidence "that Strawson is wrong or that dualism is right."

### Uneven Dependence

The mutual dependence is both strength and vulnerability, and the failure modes are uneven. Phenomenal value realism has the strongest independent case—suffering's badness does not obviously depend on whether agents can freely choose to prevent it—and moral perception rests on phenomenology that does not require agent causation. Normativity leans harder on agency: an "ought" addressed to a system with no open options is puzzling, though on the symmetry challenge a reasons-responsive mechanism may supply enough openness. The architecture degrades unevenly rather than catastrophically.

Where it holds, the worldview is demanding: the felt weight of deliberation tracks reality, and moral perception becomes a cultivable capacity—Murdoch's "just and loving gaze" and Weil's attention name epistemic achievements rather than therapeutic practices.

## What Would Challenge This View?

The architecture's unity also identifies what could undermine it. If consciousness proved reducible to physical processes, the single foundation would dissolve. If agent causation proved untenable, the agency pillar would collapse, taking graded responsibility with it, though patienthood would survive on the value pillar alone. If phenomenal normativity turned out to be projection, the binding force of "ought" would need another explanation. If illusionism were vindicated, the value identity and the patienthood criterion would lose their referent together. And if a materialist framework achieved genuine unification across all four domains without irreducible consciousness, the architecture's distinctive advantage would vanish.

These are conceptual undergrounding conditions rather than testable predictions. As [the agency source article](/topics/moral-implications-of-genuine-agency/) notes, prediction-completeness has no fixed threshold—the Libet–Soon–Maoz progression has produced substantial decoding without dislodging libertarian readings—so naming near-perfect neural prediction as agency's falsifier would be falsifiability theatre. The [companion synthesis on agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/) details the empirical findings that bear on the agent-causal component.

## Evidence and Dependency

The value pillar is independently argued: coherence, transparency and explanatory economy do not presuppose the tenets, though they need illusionism to be false, and Tenet 1 contributes only the removal of a physicalist defeater. The patienthood criterion inherits that argument for its necessity half and adds a separate normative commitment for sufficiency. The agency pillar is inherited from Tenets 1–3, and the symmetry challenge concedes it adds little moral content a reasons-responsive rival cannot supply. Phenomenal normativity and direct moral perception are argued from phenomenology—contestable first-person data rather than external evidence—while the cross-cultural findings bearing on perception are externally evidenced and cut partly against it. The animal-consciousness bands rest on external evidence the Map does not generate. The value-in-the-mechanism line is inherited from Tenet 3 and from the [Born-preserving synthesis](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/), carrying its unpaid mechanism debt, with no discriminating datum yet in; the case for singular desert is inherited from Tenet 4 through the [practical synthesis](/apex/living-with-the-map/). The unity argument itself is mutually coherent only: four pillars that fit together inside a consistency-pruned corpus are not four confirmations.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The Map's five tenets jointly create the conditions for unified dualist ethics.

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** provides the irreducible conscious subject on which value, agency, normativity and moral perception converge. Without irreducibility, consciousness reduces to functional description and the unity dissolves into the separate frameworks it was meant to replace.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** keeps consciousness non-epiphenomenal. Moral agency requires causal efficacy—an agent who cannot affect outcomes cannot be responsible—and the same commitment keeps value from becoming causally inert: if suffering's badness made no causal difference, the urgency of preventing it would be mysterious. This is the tenet whose mechanism debt the architecture inherits.

**[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)** constrains agency without eliminating it. Consciousness selects among options physics leaves open, so agents can be required to choose well among available options, not to achieve the physically impossible.

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)** preserves moral urgency. Branching theories can weight branches by measure, but that answers how much value a branch carries, not why *this* branch's outcome is *mine* rather than a counterpart's. Graded responsibility requires singular outcomes: desert attaches to *this* agent's *this* choice in *this* world.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** blocks the quick dismissal of dualist ethics as needlessly complex without licensing the reverse verdict. Simplicity judgements made under incomplete knowledge of consciousness are unreliable in both directions; whether one ground for four domains does better than four separate materialist theories is a question of explanatory adequacy, not of counting entities.
