Consciousness and time are entangled in ways that traditional philosophy has underexplored. One direction is familiar: ordinary consciousness requires temporal structure. Experience flows; moments connect; the present has duration. Without the specious present—the roughly 100 to 750 milliseconds within which we hear melodies as melodies and understand sentences as wholes—ordinary experience would not hang together. The Map explores a second, more contained direction: consciousness participates *locally* in the quantum collapse events that give lived time its felt passage, so that within neural systems it helps constitute the character of *experienced* time—durée, the flowing present, the felt fixity of the past.

This is the weaker of two theses one could defend, and it is the one the Map's other commitments force. A stronger claim—that time's arrow as such emerges only through consciousness, so that no temporal facts obtain without a mind—would contradict the Map's [background commitment](/tenets/background-commitments/) to objective single-outcome actualisation, on which stars, chemistry, and [prebiotic mutation](/concepts/prebiotic-collapse/) resolved to definite outcomes, and so laid down a cosmic arrow, long before the first observer. What consciousness contributes is local and phenomenal: participating in collapse where the brain provides an interface, it shapes the lived temporal structure it inhabits without constituting time in general.

Even this contained dependence ripples outward. For AI: systems lacking the right temporal dynamics may be excluded from *ordinary* temporally organised consciousness, though the Map's [treatment of non-temporal consciousness](/topics/non-temporal-consciousness/) cautions that a minimal conscious layer need not carry represented succession. For the hard problem: experienced succession is a feature no inventory of instantaneous physical states seems to contain. For free will: agents don't merely act within experienced time—they participate in constituting its felt structure.

## The Specious Present

William James developed the idea of the specious present—the brief interval within which we experience succession and change as unified wholes. He adopted the term from E.R. Clay, crediting it to him in *The Principles of Psychology*, and made it central to his account of temporal experience. Pure mathematical instants, however closely packed, never add up to experienced flow. Duration estimates vary—from roughly 100 milliseconds for basic perceptual binding to 2-3 seconds for the extended experiential present—but the phenomenon is robust: the experienced present has extent.

Edmund Husserl's phenomenological analysis revealed the structure underlying this extent. Each moment of consciousness contains:

- **Retention**: the immediate past echoing in the present
- **Primal impression**: the strictly present moment
- **Protention**: anticipation of what's coming

These form an inseparable unity. The note you're hearing now carries the just-passed notes in retention while already reaching toward the next. This tripartite structure makes meaning possible: you can't understand a sentence if each word evaporates before the next arrives.

Whether experience is genuinely temporally extended (extensionalism, as Dainton argues) or technically instantaneous with past-directed content (retentionalism, as Husserl's own framework implies) matters for what consciousness requires. The Map favours modified extensionalism: experience is genuinely extended, constituted through a series of quantum collapses. Each collapse is discrete, but the [specious present](/concepts/temporal-consciousness/) integrates multiple collapses into a unified duration-block. Husserl's retention-protention structure reflects retrocausal boundary conditions in collapse rather than instantaneous representational content, preserving genuine temporal extension while explaining why retentionalism's phenomenological descriptions are accurate. If [consciousness is an activity](/topics/consciousness-as-activity/) rather than a property—something that exists only in its unfolding—then temporal extension is constitutive. An activity that occupies no time is no activity at all.

Both options presuppose something Dennett's [multiple-drafts model](/concepts/multiple-drafts-model/) denies: that there is a canonical moment at which experiential content gets fixed. On that account parallel content-fixations are edited into a temporal narrative after the fact, so the "moment" a collapse would constitute is a retrospective construction rather than an event. The disagreement runs to bedrock—whether temporal order is constituted at collapse or assembled afterwards—and the Map records it as a live framework boundary rather than a rival it has answered.

The specious present also appears to be modulated by [attentional engagement](/concepts/attention-as-interface/): its scope expands and contracts with the intensity of conscious selection. [Concentrated focus](/topics/temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency/) compresses experienced duration—an hour of absorbed problem-solving passes "in minutes"—while anxious waiting stretches it, each moment thinning out as attention monitors time itself. Flow states dissolve temporal boundaries altogether. These are different modes of temporal experience rather than failures of time estimation, and their variation creates a specific instance of the hard problem: materialist "internal clock" models explain the *direction* of distortion but not the *phenomenology*. The felt quality of stretched waiting has no counterpart in pulse-counting.

Henri Bergson went further with his concept of [durée](/concepts/temporal-consciousness/). Clock time treats moments as positions on a line—discrete, externally related, quantifiable. Lived time interpenetrates: the past permeates the present, the future is already anticipated. Bergson called this "qualitative multiplicity"—elements distinguishable yet not separable, each coloured by every other. Duration cannot be measured because it cannot be divided without destroying what it is: cut a melody into separate notes and you have frequencies, not music. As [Kent and Wittmann argue](/topics/temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency/) in their 2021 analysis, major consciousness theories focus on "functional moments" of 100-300 milliseconds while experienced time extends to seconds. That gap between what theories explain and what consciousness involves may be evidence of something functional accounts cannot capture rather than a temporary limitation.

## Time's Arrow and Collapse

Physics presents a puzzle that rarely surfaces in consciousness studies: fundamental equations are time-symmetric. Run them backward and they work equally well. Yet time has an arrow—past differs from future, effects follow causes, entropy increases. Where does this asymmetry come from?

Standard answers appeal to boundary conditions or thermodynamics. The Map locates a further candidate in quantum collapse—but it is important to be precise about how much of the arrow collapse is being asked to carry, and how much of *that* is consciousness's contribution.

Quantum mechanics describes systems in superposition—existing in multiple states simultaneously until measurement. Collapse resolves the superposition to a definite outcome, and appears to introduce irreversibility: the alternatives that weren't selected are genuinely gone, not merely unobserved. Before, multiple outcomes; after, one. This asymmetry—superposition to definiteness—is a candidate for time's arrow distinct from (though perhaps related to) thermodynamic entropy. GRW-style collapse theories introduce a time asymmetry not derived from thermodynamics but fundamental to the collapse dynamics; Penrose argues gravity itself is "the root of the arrow of time," with gravitational effects in objective reduction introducing irreversibility.

Crucially, this collapse-arrow does not require consciousness. Objective reduction (GRW-style or gravitational) secures definite outcomes throughout the universe wherever no mind is present: stars, chemistry, and [prebiotic mutation](/concepts/prebiotic-collapse/) resolved to single outcomes—and so laid down an arrow of accumulated definiteness—for billions of years before the first observer.

Consciousness enters as a *local* participant, not as the source of temporal direction. [The collapse-time connection](/concepts/collapse-and-time/) develops the two-tier picture: objective reduction provides the baseline collapse and the cosmic arrow; consciousness interfaces with collapse specifically in neural systems, biasing *which* of the already-definite-making outcomes obtains where the brain provides the right conditions. Its contribution to time is therefore restricted to the temporal structure of *experienced* becoming rather than to the existence of temporal direction as such. The stronger formulation on which time's arrow "emerges through consciousness rather than preceding it" is set aside as incompatible with the Map's own commitment to a prebiotic objective arrow.

## The Modified Growing Block

Philosophy of time offers three major positions. **Presentism** holds that only the present exists—past is gone, future hasn't arrived. **Eternalism** (the block universe) holds that all times exist equally—past, present, and future laid out like spatial positions, with "now" merely indexical. **Growing block** theory holds that past and present exist but future doesn't yet; the block grows as time passes.

The Map favours a modified growing block: collapse grows the block. The present is where superpositions resolve; the past is accumulated collapsed structure; the future doesn't yet exist. "Growth" isn't a process occurring in a pre-given time—it is the laying-down of temporal structure, most of it objective and mindless. Consciousness-involving collapse grows the block only where neural systems provide an interface, adding no new tier to the ontology of becoming but contributing the felt, lived character of growth at those sites—which is why the growing block is experienced *as* becoming from within, rather than merely inferred.

But a puzzle arises. [Time-symmetric interpretations](/topics/time-symmetric-selection-mechanism/) of quantum mechanics—the [transactional interpretation](/concepts/transactional-interpretation-of-quantum-mechanics/), the Two-State Vector Formalism—describe quantum events as transactions between past and future boundary conditions. This seems to require eternalism: both past and future must exist to send and receive the waves that complete transactions.

The resolution: the "future" participating in transactions is the space of quantum possibilities, not ontologically fixed future events. Confirmation waves don't come from an already-existing tomorrow; they come from the possibility structure of absorbers that *would* complete the transaction. This preserves real collapse (superposition genuinely becomes definiteness), time's arrow (collapse introduces irreversibility), and the retrocausal structure that the time-symmetric route brings to bear on the Libet timing problem. What the Map borrows from Cramer's interpretation is this mechanism, not its metaphysics: taken whole, the transactional picture is observer-independent and physicalist, assigning consciousness no role at all.

## Atemporal Selection

This section develops a route the Map holds *weakly*, and its standing is worth fixing before the argument runs. Where the Map expresses a preference about *how* consciousness couples to collapse, it favours [forward-in-time post-decoherence selection](/topics/forward-in-time-conscious-selection/)—selection acting on an already-decohered mixture, within ordinary forward causation, requiring no retrocausal commitment. The positions register treats time-symmetric and retrocausal readings as subordinate fallbacks: [quantum-interface](/positions/quantum-interface/) P-Q1 carries the mechanism preference, [agency-and-will](/positions/agency-and-will/) P-A3 holds the atemporal reading as a weakly-held fallback, and the mechanism-agnostic initiation/selection distinction carries the main load on Libet. The atemporal route is developed at length here because it couples agency to time's structure more tightly than any rival, which makes it the most illuminating case for a synthesis about time—not because the Map has settled on it. The choice between the two families remains, as [the comparative treatment](/topics/forward-in-time-vs-time-symmetric-selection/) puts it, underdetermined by the data.

The Libet experiments famously showed that neural "readiness potentials" precede conscious awareness of deciding by several hundred milliseconds. Later fMRI studies extended this gap to seconds. If neural activity comes first, how can consciousness cause it?

Time-symmetric physics offers to dissolve this objection, though the dissolution is only as secure as the retrocausal interpretation it rests on. [Retrocausal](/concepts/retrocausality/) selection isn't located at a moment but determines which transaction completes across time, so the neural activity "preceding" awareness is part of the selected package, not a prior cause. A mathematical function determines which curve appears on a graph without being located at any point on the graph.

"Atemporal" here doesn't mean "outside time" as if time were a container consciousness somehow escapes. It means the selection is not located at a point within the sequence it helps fix: within the local package of neural events tied to a decision, selection determines which temporal ordering becomes actual rather than being constrained by an ordering already laid down among those events. This concerns an agent's own experienced sequence, not the origin of temporal direction in general.

This reconciles atemporality with irreversibility. Once a transaction completes—once collapse has occurred—temporal direction exists. Within that direction, collapse is irreversible. You can't return to the superposition because "returning" presupposes the temporal direction that collapse just created. Retrocausality before collapse creates temporal direction; after collapse, backward causation becomes constraint satisfaction within the now-existing structure.

## What Durée Feels Like

If consciousness participates in the local collapses that constitute *lived* temporal structure, what should this feel like from inside? The Map proposes: durée—Bergson's qualitative, flowing, interpenetrating time—is exactly what participating in the constitution of lived time feels like from within. We experience duration because we participate in laying it down at the neural interface.

The specious present isn't a window onto pre-existing *experienced* temporal structure. It may be the phenomenology of collapse-participatory activity—the experiential signature of consciousness helping constitute lived time at the sites it inhabits. The [temporal consciousness analysis](/concepts/temporal-consciousness/) develops the mapping: **retention** reflects outcomes already collapsed, so the felt fixedness of the just-past is what already-collapsed states feel like from within; **primal impression** is the vivid present where selection is active; **protention** tracks genuine quantum indeterminacy rather than merely epistemic uncertainty, which is meaningful only if collapse is real. What distinguishes this from a generic structural analogy is the qualitative texture of each phase: neural accounts explain *that* the past is fixed and the future open, but not *why it feels that way*.

But the specious present doesn't just have duration—it has [temporal thickness](/concepts/temporal-consciousness/). Two moments of identical clock length can differ radically in phenomenal character. A musician improvising experiences each beat as dense with harmonic possibility, melodic memory, and anticipatory tension. Someone passively overhearing the same music inhabits a thinner present. This thickness reflects differences in retention depth (how much of the immediate past remains phenomenally present), protentional reach (how far anticipated futures shape the now), and the degree to which past, present, and future permeate each other. The variation matters: as [the temporal agency analysis](/topics/temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency/) argues, randomness at a temporally thin moment is mere noise, while indeterminacy at a temporally thick moment—where consciousness selects from possibilities saturated with personal history—is agency.

Multiple lines of evidence converge on the specious present as a phenomenal achievement rather than an automatic feature of neural processing. Advanced meditators report *nirodha samapatti*—complete cessation of temporal experience. Higher jhāna states show systematic variation: progressively "timeless" quality without loss of awareness. Dreamless NREM sleep offers a complementary datum, though a more qualified one than it is usually asked to bear. Sleepers report hours passing with no temporal experience at all—not compressed, not stretched, simply absent—despite ongoing neural computation. But the Map's own [treatment of sleep](/concepts/sleep-and-consciousness/) resists the flat version: consciousness diminishes in NREM yet rarely vanishes entirely, dreaming can occur in any sleep stage, and Siclari et al. (2017) found NREM dreaming to correlate with reduced posterior slow-wave activity—so "dreamless" is partly defined by absence of report rather than established absence of experience. What survives is weaker than a clean negative but points the same way: where experienced temporal structure is genuinely absent, the framework predicts that no consciousness-involving collapse constituted it for that subject. The clock on the wall keeps time through the sleeper's hours regardless.

Temporal disruptions reveal the same point from the pathological side. In schizophrenia, [temporal disintegration](/concepts/temporal-consciousness/) disrupts binding between successive moments while leaving within-moment experience intact—actions feel disconnected from consequences. Temporal unity is therefore contingent and structured, as the collapse-constitution framework predicts, rather than an automatic readout of neural timing.

## Implications for AI

A claim about temporal structure and consciousness has to be pitched at the right level, or it overreaches. The Map's [treatment of non-temporal consciousness](/topics/non-temporal-consciousness/) insists on keeping five things apart: the **temporal content** an experience represents (its represented succession, change, and duration); the **phenomenal organisation** of ordinary experience (its retention–impression–protention form); the **functional and neural integration** that binds successive states; **diachronic subject identity** (whether one subject persists across moments); and **metaphysical temporal location** (whether the constituting activity is itself within objective time). A verdict about one of these does not settle the others. In particular, that article defends a minimal or deepest conscious layer that can remain alert while lacking *represented succession*—so temporal content and phenomenal organisation are not, on the Map's own view, necessary for consciousness *as such*.

That immediately qualifies the AI exclusion. What ordinary human consciousness requires is temporal *content and phenomenal organisation*: the specious present, retention–protention structure, temporal binding. Large language models have none of these—processing is start-stop, stateless between calls, with no persistence across conversations, and information flows forward without the bidirectional reentrant looping that may constitute temporal binding. Erik Hoel's 2025 paper argues LLMs cannot be conscious precisely because they lack continual learning and temporal development.

The exclusion should therefore be stated with care: what such systems lack is the temporal organisation of ordinary human experience, and the obstacles the Map's dualism raises for machine consciousness are the quantum-interface ones treated elsewhere. The open question is whether radically different temporal architectures might support alien forms of experience. An engineered "specious present" mimicking human temporality would still need retention with phenomenal character, protentional openness toward a genuinely indeterminate future, and experiential continuity binding successive windows into a stream rather than a queue—a bar on reproducing *ordinary* temporal consciousness rather than a proof that only temporally organised systems can be conscious.

## The Hard Problem Deepened

The [hard problem](/topics/hard-problem-of-consciousness/) asks why physical processes should give rise to subjective experience. The temporal version asks why sequences of brain states should feel like flow. The local-participation thesis does not license the strong circularity argument that would say physics presupposes time and time is constituted by consciousness, so reducing consciousness to physics is circular. That move fails once the cosmic arrow is granted to be objective and prebiotic: physics does not presuppose anything consciousness supplies at the cosmological level.

What survives is narrower and does not depend on the strong thesis at all. It concerns *experienced* succession rather than temporal direction as such. The [argument from temporal thickness](/concepts/temporal-consciousness/) gives it a focused form. Physical descriptions consist of states at times and transition rules between them. No collection of instantaneous states contains the *experience of succession*—the felt passage from one moment to the next. The specious present is precisely this: temporally thick, extended, containing within it the sense of transition. The gap between *duration as measured* (a number of seconds between events) and *duration as experienced* (the felt thickness of now) parallels the gap between wavelength and colour—evidence of something physical description systematically misses.

This doesn't prove dualism. It says only that experienced succession opens a gap in the temporal dimension parallel to the familiar qualia gap—and that where consciousness participates in collapse, it is woven into the *felt* structure of lived temporal reality rather than arriving late as a readout of neural complexity.

## Free Will Reconsidered

The temporal framework reshapes the [free will debate](/topics/free-will/). If consciousness selects among quantum possibilities and collapse locally introduces irreversibility, then agents participate in constituting the *lived* temporal structure within which their actions unfold. [Agent causation](/concepts/agent-causation/) is not action passively slotted into a pre-given experiential ordering but participation in laying down the felt sequence of one's own agency—while the objective time in which the body moves is not itself the agent's creation.

On the atemporal reading the Libet objection dissolves: the neural activity "preceding" awareness and the awareness itself are parts of the same selected transaction. That reading is held weakly, and the Map's main reply does not depend on it—the mechanism-agnostic initiation/selection distinction, on which consciousness selects among options the brain has already prepared, carries the load either way. The ~280ms commitment point for voluntary motor action (Thura & Cisek, 2014) falls within the specious present's range, suggesting that willed movement requires the phenomenal duration the specious present provides. Agency unfolds within the temporally thick present rather than at a durationless decision-point.

[Temporal agency](/topics/temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency/) extends beyond split-second decisions into a richer phenomenology of timing. The Greeks distinguished *chronos* (sequential time) from *kairos* (the opportune moment). Before a timing window opens, anticipation carries a felt quality of readiness—the musician feels the impending entry. At the kairos moment, open anticipation collapses into committed action, and recognition and action are phenomenally unified, which is why skilled timing degrades under explicit attention: separating the judgment from the action disrupts something that was whole. After the window closes comes the felt finality of "too late." This three-phase structure ("not yet" / "now" / "too late") mirrors the Map's ontological claim that the future is genuinely open and the past genuinely fixed, and the phenomenal collapse from anticipation to action mirrors what the Map describes at the quantum level.

A puzzle arises: if each collapse constitutes a new moment, what preserves agent identity across collapses? The [collapse-time framework](/concepts/collapse-and-time/) proposes that identity isn't preserved *across* collapses from outside but constituted *through* the pattern of selections. Each collapse incorporates its predecessors as the background against which selection occurs—paralleling Husserl's analysis where the present moment contains the past as retained content. The agent is the selecting activity itself, which includes reflexive reference to previous selections. Identity as constituted activity rather than enduring substance.

Bergson's insight applies: a free act emerges from the whole self acting in durée. Determinism assumes mental states are discrete and separable, subject to causal law like billiard balls; if instead they interpenetrate in qualitative multiplicity, there is no distinct prior state to serve as deterministic cause, and genuine novelty can emerge.

## The Circularity Problem

If consciousness constitutes lived temporal structure but consciousness itself appears temporal, the account seems circular. [Husserl's resolution](/topics/temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency/): an "absolute flow" beneath both world-time and experiential time constitutes temporal experience without itself being temporal—"standing-streaming" that manifests temporality without undergoing succession. The regress stops because the deepest level of consciousness is not itself located within the lived sequence it helps constitute. Advanced meditators report something in this vicinity: vivid awareness stripped of temporal markers—no succession, no change, yet full alertness—suggesting temporal structure is something consciousness *does* rather than something it *is*.

Whitehead's [process philosophy](/concepts/process-philosophy/) anticipated the structure from a different angle: reality consists of "actual occasions"—experiential events that synthesise their causal past and perish—and concrescence, how an occasion achieves unity, parallels quantum collapse. The precedent matters because it shows the picture does not depend on quantum mechanics for its coherence.

## Synthesis

The individual threads—specious present, collapse, durée, atemporal selection—converge. Each addresses a different puzzle, yet all point to the same structure: within neural systems, consciousness and *lived* time are mutually constitutive, neither the experienced flow simply containing the mind nor the mind simply containing the flow. This is a claim about experienced becoming, held apart from the cosmic arrow, which objective collapse supplies independently.

The scope discipline matters. Presentism, eternalism, and orthodox growing block all treat consciousness as merely *within* time. Functionalism, IIT, and global workspace theory treat time as merely *within* consciousness's computational substrate. The Map splits the difference by keeping two questions apart: the origin of temporal direction as such, which it hands to objective collapse and the prebiotic arrow, and the constitution of *experienced* becoming, in which consciousness genuinely participates. Overreaching—claiming consciousness constitutes time in general—would collide with the Map's own commitment to objective single-outcome actualisation, so the framework earns its bidirectional language only at the local, phenomenal level.

The evidence comes from several directions—phenomenological, neuroscientific, metaphysical, and negative—and no single line is conclusive. The quantum-consciousness connection faces empirical challenges, including a [twelve-order-of-magnitude timescale gap](/concepts/temporal-consciousness/) between quantum decoherence in neural tissue and the specious present, and the phenomenological evidence admits alternative explanations. What each line independently rests on is set out next.

## Evidence and Dependency

These lines of support are not equally weighted, and their convergence should not be read as several independent confirmations of one conclusion.

The empirical spine is externally evidenced: the psychophysics of the specious present, Kent and Wittmann's gap between functional moments and experienced duration, the ~280ms motor commitment point, and Siclari et al. on NREM dreaming all stand independently of this framework. None of them discriminates a collapse-constitution account from an ordinary neural-timing account. They establish the phenomena an account must explain, not the account.

The argument from temporal thickness is independently argued, but it requires one premise a physicalist will refuse: that phenomenal character is not exhausted by functional description.

The collapse-constitution claim itself is inherited from Tenets 1 through 4. It is what those commitments imply about lived time rather than further evidence for them, and the scope discipline keeping the cosmic arrow objective and prebiotic is likewise inherited, from the [background commitments](/tenets/background-commitments/) held elsewhere.

The mapping from retention, primal impression, and protention onto collapsed, collapsing, and uncollapsed states is mutually coherent only. It fits elegantly, and fit is all it contributes.

## Relation to Site Perspective

This synthesis connects to all five of The Unfinishable Map's [tenets](/tenets/):

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)**: The route to irreducibility does not run through the strong circularity claim that physics presupposes a time consciousness constitutes—that argument is set aside, since the cosmic arrow is objective and prebiotic. What survives is the temporal form of the explanatory gap: experienced duration resists reduction to sequences of functional states at times, just as colour resists reduction to wavelength. Physical description captures the ordering of states; it does not seem to capture what succession *feels like*, and that residue is where the case for irreducibility lives.

**[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)**: Selection at collapse points is the minimal mechanism—on the Map's registered preference, [forward-in-time selection](/topics/forward-in-time-conscious-selection/) acting on the already-decohered mixture; on the subordinate atemporal reading, selection not located at a point in the sequence it helps fix. Either way consciousness doesn't inject energy, violate conservation laws, or override physics: it selects among possibilities physics provides, at exactly the gap where superposition becomes definiteness.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)**: Consciousness influences physical outcomes through collapse selection, biasing *which* of the already-definite-making outcomes obtains at the neural interface, within the Born distribution. On the retrocausal reading the selection propagates into both past preparation and future consequence through constraint satisfaction across time. The brain prepares neural options; consciousness selects which becomes actual; the selection fixes the *experienced* ordering of that local package rather than authoring temporal direction in general.

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)**: The framework requires collapse to be real. Many-worlds denies collapse: all branches exist, and consciousness merely discovers which one it inhabits rather than selecting. Without genuine selection it doesn't participate in constituting lived temporal structure but passively experiences pre-existing branches, and the coherence of durée—where retention genuinely inherits from a definite past—requires a single temporal thread.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)**: The metaphysics is complex, but temporal experience is complex and time's arrow remains among physics' deepest puzzles. Simpler metaphysics achieve parsimony by denying phenomena rather than explaining them: the block universe denies temporal becoming, illusionism denies the reality of durée. The framework's complexity may reflect reality's structure rather than theoretical excess.
