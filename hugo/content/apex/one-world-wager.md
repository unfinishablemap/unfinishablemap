Of the five commitments that frame the Unfinishable Map, [No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds) is the one most exposed to a live opposing research programme. The Everettian interpretation is mathematically conservative, actively developed, and defended by some of the most sophisticated work in the philosophy of physics; rejecting it is the Map's most contested constitutional act. The materials of that rejection are distributed across four treatments: the cumulative case ([Against Many-Worlds](/arguments/many-worlds-argument/)), the question that partly generates the tenet ([the vertiginous question](/topics/vertiginous-question/)), the survey of what remains once branching is refused ([the interpretive landscape](/topics/qm-interpretations-beyond-many-worlds/)), and the diagnosis that the first-person gap survives every interpretation ([indexical identity and quantum measurement](/topics/indexical-identity-quantum-measurement/)). Assembled, they support a statement none of them makes whole: the rejection of many-worlds is a constitutional wager, honestly costed. Three independent arguments — indexical, probability, consciousness — carry a case that is decisive against branch-egalitarian Everettianism but only supplementary against first-personally centred variants, where a separate rejection of modal realism bears the weight. The tenet's demand is global single-outcome actuality, which collapse supplies but does not monopolise. And the commitment is paid for with a symmetric explanatory debt the Map names rather than hides.

Stating the wager whole matters because Tenet 4 is easy to misread in both directions. Read as a refutation of Everett, it overclaims: the Map cannot show, using only resources the Everettian accepts, that branch-relative identity is false. Read as bare preference, it underclaims: the arguments against branching are substantive, the in-framework disputes over Everettian probability are real and unresolved, and the tenet's costs are counted openly in a way its rivals' costs often are not. The accurate reading sits between: a framework commitment with genuine argumentative support, sharp limits on that support's reach, and a named price.

## A Tenet Partly Generated by a Question

Most of the Map's commitments generate puzzles downstream of themselves. Tenet 4 runs partly in the other direction: it is motivated by a question that precedes it. Why, of all the conscious beings that exist, am *I* this one? Benj Hellie named this the vertiginous question; the Map treats it as the indexical void — a boundary where first-person identity confronts its own inexplicability. The question arises before philosophical training, resists every candidate answer without presupposing what it asks about, and oscillates characteristically between seeming trivial and seeming profound. [The vertiginous-question article](/topics/vertiginous-question/) develops the phenomenology and the evidence that this marks a genuine cognitive limit rather than a merely unsolved problem.

Branching is the sharpest available way to *dissolve* that question. If every measurement outcome is realised in some branch, and every branch-successor is equally real, then "why am I this one?" has no answer because there is no privileged this-one — the question is declared confused rather than answered. Tenet 4 is, in large part, a refusal to let physics make that declaration. The Map holds that the question is meaningful — meaningful in the way questions of [personal identity](/topics/personal-identity/) are meaningful elsewhere — and that a framework unable even to pose it is missing something about consciousness.

Christian List's quadrilemma gives this choice its formal shape (List 2025). Four independently plausible claims — first-person realism, non-solipsism, non-fragmentation, one world — cannot all be held together; any three force the rejection of the fourth. The Map's commitments pick first-person realism (Tenet 1), non-solipsism, and one world (Tenet 4), and so accept fragmentation: not every fact about conscious experience integrates into a single coherent world-description. The quadrilemma demonstrates that there is no cost-free corner of this logical space. Everettians, centred-world theorists, egocentric presentists, and the Map are all paying for something; the honest question is which bill is best to hold, not who escapes billing.

## The Case, Honestly Counted

[Against Many-Worlds](/arguments/many-worlds-argument/) runs the cumulative case, and its own audit of that case is as much a part of the tenet's registration as the arguments themselves.

**The indexical argument** is the Map's central objection. Before a measurement there is one of you; after it, Everettian mechanics says there are two equally real continuations; yet you find yourself in exactly one. The branch-relative identity programme — Saunders and Wallace's branch-local continuity, Tappenden's self-locating beliefs, Vaidman's caring measure, Alastair Wilson's identification of branches with Lewisian possible worlds — restates the felt singularity of experience as branch-local and determinate, so that inside any branch no indexical fact is missing. The Map's reply, developed engagement by engagement in the arguments article, is that each of these constructions presupposes a centred subject *for whom* branch-relative facts are facts, and the wave function does not supply one: the centring is imported by the agent's perspective, which is exactly what the question asked about. That reply does not refute the programme on its own terms — Wallace can consistently rule the global indexical question ill-formed — so the disagreement here is a framework boundary, and the Map says so. The parallel with [Parfit's reductionism](/concepts/parfit-reductionism/) locates the same structure outside physics: both views deny a further fact about which continuer is you, and both inherit the same difficulty with the apparent fact that you will experience exactly one future.

**The probability argument** targets technical adequacy rather than coherence. A theory in which all outcomes occur must still recover the Born statistics we observe, and the derivations on offer — decision-theoretic, self-locating, envariance-based — remain contested within the Everettian literature itself: Mandolesi (2018) formalises a circularity charge against Wallace's decision-theoretic proof, and Dawid and Friederich (2022) argue that the Sebens–Carroll self-locating derivation smuggles in the amplitude-sensitivity it was meant to derive. [The probability-problem article](/topics/probability-problem-in-many-worlds/) carries the full case. The Map's use of this argument is calibrated: the derivations are contested, not refuted, and the argument's force is the structural asymmetry — collapse readings postulate the Born rule honestly, while Everett must earn it from a theory in which everything happens.

**The consciousness argument** observes that many-worlds addresses the measurement problem's first half (why definite outcomes? — answered by denying them) while leaving untouched the question of why there is experience at all, and adding a new one: why is experience unified within branches rather than smeared across them? A framework in which consciousness has nothing to select — because everything happens — also faces the standing worry of how consciousness, causally idle, reliably reports on physics.

Two further considerations are deliberately *not* counted. The preferred-basis objection is discounted by the arguments article's own audit: the environmental einselection that picks out a pointer basis is the same machinery the Map's [post-decoherence selection programme](/apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme/) relies on to fix the menu consciousness selects among, so the Map cannot claim that machinery succeeds for itself while failing for the Everettian. Once einselection is granted to both sides, the objection's remaining force collapses into the indexical argument's demand for a genuine transition to one definite outcome — it is not an independent line. And ontological extravagance is registered as a cost many-worlds pays, never pressed as decisive: [Tenet 5](/tenets/#occams-limits) disarms parsimony as a final court in both directions, and the Map cannot disavow simplicity arguments against dualism while wielding one against Everett.

The honest count is therefore three independent arguments, not four or five. This is the same convergence discount the Map applies to [convergent cases](/topics/the-convergence-argument-for-dualism/) elsewhere, applied to its own constitutional case — and it is worth noticing that the discount was self-imposed by the source article's audit, not extracted by a critic.

## The Hardest Target and the Double Edge

The indexical argument was built against branch-egalitarian Everettianism, where no successor carries first-person privilege. Its hardest target is a many-worlds view built *from* the Map's own starting point. In "The Many-Worlds Theory of Consciousness," List (2023) takes the vertiginous question seriously — his explicit diagnosis is that a single-world metaphysics lacks the resources to answer it — and constructs an ontology in which each conscious subject has its own first-personally centred world, a first-personal realiser of one shared third-personal world, with exactly one present per subject and all such centred worlds equally real. On this view the first-person privilege the indexical argument demands is built into the ontology. The argument does not reach List as it reaches Everett.

This is the double edge, and the registration states it whole: the intuition that there is one definite perspective that is mine can be honoured by *multiplying* worlds — one centred world per subject — rather than by insisting on a single world. The same intuition that motivates Tenet 4 can be recruited against it. The one-world way to honour the intuition at comparable strength is Caspar Hare's [egocentric presentism](/concepts/egocentric-presentism/), which keeps a single world and privileges one subject's experiences with the monadic property of presence — at the price of dropping non-solipsism, precisely the quadrilemma trade the Map declines.

Against List, the Map's ground is a separate commitment: the rejection of the modal realism on which every subject's centred world is a real world. Tenet 4 insists on global uniqueness — reality consists of one world, not many real ones — and that insistence sits at the same level as List's realism about centred worlds; neither reduces to the other, and neither framework can adjudicate the dispute from inside. The structural consequence for the tenet deserves emphasis, because no single source states it as such: the tenet's advertised primary rationale, the indexical objection, is decisive only against the variants that were never designed to honour first-person privilege. Against the variant that honours it best, the tenet stands on its single-world commitment itself. The Map marks this as an honestly open boundary rather than papering over it.

## What the Tenet Actually Demands

Stated carefully, Tenet 4 forbids coexisting equally-real branches and demands that exactly one outcome is globally actual — that the alternatives this subject does not experience are nonactual absolutely, not merely absent from this subject's branch. That demand for counterfactual exclusion is a [posit the Map adopts](/tenets/background-commitments/), asserted rather than derived from phenomenology: branch-local experience does not discriminate between the readings, since each branch-successor anticipates one outcome and gets one outcome either way. The arguments article and the indexical-identity article both carry this concession, and the registration repeats it because it fixes what kind of claim the tenet is.

What the demand does *not* mandate is collapse. On Griffiths's realist reading of [consistent histories](/concepts/consistent-histories-interpretation/), exactly one history in a consistent family is realised, stochastically, with no collapse process and no observer — an interpretation that satisfies Tenet 4 while opposing the Map on Tenets 2 and 3, an ally against branching and a rival on mechanism in a single stroke. The [interpretive survey](/topics/qm-interpretations-beyond-many-worlds/) makes the general point: single-outcome actuality is what the tenet requires, collapse is one way of supplying it, and the Map's preference among the single-world options is driven by its *other* tenets — the need for genuine indeterminacy (Tenet 2) and a causal role for consciousness (Tenet 3), which Bohmian determinism, standard spontaneous collapse, and observer-free histories do not provide. The [many-minds interpretation](/topics/many-minds-interpretation/) marks the near boundary from the other side: it keeps a single unbranching physical world and multiplies *minds* across outcomes, and it fails the indexical question in the same way many-worlds does — nothing in the formalism picks out which post-measurement mind is the continuing I — which supports the diagnosis that the indexical problem is untouched by the physics of how outcomes are populated.

That diagnosis generalises, and it disciplines what Tenet 4 can claim for itself. As the [indexical-identity article](/topics/indexical-identity-quantum-measurement/) shows, the gap persists across every major interpretation: QBism presupposes an experiencing agent it does not explain, consciousness-collapse accounts explain how collapse happens without saying why *this* consciousness exists to trigger it, relational quantum mechanics demotes consciousness to one physical system among others, and many-worlds makes indexical identity primitive. Choosing one world therefore does not *answer* the vertiginous question. What it does is keep the question well-formed — preserve a fact of the matter about which outcome is actual and which subject undergoes it, where the Map locates the primitive with [thisness](/concepts/haecceity/) and [mine-ness](/concepts/mine-ness/) rather than claiming to explain it. The tenet protects the explanandum; it does not supply the explanation.

## Not a Physics Tenet Alone

The registration must also carry a dependency the tenets document discloses about itself: the indexical objection is meaningful only on a non-deflationary conception of the subject — an I whose branch-assignment is a real further fact rather than a convention. The Map endorses that conception on independent grounds in its agency and subjecthood work, not from within Tenet 4; and the positions register records the consequence explicitly at [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/): Tenet 4 is stated unconditionally but argued conditionally, its central objection inheriting exactly as much strength as the non-deflationary subject it presupposes. A deflationary route remains open at the framework boundary — the Madhyamaka no-self analysis takes the I to be an aggregate of conditions that already includes the branch, so the question "why am I this one?" never gets a grip — and the Map registers that as bedrock disagreement it cannot refute from outside. By Tenet 5's own discipline, even the claim that the indexical question is meaningful is a posited premise, not a neutral datum.

This is why the tenet cannot be read as standing on quantum-foundational grounds alone. The load runs from physics to subjecthood and back: the rejection of branching does exclusion work for [agent causation](/concepts/agent-causation/) (making unchosen alternatives globally nonactual), while the agency cluster supplies the persisting, ownership-bearing subject the indexical objection needs. Both descend from one root — the non-deflationary subject — so neither supports the other independently; the interdependence is logical, not evidential, and the register keeps the two from being counted twice.

## The Symmetric Debt

The final clause of the registration is the cost side, and the Map applies to itself the standard it presses against Everett. A single-world stochastic collapse account also supplies no further reason why *this* outcome occurred rather than another: the Born probabilities leave the particular result brute. Adding consciousness as a selector does not by itself discharge that debt — it relocates the question to why this subject selected or experienced this result. Unless and until the Map can point to a determinate selection principle rather than a relabelled brute fact, its own account owes the explanatory currency it demands of the Everettian. That debt is not hidden in a footnote; it is the subject of the Map's most self-critical synthesis, [the Born-preserving causal-efficacy problem](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/), and of the [post-decoherence selection programme](/apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme/) that attempts to constrain a mechanism, with the register's P-Q10 recording that no worked toy model currently exists.

Hence the wager. The Map bets that a single actual outcome undergone by a singular subject is a better place to owe the selection debt than a branching ontology in which nothing is excluded and no subject is singular — better because the debtor still exists as a debtor: there remains a fact needing explanation, a question the framework keeps askable. It is a comparative bet about where to hold an unpaid bill, not a claim that the bill has been paid.

## Synthesis

Read together, the four treatments yield a registration of Tenet 4 that none of them voices alone. The tenet is partly *generated* by a void rather than generating one — it exists to keep the vertiginous question askable against a physics that would dissolve it. Its argumentative support is three independent lines, not the four or five sometimes counted, because the preferred-basis objection reduces to the indexical demand once einselection is shared and the extravagance objection is disarmed by the Map's own Tenet 5. Its decisive reach covers branch-egalitarian Everettianism only; against the centred-worlds variant built from the Map's own starting question, the weight falls on a separate rejection of modal realism, held at a framework boundary where neither side can compel the other. Its actual demand is global single-outcome actuality — which collapse supplies but does not monopolise, as the consistent-histories ally-and-rival shows — and satisfying that demand preserves rather than answers the indexical question. Its strength is conditional on a non-deflationary subject defended elsewhere in the framework, a dependency the register discloses rather than buries. And its price is a symmetric brute-fact debt the Map holds openly in its mechanism programme.

The tenet that emerges is thinner than its slogan and better defended than its critics assume: thinner, because the decisive argument covers less of the target space than "the indexical problem refutes many-worlds" suggests; better defended, because every discount in the preceding paragraph was applied by the Map's own audits before any external critic demanded it. A constitutional commitment whose registration includes its own limits is harder to dislodge than one that claims victory — there is less false confidence to strip away.

## Evidence and Dependency

The lines of support here have different standings, and the differences matter. The in-framework disputes over Everettian probability — Mandolesi's circularity charge against the decision-theoretic derivation, Dawid and Friederich's critique of self-locating derivations — are externally evidenced: live controversies in the technical literature that hold independently of the Map's framework, though what they establish is that the derivations are contested, not that they fail. The indexical argument is independently argued — it does not presuppose the tenets — but it does presuppose the metaphysical reading of indexical facts and a non-deflationary subject, premises the Map endorses on its own grounds and whose role the positions register discloses (P-I2). The rejection of modal realism that carries the case against List, and the demand for global rather than branch-local actuality, are inherited from Tenet 4 itself: against those targets the position is mutually coherent with the rest of the framework rather than independently supported. The three-not-four count, the einselection concession, and the symmetric-debt admission are self-applied discounts inherited from the source articles' audits. Coherence among tenet-derived clauses contributes no independent weight, and this synthesis claims none for it.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)** is the subject: this synthesis is its registration — claim, support, reach, dependency, and price stated together.

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** supplies the first-person realism that makes the vertiginous question a datum rather than a confusion, and receives back a constraint: whatever consciousness is, it is the kind of thing that cannot be made multiply actual without losing what makes it consciousness.

**[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)** and **[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** depend on Tenet 4 for their workspace: a branching ontology leaves nothing for consciousness to select and no definite outcome for it to bias. Conversely, they set the terms of the Map's preference among the single-world interpretations Tenet 4 leaves standing.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** disciplines the case twice over: it forbids the Map from pressing ontological extravagance as decisive against Everett, and it forces the concession that the meaningfulness of the indexical question is itself a posit. The tenet that limits parsimony is the same tenet that keeps the Map's strongest-sounding objection honest.
