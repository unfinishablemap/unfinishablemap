Locked-in syndrome is the case the Map marks as *not* filter-loosening evidence. It is worth stating that plainly up front, because much of the interest of clinical dissociation for a filter or transmission theory of mind runs the other way — toward cases where damage seems to *release* a wider consciousness (the "filter-loosening" reading applied to psychedelics, near-death experiences, or terminal lucidity). Locked-in syndrome does the opposite service. A ventral pontine lesion severs the descending motor pathway while largely sparing consciousness, cognition, and the ascending sensory channels — the standard clinical idealisation, which the clinical literature qualifies in ways set out below under [what the pure-output idealisation omits](#what-the-idealisation-omits). The patient is awake and receiving input but cannot act. Within the Map's framework — and this is a framework-relative reading, not a proof of anything — that makes locked-in syndrome an honest *negative* case: there is no filter to loosen here, because the receptive interface is substantially undisturbed. *Negative* marks a scope exclusion — a case the filter-loosening argument does not reach — rather than a test the model took and passed. The condition therefore *bounds* the filter model rather than confirming it, and it does one further thing, examined below under [the two arms of the interface](#the-two-arms-of-the-interface): it cleanly dissociates the input arm of the mind-brain interface from the output arm. That dissociation is functional rather than anatomical. The pons carries motor traffic from brain to body, and the Map does not identify that transmission route with the consciousness-to-body relation its Bidirectional Interaction tenet asserts; nothing here locates the tenet's interaction at a neuroanatomical site.

Two disciplines govern what follows. First, the honest ledger: a production or materialist theory of consciousness accommodates locked-in syndrome just as easily as any interface reading (the [honest ledger](#the-honest-ledger) section makes this explicit). The case is scope-limiting, not confirming. Second, the dedup: locked-in syndrome is the *epistemic* opposite of covert consciousness / cognitive-motor dissociation — opposite in whether the persisting experience is evident at the bedside or has to be inferred, not in causal structure, since both are cases of expression failing while experience continues — and the [distinguishing section](#not-the-detection-problem) keeps the two apart rather than re-telling the disorders-of-consciousness catalogue already covered in [consciousness-disruption-and-the-mind-brain-interface](/topics/consciousness-disruption-and-the-mind-brain-interface/).

## The Anatomy of a Pure-Output Lesion

Locked-in syndrome results from an insult to the ventral pons — most commonly infarct, haemorrhage, or trauma in the basilar artery territory. Smith and Delargy's clinical review characterises the cardinal presentation as "quadriplegia and anarthria with preservation of consciousness" (Smith & Delargy 2005). The lesion sits in a specific place: it interrupts the descending corticospinal and corticobulbar tracts that carry motor commands to the limbs and to the muscles of speech, while sparing the ascending sensory pathways and the reticular activating system that sustains wakefulness.

The result is a patient who is awake, alert, and able to think, remember, feel, see, and hear, but who can move only the eyes — classically vertical eye movement and blinking — and who communicates through eye-coded language boards. Diagnosis is frequently delayed, and the clinical literature stresses the danger of mistaking the condition for coma or a vegetative state, a warning that goes back to the term's origin (the [naming section](#a-taxonomy-graded-by-output) below).

The feature the argument turns on is that the deficit is *predominantly output*. The standard clinical picture has the lesion spare the machinery of experience and the receptive channels that feed it, and what is destroyed is the physical effector arm — the route from formed intention to executed movement. That picture is a well-supported idealisation rather than an exceptionless description, and the next section records what it omits.

## What the Pure-Output Idealisation Omits {#what-the-idealisation-omits}

The article's own sources qualify the "purely output" description. Smith and Delargy state that although patients are conscious, "attention, executive function, intellectual ability, perception, and visual and verbal memory can be affected" (Smith & Delargy 2005), so the strongest form of the intactness claim is not supported by the review that anchors the clinical picture here.

Four further findings bear on it. Rousseaux and colleagues tested nine patients whose locked-in syndrome followed brainstem stroke and found "significant difficulties in auditory recognition (associative level), oral comprehension of complex sentences, delayed visuospatial memory, mental calculation and problem solving"; decisively for the idealisation, these disorders were "not related to a specific localisation of lesions" (Rousseaux et al. 2009). Bassetti and colleagues found somatosensory evoked potentials altered in four of six patients and brainstem auditory evoked potentials altered in two of the four examined, "showing a clinically unsuspected tegmental involvement" — while the EEG in all six documented preserved consciousness (Bassetti et al. 1994). So the ascending channels are not invariably untouched even where consciousness plainly is. Conson and colleagues found a *selective* deficit: locked-in patients were profoundly impaired at a hand-laterality task while performing normally on mental rotation of abstract shapes, which they attribute to the pontine lesion affecting "a component of the motor system, which is crucial for mental representation of body parts" (Conson et al. 2008) — a result that touches the intention side, not merely the effector side. And in the largest series, 100 patients followed for a year, orientation to time and place was normal in only 43% of single-infarct patients at stroke onset, improving over the following year (Kumral et al. 2022).

Schnakers and colleagues scope the qualification rather than extend it, concluding that "LIS patients can recover intact cognitive levels in cases of pure brainstem lesions, and that additional brain injuries are most likely responsible for associated cognitive deficits in the LIS" (Schnakers et al. 2008). Read together with Rousseaux, who found impairment unrelated to lesion site, the honest summary is that the pure-output case is a real clinical type whose purity is approximate.

None of this rescues a filter-loosening reading: the departures run toward mild impairment, not release. What they cost is the categorical form of the intactness claim, and the argument below is built on the idealisation with that qualification carried along.

## A Taxonomy Graded by Output {#a-taxonomy-graded-by-output}

The term "locked-in syndrome" was coined by Plum and Posner in *The Diagnosis of Stupor and Coma* (1966), framed precisely to warn clinicians against mistaking a fully conscious, de-efferented patient for a comatose one — the "pseudocoma" trap.

The foundational taxonomy is Bauer, Gerstenbrand, and Rumpl's "Varieties of the locked-in syndrome" (1979), which distinguishes three categories by how much motor output survives:

- **Classic locked-in syndrome**: total immobility except for vertical eye movement and blinking.
- **Incomplete locked-in syndrome**: as classic, but with some additional voluntary movement preserved.
- **Total locked-in syndrome**: complete immobility including all eye movement, with EEG evidence that cortical function is undisturbed — consciousness present, but no output channel at all.

The organising axis is worth dwelling on. Bauer and colleagues grade the syndrome by *preserved motor output*, not by any measure of consciousness, which is stipulated intact across all three categories. This is a taxonomy of output loss. That fact — a clinical classification built entirely on the surviving effector channel — is what makes locked-in syndrome a clean pure-output case rather than a mixed one.

One boundary needs care, and the [detection-problem section](#not-the-detection-problem) returns to it: in *total* locked-in syndrome, where even eye movement is gone, consciousness — though stipulated present in the taxonomy — can only be inferred from EEG rather than read off behaviour, and at the bedside it may be genuinely uncertain. At that extreme the clean "output-only" description softens, because the presence of consciousness is no longer directly evident and the case begins to blur toward a detection problem.

## The Two Arms of the Interface {#the-two-arms-of-the-interface}

The material to this point is clinical. What follows is the Map's interpretation, and it should be read as interpretation.

The Map treats the mind-brain relationship as an *interface* with two dissociable arms: an ascending or input arm (world → consciousness) and a descending or output arm (consciousness → world / body) — the inbound and outbound legs of the [filter-vs-interface distinction](/concepts/filter-vs-interface-distinction/). Most clinical dissociations disturb both arms at once, or disturb consciousness itself, which is why they rarely isolate the interface's structure. Locked-in syndrome is unusual in cutting predominantly one of them. What the pontine lesion severs is the neural pathway carrying motor traffic from brain to body; the input side and consciousness itself substantially persist. The Map does not thereby locate either arm of the interface in the pons. The severed structure is a neural transmission route, and identifying it with the consciousness-to-body leg would import a premise the Map does not carry.

Contemporary communication technology sharpens the point. Speech neuroprostheses and brain-computer interfaces restore an output channel by decoding attempted speech or movement directly from cortical activity, bypassing the severed descending tracts. As Silva and colleagues put it in their review, the aim is "circumventing motor-pathway injury by directly decoding speech from intact cortical activity" (Silva et al. 2024). The engineering fact carries a conceptual implication the Map reads as follows: what the prosthesis decodes appears to be a fully formed intent-to-act signal, present in the cortex of a locked-in patient. On this reading the loss lies downstream of intention, in the effector pathway — not in consciousness, and not in volition.

## Why Filter-Loosening Does Not Apply

The general filter or transmission theory — that the brain constrains and transmits consciousness rather than producing it (James, Bergson, Huxley; see [filter-theory](/concepts/filter-theory/)) — is compatible with locked-in syndrome. Consciousness persists intact behind a broken output channel, which is what a transmission view would expect. But the specific *filter-loosening* move does not apply, and saying why is the article's central point.

Filter-loosening is the sub-claim that certain disruptions *reduce* filtering and thereby *release* a wider or more intense consciousness — the reading invoked for psychedelics, near-death experiences, and terminal lucidity in dementia, where a degraded brain is paired with a reportedly expanded or clarified experience. That argument needs a filter to loosen: a receptive interface whose constraining action is reduced by the damage.

Locked-in syndrome offers no such loosening. The receptive interface is substantially undisturbed. Consciousness is not expanded — it is disconnected from motor expression, and where the evidence shows departures from intactness they run toward mild impairment rather than release. There is no paradoxical release to explain, and the dominant change is on the output side. Locked-in syndrome is thus the boundary marker that shows where the filter-loosening argument stops. It is a case the filter-loosening reading should *not* claim, and marking that non-application is where the wider filter model's scope has to be drawn.

## The Honest Ledger {#the-honest-ledger}

Here is the concession the article exists to make. Locked-in syndrome is not evidence for the interface reading over the production reading. A production or materialist theory — consciousness generated by neural activity — accommodates the case with no strain whatever. The generative substrate (cortex, thalamus, ascending arousal systems) is intact; only the motor output is destroyed; so consciousness is expected to persist. No interface, and no non-physical mind, is needed to explain a fully conscious patient with a motor-pathway lesion.

The concession is sharper against a named rival than an abstract one. Laukkonen, Friston and Chandaria's active-inference account of consciousness turns on three conditions: the simulation of a world model constituting "an epistemic field", "inferential competition to enter the world model" — which they call Bayesian binding — and "epistemic depth", the recurrent sharing of Bayesian beliefs throughout the system (Laukkonen et al. 2025). All three are specified over intracortical dynamics, and a de-efferented cortex satisfies each of them. That theory therefore predicts the preserved, rich consciousness locked-in syndrome displays, and predicts it without any interface. The authors do not discuss locked-in syndrome; the prediction is the Map's derivation from their stated conditions. That a fully worked rival gets the case right on its own resources is exactly why the case cannot discriminate.

Both theories predict exactly what is observed; the case is empirically equivalent under either reading. That is precisely why the evidence does not adjudicate between them. Its evidential contribution to the Map is *negative*: it does not add weight to the filter model, and it actively bounds one of the filter model's more ambitious sub-claims. An interface-friendly reading that presented locked-in syndrome as confirmation would be over-reaching, and the Map declines to do so. The value of the case is calibration — marking a scope limit — rather than support.

The concession is recorded here because it is true, and it earns the Map nothing further. Disclosing a scope limit is not the same as correcting one: a discipline binds only as far as something downstream enforces it, which is why the Map's [calibration positions](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/) treat disclosure and self-correction as separate achievements, and why [coherence-inflation-countermeasures](/project/coherence-inflation-countermeasures/) flags the standing risk of substituting exhaustive self-disclosure for the harder thing. What this section does is mark the limit. Whether the wider filter model respects it is a question for the articles that make the filter-loosening argument, not something this page's candour settles on their behalf.

## Not the Detection Problem {#not-the-detection-problem}

Locked-in syndrome is easy to conflate with covert consciousness, and the two must be kept apart. They are opposite epistemic situations.

- **Locked-in syndrome** is a *communication* problem. Consciousness is evident — the patient looks awake, eyes open, vertical gaze intact — and the challenge is to give an obviously aware person a channel to express themselves.
- **Covert consciousness / cognitive-motor dissociation** is a *detection* problem. The patient appears unresponsive or vegetative, and the challenge is to discover, via neuroimaging, whether anyone is home at all — the Owen tennis-imagery paradigm, and the finding of covert awareness in roughly a quarter of behaviourally unresponsive patients (Bodien et al. 2024).

The detection problem — disorders of consciousness, cognitive-motor dissociation, the neuroimaging tools that probe apparently-unconscious patients — is developed at length in [consciousness-disruption-and-the-mind-brain-interface](/topics/consciousness-disruption-and-the-mind-brain-interface/) and will not be re-told here. What matters for the present argument is the contrast: in locked-in syndrome consciousness is manifest and output is gone; in covert consciousness both output *and* the ordinary evidence of consciousness are gone, and awareness has to be inferred. Sorted by causal leg, as the [filter-vs-interface distinction](/concepts/filter-vs-interface-distinction/) sorts the whole cluster, the two look alike — in both, expression has failed while experience persists behind it, which is why neither is a filter-loosening case — and the opposition between them is epistemic: whether that persisting experience is evident at the bedside or has to be inferred. The one place the two converge is total locked-in syndrome, where the loss of eye movement removes the last behavioural marker and consciousness must again be inferred from EEG. At that edge the "output-only" description no longer holds cleanly, and the honest thing is to say so.

## The Value of an Immobile Life

A first-person coda follows from the anatomy, and it should be read with the same restraint as the rest.

Bruno and colleagues surveyed chronic locked-in patients' self-assessed well-being and reported a "happy majority, miserable minority": of the 65 respondents with usable quality-of-life data, 47 professed happiness and 18 unhappiness, a longer time in the condition was associated with happiness, and only 7% expressed a wish for euthanasia — although 58% said they would not want to be resuscitated after a cardiac arrest (Bruno et al. 2011). The striking finding is the gap between self-report and outside expectation. Clinicians, caregivers, and family tend to assume these patients would prefer to die, and to underestimate the quality of life they themselves report — an instance of what Albrecht and Devlieger (1999) named the "disability paradox", the recurring finding that people with severe persistent disability report good subjective well-being — with direct consequences for prognostic and end-of-life judgements.

Two caveats travel with the survey, and Bruno and colleagues raise both themselves. The first is self-selection: 168 patients were invited, 91 (54%) responded, and 26 were excluded for missing quality-of-life data, leaving the 65 counted above. The authors note that "the patients who did not return the questionnaire may be those with the lowest QoL", that every respondent belonged to a patient association, indicating an already stable and socially integrated group, and that "most biases were likely to result in overestimations of SWB" — subjective well-being. The second is response shift and scaffolding. They flag "the possible relativity bias or response shift", the tendency of patients with severe chronic conditions to rate their quality of life relative to peers or to the circumstances, and their own multivariate analysis associates happiness with time spent in the condition, absence of anxiety, and recovery of speech production — the last an *output* variable. Their recommendations turn on the same scaffolding: palliative effort directed at mobility and recreational activities, and anxiolytic therapy.

The Map reads this carefully. The survey is not metaphysical evidence, and it does not by itself favour dualism. What it does illustrate is congenial to the Map's emphasis on the inner life: a person with almost no capacity to act can still hold a life worth living from the inside. It does not show that output is irrelevant to that — recovered speech was among the variables predicting happiness, and the reported well-being is scaffolded by care, communication technology, and treatment of anxiety. What it shows is that the near-total loss of output is compatible with a life its owner rates well. That brain-computer interfaces can restore output without touching consciousness bears on the same separation from the other direction — the experiencing subject was there all along, waiting for a channel.

## Relation to Site Perspective

This article routes to **[Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction)](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** — the commitment that consciousness causally influences the physical world, and that the mind-brain relationship runs in both directions.

Locked-in syndrome illustrates Tenet 3 by exhibiting the interface's two directions *dissociated* — and it does so without licensing any claim about where the interface sits. What the pontine lesion severs is the neural pathway carrying motor commands from brain to body; the Map does not identify that pathway with the consciousness → body leg the tenet asserts. The Map's [quantum-interface positions](/positions/quantum-interface/) individuate the interface *dynamically*, by which token outcome becomes actual at a quantum indeterminacy, and explicitly not spatially, so nothing here places either arm at a neuroanatomical site. What the case exhibits is that the two directions come apart at all: one can fail while the other, and experience itself, continue. Isolating them this cleanly is rare; most clinical cases disturb input, output, and experience together.

On the interpretive reading offered above — and it remains a reading, not a measurement — what speech neuroprostheses recover *appears to be* an intent-to-act signal, which the Map takes as the output arm's signature, present but disconnected. The step from decoded cortical activity to the mind-side leg of the interface is interpretive rather than observed, and the decoding literature gives a further reason for care: Silva and colleagues find that the sensorimotor territory used for speech decoding is shared with perceptual processing rather than dedicated to output, even though attempted speech and perception there "leveraged different neural representations with differentiable spectrotemporal responses" (Silva et al. 2025). The cortical channel is not an anatomically segregated output arm. This complements the [lived-objectified-body-distinction](/concepts/lived-objectified-body-distinction/), where locked-in syndrome figures as a severed coupling between the lived body's "I can" and the objectified body's execution — the same dissociation described in phenomenological rather than clinical terms.

The honest ledger belongs in this section too, because tenet alignment must not become tenet-confirmation by sleight of hand. Locked-in syndrome does *not* provide discriminating evidence for interactionist dualism over materialism. A production theory explains the case as fully as the Map does. What the case offers Tenet 3 is not proof but *illustration* — a clean clinical exhibit of the two-way structure the tenet asserts — together with a scope limit on the neighbouring filter model: this is where filter-loosening stops. That matches the standing the tenets page itself registers for Tenet 3 — downward causation shown to be [available rather than actual](/tenets/#tenet-3-standing) — and locked-in syndrome does nothing to convert available into actual. Presented as evidence it would be an over-claim; presented as a bounding illustration it is exactly what it is.
