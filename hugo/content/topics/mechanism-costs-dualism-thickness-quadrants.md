Naming the [mind-side × physical-side thickness axes](/topics/four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy/) sorts dualist positions by *how much* ontology each side carries. It does not yet say what each cell of the resulting grid *commits its inhabitants to* mechanistically. Two positions that share a kind-of-relation classification (both Type-D, say) can sit in different thickness cells and owe entirely different debts: a different account of mental causation, a different specification of the mind–physical interface, a different engagement with conservation laws. This article walks the four quadrants — min-mind / min-physical (Q1), min-mind / max-physical (Q2), max-mind / min-physical (Q3), max-mind / max-physical (Q4) — and reads off, for each, what the inhabitant owes on three mechanism questions. The exercise is cartographic rather than adjudicative, and "cost" here labels an inventory rather than a quantity. With no scale, zero point or aggregation rule (the grid's "thickness" and a channel's "narrowness" are ordinal at best), the article records *which* items each cell is charged, never how heavy any item is.

The taxonomy and quadrant population are stated in the [companion taxonomy article](/topics/four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy/); readers wanting the axes, the named-position inventory, or the orthogonality argument against Chalmers' Type-D/E/F classification should read that first. This article inaugurates the [mechanism-costs cartography](/project/mechanism-costs-cartography/) discipline — exposing each cell of a prior locating taxonomy to a fixed battery of mechanism questions and reading off cell-relative debts, without converting per-cell lightness on the chosen battery into endorsement.

## The Three Mechanism Questions: A Debt Inventory {#three-questions}

Every dualism owes answers to three questions about how mind and matter relate. The cells differ in which of the three they are charged, and in how each charge arises.

1. **Causation account.** How does the mind contribute to producing physical effects (or fail to)? Is mental influence overdetermining, preemptive, selective, biasing, or absent?
2. **Interface specification.** What is the mechanism of mind–physical contact? Where does it happen? What ontological structure carries the contact? How narrow is the channel?
3. **Conservation engagement.** How does the position relate to conservation laws — energy, momentum, probability? Does it owe a violation, a workaround, or nothing at all?

These three questions are not exhaustive — articulability, intelligibility under intersubstitution, and overdetermination-avoidance also matter — but they are the three where thickness shifts the debt most visibly.

The trio over-emphasises physical-side debts deliberately. The conservation-law and interface questions originate in the physicalist's challenge to interactionism, and the literature has weighted them heavily. The cell-by-cell exposition below suggests that Q1 owes all three by one of two routes, Q2 strict evades all three in exchange for an exclusion debt, Q3 dissolves all three in exchange for a different item (why physics looks physical), and Q4 is charged on all three with no deflation to fall back on. Left there, the battery would score the Map's rivals only, so two further items charge the cell the Map itself occupies ([entered below](#map-position)).

## Q1: Min-Mind / Min-Physical — The Authority Debt {#q1}

Q1 inhabitants — Bradford Saad's [delegatory-dualism](/topics/delegatory-dualism/), some readings of [interactionist-dualism](/concepts/interactionist-dualism/) confined to indeterminacy, certain neutral-monist limit cases — keep both sides as lean as the position permits.

**Causation account.** On one route, the causation owed is plausibly read as *authority* without *overdetermination*. If the physical base is counterfactually sufficient (no max-physical hidden variables), and the mental side adds no thick subliminal layers, then any genuine mental contribution must operate as something other than an extra force pushing on a complete physical story. Saad's framework deploys [trumping-preemption](/concepts/trumping-preemption/) — Schaffer's (2000) species of redundant causation in which a higher-order rule selects one of two sufficient processes as authoritative — to give experience preemptive priority over the physical state's *default causal profile* without requiring it to outpace physics. On this delegatory route the price of admission appears to be an authority law: a non-stipulative specification of *what* makes the mental rule authoritative, rather than the stipulation that does the work in Schaffer's fictional cases (Merlin's spell, military rank). The [interface-formalization-void](/voids/interface-formalization-void/) flags this articulability burden as what may be the live open problem.

Q1 holds a second route, which parts from the first on sufficiency: at a genuine quantum indeterminacy the physical cause leaves the token open, conscious selection settles it, and nothing is preempted. The two cannot be fused, because an undetermined token offers trumping no sufficient cause to reassign. The Map's [specification programme](/apex/interface-specification-programme/) withdrew its earlier fusion, takes this difference-making route, and logs trumping as a distinct alternative (see [delegatory-causation](/concepts/delegatory-causation/)).

**Interface specification.** On the difference-making route the interface is *probabilistic and narrow*: consciousness biases among quantum-permitted alternatives at neural indeterminacy sites. No new field, particle, or force. The channel-width parameter is set as small as it can be while still doing genuine causal work. This is the Map's preferred location and the [interface problem](/topics/the-interface-problem/)'s tightest formulation.

**Conservation engagement.** Q1 appears to answer the conservation question, on either route, from commitments it already holds. Because experience does not inject energy, momentum, or probability mass beyond what the wavefunction permits, the [standard conservation objection](/concepts/conservation-laws-and-mental-causation/) seems to gain little traction here: selecting or reassigning among equal-energy outcomes injects nothing, and needs no further ontological commitment on either side. Conservation charges may return if the authority law turns out to require additional structure.

Q1's items therefore differ by route: an authority law on the delegatory one, and on the difference-making one a specification of where in the brain the indeterminacy sites sit plus a control law (how phenomenal state maps to selection bias). Nothing licenses banking both routes' advantages at once. Whether such sites exist at biologically relevant scales (Tegmark 2000; see [born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface](/topics/born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface/)) bears on the cell's viability rather than on its inventory.

Q1's authority-law debt has an epistemological counterpart in [phenomenal authority's account of acquaintance](/topics/phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence/#acquaintance-and-the-dualism-connection): direct phenomenal concepts, partially constituted by phenomenal qualities, need an ontology that can carry the relation, and Q1's articulability burden is what that ontology owes at the mechanism layer. The two are the same problem at different layers.

## Q2: Min-Mind / Max-Physical — The Exclusion Debt {#q2}

Q2 inhabitants — epiphenomenalism (Chalmers' Type-E), textbook property dualism, [Stoljar's epistemic physicalism](/concepts/ignorance-hypothesis/) (a physicalist limit case, to which the exclusion debt below does not attach), Bohmian physicalism with thin qualia — keep the mental thin while letting physical ontology expand.

**Causation account.** Under the strict reading — phenomenal properties supervene on a physically complete base — the mind seems to owe *no* causation account at all, because it does no causal work on this reading. The cell's debt is therefore not a mechanism debt but what may be characterised as a *bidirectional-interaction* debt: in the Map's framework, this cell is ruled out by Tenet 3. Outside the Map's tenets, Jaegwon Kim's (2005) exclusion argument presses an analogous concern from within physicalism: if the physical base is sufficient, mental properties either overdetermine effects (which physicalists may find costly) or do nothing (which dualists could find evasive). Any Q2 reading that wants to preserve mental causation appears to drift toward Q1 by adding indeterminacy or toward Q4 by enriching the mental side.

**Interface specification.** Q2 strict has *no interface* — supervenience replaces interaction. Q2 loose (weak causal efficacy) owes one but cannot give one without sliding off the cell.

**Conservation engagement.** Q2 strict pays no conservation cost — the physical story closes without help. Q2 loose owes an account similar to Q1's but without Q1's lean physical ontology to absorb it, which is why Q2-with-causation tends to be unstable as a distinct cell.

Q2 appears to be the dialectical default for non-reductive positions in contemporary analytic philosophy of mind. Many positions classified as dualist may sit nearer this cell than Q4. The cell is charged nothing on mechanism precisely because the mind has been deflated to the point of doing no work, or so the strict reading suggests. Whether that purchase is worth its price — a phenomenal property whose having makes no difference — is the standard epiphenomenalism debate.

## Q3: Max-Mind / Min-Physical — The Appearance Debt {#q3}

Q3 inhabitants — Berkeley's idealism, Kastrup's analytic idealism, Goff's priority cosmopsychism, Myers–James subliminal-mind views paired with lean physics, certain Jungian frameworks — let the mind expand while the physical side stays as lean as is consistent with the position's commitments. Most pure Q3 positions are strictly monist; they appear here for their illumination of what an adjacent dualism would commit to.

**Causation account.** Q3 appears to dissolve the mental-causation question. If everything is mental at base, then mental–physical causation may not be a relation between two ontological kinds; it could be the intrinsic dynamics of the one kind, appearing as physical-on-physical causation when viewed extrinsically. The dualist's interaction problem evaporates on this reading; in its place sits what might be called the *[transparency problem](/concepts/lawful-appearance-problem/)* — explaining why the intrinsic mental reality looks, from the outside, exactly like impersonal lawful physical structure. Kastrup's claim that matter is the extrinsic appearance of mental processes is the canonical move (see [analytic-idealism-and-mind-centric-metaphysics](/topics/analytic-idealism-and-mind-centric-metaphysics/) for the Map's framing).

**Interface specification.** Q3 has no interface in the dualist sense — only the extrinsic/intrinsic distinction. The "where does mind contact matter" question is replaced by "why does the intrinsic side appear as matter at all?" That is a different question with a different shape and a different burden of proof; the inventory records the substitution without ranking the two.

**Conservation engagement.** Conservation laws become *descriptions of mental regularities*. Energy is conserved because the underlying mental dynamics happens to display a regularity that, when viewed extrinsically, looks like energy conservation. Why there is regularity at all, and why this regularity rather than another, is a debt every cell owes: each view takes some laws as basic, and the dualist cells add psychophysical laws to the physical ones ([lawfulness is a shared item](/concepts/lawful-appearance-problem/#lawfulness), as the Map's [idealism](/concepts/idealism/) article already concedes). What Q3 owes on its own account is narrower: why a mind's dynamics would be as austere as physics records, and, for anti-realist versions, what grounds the order among possible experiences. Q3 substitutes a metaphysical question (why does the mental ground produce this appearance?) for the dualist's physical-engagement question (how does the mental affect the physical?).

A genuine Q3 dualism — mind thick, physics thin, but both ontologically real — appears rare in the literature precisely because, once the mind does this much work, the physical side tends to lose explanatory weight and the position seems to slide toward Q3-monism. [The Q3–Q4 sliding boundary and the transparency problem](/topics/q3-q4-sliding-boundary-and-transparency-problem/) gives this observation its standalone treatment: the historical record of who slid where, the structural forces driving the slide, and the conditions (a selection-only channel doing real physical work, plus bidirectional symmetry) that might hold a thick-mind position stably at Q3-as-dualism rather than letting it shed the physical pole. Where the position holds as dualism, the debt differs in kind rather than amount: an appearance item (why the mental presents as austere, lawful physics) replaces the interface item (where mind touches matter), and the inventory has no scale on which to call either heavier.

## Q4: Max-Mind / Max-Physical — Charged on All Three {#q4}

Q4 inhabitants — Cartesian substance dualism, Aristotelian–Thomistic hylomorphism, Henry Stapp's quantum interactive dualism, Russellian panpsychism paired with full scientific realism — accept thickness on both sides. None can answer the three questions by deflation.

**Causation account.** Q4 appears to owe a *robust* mental-causation account because mind is doing thick causal work in a physical world that is itself ontologically rich. Cartesian energy-transfer is the classical formulation and the historical cost; Stapp's quantum-Zeno-effect account is a contemporary attempt to keep the channel narrow (the agent's "Process 1 choice of which probing action to take") while letting both endpoints stay thick. On the Map's reading, Stapp's framework seems to place a thick observer — a full agent with selection power — and a thick quantum ontology — full state-vector realism with potentialities treated as real — on either side of a narrow selection channel; thickness on both ends, parsimony on the contact. Whether the channel stays narrow despite the thick endpoints is contested; Tegmark's (2000) decoherence-timescale objection bears on it directly.

**Interface specification.** Q4 appears to owe a thick-to-thick interface. With both sides thick, the interface is the locus where the two rich realms touch — and the historical difficulty of specifying such a contact non-arbitrarily (Princess Elisabeth's challenge to Descartes; the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's "Dualism" entry's discussion of the interaction problem) seems to be Q4's signature burden. Hylomorphic dualism handles this by treating mind and matter as constitutive principles of one substance rather than two interacting substances — moving the interface problem from "between two things" to "internal to one." Whether that move dissolves the difficulty or merely renames it is a long-standing dispute the Map does not adjudicate here.

**Conservation engagement.** Q4 appears to owe a direct conservation engagement. With thick mind and thick physics, the mind's causal contribution may be most naturally read as an energy-bearing influence, which puts the Cartesian objection in what seems its most pointed form. Q4 inhabitants pay either by accepting that conservation is *local and symmetry-conditional* (Noether's theorem, Cucu and Pitts 2019; Pitts 2020) — and thus that interactionist intervention breaks symmetry at the interface — or by routing influence through a probabilistic channel (Stapp's quantum-Zeno mechanism) that is structurally Q1-like even when sitting in a Q4 ontology. This narrow-channel-in-a-thick-ontology pattern is what the [channel-width third axis](/topics/dualism-channel-width-axis/) isolates as its own coordinate: interface narrowness varies independently of pole thickness, so Stapp's thick endpoints and Saad's thin ones can share a channel class.

Q4 appears under-discussed in contemporary analytic philosophy, perhaps because it is charged on all three questions and parsimony norms penalise carrying every charge. Whether the charges are worth carrying turns on what each ontological commitment buys in explanatory power — a case-by-case question the [parsimony argument](/topics/parsimony-case-for-interactionist-dualism/) treats specifically for the Map's commitments.

## The Map's Cell and Its Neighbours {#map-position}

The Map's [five tenets](/tenets/) locate its commitments near Q1, with room along the mind-axis. The cell is *physical-thin* (MQI pushes physics toward leanness at the indeterminacy level) and *mind-medium* (a unified subject with minimal causal intervention, not yet committed to thick subliminal layering). Reading off the mechanism implications:

- **Causation account.** Selection / biasing of quantum-permitted outcomes on Q1's difference-making route, with room to expand toward thicker Stappian or Myersian accounts if evidence warrants; Saad's [delegatory-dualism](/topics/delegatory-dualism/) lends route-level results, not mechanism. The route carries a charge that falls on the Map's cell: with the ensemble average held at the Born weights, the bias is empirically indistinguishable from chance under any unconditioned aggregate test, though a deviation conditioned on intention, task or subject would still test it ([P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#mechanism-debt)). That held average is also the Map's own appearance item, of Q3's shape but confined to selection events, and posited rather than explained ([the lawful-appearance page](/concepts/lawful-appearance-problem/#the-maps-own-appearance-debt)).
- **Interface specification.** Narrow probabilistic channel at neural indeterminacy sites; consciousness biases the wavefunction's actualisation without injecting energy. The [interface problem](/topics/the-interface-problem/) remains the cell's open methodological burden.
- **Conservation engagement.** Selection-without-injection: biasing among equal-energy outcomes adds no energy, and in non-degenerate cases Born-frequency-preserving selection leaves the ensemble energy expectation intact—the no-violation constraint [Tenet 2](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) fixes. The Noether-symmetry response—conservation as locally symmetry-conditional, so an objector cannot presuppose it at the interface—remains a dialectical resource, but adopting local symmetry-breaking as the Map's own engagement would permit conservation failure at the interface and require revising Tenet 2. [conservation-laws-and-mental-causation](/concepts/conservation-laws-and-mental-causation/) develops both.
- **Global exclusion.** No Many Worlds moves neither thickness coordinate, yet the [taxonomy](/topics/four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy/) grants that an Everettian dualist would be locatable, so the Map's quarrel with branching rests on a posit the grid cannot deliver: a charge, entered here.

The Map's *neighbouring* cells deserve explicit comparison:

- **Drift toward Q2** would relinquish the Bidirectional Interaction tenet and resolve the interface problem trivially (no interface needed). The cost is accepting epiphenomenalism's evasion of mental work.
- **Drift toward Q4** would inflate the physical side (Russellian quiddities, Bohmian hidden variables), usually with a thicker mind beside it (a Stappian thick observer). The cost is taking on interface and conservation items the lean reading avoids, in exchange for explanatory resources it lacks.
- **Drift toward Q3** would inflate the mental side alone (Myers–James subliminal selves) while physics stays lean. Short of the limit this is still dualism, and [the sliding-boundary analysis](/topics/q3-q4-sliding-boundary-and-transparency-problem/) names what would hold it there. At the limit the physical side becomes extrinsic appearance, putting the Map adjacent to Kastrupian analytic idealism; the cost is the Dualism tenet, and Bidirectional Interaction with it, since causation becomes intra-mental.

The Map is not committed to a single cell; it is committed to a *region* of the taxonomy — physical-thin, mind-flexible, bidirectional — that excludes Q2 strict and the idealist limit of Q3.

The [somatic interface](/concepts/somatic-interface/#what-the-somatic-interface-reveals) gives the cell an embodiment-level anatomy: layered channels (interoception, proprioception, nociception, somatic markers) that shape phenomenal character and carry traffic both ways, which the narrow-channel reading takes as where consciousness engages a body. The somatic page counts its data neutral between materialist and interface readings, so the anatomy illustrates the cell rather than evidencing it.

## What the Mechanism Lens Reveals {#what-it-reveals}

Reading the thickness taxonomy through mechanism cost makes three patterns visible.

First, the conservation-law objection that has driven the analytic dualism debate since Leibniz appears *cell-specific*: an energy-transfer charge in Q4, a selection-without-injection answer in Q1, a redescription of the laws as mental regularities in Q3 on the relevant reading, and structurally inapplicable in Q2 strict. A blanket dismissal of dualism on conservation grounds may therefore treat the whole grid uniformly when the grid does not appear uniform.

Second, the interface-formalization burden varies by cell the same way: Q1 owes a narrow probabilistic interface, Q4 owes a thick-to-thick contact, Q3 dissolves the interface, Q2 strict has none. The [interface-formalization-void](/voids/interface-formalization-void/) is therefore a cell-specific debt for cells where the interface is real and lean, Q1 among them, rather than a uniform burden across all dualisms.

Third, the cell-by-cell distribution suggests why some debates do not yield productive disagreement. A defender of Q4 and a defender of Q2 strict may both call themselves dualists yet appear to face entirely non-overlapping mechanism bills. Naming the cell first lets the mechanism question be asked at the right level of granularity.

## Relation to Site Perspective

This article extends the [four-quadrant taxonomy](/topics/four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy/) from cartography of *what positions occupy* the grid to cartography of *what each cell is charged* for inhabiting it. The Map's tenets shape this reading: Tenet 1 (Dualism) and Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction) require the inhabitant to pay *some* mechanism cost (Q2 strict's evasion is closed off); Tenet 2 (Minimal Quantum Interaction) pushes that cost toward Q1's narrow probabilistic channel; Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits) authorises drift toward Q4 if the explanatory gain warrants the further items.

The mechanism lens also bears on what the [parsimony argument](/topics/parsimony-case-for-interactionist-dualism/) can claim. Earlier versions of this article read it as showing that the Map pays the *least mechanism cost compatible with the tenets*. That verdict is withdrawn on two grounds: the inventory has no scale on which unlike items could be totalled, and the verdict was reached before the Map's own cell was charged the two items entered above. What survives is narrower: parsimony, on the Map's reading, operates within the region the tenets carve out, not as a universal preference for thinness.

Nothing in this article argues that any cell is *wrong*. The cells differ in what they owe, not in whether they are defensible. A reader who does not share the Map's tenets is free to read Q2 strict's escape from the mechanism question as a virtue rather than as an evasion; a reader who shares the tenets sees the same evasion as foreclosed. The taxonomy speaks; the tenets adjudicate.
