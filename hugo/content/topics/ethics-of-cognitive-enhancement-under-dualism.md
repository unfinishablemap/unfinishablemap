Cognitive enhancement—through pharmaceuticals, brain-computer interfaces, genetic modification, or neurostimulation—is typically framed as upgrading the brain's computational capacity. Under dualism, this framing is incomplete. If consciousness is not reducible to neural processes but instead interacts with them through a [mind-matter interface](/concepts/mind-matter-interface/), then enhancement technologies modify the *interface* rather than the mind itself. That reframing changes the *description* of enhancement more than it changes the verdicts. On identity, responsibility, consent, and equity, the conclusions below are reachable by a physicalist on the same normative route: bridge principles dualism neither supplies nor needs do the work. One conclusion is genuinely dualism-specific and it is speculative—conditional on an interactionist interface existing, enhancement introduces an additional failure mode in which measured performance improves while the interface's contribution to authorship diminishes.

## Enhancement as Interface Modification

Materialist bioethics treats cognitive enhancement as upgrading mental capacity by upgrading the brain. The two canonical statements invoked below need care, because neither argues from a materialist premise; the quarrel here is with the reading built on them in practice rather than with the definitions themselves. Bostrom and Sandberg (2009) define cognitive enhancement as the amplification of core mental capacities through improvement of "internal or external information processing systems"—a substrate-neutral definition whose "external" disjunct already declines to locate the mind in the brain—while noting that the line between therapy (restoring normal function) and enhancement (exceeding it) is often difficult to discern and arguably lacks practical significance. Savulescu (2001) argues for a "principle of procreative beneficence": parents should select the child with the best chance of the best life, where the traits that count are whichever ones bear on expected wellbeing, and cognitive capacity enters as one of those rather than by way of any claim about what minds are made of. What supplies the assumption that cognitive ability *is* brain function is the standard practice of reading such frameworks as licensing straightforward substrate-improvement—and the instability of the therapy/enhancement line even on those terms suggests that axis may not be where the ethically significant joints lie.

Dualism reframes each of these interventions. If the brain functions as a filter, transceiver, or interface for consciousness—as [filter theory](/concepts/mind-matter-interface/) proposes—then enhancement technologies modify the [bandwidth](/topics/bandwidth-of-consciousness/), fidelity, or range of the channel through which consciousness operates, rather than augmenting consciousness itself. A nootropic might widen the filter, as the Map's analysis of [psychedelics and filtering](/topics/psychedelics-and-the-filter-model/) describes. A [brain-computer interface](/topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary/) might add new input-output channels. But consciousness, the subject experiencing and acting through these channels, remains what it was.

The Map's analysis of [altered-states-of-consciousness](/concepts/altered-states-of-consciousness/) fits this framing: psychedelics, meditation, and other states read as consciousness operating in distinct modes, suggesting a configurable interface—though the altered-state data alone does not discriminate that reading from production accounts. Enhancement would be another reconfiguration, deliberate and lasting rather than temporary.

Enhancement technologies are largely hypothetical at the magnitudes this article invokes, and the distance from current capability is wide. Roberts et al.'s (2020) meta-analyses of 47 studies in healthy, non-sleep-deprived adults found small overall effects—modafinil at SMD 0.12 (p = .01), methylphenidate at SMD 0.21 (p = .0004), and no effects for d-amphetamine—concluding that "there is a user perception that these drugs are effective cognitive enhancers, but this is not supported by the evidence so far." Greely et al. (2008) had argued for responsible use by the healthy on the assumption that the drugs work; the evidence since is thinner than that debate assumed. The questions below concern what enhancement *would* mean, so nothing turns on present magnitudes—but the doubled working memory invoked later is a thought experiment, not a near-term prospect.

## Which Premise Is Doing the Work

Whether the reframing changes any *verdict*, as opposed to the description, is testable directly. Holding welfare, autonomy, experiential effects, personal continuity, social pressure, and distribution constant, does adding the dualist premise change what one ought to do? On identity, responsibility, consent, and equity the answer below is no. Each of those conclusions runs on a bridge principle—indexical individuation, quality of will, open futures, sentientist standing—that physicalists hold too, and the sections say so at each point. The quantum design constraint is the one candidate divergence, and even it does not cleanly pass: the Map's own commitments make the difference unobservable by construction, since conscious selection preserves Born statistics exactly ([P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2)). What dualism supplies there is a risk hypothesis and a reason for precaution rather than a divergent verdict.

## Identity and the Enhanced Self

The most pressing ethical question cognitive enhancement raises under dualism concerns [personal-identity](/topics/personal-identity/). Irreducibility alone does not settle it: consciousness being irreducible to its physical substrate rules out identity with that substrate, but Tenet 3's bidirectional interaction means physical interventions genuinely alter mental life, so nothing in irreducibility by itself guarantees the subject immunity from radical enhancement. The claim that the fundamental subject survives rests on a further commitment the Map adopts explicitly: that *which* subject one is—indexical identity—is individuated independently of the interface's configuration. Given that premise, the person who receives a hypothetical neural implant doubling their working memory capacity is the same conscious subject afterward—experiencing the world through a modified interface, but not replaced or fundamentally altered.

Against materialist views where identity reduces to psychological continuity or brain-state patterns, that premise secures a continuity radical enhancement cannot break: [indexical identity](/topics/personal-identity/)—the irreducible fact of being *this* particular conscious subject—persists through interface modifications because it was never constituted by the interface in the first place. Two limits sit on the advantage. A physicalist animalist reaches the same verdict from the persistence of the organism, so the comparison marks a difference in route rather than in outcome. And the Map's register books a debt: it has no subject-pairing law fixing which physical system a subject is paired to, or when a pairing begins and ends ([P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2)), so persistence through radical intervention is asserted conditionally rather than derived. Numerical persistence also delivers no reassurance by itself. That the same subject is on the far side of the intervention is a metaphysical fact; that this is what matters prudentially is a further value premise, not a corollary.

The *character* of conscious experience may also shift dramatically while the subject persists, since an interface that filters differently produces different experiential content. Someone whose enhanced brain processes information far faster may find continuity of selfhood, secure though the Map's interface-independence commitment makes it, phenomenologically strained. You are still you, but the you-ness feels alien. Sandel (2007) articulates a related concern from a different angle: enhancement threatens the "gifted" character of human capacities, replacing openness to the unbidden with mastery and control. His argument is communitarian, running on humility, solidarity, and what a society owes those whose talents fall out differently, and it requires no view about what minds are made of. The interface redescription that follows is the Map's extension of Sandel rather than a commitment of his: on that extension the giftedness attaches to the contingent interface through which consciousness operates rather than to consciousness itself. The relocation leaves his worry standing, since the solidarity he is protecting was never grounded in a metaphysics of mind and is not disturbed by changing one.

## Moral Responsibility Under Enhancement

[Libertarian free will](/topics/free-will/) grounded in agent causation—the position this Map defends—meets enhancement at a specific point: if consciousness exercises causal power through quantum-level interactions at the [mind-matter-interface](/concepts/mind-matter-interface/), modifying that interface changes the landscape within which free choices operate.

Consider a pharmaceutical that eliminates impulsive aggression by altering neurotransmitter dynamics. Under dualism, this need not remove the agent's capacity for free choice: consciousness still selects among quantum possibilities at the interface—though, as the quantum-interface section below develops, interventions in neural microstructure could in principle narrow the domain where that capacity operates. What has clearly changed is the *menu of readily available actions*: the impulse consciousness might have chosen to override—or to indulge—is no longer present. Has [moral-responsibility](/concepts/moral-responsibility/) shifted?

Two positions emerge:

**Interface neutrality**: The moral status of a choice depends on the conscious agent's exercise of causal power, not on the interface configuration. Just as a good upbringing does not make someone less responsible for good behaviour, enhancement does not make an agent less responsible for improved conduct. The interface provides options; consciousness chooses.

**Interface dependence**: The moral texture of a choice partly depends on what it costs the agent. Choosing kindness when aggression is a live temptation is morally different from choosing kindness when the temptation has been pharmacologically removed. Enhancement that narrows the choice space may produce better outcomes while diminishing the moral significance of choosing them.

The second position is not new here: it is Harris's (2011) freedom-to-fall objection to moral enhancement, on which interventions targeting specific moral capacities would compromise the freedom that makes right action creditable. Savulescu and Persson (2012) reply with the God Machine—a system that monitors intentions and intervenes only against grave harm—arguing that the freedom so removed is not freedom worth having. The interface framing redescribes the disputed quantity as belonging to the interface rather than the subject; it does not adjudicate.

The Map's commitment to [agent causation](/topics/free-will/) suggests a position between these poles: if consciousness is causal at the interface, moral agency persists across configurations, while the *difficulty* and *meaning* of particular choices shift with the interface, changing how specific acts are evaluated without unseating responsibility itself. This parallels the Map's analysis of [moral agency under duress](/topics/consciousness-and-moral-agency-under-duress/), where extreme pressure constrains the menu of live options without eliminating agency. Neither pole nor the resolution belongs to dualism: both run in the vocabulary of quality of will, where compatibilists have debated the same question for decades, and the register agrees—[P-A5](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a5) holds that the libertarian framing does no unique explanatory work here.

## The Consent Problem

If a developing brain is enhanced in utero or during childhood—through genetic modification, pharmaceutical intervention, or early neural implants—the conscious subject who will live with that modified interface had no say in its configuration.

This concern exists under any metaphysics, and dualism changes the image rather than the stakes. If consciousness uses the brain as an interface, prenatal enhancement is like building a custom instrument for a musician not yet consulted about what music they want to play: the interface shapes what consciousness can readily express, so choosing it for another is choosing the parameters of their experiential life. But a physicalist who holds that brain modification configures the mind *constitutively* has at least as strong a ground for the same worry, and arguably a stronger one, since on that view the intervention shapes the subject rather than the subject's instrument.

The counterargument is that all parents already make this choice through genetics, environment, nutrition, and education. Enhancement merely makes the choice more deliberate. Under dualism the difference is one of description rather than stakes: the intervention configures the channel through which an irreducible subject will engage with reality rather than composing the subject itself, and whether that redescription carries extra normative weight needs a bridge principle the Map has not supplied. What does add specificity is timing. The Map's analysis of [childhood interface development](/topics/consciousness-interface-development/) notes that synaptic pruning during critical periods forecloses perceptual territories durably and region-specifically—a high barrier rather than a sealed door. Enhancement inside those windows would interact with natural development in ways that could durably reshape which territories consciousness can access, rather than only adding capacity.

## Enhancement and the Quantum Interface

The Map's commitment to [minimal quantum interaction](/concepts/mind-matter-interface/) introduces a speculative concern. If consciousness influences physical outcomes through quantum-level effects—by whatever mechanism obtains—then technologies that modify neural microstructure might inadvertently alter the quantum interface itself.

Consider a neural implant that introduces artificial synaptic connections. If consciousness acts at specific biological sites, the implant's pathways might bypass those sites entirely, so that the enhanced individual processes information faster and more accurately while having *less* conscious control over the processing—computational capacity up, domain of free action down. The Map's analysis of [BCIs and the interface boundary](/topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary/) bears on this: BCI adaptation works by repurposing existing neural circuits rather than extending consciousness to new hardware, which rules out the rival hypothesis that consciousness extends into the machine. That evidence does not discriminate the interface reading from a no-interface physicalist one, though—as the BCI article concedes, a physicalist predicts the intrinsic manifold, the overnight consolidation, and the limits on plasticity exactly as the Map does. The bypass argument also asks for more than the register licenses: [the quantum-interface positions](/positions/quantum-interface/) concern Born-rule treatment and token selection at quantum indeterminacies rather than spatially localised neuroanatomical sites, so "the sites an implant could bypass" imports a locality assumption the Map has not registered.

What follows is a conditional risk hypothesis rather than a practical imperative, because the argument inherits the Map's [mechanism debt](/positions/quantum-interface/#mechanism-debt)—the bias-without-deviation dilemma ([P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3)) and the absence of any worked toy model ([P-Q10](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q10))—under which the causal-selection thesis is citable downstream as a framework-internal coherence result, never as established mental causation. Conditional on the mechanism obtaining, enhancement that improved performance while narrowing conscious agency would be a net loss; that verdict imports the premise that intrinsic value resides in phenomenal experience ([P-VS3](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs3)), stated here rather than assumed. The [phenomenology of creative insight](/concepts/creative-consciousness/#the-phenomenology-of-creative-insight) illustrates the risk: the Map *reads* the involuntary gestalt shift at the core of creative discovery as suggesting consciousness operating through specific biological channels, a reading the creativity article marks as a further claim rather than a finding. On it, enhancement that accelerated analysis while eliminating the conditions for insight would diminish the creative mind.

## Equity and Access

Unequal access to cognitive enhancement reads differently under dualism. The strongest form of the argument assumes that all conscious subjects possess the same fundamental capacity for experience and agency, differing only in the interfaces through which they operate—but that universal premise is stronger than the Map can defend. [Phenomenal variation within our species](/apex/phenomenal-variation-within-a-species/) is documented and structural—aphantasia, synaesthesia—and the equality premise can absorb it only by booking all such variation to the interface, an accounting move that should be made explicitly rather than assumed. The defensible claim is conditional: to whatever extent observed cognitive inequality is interface-side, unequal access to enhancement is unequal instrumentation for subjects whose fundamental standing may be equal—and radical inequality in interface quality becomes correspondingly harder to justify. The equal-standing half of that conditional is carried by the moral-status register, where capacity for valenced experience grounds standing ([P-MS1](/positions/moral-status/#p-ms1)), and not by dualism: a sentientist reaches it without any metaphysics of mind.

Materialist frameworks can appeal to natural variation: some brains simply work better, and enhancement extends that variation. Under dualism the asymmetry between subjects and their unequal interfaces is more visible. [The moral architecture of consciousness](/apex/moral-architecture-of-consciousness/) grounds the concern: if consciousness grounds value, agency, normativity, and moral perception as one structure, unequal interface quality constrains the very capacities through which moral life is lived, not just its outcomes—so enhancement that widens the gap without addressing access leaves subjects of equal standing with increasingly unequal conditions for agency.

### Four Rival Justice Traditions

What precedes is a concern rather than a theory of justice, and this article runs on none of the four traditions that would supply one. Each reaches determinate guidance the interface premise does not, which extends the verdict of "Which Premise Is Doing the Work" above rather than qualifying it.

**Welfarism** asks which intervention maximises expected lifetime wellbeing, counting adverse effects, adaptation, social coercion, and effects on others. Savulescu is cited above but not answered, and nothing in the interface redescription touches that calculation. Levin (2023) presses the tradition from the opposite flank: welfarism about disability, once applied to procreative choice, turns out to be staunchly objectivist about what decision-makers are obliged to do, giving "no credence to first-personal values, aspirations, and experiences"—so it collides with disability advocacy rather than sharing its commitments, as its proponents claim.

**Capability theory** asks what people are substantively able to do and be, given the social and environmental conditions that convert resources into functioning. Jecker and Ko (2022) build two tests for brain-computer interfaces on that basis—a threshold test for restoring participation, a flourishing test for interventions reaching beyond it—and extract more operational guidance from them than the equality claim above supplies. The approach can favour an implant restoring communication to someone who has none while opposing one that raises test performance and narrows social or political agency. The interface premise does not discriminate those cases.

**Disability rights** is the tradition the Map engages least. The social model of disability and ableism appear nowhere else across the corpus, and neurodivergent cognition appears only as a device for triangulating the limits of shared experience rather than as an ethical position. Chaproniere (2022) argues that enhancement need not conflict with disability justice provided accessible environments and genuine choice over whether to use any enhancement at all, an argument assembled entirely from disability-rights resources and requiring no metaphysics of mind. The tradition also charges this article directly. Describing subjects as metaphysically equal while operating instruments of unequal quality preserves the equality and delivers a ranking of embodied lives along with it; the "interface quality" vocabulary carries that expressive cost, and dualism does not offset it.

**Distributive justice** asks which principle governs access—strict equality, fair equality of opportunity, priority, sufficiency, relational equality—and "access should not widen the gap" selects none of them. Nor can the article help itself to the convergence thesis on which prioritarians, sufficientarians, and egalitarians all end up limiting unequal distribution of positional goods: Cass (2023) examines that thesis and finds it faces problems for education and wealth, rescuing only a variant keyed to a person's standing as a citizen. Equal access is insufficient on its own besides, since a universally available enhancement can still compel everyone to take it.

## What Would Challenge This View?

The dualist reframing of enhancement ethics would be undermined by:

1. **A successful materialist account of consciousness**: If neuroscience demonstrated that consciousness is fully constituted by neural activity, the interface metaphor would collapse and enhancement really would upgrade the mind rather than a channel.

2. **Enhancement without experiential change**: The interface model predicts phenomenal change under enhancement. Dramatic performance gains with *no* change in phenomenal character would suggest the enhanced processing runs outside the conscious channel entirely—consistent with the interface having boundaries, but pressing on the claim that consciousness is causally efficacious in the enhanced cognition (Tenet 3).

3. **Identity disruption through enhancement**: Consistent reports of being a *different* subject would be evidence of phenomenological or narrative discontinuity rather than of numerical replacement, which self-report cannot reach. What it would challenge is the article's use of indexical persistence as reassurance.

4. **Quantum interface irrelevance**: If consciousness-brain interaction proved entirely classical, the bypass concern would be moot, though the broader interface ethics would survive.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The Map's dualist commitments bear on enhancement ethics as follows:

**Tenet 1 (Dualism)** grounds the central reframing: enhancement modifies an interface rather than consciousness itself. It does not by itself preserve personal identity through radical enhancement, as the identity section above sets out—that conclusion runs on the separate commitment to interface-independent indexical individuation ([P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1)) and remains subject to the unpaid pairing debt at [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2).

**Tenet 2 (Minimal Quantum Interaction)** introduces the concern that enhancement technologies might inadvertently compromise the quantum interface through which consciousness exercises causal influence. As registered, the tenet concerns Born-rule treatment and token selection at quantum indeterminacies rather than localised anatomical sites, so the design constraint sits on top of the register rather than following from it.

**Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction)** is what makes enhanced agency possible rather than what guarantees it: *if* agent causation obtains, and *if* an intervention leaves its enabling conditions intact, consciousness continues to influence the physical world through the modified interface. The register declines the stronger reading—[P-A5](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a5) holds that the Map's libertarian framing does no unique explanatory work over sophisticated compatibilism at the level of moral practice, with the residue confined to ultimate desert and the metaphysics of genuine alternatives.

**Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds)** means that the enhanced individual is a single, indexically determinate conscious subject—not a branching entity spread across quantum worlds. The point is not that many-worlds would make enhancement decisions lighter: Everettian branching occurs over quantum events rather than deliberations, and Deutsch–Wallace decision theory derives the standard decision weights as a representation theorem (Wallace 2012, "Symmetry, Rationality, and the Born Rule"). That derivation carries less weight than it is usually reported to: its rationality constraints operate inside pure wave mechanics and read better as auxiliary assumptions than as principles any rational agent must accept. The point is indexical: under Tenet 4 there is a unique fact about which experiential trajectory is yours, so an enhancement decision is a wager with a single resolution rather than a portfolio of outcomes across branches—a difference in the metaphysics of the wager, which Everettians dispute has any decision-theoretic consequence.

**Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits)** cautions against assuming that materialist bioethics captures the full ethical landscape: the simpler framework—enhance the brain, enhance the mind—may miss dimensions that only become visible when consciousness is understood as irreducible to its physical substrate. The tenet binds both ways. It licenses no preference for the filter reading over a production reading, and none for the quantum design constraint over its absence; bounded parsimony withholds a verdict rather than supplying one for the more complex account.
