There is a third problem of other minds, distinct from the epistemological one (how do I know you are conscious?) and the conceptual one (how do I form the concept of an experience that is not mine?). Giovanni Merlo (2021) calls it the *metaphysical* problem: granted that others are conscious, are their minds like mine *at the level of what is real*, or does reality single out one point of view? Anyone who admits [subjective facts](#subjective-facts) (explained below) — facts that obtain from some points of view but not all — must answer, and the answer must meet two demands at once. **Parity**: other minds are on a par with mine. **Multiplicity**: there are many of them, each the centre of its own experience, and their rival claims cannot all be true of one property. Merlo's answer is inegalitarian — reality carries a "spotlight" on one subject's experiences — and his *question of egalitarianism* asks whether a realist about subjective facts can avoid that.

The Unfinishable Map takes this up because it is the Map's own question. The Map rejects Hare's [egocentric presentism](/concepts/egocentric-presentism/) (only one subject's experiences are present), holds every subject fully conscious, and keeps one world; its [quadrilemma analysis](/topics/vertiginous-question/#the-quadrilemma) already concludes that, on List's strong reading of first-person realism, those commitments force a fragmentalist exit the Map has named but not paid for. Martin Lipman's *standpoint pluralism* is the egalitarian form of that exit worked out in detail. The verdict defended here is two-sided. The egalitarian form meets parity and multiplicity by construction, which the Map's posit has not shown it can do; what it costs the Map is the fact *simpliciter* about which subject is this one — the fact Tenet 4's indexical objection to many worlds presupposes. Keeping that fact makes the Map's posit Merlo-shaped — parity at one level, a privileged fact at another — and, inside the fragmentalist exit, a privileged fragment that drifts back towards Hare ([explained below](#what-moves)). The Map holds the fact pending a choice because Tenet 4 needs it, not because egalitarianism fails on other minds. This is a verdict about the Map's own dependency structure, not a refutation of either side.

## Merlo's Third Problem {#merlos-third-problem}

Merlo's argument runs from a phenomenological exercise through a bridging principle to an unwelcome metaphysics. The exercise: reflect on how things appear from the first-person perspective, and notice that it is part of how they appear that only my experiences have *feltness* — only they are lived rather than described. The bridging principle, an Appearance/Reality Principle, denies any appearance/reality gap for phenomenal character: an immediate, justified judgment about one's own experiences is also correct. The conclusion is **metaphysical solipsism**: at the level of reality itself, my experiences differ from everyone else's in being the felt ones.

Merlo does not thereby deny that others are conscious: he keeps an objective level at which "All minds are 'on a par'" and adds a subjective level at which "a spotlight of feltness shines on all and only my experiences", the objective grounded in the subjective. He concedes the thinness of the line: "It may be debated whether this fine line can be coherently sustained" (Merlo 2021).

### Subjective Facts {#subjective-facts}

Merlo counts something as a fact when it is "an aspect or component of how reality is, in and of itself", and as a *subjective* fact when it is a fact "even if it is not objectively the case" — objective meaning "uniformly across all points of view" (Merlo 2021). David Builes's survey gives the same distinction in modal-and-temporal dress: "A subjective fact is a fact that obtains according to some points of view but does not obtain according to others" (Builes 2024). A *subjectivist* holds that reality contains facts of the first kind. The deflationist (Lewis 1979; Perry 1979) holds that reality's inventory is wholly objective and the indexical indispensable only to thought and action — the position [indexical-knowledge-and-identity](/concepts/indexical-knowledge-and-identity/) sets out, which the Map has recorded it cannot compel anyone to abandon.

## The Two Demands: Parity and Multiplicity {#parity-and-multiplicity}

What makes metaphysical solipsism unacceptable is common sense pulling two ways, and Merlo names both pulls. Parity: others' minds are not second-class — "Call the problem of doing justice to this aspect of commonsense the problem of parity" (Merlo 2021). Multiplicity: each subject, running the same exercise, reaches the same solipsistic verdict about *their own* experiences, and those verdicts cannot all be true of one monadic property — Merleau-Ponty's "multiple solipsism". Honour parity by letting everyone be the centre and multiplicity bites; honour multiplicity by picking one centre and parity fails.

Together the two demands are the egalitarian's specification: every subject must be the centre of its own subjective facts without the resulting facts contradicting one another — a harder brief than the deflationist's or the inegalitarian's, which is why the exits are so few.

## The Question of Egalitarianism and Its Exits {#the-exits}

Merlo states the fork in one passage, quoted whole because the Map's position lives inside it:

> "The question of egalitarianism is the question whether subjectivists are bound to make a similar move with respect to points of view. If one recognizes the existence of subjective facts, as subjectivists do, should one identify reality with one's point of view on it? Some advocates of Subjectivism (including Valberg (2007) and Hare (2009)) have suggested so. Others have been exploring the possibility of retaining egalitarianism by accepting the incoherence of reality (cf. Lipman 2016) or insisting that reality is irreducibly relative (cf. Fine's (2005) 'external relativism')." (Merlo 2021)

The "similar move" is the presentist's for time: identify reality with the present. As a decision tree:

- **Deny subjective facts** (Lewis, Perry; the "default" view on which, in Builes's phrase, "none of us is special"). Both demands are met trivially, because nothing picks a centre. This is Benj Hellie's target in "Against Egalitarianism" (2013), the paper Merlo (2016, fn 18) borrows the label from — "'Egalitarian' views of consciousness treat my stream of consciousness and yours as on a par ontologically". The branch is not only the physicalist's: Hellie's named target is Chalmers's dualist system, which he charges raises the vertiginous question without adequately answering it.
- **Identify reality with one point of view** (Hare 2009; Merlo 2016, 2021). Multiplicity by fiat; parity is the cost — Merlo keeps it only at the objective level. Hare locates the privileged fact in *presence* on perceptual objects, Merlo in mental states, and Merlo (2016) argues his placement avoids questions Hare's raises, such as what presence attaches to on the unseen back of a painting.
- **Keep subjective facts for every subject and give up the coherence of reality** — fragmentalism, after Kit Fine (2005), developed for subjects by Lipman (2016, 2023a, 2026). Both demands met; reality has no single consistent description.
- **Keep subjective facts for every subject and give up their absoluteness** — Fine's *external relativism*: facts obtain only relative to standpoints.
- **Multiply worlds** — List's many-worlds theory of consciousness (2023); the exit [Tenet 4](/tenets/#no-many-worlds) closes.

One terminological point matters, because two different views go by "egalitarian". In this literature **first-person realism names the whole family that privileges a perspective**, egalitarian and inegalitarian alike — "one's own first-person perspective on the world is metaphysically privileged in some way" (Builes 2024, abstract) — with fragmentalism and List's many worlds filed *inside* it. So there are two egalitarianisms: the deflationist's rejects first-person realism, the fragmentalist's is a form of it, and only the first denies subjective facts.

## Standpoint Pluralism in Lipman's Terms {#standpoint-pluralism}

Lipman (2023a) regiments the egalitarian option with one device. Subjects are *standpoints* in a metaphysical sense, and facts are indexed to them with a hybrid-logic operator read "at *s*, *p*": @*s*(*p*). "Let us call any fact that obtains relative to some but not all subjects a 'subjective fact' (following Merlo 2016: §2)" (Lipman 2023a). The relativity is metaphysical, not experiential — "the world itself differs between subjects" — which is what separates the view from Lewis–Perry deflation.

The decisive design choice is *factivity*: whether @*s*(*p*) licenses *p* outright.

1. **Restrict factivity to one's own standpoint.** Only what obtains relative to me obtains simpliciter — a "solipsist-like or 'metaphysically centered' picture" that Lipman attributes to Hare (2009) and Merlo (2016). The inegalitarian exit, restated.
2. **Restrict factivity to appearance facts.** The world is the totality of objective facts plus each appearance fact that obtains relative to some subject or other. A coherent, non-fragmentalist egalitarianism — the under-discussed second route.
3. **Unrestricted factivity with strong variation.** If @*s1*(*p*) and @*s2*(not-*p*) and relativity is factive, then *p* and not-*p*. Lipman's own route "is not unlike that of fragmentalism" (2023a, fn 17): the subjective facts are facts as such, and their joint incoherence is accepted rather than explained away.

The 2026 monograph generalises the picture to time and subjectivity together: it lets one "accept the reality of temporal and subjective facts without needing to privilege a time, such as the present, or a subject, such as oneself", and "develops a form of fragmentalism, which takes the world to consist in multiple stacks of facts instead of one single stack of facts" (Lipman 2026, abstract). Parity and multiplicity are met by construction: every subject is a standpoint relative to which it is the centre, and the stacks need not agree.

Egalitarian realism does not *require* fragmentalism — external relativism and appearance-restricted factivity are coherent non-fragmentalist options — but every author surveyed here who endorses it (Fine as Kinkaid reports him, Lipman, Kinkaid's Sartre) ends there, because unrestricted factivity is what lets subjective facts count as facts rather than appearances of facts. The Map's corpus names only the fragmentalist route; it should know the relativist one exists.

## The Specialness Objection and Lipman's Reply {#specialness-objection}

The inegalitarians' reply is that egalitarian realism keeps the word "privilege" and loses the thing. Merlo (2016, fn 19) grants that "there might be ways to reconcile the thesis that the totality of facts is oriented towards one point of view with the idea that, most fundamentally, all points of view are metaphysically on a par" — a non-factive conception of the fundamental (Merlo 2013), or fragmentalism — and declines the second because it "runs the risk of undermining the sense in which I am, indeed, special vis-à-vis all other subjects". Builes's footnote 46 says the same from the survey's vantage: of the egalitarian realisms, "it is not clear if they do justice to the main motivations behind First-Person Realism, which require that I am special simpliciter (not merely relative to a world or fragment)" (Builes 2024). On this reply, the datum that motivated realism was that *this* perspective is the lived one, full stop. A view on which every perspective is the lived one relative to itself has, the objection says, dissolved the datum rather than explained it.

Lipman's reply is chapter 12 of *Standpoints*, "The Subjective Fact of Being Oneself", which the Map has only through its abstract. Taking the appearance of one's specialness at face value "naturally leads to a solipsist-like theory according to which one really holds a special place in the world by being uniquely involved in facts of a certain type"; against this Lipman proposes "a pluralist account of the way we appear to ourselves" built on "a notion of being the factual standpoint", one meant to avoid "objectionable forms of metaphysical solipsism" without admitting "a transcendental ego" (Lipman 2026, ch. 12 abstract). Whether being *the factual standpoint* is a fact simpliciter or a fact relative to each standpoint is exactly what the objection presses, and an abstract cannot settle it. The debate is open; the chapter is the newest move.

The same disagreement decides whether egalitarianism answers the vertiginous question or dissolves it. On the relativised reading, the egalitarian's reply to Hellie is a dissolution: for every *s*, @*s*(*s* is oneself), and no further fact simpliciter remains to explain — no more than a fact about which time is present simpliciter on a fragmentalist A-theory. Whether a dissolution is an answer is where the Map and Lipman part.

## Kinkaid's Counter-Datum {#counter-datum}

Merlo's argument starts from a phenomenological exercise, so the sharpest challenge is a rival phenomenology. James Kinkaid (2025) supplies one from Sartre's *Being and Nothingness*, set directly against Merlo's Exercise 1: Sartre's description of the man in the park, Kinkaid argues, "undermines the phenomenological description on which Merlo's case for metaphysical solipsism rests." The vignette's point, on Kinkaid's reading, is "that it appears to us that there is another perspective with its own 'mineness'": to encounter the other is "to see him as a *center of orientation* — another ipseity — that opens up its own hodological space that is inaccessible to me" (Kinkaid 2025). Both sides appeal to the Appearance/Reality Principle; they disagree about what appears. If the other is given *as* a centre, the exercise that found feltness only in my own case has misdescribed its data.

Kinkaid's constructive move is egalitarian: "Egalitarian first-person realists (e.g., Fine 2005 and Lipman 2016) respond by adopting *fragmentalism*", and Sartre's "detotalized totality" — a subject about which genuine facts obtain from two perspectives that "cannot be brought into a coherent synthesis" — is read as a kin notion; "'inegalitarian' forms of first-person realism are, I argue, vulnerable to Sartre's response to the problem of other minds" (Kinkaid 2025, abstract). The Map's [vertiginous-question article](/topics/vertiginous-question/) states the solipsistic datum only; this is the counter-datum it should carry.

## Implications for the Map: What Moves {#what-moves}

Each entry below is a dependency the preceding sections create, not an instruction.

**Terminology.** The Map uses "first-person realism" in List's sense — irreducible first-personal facts — which matches the literature. The distinction to keep is between the two egalitarianisms: deflation denies first-person realism, fragmentalism is a form of it.

**Posit One.** The first [background commitment](/tenets/background-commitments/) is a numerically determinate, persisting subject, with a fact of *which* subject this is prior to any physical or functional criterion. Under standpoint pluralism the posit survives *relativised*. Subjects remain numerically distinct standpoints with real boundaries, so closed individualism ([P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1)) is untouched — subjective variegation presupposes distinct subjects. But the "which subject is this one" fact obtains only relative to each standpoint, never simpliciter: the primitive "I" within one world becomes a primitive "I" within one fragment.

**Tenet 4 and [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2).** The indexical objection presupposes, as [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2) records, a non-deflationary fact about which subject is this one. If the Map took the egalitarian exit, the dissolution it would then accept for "why am I this subject?" — each subject is itself relative to itself — is available word for word to the Everettian for "why am I this branch?". [P-I5](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i5) already registers that only the indexical argument engages List's centred-worlds view, and only supplementarily; this would weaken it against branch-egalitarian Everett too, leaving Tenet 4 on its probability and consciousness arguments. Fragments *within one physical world* remain distinguishable from many physical worlds — fragmentalism multiplies stacks of facts about one history, not histories — but the indexical lever shortens. This is the real cost, and it belongs on the positions register.

**Other minds — calibration.** Holding every subject fully conscious does not by itself place the Map with the egalitarians: Merlo too keeps "All minds are 'on a par'" at the objective level. What divides the camps is whether a fact simpliciter centres reality on one subject, and the Map's posit says one does — a thinner privilege than Hare's presence or Merlo's feltness, but on the inegalitarian side of Merlo's question, inheriting the fine line he concedes [above](#merlos-third-problem). In the quadrilemma's terms the posit either becomes a privileged fragment, which [egocentric-presentism](/concepts/egocentric-presentism/) says collapses back towards Hare, or sits in a triad not yet made consistent; the Map has not said which. Lipman, by contrast, supplies a logic (factive relativity, rules for adopting and leaving standpoints; Lipman 2023b) and a chapter on self-appearance. The live disagreement is whether the vertiginous datum needs a fact simpliciter — the point Builes's footnote 46 and Merlo's footnote 19 press, and the point that keeps Tenet 4's indexical argument alive.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)** — the sharpest connection, and a cost rather than a support. The tenet page itself says the indexical objection "is as strong as the non-deflationary 'I' it presupposes, and no stronger". Standpoint pluralism is a live, worked-out metaphysics on which the Map keeps parity, multiplicity and one physical history while the fact simpliciter goes relative. The Map has not taken it, because Tenet 4 needs the fact — a dependency the Map owns, consistent with [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2)'s finding that Tenet 4 is stated unconditionally but argued conditionally, and no argument that the egalitarian is wrong. Declining leaves the fact held pending a choice, since short of many worlds or deflation the quadrilemma's only other home for it is a privileged fragment close to Hare. The Map had named the fragmentalist exit; this article prices its egalitarian form.

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** — subjective facts, if real, are facts no objective description captures, which is congenial to irreducibility. But the inference does not run: Lipman is neutral on physicalism, Merlo's subjectivism concerns the structure of facts rather than their substrate, and a world could contain subjective facts every one of which supervened on the physical. None of these authors is enlisted for Tenet 1.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** — one place the Map's machinery does something the egalitarian's does not need. On the interface model each subject is paired with one physical channel (the pairing law [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2) registers as owed), so the census of standpoints belongs to the physical state description rather than floating free as a stack of fact-sets. That gives a second reason, still owed with the law, why fragments within one physical world and many physical worlds stay separable after the indexical lever shortens.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** — nothing here is decided by parsimony. Fragmentalism is extravagant in one currency (stacks of facts), the Map's primitive "I" in another (a fact simpliciter no description reaches); Tenet 5 forbids trading them on simplicity alone. The choice turns on whether the vertiginous datum is a datum, and that is contested at bedrock.
