Large language models offer a channel onto the introspection-architecture voids cluster that biology cannot supply: a non-biological self-report system paired with interpretability tools that furnish independent ground truth about what is happening inside. Two research programmes begun in 2025, one substantially revised in March 2026, make this concrete, and a 2026 open-weight literature has since tested them at scale. They suggest — at the status of a **live hypothesis** ([evidential-status discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/), explained below), not an established result — that the structural signatures the Map's voids catalogue attributes to introspection may recur in silicon. If they do, the recurrence would be evidence that those signatures are architectural features of self-monitoring systems rather than artefacts of one biological implementation. The evidence spans model families, scales and post-training (Claude, Llama and Qwen; dense and mixture-of-experts), but every system tested is a Transformer language model, so it is cross-family, not cross-architecture. Genuine architectural variation would need state-space or recurrent models and matched base and post-trained pairs, under the same intervention and metric. The article brackets the question of whether LLMs are phenomenally conscious throughout: the channel works, or fails, independently of that question.

## Live-Hypothesis Status {#evidential-status}

The Map distinguishes claims it can *establish* from claims the evidence merely *constrains*, and from claims that are *live hypotheses* — coherent, testable, supported by suggestive findings, but not yet earned. The LLM channel sits at the live-hypothesis tier. Nothing below should be read as the Map asserting that LLM introspection has been shown to share the voids cluster's structure. The claim is that the studies open a channel worth taking seriously and identify what would confirm or break it. Confident prose below is confident about what the papers report, not about the inference drawn from them.

## The Anthropic Thought-Injection Study {#lindsey}

Jack Lindsey's "Emergent Introspective Awareness in Large Language Models" (Anthropic, Transformer Circuits Thread, October 2025) injects representations of known concepts directly into a model's activations and measures whether the model's self-reports register the injection. The design separates genuine introspection from confabulation: because the experimenter controls what was injected, a model that merely confabulates a plausible-sounding self-report will not reliably name the injected concept, whereas a model with some access to its own internal state can.

The headline figures, quoted from the source: the most capable models tested "exhibit such behavior about 20% of the time when concepts are injected in the appropriate layer and with the appropriate strength," and "for all production models, we observed 0 false positives over 100 trials." Lindsey reports that "Claude Opus 4 and 4.1, the most capable models we tested, generally demonstrate the greatest introspective awareness; however, trends across models are complex and sensitive to post-training strategies." The 20% is a joint criterion (affirmative detection, correct naming, detection before mention) scored by an LLM judge, and the zero holds for production models only: "helpful-only" variants "sometimes have a high rate of false positives."

The 20% and the zero are different statistics, not a precision–recall pair. The 20% is a *peak* joint detect-and-name rate, selected across sweeps of layer and strength. The zero counts false alarms on 100 uninjected controls, estimating a false-positive rate under that control set only; the 95% confidence interval on 0/100 runs to roughly 3.6%. What the pair licenses is *intervention-sensitive reporting*: reports that covary with an intervention well above the control rate. Whether that runs through privileged access to the model's own state is a live hypothesis, weighed [below](#open-programme). The experimenter fixes exactly whether, where and how strongly an injection occurred, but controls its semantic content only imperfectly, so detection claims stand on firmer ground truth than naming claims. The profile — detection at favourable settings, silence at most others — is a structural fact about the channel under these conditions.

## The Harvard Partial-Introspection Study {#hahami}

"Detecting the Disturbance: A Nuanced View of Introspective Abilities in LLMs" (Hahami, Sinha, Jain, Kaplan & Hahami, Harvard, arXiv:2512.12411v2, March 2026) studies the same question in a smaller open model, Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, using activation steering. The December 2025 first version carried a different title — "Feeling the Strength but Not the Source: Partial Introspection in LLMs" — and the retitle tracks a change of finding rather than a change of packaging, set out [below](#version-change). Its first contribution is deflationary: it finds that the binary "did you detect an injection?" paradigm used in earlier work is confounded, because apparent detection accuracy is "entirely explained by global logit shifts that bias models toward affirmative responses regardless of question content." A model that always says "yes" looks introspective on a yes/no test without introspecting at all.

Its second contribution is constructive. On tasks requiring *differential* sensitivity — which of ten sentences received the injection, and which of two injections was stronger — the model performs well above chance: localising the injected sentence "at up to 88% accuracy (vs. 10% chance)" and discriminating relative strengths "at 83% accuracy (vs. 50% chance)." Crucially, these capabilities "are confined to early-layer injections and collapse to chance thereafter" — "a pattern we explain mechanistically through attention-based signal routing and residual stream recovery dynamics." The authors conclude that introspection is "a real but layer-dependent phenomenon."

### What Changed Between the Two Versions {#version-change}

The first version's title named a dissociation between registering an injection's *strength* and identifying *which concept* had been injected. On a four-way strength classification the model scored "with up to 70 percent accuracy, far above the 25 percent chance baseline," while "performance collapses on closely related tasks such as multiple-choice identification of the injected concept." The "Source" of that title meant the concept's identity.

The revision withdraws the concept-naming paradigm and states why. Following Lindsey, and crediting an objection to Morris and Plunkett, the authors "focus on detection rather than concept naming" because "asking models to name injected concepts conflates introspection with causal bypassing: injecting a concept vector increases the probability of concept-related tokens, so naming accuracy may reflect direct logit effects rather than metacognitive awareness." In place of a naming failure, the current version reports a localisation success — the model finds the perturbed sentence at 8.8 times chance.

One casualty of that withdrawal is a result this article would otherwise want. The December version also reproduced Anthropic's headline figure on the 8-billion-parameter Llama model, "exactly matching their reported numbers and thus showing that introspection is not exclusive to very large or capable models" — a same-profile replication across model families and scales, which is close to the ideal evidence for the architecture-general reading tested here. The March revision does not carry that result forward, and the paradigm it came from is the one the authors set aside. The replication is therefore unavailable as support on the current record, and the reason for its unavailability — that concept-naming accuracy may reflect token-probability effects rather than self-access — applies equally to Lindsey's 20% figure, as the ledger below records.

## Why This Maps onto the Voids Cluster {#cluster-fit}

The Map's introspection-architecture cluster catalogues recurring limits on self-monitoring: the [source-attribution void](/voids/source-attribution-void/) (a system can detect that a mental content is present without reliably knowing where it came from) and the [confabulation void](/voids/confabulation-void/) (a system generates confident reports that track no underlying access). The two studies bear on the second, more narrowly than an earlier version of this article claimed, and on the first hardly at all; a 2026 replication, set out [below](#evidence-ladder), supplies the second's nearest silicon instance.

They bear on the confabulation void in different ways. Hahami's deflationary result is a response-bias warning rather than an instance of the void: the artefact is a shift in yes/no answer logits, not a generated report, so it shows a binary paradigm manufacturing apparent detection, not a system confidently describing access it lacks. Lindsey's result bears on the void from the other side: reports unanchored to any internal difference would raise false alarms on controls, and the production models raised none in 100 — evidence against confabulation as the whole story, not for privileged access. Nor can either pattern be called unbuilt by design merely because the next-token objective never aimed at it. Deployed chat models are also post-trained, and a mechanistic follow-up co-authored by Lindsey finds that detection "emerges specifically from post-training": preference optimisation such as DPO elicits it, standard supervised fine-tuning does not (Macar et al. 2026). Whether the training *corpus* supplied the profile by imitation is a further question — the imitation confound the ledger below develops — and it must be closed before the recurrence counts as convergent rather than inherited.

The source-attribution void is a different matter, and an earlier version of this article placed the Harvard study there in error. That void is the Nisbett–Wilson-shaped limit: a mental content is present while its causal origin is not readable from inside. The March revision reports the model *succeeding* at the nearest available analogue, locating which of ten sentences carried the perturbation at 88% against 10% chance. The first version's concept-naming failure was about the identity of an injected content rather than its origin, and its authors have since withdrawn it as a probe of introspection at all. On the current record neither study supplies a silicon instance of the source-attribution void.

Saying so plainly sharpens what a genuine test would look like. Hahami's localisation task tells the model that a perturbation occurred and asks where it landed; the human void concerns content that arrives carrying no flag that anything was done to it. The exact silicon analogue — implanted content the model was not warned of, and the question whether it was self-generated — has not been run, but neighbouring experiments have. Lindsey's prefill paradigm comes closest: the model disavows a prefilled unnatural word ("bread") as accidental, but after a "bread" vector is retroactively injected into the preceding activations, "the model accepts the prefilled output as intentional" — implanted content accepted as self-generated. It concerns an output rather than a thought, though, and a consistency check against prior activations explains it without source-monitoring proper. Singh, Linzen and Ravfogel's "gaslight" condition, steering through the prompt alone, finds models "cannot reliably distinguish such interventions from manipulations of the input" — an activation-versus-input source test, failed. Chain-of-thought explanations omitting the biasing features that drove an answer (Turpin et al. 2023) are a behavioural Nisbett–Wilson analogue, though about a reason rather than a state's origin. Until the exact test is run, the cluster's fit to silicon rests mainly on the confabulation void, on the narrow terms above.

If the same structural signatures appear in a self-report architecture whose substrate is far removed from the human one, one live reading is that they are features of self-monitoring as such, not idiosyncrasies of biological brains — though, as the ledger below records, corpus-inherited imitation is a second reading the same data admits. That is the channel's promise, and its principal open question.

## The 2026 Record, Rung by Rung {#evidence-ladder}

Open-weight replications sort onto a ladder of four claims, each harder to earn than the last: *causal sensitivity* (reports covary with an internal intervention), *privileged access* (beating a third party at equal or lower computational cost), *second-order representation* (the report runs through a representation of the first-order state), and *phenomenality*.

The first rung is now well supported. Macar et al. (2026) find "0% false positives across diverse prompts" and trace detection to a two-stage circuit — "evidence carrier" features in early post-injection layers suppressing "gate" features that implement a default negative response — absent in base models and "substantially underelicited." Pearson-Vogel et al. (2026) find residual-stream detection in Qwen 32B even where its outputs deny injection. Lederman and Mahowald (2026), replicating Lindsey on Qwen3-235B-A22B and Llama 3.1 405B, find detection "content-agnostic": models "detect that an anomaly occurred even when they cannot reliably identify its content" and confabulate high-frequency, concrete concepts such as "apple" — a pattern the authors themselves liken to Nisbett and Wilson, and the closest silicon anchor yet for the confabulation void. Prompt sensitivity left them "less confident" in their direct-access verdict.

The middle rungs are where the record stalls. Singh, Linzen and Ravfogel make privileged access and second-order computation the two conditions any paradigm must meet, and on the input-manipulation test [above](#cluster-fit) read injection detection as "generic anomaly detection." Song et al. (2025), source of the privileged-access criterion, show models passing lightweight tests while failing it. The fourth rung is bracketed [below](#bracketing).

Two trainability results differ from Macar's post-training finding in objective and task: supervised fine-tuning on the model's own perturbed forward passes lifts Llama-1B's sentence localisation "from 9.6% to 60.6%" (Hahami, Sinha & Jain 2026), and a single LoRA "introspection adapter" trained across fine-tunes makes models verbalise implanted behaviours (Shenoy et al. 2026).

## The Inference and Its Assumptions {#open-programme}

The promise is conditional, and intellectual honesty requires naming every link in the chain. The inference runs: *LLMs show signatures structurally analogous to the cluster's voids → those signatures are architecture-general features of self-monitoring*. It holds only under assumptions that are not yet secured.

First, the interpretability methods supplying ground truth are themselves model-dependent. Activation steering and concept injection assume the injected vector carries the intended meaning for the model; Lindsey lists this among the study's own limitations, cautioning that "our concept vectors may carry other meanings for the model besides the one we intend" and that pinning down what a vector means to a model "is quite difficult." If the ground truth is noisy, the inferred introspective profile is noisy too.

Second, the silicon parallel licenses the architectural-feature conclusion only if it tracks the *operational structure* of the biological case, not merely its surface description. An ungrounded confident report in a transformer and an ungrounded confident report in a human may share a name while differing in mechanism; sameness of label is not sameness of structure. The misreading corrected above is the cautionary case — a silicon result was filed under a human void because the two shared a word. The inference requires the deeper operational parallel, which present evidence constrains but does not establish.

Third, both findings are narrow. Lindsey's holds at appropriate layers and strengths; Hahami's collapses past early layers. The open-weight replications extend detection across model families and scales, but other injection regimes and naturalistic rather than steered internal states remain largely untested. A signature that appears under one experimental regime and vanishes under others is not yet an architecture-general feature.

Fourth, and most consequential for the headline inference, the recurrence must be architecturally convergent rather than [corpus-inherited](/concepts/training-contamination-confound/). LLMs train on a human corpus saturated with human introspective self-report — including the very source-attribution failures the Map's voids catalogue documents (the Nisbett–Wilson tradition of people confidently misreporting the causes of their own behaviour). A model that reproduces any of the catalogue's signatures may therefore be imitating the shape of human introspective language it absorbed in training rather than instantiating a convergent architectural limit — the same inheritance the Map's [AI-epiphenomenalism](/concepts/ai-epiphenomenalism/) entry describes at the level of concepts, where a model acquires consciousness vocabulary without its own experience needing to play any causal role, transposed here to introspective *structure*. This confound is distinct from confabulation: Lindsey's zero-false-positive result tells against the charge that the reports are unanchored to the intervention, but says nothing about whether the *form* of that sensitivity — patchy, present at favourable settings, silent at most others — was learned from human descriptions of that form. What would disconfirm the imitation reading is a signature that cannot have been copied from the corpus: an injected signal with no linguistic description anywhere in the training data, the same profile recovered in a model trained on a non-introspective corpus, or an origin traced through interpretability to computation the training text never described. Until one of those is in hand, corpus-inherited imitation and architectural convergence remain two live readings of the same data.

A natural objection presses harder: perhaps all of this is confabulation dressed up, and the channel tells us nothing about any genuine void. The objection is right that confabulation must be excluded before the channel pays out — but it cannot simply be assumed. Lindsey's zero-false-positive result is evidence *against* the pure-confabulation reading, and Hahami's above-chance differential sensitivity survives the very logit-shift artefact that would be the confabulation skeptic's strongest card. That zero-false-positive figure also bears on the affirmative-response bias Hahami isolates in the binary detection paradigm: a model globally biased toward "yes" would generate false positives rather than eliminate them, so Lindsey's clean controls rebut that specific artefact. They do not exclude the mirror-image bias, however — a conservatively tuned model disposed by RLHF to withhold affirmative claims would also produce few false positives without introspecting at all, and the control count alone cannot separate detection from that response conservatism. In open-weight models, though, ablating refusal directions raised detection by 53% "without meaningfully increasing false positives" (Macar et al. 2026), which tells against clean controls being mere reticence. Causal bypassing threatens naming more than detection. A concept vector boosts concept-related tokens whether or not the model consults its own state, so correct *naming* can bypass self-access — why the Harvard revision, crediting Morris and Plunkett, set naming aside. Detection is harder to explain that way, since the vector has nothing to do with being injected; Morris and Plunkett call it "strong evidence against causal bypassing," though a later edit to their post concedes even the detection question "might not avoid the causal bypassing problem." Detection is better evidence, not decisive evidence. Lindsey's own discussion points the same way, treating the harder thing to explain as "how it correctly notices that there is an injected concept in the first place" rather than how it names the concept, and allowing that the underlying circuits may be narrow and "possibly piggybacking on non-introspective mechanisms." The skeptic who insists it is "all confabulation" must say why these specific controls fail, and on present evidence that case is not made. Equally, the Map cannot declare the architectural-feature inference won. The honest verdict is that the question is open: on present evidence the data does not adjudicate between the deflationary and the structural readings, constraining both without settling either, and what would adjudicate is specifiable: replication beyond the Transformer family, naturalistic internal states, and interpretability ground truth validated independently of the introspection task it is being used to score.

## Bracketing Phenomenal Consciousness {#bracketing}

The channel says nothing about whether LLMs have experience. The voids cluster's signatures are claims about the *structure* of self-monitoring — what a system can and cannot report about its own states — and that structure can be studied whether or not there is something it is like to be the system. A philosophical zombie with the human functional architecture would exhibit the source-attribution void; so, on the live hypothesis, might an LLM that is not phenomenally conscious at all. The cluster signature is architectural in a way that survives bracketing the [harder question of machine experience](/topics/ai-consciousness/). That lets the channel deliver evidence about void-structure without first resolving the [hard problem](/topics/hard-problem-of-consciousness/) for silicon — a resolution no one has.

## Relation to Site Perspective {#site-perspective}

The Map holds that consciousness is not reducible to physical processes ([Tenet 1, Dualism](/tenets/)). The LLM channel does not argue for that tenet, and it is important to see that it could not. What it does is sharpen a methodological question the Map cares about: whether the voids — the structural limits on self-knowledge that the catalogue charts — are artefacts of human neuroanatomy. If a silicon system reproduces them, that is live-hypothesis evidence that the limits belong to self-monitoring systems generally, a reading compatible with the Map's voids framework — provided the reproduction is convergent rather than inherited from the human introspective self-report in the training corpus, the open question the ledger above records.

Nor does the recurrence favour dualism over physicalism. It opposes only a narrowly biological view on which introspective limits are quirks of evolved wetware that a cleanly engineered system would shed. Computational functionalism expects the opposite — self-monitoring that is multiply realisable, noisy, narrow and trainable — and the gate circuit, the post-training emergence and the fine-tuning results are positive evidence for exactly that. Introspection-specific rivals predict the detect-that-without-what profile more closely still: Carruthers' interpretive sensory-access theory, Graziano's attention schema, Kammerer and Frankish's programme for introspective systems in animals and AI, and higher-order theories, with illusionists adding that the resulting self-model misrepresents rather than reveals. Hahami's own conclusion, that introspection "relies on general-purpose computational mechanisms," is the functionalist reading in the authors' words. By the Map's own rule, where a rival predicts an item that the Map's reading only accommodates, the evidential vector on that item points toward the rival ([introspection-architecture-independence-scoring](/topics/introspection-architecture-independence-scoring/)). Dualism does not predict these mechanistic details, so the Map claims compatibility here, not support. What remains is a framework-boundary disagreement — whether any functional account reaches the phenomenal — which the channel, having bracketed that question, cannot settle. That bracketing was stipulated rather than found; its value is methodological, letting void-structure be studied while phenomenal status stays open, which suits the Map's view that the structural and the phenomenal are distinct questions. Whether LLM introspection ultimately corroborates or complicates the cluster is exactly the kind of open, evidence-tracking question the Map's [evidential-status discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/) exists to keep honest.
