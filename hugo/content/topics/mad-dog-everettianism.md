**Mad-Dog Everettianism** is the name Sean Carroll and Ashmeet Singh (2019) gave to the most austere ontology yet proposed for quantum mechanics: the world is a vector in Hilbert space evolving under a Hamiltonian, and nothing else is fundamental. Space, fields, particles, subsystems, branches and observers are all meant to emerge. Carroll (2022) adds that "'Hilbert Space Fundamentalism' would be equally accurate", and the critical literature now calls the programme HSF. Whether it works turns on a technical question: do the Hamiltonian and the state alone fix a *unique* emergent structure, and so a unique division into subsystems, preferred basis and branch structure? Stoica (2022), in a peer-reviewed paper, argues that no structure fixed this way can be both unique and physically relevant. Soulas, Franzmann and Di Biagio (2025) offer a construction they say contradicts "one of Stoica's strongest claims", and Stoica (2026) replies that his theorem never excluded uniqueness alone, only uniqueness together with physical relevance. Both of the later papers are preprints, and the dispute is unresolved.

For the Unfinishable Map's [No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds) tenet, austerity cuts both ways, as the [section on the indexical objection](#indexical) explains. It gives the Everettian the strongest available form of the deflationary reply to the Map's question "why am I *this* branch?", since on HSF neither branches nor observers, nor even the space they occupy, were ever fundamental. And if Stoica is right, it opens a line the Map can press from inside HSF: an ontology that cannot fix its own branch structure leaves branch-relative identity nothing determinate to be relative to. That second line is contested, binds HSF alone, refutes nothing about branch-relative identity as such, and its worry about non-uniqueness reaches the Map's own reliance on decoherence.

## The Thesis {#thesis}

Carroll and Singh's essay, written for the FQXi contest "What is Fundamental?" and published as a Springer chapter in 2019, says that the world's most basic description "is given by the spectrum of the Hamiltonian (a list of energy eigenvalues) and the components of some particular vector in Hilbert space." Then: "Everything else — including space and fields propagating on it — is emergent from these minimal elements." The name advertises the ambition, which is "as far as we can imagine taking the program of stripping down quantum mechanics to its most pure, minimal elements." Carroll (2022) restates the view alone: the fundamental ontology is "completely and exactly represented by a vector in an abstract Hilbert space, evolving in time according to unitary Schrödinger dynamics."

Three features locate the view. It is distinct from the authors' 2021 paper on "quantum mereology", which supplies part of its machinery. Its probability story is borrowed: the Born rule "isn't assumed as part of the theory", the essay says, but is to be derived by decision theory or self-locating uncertainty, the programmes [probability-problem-in-many-worlds](/topics/probability-problem-in-many-worlds/) examines. What is new is the ontology. And in the philosophy of physics it is a third option beside the two that [wavefunction-realism-vs-primitive-ontology](/concepts/wavefunction-realism-vs-primitive-ontology/) compares: neither a field on configuration space nor matter in spacetime, but a vector with no arena at all. Ney (2023), who calls it Hilbert space realism, grants that it "is certainly even simpler" than configuration-space wavefunction realism, and names the cost: "it is much less clear how to recover all that is emergent from a single vector in Hilbert space."

The authors are candid about that cost. The essay's account of measurement assumes "an initially unentangled state, and a particular factorization into object/apparatus/environment", concedes "an infinite unitary freedom in the choice of our system and environment", and calls the question "murky at the present time". Carroll (2022) describes HSF as "an ambitious research program (and one that may ultimately fail)", and states the point that matters most for the Map: "Clearly, 'observers' are not represented directly by a vector in Hilbert space, nor is the world that they observe."

## The Emergence Programme {#emergence}

How could space emerge from a list of numbers? The first step is a *tensor product structure* (TPS), a division of Hilbert space into subsystems. Carroll and Singh lean on Cotler, Penington and Ranard (2019): most Hamiltonians are local in no decomposition, but for those that admit a local form, "the decomposition in which that works is essentially unique." Given such factors, the programme reads geometry off the state's entanglement, for states in which "nearby degrees of freedom are highly entangled, while faraway ones are unentangled", and the companion mereology paper seeks the system/environment split in quasi-classical dynamics (Carroll & Singh 2021). The uniqueness clause is what lets the programme speak of *the* emergent space rather than *an* emergent space.

Both critics agree that this use of Cotler et al. overreaches. The theorem fixes a local factorisation only up to an equivalence defined relative to the Hamiltonian, and once the state is added, Soulas et al. observe, "Different choices of TPSs within the equivalence class provided by Cotler et al.'s theorem will lead to different spacetime geometries". Stoica (2022), who does "not contest their results", likewise argues that the theorem cannot do the job asked of it. The two sides part over whether adding the state can make up the shortfall.

## The Uniqueness Dispute {#uniqueness-dispute}

**Stoica's theorem.** Stoica (2022, *Advances in Theoretical and Mathematical Physics*) argues that "whenever such a structure emerges from the Hamiltonian and the state vector alone, if it is physically relevant, it is not unique." The idea, as he restates it in 2026, turns on time. The state and the Hamiltonian stand in a relation that never observably changes: the weight of the state in each energy eigenspace is constant. A structure that matters physically, such as a set of subsystems whose entanglement grows or a space in which things move, must stand in a *changing* relation to the state. A structure constructed invariantly from the Hamiltonian and state alone inherits their changeless relation, so a unique construction cannot register change; and any structure that does register change has rivals, its images under the Hamiltonian's symmetries, which the Hamiltonian and state cannot tell apart from it but which differ physically. Stoica allows uniqueness in the loosest sense, permitting instances to differ wherever no physical difference follows, so the result does not trade on strict identity. His Theorem 6 applies it to the target: "The emergent 3D-space of Carroll and Singh ... either is physically irrelevant, or it is not unique (not even essentially unique)." The paper names Mad-Dog Everettianism and "various proposals based on decoherence" within its scope, and draws the consequence for many-worlds: "for every branching structure there are more alternative branching structures." Its abstract adds that HSF leads to "'passive' travel in time and in alternative realities", realised by a mere change of basis.

**The counterexample.** Soulas, Franzmann and Di Biagio (2025, preprint) argue that the right notion of uniqueness is relational: a structure need only be unique up to a global unitary applied to everything at once, "because two theories yielding the same predictions should be deemed equivalent." On that notion they prove (their Theorem 3.9) that "a Hamiltonian and a state are enough structure to uniquely select a preferred tensor product structure", and conclude that this "revives the hope that HSF is tenable." They contest Stoica's claim that physically relevant emergent structures are non-unique. The construction assumes a finite-dimensional space and a non-degenerate spectrum, and, as they note, "assumes prior knowledge of the number n of tensor factors as well as their dimensions."

**The reply.** Stoica (2026, preprint) answers that his theorem says two properties cannot both hold: it excludes uniqueness *together with* physical relevance, and his 2022 paper had already given unique constructions that fail physical relevance (its Remark 15). "So constructing a unique TPS doesn't, by itself, contradict" the theorem; a counterexample must satisfy both conditions at once. He argues that Soulas et al.'s construction "can't be both invariant and compatible with physical observations, so it can't be a counterexample": once entanglement changes in time, the invariants it fixes must be pinned to one absolute moment, be made to change in time in exactly the compensating way, or contradict observed changes of entanglement. He holds that their relational uniqueness is already covered by the essential uniqueness his theorem allowed, and that by their method "one could claim as well that the masses of elementary particles emerge uniquely just by fixing their values by hand."

**Where it stands.** Both 2025–26 papers are unrefereed. The peer-reviewed result in the exchange is Stoica's 2022 theorem, and its reach is what is disputed. No published reply by Carroll to Stoica was located as of 2026-10-14, and neither side discusses minds. The dispute is open; Stoica's reply is the latest word.

## Austerity and the Indexical Objection {#indexical}

The Map's case against many-worlds rests on an indexical question: if every branch is equally real, why am I *this* one? The [tenets](/tenets/) page states the objection's precondition: it is meaningful only on a non-deflationary conception of the subject, "an 'I' whose branch-assignment is a real further fact, not a convention". The Everettian's best reply, due to Saunders (2010) and Wallace (2012), is branch-relative indexicality: "I" refers to the speaker's own branch, as "here" refers to the speaker's place (see [MWI's indexical reply](/concepts/many-worlds/#mwis-branch-relative-indexicality-reply)). The Map does not claim to show this false on the Everettian's own resources; [qm-interpretations-beyond-many-worlds](/topics/qm-interpretations-beyond-many-worlds/) treats the disagreement as a framework boundary. HSF moves this in two directions.

**Reading A: austerity strengthens the deflationary reply.** If branches, systems and observers are all emergent descriptions, "why am I this branch?" demands a fundamental fact about a level HSF never claimed was fundamental. Carroll says as much: observers are not represented directly in the vector, "nor is the world that they observe." The austere Everettian can borrow the "Dennett's criterion" Wallace (2003) adopts for emergent structure: "A macro-object is a pattern", real according to "the explanatory power and predictive reliability" of theories that admit it, and since "the decoherence process is only approximate", branches never had to be exact. Joined to a view on which the "I" is such a pattern, HSF is the deflationary route the tenets page concedes stays open at the framework boundary, now with a physics programme attached, and the demand for a further fact is harder to press against an ontology that makes a principle of having nothing beyond the vector.

**Reading B: austerity exposes the branch structure.** That reply presupposes that there *is* a determinate branch structure, however approximate, for "I" to be relative to. If Stoica's theorem stands, HSF cannot supply one: whatever branching it decodes from the Hamiltonian and state has physically distinct rivals decodable equally well. Branch-relative identity then lacks a determinate relatum, and the pressure uses only what HSF itself grants. The Map says it cannot bring in-framework pressure of this kind against Wallace-style Everettianism; against HSF specifically there may be an exception. Four limits keep the reading honest.

1. **It refutes nothing about branch-relative identity as such.** Stoica's result concerns the uniqueness of emergent structure. Branch-relative identity is coherent in any framework that takes the branch structure as given; the theorem says only that HSF cannot take it as given and cannot derive it uniquely.
2. **It binds HSF alone.** Everettians who help themselves to spacetime, fields or a preferred algebra of observables are untouched, and HSF can always retreat to their position. The retreat costs the austerity that made HSF the strongest deflationary reply; against the less austere Everettianism that results, the Map's objection has its ordinary standing.
3. **It is contested.** If Soulas et al.'s relational uniqueness is the right notion, Stoica's rivals are redescriptions of one world, and the branch structure is as determinate as physics makes anything. The Map cannot adjudicate a dispute in mathematical physics, and Reading B is conditional on its outcome.
4. **It cuts against the Map too.** The Map's preferred mechanism, [post-decoherence selection](/concepts/post-decoherence-selection/), has consciousness select among outcomes fixed by einselection, and einselection is defined relative to a system/environment split: one of the "various proposals based on decoherence" Stoica's result claims to cover. The theorem does not bind the Map, which never claimed the Hamiltonian and state suffice; like Wallace, it can take the split as an input. Taking that input costs the Map nothing Everett does not also pay. The debt the [factorisation page](/concepts/quantum-factorisation-problem/) books lies further in: which split is the paired subject's, a question no derivation of the split answers. The parity recorded there reaches the indexical argument itself, which needs a determinate "I" as Everett's branches need a determinate split, and so is weakened on both sides. Reading B therefore cannot become a claim that single-world views have determinate structure where Everett lacks it. Every view that takes the structure as input gets the same determinacy; only the view that refuses all input is exposed.

HSF could instead accept the multiplicity, which deepens the question rather than relieving it. Stoica (2022) considers that "Maybe this is how the universe is, with multiple alternative 3D-spaces and factorizations into subsystems", in which case HSF "implies multiplicity of experienced worlds for the same state vector", over and above Everettian branching. The [vertiginous question](/topics/vertiginous-question/) then multiplies: why this decoding, as well as why this branch. An austere Everettian could extend branch-relative indexicality to decoding-relative indexicality; whether self-locating credence can be extended across decodings, and with what measure, is a question none of the texts consulted here take up. Stoica (2026) argues that the inclusive version is empirically untenable anyway, since it implies "no correlations between the properties of external objects and the records of these properties from our brains".

**The net.** Against HSF the indexical objection loses grip on Reading A and gains a conditional, contested supplement on Reading B. The two meet at one point: the feature that makes HSF the strongest deflationary reply, that nothing beyond the Hamiltonian and state is fundamental, is the feature Stoica's theorem targets. An austere Everettian who says the "I" was never fundamental owes, on Stoica's view, a determinate pattern for the "I" to be; one who secures the pattern by adding structure gives up the austerity. Neither horn refutes the Everettian.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds)](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)** gains no refutation here. The register's verdict that the case against branch-egalitarian Everettianism marks "a framework boundary, not an in-framework refutation" ([individuation-and-subjecthood](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/), [P-I5](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i5)) holds for HSF as it does for Wallace. What HSF adds is a candidate exception to that verdict, conditional on Stoica's theorem surviving the 2025–26 exchange and on HSF declining both the retreat to added structure and the many-decodings option. The tenet's standing stays where [tenets](/tenets/) locates it, in the non-deflationary subject it presupposes.

**[Tenet 1 (Dualism)](/tenets/#dualism)** meets HSF only by construction. Neither Carroll and Singh (2019) nor Carroll (2022) discusses consciousness; the word occurs in each only in the reference-list title of Tegmark's (2015) "Consciousness as a State of Matter", which the essay cites for a way to "define the system/environment split in the first place". The confrontation is the Map's to stage: on the most austere physical base on offer, a phenomenal state would have to be an emergent pattern in eigenvalues and vector components, which makes the explanatory gap as conspicuous as it can be. HSF offers no account of that, and the Map draws no refutation from its silence.

**[Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits)](/tenets/#occams-limits)** finds a conditional case. Ney grants that HSF is simpler than configuration-space wavefunction realism; whether the simplest base can fix its own subsystems is exactly what the uniqueness dispute is about. Simplicity of the base did not settle which parts the world has. The discipline binds the Map in both directions: the Map cannot treat the austerity as decisive evidence for HSF, nor count it against HSF.

**[Tenets 2](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) and [3](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** inherit the fourth limit in the indexical section. A minimal influence exercised at post-decoherence selection acts within a factorisation-relative menu, so the mechanism owes an account of which split its menu belongs to and which subsystem is the subject's, a debt that even a successful derivation of the split would leave open, as the [factorisation page](/concepts/quantum-factorisation-problem/) records.
