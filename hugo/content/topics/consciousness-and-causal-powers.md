If consciousness has genuine causal powers—if it does more than accompany physical processes—then the nature and scope of those powers becomes central to philosophy of mind. Beyond whether consciousness causes anything (addressed in [mental causation](/concepts/mental-causation-and-downward-causation/) and [evolutionary-case-for-mental-causation](/topics/evolutionary-case-for-mental-causation/)) lies what *kind* of causation it exercises and what limits constrain it. The Unfinishable Map holds that consciousness exercises a specific and limited causal influence: biasing quantum indeterminacies in neural systems toward certain outcomes, narrower than unconstrained psychokinesis and bounded in ways this article specifies.

The answers diverge sharply. If consciousness has no causal powers ([epiphenomenalism](/concepts/epiphenomenalism/))—the default [the epiphenomenalist threat](/topics/the-epiphenomenalist-threat/) shows most frameworks converge on—everything we do would happen the same way if we were unconscious automata. Unlimited powers land in pseudoscience. The Map stakes out middle ground: real but circumscribed influence, operating within physical constraints.

## What Causal Powers Could Mean

**Direct physical force**: Consciousness could push particles around, inject energy, exert forces—classical psychokinesis, ruled out by the Map's [Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) tenet. Parapsychology's positive findings have not survived methodological scrutiny, and the move would violate conservation laws.

**Selection among alternatives**: Consciousness could be the locus of choice among physically permissible outcomes where the laws of nature leave the result undetermined. This is the Map's position. Physics specifies probabilities; phenomenal selection biases which actualizes—no energy added, no force exerted. The [metaphysics of information under dualism](/concepts/metaphysics-of-information-under-dualism/) frames this as syntactic constraints from physics, semantic selection from consciousness.

This framing presupposes a non-Everettian interpretation: under [Many-Worlds](/concepts/many-worlds/), all physically permitted outcomes actualise on parallel branches and there is nothing for consciousness to select. The Map's rejection of MWI is not free: whichever interpretation is right, *something* must be added—MWI adds branches the Schrödinger equation does not single out for actualisation; the Map adds a selection mechanism. The Map judges branch-multiplication as the higher cost, carried by the indexical argument rather than parsimony (see [Tenet 4](/tenets/#no-many-worlds) and [The Map's Case for One-World Actuality](/arguments/many-worlds-argument/)).

**Pattern amplification**: Consciousness could sustain neural patterns through repeated "observation" in the quantum sense. The [quantum Zeno effect](/concepts/stapp-quantum-mind/) suggests attention holds neural states in superposition longer than they would naturally persist—causal power as holding rather than pushing. Stapp's own proposal covers the holding and stops short of the outcome-biasing described under §"Selection of Action"; §"Sustained Attention" marks that boundary. Seven [amplification mechanisms](/topics/amplification-mechanisms-consciousness-physics/) have been proposed for how quantum-level influence scales to macroscopic neural effects.

**Organizational influence**: Consciousness might affect how neural components organize without affecting their individual behaviour. The challenge is specifying how organization contributes novel powers without violating lower-level laws.

## Scope and Limits

Those powers must have limits, or every conscious wish would reshape reality. Four constraints bound them.

### The Interface Constraint

Consciousness interfaces with the physical world at quantum indeterminacies in neural systems, which localizes the causal power: no direct influence on distant objects, other people's brains, or macroscopic events. That locality accounts for an asymmetry—you can decide to raise your own arm, but not someone else's. See [attention-as-interface](/concepts/attention-as-interface/) and [the interface location problem](/topics/the-interface-problem/).

### The Physical Constraint

Even at the interface, consciousness doesn't violate physics. The [Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) tenet specifies biasing outcomes physics already allows. This is where [conservation laws](/concepts/conservation-laws-and-mental-causation/) bear, and the constraint is narrower than the energetically degenerate case suggests. Superposed neural outcomes need not share an energy eigenvalue, so a realised outcome may differ in energy from the counterfactual chance outcome on that trial. What selection preserves is the *ensemble* expectation: redistributing which trials yield which outcome, without shifting any outcome's long-run frequency from its Born weight, leaves the per-trial differences cancelling rather than accumulating, and conservation read as the statistical and local law it is survives that.

### The Probabilistic Constraint

[Quantum mechanics provides probabilities, not certainties](/concepts/quantum-probability-consciousness/), so an extremely unlikely outcome cannot be guaranteed by wanting it strongly enough. The [causal consistency constraint](/concepts/causal-consistency-constraint/) (Torres Alegre 2025, a recent arXiv preprint not yet independently confirmed) argues those bounds are structurally fixed: under purification, no-signalling forces the Born form. This may explain why voluntary action feels effortful—consciousness shifting probabilities against statistical noise from countless quantum events.

### The Temporal Constraint

[Psychophysical coupling](/concepts/psychophysical-laws/) operates in real time. Consciousness cannot retroactively change past outcomes or influence future states disconnected from present neural activity.

## What the Map Hypothesises Consciousness Does

Given these constraints, what causal work would consciousness perform under the Map's framework?

### Architecture and Implementation Candidates

The Map's commitment is layered. Phenomenal Biasing is the *architecture-level* claim: consciousness exercises causal influence by being phenomenally laden, with felt qualities (valence, value, salience) entering the causal story in a way sub-personal computational role does not exhaust. Selection of Action, Sustained Attention, and Volitional Modulation are *candidate implementations* of that architecture, and the framework commits to the architecture rather than to any one of them.

This matters for quantitative objections. The twelve-to-nineteen-orders-of-magnitude decoherence gap discussed under Sustained Attention is a constraint on the Zeno *candidate*, not on the architecture. If unresolvable, an alternative implementation would carry the load; Phenomenal Biasing itself does not require a specific quantum-physical mechanism.

These mechanisms are hypothesised, not empirically established. Rival accounts predict the same phenomenology by different mechanisms—identity theories, and the active-inference family whose strongest current form is engaged under §"Phenomenal Biasing." The case for the Map's reading rests on framework coherence and phenomenological fit; evidence-grade is laid out in §"The Evidential Question."

### Selection of Action

Faced with multiple options—speak or stay silent, approach or avoid—consciousness biases which neural pattern wins: alternatives exist as superposed possibilities, and consciousness influences which collapses into actuality. The selection is a genuine causal contribution rather than unlimited free will (see [free-will](/topics/free-will/)). Without consciousness, outcomes follow physical probability distributions; with it, phenomenal factors—desires, values, deliberations—shift those distributions.

### Sustained Attention

Consciousness can hold neural patterns active through sustained attention. The quantum Zeno effect proposes that repeated "observation" of a neural state prevents it from decohering—watching a pot that never boils (see [stapp-quantum-mind](/concepts/stapp-quantum-mind/) and [structure-of-attention](/topics/structure-of-attention/)). The Map's own mechanism ranking puts this candidate *below* post-decoherence selection on the timescale grounds set out next ([P-Q4](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q4)); it appears here as the historically prominent proposal, not the preferred one.

The Zeno proposal faces a serious quantitative gap. Standard decoherence calculations for neural superpositions in warm, wet brain tissue give coherence times of $10^{-13}$ to $10^{-20}$ seconds; attention runs at 10–30 Hz. The mechanism would need observations at $10^{13}$ Hz at the slow end of that range and $\geq 10^{20}$ Hz at the fast end—twelve to nineteen orders of magnitude faster than the attentional phenomenology. Closing the gap requires protected microenvironments (the contested Penrose-Hameroff microtubule conjecture) or an alternative quantum mechanism. The coherence-time requirement remains a critical vulnerability.

Stapp's own boundary is narrower than the use this article makes of him. In his von Neumann taxonomy the agent supplies Process 1—which question is put to nature—while the answer falls to a third process, nature's Born-obeying choice between "Yes" and "No." Direct control over *which* outcome obtains is a move he declines. The outcome-biasing posited under §"Selection of Action" is therefore the Map's commitment, not an inheritance from Stapp; [stapp-quantum-mind](/concepts/stapp-quantum-mind/) states the disavowal at its source.

Whatever the mechanism, the causal power posited is persistence: consciousness makes certain neural configurations more stable, enabling planning and deliberation. The phenomenology fits this picture and fits rivals in which felt effort reflects mesoscopic reverberation, attentional gain modulation, and noradrenergic locus coeruleus modulation of cortical signal-to-noise. Suggestive, not decisive; see [phenomenology-of-consciousness-doing-work](/apex/phenomenology-of-consciousness-doing-work/).

### Phenomenal Biasing

The character of conscious experience—what it feels like—might itself contribute causal influence. [Pain](/topics/pain-consciousness-and-causal-power/) provides the paradigm case: pain's unpleasantness doesn't merely represent tissue damage but causally motivates avoidance. [pain-asymbolia](/concepts/pain-asymbolia/)—patients detect nociceptive stimuli but lack the felt awfulness—shows intact sensory detection without the extended avoidance behaviour pain's felt character normally drives.

The dissociation constrains the simplest representational claim (that felt awfulness just is nociceptive activation) but does not on its own establish the Map's reading. A predictive-processing account treating affective valence as a separate hierarchical prior predicts the same dissociation.

Predictive processing's most demanding form competes on the Map's own ground. Laukkonen, Friston and Chandaria (2025) make *Bayesian binding*—the competitive entry into a single world model of only those inferences that coherently reduce long-term uncertainty—one of three jointly sufficient conditions for phenomenal experience. That is itself a selection-for-consciousness mechanism, built to entail consciousness from the dynamics: where the self-referential loop closes with sufficient depth, nothing further need be supplied. The Map's disagreement sits at the bridge from sub-personal prior to felt awfulness, which the beautiful-loop account treats as constituted by the recursion and the Map treats as adding causal work the recursion alone does not perform. [The dedicated engagement](/topics/predictive-processing-and-dualism/) steelmans the account in its own vocabulary and finds the residue a framework boundary rather than a refutation either way.

Different phenomenal qualities produce different responses: sharp pain provokes withdrawal, burning pain motivates cooling, aching pain produces protective immobility. The [selection currency thesis](/topics/valence-and-conscious-selection/) makes valence the common metric by which consciousness evaluates competing options, which requires [psychophysical-laws](/concepts/psychophysical-laws/) specifying how phenomenal character maps to action tendencies. See [phenomenal-value-realism](/topics/phenomenal-value-realism/) for ethical implications.

### Volitional Modulation

The [phenomenology of volitional control](/topics/volitional-control/)—including conscious veto and the "whether" component of intentional action (Brass & Haggard 2007, 2008)—offers first-person evidence consistent with variable causal engagement. On the Map's reading, the phenomenology of variable effort tracks variable causal engagement; the rival reading takes it as variable noradrenergic gain. The two are observationally indistinguishable at the phenomenology—a live underdetermination flagged in §"Absence of Direct Evidence." The Map's residual claim is that the phenomenally laden component of effort is not paraphrasable as locus-coeruleus modulation without remainder, because that description omits the felt valence the Phenomenal Biasing architecture treats as causally relevant.

Resisting a strong impulse feels effortful in a way going along with it does not, and the framework registers graded effort as evidence for variable rather than binary engagement, without committing to a specific physical implementation.

A deeper objection sits one register lower than the noradrenergic rival. [Eliminative materialism](/topics/eliminative-materialism/) holds that the grammatical scheme this article uses—"consciousness selects," "consciousness sustains"—is folk-psychological reification awaiting redescription in sub-personal vocabulary. The Map carries a referent for "consciousness" as bearer of phenomenal properties, argued for on independent grounds ([arguments-against-materialism](/topics/arguments-against-materialism/), [phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence](/topics/phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence/)), and disputes not the redescription's in-principle availability but its adequacy to the explanandum: felt phenomenal valence as the thing being modulated, not just the modulation. This is a framework-boundary disagreement, honestly noted.

## What Consciousness Does Not Do

Equally important are the limits if the Map's framework is correct. Three restate the constraints above as prohibitions: no influence outside the neural systems consciousness is coupled to (no mentally moving objects, reading minds, or affecting distant quantum systems); no guaranteed outcomes, since biasing probabilities leaves voluntary action able to fail; and no retroactive influence, the causal arrow pointing forward. Two require more care.

**No net energy gain**: Selection redistributes which trials yield which outcome without shifting the ensemble energy expectation, answering the conservation objection in its energy-accounting form. Its thermodynamic form is unanswered. Preserving the ensemble mean does not establish that a Born-exact selector with a context-sensitive policy could not raise expected *extractable work*—favouring higher-energy outcomes where a work reservoir is coupled and compensating where it is not, every marginal untouched and the demon readmitted. No theorem settles this either way, and [the conservation dependency](/concepts/conservation-laws-and-mental-causation/) carries it as open debt at the [mechanism-debt](/positions/quantum-interface/#mechanism-debt) grade.

**No macroscopic violations**: No suspending gravity, reversing entropy, or breaking statistical patterns at scales where quantum effects average out.

These constraints distinguish the Map's position from pseudoscience while preserving genuine mental causation.

## The Philosophical Significance

### For Free Will

If consciousness has no causal powers, libertarian [free-will](/topics/free-will/) is impossible. The [trilemma-of-selection](/topics/trilemma-of-selection/) makes this explicit: at any indeterminate decision point the options are determinism, randomness, or mental causation, and only genuine causal powers deliver the third. The [luck objection](/concepts/quantum-indeterminacy-free-will/) cuts against constrained and unconstrained powers alike—whatever consciousness does at the gap, what grounds the specific bias remains live. The Map anchors selections in phenomenal contents rather than uncaused arbitrary choices, leaving open whether those contents need further grounding.

### For Ethics

If consciousness causally influences behaviour through its phenomenal character, then what experiences feel like enters the causal story of what agents do. This is not what grounds [phenomenal-value-realism](/topics/phenomenal-value-realism/): that position grounds value in phenomenal character by identity—felt badness *is* disvalue—and would stand even if the character turned out inert. What causal efficacy supplies is the connection between phenomenal value and behaviour: pain's felt awfulness bears on avoidance, making phenomenal value a term in selection rather than a fact registered alongside it. Inferring intrinsic value *from* the causal role would run the grounding backwards.

### For the Mind-Body Problem

Specific, limited causal powers operating through quantum selection constrain what a theory of consciousness can look like: it must explain how phenomenal properties acquire that causal profile—influence at neural quantum indeterminacies, nowhere else. Empirical calibration is in §"The Evidential Question."

### For Metaphysics

If consciousness has causal powers irreducible to physical forces, this supports [dualism](/concepts/interactionist-dualism/). The physical world is not [causally closed](/concepts/causal-closure/)—a denial that must engage the closure literature rather than assert its way past it, and the [history of the closure debate](/topics/causal-closure-debate-historical-survey/) traces how Papineau's inductive argument has been pressed by Montero, Lowe and Gibb. Mental properties add to the causal story, with implications for reductionism, emergence, and natural law. The deeper question is whether consciousness *is* a disposition, *has* dispositions, or reveals a category [dispositional ontology](/topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions/) cannot accommodate. See [psychophysical-laws-bridging-mind-and-matter](/topics/psychophysical-laws-bridging-mind-and-matter/) for formalisation attempts.

## The Evidential Question

The paired [Born-rule article](/topics/born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface/) and [Born-preservation synthesis](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/) grade each body of evidence on a three-rung ladder, and the same discipline applies here. Evidence is *interface-compatible* when it does not conflict with the causal-powers reading; *interface-suggestive* when it fits that reading better than a named rival on a named feature; *interface-discriminating* when it carries a signature only that reading predicts. Each class below is graded, and none reaches the third rung.

### Introspective Evidence

We seem to experience our conscious states influencing our actions. The evidential weight of this is limited. Introspective causal attributions are subject to systematic distortion: Nisbett and Wilson (1977) documented confident confabulation of causal narratives in cases where the reported cause was demonstrably not the operative one; Wegner (2002) showed the *feeling* of conscious will dissociates from actual causal involvement under controlled conditions. What Nisbett and Wilson established is limited introspective *access* to the processes generating behaviour, and no more should be claimed on their behalf: their designs do not show the behaviour was unconsciously initiated, and reading them as though they had concedes more than the evidence requires, in the direction that cuts against the Map. The Map's reading does not need token-by-token reliability—only that the global pattern of phenomenally laden action is not exhaustively explained by sub-personal confabulation. **Interface-compatible, non-discriminating**: every rival predicts the confabulation findings equally, and the *prima facie* phenomenology carries little weight of its own. The weight sits elsewhere (evolutionary, comparative, clinical).

### Evolutionary Evidence

If consciousness evolved by natural selection, it must have affected fitness. The [evolutionary-case-for-mental-causation](/topics/evolutionary-case-for-mental-causation/) argues only causally efficacious traits are selected. This doesn't tell us *what kind* of causal powers consciousness has, only that it has some. **Interface-compatible, non-discriminating**: an identity theorist reads the same selection pressure as acting on the neural states consciousness is identical to.

### Comparative Cognitive Evidence

Humans possess cognitive capacities (logical reasoning, metacognition, cumulative culture) that great apes with sophisticated unconscious processing lack. If these require conscious processing—and the cognitive-load literature offers contested but suggestive evidence that they do—consciousness causally contributes to cognition. Dual-process distinctions, debates over whether "unconscious System 1" is conscious-but-inaccessible, and meta-analytic challenges to dual-task interference complicate the inference. **Interface-compatible, non-discriminating**: the rival reads conscious access as the marker of a computational architecture doing the work rather than as its cause. See [baseline-cognition](/concepts/baseline-cognition/).

### Clinical Evidence: Cognitive Work Without Motor Output

Cognitive motor dissociation (CMD) is the clinical exhibit that bears most directly on epiphenomenalism. Patients with no observable response to command show, via fMRI or EEG, robust task-locked neural responses to instructions like "imagine playing tennis"—understanding language, sustaining attention, forming task-relevant patterns with no overt behaviour. Bodien et al. (2024, *NEJM*) found this pattern in 60 of 241 such patients, approximately 25%, drawn from a convenience sample of 353. The denominator matters: those 241 spanned mixed diagnostic categories united by absent observable command-following rather than by a diagnosis of unresponsive wakefulness, so the figure is not a 25% misdiagnosis rate for that syndrome. Consciousness continues to *do* cognitive work when the motor channel is severed.

**Interface-compatible, non-discriminating.** The grade needs stating plainly, because the exhibit invites over-recruitment. An epiphenomenalist can grant the covert processing entire and deny only that its phenomenal character contributes to it; an identity theorist reads the task-locked response as *being* the cognitive work rather than its trace. What the finding rules out is the inference from absent behaviour to absent cognition, which no rival here held. It favours the interface reading by coherence rather than compulsion. See [Consciousness Disruption](/topics/consciousness-disruption-and-the-mind-brain-interface/#covert-consciousness-awareness-without-expression).

### Absence of Direct Evidence

We lack direct empirical evidence that consciousness biases quantum outcomes in brains: the hypothesis is *compatible* with current evidence but not *required* by it, and a physicalist can identify mental states with neural states, making mental causation a subset of physical causation. [brain-internal-born-rule-testing](/topics/brain-internal-born-rule-testing/) catalogues what evidence would close this gap mechanism by mechanism.

The Map's reply has to describe the position accurately. The identity theorist posits no phenomenal item standing idle beside a neural cause: one event admits two descriptions, so whatever causal work the neural description reports is the same work the phenomenal description reports, and qualitative character is efficacious precisely *because* it is the neural state's character. The complaint that phenomenal character is left with nothing to do does not land.

What the position does help itself to is the identity. Water is H₂O because the two descriptions converge on the same properties; here the phenomenal description attributes a property—felt awfulness, felt redness—the neural description does not, and the identity is asserted rather than derived from either vocabulary. Identity theorists answer that the residue is an artefact of two modes of access to one property; the Map finds that unpersuasive absent an account of why *this* configuration carries *that* character, while granting that whether the residue marks a gap in the theory or a limit on what explanation can deliver is a disagreement at the framework boundary rather than a fault inside the account. The Map's position rests there on the independent [dualism](/concepts/dualism/) argument; the causal powers framework describes *how* non-physical causation might work if dualism holds, not that it does.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The [Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction) tenet commits to consciousness having causal powers; [Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) specifies their scope: influence at quantum indeterminacies, nowhere else.

**Ontological restraint**: It grants consciousness causal efficacy without multiplying unexplained powers. One *kind* of mechanism—quantum selection at the phenomenal-physical interface—suffices. The restraint presupposes a single conscious subject coordinating candidate mechanisms—a [unity-of-consciousness](/concepts/unity-of-consciousness/) assumption the split-brain literature complicates (Bayne 2010; Schechter 2018) and the Map carries as an undischarged background commitment.

**Empirical compatibility**: Current physics permits the architecture; specific candidates face unresolved quantitative constraints. The Zeno proposal needs protected microenvironments extending neural coherence by twelve to nineteen orders of magnitude (the contested Penrose-Hameroff conjecture; see [quantum-biology-and-neural-mechanisms](/concepts/quantum-biology-and-neural-mechanisms/)) or an unspecified alternative.

**Explanatory power**: One mechanism covers effort, voluntary-control limits, attention-persistence correlation, and the evolutionary value of consciousness.

**Philosophical coherence**: Locating mental causation at genuine physical indeterminacies aims to avoid both epiphenomenalism and overdetermination. Kim's exclusion objection assumes physical causes are *sufficient* for every physical effect; if so, an additional mental cause looks either redundant or idle. The responses in the literature do not divide into those two options: [the exclusion argument](/concepts/causal-exclusion-argument/) catalogues five response families—Bennett's compatibilism, the Shoemaker–Wilson subset account, Yablo's determinable–determinate reading, the List–Menzies–Woodward interventionist route, and Kim's own reductionist horn—and maps three routes available to a dualist, of which the Map takes the third. Selection-only interactionism denies the sufficiency premise locally: physics specifies candidate set and Born-rule probabilities, not which candidate actualises, so the physical contribution is necessary but not sufficient and mind realises one candidate. See [overdetermination-dissolution-under-selection-only-interactionism](/topics/overdetermination-dissolution-under-selection-only-interactionism/) for the full argument.

The dissolution has a price. The [causal-consistency constraint](/concepts/causal-consistency-constraint/) (Torres Alegre 2025) requires mental selection preserve Born statistics: any scheme respecting no-signalling must be indistinguishable from unbiased Born per event. On the strictest reading of [Tenet 2](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) the hypothesised bias therefore leaves no signature in *unconditioned* outcome frequencies, Born-preservation constraining the long-run marginal rather than the mind-conditioned distributions ([born-preserving-causal-efficacy](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/)). Honestly characterised: framework-coherence-supported, operationally undiscriminable by unconditioned aggregate test—the grade the positions register prices as [mechanism debt](/positions/quantum-interface/#mechanism-debt). Discrimination survives at ensemble and correlation levels, and [brain-internal-born-rule-testing](/topics/brain-internal-born-rule-testing/) catalogues the families (sequential-dependency tests, intention-versus-spontaneity contrasts, Bell-inequality-style tests on phenomenally correlated events).

The Popperian objection that this is a *degenerating research programme* deserves direct answer. The framework forbids per-trial discrimination by design—a structural feature of strict Tenet 2 plus no-signalling, not a refinement introduced to evade falsification. A degenerating programme closes discriminating tests at all levels, whereas this one stays discriminable in principle at the ensemble and correlation levels. Were every adjacent-level test to return null at the required precision, the framework would inherit the problem then; those families have not yet been run at brain-internal precision, and the verdict is open.

The cost is commitment to quantum effects in the brain at timescales relevant to neural processing—speculative. The Map accepts this as the price of coherent [interactionist dualism](/concepts/interactionist-dualism/).
