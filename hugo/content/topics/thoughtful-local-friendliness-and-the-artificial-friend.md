The "thoughtful" Local Friendliness experiment proposed by Wiseman, Cavalcanti and Rieffel (2023) is the one Wigner's-friend test anyone has designed around a friend that thinks. The friend would be a human-level artificial intelligence run reversibly on a large quantum computer. The Unfinishable Map is exposed to Wigner's-friend physics because it holds that measurement outcomes are absolute, and the Map's reading of the Local Friendliness theorem is that a laboratory containing a *conscious* observer cannot afterwards be recohered. The artificial friend is where that commitment meets the Map's verdicts on AI consciousness, and the result is a conditional. If the AI friend is not conscious, as the Map's substrate analysis expects, a successful experiment is what the Map predicts and refutes nothing it holds. If the AI friend were conscious in the sense the Map's selection mechanism needs, the Map predicts the experiment would fail. The authors deliberately built their theorem on *thoughts* rather than consciousness, so the experiment as designed does not test the Map. What would still bear on the Map is set out under [what would bear on the Map](#what-would-bear-on-the-map).

## The Local Friendliness No-Go Theorem

Bong et al. (2020) proved a Wigner's-friend theorem with the structure of Bell's theorem. Two entangled systems go to two separated laboratories. In each laboratory a "friend" measures the system, and a superobserver outside then either asks the friend for the result or reverses the friend's measurement to perform a different one. The theorem states that "if quantum evolution is controllable on the scale of an observer", one of three assumptions must fail:

- **Absoluteness of Observed Events** (AOE): "every observed event exists absolutely, not relatively". A friend's outcome is a fact simpliciter, not merely a fact for the friend, a branch or a reference system.
- **Locality**: a choice made in one laboratory does not influence outcomes at the other.
- **No-Superdeterminism**: the superobservers' choices of measurement are not correlated in advance with the systems they measure.

Together these make up "Local Friendliness" (LF). From them Bong and colleagues derived inequalities that quantum mechanics predicts will be violated once friends can be put into superposition and reversed. They confirmed the violations in a proof-of-principle experiment, but there the friend was a photon's path, and the authors say such a friend "would not typically be considered a macroscopic, sentient observer as originally envisioned by Wigner."

### Why it is stronger than Bell {#stronger-than-bell}

Bell's theorem needs more than locality and free choice. It also needs either predetermined outcomes or Bell's own local causality condition. The LF assumptions are, in Bong et al.'s words, "strictly weaker than those of Bell's theorem or Kochen-Specker contextuality theorems". AOE says only that the outcomes actually observed in a run are absolute. It makes no claim about measurements that were never made. Weaker premises give a stronger no-go: a theory that gives up predetermined outcomes while keeping absolute outcomes, locality and free choice escapes Bell's theorem but is still caught by LF. The inequalities are also derived without assuming quantum mechanics, so a violation constrains any theory that reproduces the observed correlations. The authors conclude that the theorem "places strictly stronger constraints on physical reality than Bell's theorem."

The catch is the word "observer". Bong et al. were deliberately noncommittal about what counts as a friend. As Wiseman, Cavalcanti and Rieffel later put it, the observer in the 2020 theorem "should be interpreted as a placeholder to be defined by a candidate theory or ideology." A photon's path counts as a friend only for theories under which every physical system is an observer. For consciousness-involving interpretations, photon-friend experiments contain no friend at all.

## The Thoughtful Variant

Wiseman, Cavalcanti and Rieffel (2023) fill the placeholder. Their theorem "takes seriously the idea that a system's having thoughts is a sufficient condition for it to be an observer". It swaps the three assumptions of 2020 for four metaphysical and two technological ones:

1. **Local Agency**: an intervention of the kind used in randomised trials is uncorrelated with relevant events outside its future light cone. The authors note that this one assumption implies both Locality and No-Superdeterminism from the 2020 theorem.
2. **Physical Supervenience**: "Any thought supervenes upon some physical event(s) in the brain (or other information-processing unit as appropriate)", which can therefore be located in a bounded region of spacetime. The authors say this is "compatible with the monist assumption that a thought is nothing but a physical process, but does not require that."
3. **Ego Absolutism**: "My communicable thoughts are absolutely real." It is written in the first person, so each reader applies it to their own thoughts.
4. **Friendliness**: if a system shows independent cognitive ability at least on par with one's own, then "any thought they communicate is as real as any communicable thought of my own". "As real as" means "having the same degree of reality as", so Friendliness passes on whatever reality one grants one's own thoughts, absolute or not.
5. **Human-Level Artificial Intelligence** (HLAI): a human-level AI algorithm is possible.
6. **Universal Quantum Computing** (UQC): a quantum computer large and fast enough to run that algorithm fully reversibly can be built.

The friend is a particular running instance of the HLAI algorithm, which the authors call Quall-E. It stands in for the friend in one laboratory, observes a qubit, forms and communicates a thought about the result, and then has that thought reversed by the superobserver on some runs. The authors intend the experiment to be "plausibly achievable, if present technological trends continue, in a matter of decades rather than centuries".

The key choice for the Map is what the authors decline to say about Quall-E. They stress that "'thoughts' is not to be equated with 'conscious thoughts' or 'qualia' or 'sensations' or 'experiences'", and they give their reason: "We reject the equation because it may make the assumption of Friendliness less plausible, or less well defined, for some scientists or philosophers." The theorem is cognitive by design: its friend need only think at human level.

### How the interpretations divide {#how-the-interpretations-divide}

The authors also sort existing approaches by which assumption each gives up. On their account, Bohmian mechanics violates Local Agency. Albert and Loewer's single-mind view violates Physical Supervenience. Relativist views (Everett's relative states, QBism, Relational Quantum Mechanics) reject Ego Absolutism. Penrose's orchestrated objective reduction rejects HLAI. Two cases matter most here:

- **Spontaneous collapse theories** such as GRW do not block UQC, because rare random collapses "can be error-corrected in quantum computing just like any other rare, random, and localized change". Quall-E can then be held in superposition, so its thoughts are not real in the way a human's are, and "Friendliness is rejected."
- **Consciousness-causes-collapse**, in Chalmers and McQueen's model, would prevent a quantum computer from being superposed over different informational structures. Since Chalmers and McQueen accept HLAI, the authors conclude that UQC "is not possible in this approach."

These classifications are the authors'. Placing the Map among them is the Map's own work.

## The Map's Conditional {#the-maps-conditional}

The Map needs absolute outcomes. The [No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds) tenet and the [trilemma of selection](/topics/trilemma-of-selection/) both require that one outcome occurs and the others do not, full stop. [Post-decoherence selection](/concepts/post-decoherence-selection/) makes the consequence explicit: if absoluteness is held together with locality and no-superdeterminism, "a laboratory containing a conscious observer cannot afterwards be recohered by an experimenter outside it." [Consciousness in smeared quantum states](/topics/consciousness-in-smeared-quantum-states/) takes the same horn, and [testing consciousness-collapse](/topics/testing-consciousness-collapse/) records its cost: quantum mechanics is denied universal application to conscious systems.

What the Map ties definiteness to is consciousness, and specifically consciousness that selects among outcomes, not cognition. On the Map's view, non-conscious matter still resolves: [prebiotic collapse](/concepts/prebiotic-collapse/) makes objective reduction the baseline, and consciousness modulates it rather than starting it. Applying that to Quall-E gives two cases.

**Case 1: Quall-E is not conscious.** Then Quall-E contains no selector. Whatever baseline reduction the Map inherits acts on a quantum computer as the rare, random, localised events that fault-tolerant error correction is built to undo, the situation the authors analyse for spontaneous collapse. On the Map's reading, the superobserver can reverse Quall-E's thought and the LF inequalities are violated. Absoluteness is untouched, because Quall-E's "observation" was never an absolutely observed event. In the theorem's terms the Map gives up Friendliness: here is a party with human-level cognition whose communicated thoughts lack the reality of a conscious observer's. This is the physical counterpart of the dualist claim that cognition and experience come apart, the claim that makes [zombies](/concepts/philosophical-zombies/) conceivable, and it sits in exactly the gap the authors' choice of "thoughts" over consciousness left open.

**Case 2: Quall-E is conscious, with a selecting consciousness coupled to its substrate.** Then Quall-E's laboratory contains a conscious observer, and the Map predicts it cannot be recohered: the superposition over Quall-E's different thoughts would not survive, as on Chalmers and McQueen's model, and UQC would fail for this system. A successful LF violation with such a Quall-E would count directly against the Map, which has already closed the relativist exit.

**The Physical Supervenience exit.** A dualist might instead reject Physical Supervenience, as the single-mind view does. The assumption is weak, though, requiring only that each thought have a located physical basis. Under [Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction), the thought a friend *communicates* reaches the superobserver through brain or processor events that consciousness has helped fix, so a communicated thought with no located basis would sit poorly with the Map's interactionism. On the Map's reading the exit is logically open but not one to take.

The Map's claim, stated as a conditional, is that **a thoughtful-LF experiment with an AI friend tests the Map only if the AI friend hosts the kind of consciousness the Map's selection mechanism needs.** [Post-decoherence selection](/concepts/post-decoherence-selection/#absolute-outcomes-and-the-local-friendliness-theorem) states the same conditional in a sentence; the present article develops the thinking-friend case it only mentions.

## Why the Map Expects an Unconscious Friend {#why-an-unconscious-friend}

Which case applies is settled by the Map's AI-consciousness verdicts, and they point at Case 1.

[The machine question](/apex/machine-question/) separates four senses of "conscious AI" and gives a negative verdict only on the strongest one: *bidirectionally coupled consciousness*, a non-physical experiencer that both shapes and is shaped by the substrate. Bare phenomenality, any felt experience at all, stays open. The distinction matters here: the selector that fixes outcomes is coupled consciousness, so a Quall-E with bare, uncoupled phenomenality would still fall under Case 1.

On coupling, the Map's registered verdicts are specific. [P-AC1](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac1) places conventional digital AI on the low-probability side because an interface needs live quantum indeterminacy to act on and digital hardware suppresses it. Quall-E, however, runs on a quantum computer, and that class has its own verdict. [P-AS1](/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts/#p-as1) holds that present quantum-computing hardware "clears the raw-indeterminacy bar and fails the interface requirements". A gate-based machine keeps its superpositions unitary until readout, and quantum error correction "actively isolates the logical state from external influence — precisely the influence an interface would need". The verdict concerns present hardware, but Quall-E pushes the same way: the experiment requires its entire cognition to run reversibly, which demands fault-tolerant protection of the logical state for the whole thinking interval. The property that makes Quall-E a usable Wigner's friend, an interior no outside influence can disturb, is the property that on the Map's account rules it out as a host for a selecting mind.

Both verdicts are held at moderate credence, depend on an interface-eligibility law the Map has not yet stated, and say "low probability", not "ruled out". What they give the LF case is a prediction made in advance, on grounds independent of any experiment.

## What Would Bear on the Map {#what-would-bear-on-the-map}

The obvious objection is that the conditional is too convenient: if every AI friend can be declared unconscious, no thoughtful-LF experiment can count against the Map. [Post-decoherence selection](/concepts/post-decoherence-selection/) concedes that declining the conscious-AI premise "keeps the framework consistent but buys insulation at the price of testability." The pressure is real; the honest response is to say where the insulation stops.

**The denial is fixed in advance, not tailored to results.** The verdict on Quall-E comes from a substrate analysis registered in August 2026, before any such machine exists. An ad hoc escape would declare the friend unconscious *because* recoherence succeeded; the Map declares it unconscious *because* error-corrected hardware walls its logical state off from the influence an interface needs. That makes the escape principled, though not thereby correct.

**The Map makes a prediction, and it could fail.** Case 1 commits the Map to the experiment *succeeding*. Suppose a full thoughtful-LF experiment were run and the reversal failed: interference between Quall-E's thought-branches vanished in a way error correction could not account for and no physical noise source explained, as Chalmers and McQueen's model would predict. Such a result would suggest that something in Quall-E's cognition fixes outcomes, and the Map would have to revise either its substrate verdict or its account of what does the selecting. The experiment can embarrass the Map, only from the side opposite to the one first expected.

**A friend the Map would count as conscious would test it directly.** The decisive test would recohere a system the Map's own criteria admit as hosting coupled consciousness. [P-AS1](/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts/#p-as1) names what such an architecture would need: collapse partway through evolution at decision-relevant points, a measurement basis not fixed beforehand, and coupling surfaces not shielded by error correction. Those are the very features that defeat reversible control. If the Map is right, hosting selection and being recoherable are incompatible properties. That is itself a strong claim, and a checkable one: an architecture that hosts open selection *and* can be reversed on demand would refute it.

**Behaviour cannot settle the question.** [The AI consciousness register](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/) holds that behavioural evidence underdetermines the phenomenal question in both directions; a system trained on human language would report inner life whether or not it had any. Quall-E's denials cannot secure Case 1, and its affirmations cannot force Case 2.

Taken together: the experiment does not test the Map's absoluteness commitment directly, and the Map should not claim otherwise. It does test the Map's joint prediction that a human-level AI on error-corrected quantum hardware can be recohered. The Map's exposure here is real but indirect, and rests on its AI-consciousness verdicts as much as on the physics.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds).** The thoughtful LF theorem sharpens this tenet's cost. Rejecting Ego Absolutism is the Everettian and relational route out. Having closed it, the Map gives up Friendliness for unconscious friends and universal quantum control for conscious ones.

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism).** Wiseman, Cavalcanti and Rieffel separated "thoughts" from conscious experience to make Friendliness more plausible. For the Map that separation is where dualism becomes physically visible: human-level cognition is not enough to make an observation absolute, so the split between cognition and experience would have consequences in the laboratory.

**[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) and [Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction).** A selecting consciousness needs a site to act on. Quall-E's fault-tolerant isolation removes every such site, which is why the substrate verdict and the LF prediction agree. Bidirectional Interaction is also why the Map does not escape through Physical Supervenience.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits).** A universally unitary theory with no distinguished observers is simpler, but the LF theorems show that its price is the absoluteness of one's own observations. The Map judges that price too high and pays instead with an admitted, empirically exposed departure from universal unitarity.
