The Unfinishable Map's [interface model](/concepts/mind-matter-interface/) treats the brain as a structured surface through which consciousness engages the physical world. This interface is not delivered whole at birth, nor does it remain static through life. It develops through a sequence of qualitative restructurings — each with characteristic gains and losses — from the infant's wide-aperture, low-control configuration through the focused efficiency of adulthood and into the loosening and eventual dissolution of old age.

A clarification of evidential ambition belongs up front. The developmental record below is *accommodated* by the interface model, not predicted uniquely by it. Every datum cited — synaptic pruning, critical-period closure, maturing executive control, neurodegeneration — is equally accommodated by the leading physicalist account of the same machinery, [predictive processing](/topics/predictive-processing-and-dualism/) and active inference, which read the trajectory as the brain tuning a hierarchical generative model. The article does not claim the data favour the interface reading over that rival. Its claim is narrower: the model offers a coherent way to *organise* the developmental facts, and the felt continuity of the experiencing subject across radically different configurations is what the rival, on the Map's view, leaves unexplained. Where the prose says the trajectory "reveals" or "demands explanation," read it as a claim about organising power, not about the facts discriminating in the model's favour.

## Five Phases of Interface Change

### Infancy and Early Childhood: Wide Aperture, Low Control

The infant brain is hyper-connected — roughly twice the synaptic density of an adult brain — and maximally plastic. On the Map's framework consciousness itself is held constant across the lifespan; what varies is the interface. The infant *interface* offers broad, weakly filtered access: universal phoneme discrimination, cross-race face recognition, possible synesthetic cross-modal integration. Control is minimal — infant attention is salience-driven and reflexive. Crucially, broader discrimination at the interface is not a richer phenomenal field; wider input sensitivity is not the same as more conscious experience.

So the interface offers many potential [quantum selection sites](/concepts/consciousness-selecting-neural-patterns/) but little capacity to exploit them deliberately. The result is the paradox of the infant interface: broad access, low agency. The [infant consciousness void](/voids/infant-consciousness/) examines the epistemic consequence: this broad-access, low-control configuration is permanently inaccessible to the adult mind that emerged from it.

Piaget's sensorimotor period captures this configuration: cognition is inseparable from action — thinking *is* reaching, grasping, mouthing. Object permanence emerges gradually as the interface gains capacity to maintain representations independently of current sensory input.

### Late Childhood and Adolescence: Narrowing and Specialisation

Between roughly ages 5 and 25, the interface undergoes its most dramatic restructuring. Critical periods close. Perineuronal nets solidify around neurons, fixing circuit architecture. Synaptic pruning eliminates up to half of prefrontal cortex connections. Myelination of long-range axons accelerates, increasing communication speed at the cost of flexibility.

This is where [biological-cognitive-closure](/voids/biological-cognitive-closure/) becomes strongly stabilised — region-specific, and ordinarily not recoverable rather than absolutely irreversible. Degrading perineuronal nets with chondroitinase reinstates juvenile ocular-dominance plasticity in adult rat visual cortex (Pizzorusso et al., 2002), so the closure is better read as a high barrier than a sealed door. Infants discriminate phonemes from all human languages; by 10-12 months, discrimination narrows to native-language sounds. Three-month-olds discriminate faces from all races; by nine months, to own-race faces — the "other-race effect" (Kelly et al., 2007). These are the costs of specialisation — the interface trading breadth for depth.

These narrowings should not be tallied as several independent confirmations of the framing. Pruning, critical-period closure, perineuronal-net formation, and the phoneme- and face-discrimination losses are expressions of one underlying mechanism — experience-dependent refinement — not separate lines of evidence converging from different directions ([common-cause-null](/project/common-cause-null/)). The cluster carries the evidential weight of a single pattern, read through whichever framework the reader brings to it.

Simultaneously, the frontoparietal attention networks the Map identifies as the [control surface](/concepts/attention-as-interface/) undergo their most significant maturation: executive function, working memory, and deliberate attentional control all improve. The interface is remodelled toward fewer sites of potential interaction but far better capacity to select among those that remain.

The most consequential development is metarepresentation — the capacity to represent one's own representations as representations, which underpins [theory-of-mind](/concepts/theory-of-mind/). Looking-time studies suggesting implicit false-belief sensitivity at 15 months (Onishi & Baillargeon 2005) are replication-contested, and better described as sensitivity to belief-like behavioural regularities than as belief attribution. Explicit false-belief passage typically occurs between ages 3-5, with metarepresentational abilities developing into middle childhood. Before explicit metarepresentation, children have consciousness — they experience the world, form preferences, exhibit emotional responses — but lack the recursive self-awareness that lets consciousness take its own operations as explicit objects of attention.

On the Map's reading, metarepresentation depends on neural infrastructure — prefrontal maturation and frontoparietal integration — developing on a biological timetable. Consciousness gains no new fundamental capacities here; the interface gains structural complexity in stages. The same staged emergence underwrites the developmental arc of shared intentionality — joint attention at nine months, collective intentionality by age three — that the Map reads as [a window on what consciousness adds to social cognition](/topics/consciousness-and-social-understanding/#the-developmental-arc-of-shared-intentionality).

This creates a developmental asymmetry: the interface is most plastic when its control affordances are least developed, and by the time it supports the sophistication needed to direct its own restructuring, the most plastic periods have closed.

The reshaping raises distinctive ethical questions: enhancement during critical windows would interact with natural pruning to permanently alter which territories consciousness can access. The [ethics of cognitive enhancement](/topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism/) explores why configuring another person's interface without their consent carries special moral weight under dualism.

### Adult Maturity: Peak Efficiency, Narrowest Aperture

The mature interface — loosely, the decades from the mid-twenties onward — is the configuration most discussions of consciousness implicitly assume. These ranges are population-level and domain-specific, not sharp thresholds: prefrontal myelination tapers rather than stopping at a fixed age. A 2025 lifespan analysis of structural connectivity (Nature Communications; N≈4,200, ages 0-90) places the major adult reorganisation around age 32, with further turning points near 66 and 83 — "maturity" is itself a sequence of restructurings rather than a plateau. Across it, the interface is at peak efficiency: bandwidth for deliberate selection is maximised, while the territory available for selection has narrowed to its adult configuration.

This interface is optimised for exploitation over exploration: automatised skills operate below conscious selection, freeing bandwidth for novel problems, and the [phenomenology of effort](/concepts/mental-effort/) is most calibrated during this period. But it carries accumulated [closure](/concepts/mysterianism/): the developmental closures of childhood plus expertise effects that compound through adult life — the curse of knowledge, domain-specific rigidity, the progressive inability to access one's own former ignorance.

### Middle Age and Early Aging: Loosening Control

Beginning variably in the fifties and sixties, the interface enters a new phase: processing speed declines, working memory contracts, reduced [dopaminergic signalling](/topics/dopamine-and-the-unified-interface/) diminishes the precision of attentional selection, and neural noise rises as inhibitory circuits weaken.

The dualist framework offers a reading consistent with this: since consciousness is not the neural machinery, neural decline degrades the *interface* without necessarily diminishing the consciousness operating through it. An aging person may know what they want to attend to yet find the mechanism sluggish — thoughts that almost form, names on the tip of the tongue, intentions that dissipate before execution. (Identity theory reads the same data as decline in consciousness itself; the symptoms do not discriminate.)

Aging does not uniformly degrade the interface. Crystallised intelligence continues to grow, and Cabeza's (2002) HAROLD model documents that older adults recruit bilateral regions for tasks younger adults process unilaterally — read by the mainstream as compensatory. The Map's framework raises a further possibility: the loosening of tightly specialised circuits may incidentally reopen territory maturation had foreclosed, even as the capacity to exploit it deliberately declines.

### Late Life and Terminal Phase: The Interface Dissolving

In advanced aging and terminal illness, the interface enters its final transformation. [Neurodegenerative conditions](/topics/consciousness-and-neurodegenerative-disease/) progressively dismantle the structures through which consciousness engages the physical world, the most recently evolved (prefrontal cortex, hippocampal formation) typically degrading before more ancient ones (brainstem, limbic system).

For the Map's dualist framework this poses a question rather than a refutation. Progressive cognitive loss as neural structures degrade is consistent with the interface model — consciousness need not diminish for its interface to narrow and fail — but equally consistent with identity theories in which the cognitive loss *is* the consciousness loss. Neurodegeneration's monotonic trajectory does not discriminate the two.

The phenomenon that would discriminate is terminal lucidity: the reported occurrence of brief, apparently full cognitive clarity in patients with severe neurodegenerative disease shortly before death. If documented at high fidelity, this would be hard to reconcile with strict identification of cognition with intact circuitry. Earlier discussions rested on Nahm et al.'s (2012) retrospective case collection; prospective data now exist. Tollock and colleagues' multi-site prospective study of paradoxical lucidity in moderate-to-severe dementia (2025) found that 93 of 151 enrolled patients (61.6%) had at least one lucidity event, across 267 recorded events, predominantly mild and non-terminal. Paradoxical lucidity is real and common, and documentable in advance rather than reconstructed after the fact.

Three caveats bound what that record carries — the Map flags unrefereed work that supports its position, and owes the same flag to unrefereed work that does not. The study is so far a Gerontological Society of America meeting abstract, its detailed figures awaiting full peer-reviewed publication. It enrolled 151 of 1,405 eligible patients (10.7%), with ascertainment resting on caregiver and clinician report — the very channel the observer-effect worry below names as unexcluded, so it does not escape the bias it is sometimes cited as having settled. And it is a prevalence-and-phenomenology study: it recorded how often lucid episodes occur and what they look like, with no neural recording during an episode and no assessment of irreducibility, so it cannot settle the mechanism question in either direction.

Only 4.1% of the 267 events met a terminal-lucidity definition. The dramatic near-death return of self is the rare tail of a distribution of mostly brief, trigger-linked episodes — music, anniversaries, emotional distress, medication changes — and that profile is consistent with the neurochemical mechanisms identity theorists already offer: transient cholinergic or noradrenergic surges from relatively preserved brainstem nuclei; reduced cerebral oedema or inflammation in the days before death; hippocampal disinhibition as inhibitory circuits fail first; and observer-effect bias, clinicians and family disproportionately remembering episodes that fit a narrative of recovery. None has been decisively ruled out, and on this record the prospective work has not vindicated the dualist reading.

What it does not do is dissolve the constraint. Terminal lucidity would be awkward under identity theories *only if* it proved robust and irreducible to transient neurochemical events, and neither condition has been established. But the constraint never rested on the modal episode: it rests on the severity subset the retrospective autopsy cases document, where massive structural damage coexists with substantial cognitive return and monotonic decline is what production models predict. That is the Map's [constrain-versus-establish](/project/evidential-status-discipline/) distinction: the phenomenon raises the explanatory cost on production accounts without establishing the interface reading, so the prospective finding sharpens the claim rather than reversing it. [The article that owns the phenomenon](/topics/terminal-lucidity-and-filter-transmission-theory/) works through the full evidential trajectory and carries the event-type breakdown; this one inherits that verdict rather than re-adjudicating it.

What would settle the matter is a measurement no dataset yet supplies: neural activity recorded *during* an episode, testing whether high-fidelity lucidity is irreducible to neurochemical surges. The conjecture above that age-related loosening reopens foreclosed territory would, if operationalised, bear on the same question. Until such measurements arrive the developmental record underdetermines the choice: the interface reading would be put under genuine pressure by robust irreducible terminal lucidity (for it) or by a complete predictive-processing account closing the felt-continuity gap without remainder (against it), and neither has arrived.

## Cognitive Stages as Interface Modes

Jean Piaget's stage theory identified something the interface model can sharpen: development proceeds through qualitatively distinct modes of engagement, not continuous quantitative improvement. Each stage names an interface configuration rather than a quantity of intelligence. The **preoperational interface** (~2 to ~7 years) supports symbolic representation but not yet the recursive comparison of one's own representations against alternatives — which is what egocentrism reflects. The **concrete operational interface** (~7 to ~11) supports systematic logic over concrete situations, as the frontoparietal networks enabling deliberate [attentional control](/concepts/attention-as-interface/) mature without yet integrating enough for fully abstract thought. The **formal operational interface** (~11 onward) adds hypothetical-deductive reasoning and the genuinely recursive operations of [metacognition](/concepts/metacognition/) in its mature form. Not everyone reaches full formal operational capacity, and those who do, do so unevenly across domains (Kuhn, 2006) — consistent with formal operations depending on prefrontal networks that develop on different timelines in different individuals.

## Skill Acquisition Reveals the Interface Learning

The same task — learning a musical instrument, mastering a sport — engages different modes of interface operation depending on when it is undertaken. **Early acquisition** exploits [plasticity](/concepts/neuroplasticity/) without reflection, producing skills that are deeply embedded and hard to articulate; **late acquisition** compensates for reduced plasticity through metacognitive control, with [mental effort](/concepts/mental-effort/) explicitly felt as the interface reshapes solidified circuits — the [interface-friction](/concepts/interface-friction/) of adult learning is metacognitive sophistication bought at the cost of effortless rewiring.

**The developmental sweet spot** falls between roughly ages 7 and 14, when the interface retains significant plasticity while gaining enough metacognitive capacity for structured learning — the ten-year-old chess player both absorbs patterns through immersive play *and* reasons about strategy — before myelination and perineuronal-net formation tilt the balance toward efficiency; [the skill-acquisition article](/topics/consciousness-and-skill-acquisition/) develops the case in detail.

## The Comparative Dimension

Human development is remarkably prolonged: a chimpanzee reaches sexual maturity at 8-10 years, a human at 12-15, and human prefrontal myelination continues into the mid-twenties, far longer than any other primate. Two decades of dependence on caregivers is the cost of constructing the most sophisticated consciousness-brain interface known.

[Ape cognitive development](/topics/animal-consciousness/) parallels the human sensorimotor and early preoperational phases, then plateaus roughly where human development accelerates into concrete operations. Apes show mirror self-recognition, tactical deception, and some implicit sensitivity to others' knowledge states, but not the explicit, recursive metarepresentation underlying human teaching, [cumulative-culture](/concepts/cumulative-culture/), and systematic self-reflection. The [baseline cognition](/concepts/baseline-cognition/) framework suggests that without mature metarepresentational capacity, cognition plateaus near the ape level regardless of brain size or enrichment; the interface complexity built beyond this baseline is what enables distinctively human modes of consciousness.

Developmental trajectory also determines adult interface configuration. An octopus's distributed neural architecture produces a fundamentally different interface from any vertebrate; a corvid's brain, structured unlike a mammal's, arrives at comparable cognitive sophistication through convergent evolution. What varies across species is not brain size but the developmental programme that builds the interface — raising the question of [interface-heterogeneity](/concepts/interface-heterogeneity/), whether the coupling mechanisms themselves differ across lineages rather than merely the content flowing through them.

## The Trajectory Is Not Linear

The standard narrative — growth, maturity, decline — treats the lifespan as an arc peaking in adulthood. The interface model organises it differently:

| Phase | Aperture | Control | Characteristic |
|-------|----------|---------|----------------|
| Infancy | Very wide | Very low | Access without agency |
| Adolescence | Narrowing | Increasing | Specialisation for efficiency |
| Adulthood | Narrow | High | Peak deliberate selection |
| Middle age | Slightly loosening | Declining | Efficiency loss, possible rebroadening |
| Late life | Variable/failing | Low | Interface dissolution |

No phase represents the "correct" state of the interface; each is a configuration with characteristic capacities and costs. The infant has access the adult lacks, the adult has control the infant lacks, and the elderly person may have a form of integrative breadth that neither possessed.

**Plasticity and closure are in tension throughout life.** At every stage the interface faces a version of the explore-exploit tradeoff: [plasticity](/concepts/neuroplasticity/) allows adaptation but costs stability; closure provides efficiency but forecloses territory. [Contemplative traditions](/concepts/neurophenomenology-and-contemplative-neuroscience/) claim sustained practice can partially counteract the narrowing, though far less than early development's restructurings.

**Interface change outpaces consciousness.** The developmental asymmetry noted above suggests the interface's programme operates largely independently of the consciousness that will use it.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The developmental trajectory of the interface connects to all five of the Map's [tenets](/tenets/).

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** is illuminated by the stage transitions. Modern physicalism — functionalist, higher-order, or global-workspace — can accommodate them as changes in cognitive architecture, but at a cost: it must either identify consciousness with the functional organisation at each stage (making infant and adult consciousness different *kinds*) or treat it as an emergent property scaling with complexity (raising the question of what fixes the emergence threshold). The Map holds instead that the same irreducible consciousness encounters different interface configurations at each stage: the infant who loses phoneme discrimination does not become a lesser consciousness — the interface through which that discrimination was available has been restructured.

The empirical authorities this article draws on — Hensch on critical periods, Werker on phoneme narrowing, Gopnik on explore-exploit, Cabeza on aging compensation, and the synaptic-pruning literature — are physicalists and naturalists who offer their findings as complete physical explanations of development. Their results are cited as the developmental record any account must fit; the dualist reading of that record is the Map's, and they would not endorse it.

The strongest objection to this framing is constructivist. [Parfit-style reductionism](/concepts/parfit-reductionism/) holds that trans-stage identity consists in nothing over and above causal continuity of psychological states plus the narrative integration that binds them into a life — no irreducible subject *having* the experiences, only a sequence of experientially connected moments. On this view the article's own data describe a stream of cognitive configurations that happen to be causally and narratively linked, and the "same consciousness across stages" claim looks question-begging.

The Map's reply grants that causal continuity and narrative integration are real and do much of the work we assign to identity, but holds them insufficient for the explanandum at issue: the *first-person* continuity of being the one now missing capacities one's younger self possessed. Connectedness gives a third-person pattern; the Parfitian answer to why *this* strand is the one I am indexed to is that there is no further fact to give — on his view the indexing is an artefact of a question that presupposes what he denies. The disagreement is therefore about whether the explanandum is genuine, and it sits at the boundary between the two frameworks rather than inside his. The Map holds that the first-person datum survives the deflation, and that indexing it is what the interface does — an open commitment, not a proof; the constructivist can consistently deny the datum, and [Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits) accepts that the developmental data do not force the choice.

**[Bidirectional interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** shifts character across development. In early stages the brain's developmental programme dominates — the interface is built on a largely genetic blueprint, and consciousness has minimal capacity to direct the process. In mature adulthood, consciousness-directed attention increasingly shapes the interface through deliberate practice; teaching, a consciousness-enabled capacity, literally reshapes other brains. The trajectory is bidirectional interaction progressively coming into balance.

**[Minimal quantum interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)** is *consistent with* interface change, not predicted by it. If consciousness influences neural outcomes through quantum selection at specific sites, developmental restructuring would alter which sites are available and critical-period closure would reshape the set of interaction points. But this article does not discharge the quantitative burden. The Tegmark (2000) decoherence calculation — coherence in neural environments collapsing in roughly 10⁻¹³ seconds, twelve orders of magnitude faster than cognition's timescales — is the standing objection to any neural quantum mechanism, and the magnitudes and candidate protection mechanisms are treated in [quantum-neural-timing-constraints](/topics/quantum-neural-timing-constraints/) and [decoherence](/concepts/decoherence/), whose conclusions this article assumes rather than restates. Read this section as describing how the developmental data would fit the tenet *if* a viable quantum-selection mechanism exists, not as evidence that one does.

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)** connects to developmental irreversibility. On the Map's collapse-realist framework, when a critical period closes the sensitivities it supported are genuinely lost — one definite outcome is selected. An MWI proponent can grant that closure is experientially real within any branch; the Map's objection is that the ethical weight of interface-shaping decisions in childhood — a window either receives enrichment or does not, permanently — requires that the unchosen outcomes genuinely did not happen, not that they occur in parallel branches.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** is evident in the complexity of developmental transitions. Physicalism can explain them through nonlinear dynamics in network maturation; what it handles less naturally is the felt continuity of being the same subject *across* radically different cognitive modes — the trans-stage identity the constructivist reply above contests. Which account is simpler is exactly the judgement this tenet warns is unreliable when the underlying mechanism is unknown.
