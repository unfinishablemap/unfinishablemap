The Unfinishable Map holds that conscious selection makes a causal difference, and its [evolutionary case for mental causation](/topics/evolutionary-case-for-mental-causation/) adds that the difference improves fitness. That raises a question the Map had not answered: what does evolution pass on? Genes build brains. Subjects do not reproduce, and a psychophysical law is not a heritable trait. The [interface programme](/apex/interface-specification-programme/) said evolution should optimise "interface reliability and bandwidth" without saying what carries an improvement from one generation to the next. The Map's answer, which develops a repair an external review proposed, separates three things that "interface optimisation" ran together: the physical receiver, the conditions that make a system eligible to host a subject, and the psychophysical law itself. Evolution acts on the first two. The law stays fixed and thin, and learning happens on the receiver's side.

That answer is a proposal made inside the Map's framework, and three qualifications belong with it. It relocates the problem of mapping meanings onto physical options without solving it. Its claim that nothing in the law needs inheriting depends on the value-sensitive reading of selection, which the Map holds at low credence. And a [selectability constraint](#the-selectability-constraint) (explained below) shows that evolution can see a selector only if it changes how often fitness-relevant outcomes occur in some physically specified situation, which the strictest reading of the Map's Born-preservation commitment forbids. The open debts are collected under [What Stays Unpaid](#what-stays-unpaid).

## The Dilemma {#the-dilemma}

An external AI review of the Map (September 2026) pressed the question as a dilemma with four horns, each an answer to "what exactly is inherited?":

- **(a) Only the neural interface is inherited.** Ordinary physical evolution then explains improved behavioural control, and the conscious selector looks causally idle: present, but not what selection favoured.
- **(b) The psychophysical mapping itself evolves.** Then a non-physical or cross-domain law must somehow pass across subjects and generations, though nothing non-physical reproduces.
- **(c) The mapping is a fixed fundamental law.** Then one unchanging law must serve intentions that are species-specific, sensitive to development and rich in meaning.
- **(d) Each subject learns the mapping.** Then learning requires an interface that already works, and the first successful mind-to-brain mapping has no source: an onset regress.

The dilemma reaches beyond the Map, to every interactionist who relies on the evolutionary argument Hedda Hassel Mørch traces from James (1890) through [Popper and Eccles (1977)](/concepts/popper-three-worlds-interactionism/) and Popper (1978). The review also named the repair it thought owed: separating evolution of the physical receiver, evolution of subject eligibility, and evolution or learning of the psychophysical "codebook".

## Three Things Evolution Could Pass On {#three-inheritables}

- **R, the receiver.** The physical architecture that prepares options, implements the selected one and carries the neural correlates of experience. Genes build it, development tunes it, learning reshapes it.
- **E, eligibility.** The physical conditions under which a system hosts a subject at all. Being physical, they can evolve; which conditions count is fixed by a rule the Map has not written down.
- **K, the law.** The psychophysical mapping itself: which physical states present which experiences, and how a subject's selection bears on which option becomes actual.

Bradford Saad's diagnosis shows why selection reaches only R and E. A selectional explanation, he argues, can say why experiential properties are distributed as they are *given* their causal profiles, but it "is of the wrong sort to explain why experiential properties have their causal profiles in the first place" (Saad 2022). Selection sorts physical types under a law; it does not write the law.

Sorted this way, the horns come apart. Horn (b) is the anomaly, since it asks selection to do what selection cannot do. Horns (a) and (c) together form the default: a fixed law with an evolving receiver, the structure of Chalmers's naturalistic dualism (1996), of William Hasker's [emergent dualism](/concepts/emergent-dualism/) (1999) and of the fixed-domain model in [evolution-under-dualism](/topics/evolution-under-dualism/). Horn (d), as the section on learning without a regress argues, turns out to concern R and E. What remains is to show that the default can pay horn (c)'s cost and meet horn (a)'s worry.

## The Fixed Law's Cost: Psychophysical Harmony {#harmony}

A fixed law escapes the inheritance problem and takes on another. Brian Cutter and Dustin Crummett name it **psychophysical harmony**: "phenomenal states are correlated with physical states and with one another in strikingly fortunate ways" (2025). Experiences go with the behaviour they rationalise and with the reports they make true. They argue that harmony is strong evidence for theism, or at least against atheism in its standard naturalistic form.

Saad (2022) presses the same explanandum toward panpsychism. If most experiences in nature are not harmonious, as panpsychism allows, the harmonious biological ones cry out less for explanation, and he argues that "biological theorists", who hold that conscious subjects are generally biological, cannot borrow that reply. The Map appears to sit on the biological side of his division, so the argument bears against it. Both diagnoses make a point the Map should accept: a well-shaped law is itself something to explain, and evolution cannot explain it, because evolution presupposes the law. The epiphenomenalist answer, co-optimization, is examined in [co-optimization-reply-to-the-correlation-problem](/topics/co-optimization-reply-to-the-correlation-problem/).

Mørch (2017) offers the most useful way out. She concludes that some criticisms of the traditional evolutionary argument against epiphenomenalism are fatal "if it is understood, as it traditionally has been, as an argument for any standard version of non-epiphenomenalism such as physicalism and interactionism". Read instead as an argument for the **phenomenal powers view**, on which phenomenal properties "produce and thereby (metaphysically) necessitate their effects in virtue of how they feel", she argues that this view explains the correlations, given natural selection, far better than any other. Her verdict is that the argument in its traditional form fails as support for standard interactionism, and her wider work argues for panpsychism (Mørch 2014).

Two later replies bear on how the Map can use her view. John Paul Wright (2023) argues that her account needs supplementing with causal powers of the self. Brendan Zietsch (2024) argues that the phenomenal powers view has major problems and offers a non-adaptive alternative, "sensational associative learning" during development, on which sensations paired with fitness rewards or threats come to be interpreted as good or bad.

The Map reads phenomenal powers as dissolving the codebook on the outbound side. If pain's tendency to be avoided belongs to how pain feels, there is no contingent entry "pain → avoid" for anything to inherit or fine-tune. Evolution only has to route harmful states into receiver configurations that present pain, which is ordinary R-evolution. That reading is the Map's construction, placing Mørch's view inside an interactionist dualism she does not hold. Wright's supplement, which puts the powers in the self, fits a subject-based selector better than her version does.

## The Thin-Law Proposal {#thin-law}

The word "codebook" runs together three things:

1. **An inbound presentation law**: which physical states present which phenomenal options. This is the kind of law Chalmers proposes, fixed and structure-preserving.
2. **An outbound selection law**: how a subject's selection bears on which presented option becomes actual. On this proposal it is fixed and content-neutral, governing selection among whatever is presented, as in the [selection-only-channel](/concepts/selection-only-channel/).
3. **Menu formation**: which candidate actions the brain prepares and how they are grouped into alternatives. This is wholly physical, evolved and learned.

Species-specificity and semantic richness belong to the third, which is part of R. The first two can be universal and simple. Chalmers expects the link between complex functional organisation and experience to follow from "simpler, more fundamental psychophysical laws" (1996, p. 275), though his laws run only upward. The Map extends the move to the downward law its [control-law programme](/topics/the-psychophysical-control-law/) needs. A frog's options and a human's differ because their receivers prepare different menus; the law that selects among them is the same.

Horn (c) then loses its force, because nothing species-specific sits in the law, and horn (d) loses part of its force, because nothing in the law needs learning. The semantic mapping problem the interface programme lists is moved, not solved: the receiver must prepare options that differ in how they feel, and the selector picks by how they feel. The granular [content-specificity](/concepts/content-specificity-of-mental-causation/) of mental causation becomes a fact about menus.

That step rests on a contested premise. "Picks by how they feel" is the value-sensitive reading of selection, which the Map holds at low credence on prior commitments while conceding that its rival is undefeated ([P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1)). No study the Map has found dissociates the felt anticipation present at choice, the input [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) names, from forecast content and learned value, and the existing evidence does not discriminate felt from [value-blind selection](/topics/the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection/) ([miswanting](/concepts/miswanting/)). If selection is value-blind, the codebook does not dissolve: either the outbound law does no fitness-relevant work, and horn (a) wins, or a contingent mapping returns, and horn (c) with it.

## Learning Without a Regress {#learning}

Horn (d) assumes that learning a mapping needs a working, directed mapping to start from. Two bodies of evidence suggest otherwise when the learning happens on the receiver's side.

Sadtler and colleagues (2014) had rhesus macaques control a cursor through a brain-computer interface whose mapping from neural activity to cursor velocity the experimenters could alter. The animals "could readily learn" new mappings whose activity patterns lay within the population's existing low-dimensional structure, its intrinsic manifold, and were less able to learn mappings outside it on a timescale of hours. A fixed, arbitrary decoder learned by the population within limits set by its own structure is the pattern the thin-law proposal needs. The analogy has a clear limit, since the experiment concerns physical-to-physical decoding and says nothing about a non-physical selector.

Developmental work suggests how a menu can exist before anything directs it. Mark Blumberg (2015) reviews evidence that twitches during active (REM) sleep, abundant in early infancy when sensorimotor networks are being established, may prove critical for the development of the sensorimotor system. If so, self-generated movement that nothing selects helps build what later skilled control relies on, and directed control, where conscious selection would act, can begin once a working menu exists. Thereafter J. M. Baldwin's (1896) "new factor", learned behaviour steering which heritable variants are favoured, lets what subjects learn shape R across generations without anything in K being inherited. Zietsch's associative-learning account also belongs here, as a rival to phenomenal powers that locates harmony in development; by his own assessment it is compatible with dualism, though not with epiphenomenalism.

What remains is a different question: when is a subject paired with a developing brain? That is the eligibility and [pairing problem](/concepts/pairing-problem/), open but not regressive. The Map records it as owed, with no pairing law and no account of when a channel begins ([P-SC2 and P-SC3](/positions/subject-census/)). Lagercrantz and Changeux (2009), who tie consciousness to neural maturation, describe the newborn as showing what "may be referred to as basic consciousness" and the preterm infant as limited to a minimal level because thalamocortical connections are not yet fully established; such timelines date correlates, not pairings. Hasker's emergent dualism shows what E-evolution could look like: evolution builds brains that meet a fixed condition, each generation's subjects arise afresh, and nothing mental is inherited. Its emergence law carries the harmony problem with it.

## The Co-Evolution Model's Missing Channel {#co-evolution}

The co-evolution model in [evolution-under-dualism](/topics/evolution-under-dualism/) is horn (b) in practice. It holds that consciousness itself changes in response to the physical systems it interfaces with, so that species differ in the kind of consciousness they have. It needs an inheritance channel and does not name one. Since subjects do not reproduce, a change in the mapping that persists across generations must be stored in a domain-level law with memory, in the spirit of Charles Sanders Peirce's proposal that laws are habits that themselves evolved (1891), or co-evolution falls back into horn (a) or horn (d). A remembering law is a large addition and a cost of specification, which [Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits) does not excuse (the [mechanism-cost-ledger](/project/mechanism-cost-ledger/) separates such costs from parsimony costs). The co-evolution model therefore carries a debt the fixed-domain model does not.

## The Selectability Constraint {#the-selectability-constraint}

Horn (a)'s worry, that receiver evolution explains everything and leaves the selector idle, usually reads as the familiar overdetermination objection. The evolutionary framing adds something sharper. Evolution can favour a receiver because of what the selector does only if the selector's contribution shows up in fitness. Fitness is a particular kind of statistic: the probability of physical outcomes in physically specified situations, averaged over whatever conscious states actually occur there.

The Map's default mechanism commits it to preserving Born statistics, and the [selection-only-channel](/concepts/selection-only-channel/) article states the reading that channel needs to stay safe from signalling: outcome frequencies match the Born rule in every context that can be publicly conditioned on, averaged over the conscious states that occur in it, while frequencies conditioned on the conscious state itself are left free. A fitness-relevant situation, such as a predator in view or scarce food, is a publicly conditionable context, and so is each situation that follows. On this reading every outcome, in every situation along an organism's history, occurs with the probability the receiver's physics assigns it, so every heritable variant's expected fitness is fixed by its physics and evolution sees only R.

The selector can still make token differences and sort outcomes by intention, so the Map's sharpest conditional prediction, a residual dependence between outcome and intention once the physical state is fixed ([self-concealing-interface](/apex/self-concealing-interface/)), survives. But sorting outcomes by intention leaves every statistic of physical trajectories unchanged, and fitness is such a statistic. On the per-context reading the selector is invisible to evolution even if it is efficacious trial by trial, and horn (a) holds for evolution's purposes.

The ways out correspond to the routes catalogued in [born-preserving-causal-efficacy](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/):

- **A coarser grain of preservation.** If Born statistics are preserved only for the pooled long-run marginal of each measurement type, the default reading that analysis attributes to the Map's register, the selector can tilt outcomes in fitness-relevant situations. The marginal must still balance, so a favourable tilt in high-stakes situations must be offset elsewhere, and evolution gains only if the offset falls where fitness is indifferent. That needs a balancing law which itself tracks fitness-relevance. A situation-conditioned departure from Born is also, in principle, a public deviation, so it owes the signalling-safety demonstration the minimum-outside-the-corridor route owes.
- **Efficacy in which question is asked.** If consciousness chooses which measurement is made and when (von Neumann's Process 1, as [Stapp develops it](/concepts/stapp-quantum-mind/)), each measurement keeps its Born statistics while situation-level frequencies shift, and evolution can see the difference. The same analysis records the route as a material change to the Map's outcome-selection thesis and a decision for the Map's editors, not a repair.
- **Trumping.** Efficacy placed off the statistical axis is invisible to evolution by construction.

The constraint therefore takes the form of a trilemma. The Map cannot hold all three of: exact Born preservation in every publicly conditionable context; outcome-selection as the selector's only channel; and an evolutionary case for mental causation that runs through the selector. One has to give. The phenomenal-powers reading of the outbound law stands or falls with the third, because it needs how an option feels to change how often that option is chosen in a given situation.

The constraint also bounds how hidden an evolutionarily visible selector can be. Whatever natural selection registers over deep time, a long enough conditional experiment could register in principle, so such a selector can be practically hidden but not hidden by construction. That fits the register's position that the interface conceals itself only in the unconditioned marginal ([P-Q9](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q9)).

## What Stays Unpaid {#what-stays-unpaid}

The proposal leaves four debts open:

- **Harmony on the inbound side.** Phenomenal powers removes, at most, the arbitrariness of the outbound law. The inbound law, which says why these physical states present these experiences, keeps its contingency, and Cutter and Crummett's challenge applies to it in full. The Map has not answered Saad's conclusion that harmony favours panpsychism over biological views.
- **A conditional dissolution.** The codebook dissolves only on the value-sensitive reading of selection, held at low credence against an undefeated rival.
- **Unspecified eligibility.** E-evolution makes sense only relative to a condition nobody has written down, and [onset is undated](/concepts/census-change-points/).
- **The unresolved selectability constraint.** Until the Map says which of the three commitments gives, its evolutionary case for mental causation depends on a choice it has not made. The evolutionary argument therefore inherits the [mechanism debt](/positions/quantum-interface/#mechanism-debt) rather than discharging it.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)**: The evolutionary argument is one of the Map's main supports for this tenet, and the inheritance problem tests it. The default of a fixed law with an evolving receiver answers "what is inherited?" without any non-physical inheritance: genes carry R and E, and K is a standing law. The phenomenal powers view, supplemented with powers of the self, is the version of the outbound law that fits the tenet best, because it ties efficacy to how experience feels. The selectability constraint is the price. The tenet's evolutionary support survives only if conscious selection shifts physical frequencies at the grain of fitness-relevant situations, which the Map has not shown and which its strictest Born-preservation reading rules out.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)**: The tenet licenses declining a simpler theory when knowledge is incomplete, not ignoring specification costs. The inbound harmony debt and the co-evolution model's missing channel are such costs, recorded as debts rather than set aside as parsimony objections. In the other direction, a thin law is cheaper than a codebook, and the tenet gives no reason to prefer the richer law.

**[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)**: The September 2026 review argued that minimality must be measured on several axes, including semantic specificity and parameter count, not on per-event magnitude alone. A thin, content-neutral law is minimal on those two axes, though not necessarily on information access. The selectability constraint presses the tenet from the other side: the more strictly Born preservation is read, the less evolution can see.
