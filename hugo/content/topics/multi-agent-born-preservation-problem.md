The Unfinishable Map's default reading of its quantum-interface tenets — *corridor dualism* — holds that a conscious agent selects which Born-weighted outcome actualises on a single measurement while the agent's long-run statistics stay exactly |⟨φ|ψ⟩|². That construction is built for **one** agent. Born statistics, however, are not only single-agent marginals: for entangled systems they are *joint correlations*, and they carry a *no-signalling* structure that spans agents, including spacelike-separated ones. Whether N independent corridor selectors can jointly preserve all of that — while each selection stays genuinely free and the interaction stays minimal — is a constraint the Map's formalism has never written down. This article states the problem, shows where the Map's existing multi-mind answer runs out, and lays out the option space honestly.

Two calibrations belong in this opening rather than after it. First, the *multi-agent Born-preservation problem* is a Map-internal synthesis, not a named problem in the external literature: the components — no-signalling constraints on probability assignments, intersubjective agreement, nested-observer consistency — are well studied, but their assembly into a preservation constraint that scales with the number of selectors is the Map's own framing. Second, the honest verdict defended throughout is that joint-level preservation across genuinely plural agents is currently *unspecified* in the Map's framework: an open debt in the same register as the [mechanism debt](/positions/quantum-interface/#mechanism-debt), not a solved case and not a refutation.

## Three Preservation Demands, Not One {#three-demands}

Corridor dualism for one agent is the working hypothesis of [the Born-rule article](/topics/born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface/): on measurement of |ψ⟩ in basis {|φᵢ⟩}, the agent's selection realises outcome *i* on a given trial, and over the agent's own long run P(i) converges to |⟨φᵢ|ψ⟩|². The influence is ensemble-invisible by construction.

Generalising to N agents raises three demands that are not reducible to the first:

**1. Marginal preservation** — the solved case. Each agent's own unconditioned long-run statistics stay Born. Corridor dualism delivers this by definition; [the agency budget](/concepts/agency-budget/) shows formally that marginal preservation is even compatible with reasons-correlated selection.

**2. Joint-correlation preservation** — the new demand. For an entangled multi-party state, the Born rule prescribes specific *joint* statistics: a Bell pair supports CHSH correlations up to 2√2, a GHZ triple supports perfect three-way correlations. If Alice freely selects her outcome and Bob freely selects his, their joint distribution must still equal |ψ|² on the composite system. Two independent selectors, each preserving only its own marginal, are not thereby guaranteed to reproduce the right correlations — and getting the correlations wrong is empirically detectable in a way marginal deviations are not.

**3. No-signalling preservation** — the causality demand. The marginals of an entangled state must be independent of the far party's measurement choice. If agents' selections are jointly constrained so that correlations come out Born, that constraint must not become a channel by which Alice's choice shifts Bob's marginal — otherwise the corridor is a superluminal signalling mechanism. Torres Alegre's causal-consistency result (2025, an unrefereed arXiv preprint — a theorem within generalised probabilistic theories, conditional on the purification axiom) gives this demand its sharpest form: within that class, the Born rule is the *unique* probability assignment compatible with no-signalling, so any joint deviation is not merely detectable but a relativistic-causality violation. The framework treatment is in [causal-consistency-constraint](/concepts/causal-consistency-constraint/).

The tension is that demands 2 and 3 are *cross-agent* constraints, sometimes across spacelike separation, while corridor freedom was defined agent-by-agent. No-signalling is inherently a multi-party axiom: it is exactly the structure that a plurality of independent selectors could break even while every one of them preserves its own marginal.

## Where the Interface-Locality Answer Runs Out

The Map has an existing answer to multi-observer worries, and it does real work. [The multi-mind collapse problem](/concepts/multi-mind-collapse-problem/) resolves the question of *whose* consciousness collapses a shared system: no one's. Each consciousness selects only within its own brain; systems outside any neural interface reach definite outcomes through baseline objective collapse. When Alice and Bob watch the same photon, its position was fixed physically before reaching either brain, and each mind then selects only among its own neural representations of an already-settled fact.

That answer covers the generic case of the present problem too, and the coverage should be stated plainly before the residue is. In an ordinary Bell experiment the entangled photons decohere and settle at the *detectors*, outside both corridors. The cross-agent correlation is carried entirely by the physics; each agent's corridor acts downstream, inside one brain, on candidate sets conditioned on a locally recorded outcome. Joint Born statistics are then physics' business, and per-brain marginal preservation is all the corridors owe. Interface locality converts the multi-agent problem from ubiquitous to boundary-case.

Three boundary cases survive the conversion, and they are where the problem lives.

**Cross-brain entanglement.** If quantum indeterminacy ever penetrates to two agents' neural interfaces *while still entangled across them* — the brain-to-brain entanglement scenario the multi-mind article itself flags as a falsifier — then two corridors act on one joint state, and demands 2 and 3 bind the selections directly. Nothing in the corridor formalism says what happens then. No evidence indicates such entanglement exists at consciousness-relevant scales, so this case is currently hypothetical; but a framework that claims lawlike generality owes an answer in principle, because "the case never arises" is an empirical bet about neural physics, not a theorem.

**Nested agents.** In Wigner's-friend scenarios the measured system *contains* another selector: the friend's brain, corridor included, is the superposition Wigner measures. Here interface locality cannot help, because the boundary between "agent" and "measured system" is exactly what the scenario crosses. The Frauchiger-Renner no-go theorem (2018) shows that three assumptions — quantum theory's universal applicability (Q), a single actual outcome (S), and consistency of agents reasoning about each other's predictions (C) — cannot jointly hold; the paper's title states the moral: "Quantum theory cannot consistently describe the use of itself." The Map keeps S, so it must restrict Q or C like every single-world reading. Polychronakos (2024) argues the restriction can be principled: observers who will themselves be subject to measurements in superpositions of macroscopic states ("cat" measurements) "cannot make reliable predictions on the results of experiments performed after such measurements," and Born reasoning restricted to non-cat-measured agents is consistent. Bong et al. (2020) derived Bell-type "local friendliness" inequalities showing the nested-observer tension is theorem-grade, not verbal. The multi-agent problem inherits this hazard rather than creating it — unrestricted cross-agent Born reasoning is already inconsistent inside orthodox quantum mechanics — but a corridor account must still say *whose selection is authoritative* when one selector sits inside another's measured system, and it currently does not.

**The composition question.** Even in the generic case, the guarantee that N marginal-preserving corridors compose into one globally Born-consistent history rests on the architectural assumption that selections never touch shared indeterminacy — which in turn rests on baseline objective collapse fixing everything outside brains. That baseline is itself a contested empirical bet (GRW/CSL parameter space is being narrowed by experiment; gravitational collapse is unconfirmed). The composition guarantee is inherited from an unproven mechanism, not derived within the corridor formalism. No formal demonstration exists either way of whether two independent corridor selectors acting on a shared entangled state would generically fail joint-correlation preservation without coordination; a two-agent, one-Bell-pair toy model would settle the shape of the problem and is currently missing — the N≥2 extension of the missing toy model that [P-Q10 records](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/) for the single-agent case.

## The Option Space

Four ways of meeting, or dissolving, the joint demands are visible from the current corpus. Each has a cost the Map should own rather than blur.

**(a) Global harmonisation.** Posit a constraint coordinating all agents' selections so that joint statistics come out Born. The cost is that lawlike cross-agent coordination — binding spacelike-separated free selections — has the structure of superdeterminism's measurement-dependence, and it multiplies with agent number. Brownstein's preprint on qualia efficacy (2025, not peer-reviewed, argued within a de Broglie-Bohm-style collapse framing) illustrates the scaling honestly from the neighbouring route: there, keeping qualia causally efficacious Born-consistently requires fine-tuned initial conditions, and whatever tuning one selector needs, N selectors need jointly. The defence available is the same one the Map gives for [psychophysical laws](/concepts/psychophysical-laws/) generally — a lawlike regularity is not a conspiracy — but whether a law that makes N independent selectors jointly Born-consistent is explanatory or merely stipulated is open.

**(b) Joint-level idleness.** Let the selections wash out jointly as well as marginally, so the multi-agent channel does no distinctive work. This is the safe answer and the costly one: it deepens [ensemble-level epiphenomenalism](/concepts/ensemble-level-epiphenomenalism/) from the single-agent to the joint level, widening the strongest live challenge the framework already faces. Notably, the formal machinery the Map uses to price single-agent concealment cannot yet price this: the agency budget's coupling framework has, by its own account, no notion of measurement context, so its no-signalling compliance "would need ... argued rather than assumed." The joint-level budget is unwritten.

**(c) Relational dissolution.** Deny that there is a single agent-independent joint distribution to preserve. [QBism](/concepts/qbism/) takes this route explicitly: on Schack's treatment of intersubjective agreement (2024), "there is never a necessity for two agents to agree on their respective measurement outcomes" — agreement is a goal an agent can create conditions for, not a formal consequence. [Relational quantum mechanics](/concepts/relational-quantum-mechanics/) relativises outcomes to observers; the many-minds interpretation of Albert and Loewer (1988) lets correlations between one observer's minds and another's arise only when the observers interact, at the price of a continuous infinity of minds per observer ([treated in full separately](/topics/many-minds-interpretation/)). These dissolutions genuinely remove demands 2 and 3. Their shared cost is the Map's own commitments: observer-independent facts, a single actual world, and the indexical identity that the No Many Worlds tenet protects. Many-minds is instructive precisely as the interpretation that took multi-agent correlation most seriously — and as a demonstration of how much machinery a real solution seems to demand.

**(d) A single global experiencer.** If the "many agents" are facets of one experiencer that selects the joint outcome at once, correlations and no-signalling are trivially preserved. The Map's work on [phenomenal non-compositionality](/concepts/composition-and-consciousness/) and [quantum holism](/topics/quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity/) keeps this metaphysically adjacent rather than absurd. But the move dissolves the plurality that generated the problem, and it sits badly with the everyday datum of distinct conscious persons. The philosophically interesting question is whether genuine agent-plurality can be reconciled with joint Born preservation *without* collapsing into (d) — and that question is exactly what remains unanswered.

## What the Aggregate Record Shows

Micro-psychokinesis experiments are the multi-agent aggregate case par excellence: many subjects, many trials, one shared prediction. Bösch, Steinkamp and Boller's meta-analysis of 380 intention-on-random-number-generator studies (2006) found a very small, extremely heterogeneous effect with the signature of publication bias; Maier, Dechamps and Pflitsch's preregistered Bayesian study (2018) found evidence favouring the null. Persistent nulls are what corridor dualism predicts — no ensemble signature, single-agent or joint — and what any gross multi-agent Born violation forbids. The calibration to state with the record rather than after it: these nulls are *consistency*, not corroboration, since every no-influence view predicts them identically; and on the joint demands specifically, nothing has come in — no experiment has probed correlated selection across agents at the quantum grain, which is weaker than the demands having survived a test. The empirical framing is developed in [brain-internal-born-rule-testing](/topics/brain-internal-born-rule-testing/) and [the parapsychology firewall](/topics/parapsychology-firewall/).

## Relation to Site Perspective

The Map interprets this problem as the price of taking its own tenets seriously at N > 1, and registers it as a debt rather than resolving it by fiat.

**[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)** is where the constraint bites hardest. The tenet's empirical corridor was specified against single-agent marginals; demands 2 and 3 show the corridor's wall is really a multi-party structure. A selection law that preserved marginals but broke joint correlations would be detectable, and one that preserved correlations by cross-agent coordination would flirt with the non-minimal, spacelike-reaching interaction the tenet forbids. The multi-agent case does not refute the tenet; it shows the tenet's "minimal" has an unstated joint clause that no Map document yet writes down.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** inherits the scaled version of its standing caveat. Everything here is governed by the mechanism-debt citation grade: the single-agent selection being generalised is itself citable only as framework-internal coherence, not established mental causation, and the joint-level constraint adds a new unpaid item to that same ledger rather than discharging any old one.

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)** fixes which horn of Frauchiger-Renner the Map must take. Keeping a single actual outcome (S) means restricting universal applicability (Q) or cross-agent reasoning (C); a Polychronakos-style restriction on Born reasoning for cat-measured observers is the congenial route, and its cost — conceding that cross-agent Born reasoning is not unrestrictedly valid — should be owned explicitly. The relational dissolutions in option (c) are cheaper on physics and dearer on this tenet, which is why the Map declines them.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** binds symmetrically, as always: the Map cannot dismiss option (a)'s coordinating law as unparsimonious while defending dualism against the same weapon. What it can say is that the law is currently unstated, which is a different defect than being complex.

The Map speculates that the eventual answer runs through the composition question: if baseline objective collapse confines shared indeterminacy outside all corridors, joint preservation reduces to marginal preservation everywhere it is tested, and the residue is confined to the hypothetical cross-brain and nested cases. That is a conjecture about where the problem's weight sits, not a solution to it — and it makes the two-agent Bell-pair toy model the single highest-value piece of missing work.
