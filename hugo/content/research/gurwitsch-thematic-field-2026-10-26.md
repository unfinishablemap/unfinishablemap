## Executive Summary

Gurwitsch's field of consciousness has three domains: the **theme** (focal, organised by Gestalt-coherence), the **thematic field** (co-present data organised by material *relevancy* to the theme), and the **margin** (co-present data with no relevancy to the theme). Both Map hosts state the triad correctly. The premise they build on, "making the margin thematic creates a new margin", is **not a sentence of Gurwitsch's that this research could locate; it is an entailment** of two theses he does hold and that the editorial apparatus of his own works states explicitly: (i) marginality is relational ("What is marginal is so in respect of some theme", Zaner 2010: xxviii), and (ii) three "marginal constants" (the stream of consciousness, embodied existence, the perceptual world) are co-present with whatever the theme is (Embree 1985: xxxi–xxxii, xlv). The margin is **organised** (Zaner: the margin "is by no means unorganized"), but by the internal order of those three domains rather than by relevancy to the theme, which is what the host's "different organisational principles" sentence needs. Marginal items **can** become thematic; when they do the item changes qualitatively and the old theme recedes to field or margin. Gurwitsch's correction of James is verified at the editorial level: James's "margin" (the transitive parts, the fringe) needs a further distinction "between 'thematic field' and 'margin' proper" (Zaner 2010: xxvii); the 2010 edition's Part Five opens with "§ I. James's Concept of 'Fringes'" (p. 301). The subject is a 450-page book plus a 200-page secondary monograph, but the Map-relevant payload is three sentences, and `concepts/` has two slots; a fold is the right size.

### Zaner, R. M. (2010), "Editorial Introduction" to the above, pp. xv–xl
- **URL**: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bfm:978-90-481-3346-8/1.pdf (free front-matter PDF; text extracted and grep-verified)
- **Key points** (all with page):
  - The noematic field "has three basic components: a central 'theme,' a background 'field,' and a surrounding 'margin'" (xxvi).
  - Gurwitsch's analysis proceeds by "modifications of James's theory of 'focus'" ("topic" / "substantive" part) and "margin" ("object" / "transitive" part): "To James, he insists that the 'margin' itself shows a further distinction: between 'thematic field' and 'margin' proper" (xxvi–xxvii). To the Gestaltists he adds the margin and a richer figure/ground account.
  - Thematic field = context organised by "relations of material relevancy"; the theme has a "positional index" that varies with the field (xxvii).
  - "The loss of this relevancy is thus equivalent to the theme's now receding into the margin" — the former theme "becomes part of the sphere now irrelevant to what has in the meantime become thematically focused" (xxvii–xxviii). The margin is the sphere of what is merely "copresent" but "irrelevant" (xxviii); Zaner's example: attending to Napoleon (theme) as victor at Jena (field), while room noise, hunger and stray recollections are marginal "fringe" affairs "having no relevance to either" (xxviii — note Zaner's own loose use of "fringe" for marginal data).
  - "the 'margin' is by no means unorganized"; marginal items are "structured and unified" and "could become themes — a point he goes to some lengths to emphasize in Field of Consciousness as well" (xxviii).
  - "What is marginal is so in respect of some theme; what is field (context) is so in respect of some theme" (xxviii).
  - Gurwitsch against Husserl/Stumpf on singling out: an item singled out does not remain phenomenally the same; "a new theme, results from such isolation" (Gurwitsch, "Thematics", *Studies in Phenomenology and Psychology* p. 293, quoted at xxvi); the singled-out content "undergoes a qualitative change and is, phenomenally speaking, no longer 'the same.'" (quoted at xxv).
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral. Nothing here bears on dualism; the material is descriptive phenomenology of organisation. Do not enlist it for Tenet 1.

## Major Positions

### Gurwitsch's field theory (theme / thematic field / margin)
- **Proponent**: Gurwitsch (dissertation 1929; *Théorie du champ* 1957; FC 1964; *Marginal Consciousness* posthumous 1985). Developed by Arvidson (1992, 2003, 2006) and Yoshimi & Vinson (2015).
- **Core claim**: organisation is autochthonous to experience; the noematic field has three domains with distinct principles: Gestalt-coherence within the theme, material relevancy for the thematic field, mere co-presence for the margin, whose contents nonetheless belong to internally organised "orders of existence" (stream, body, perceptual world), each of which is "an indefinitely extended thematic field" in its own right (Embree 1985: xxxii, citing FC 381).
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral. The theory is an account of the structure of what is given, compatible with physicalist and dualist readings alike. **Do not enlist Gurwitsch for dualism**; the only tenet connection the hosts legitimately draw is that structural limits of attention (Tenet 5, Occam's limits; the voids taxonomy) are not contingent failures.

### James's focus / fringe
- **Proponent**: James 1890 (*Principles*, ch. IX): substantive and transitive parts; "fringe" or "psychic overtone" of felt relations.
- **Gurwitsch's correction**: James's single "margin" runs together two things — data relevant to the topic (which Gurwitsch names the thematic field) and data merely co-present (margin proper). Zaner 2010: xxvi–xxvii; FC Part Five ch. I §§I–II; Gurwitsch 1943. "Conflated" is the Map's word; Zaner's is that the margin "shows a further distinction".

### Watzl's priority structure
- SEP places Watzl 2017 as a "similar suggestion"; `attention-and-consciousness` already uses Watzl at L109 ("Prioritization necessity"). The fold can say the field theory is what the priority view inherits, without new citation.

## The Fidelity Question, answered

**1. Is "making the margin thematic creates a new margin" Gurwitsch's claim, an entailment, or the Map's extrapolation?**
An **entailment**, stated in the editorial apparatus, not a located sentence of Gurwitsch's. The premises: (a) margin and field are defined relative to a theme ("What is marginal is so in respect of some theme", Zaner xxviii); (b) marginal items can become themes (Zaner xxviii); (c) when a content is singled out it becomes "a new theme" and is "no longer 'the same'" (Gurwitsch quoted at xxv–xxvi), while the old theme recedes into field or margin (xxvii–xxviii); (d) the three marginal constants are "concomitant with whatever may be the theme" (1985: xlv). From (a)–(d): a thematised marginal item is no longer marginal, the field reorganises around it, and the remaining constants (and the demoted former theme) constitute its margin. Nothing in Gurwitsch's text, as far as this research reached, says "a new margin always forms" in those words, and his own emphasis is on theme ↔ field shifts (Embree xxx). The host sentence is defensible as a consequence; a writer should phrase it as what the theory *implies* and cite Zaner's "in respect of some theme" rather than attributing the sentence to Gurwitsch.

**2. Does Gurwitsch treat the margin as having its own organisational principles?**
Yes, in the following sense: the margin is "by no means unorganized" (Zaner xxviii), but its organisation is not relevancy to the theme. Its data are organised by the orders of existence they belong to — the temporal order of the stream, the body-centred spatial order of the perceptual world (1985: 61), the somatic order of embodied existence — each an indefinitely extended thematic field of its own (FC 381 via Embree xxxii). The host's "different organisational principles than the theme" is therefore correct; the 01-29 note's specific formula "simultaneity is the principle of organization of marginal consciousness" is **unverified** (see Gaps) and should not be propagated.

**3. The James critique.**
Verified at the editorial level and in the 2010 TOC (above). Consequence for `tacit-integration-void` L65, which lists James's fringe and Gurwitsch's field side by side: the two are not equivalent, but the host's use is internally consistent, since it assigns James the "attending to it converts it into a centre and produces new fringes" role and Gurwitsch the relevance-structure role — which is exactly the half of James's margin that Gurwitsch promoted to "thematic field". A 20–40 word clause saying that Gurwitsch split James's fringe into relevant field and irrelevant margin fixes the apparent equivalence.
