## Headline Findings (read first)

1. **"Type-O" occurs once in Chalmers's paper, in the Conclusions, with a one-line footnote.** The label is his: "We might call this class type-O dualism (taking overdetermination as a paradigm case)." He names two routes (overdetermination, mediation), says "there may be further strategies", grants that the class "escapes the charge of epiphenomenalism", and says it shares type-E's problems. His footnote names three advocates: Bealer (forthcoming), Lowe 1996 and Mills 1996.
2. **Chalmers's three advocates take three different routes, and two of them reject overdetermination.** Mills defends overdetermination. Lowe calls Mills's strategy "suspiciously ad hoc" and builds mediation and fact-causation models that he says are not overdetermination. Bealer's account, on Saad's description, is a preemption account. So type-O is wider than overdeterminist dualism, and the Map should not treat the two names as synonyms.
3. **Whether a view "keeps closure" depends on which closure principle is meant.** Lowe argues his models satisfy several closure principles. Saad then writes a stricter Closure, says he does so because Lowe showed the standard ones "are consistent with interactionist dualist views", and under it counts Lowe-style and preemptive views, his own included, as violating Closure. The Map's sentence that Saad is type-O's "neighbour, not an instance" is true under Saad's Closure and unsettled under Chalmers's looser wording, since one of Chalmers's own exemplars is a preemption account.
4. **"Overdeterminist dualism" is Saad's stipulated label, and he expects its bearers to disown it.** He uses "overdetermination" in a broad, non-pejorative sense. Of the listed defenders that could be read, Vaassen denies that his view involves overdetermination, List and Stoljar set "genuine overdetermination" aside and never apply the word to their view, and Kroedel offers overdetermination only as a fallback. No earlier use of the phrase was found; the coiner is unconfirmed.
5. **Several listed "defenders" are not dualists.** Vaassen writes as one of "us physicalists" and ends "I'm not particularly happy about it either". List and Stoljar argue only that a dualist has a reply, "whether or not their position is ultimately true". Kroedel argues for non-reductive physicalists and dualists alike.
6. **The Map misdescribes Vaassen's 2024 paper.** [interventionist-and-counterfactual-dualism](/topics/interventionist-and-counterfactual-dualism/) says his "crucial structural claim" is that "difference-making is not energy transfer, so causal closure read as a claim about energy or forces is simply untouched", and that mental states are "points of control". The full text has 0 hits for "energy" and 0 for "points of control". His closure principle is "Physical Completeness", stated as physical necessitation, and his account rests on "sufficiently robust patterns of correlation". See [Corrections](#corrections-for-existing-pages).
7. **The Map groups Lowe with Mills as a "lawful-overdetermination lineage". Nothing read supports that.** Lowe 2000 distances himself from overdetermination in a footnote; Saad files Lowe's model with joint causation; Kroedel and Mills both cite Lowe among critics or deniers of closure.
8. **Saad gives almost no argument for Non-Overdetermination in the paper.** He says its plausibility's sources "are disputed and often left implicit" and defers to other work. He names two vices of overdetermination and concedes in a footnote that a delegatory dualist could instead adopt the overdeterminists' defences, at the cost of his theory's advantage over them.

## Executive Summary

Chalmers (2003) set aside a class of dualist views that keep microphysics causally closed and still give phenomenal properties a causal role, and called it type-O. Saad (2025) names a narrower class, overdeterminist dualism, and builds his own theory to avoid it. The two classes overlap in Mills and come apart elsewhere. The contemporary members of the closure-keeping family (List & Stoljar, Kroedel, Vaassen) mostly reject the word "overdetermination" for their view and secure mental causation through nomological connection, counterfactual dependence or interventionist difference-making. The Map rejects strong closure at quantum indeterminacies as a tenet, so this family is a rival. The Map's reasons against it are commitments and unresolved worries. None is a refutation, and the family has replies.

## Key Sources

### Chalmers, "Consciousness and its Place in Nature" (2003)

- **URL**: https://consc.net/papers/nature.html
- **Type**: Book chapter. Crossref (doi:10.1002/9780470998762.ch5): *The Blackwell Guide to Philosophy of Mind*, pp. 102–142, print 2003. Editors per Chalmers's own header: S. Stich and F. Warfield. [metadata]
- **Stance**: Chalmers surveys; he does not endorse type-O. Saad lists him among "Defenders (if not outright proponents) of epiphenomenalist dualism" and, with "cf.", beside the Closure-violating dualists.
- **The whole passage** (section 12, "Conclusions"):
  - "First, some nonmaterialists hold that phenomenal properties are ontologically wholly distinct from physical properties, that microphysics is causally closed, but that phenomenal properties play a causal role with respect to the physical nevertheless." [raw]
  - "One way this might happen is by a sort of causal overdetermination: physical states causally determine behavior, but phenomenal states cause behavior at the same time." [raw]
  - "Another is by causal mediation: it might be that in at least some instances of microphysical causation from A to B, there is actually a causal connection from A to the mind to B, so that the mind enters the causal nexus without altering the structure of the network." [raw]
  - "And there may be further strategies here. We might call this class type-O dualism (taking overdetermination as a paradigm case)." [raw]
  - "These views shares much of the structure of the type-E view (causally closed physical world, distinct phenomenal properties), but escapes the charge of epiphenomenalism." [raw] (The grammatical slip is in the source.)
  - "The special causal setups of these views may be hard to swallow, and they share some of the same problems as the type-E view (e.g., the fragmented view of nature, and the "lucky" psychophysical laws), but this class should nevertheless be put on the table as an option." [raw]
  - Footnote: "Type-O positions are advocated by Bealer (forthcoming), Lowe 1996 and Mills 1996." [raw]
- **The contrast classes**:
  - "Type-D dualism holds that microphysics is not causally closed, and that phenomenal properties play a causal role in affecting the physical world." [raw]
  - "Type-E dualism holds that phenomenal properties are ontologically distinct from physical properties, and that the phenomenal has no effect on the physical." [raw]
- **The two type-E problems he says type-O inherits**:
  - a "fragmented picture of nature, on which physical and phenomenal properties are only very weakly integrated in the natural world" [raw]
  - "the relationship between consciousness and reports about consciousness seems to be something of a lucky coincidence, on the epiphenomenalist view" [raw]
- **Bibliography entries**: "Bealer, G. (forthcoming). Mental causation." and "Mills, E. 1996. Interactionism and overdetermination. American Philosophical Quarterly 33:105-115." [raw] The Bealer title matches his 2007 *Philosophical Perspectives* paper. That the two are the same work is an inference from the title.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. A survey.

### Saad, "A dualist theory of experience" (2025)

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-025-02290-3 (text from Europe PMC, PMC12062107)
- **Stance**: Dualist. Defends delegatory dualism.
- **His definition of overdetermination** (footnote 3): "I'll use 'overdetermination' throughout as shorthand for discussing causal structures that feature an effect with both a sufficient cause and a distinct cause, neither of which makes its contribution to the effect via the other." [raw] He adds that this "contrasts with ones that build into the notion of overdetermination that it involves more than one sufficient cause, that it is analogous to paradigm cases such as firing squad cases, and/or that it is problematic". [raw]
- **The premise**: "Non-Overdetermination : It is not generally the case that experiences' effects have both non-physical causes and concurrent causes from their ancestries of immediate sufficient physical causes." [raw]
- **His motivation for it, in full**: "Something like Non-Overdetermination is widely regarded as plausible, though the sources of its plausibility are disputed and often left implicit. I investigate the sources of its plausibility elsewhere." [raw] The footnote points to his dissertation (Saad 2019a, pp. 162–173), which was not read.
- **The list** (footnote 11): "Defenders of overdeterminist dualism include Fumerton ( 2013 : Ch. 7), List & Stoljar ( 2017 ), Meixner ( 2004 ), Mills ( 1996 ), Kroedel ( 2015 : §5; 2020 : 171, 175), Vaassen ( 2024b ) and White ( 2018 )." [raw]
- **On the label**: "So, in my terminology, 'overdeterminist dualism' is not a pejorative label, though the label will understandably be disavowed by defenders of the labeled position who use 'overdetermination' pejoratively." [raw]
- **"Vaassen 2024b" resolves** from Saad's reference list to "Mental causation for standard dualists", *AJP* 102, 978–998. "2024a" is "Against causal arguments in metaphysics", *Asian Journal of Philosophy* 3(2), 54. [raw]
- **The same footnote on the third family**: "Defenders of Closure-violating dualism include Elitzur ( 1989 ), Eccles et al. ( 1977 ), Foster ( 1991 : Ch. 6), Swinburne ( 2013 : Ch. 4), and Stapp ( 1993 ); cf. Bealer ( 2007 ), Chalmers ( 2010 : 128), Libet ( 1994 ), and Lowe ( 2003 )." [raw]
- **Why his Closure is stricter** (footnote 8): "As Lowe ( 2000 , 2003 ) shows, standard formulations of these claims are inapt, as they are consistent with interactionist dualist views." [raw]
- **Where he places Lowe**: after describing Won's joint-causation model, "A similar model is also proposed by Lowe ( 2000 , 2003 )." [raw]
- **Where he places Bealer** (footnote 27): "Bealer ( 2007 ) develops a non-reductive account of mental causation that construes mental-to-mental and mental-to-physical causation as a form of preemption" [raw]
- **Where he places himself**: "Like other forms of interactionist dualism, delegatory dualism must posit violations of Closure in order to respect Non-Overdetermination and Efficacy." [raw] "Delegatory dualism will turn out to be a form of preemptive dualism" [raw]
- **Subset dualism is his main overdeterminist target.** It is "the subset dualist theory proposed (but not endorsed) by Pautz ( 2010 : 357–8; fn40)". [raw] It "impressively satisfies four of the five constraints, flouting only the non-overdetermination constraint". [raw] "Indeed, subset dualism entails that experiences overdetermine all of their effects." [raw]
- **The two vices** (section 10):
  - "One vice of systematic experiential-physical overdetermination is that it seems to involve a striking but unexplained coincidence." [raw] He specifies: "such a coincidence between which experiential and physical properties are co-instantiated and which such properties share causal powers". [raw] He then notes "Subset dualism is free of this vice for the same reason, per its appeal to Subset Law." [raw]
  - "Another vice of experiential-physical overdetermination is that it involves additivity failure whereby effects we'd expect to be additive functions of their causes' contributions turn out to sum to less than the sum of those contributions." [raw]
- **The context of those vices.** He raises them while answering "the most pressing objection to delegatory dualism—at least on the assumption that positing physical-experiential overdetermination is problematic", namely that his preemptive structure "inherits the theoretical vices of physical-experiential overdetermination". [raw] The full answer is deferred to "a companion work in progress". [raw]
- **The concession** (footnote 61): "Delegatory dualists could instead respond by arguing that overdetermination is not problematic and hence that there are no vices of overdetermination for delegatory dualism's preemptive posit to inherit." [raw] "Although I think that strategy is worth exploring, I will not pursue it here." [raw] "One dialectical advantage of the strategy pursued in the main text is that it enables delegatory dualists to maintain that avoiding overdetermination is a point in favor of their theory over overdeterminist dualism." [raw] "No such advantage is available to delegatory dualists who instead respond to the objection by denying that overdetermination is problematic." [raw]
- **Overdeterminist dualism counts as interactionist and as satisfying Observational Closure in his closing argument**: "Instead, what's true is an interactionist dualist theory on which experiences either overdetermine their effects or non-redundantly cause only unobservable violations of Closure, as on cooperative and preemptive dualist theories." [raw]
- **On the Map's own family** (footnote 15): "Quantum interactionist dualism violates Observational Closure because nomically possible tests distinguish different collapse interpretations of quantum mechanics, including quantum interactionist dualism (although these tests would be very hard to run in practice)" [raw]
- **Tenet alignment**: Aligns with Tenets 1 and 3. Conflicts with Tenet 2 as a locus: he sets quantum interactionism aside.

### List & Stoljar, "Does the Exclusion Argument Put Any Pressure on Dualism?" (2017)

- **URL**: http://eprints.lse.ac.uk/66814/ (accepted manuscript). Version of record doi:10.1080/00048402.2016.1154080.
- **Metadata** (Crossref): Christian List and Daniel Stoljar, *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 95(1): 96–108; online 20 April 2016, print 2017. [metadata]
- **Stance**: Neither author defends dualism here. The paper cites Stoljar's own 2006 book for the view that "the positive arguments for dualism are not convincing". [raw-am]
- **The thesis**: "We argue to the contrary that, whether or not their position is ultimately true, dualists have a plausible response." [raw-am]
- **The argument**. The exclusion principle and the dualist's distinctness claim both use "distinct", and the word has several readings. The four claims are inconsistent "if and only if the interpretation of 'distinctness' in (4) is at least as fine-grained as the one in (3)". [raw-am] The dualist's claim is metaphysical distinctness. Pain and the physical state are, for the dualist, "metaphysically distinct but they need not be nomologically distinct". [raw-am] So the dualist "may assert that what is true is only (1), (2), (3-met), and (4-nom). And from this, again, nothing of interest follows." [raw-am]
- **Why reject the metaphysical reading of exclusion**: "since F and F* are nomologically co-extensive, a natural thing to say is that either both are causally sufficient or neither is. But it is implausible that neither is causally sufficient. Hence both are, and (4-met) should be rejected." [raw-am] And: "since the worlds in question can be as remote as you please, it becomes hard to see how their existence can have any impact on causal facts that are sensitive only to worlds nomologically similar to our own". [raw-am]
- **Sufficient causation is assumed**: "we assume throughout that 'causes' means 'is a sufficient cause for', rather than merely 'is causally relevant to'". [raw-am]
- **It is not the difference-making strategy**: "this strategy is different from the one based on difference-making causation defended in List and Menzies [2009]". [raw-am]
- **On the word "overdetermination"**: it appears only in the clause "(except in cases of genuine overdetermination)" and the sentence setting such cases aside. [raw-am] They never call their own conclusion overdetermination. Under Saad's definition it qualifies, since two distinct sufficient causes are affirmed.
- **The conclusion in their words**: "any plausible version of the exclusion principle refers only to properties that are modally distinct in a nomological sense". [raw-am] And of two nomologically co-extensive properties: "since F* is not nomologically distinct from F, the principle does not apply". [raw-am]
- **Their two limits**:
  - "We have said nothing about those weaker, epistemic variants of the exclusion argument." [raw-am]
  - "There is nothing in the present paper to suggest that those who find dualism implausible are wrong." [raw-am]
- **Scope**: they "set aside" necessitarian and emergentist dualisms (footnote 4). [raw-am]
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. A conditional defence of dualists who keep sufficient physical causes.

### Kroedel, *Mental Causation: A Counterfactual Theory* (2020) and the 2015 *Noûs* paper

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108762717 (open access)
- **Metadata** (Crossref): Thomas Kroedel, Cambridge University Press, monograph; print 19 December 2019, cited in the literature as 2020. 2015 paper: *Noûs* 49(2): 357–375, doi:10.1111/nous.12028, online 2013. [metadata]
- **Stance**: Argues that non-reductive physicalists and dualists alike can explain mental causation. He does not declare for dualism in the passages read. He calls the dualist position he builds "somewhat unorthodox" and says "from a dualist perspective it seems worthwhile to adopt this position". [raw, p. 202]
- **He keeps completeness**: "Rejecting (completeness) seems implausible, not just from a general scientific point of view, but also in the case at hand." [raw, p. 155] The footnote sends readers to "dualist critiques of physical completeness claims" in "Lowe 2000, 2003, 2008, BonJour 2010, and Gibb 2015b". [raw, p. 155]
- **The dualism he needs**: "super-nomological dualism, that is, a version of dualism that assigns a special modal status to the psychophysical laws". [raw] "Put less technically, the position of the super-nomological dualist is that the relation between the mental and the physical is a matter of law, but that the relevant laws are 'more necessary' than ordinary laws of nature." [raw]
- **The two exits**: "They can deny that mental causation entails overdetermination, or they can deny that the kind of overdetermination that would ensue would be problematic." [raw, p. 201]
- **Overdetermination is the fallback**: "It would be good for non-reductive physicalists and dualists to have a contingency plan in case the arguments against (exclusion*) turn out to be unworkable." [raw, p. 165] And: "If the argument from the previous section succeeds, non-reductive physicalists and dualists have no need to admit that physical effects of mental causes are overdetermined by their mental and physical causes." [raw, p. 175]
- **Why the fallback is harmless, on his account**:
  - The firing-squad comparison equivocates: "it is consistent with mental causation's involving overdetermination that cases of mental causation have little in common with prototypical cases of overdetermination such as firing squad cases". [raw, p. 168]
  - The two causes are lawfully tied: "thus, it is no coincidence that the effect is overdetermined". [raw, p. 169]
  - "The counterfactual dependence of the physical effect on both its mental and its physical cause marks out cases of mental causation as very atypical cases of overdetermination." [raw, p. 171]
  - "In sum, there would be nothing objectionable about physical effects' being overdetermined by mental and physical causes." [raw, p. 175]
- **The empirical hostage he names for the first exit**: "Their truth is also hostage to empirical fortune, because it is partly an empirical question what the alternative bases or realizers of headaches are and how easy it is to implement them." [raw, p. 165]
- **2015 abstract**: proponents "can deny that it follows that the physical effect of a mental event is overdetermined by its mental and physical causes, or they can accept that the physical effect is overdetermined but claim that this is unproblematic because the case is sufficiently dissimilar to prototypical cases of overdetermination". [raw] The 2015 paper's §5, which Saad cites, was not read.
- **Pages 171 and 175**, Saad's pin-cites, are the two passages quoted above.
- **Tenet alignment**: Compatible with Tenets 1 and 3. Conflicts with Tenet 2: completeness is kept.

### Vaassen, "Mental Causation for Standard Dualists" (2024)

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1080/00048402.2024.2335325 (open access)
- **Metadata** (Crossref): Bram Vaassen, *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 102(4): 978–998; online 18 April 2024. [metadata]
- **Stance**: **A physicalist arguing that dualists have an answer.**
  - "For a long time, this objection reassured many of us who are inclined towards physicalism that dualism comes at a nearly unbearable cost." [raw]
  - "The problem is that it is unlikely to do us physicalists any good." [raw]
  - "The upshot is that physicalists are in need of better objections to Standard Dualism. Believe me. I'm not particularly happy about it either." [raw]
- **Standard Dualism includes closure**: its third clause is "Physical Completeness is true". [raw] "Physical Completeness For any actual physical phenomenon P and any time t, there is a purely physical phenomenon that occurs at t and physically necessitates the occurrence of P." [raw] "Physical Completeness is widely treated as a necessary ingredient for scientifically respectable ontologies. It is in the dualist's interest to accept it." [raw]
- **The view needs no change to the ontology**: others propose "minimal adjustments to the dualist ontology", and he cites "Bealer 2007; Lowe 2008; Lycan 2009; Gibb 2015; Kroedel 2015, 2020; Won 2021; Khawaja 2022". [raw] His claim is that "no such adjustments are required". [raw]
- **The basis of the account**: "Higher-level phenomena are ascribed causal status in virtue of exhibiting sufficiently robust patterns of correlation with target effects. Why couldn't dualist mental phenomena exhibit such patterns with physical effects?" [raw]

## Major Positions

### Type-E dualism (epiphenomenalism)
- **Core claim**: closed physics; the phenomenal has no physical effects.
- **Relation to site tenets**: ruled out by Tenet 3.

### Type-O dualism, overdetermination route
- **Proponents**: Mills (1996), by his own description. Meixner as an option (2002 appendix). Subset dualism, "proposed (but not endorsed)" by Pautz. Kroedel as a fallback. List & Stoljar under Saad's definition, though not in their own vocabulary.
- **Core claim**: the physical cause is sufficient and the mental cause is a further, distinct cause of the same effect. The overlap is lawful.
- **Key arguments**: exclusion bites only on distinctness finer than nomological (List & Stoljar); the overlap is unlike a firing squad because the causes are lawfully tied and the effect depends counterfactually on each (Kroedel).
- **Relation to site tenets**: satisfies Tenets 1 and 3 on their face. Conflicts with Tenet 2, which says "Physics is *not* complete at quantum indeterminacies".

### Type-O dualism without overdetermination
- **Proponents**: Vaassen (2024) as an option for dualists; Kroedel's first exit.
- **Core claim**: completeness holds, the mental is a cause by interventionist or counterfactual standards, and the case fails the tests for overdetermination.
- **Relation to site tenets**: as above.

### Mediation, fact-causation and joint causation (Lowe; Won)
- **Core claim**: the mental event lies on, or structures, the physical causal route. Lowe holds this consistent with closure principles up to (1H).
- **Classification is disputed**: type-O for Chalmers; closure-denying for Mills (on Lowe 1993), Kroedel and Vaassen (on Lowe 2008); joint causation outside Closure for Saad.
- **Relation to site tenets**: aligns with Tenets 1 and 3. Lowe's "invisible" mental causation is the ancestor of [observational-closure](/concepts/observational-closure/).

### Preemption (Bealer; Saad)
- **Core claim**: the experience causes the effect and preempts the physical state, which remains a nomic guarantor.
- **Classification**: Chalmers lists Bealer under type-O. Saad says his own theory "must posit violations of Closure" and that they are unobservable.
- **Relation to site tenets**: the Map's [delegatory-causation](/concepts/delegatory-causation/) and [trumping-preemption](/concepts/trumping-preemption/) pages develop it.

### Type-D dualism with an observable gap (quantum interactionism)
- **Core claim**: physics is incomplete at a specifiable place and the mental acts there.
- **Relation to site tenets**: the Map's own family (Tenets 2 and 3). Saad classes it as violating Observational Closure.

## Where the Disagreement Actually Lies

**1. What the closure principle says.** Chalmers writes "microphysics is causally closed" and leaves it there. Lowe distinguishes at least four principles and fits dualist models under three. Saad adds an "ancestry of immediate sufficient physical causes" clause so that Lowe's models fall outside. Vaassen uses physical necessitation at every time. A sentence of the form "X keeps closure" is incomplete until it says which of these is meant.

**2. Whether lawful overlap is a cost.** Saad says systematic overdetermination involves an unexplained coincidence and additivity failure. Kroedel replies that the lawful tie explains the coincidence. Saad agrees for the case where a law does the explaining: subset dualism "is free of this vice". The additivity charge is not answered in anything read here. Kroedel's footnote 33 touches a neighbouring point, energy transfer, and does not settle it.

**3. Whether the overlap is overdetermination at all.** This is partly verbal, as Saad and Kroedel both say. Kroedel: "'overdetermination' is a technical term, so, to a certain extent, theorists are free to stipulate how they understand it". [raw, p. 166] The substantive residue is whether the effect depends counterfactually on the mental cause. Kroedel and Vaassen say yes. On delegation the Map's own pages say no.

**4. What the Map holds against the family, stated honestly.**
- *A tenet.* Tenet 2 denies completeness at quantum indeterminacies. That is a commitment the Map adopts. It is not an argument against a view that keeps completeness.
- *The lucky-laws worry.* Chalmers says type-O shares it with type-E. If the psychophysical laws are independent of physical evolution, then reports about experience line up with experience only because the laws happen to fit. Kroedel's super-nomological status and Saad's Subset Law are both attempts to make the fit lawful. The worry is a cost to weigh. Chalmers still says the class "should nevertheless be put on the table".
- *The epistemic exclusion argument.* List & Stoljar say plainly that they do not address it: a reply to the strong argument leaves open whether we have reason to think the mental cause is there.
- *The price of Kroedel's route.* Psychophysical laws must be "more necessary" than ordinary laws. He calls the result "somewhat unorthodox".
- *Rellihan's charge*, already on [interventionist-and-counterfactual-dualism](/topics/interventionist-and-counterfactual-dualism/).

**5. What the family says back.**
- It keeps a principle that, in Vaassen's words, is "widely treated as a necessary ingredient for scientifically respectable ontologies", and the Map does not.
- Saad's footnote 15: quantum interactionism "violates Observational Closure", because tests could in principle distinguish it. The Map's Born-preserving reading is built to answer that, and the reading carries its own open question (the positions register's [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3)) about whether settling an outcome without shifting any statistic is causing. A type-O dualist can say that the Map has bought an observable gap and then narrowed it until its channel resembles theirs.
- Vaassen's 2026 survey judges the closure-keeping family "the more promising path for the dualist".

Nothing in this note shows type-O false, and nothing in it shows the Map's route false. Compatibility of either with the evidence is not support for it.

### Passage A — for [observational-closure](/concepts/observational-closure/), as a new subsection after "What Saad Does Not Grant" (348 words including the heading)

> ### The Rival That Keeps Closure Whole {#type-o}
>
> Observational closure is a middle position, and the option on its far side has a name. Chalmers (2003) sets apart dualist views on which "microphysics is causally closed" and yet "phenomenal properties play a causal role with respect to the physical nevertheless". He names two routes. One is "a sort of causal overdetermination", where "physical states causally determine behavior, but phenomenal states cause behavior at the same time". The other is "causal mediation", where "the mind enters the causal nexus without altering the structure of the network". He calls the class "type-O dualism (taking overdetermination as a paradigm case)". His verdict is mixed: the class "escapes the charge of epiphenomenalism", its "special causal setups" may be "hard to swallow", and it shares problems with epiphenomenalism, among them "the fragmented view of nature".
>

He uses "overdetermination" in a stipulated sense that leaves open whether it is a fault, and he expects the label to "be disavowed" by some of those he lists: Fumerton, List and Stoljar, Meixner, Mills, Kroedel, Vaassen and White. Kroedel (2020) offers the dualist two exits: "deny that mental causation entails overdetermination", or deny that the overdetermination "would be problematic".
>
