## Executive Summary

The review's claim is structural: before a transition law *P(O | S, C, Φ, A, X)* can be stated, something must fix which subject *S* a conscious state *C* belongs to, which factorisation *Φ* picks out "the system", which candidate set *A* is in play, how phenomenal content maps to a candidate (*σ*), when the law is active, and how several subjects' conditionals compose. The review calls that prior object an **indexing law** and says the transition kernel "is not yet well-typed" without it. Four findings. **(i) The term is the review's own.** A literature search for "indexing law" in this sense returns nothing; the components have older names — Kim's *pairing relation* and *pairing problem* (2001, 2005), which Kim and BRVH both trace to **Foster (1991)**; Bailey, Rasmussen & Van Horn's *Diachronic Pairing Question* and their counterfactual (Glued); Zurek's "what are the systems" (1998); Tegmark's *quantum factorization problem* (2015); Gómez-Emilsson & Percy's *boundary problem*; IIT's *exclusion*. The Map's own register already speaks of "the census-indexed law" ([P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2)), so the vocabulary is half-native. The article should credit the review for the *packaging* and the typing argument, name the antecedents for each component, and not present "indexing law" as established usage. **(ii) The dualist literature has three shapes of pairing law and none of the review's shape.** Location-based pairing (Hasker 1999, Lycan 2009, BRVH §4, with Kim's co-location rejoinder); origin- or emergence-based pairing, which BRVH state as a possible non-haecceitous law ("a physical structure exhibiting certain properties produces a non-spatial soul … [which] will, under certain conditions, remain causally related to certain material parts of that same structure") and which Swinburne frames as a law bringing a connected soul into being at a stage of foetal development; and singular, haecceitous or brute pairing (Foster 1991 p. 167ff; Unger 2006 ch. 7; Audi 2011; Swinburne's "thisness"; BRVH fn. 9's "'haecceitous' law applying just to C and E"). Each addresses the review's component 1 (pairing), the origin shape touches component 4 (onset), and none addresses factorisation, semantic matching or multi-subject composition, which the dualist literature does not pose. **(iii) The physics literature addresses only factorisation, and says it is presupposed rather than derived.** Zurek 1998 states that "the problem of measurement cannot be even stated without a recognition of the existence of systems" and that an explanation of "what are the systems — how to define them given, say, the overall Hamiltonian" is wanting; Zanardi, Lidar & Lloyd make the tensor-product structure relative to accessible observables; the quantum-mereology programme tries to derive one from the Hamiltonian and its uniqueness is disputed. Indexing a *subject* to a factorisation would need the three levels the 2026-09-25 note set out, and the one dualist route (the subject's accessible operations fix the TPS) converts the factorisation debt back into the pairing debt. **(iv) All six components already have register homes except one.** Pairing, counting and composition are [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2); onset and cessation are [P-SC3](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc3); factorisation is `concepts/quantum-factorisation-problem` and [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2)'s quantum face; eligibility is [P-AC1](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac1) via [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2). **Semantic matching has no register entry**: it lives in one paragraph of `concepts/selection-only-channel` ("it needs the experience's content to fix *which* candidate is realised") and in `trumping-preemption`, and no position books it. The genuinely new content for the Map is therefore (a) the typing argument — that the five debts are one object logically prior to the kernel, so a toy *P(O | C, X)* cannot discharge [P-Q10](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q10) — (b) the three-shape taxonomy of pairing laws with its honest verdict that the Map's co-construction story is a version of the origin shape plus the location shape and inherits each one's regress, and (c) the unbooked semantic-matching component. That is article-sized and has no single home, which is the review's point; hence `article`.

## Question (1): how the dualist literature treats pairing and individuation laws

### Kim's argument and its origin

Kim's presentation (2005, pp. 70–92, the pages BRVH target; first in Kim 2001) argues that causation requires a pairing relation, that pairing relations are spatial, and that souls are not in space, so souls cannot cause. BRVH reconstruct it as two premises, **Kim's Dictum** ("causation requires a spatial relation") and **Nowhere Man** ("souls can't be in space"), and attack both. BRVH's fn. 1 records the provenance the Map should use: "As Kim acknowledges, the Pairing Argument has its origin in Foster (1991)." The corpus's `concepts/pairing-problem` References list Kim 2005 and Kim 2001 correctly; the chapter title of Kim 2005 ch. 3 was not verified on a publisher page (the Princeton page shows no contents) and the article should cite by page range as BRVH do.

### Shape A — location-based pairing

Souls are in space, so spatial relations can pair them. BRVH §4 "Against nowhere man": "suppose souls are extensionless geometric points located in, say, the pineal gland of their respective bodies. There is nothing impossible about this supposition, so far as we can see." Fn. 16: "Contemporary defenders of substance dualism who endorse the spatial location of souls include Hart (1988) and Hasker (1999)." Lycan 2009 (read from the `newdualism.org` HTML copy): "For the rest of this paper, I shall assume that minds, though immaterial, have locations in physical space", and he poses the pairing question himself — "but then what accounts for the unique relation between the mind and that body?" — calling it "an embarrassing question" whose answer "is to be found in whatever would explain the appearance of minds", so that it "collapses into" his evolution objection. Hasker's field analogy (the self stands to the brain as a magnetic field to its magnet, located around its source) is already verified on `concepts/emergent-dualism`; cite it from there.

Kim's standing rejoinder (reproduced by BRVH with the "ad hoc" verdict) is co-location: what stops two indiscernible souls occupying one region? BRVH's reply is that an "impenetrability of souls" principle may be a cost dualists can pay.

Verdict for the Map: location answers *synchronic* pairing only by relocating the question to what fixes the soul's location, which is why BRVH add a diachronic account. The Map's "consciousness is located where it interfaces" (`pairing-problem` §"The Map's Position") is this shape, and inherits its regress, which the 2026-10-27 pessimistic review and the ChatGPT §5.3 both found.

### Shape B — origin- or emergence-based pairing (the "nomic origin" shape)

BRVH raise it against Wong's generality condition (§3.1): "it may be that there is a law dictating that a physical structure exhibiting certain properties produces a non-spatial soul of a certain kind, and that there is a further law dictating that any soul produced by a structure will, under certain conditions, remain causally related to certain material parts of that same structure." Fn. 12: "How does a soul continue to be related to a certain body? One possibility is that a law dictates that souls causally interact with the parts of whatever structure produced them." Two things the article must get right, because the corpus has already misreported this paper once: (i) BRVH state this as a *possibility* that defeats the motivation for Kim's Dictum, not as their own positive proposal; (ii) their positive proposal in §4 is Shape A plus the diachronic account below. The open P2 task at todo ~L3434 labels the §3.1 possibility "nomic origin-pairing"; that label is the review's, not the paper's (the word "nomic" does not occur in the text), though it describes the passage fairly. The writer should quote the passage and not the label.

BRVH's own diachronic account (§4) is the counterfactual **(Glued)**: "For any time t at which A and M both exist, if M were to have exactly occupied some region R of space at t instead of the one it actually did, A would have exactly occupied R as well (and the converse counterfactual is true, too)." They explain (Glued) by causal interaction and head off the circularity: "the substance dualist has not invoked causal interaction to explain the possibility of causal interaction. The possibility of causal interaction obtains in virtue of facts about the locations of A and M at times. It is only the counterfactual relationship between these locations at times that obtains in virtue of causal interaction." This is the published template the 2026-10-27 review pointed to: the Map's co-construction story can legitimately carry *diachronic* specificity (why the pairing stays put) but needs a separate synchronic ground, which it does not have and [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2) books.

Hasker's emergent dualism is the fullest origin-shape view: the self is generated by the brain once its organisation is sufficient, so the pairing is fixed by origin and the onset is the emergence event. That gives an answer to component 4 (onset) that the Map's history-blind [P-CS7](/positions/consciousness-scope/#p-cs7) declines ("Lineage is evidence of adequacy, never a condition of coupling"), so the article should present emergence as the origin shape's answer to onset and note the register chose otherwise on 2026-10-11. Swinburne (per the interview source in Gaps; the 2015 abstract confirms "psychophysical laws" linking souls and brain events) posits a law on which "at a certain stage of its development each human foetus gives rise to a connected soul" — origin-shape onset again. Do not enlist Hasker for the Map's quantum interactionism; his view is a rival account of the same interface (see `concepts/emergent-dualism`).

### Shape C — singular, haecceitous or brute pairing

BRVH fn. 9: a cause and effect "may be related by a singular causal relation — a relation that isn't an instance of a law. But singular causation is not the only logical possibility. It's logically consistent that they be related by an instance of a 'haecceitous' law applying just to C and E." Fn. 10: "some substance dualists explicitly endorse the possibility of what we're calling causal singularism. See Foster (1991, p. 167ff) and Unger (2006, Chap. 7, especially Sects. 6–7)." Audi 2011 (Crossref abstract): "I argue that the Cartesian can answer this objection by appeal to the possibility of primitive causal relations, a possibility I defend … I close with a discussion of what Cartesians should say about embodiment." Swinburne 2015 (Cambridge abstract): "Each soul has a thisness", and no scientific law could explain "why God created this soul rather than that possible soul" and connected it to this body — pairing is brute at the level of law and, for Swinburne, chosen by God. Saad 2017 (*Inquiry*) is titled on exactly this move (haecceities and the pairing argument); only its metadata was reached.

Verdict for the Map: Shape C is where the haecceity response *actually* lives in the literature — in Foster, Unger, Audi, Saad and Swinburne, not in BRVH's main argument. `concepts/pairing-problem` Response 2 and `concepts/haecceity` L103 attribute a haecceity dilemma to BRVH; the open P2 at ~L3434 fixes that, and the new article must not re-import it. Shape C also fits the Map's own commitments uncomfortably well: [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1)/[P-I3](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i3) hold that subject boundaries are real and not readable off physical structure, which is a haecceitist commitment about *subjects*; the question for the article is whether the Map is prepared to say the pairing law is haecceitous (a law "applying just to" this subject and this brain) or wants a general law of the origin or location shape. That fork is undecided in the corpus and the article should present it as the live choice, with the costs: a haecceitous pairing law satisfies [P-I3](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i3) but makes the census and eligibility questions unanswerable in principle (which would convert [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2) from a repayable to a permanent debt — one of [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2)'s own "would shift if" branches); a general law satisfies testability but must say what the general properties are, which is [P-CS7](/positions/consciousness-scope/#p-cs7)'s "architectural adequacy" and inherits the duplicate-and-copies problem.

### Two wrinkles the literature adds

**Indeterministic causation (Saad 2018).** Crossref abstract: "Critics have argued that the first claim [causation requires pairing] suffers from counterexamples involving indeterministic causation. … I offer two ways of revising the pairing argument." BRVH §3.2 ("Quantum tunneling") press the same counterexample. The Map's channel is indeterministic by construction, so pairing-by-causal-interaction under Tenet 2 is a probabilistic relation; the article should note that the Map cannot help itself to a deterministic (Glued) and that Saad's patches are the current form of the objection a quantum interactionist must meet. Full text not read; treat the patches' content as a lead.

**Parody (Brenner 2024).** *Personal Ontology* ch. 3 argues that physicalists who take persons to be composite objects face a "mereological pairing problem" of their own. Zimmerman 2010 runs the problem of the many toward emergent substance dualism. Both bear on [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2)'s census gap: the "which object is the subject" question is not special to dualism, which is the point Priority-1 item 5 of the same review asks `plurality-void` to make. Summaries only; neither text read.

### Does any of them state a law of the review's shape?

No. Shapes A–C are answers to component 1, with Shape B also dating onset. None of the dualist sources poses factorisation (they assume "the body" or "the brain" is a given system), semantic matching (they discuss whether a soul can move a body, not which candidate expresses which content) or multi-subject composition. That absence is itself a finding the article can state as "no treatment located as of 2026-10-28"; do not write "none exists".

## Question (2): how physics treats subsystem decomposition, and what indexing a subject would take

Zurek 1998, in the closing discussion of the decoherence programme, writes that "one issue which has been often taken for granted is looming big, as a foundation of the whole decoherence program. It is the question of what are the 'systems' which play such a crucial role in all the discussions of the emergent classicality"; that "the problem of measurement cannot be even stated without a recognition of the existence of systems. Therefore, our appeal to the same assumption for its resolution is no sin. However, a compelling explanation of what are the systems — how to define them given, say, the overall Hamiltonian in some suitably large Hilbert space — would be undoubtedly most useful." (pdftotext of arXiv:quant-ph/9805065, final section before References.) This is the physics-side statement of component 2 three years before Zanardi, and it says the division is *presupposed* by einselection. Since [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1) selects among einselected outcomes, the Map's preferred mechanism presupposes it too — the point `concepts/quantum-factorisation-problem` already books under "The preferred mechanism".

The rest is in the 2026-09-25 note and the concept built from it, and should be cited rather than re-run: Zanardi 2001 and Zanardi, Lidar & Lloyd 2004 make the tensor-product structure, and so entanglement and "the subsystem", relative to the operationally accessible observables; Tegmark 2015 names the quantum factorisation problem and ties it to what an observer is; Carroll & Singh 2021 and the quantum-mereology programme try to derive a preferred system/environment split from the Hamiltonian (quasi-classicality); Stoica 2022/2026 argues that anything fixed by *H* and |ψ⟩ alone is non-unique or physically idle, which Soulas et al. 2025 dispute; the dispute is unresolved.

**What indexing a conscious subject to a factorisation would take.** Three things, in order. *Level 1*: a TPS at all — shared with every quantum theory, contested, and not the Map's special debt. *Level 2*: a rule picking which collection of factors is the subject's system — the coarse cut — for which the only worked rule in the consciousness literature is IIT's exclusion postulate (a physicalist maximum-Φ criterion) and Chalmers & McQueen's Φ-collapse variant; nothing dualist. *Level 3*: exclusion of nested and overlapping candidates. The one dualist route the 2026-09-25 note found — read Zanardi–Lidar–Lloyd's "accessible observables" as the *subject's* interface operations, so that the pairing itself privileges the factorisation — makes Φ an *output* of the indexing law, which is exactly how the review's *𝓘ₜ* is typed, (S, C, t) ↦ (Φ, A, σ). That is the one place where the review's formalism and the Map's existing analysis agree precisely, and it is worth saying: on the Map's reading the factorisation is indexed *by* the pairing, not the pairing by the factorisation. The cost is also already booked: it is circular if the same Φ is then used to individuate *S* ([P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2)'s census-circularity gap), and it is a posit with no derivation.

## Major Positions

### The pairing law is general and location-based (Hasker, Hart, Lycan, BRVH §4)
- **Core claim**: souls or minds have spatial location, so spatial relations pair them; diachronic specificity by (Glued).
- **Relation to site tenets**: the Map's `pairing-problem` §"The Map's Position" is this view in quantum dress; its regress (what fixes the location?) is what [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2) books.

### The pairing law is general and origin-based (BRVH §3.1 possibility; Hasker's emergence; Swinburne's foetal law)
- **Core claim**: a law on which a structure of a certain kind produces, or comes to be connected with, a subject that then stays causally tied to that structure.
- **Relation to site tenets**: answers onset; the Map's history-blind [P-CS7](/positions/consciousness-scope/#p-cs7) declined the history-bound reading on 2026-10-11, so the Map holds the *location/adequacy* shape for synchronic pairing and leaves onset undated ([P-SC3](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc3)). The article must not reopen [P-CS7](/positions/consciousness-scope/#p-cs7).

### The pairing relation is singular, haecceitous or brute (Foster, Unger, Audi, Saad 2017, Swinburne)
- **Core claim**: no general law; this subject is tied to this body primitively, or by a law that mentions them.
- **Relation to site tenets**: sits closest to [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1)/[P-I3](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i3)'s haecceitism about subjects, but would make [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2) uncloseable in principle. The Map has not chosen; the article should say so.

### Physics: systems are presupposed, not derived (Zurek; Zanardi–Lidar–Lloyd); or derived from the Hamiltonian (Carroll & Singh); or underivable uniquely (Stoica)
- **Relation to site tenets**: Level 1 is shared with every quantum theory; Levels 2–3 are the Map's.

### Physicalist indexing rules exist and are worked (IIT exclusion; Chalmers & McQueen's Φ-collapse)
- **Relation to site tenets**: these are the only worked "which system" rules in the consciousness literature; the Map has none of its own, which is the asymmetry the article must concede rather than argue around.

## Key Debates

### Is the circularity in location-plus-interaction pairing vicious?
- **Sides**: Kim and the Map's 2026-10-27 pessimistic review say the Map's version is circular; BRVH say the location version is not, provided synchronic possibility rests on locations and only the counterfactual (Glued) rests on interaction.
- **Current state**: BRVH's distinction is available to the Map *only if* it supplies a synchronic location-fixer independent of interaction. It has not (the interface is defined by interaction). Open; booked as [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2).

### Can a pairing law be general at all, given subject haecceities?
- **Sides**: Swinburne, Audi, Foster, Unger: no general law fixes which subject; Wong 2007 (per BRVH): causal transactions are governed by non-haecceitous laws; BRVH: both are logically open.
- **Current state**: unresolved in the literature; undecided in the corpus.

### Is "which system" a problem for consciousness theories or solved by them?
- From the 2026-09-25 note; Tegmark's inversion vs. the circularity objection. Unchanged.

### Does indeterminism dissolve or complicate pairing?
- **Sides**: BRVH §3.2 and Saad 2018's critics: indeterministic causation is a counterexample to "causation requires pairing"; Saad: the argument can be patched.
- **Current state**: open; directly relevant to Tenet 2 and unexamined on the Map.
