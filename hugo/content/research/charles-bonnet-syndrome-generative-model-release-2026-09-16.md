## Executive Summary

Charles Bonnet syndrome is the occurrence of complex, usually silent visual hallucinations in people with acquired sight loss who retain insight and normal cognition. Pooled prevalence in low-vision populations is about one in five (Subhi et al. 2022). The mainstream neuroscientific reading since Cogan (1973) is *release*: loss of afferent input disinhibits or hyper-sensitises visual cortex, which then emits content shaped by its own functional specialisations (ffytche et al. 1998; Santhouse, Howard & ffytche 2000; Painter et al. 2018; daSilva Morgan et al. 2025). The computational version (Reichert, Seriès & Storkey 2013) models this as homeostatic plasticity over-compensating for lost input inside a learned generative model, and the predictive-processing gloss (Corlett et al. 2019) reads hyperexcitable deafferented cortex as priors imposed on noisy input. Every one of these readings agrees on the point that matters for the Map: CBS content is stereotyped, learned, non-veridical and does not restore the lost scene. **No reading in the literature is "compensation" in the phenomenal sense the Map's loci imply**; the only "compensation" in the sources is neuronal (homeostatic firing-rate recovery), and it is exactly what produces the non-corrective hallucinations. Five of the Map's six invoking loci still say "compensatory" or "fill gaps"; one (`lucid-dreaming-and-dualist-rendering` L126) already carries the release reading. A dedicated concept can define the syndrome once, adjudicate the readings, and give the five loci a calibrated cross-link. The syndrome is evidentially neutral between production and filter metaphysics, and the note recommends the article say so plainly.

## Key Sources

### Reichert, Seriès & Storkey (2013) — the generative-model account
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003134
- **Type**: Paper (computational model)
- **Key points**:
  - Abstract, verbatim: "CBS could be taken as indication that there is a *generative model* in the brain, specifically one that can synthesise rich, consistent visual representations even in the absence of actual visual input."
  - Mechanism: a deep Boltzmann machine with homeostatic regulation of neuronal activity. Verbatim: "homeostatic plasticity could serve to make the learnt internal model robust against e.g. degradation of sensory input, but overcompensate in the case of CBS, leading to hallucinations."
  - The paper frames itself against the classic release theory (which it calls "the 'release' theory of complex hallucinations, according to which the lack of input to higher areas from lower areas somehow disinhibits or releases perceptual representations") by supplying a *mechanism* for the release — homeostasis — rather than rejecting it. Corlett et al. 2019 report the model's ablation result: a DBM trained without its top layer failed to learn a generative model and produced no complex hallucinations.
  - Author summary, verbatim: neurons "overcompensate for the lack of sensory input by increasing spontaneous activity."
  - Suggests a role for acetylcholine in balancing feedforward and feedback information; lower ACh gives earlier hallucinations with less adaptation.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral on Tenet 1 (a model of cortex; makes no claim about experience). Conflicts with the Map's *compensation* framing at the loci above. Relevant to Tenet 5: it is the economical account.
- **Fabrication fence**: Gemini's outer review cited this paper as "Blom, R. E., et al. (2024)". That author/year does not exist for this title. Cite Reichert, Seriès & Storkey 2013 only.

### ffytche et al. (1998) — content maps onto cortical specialisation
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/3738
- **Type**: Paper (fMRI, *Nature Neuroscience* 1(8): 738–742)
- **Key points**:
  - Abstract, verbatim: "hallucinations of color, faces, textures and objects correlate with cerebral activity in ventral extrastriate visual cortex, that the content of the hallucinations reflects the functional specializations of the region and that patients who hallucinate have increased ventral extrastriate activity, which persists between hallucinations."
  - Framed as a study of "the neurobiology of visual consciousness" using CBS as a case in which "visual perception and sensory input have become dissociated."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The Map's `capability-division-problem` L96 cites this paper for "compensatory imagery"; the abstract says nothing of the kind.

### Santhouse, Howard & ffytche (2000) — three phenomenological clusters
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/123.10.2055
- **Type**: Paper (*Brain* 123(10): 2055–2064; 34 CBS patients, 28 pathological experiences, exploratory factor analysis)
- **Key points**: Three segregated clusters — (1) "extended landscape scenes and small figures in costumes with hats"; (2) "grotesque, disembodied and distorted faces with prominent eyes and teeth"; (3) "visual perseveration and delayed palinopsia." The clusters "mirror the segregation of hierarchical visual pathways into streams."
- **Why it matters**: the content is stereotyped across patients and tracks cortical architecture, not the individual's missing scene. This is the strongest single datum against the gap-filling reading.

### Painter, Dwyer, Kamke & Mattingley (2018) — hyperexcitability measured
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.08.058
- **Type**: Paper (*Current Biology* 28(21): 3475–3480.e3; SSVEP in macular-degeneration patients with and without hallucinations)
- **Key points**: "An influential but untested hypothesis for the visual hallucinations in CBS is that retinal deafferentation causes hyperexcitability in early visual cortex." CBS individuals "showed strikingly elevated visual cortical responses to peripheral field stimulation compared with patients without hallucinations and controls." Prevalence claim in the abstract: "Up to 40% of ocular pathology patients develop long-term hallucinations." Frames CBS alongside phantom-limb pain and tinnitus as maladaptive plasticity after peripheral damage.

### daSilva Morgan et al. (2025) — multimodal test of deafferentation
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-024-12741-2
- **Type**: Paper (*Journal of Neurology* 272(3): 199; fMRI + EEG + TMS; 19 CBS vs 18 eye-disease controls matched for acuity)
- **Key points**: Reduced BOLD activation in ventral extrastriate and V1 to visual stimulation in CBS; reduced alpha reactivity; occipital cortical slowing (greater theta); reduced TMS phosphene thresholds associated with reduced stimulus-evoked activation and with hallucination severity. Conclusion: "converging lines of evidence to support the role of increased visual cortical excitability … consistent with the deafferentation hypothesis." Note the abstract's own use of "compensatory" for *neural* activity.

### Hahamy, Wilf, Rosin, Behrmann & Malach (2021) — spontaneous activity ignites the hierarchy bottom-up
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awaa384
- **Type**: Paper (*Brain* 144(1): 340–353; 5 CBS participants, 11 non-hallucinating blind, 13 sighted)
- **Key points**: Only the CBS hallucination condition showed "a slow build-up of neural activity prior to the reported onset of hallucinations. This build-up was most pronounced in early visual cortex and then decayed along the visual hierarchy." Interpretation: "in the absence of external visual input, a build-up of spontaneous fluctuations in early visual cortex may activate the visual hierarchy, thereby triggering the experience of vision."
- **Why it matters**: the direction is *bottom-up* (early cortex first), which sits awkwardly with a purely top-down "priors imposed on input" story and comfortably with spontaneous-noise release. Any article that reduces CBS to "unchecked priors" (as `perceptual-failure-and-the-interface` L100 does on the PP rival's behalf) should carry this qualification.

### Corlett et al. (2019) — the strong-prior gloss
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2018.12.001
- **Type**: Review (*Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 23(2): 114–127)
- **Key points**: Strong-prior hypothesis: "hallucinations arise when prior beliefs exert an inordinate influence on perception." On CBS the review leans on Reichert et al.: homeostatic mechanisms stabilise activity "such that hallucinations arise when input is lacking," and reads hyperexcitable deafferented cortex as "the imposition of explanatory priors on noisy inputs." This is the source for the "unchecked priors" phrase the Map already uses.

### Schmid & Kriegleder (2024) — philosophical critique of the strong-prior reading
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2023.103620
- **Type**: Paper (*Consciousness and Cognition* 117: 103620)
- **Key points** (abstract; full text was 403 at the publisher — flagged in Gaps): "the most popular predictive processing model of hallucinatory conditions - the strong prior hypothesis - fails to fully account for the characteristics of nonveridical perceptual experiences associated with Charles-Bonnet-Syndrome"; the PP account "omits essential characteristics of stimulus-independent perception in general, which has critical phenomenological implications"; the fix proposed is to add reality monitoring.
- **Why it matters**: the Map should not swap an over-read (compensation) for an over-read in the other direction (CBS as a solved PP case). Preserved insight is the datum the strong-prior story under-explains.

### Altieri & Battaglini (2026) — "beyond hyperexcitability"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.3390/neurosci7020031
- **Type**: Review (*NeuroSci* 7(2): 31)
- **Key points**: Evidence "increasingly points toward a model involving desynchronization between bottom-up and top-down visual processing pathways, rather than mere hyperexcitability of deafferented visual cortex." Per the summariser's reading of the full text, the review treats deafferentation as necessary but insufficient — a permissive condition that "destabilizes normal perceptual inference" — and invokes acetylcholine as a precision regulator and intact reality monitoring to explain preserved insight. (Full-text quotes here came through the WebFetch summariser; re-grep before quoting in an article.)

### Collerton et al. (2023) — the integrated Visual Hallucination Framework
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2023.105208
- **Type**: Consensus review (*Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 150: 105208; 21 authors incl. ffytche and Friston)
- **Key points**: "Eight models of complex visual hallucinations have been published since 2000, including Deafferentation, Reality Monitoring, Perception and Attention Deficit, Activation, Input, and Modulation, Hodological, Attentional Networks, Active Inference, and Thalamocortical Dysrhythmia Default Mode Network Decoupling." The field agreed an integrated framework rather than a winner. Useful for calibration: "release" is one of eight, and active inference is another; nobody in the consensus paper reads CBS as compensation.

### Collerton, Perry & McKeith (2005) — the PAD model and its objections to release
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X05000130
- **Type**: Target article (*Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 28(6): 737–757)
- **Key points**: Reviews "cortical irritation, cortical hyperexcitability and cortical release, top-down activation, misperception, dream intrusion, and interactive models" and argues none "fully account for the phenomenology of RCVH"; proposes impaired attentional binding plus poor sensory activation of a proto-object within an intact scene representation. The dissent within the field that a calibrated article should acknowledge.

### Teunisse et al. (1996) — the defining clinical series
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(96)90869-7
- **Type**: Paper (*The Lancet* 347(9004): 794–797; 60 of 505 screened visually handicapped patients)
- **Key points**: "all patients had full insight into the unreal nature of their hallucinations"; in 77% the hallucinations "lacked a personal meaning"; "Sensory deprivation and a low level of arousal seemed to favour the occurrence of hallucinations"; only 1 of 16 who consulted a doctor was correctly diagnosed.

### Menon, Rahman, Menon & Dutton (2003) — "phantom vision"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0039-6257(02)00414-9
- **Type**: Review (*Survey of Ophthalmology* 48(1): 58–72)
- **Key points**: "well-defined, organized, and clear images over which the subject has little control. It is believed that they represent release phenomena due to de-afferentation of the visual association areas of the cerebral cortex, leading to a form of phantom vision." Confirms that "release" is the standard clinical framing, and supplies the phantom-limb analogy (also Schultz & Melzack 1991).

### ffytche (2005; 2007) — two syndromes, and the history of the eponym
- **URLs**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11920-005-0050-3 ; https://doi.org/10.31887/DCNS.2007.9.2/dffytche
- **Type**: Reviews
- **Key points**: De Morsier's 1936 syndrome "was intended to eponymize the association of visual hallucinations with age, but evolved into one describing their association with eye disease"; ffytche argues for "two distinct hallucination syndromes: one directly related to visual system pathology, the other to pathology in the brainstem or ascending neurotransmitter pathways" (2005), refined in 2007 to afferent, cholinergic and serotonergic circuitry. A concept article should say which syndrome it means: the *afferent* one.

## Major Positions

### Release / deafferentation (classic)
- **Proponents**: Cogan 1973; Schultz & Melzack 1991; Menon et al. 2003; Burke 2002; ffytche 1998–2008; Painter et al. 2018; daSilva Morgan et al. 2025.
- **Core claim**: Loss of afferent drive removes inhibition and/or triggers homeostatic hyperexcitability in visual cortex; spontaneous activity in specialised regions is experienced as content matching those regions' specialisations. Analogy: phantom limb, tinnitus.
- **Key evidence**: content-specific ventral extrastriate activation persisting between episodes (1998); three content clusters tracking streams (2000); elevated SSVEPs (2018); lowered phosphene thresholds and cortical slowing (2025); bottom-up build-up before report (2021).
- **Relation to site tenets**: Neutral on Tenet 1. Undercuts the Tenet 3 use at `perceptual-failure-and-the-interface` L134: nothing here needs a phenomenal-side contribution.

### Generative-model release (computational)
- **Proponents**: Reichert, Seriès & Storkey 2013 (and the 2011 BMC Neuroscience abstract).
- **Core claim**: The cortex implements a learned generative model; homeostatic plasticity keeps it robust to degraded input but over-compensates when input is lost, so the model synthesises learned content. Without the top (most abstract) layer, no complex hallucinations.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Supplies the mechanism `lucid-dreaming-and-dualist-rendering` L126 already cites. Compatible with a filter reading that grants the brain's architecture supplies the *content* of experience — but it removes any evidential purchase the syndrome had for the filter reading.

### Strong-prior / active-inference gloss
- **Proponents**: Corlett et al. 2019; Powers, Mathys & Corlett 2017 (conditioned hallucinations in healthy subjects); active-inference model within Collerton et al. 2023.
- **Core claim**: Hallucination = priors weighted above sensory evidence; deafferentation reduces sensory precision, so learned priors dominate.
- **Relation to site tenets**: This is the rival the Map's `predictive-processing` and `predictive-processing-and-dualism` articles already answer at the level of *why the inference is experienced*. CBS adds no new leverage in either direction.

### Critics and complications
- **Proponents**: Collerton, Perry & McKeith 2005 (PAD); Schmid & Kriegleder 2024 (reality monitoring); Altieri & Battaglini 2026 (desynchronisation; deafferentation necessary but insufficient); ffytche 2005 (two syndromes).
- **Core claim**: Release/priors explain that *something* is generated but not *when* (episodic, arousal-dependent), not why insight survives, and not why many deafferented patients never hallucinate (Painter's "why some individuals develop hallucinations, while many do not").
- **Relation to site tenets**: These are the honest gaps a calibrated concept can name without claiming them for dualism.

### The Map's "compensation" reading
- **Proponents**: five Map loci (table above); no external source found.
- **Core claim**: the interface "fills gaps" / "actively maintains a phenomenal field" when input drops.
- **Assessment**: unsupported. Predicts scene-restoring, veridical-tending content; the observed content is stereotyped (costumed figures, grotesque faces, tessellated patterns), often novel to the patient, recognised as unreal, and worse in low arousal and darkness. Recommend retiring the word "compensatory" at all five loci, or restricting it to the neuronal sense with the phenomenal sense explicitly denied.

## Key Debates

### Is CBS release or compensation?
- **Sides**: essentially one-sided in the literature — release (in classic, homeostatic or precision vocabulary) versus nobody. The compensation reading exists only in the Map's loci.
- **Core disagreement**: whether generated content restores what was lost. It does not.
- **Current state**: settled against compensation at the level of phenomenology (Teunisse 1996; Santhouse 2000).

### Bottom-up noise or top-down prior?
- **Sides**: Hahamy et al. 2021 (build-up begins in early visual cortex, decays up the hierarchy) versus the strong-prior reading (Corlett et al. 2019) which locates the driver in high-level priors. Reichert's model is agnostic in a useful way: the homeostatic disturbance is low-level, the content comes from the learned hierarchy.
- **Current state**: open; Altieri & Battaglini 2026 frame it as desynchronisation between the two directions.

### Does the strong-prior story explain preserved insight?
- **Sides**: Schmid & Kriegleder 2024 (no, without reality monitoring) versus Corlett et al. 2019 (hierarchies of inference can reconcile it).
- **Current state**: open. This is the one debate where the Map has something modest to add: insight is a *phenomenal* fact (the experience presents as unreal while remaining vivid) that any computational account must describe rather than derive — the same structural point the Map's `predictive-processing-and-dualism` makes about the generative model generally.

### Filter theory and release
- **Note for the article**: the Map's filter tradition is itself release-shaped. Huxley's "reducing valve" and Bergson's account of dreams both explain vivid endogenous experience as what appears when constraint relaxes. So a filter theorist has no stake in "compensation" and can accept release outright; what the filter theorist must give up is any claim that CBS content is *mind-side* — the content tracks cortical specialisation, so the brain supplies it on either metaphysics. `hypnagogic-phenomenology-and-interface-modulation` L132 already states the correct register ("the phenomenological data cannot adjudicate").
