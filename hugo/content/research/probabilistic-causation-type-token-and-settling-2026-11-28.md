## Executive Summary

The literature does separate the two grains. Good (1961–62), Sober (1985) and Eells (1991) defended distinct theories of singular and general causation, and Eells held singular causation to be "independent of general causation" (SEP 2010 supplement). On these accounts a token cause with no type-level counterpart is coherent, which is compatible with the Map.

Two findings cut the other way. First, "type level" means something different there. A type-level cause is one that changes the effect's probability *conditional on the cause*, within background contexts (Cartwright 1979; Eells 1991). No account in the SEP survey tests type causation by the effect's unconditioned frequency alone, the grain [type-token-causation](/concepts/type-token-causation/) L68 stipulates; every account conditions on the cause. In the literature's own terms, then, a mind whose conditional chances differ from those without it is a type-level cause even while the Born marginal holds (horn (a) of [the Born-preservation trilemma](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/)), and a mind that changes no conditional is "causally neutral" (horn (b)). The page's claim (L84) that consciousness can "make a difference at the token level" "without making any difference at the unconditioned type level" is true only in the page's stipulated sense, and a reader who brings the standard sense will misread it.

Second, the token-level theories (Eells's probability trajectories, Lewis's chancy counterfactuals, Kvart, Fenton-Glynn) still ask whether the cause changes the effect's probability in the actual situation. This is the Map's own analysis, which no source makes: a settling raises the outcome's probability to 1 at its own moment, exactly as a chance settling would. The criteria therefore credit the settling only in the way they credit any chancy event that fixes a later one, or they exclude it when the settling *is* the outcome's actualisation (Eells requires causes to precede effects). Either way they cannot say whether a mind did the settling. A conscious state that precedes the settling counts as a token cause only if it changes the outcome's probability, which is the propensity branch of [the probability fork](/concepts/rollback-argument-against-agent-causation/#probability-fork).

The sharpest opponent is Hitchcock (2007), who suggests that the metaphysical concept of token causation is "an unstable compromise" between causal structure and value-laden attribution. On that picture, the Map's claim about *who* settles would have to show up in causal structure, and so in conditional statistics, or else be a folk attribution of responsibility.

The literature neither denies nor supports the claim that a chance-settling mind causes anything. It restates the open [bias-without-deviation question](/positions/quantum-interface/#mechanism-debt) ([P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3)) in its own vocabulary: genuine selection shows up either as conditional structure, which is type-level in this literature's sense, or as a categorical claim about authorship that probabilistic theories do not measure. That is the trichotomy [born-preserving-causal-efficacy](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/) already draws ("Three things kept distinct"). Every tenet link below is graded *compatible* at most.

### Hitchcock, "Probabilistic Causation", SEP (current version, substantive revision Fri Mar 9, 2018)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/causation-probabilistic/
- **Type**: Encyclopedia (full text read)
- **Key points**:
  - Vocabulary: "It is common to distinguish between general, or type-level causation, on the one hand, and singular, token-level or actual causation, on the other" (§1.4). Also: "At a minimum, in claims of actual causation, "cause" functions as a success verb" (§1.4).
  - Motivation from indeterminism: "If an event is not determined to occur, then no other event can be (or be a part of) a sufficient condition for that event" (§1.1).
  - Eells's general-level taxonomy (§2.5) relative to background contexts B. A factor is a positive cause, a negative cause, causally neutral (equal probabilities in every B) or a mixed cause. In every case the comparison is P(E | C & B) against P(E | ~C & B), and "Eells claims that general causal claims must be relativized to a population." Hitchcock's own gloss: "This suggests that causal relevance, rather than positive causation, is the most basic metaphysical concept."
  - Dupré's alternative (§2.6) averages over background contexts: "Indeed, the inequality in Dupré is what one looks for in randomized trials." This is still a comparison conditional on the cause.
  - Actual causation (§4): the potential-versus-actual contrast: "Billy's throw had a tendency to shatter the bottle; it was a potential cause of the bottle shattering; it was the sort of thing that generally causes shattering; but it did not actually cause the bottle to shatter." The theories that track probabilities over time: "Some, such as Eells (1991: chapter 6), Kvart (1997, 2004), and Glynn (2011), pay careful attention to the way in which probabilities change over time."
  - Probability-lowering actual causes: "Actual causes can sometimes lower the probability of their effects in cases of preemption".
  - Lewis's (1986b) causal dependence (CD): C and E occur at times t and t′; at t the probability of E was x; had C not occurred, the probability at t would have been at most y; and x ≫ y. "On this account, the relevant notion of 'probability-raising' is not understood in terms of conditional probabilities, but in terms of unconditional probabilities in different possible worlds."
  - Fenton-Glynn (2017): "The key idea is that it is not enough for Billy's throw to raise the probability of the bottle shattering; Billy's throw together with what happens afterwards has to raise the probability of shattering."
  - State of the field: "it is safe to say that no analysis of actual causation is widely believed to perfectly capture all of our pre-theoretic intuitions about hypothetical cases" (§4.5).
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral on every tenet. It supplies the vocabulary the Map uses without citing it.

## Major Positions

### Independence: two causal relations (Good; Sober 1985; Eells 1991)
- **Core claim**: general (type) and singular (token) causation are distinct relations with distinct relata and need distinct theories. Eells: token causation relates "two actually occurring, concrete, token events", and type-level causation relates "properties," "types," or "factors".
- **Cost, stated by the SEP**: "two distinct species of causal relation, each requiring its own philosophical account".
- **Relation to the tenets**: this is the strongest support in the literature for the *coherence* of a token cause with no type-level counterpart, and it is compatible with Tenet 3. It does not say that probabilities are irrelevant to the token relation. Eells's token theory is itself probabilistic.

### Reduction of type claims to singular ones (Lewis 1973; Carroll 1991)
- **Core claim**: general causal claims generalise over singular ones (SEP 2010 §1.3).
- **Relation to the tenets**: neutral. If anything it favours the Map's priority of the token, though Lewis's own token criterion is probabilistic (CD above).

### Probability-trajectory token theories (Eells 1991 ch. 6; Kvart 2004; Glynn 2011)
- **Core claim**: a token cause changes the effect's probability, *in the actual background*, at the time of the cause, and the change persists ("because of") or is undone ("autonomously of").
- **Relation to the tenets**: see the analysis below. They credit settlings only as they credit any chance event, and they credit a conscious state only if it shifts the outcome's probability.

### Interventionist and causal-modelling accounts (Woodward 2003; Halpern and Pearl; Fenton-Glynn 2017)
- **Core claim**: token causation is difference-making under interventions with other variables held at their actual values. In indeterministic settings that difference is a difference in probabilities or expectations.
- **Relation to the tenets**: already handled in the Map (trilemma; [selection-only-channel](/concepts/selection-only-channel/)'s marginal-preserving, maximal-dependence example).

### Eliminativism about metaphysical token causation (Hitchcock 2007)
- **Core claim**, offered as a suggestion: objective causal facts are exhausted by causal structure, and binary "C caused E" verdicts beyond it are folk attributions with an evaluative component.
- **Relation to the tenets**: adverse to the Map's authorship claim. It is not a parsimony argument the Map can wave off with Tenet 5; see the grading below.

### Causal pluralism in the Anscombe line (van Miltenburg 2022)
- **Core claim**: there are fundamentally different kinds of causality, and agent-causal power can be one of them.
- **Relation to the tenets**: compatible with Tenet 3. Abstract only.

## Key Debates

### Probability-lowering causes
- **Sides**: Rosen's golf case (Suppes 1970), Salmon (1980) and Hesslow (1976) show that actual causes can lower their effects' probability. The repairs are Eells's trajectories (the slice is a cause because the ball's probability rises once it heads for the tree), Hitchcock's contrasts (1996, 2004a), path decomposition (Dowe 2004; Hitchcock 2001, 2004b) and Fenton-Glynn's causal-modelling definition.
- **Core disagreement**: whether a token cause must raise probability at all, or only relative to the right contrast, route or later time.
- **Current state**: Hitchcock (2004a) treats the first type as handled. **For the Map**, the literature already accepts token causes that do not raise type-level probability. **Against the Map**, every repair still finds a probability change *somewhere* in the actual history: along a route, against a contrast, or later on the trajectory. None credits a cause that leaves every probability unchanged up to the moment the effect occurs.

### Probability-raising non-causes (fizzlers)
- Billy's throw raises the probability of shattering and causes nothing. Hitchcock (2004a): "Counterexamples of the second sort ... still pose a threat", and they raise "fundamental questions about the nature of indeterministic causation". The question is open, so the Map should not cite any single token criterion as *the* standard.

### Contextual unanimity against fair-sample averaging (Cartwright and Eells against Dupré 1984)
- Both sides condition on the cause. Hitchcock's SEP gloss is that the dispute is "about how best to use the word 'cause'", not about which factors are causally relevant. **For the Map**, on either side the effect's unconditioned frequency is never the test by itself, so Born-exactness of the marginal does not show that a mind is type-level inert.

### Is there objective token causation over and above causal structure?
- Hitchcock (2007) suggests not, while the Anscombe line (van Miltenburg 2022), Steward's settling and agent-causalists say there is a further kind. This is the debate in which the Map's categorical "who settles" claim actually sits, and it is open.

## The Key Question: Does Settling an Undetermined Outcome Count as Causing It? {#settling}

**This section is the Map's own analysis.** No cited author applies these criteria to a chance-settling mind. The sources supply the criteria, and the application below is inference.

The question splits once the candidate relatum is fixed. Two different things in the Map's picture could be "the cause".

**(A) The conscious state M**: reasons, intention, attention, present before the outcome O.
- *Type level, in Eells's sense.* M is causally relevant to O if P(O | M & B) ≠ P(O | ~M & B) in some background context B, and causally neutral if they are equal in every B. The Born marginal does not enter.
- So under horn (a) of the Born-preservation trilemma (the conditionals differ), M is a type-level cause in the literature's sense: positive, negative or mixed. The marginal can still be Born-exact over the natural distribution of states only if other states offset M. That is horn (c), which [born-preserving-causal-efficacy](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/) shows collapses under intervention into (a) or a superselection law.
- Under horn (b) (no conditional differs), M is causally neutral.
- *Token level.* On Eells's trajectory test, M is a cause ("because of") only if O's probability is higher just after M than just before and stays higher until O. Lewis's CD, which is sufficient but not necessary for his causation (the ancestral of CD), holds only if O's chance with M much exceeds its chance without M. **If M leaves O's probability at the Born weight, M fails Eells's test and Lewis's CD.** That is horn (b) again. The Map does not hold horn (b): [P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2) leaves the mind-conditioned distributions free, and [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3) is open. So this is a verdict on a position the Map declines, and no concession of epiphenomenalism.

**(B) The selection S**, whose value is which outcome occurs.
- *If S precedes O and is a distinct event*, O's probability goes from the Born weight p to 1 at S. Eells's "because of" holds. A contrastive account counts "S = A rather than S = B". Lewis's CD holds only if 1 ≫ p, so on Lewis's (iv) the settling of a near-certain outcome would not count as causal dependence, an odd consequence for selection-only accounts (causation, the ancestral, could still run through a chain). Every account credits S, at least when p is not close to 1.
- *But* the same profile, p before and 1 after, belongs to any chancy micro-event that fixes a later macro-event. The criteria credit S exactly as they credit chance, and they **cannot say whether a mind did the settling**.
- *If S is the outcome's actualisation*, which [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1)'s wording suggests ("the selection *is* the effective reduction to a single actual branch"), then S and O coincide in time. Eells's requirement "that causes precede their effects in time" places S outside the relation "cause of O". Lewis's analysis is also usually stated for distinct events, but that detail is from memory of Lewis (1973) and was not verified today. S is then the outcome's occurrence and a candidate cause only of what follows. That moves the question from causation to constitution; see [constitution-vs-causal-work](/concepts/constitution-vs-causal-work/).

**What follows.**
1. "Settling" is not a category in these theories. They credit (A) only on the propensity branch, and (B) only as they credit any chance outcome.
2. The difference the Map claims, "which thing is the causal source" ([P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2)), is categorical. These criteria measure probability profiles, and on the Map's reading a settling's profile is the same whoever or whatever settles. This matches the parity [Bedrock at the Token](/concepts/rollback-argument-against-agent-causation/#bedrock) already concedes, and claim 3 ("Metaphysical authorship") of the trichotomy in [born-preserving-causal-efficacy](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/).
3. The literature therefore leaves the Map two honest options, and both are already on disk. One is to assert conditional structure: horn (a), where the mind is type-level relevant in the standard sense and testable by conditioning (Dupré's measure is "what one looks for in randomized trials"). The other is to hold authorship as a categorical primitive that probabilistic theories neither confirm nor refute. That second option meets Hitchcock (2007) head-on.

**It cuts both ways.**
- *For the Map*:
  - Independence makes token-without-type coherent.
  - Probability-lowering causes show that token causes need not raise type-level probability.
  - No account tests type causation by the unconditioned marginal alone, so Born-exactness of the marginal never shows that a mind is inert.
  - Eells's taxonomy gives horn (a) a standard name ("mixed cause").
- *Against the Map*:
  - Every token criterion still needs a probability change in the actual history.
  - A conscious state that changes nothing is no token cause.
  - A settling is credited only as chance is.
  - "Type-level invisibility" stops being a separate selling point once the literature's vocabulary is used.
  - Hitchcock (2007) suggests that the authorship claim has no objective content beyond causal structure.

**For the open apex task** (todo, `apex/consciousness-and-agency`, blocked on length): the ChatGPT ask "Why is one control and the other chance rather than merely being labelled differently?" gets its sharpest literature form here. Probabilistic token criteria cannot tell the two apart, so the answer has to be the categorical one, stated as such. This is a lead for whoever unblocks that task. No edit is proposed.

## Relation to Site Perspective (graded: compatible / suggestive / discriminating)

- **[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism): neutral.** Nothing here bears on whether consciousness is physical. Good, Suppes, Cartwright, Sober, Eells, Hitchcock and Woodward are naturalists working in philosophy of science. Never enlist them for dualism.
- **[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction): compatible.** The default reading constrains only the unconditioned long-run marginal, and the literature's type-level test is conditional, so Born-exactness neither shows nor denies type-level causation in Eells's sense. The cost is vocabulary: the Map's phrase "no type-level difference" must be read as "no difference in the marginal".
- **[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction): compatible on horn (a); silent on authorship.** On horn (b) the literature's verdict is "causally neutral". The Map does not hold horn (b), so this is a verdict on a declined position. **Never write that the literature shows a Born-neutral mind to be epiphenomenal, and never write that the Map concedes it.**
- **[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds): neutral.** Actual causation presupposes single actual outcomes ("cause" as "a success verb"), but no source discusses Everett. Do not cite this as support.
- **[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits).** The tenet binds parsimony on both sides (`tenets.md` L145).
  - Hitchcock's clause "we can make do without the metaphysical concept altogether" is dispensability-shaped, and the tenet denies it decisive force.
  - The tenet equally bars the Map from dismissing Hitchcock's *error theory* (that philosophers mistook folk attributions' binary grammar for an objective relation) on grounds of simplicity. That clause is no parsimony claim and must be met on the merits, by saying what the authorship claim explains that causal structure does not.
