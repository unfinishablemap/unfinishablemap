## Executive Summary

The reviewers are right that the corpus never engages the neuroeconomic common-currency programme or sequential-sampling models on the value fork, and wrong in one word: neither rival is *value-blind*. The vmPFC/OFC "subjective value" of Levy & Glimcher (2012), Chib et al. (2009), Bartra et al. (2013) and Padoa-Schioppa (2006, 2011, 2020) is a value-*laden* scalar, defined by its decisional role — Sripada's 2026 defence states the tradition's assumption outright: value as a common currency "should be understood fundamentally in terms of its decisional effects," while its links to "the subjective phenomenal experience of pleasure and pain … are not explored here" [raw]. The programme is *phenomenality-neutral*, and that is a different rival from the one the Map's own "value-blind horn" describes (content-neutral attention and intention with no value term in the selection law at all).

Three findings organise the note. **(1) The currency premise is a three-way, not a two-way, dispute.** [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) ("selection needs a criterion … the Map proposes valence"), the common-currency programme and the value-free camp — Vlaev et al. (2011) "comparison-only" choice, Hayden & Niv (2021) "direct learning of action policies," Hunt & Hayden (2017) value as "emergent rather than represented explicitly" — divide as *felt currency / functional currency / no currency*. On the premise that selection among incommensurables needs a scalar at all, the Map is an ally of neuroeconomics against Hayden & Niv, and it inherits neuroeconomics' burden: the value-free camp is precisely the non-arbitrary, currency-free selector that ChatGPT §6.3 said the Map's "without valence, arbitrary" dichotomy overlooks. Sripada (2026) is the current defence of the premise the Map shares. **(2) Sequential-sampling models are the worked dynamics of selection, and they locate the only place a Map-style selection could sit — and locate a tension there.** In a drift-diffusion model value enters as drift rate (Krajbich, Armel & Rangel 2010), urgency and dopamine set the bound (Cisek 2009; Thura & Cisek 2017), and at near-indifference (drift ≈ 0) the outcome is left to noise. Shadlen & Roskies (2012) say of that term, verbatim, "the existence of noise does not confer any special properties, like freedom, will, consciousness, etc." [raw]. The corpus already places the Map's selection in exactly that residue for the motor and dopamine cases (`concepts/motor-selection` L131; `topics/dopamine-and-the-unified-interface` L62) without connecting it to the valence fork — and the connection exposes a problem: the residue opens where the currency, felt or computed, reads zero. A value-sensitive bias inside the noise needs felt anticipatory valence to carry information the neural subjective value lacks at the margin, which is a dissociation claim, not a denomination claim. **(3) The attentional drift-diffusion model is the formal skeleton of the graduated middle path.** Krajbich's aDDM has evidence accumulate "in proportion to its subjective value, amplified by attention" [abstract]; Smith & Krajbich (2019) confirm the amplifying, not additive, reading. "Valence sets attentional gain, attention drives selection" and "attention multiplies value into drift" are two orderings of one product term, and the aDDM is fully classical — which is why the middle path and the steelman's "valence only informs the channel" concession are co-extensive, now with a formalism showing exactly where. The urgency signal, the other candidate role for felt valence, is a *global* multiplicative gain that sets when to commit rather than what to choose; routing valence through it lands on the middle path again.

Where consciousness enters these models is settled by the models' authors in the deflationary direction: Kang et al. (2017) find that "conscious awareness of having reached a decision appears to arise when the brain's representation of accumulated evidence reaches a threshold or bound" [raw]; Pereira et al. (2021) show single-neuron evidence accumulation tracking perceptual consciousness itself; Vlassova et al. (2014) fit unconscious evidence with "a simple diffusion model in which the rate and variability of evidence accumulation is reduced but not eliminated by the removal of conscious awareness" [abstract]; Pessiglione et al. (2007) show effort scaling with subliminal incentives. The felt-currency premise, if it is to out-argue rather than ignore these, has to be split into three claims of different standing — a scalar exists at choice (grade C, contested by the value-free camp), the scalar is felt (grade D), and the felt scalar does selective work the neural scalar does not (grade D plus the mechanism debt) — with the third stated as the dissociation claim it is. Two corpus defects surfaced in passing and are logged under [Gaps](#gaps-in-research): the wanting/liking article's "no single neural value currency" inference, which Berridge's own 2014 neuroeconomics chapter with O'Doherty contradicts, and the valence article's use of Cleeremans & Tallon-Baudry (2022) for "a mental currency of sorts" without recording that the authors answer "Are we defending a dualist position?" with "an emphatic 'no'" [raw].

## Key Sources

### Levy & Glimcher (2012), "The root of all value: a neural common currency for choice" — *Current Opinion in Neurobiology* 22(6):1027–1038 [abstract]
- **Key points**: "Economists have long argued on theoretical grounds that humans typically make these choices as if the values of the options they consider have been mapped to a single common scale for comparison." Meta-analysis of thirteen fMRI studies: "the principle brain area associated with this common representation is a subregion of the ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC)/orbitofrontal cortex (OFC)." Framed as "a neurobiological foundation for standard economic theory."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral on Tenet 1; the "as if" is the economist's revealed-preference construct, and nothing in the abstract addresses whether the scale is felt. This is the canonical statement of the rival Claude §2.4 named. Note the definitional move: the common scale is defined *by* choice behaviour, so "selection is denominated in it" is close to analytic — the same near-circularity the Map's terminology map already flags for Kahneman's decision utility.

### Chib, Rangel, Shimojo & O'Doherty (2009), "Evidence for a common representation of decision values for dissimilar goods in human ventromedial prefrontal cortex" — *J. Neurosci.* 29(39):12315–12320 [abstract]
- **Key points**: Real purchasing decisions across food, non-food consumables and monetary gambles; "a single area in vmPFC to be correlated with the subjects' valuations for all categories of goods … the brain encodes a 'common currency' that allows for a shared valuation for different categories of goods."
- **Tenet alignment**: The direct empirical form of the "commensurating currency for qualitatively different outcomes" that the Map's L105 says valence provides. The Map's argument that only valence can make colours and movements commensurable has a worked neural competitor here.

### Bartra, McGuire & Kable (2013), "The valuation system: a coordinate-based meta-analysis of BOLD fMRI experiments examining neural correlates of subjective value" — *NeuroImage* 76:412–427 [abstract]
- **Key points**: 206 studies. Two patterns: regions where "both positive and negative effects of SV on BOLD are reported" (anterior insula, dmPFC, dorsal/posterior striatum, thalamus), "potentially reflect[ing] an underlying U-shaped function, indicative of signal related to arousal or salience"; and vmPFC/anterior ventral striatum where "positive effects predominate … seen both when a decision is confronted and when an outcome is delivered, as well as for both monetary and primary rewards. These regions appear to constitute a 'valuation system,' carrying a domain-general SV signal."
- **Tenet alignment**: Supplies the salience/value dissociation the graduated middle path needs on the neural side: the U-shaped (salience) regions are the attentional-gain candidates, vmPFC the currency candidate. Neutral on phenomenality.

### Padoa-Schioppa & Assad (2006), "Neurons in the orbitofrontal cortex encode economic value" — *Nature* 441:223–226 [abstract]; Padoa-Schioppa (2011), "Neurobiology of economic choice: a good-based model" — *Annu. Rev. Neurosci.* 34:333–359 [abstract]; Padoa-Schioppa & Conen (2017) — *Neuron* 96(4):736–754 [abstract]; Ballesta, Shi, Conen & Padoa-Schioppa (2020), "Values encoded in orbitofrontal cortex are causally related to economic choices" — *Nature* 588:450–453 [abstract]
- **Key points**: 2006: "neurons in the orbitofrontal cortex (OFC) encode the value of offered and chosen goods … independently of visuospatial factors and motor responses," so "economic choice is essentially choice between goods rather than choice between actions." 2011: "Values are not learned as such, but rather computed at the time of choice. Most importantly, values are compared within the space of goods, independent of the sensorimotor contingencies of choice." 2017: OFC cell groups "encode the input and the output of the decision process," and "are computationally sufficient to generate decisions." 2020: "Low-current stimulation increased the subjective value of individual offers and thus predictably biased choices. Conversely, high-current stimulation disrupted both the computation and the comparison of subjective values, and thus increased choice variability."
- **Tenet alignment**: The strongest form of the rival, because Ballesta 2020 makes it *causal*, not correlational — electrical stimulation of the value code moves the choice. For [P-VS2](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs2)'s tier-2 test ("disrupting the conscious hedonic signal while leaving wanting circuitry intact should change selection"), this is the mirror experiment: disrupting the *neural* value signal changes selection, with the phenomenal side unmeasured. Also important: "computed at the time of choice" means Padoa-Schioppa's subjective value is an *occurrent* construction, like the "presently felt anticipation" [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) relocated to on 2026-08-27, not the cached V/Q of the terminology map. The relocation therefore does not by itself separate the felt currency from the neural one.

### Rangel, Camerer & Montague (2008), "A framework for studying the neurobiology of value-based decision making" — *Nat. Rev. Neurosci.* 9:545–556 [abstract; body via summariser]
- **Key points**: The field's canonical process decomposition — representation, valuation, action selection, outcome evaluation, learning — with three valuation systems (Pavlovian, habitual, goal-directed) and a separate comparison step ("options with different values need to be compared in order to make a choice" [summariser]).
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Its use here is architectural: it separates *valuation* from *selection*, which is the distinction the Map's currency argument runs together ("valence denominates them; consciousness selects"). Under this framework the Map's claim is about the valuation stage's denomination *and* the selection stage's mechanism at once.

### Vlaev, Chater, Stewart & Brown (2011), "Does the brain calculate value?" — *Trends Cogn. Sci.* 15(11):546–554 [abstract]
- **Key points**: A spectrum from the "'value-first' view … that the brain computes the value of different options and simply favours options with higher values" to the "'comparison-only' view [which] argues that choice depends directly on comparisons, with or even without any intermediate computation of value"; the review reports "an increasing focus on comparison rather than value as the driver of choice."
- **Tenet alignment**: This is the *no-currency* option. Neutral on dualism, but it attacks the premise the Map shares with neuroeconomics: that selection among incommensurables needs a scalar. If comparison-only choice is real, "without a currency, selection would be arbitrary" is false at the level of the selector's structure.

### Hayden & Niv (2021), "The case against economic values in the orbitofrontal cortex (or anywhere else in the brain)" — *Behavioral Neuroscience* 135(2):192–201 [abstract; body via summariser]
- **Key points**: "Much of traditional neuroeconomics proceeds from the hypothesis that value is reified in the brain, that is, that there are neurons or brain regions whose responses serve the discrete purpose of encoding value." Their alternative: "That economic value, in the cardinal, common-currency sense, is not represented in the brain and used for choice by default," motivated by "the behavioral economics literature, especially work on heuristics, which proposes value-free process models for much if not all of choice," and by "direct learning of action policies." The body [summariser] contains no substantive discussion of feeling or consciousness; a footnote references the "wanting" not "liking" side of value.
- **Tenet alignment**: The sharpest no-currency statement from inside systems neuroscience, by two leading figures in the field. It is the rival to the *premise* of [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1), where Levy & Glimcher are the rival to its *content*. Note for calibration: Hayden & Niv are arguing a position, and the field has not conceded it — Ballesta 2020 (causal OFC value) and Sripada 2026 are on the other side. The Map should cite the dispute as live, not pick a winner.

### Juechems & Summerfield (2019), "Where does value come from?" — *Trends Cogn. Sci.* 23(10):836–850 [abstract]
- **Key points**: Open challenges for reinforcement learning as a model of natural behaviour: "how the external reward function is designed for biological systems, and how we can account for the context sensitivity of valuation"; the proposal that "animals track current and desired internal states and seek to minimise the distance to a goal across multiple value dimensions."
- **Tenet alignment**: Two uses. First, a *vector* account of value, in which the scalar is a distance in a multi-dimensional state space — Cleeremans & Tallon-Baudry 2022 [raw] group it with Hayden & Niv as "distance in a multi-dimensional quality space" against the "'common currency' scalar signal." This is a worked reply to ChatGPT §6.5's demand that [P-VS4](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs4)'s pluralism specify "an aggregation or vector-selection rule": the neuroscience offers two, scalarisation at choice (common currency) and distance minimisation (Juechems & Summerfield). Second, the homeostatic framing is the "homeostatic RL" rival ChatGPT §7 listed separately.

### Grueschow, Polania, Hare & Ruff (2015), "Automatic versus choice-dependent value representations in the human brain" — *Neuron* 85(4):874–885 [abstract]
- **Key points**: Values affect behaviour "when people explicitly base their actions on such values, and second, when values attract attention despite being irrelevant for current behavior." "In the medial prefrontal cortex, value-related activity is enhanced when subjective value becomes choice-relevant … In contrast, activity in the posterior cingulate cortex represents values similarly when they are relevant or irrelevant for the present choice, and the strength of this representation predicts attentional capture by choice-irrelevant values."
- **Tenet alignment**: The task brief described this paper as "relative vs absolute value coding"; that is not its finding, and an article must not carry the mis-description. Its actual contribution is a neural dissociation between value-as-currency (mPFC, choice-gated) and value-as-attentional-magnet (PCC, choice-independent) — a second neural handle on the middle path's valence→attention arrow. Neutral on phenomenality.

### Louie, Khaw & Glimcher (2013), "Normalization is a general neural mechanism for context-dependent decision making" — *PNAS* 110(15):6139–6144 [abstract]; Hunt & Hayden (2017), "A distributed, hierarchical and recurrent framework for reward-based choice" — *Nat. Rev. Neurosci.* 18:172–182 [abstract]; Stott & Redish (2015), "Representations of value in the brain: an embarrassment of riches?" — *PLOS Biology* 13(6):e1002174 [summariser]
- **Key points**: Louie: divisive normalisation of value signals generates "classically irrational" context effects, so "the neural mechanism of value coding critically influences stochastic choice behavior." Hunt & Hayden: "choices emerge from repeated computations that are distributed across many brain regions … certain correlates of value are emergent rather than represented explicitly." Stott & Redish [summariser]: the multiplicity of value signals raises whether regions "are performing similar or distinct functions."
- **Tenet alignment**: Together these show the common-currency thesis is *internally* contested — relative not absolute coding, emergent not represented, many signals not one — so the Map should not cite "the vmPFC common currency" as a settled finding it must beat. Louie's normalisation is also directly relevant to the Map's own claim that the currency is unstable and context-built (middle path L46: "constructed in the anticipating — context-dependent and unstable"): the neural scalar has the same property.

### Sripada (2026), "The case for value as a common currency in decision-making and intersystem competition" — *Frontiers in Cognition* 5:1767189 [raw]
- **Key points**: "A common currency is a single continuous dimension that represents the overall expected goodness or badness of the alternatives. Potentially dissimilar alternatives are located on this dimension, enabling comparison on a common scale." Distinguishes decision-phase from learning-phase currencies and options-level from meta-level currencies. Against Hayden & Niv's policy-gradient argument: "these cumulative reward signals constitute a scalar medium of value, and they play all the critical functional roles that value is supposed to play." Against the heuristics argument: "a two-step decision-making process in which the agent uses value to select among heuristics and then applies the selected heuristic to bypass value in selecting among the options." On phenomenality: "This article follows the perspective of most work in the neuroeconomic tradition that assumes that value as a common currency should be understood fundamentally in terms of its decisional effects — the core functional role of value is to serve as a basis for selecting among competing alternatives. It is likely that, in addition, representations of scalar value are richly interconnected with the subjective phenomenal experience of pleasure and pain. But these links are highly complex and potentially context-specific (Berridge and Kringelbach, 2008, 2015; Shenhav, 2024; Carruthers, 2024), and they are not explored here." Zero occurrences of "consciousness" or "feel" in the body.
- **Tenet alignment**: The single most useful source for the article. It (i) is the current, systematic defence of the premise the Map shares; (ii) states the tradition's phenomenality-bracketing explicitly, which licenses the note's "phenomenality-neutral, not value-blind" correction of the reviewers; (iii) shows that the currency premise survives the value-free critique only by *relocating* the currency (into the learning phase, or to a meta-level that chooses among systems) — which is a template the Map could copy for the felt currency, and a warning that relocation can make a currency claim unfalsifiable. Note also that Sripada's "meta-level common currency" (which *system* to follow) is structurally the Map's own attention question (which subsystem receives observation).

### Ratcliff (1978) — *Psychol. Rev.* 85(2):59–108 [metadata]; Ratcliff & McKoon (2008), "The diffusion decision model: theory and data for two-choice decision tasks" — *Neural Computation* 20(4):873–922 [metadata]; Usher & McClelland (2001), "The time course of perceptual choice: the leaky, competing accumulator model" — *Psychol. Rev.* 108(3):550–592 [metadata]
- **Key points**: The canonical accumulator family. In the DDM a single decision variable drifts at rate *v* (evidence quality) with Gaussian noise toward one of two bounds ±*a*; the starting point *z* encodes prior bias; non-decision time covers encoding and motor output. Usher & McClelland replace the single variable with mutually inhibiting, leaky accumulators, one per option — the form closest to Cisek's competition.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The parameters are the vocabulary the article needs: ChatGPT §7's "valence into changed drift rate, starting point, boundary or sampling allocation" names four distinct places a valence effect could enter, each with a different empirical signature, and [P-VS2](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs2)'s "selection efficacy" is unoperationalised until it says which.

### Krajbich, Armel & Rangel (2010), "Visual fixations and the computation and comparison of value in simple choice" — *Nat. Neurosci.* 13(10):1292–1298 [abstract]; Krajbich & Rangel (2011) — *PNAS* 108(33):13852–13857 [metadata]; Krajbich (2019), "Accounting for attention in sequential sampling models of decision making" — *Curr. Opin. Psychol.* 29:6–11 [abstract]; Smith & Krajbich (2019), "Gaze amplifies value in decision making" — *Psychol. Sci.* 30(1):116–128 [abstract]
- **Key points**: 2010: "a computational model of value-based binary choice in which fixations guide the comparison process," explaining "fixation-driven decision biases." 2019 review: sequential sampling models "assume that evidence for a given alternative is collected over time in proportion to its subjective value, amplified by attention. Furthermore, findings from a number of studies support a causal effect of attention on choice," and "attention is drawn to noisier and more salient stimuli in two-alternative choice, as well as higher-value options in multi-alternative choice." Smith & Krajbich test "whether it amplifies the subjective value of the looked-at option or instead adds a constant, value-independent bias," finding results "in line with an amplifying role of attention."
- **Tenet alignment**: The formal home of the graduated middle path — see [Where consciousness enters](#where-consciousness-enters-and-where-it-does-not). The aDDM's drift is a product of value and an attentional weight; the middle path's specification is that valence sets attentional gain and targeting. Same product, opposite emphasis. And Krajbich 2019's "attention is drawn to … higher-value options" closes the loop the middle path needs (value → attention → amplified value). The "causal effect of attention on choice" from exogenous fixation manipulation is the experimental form of the Map's attention clamp, already run, and the results are what both the middle path and the steelman concession predict.

### Busemeyer, Gluth, Rieskamp & Turner (2019), "Cognitive and neural bases of multi-attribute, multi-alternative, value-based decisions" — *Trends Cogn. Sci.* 23(3):251–263 [abstract]
- **Key points**: Sequential sampling models extended to preferential choice "account for the context effects associated with preferential choice tasks," at the cost that "more complex decision mechanisms have had to be introduced."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral; the review to cite for the state of the art rather than the 2008 DDM alone.

### Cisek & Kalaska (2010), "Neural mechanisms for interacting with a world full of action choices" — *Annu. Rev. Neurosci.* 33:269–298 [abstract]; Cisek (2012), "Making decisions through a distributed consensus" — *Curr. Opin. Neurobiol.* 22(6):927–936 [abstract]
- **Key points**: 2010: an "ethologically-inspired view of interactive behavior as simultaneous processes that specify potential motor actions and select between them." 2012 asks directly whether the brain decides "through comparisons of abstract representations of outcomes or through a competition in a sensorimotor map defining the actions themselves," answering that "decisions emerge through a distributed consensus across many levels of representation."
- **Tenet alignment**: Cisek 2012 is the explicit bridge between the two rivals this note covers — the good-based common currency (Padoa-Schioppa) and the action-based competition (affordance competition, already in the steelman). The Map should present them as one family that disagrees about *where* the currency lives, not as two rivals.

### Cisek, Puskas & El-Murr (2009), "Decisions in changing conditions: the urgency-gating model" — *J. Neurosci.* 29(37):11560–11571 [abstract]; Thura, Beauregard-Racine, Fradet & Cisek (2012) — *J. Neurophysiol.* 108(11):2912–2930 [abstract]; Thura & Cisek (2014), "Deliberation and commitment in the premotor and primary motor cortex during dynamic decision making" — *Neuron* 81(6):1401–1416 [abstract]; Thura & Cisek (2017), "The basal ganglia do not select reach targets but control the urgency of commitment" — *Neuron* 95(5):1160–1170 [abstract]
- **Key points**: 2009: the build-up may be "attributable to a growing signal related to the urgency to respond, which multiplicatively modulates updated estimates of sensory evidence." 2012: "the policy that maximizes reward rate is to estimate evidence by accumulating only novel information and then compare the result to a decreasing accuracy criterion … the brain approximates this policy by multiplying an estimate of sensory evidence with a motor-related urgency signal." 2014: PMd/M1 activity "did not integrate sensory information but instead tracked it and combined it with a growing urgency signal," with commitment ~280 ms before movement. 2017: "pallidal output does not contribute to the process of deliberation but instead provides a time-varying signal that controls the SAT and reflects the growing urgency to commit."
- **Tenet alignment**: Urgency is the model's second candidate for what felt valence might be. It is *global* — one multiplicative gain over all options — and reward-rate-derived; it decides *when* to commit, not *which* option wins. If [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) relocated the felt currency to urgency, valence would occupy the gain slot, which is the graduated middle path's slot. The 280 ms commitment figure already lives in `concepts/motor-selection` L92.

### Shadlen & Kiani (2013), "Decision making as a window on cognition" — *Neuron* 80(3):791–806 [abstract]; Shadlen & Shohamy (2016), "Decision making and sequential sampling from memory" — *Neuron* 90(5):927–939 [abstract]
- **Key points**: "A decision is a commitment to a proposition or plan of action based on information and values associated with the possible outcomes." 2016 extends the framework to value-based choice by proposing that "the sequential character of such tasks involves retrieval of evidence from memory," so that "mnemonic processes can account for regularities in choice and decision time."
- **Tenet alignment**: 2016 matters for the Map's anticipatory-valence mechanism: the "samples" a value-based accumulator integrates are, on this account, retrieved memories of outcomes — which is the mechanistic counterpart of "the felt valence of anticipating each outcome" and, like it, occurrent at choice. The rival has a worked story for *what is sampled*; the Map's story ("consciousness assesses the predicted felt quality") has none beyond the phenomenology.

### Shadlen & Roskies (2012), "The neurobiology of decision-making and responsibility: reconciling mechanism and mindedness" — *Front. Neurosci.* 6:56 [raw]
- **Key points**: "As far as we understand, the existence of noise does not confer any special properties, like freedom, will, consciousness, etc. However, the noise does have very real effects. For example, errors in perceptual decisions can be traced to the variable discharge of cortical neurons." And: "Noise puts a limit on an agent's capacities and control, but invites the agent to compensate for these limitations by high-level decisions or policies that may be (a) consciously accessible; (b) voluntarily malleable; and (c) indicative of character."
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with the Map's residue localisation *as the authors read it*; the Map's reply must be that it does not claim noise *confers* anything, but that a bias within the noise term at near-indifference is the only place a Tenet-2-compliant influence could sit — and then face the tension recorded below, that the currency reads zero there.

### Kang, Petzschner, Wolpert & Shadlen (2017), "Piercing of consciousness as a threshold-crossing operation" — *Current Biology* 27(15):2285–2295 [raw]
- **Key points**: Participants set a clock to when they felt they had decided; "The subjective decision times … were well fit by a bounded drift-diffusion model. The fits to the [subjective times] alone furnished parameters that fully predicted the choices (accuracy) of four of the five participants." "Thus, conscious awareness of having reached a decision appears to arise when the brain's representation of accumulated evidence reaches a threshold or bound. We propose that such a mechanism might play a more widespread role in the 'piercing of consciousness' by non-conscious thought processes." The threshold is "registered, subjectively, as an 'aha' moment, similar to the moment that Libet's participants reported about their will to move."
- **Tenet alignment**: The single sharpest datum on where consciousness enters an accumulator: at the bound, *after* the accumulation that fixed the outcome. For the value-sensitive horn this is the wrong place — felt valence would need to be operative during accumulation, in the drift, not at commitment. The Map can consistently say the *feeling of having decided* is downstream while the *felt valence of options* is upstream, but that is a distinction the article must draw explicitly, and Kang et al. give it no support.

### Pereira, Mégevand, Tan et al. (2021), "Evidence accumulation relates to perceptual consciousness and monitoring" — *Nat. Commun.* 12:3261 [raw]
- **Key points**: Single-neuron recording in human posterior parietal cortex during near-threshold vibrotactile detection: "detected stimuli elicited neuronal responses resembling evidence accumulation during decision-making, irrespective of motor confounds or task demands"; modelled as "a stimulus as detected if accumulated evidence reaches a bound, and confidence as the distance between maximal evidence and that bound." The introduction states the global-workspace assumption in accumulator terms: the global broadcast "is triggered when an (unconscious) evidence accumulation process reaches a threshold similar to the physiological processes underlying decision-making," and the model is "compatible with all-or-none views of consciousness."
- **Tenet alignment**: Extends the Kang result from *deciding* to *perceiving*: conscious access itself modelled as bound-crossing. This is the "Dehaene's ignition as accumulation to bound" item in the task brief, stated by a 2021 empirical paper rather than reconstructed from the 2011 review (Dehaene & Changeux 2011 [abstract] describes ignition as "late amplification … long-distance … synchronization … and 'ignition' of a large-scale prefronto-parietal network" without the accumulator language; Dehaene's 2008 chapter title "Conscious and nonconscious processes: distinct forms of evidence accumulation?" [metadata] is the earliest statement).

### Vlassova, Donkin & Pearson (2014), "Unconscious information changes decision accuracy but not confidence" — *PNAS* 111(45):16214–16218 [abstract]; Pessiglione et al. (2007), "How the brain translates money into force: a neuroimaging study of subliminal motivation" — *Science* 316:904–906 [abstract]
- **Key points**: Vlassova: "unconscious information can be accumulated over time and integrated with conscious elements … These data are fit well by a simple diffusion model in which the rate and variability of evidence accumulation is reduced but not eliminated by the removal of conscious awareness," with "poor metacognition for unconscious decisional evidence." Pessiglione: "even when subjects cannot report how much money is at stake, they nevertheless deploy more force for higher amounts."
- **Tenet alignment**: Both are value-processing-without-feeling results of the Winkielman & Berridge kind, and both are in the *accumulator* vocabulary. Vlassova's is the more interesting for the middle path: awareness raises drift rate and lowers its variability — which is what "attention sets gain" would predict — but for perceptual evidence, with no value term. Pessiglione's is an incentive-to-vigour result (effort, not choice among goods), so it bears on the Map's "selection efficacy vs vigour" problem (ChatGPT §5.4) rather than on the currency.

### Berridge & O'Doherty (2014), "From experienced utility to decision utility," in Glimcher & Fehr (eds.), *Neuroeconomics: Decision Making and the Brain*, 2nd ed., pp. 335–351 [raw, pdftotext]
- **Key points**: "Experienced utility is the endpoint of the decision process … However, other signals are needed in order for decisions to actually be made." "Decision utility is proposed to be the utility signal used at the point of choice to guide decisions about future actions." On wanting: "'Wanting' typically gives a felt 'oomph' to conscious desires that makes a desire feel more urgent, able to influence choice and produce action … Yet the core process of 'wanting' can also occur unfelt as a relatively unconscious process," citing Pessiglione 2007 and Winkielman; "'wanting' is intrinsically an unconscious process, perhaps because it is mediated chiefly by subcortical brain systems, but can be elaborated into conscious cravings when additional brain systems of awareness are recruited." The chapter locates experienced-utility correlates in medial OFC and discusses "the extent to which vmPFC is causally" involved. The string "currency" occurs nowhere in the chapter.
- **Tenet alignment**: Decisive for the ChatGPT §2.2 objection. Berridge, writing inside the neuroeconomics handbook, treats wanting/liking fractionation and a decision-utility signal at the point of choice as *complementary*, not as evidence against a later-stage common representation. The Map's "no single neural 'value currency'" therefore over-reads its own witness. What the chapter does *not* do is use the "common currency" phrase — so the correct fix is "no single neural *hedonic* currency; fractionated wanting and liking are compatible with a decision-utility signal at choice (Berridge & O'Doherty 2014)," not a claim that Berridge endorses the vmPFC common currency.

### Cleeremans & Tallon-Baudry (2022), "Consciousness matters: phenomenal experience has functional value" — *Neurosci. Conscious.* 2022(1):niac007 [raw]
- **Key points**: "Phenomenal experience might act as a mental currency of sorts, which not only endows conscious mental states with intrinsic value but also makes it possible for conscious agents to compare vastly different experiences in a common subject-centred space." But: "Are we defending a dualist position? The short answer is an emphatic 'no'. Here, we assume 'phenomenal realism' … and 'phenomenal efficacy', i.e., the thesis that phenomenal states are causally efficacious. In this, we follow List (2019) … mental states are entirely caused by neural activity, but, contra Kim (2005), this does not mean they are epiphenomenal." And the hypothesis "makes the 'hard problem' of consciousness more tractable, since it can then be reduced to a problem about function." They also survey the value definitions: "a 'common currency' scalar signal (Montague and Berns 2002; Chib et al. 2009; Levy and Glimcher 2012), as distance in a multi-dimensional quality space (O'Doherty 2014; Juechems and Summerfield 2019; Hayden and Niv 2021)."
- **Tenet alignment**: The Map's source for "mental currency" is a physicalist, compatibilist, hard-problem-deflating paper. `topics/valence-and-conscious-selection` L103 cites it with no stance caveat — the author-stance discipline the steelman applies to Berridge and Hesp is missing here. Their "phenomenal efficacy" is List's level-of-description efficacy, which the Map elsewhere classes as no downward work at all. The identity reading (felt valence *is* the common currency, on a physicalist ontology) is theirs; the Map needs the felt currency to be a *distinct* variable.

### Shuman, Sander & Scherer (2013), "Levels of valence" — *Front. Psychol.* 4:261 [raw in the sibling note]
- **Key points**: "Micro-valences correspond to qualitatively different types of evaluations, potentially resulting in mixed feelings, whereas one-dimensional macro-valence corresponds to an integrative 'common currency' to compare alternatives for choices."
- **Tenet alignment**: See [Agreement with the sibling note](#agreement-with-the-sibling-note-on-macro-valence).

### Shenhav (2024), "The affective gradient hypothesis: an affect-centered account of motivated behavior" — *Trends Cogn. Sci.* 28(12):1089–1104 [abstract]; Carruthers (2024), *Human Motives: Hedonism, Altruism, and the Science of Affect*, OUP [metadata]
- **Key points**: Shenhav: "According to dominant models, actions are directed by estimates of value and these values shape or are shaped by affect. I propose instead that affect is the only form of value that drives actions. Our mind constantly represents potential future states and how they would make us feel … Motivated behavior reflects the process of traversing this affective gradient." Carruthers 2024 is the book-length statement of the "new hedonism" on which affect is the motivating currency.
- **Tenet alignment**: The nearest physicalist neighbours to [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1): an *affect-as-the-only-currency* thesis, in 2024, in the field's flagship review journal — Sripada cites both as the links he brackets. Shenhav's "how they would make us feel" is the Map's anticipatory valence mechanism in physicalist dress. This is the "affect-as-information / somatic-marker" middle position ChatGPT §7 called "a more serious middle position than 'mere accompaniment'," and it is the ally-and-rival the article should engage: it grants everything [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) says about denomination and denies that anything non-physical does the work.

## Major Positions

### Felt currency (the Map, [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1))
- **Proponents**: The Map; nearest external neighbours Shenhav 2024 and Carruthers 2024 (physicalist), Cleeremans & Tallon-Baudry 2022 (physicalist, compatibilist).
- **Core claim**: Selection among incommensurable outcomes is denominated in the presently felt valence of anticipating each; the feeling is what selection consults.
- **Key arguments**: Commensurability (every experience shares the valence dimension); non-arbitrariness (a selector without a criterion is noise); the correlation problem for epiphenomenalism.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Tenets 1 and 3; held at low credence, grade D, per the register. The physicalist neighbours accept the denomination claim and reject the causal one — which shows the denomination claim carries no dualist weight on its own.

### Functional currency (common-currency neuroeconomics)
- **Proponents**: Montague & Berns 2002; Padoa-Schioppa & Assad 2006; Rangel, Camerer & Montague 2008; Chib et al. 2009; Kable & Glimcher 2009; Levy & Glimcher 2012; Bartra et al. 2013; Padoa-Schioppa & Conen 2017; Ballesta et al. 2020; Sripada 2026.
- **Core claim**: A scalar subjective value, computed at choice and represented in vmPFC/OFC, is what comparison operates on; defined by its decisional role; phenomenality bracketed.
- **Key arguments**: Behavioural transitivity; a common vmPFC signal across goods; causal stimulation results; formal defence against value-free critics.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Neutral on Tenet 1, silent on Tenet 3. Compatible with the Map's denomination claim under an identity reading (felt = neural SV) that is physicalist, or a correlate reading that is epiphenomenal about the feeling, or a dissociation reading that is the Map's and needs evidence.

### No currency (value-free / comparison-only / policy learning)
- **Proponents**: Vlaev, Chater, Stewart & Brown 2011; Hayden & Niv 2021; Hunt & Hayden 2017 (value as emergent); Zhou, Gardner & Schoenbaum 2021 (OFC as predictor, not valuer) [metadata].
- **Core claim**: Choice can be produced by direct comparison, heuristics or learned policies without any cardinal value being computed; putative value signals are correlates or emergent quantities.
- **Key arguments**: Policy-gradient RL learns without values; context effects and preference reversals; the epistemic circularity of inferring value from the choices it is supposed to explain.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Attacks the premise [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) shares with neuroeconomics. If sound, "selection needs a criterion" is false as a claim about the selector's structure, and the Map's L99 dichotomy fails for the reason ChatGPT §6.3 gave. The Map cannot answer by appeal to consciousness; it must answer, with Sripada, that policies and heuristics are themselves value-selected — and then note that the relocation is the same move it would be accused of.

### Selection as bounded accumulation (sequential-sampling models)
- **Proponents**: Ratcliff 1978; Usher & McClelland 2001; Ratcliff & McKoon 2008; Krajbich, Armel & Rangel 2010; Cisek, Puskas & El-Murr 2009; Thura & Cisek 2014, 2017; Shadlen & Kiani 2013; Busemeyer et al. 2019.
- **Core claim**: Options are selected when a noisy evidence variable, whose drift is set by value (and attention), reaches a bound set by urgency/speed–accuracy policy; the decision is the bound-crossing.
- **Key arguments**: Joint fit of choice and reaction-time distributions; neural build-to-threshold in LIP, PMd, and pallidal urgency; the aDDM's fixation–choice relations; subjective decision times (Kang 2017).
- **Relation to site tenets**: Neutral on Tenet 1, but it fixes *where* a Tenet-2-compliant selection could sit — the noise term at near-zero drift — and shows that the model's authors (Shadlen & Roskies) deny the term confers anything. The formalism is what gives the Map's "value-sensitive vs value-blind" a parameter-level meaning: valence in the drift (currency), in the attentional weight (middle path), in the bound (urgency), or in the noise (residue).

## Key Debates

### Does selection need a currency at all?
- **Sides**: Common-currency neuroeconomics (Levy & Glimcher; Padoa-Schioppa; Sripada 2026) versus comparison-only and policy-learning accounts (Vlaev 2011; Hayden & Niv 2021).
- **Core disagreement**: Whether a cardinal scalar is computed and compared, or whether choice is produced by procedures that never compute one.
- **Current state**: Live. Ballesta 2020's causal result and Sripada's relocation defence are the currency side's strongest cards; Hayden & Niv's is the field's most-cited statement of the opposite. The Map's L99 premise is a currency-side thesis and should be labelled as such, at the currency side's grade, not as a framework-internal given.

### Is the scalar the currency, or a by-product of the comparison?
- **Sides**: Good-based comparison in OFC (Padoa-Schioppa) versus distributed consensus and emergent value (Cisek 2012; Hunt & Hayden 2017) versus normalisation-relative coding (Louie 2013).
- **Core disagreement**: Whether value is represented once and read, or reconstructed context-dependently at many levels.
- **Current state**: Unsettled; the Map's own "constructed in the anticipating — context-dependent and unstable" (middle path L46) sits nearer the distributed side, which is worth saying.

### Where does consciousness enter an accumulator?
- **Sides**: At the bound, as the registration of commitment (Kang 2017; Pereira 2021 for perception); during accumulation, as a modulator of drift (Vlassova 2014's awareness-raises-drift result; the Map's middle path); nowhere in particular, with awareness downstream of both (the default physicalist reading).
- **Core disagreement**: Whether felt states are inputs to the accumulation or reports of its termination.
- **Current state**: The empirical work supports the bound reading for the *feeling of deciding* and says nothing about the *felt valence of options*; the Map's article must keep the two apart or the Kang result refutes a claim the Map does not make.

### Is felt valence the drift, the gain, the bound, or the noise?
- **Sides**: Currency reading (drift); middle path (attentional weight θ in the aDDM); urgency reading (bound); residue reading (bias within the noise at near-indifference).
- **Core disagreement**: Each places valence in a different parameter with a different empirical signature: drift changes accuracy and speed together; gain changes the fixation–choice relation; bound changes speed–accuracy trade-off without accuracy gain (Chakroun 2023 for dopamine, already in the corpus); noise-bias changes nothing in aggregate.
- **Current state**: Only the first three are measurable, and the corpus's clamp battery addresses only the gain reading. The residue reading is the Map's, and it is the one that is invisible by construction.

## Where consciousness enters, and where it does not

Four results, all in the accumulator vocabulary, bracket the space.

1. **The feeling of having decided is the bound-crossing** (Kang et al. 2017 [raw]). The subjective decision time is not a post-hoc report; it predicts accuracy from an accumulator fit. On the mechanism-sufficiency reading, consciousness arrives at commitment. The value-sensitive horn is untouched only if it keeps "felt valence of the options during deliberation" distinct from "feeling of commitment" — a distinction the current articles do not draw.
2. **Conscious perception is itself modelled as bound-crossing** (Pereira et al. 2021 [raw]; Dehaene 2008 [metadata]). The global-workspace assumption that broadcast "is triggered when an (unconscious) evidence accumulation process reaches a threshold" makes access a threshold event downstream of value-blind accumulation. If the Map's interface acts through attention, and attention is what raises evidence to ignition, the interface acts *before* access — which is the Map's existing claim (attention as the channel) restated in the rival's terms, and no new problem.
3. **Awareness raises drift rate and lowers its variability without being necessary** (Vlassova et al. 2014 [abstract]). This is the closest empirical shape to "attention sets gain": a parameter change, not a new term. It supports the middle path's causal skeleton and, equally, the physicalist's reading of it.
4. **Value energises behaviour below awareness** (Pessiglione et al. 2007 [abstract]; Berridge & O'Doherty 2014 [raw] on unfelt "wanting"). The value-to-vigour channel runs without feeling. This bears on effort, not on choice among goods, and is the reason [P-VS2](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs2)'s "selection efficacy, not motivation" is unoperationalised until it names a DDM parameter.

Against this, the tension the note adds to the corpus. The Map's own localisation of its selection (motor-selection L131; dopamine L62) is the noise term at near-indifference — the trials where drift ≈ 0 because the options' values are matched. But drift is set by the value difference: on the currency reading felt valence *is* what sets drift, so at drift ≈ 0 felt valence is also indifferent, and a valence-denominated bias has nothing to denominate. The residue is open exactly where the currency reads zero. Three escapes are available, each with a cost. (a) Felt anticipatory valence carries information the neural subjective value lacks at the margin — a *dissociation* claim, testable in principle (choice at neural-SV indifference tracking reported anticipatory affect), and the one [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) must actually make. (b) The relevant selection is not among goods but among neural patterns finer than the good-level SV — which reintroduces the ~10-bit bandwidth problem [P-VS2](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs2) just retired from the battery, since the finer-grained the residue, the less a 10-bit-per-second selector can address it. (c) Valence acts through gain, not drift — the middle path — in which case the strong horn is abandoned as before. The article should say that (a) is the horn's real content, and that every other reading collapses into the middle path or the rival.

## Is a computationally specified currency value-blind in the Map's sense?

No, and the distinction is worth an article section because the reviewers' word is doing quiet work. The Map's fork defines the value-blind horn as selection through "attention and intention as *content-neutral mechanisms* … None of these requires valence to function" (valence L69). The common-currency programme is the opposite: selection *is* denominated in a value scalar; the scalar simply has its phenomenal status bracketed (Sripada [raw]). So the neuroeconomic currency is structurally *value-sensitive* selection on a functional definition of value, and the fork the Map needs is three-way: content-neutral (no value term), functional-value (a value term of unstated phenomenal status), felt-value (a value term whose phenomenal character is what selection consults).

The constitution-versus-causal-work distinction (ChatGPT §6.2, §7) then sorts the readings of "the felt valence is the currency":

- **Identity**: felt valence just is the vmPFC subjective value, physically constituted; efficacy is List-style, level-of-description efficacy (Cleeremans & Tallon-Baudry 2022 [raw]). Available to the Map only as a psychophysical *correlation* law, since the Map denies the identity; and on the Map's own accounting, correlation-without-downward-work is epiphenomenalism about the feeling.
- **Correlate**: felt valence co-varies with SV and adds nothing; the rival's default.
- **Dissociation**: felt valence and neural SV come apart in some cases and choice follows the feeling. This is the only reading on which [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) asserts something the rival denies, and it converts the currency premise into a prediction: at neural-value indifference (Padoa-Schioppa 2013's "choice variability" trials; Ballesta 2020's high-current disruption), choice should track reported anticipatory affect. It also inherits the explanatory-gap screen — reported affect is a report — so it moves the horn from untestable to testable-in-principle-with-a-confound, which is a real gain in calibration terms and no gain in evidence.

The honest summary for the register: the value-sensitive horn is not "selection has a felt currency" but "felt valence is a selection variable *distinct from* the computed one," and its external-evidence grade is D because no dissociation datum exists — while the functional-currency claim it presupposes is grade C and contested by a value-free camp the Map has never named.

## Agreement with the sibling note on macro-valence

The sibling note (`research/constructed-emotion-and-valence-as-a-natural-kind-2026-09-05`) reads Shuman, Sander & Scherer 2013 as a challenge to [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) rather than [P-VS3](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs3): micro-valences are felt, and "one-dimensional macro-valence corresponds to an integrative 'common currency' to compare alternatives for choices," so the scalar the currency premise needs is manufactured at the point of selection rather than consulted there. The neuroeconomic material here says the same thing from a second discipline: Padoa-Schioppa's subjective value "integrate[s] different determinants" and is "computed at the time of choice" [abstract]; Louie's normalisation makes it context-relative; Cisek's consensus makes it emergent. Both notes therefore converge on one structural claim — *the scalar at choice is an integration, not a primitive* — and differ only on what is integrated (appraisal micro-valences; option attributes and states) and on whether the inputs are felt (the appraisal route says yes; the neuroeconomic route brackets it). The division of labour holds: the sibling handles the appraisal version, this note the neuroeconomic one, and neither lands on [P-VS3](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs3), whose distributive identity over evaluative phenomenal character survives integration. The two notes also agree that [P-VS4](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs4)'s pluralism needs an aggregation story; this note adds that the neuroscience offers two (scalarisation at choice; vector-distance minimisation, Juechems & Summerfield 2019), and the Map has not chosen.

## How [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) could be reformulated to out-argue rather than ignore the rivals

Proposals for `/positions-evolve` and for the article's "Relation to Site Perspective."

1. **Split the assertion into three graded claims.** (i) Selection among incommensurables operates on a scalar computed at choice — shared with common-currency neuroeconomics, grade C, contested by Vlaev 2011 and Hayden & Niv 2021, currently defended by Sripada 2026. (ii) The scalar is felt — the Map's, grade D, with Shenhav 2024 and Carruthers 2024 as physicalist co-proponents of the denomination and opponents of the metaphysics. (iii) The felt scalar is a selection variable distinct from the neural one — the dualist add-on, grade D plus the mechanism debt, with a named dissociation test and no datum. State that (iii) is the horn's whole content.
2. **Retire the "otherwise arbitrary" dichotomy** (valence L99; ChatGPT §6.3), replacing it with the currency-side argument Sripada actually runs: policies and heuristics are themselves value-selected. Then flag that this relocation is available to the Map's critics too — a felt currency relocated to the learning phase or to a meta-level is harder to falsify, not better supported.
3. **Give the middle path its formalism.** Valence as attentional weight θ in the aDDM; the clamp as exogenous fixation control (Krajbich 2019's "causal effect of attention on choice"); co-extensiveness with the steelman concession stated as the aDDM being classical. This makes the concession precise rather than rhetorical.
4. **Name the urgency route and close it.** "Felt valence sets the urgency signal" places valence in the bound, a global gain that fixes commitment timing, not choice content (Thura & Cisek 2017). It is a middle-path variant, not a rescue of the strong horn.
5. **State the residue tension** as a "would shift if" condition: if felt anticipatory valence never dissociates from decoded subjective value at choice, the noise-residue localisation and the currency premise cannot both hold, and [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) retires to the middle path.
6. **Carry stance caveats into the register** for Cleeremans & Tallon-Baudry (compatibilist, "emphatic 'no'" to dualism) and for Berridge & O'Doherty 2014 (decision utility at choice compatible with fractionation) — the register-seam fix the 2026-09-04 synthesis already asks for, applied to two new sources.

## Gaps in Research

- **Corpus defect 1 — over-read inference.** `topics/wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork`, "What the dissociation does and does not settle," lists "there is no single neural 'value currency'" among what the dissociation *secures*. Berridge & O'Doherty 2014 [raw] locate "decision utility … used at the point of choice" alongside fractionated wanting/liking; ChatGPT §2.2's objection stands. Fix: "no single neural *hedonic* currency; fractionation is compatible with a decision-utility signal at choice (Berridge & O'Doherty 2014) and with a common-currency representation in vmPFC/OFC (Chib et al. 2009)." Do not attribute the "common currency" phrase to Berridge; it does not occur in the chapter.
- **Corpus defect 2 — missing stance caveat.** `topics/valence-and-conscious-selection` L103 cites Cleeremans & Tallon-Baudry 2022 for "a mental currency of sorts" with no note that the authors give "an emphatic 'no'" to dualism, adopt List's compatibilism, and aim to reduce the hard problem "to a problem about function" [raw]. Same author-stance discipline the steelman applies to Berridge and Hesp; same register-seam risk the 2026-09-04 synthesis flagged.
- **Task-brief mis-description.** The harvest task describes Grueschow et al. 2015 as "relative vs absolute value coding"; its finding is automatic (PCC, attention-capturing) versus choice-dependent (mPFC) value representation [abstract]. Relative-versus-absolute coding is Louie, Khaw & Glimcher 2013 (normalisation). An article must not inherit the brief's label.
- **No direct Padoa-Schioppa reply to Hayden & Niv was found.** WebSearch located no 2022 reply article; the Behavioral Neuroscience piece came out of the 2019 Paris OFC meeting's special issue. The currency side's answers are Ballesta 2020 (causal) and Sripada 2026 (conceptual); Zhou, Gardner & Schoenbaum 2021 ("signal values or make predictions?") is a third voice not read this session [metadata]. Confirm before citing any "reply."
- **Hayden & Niv body and Rangel 2008 body read through the summariser only.** The abstract quotations are verbatim; the section headings and the five-process list are summariser-grade and should be checked against the PMC author manuscript before an article quotes them.
- **No value-based analogue of Kang 2017.** The subjective-decision-time result is perceptual. Whether the felt moment of *preferring* also sits at a bound in value-based choice (Shadlen & Shohamy's memory-sampling regime) is, as far as this session found, untested — and it is the experiment that would bear on the Map's "felt valence during deliberation vs at commitment" distinction.
- **No dissociation datum.** Nothing found tests choice at neural-SV indifference against reported anticipatory affect. This is the experiment reading (a) of the residue tension needs, and the note's proposed "would shift if."
- **Somatic-marker replication problems, PIT/vigour, homeostatic RL** (ChatGPT §7's other items) not researched here; they are separate rivals and the vigour item is where "selection efficacy" should be operationalised.
