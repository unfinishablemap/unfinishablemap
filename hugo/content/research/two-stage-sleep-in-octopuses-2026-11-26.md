## Executive Summary

Two independent groups, working on two octopus species, report two sleep states. In *Octopus insularis* (Medeiros et al. 2021, Ribeiro lab, Natal), long "Quiet sleep" bouts (median 415.2 s) alternate with brief, periodic "Active sleep" (median 40.8 s), which shows dynamic skin patterns, rapid eye movements and a raised arousal threshold. In *Octopus laqueus* (Pophale et al. 2023, Reiter lab, OIST), active sleep rebounds after deprivation and is rapidly reversible. In head-fixed animals it is accompanied by wake-like local field potentials, strongest in the superior frontal and vertical lobes. Skin patterns during active sleep run through patterns seen in waking animals. Quiet sleep shows spindle-like 12–18 Hz oscillations, but not the brain-wide slow waves of vertebrate slow-wave sleep. Cuttlefish show a comparable cyclic state (Frank et al. 2012; Iglesias et al. 2019). Both octopus papers judge the resemblance to vertebrate REM/NREM sleep **probably convergent** (analogous, not homologous). Both leave its function open.

**Neither paper claims that octopuses dream.** "Dream" does not occur in Medeiros's full text. In Pophale it occurs only in the title of a cited 1957 paper. Dreaming was raised in press coverage, at a hedged grade (see the [Quotation Ledger](#ledger)). **Sleep architecture is not a consciousness marker in the current frameworks.** The 2021 LSE/Defra cephalopod sentience review never uses the word "sleep" in its 56,951 words. The 2024 New York Declaration page uses it only in a signatory's affiliation. The 2012 Cambridge Declaration did cite sleep circuitry as evidence. The finding bears on comparative questions about what complex brains need offline. On the tenets it is **compatible and nothing more**.

## Key Sources

### Medeiros et al. (2021), "Cyclic alternation of quiet and active sleep states in the octopus", *iScience* 24(4): 102223
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2021.102223 · full text PMC8101055 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8101055/)
- **Type**: Primary behavioural study. Four adult *O. insularis*, video, automated skin and eye analysis, visual arousal-threshold test. Senior author Sidarta Ribeiro.
- **Key points** (all VERIFIED, full text):
  - "Two distinct states unresponsive to stimulation occurred in tandem." Quiet sleep: "uniformly pale skin, closed pupils, and long episode durations (median 415.2 s)". Active sleep: "dynamic skin patterns of color and texture, rapid eye movements, and short episode durations (median 40.8 s)".
  - "'Active sleep' was periodic (60% of recurrences between 26 and 39 min) and occurred mostly after 'Quiet sleep' (82% of transitions)."
  - **Internal inconsistency**: the Results and Discussion give the same 60% band as "between 29.58 min and 32.98 min" (79 intervals). The abstract's "26 and 39 min" and the body's figures disagree. On a page, say "roughly every half hour" or omit the figure.
  - Arousal threshold rose "from the 'Alert' state to QHH, 'Quiet sleep', and 'Active sleep'".
  - The authors flag that they "did not assess sleep rebound". Homeostasis is inferred from *O. vulgaris* work (Brown et al. 2006; Meisel et al. 2011).
  - Homology: "it is likely that the sleep behaviors observed here, despite their similarity to those found in amniotes, are analogous rather than homologous to these states". The similar wake-sleep sequences in non-homologous brains are "strongly suggestive of convergent evolution".
  - The independent-evolution inference is conditional: "If extended to multiple species of non-cephalopod mollusks, this difference suggests an independent evolution of 'Active sleep' in cephalopods and amniotes" (contrast with *Aplysia* and *Lymnaea*, which appear to have one sleep state).
  - Function left open: "It remains to be investigated whether the physiological functions of sleep ... also resemble the functions performed in amniotes, such as metabolic detoxification ... and cognitive processing".
  - The abstract's conclusion: "These results suggest that cephalopods have an ultradian sleep cycle analogous to that of amniotes."
  - No occurrence of "dream" or "conscious" in the full text (positive controls: `REM` 14, `octopus` 71).
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.

### Pophale et al. (2023), "Wake-like skin patterning and neural activity during octopus sleep", *Nature* 619(7968): 129–134
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06203-4 · full text PMC10322707 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10322707/)
- **Type**: Primary behavioural and electrophysiological study. *O. laqueus*, OIST, senior author Sam Reiter. Teresa Iglesias, an author of the cuttlefish work below, is a co-author.
- **Key points** (all VERIFIED, full text):
  - Octopuses "evolutionarily diverged from vertebrates roughly 550 million years ago".
  - Active bouts "are homeostatically regulated, rapidly reversible and come with increased arousal threshold, representing a distinct 'active' sleep stage". The deprivation test: "Preventing sleep for 2 days ... resulted in a notable increase in the rate of active bouts in the two nights following deprivation". Interrupting one active bout brought the next "roughly 22 min sooner".
  - In a typical 24 h at 22 °C: "10 ± 3.5 active bouts of 75 ± 28 s in duration and 12 ± 3 QS bouts of 50.5 ± 16.43 min" (n = 3 animals). Bout interval shortens about 5 min per 1 °C rise.
  - "the local field potential (LFP) activity during active sleep resembles that of waking". It is strongest in the superior frontal and vertical lobes, "regions associated with learning and memory function".
  - **Method caveat**: the recordings are from "head-fixed octopuses using multi-site Neuropixels probes". Before fixation "the distal 2–4 cm of all arms were surgically shortened". The authors report that sleep under fixation had AS "duration and interval similar to those of AS in freely behaving animals". Do not write "EEG" or "brain scans".
  - Quiet sleep: "12–18-Hz oscillations ... resembling mammalian sleep spindles", **but** "QS seems to differ from vertebrate slow wave sleep in showing no low-frequency oscillations entraining large areas of the brain".
  - Active-sleep skin patterns are "conserved across octopuses and strongly resembling those seen while awake".
  - Atonia: "a lack of anatomical homology complicates comparison with the atonia of skeletal muscles found in vertebrate REM sleep".
  - Function, the authors' two hypotheses: "offline refinement of skin pattern control, analogous to processes thought to occur during vertebrate motor learning", or "the reactivation of neural activity underlying waking experience more broadly, reminiscent of vertebrate phenomena linked to memory consolidation such as rodent hippocampal replay". They also say AS dynamics "are consistent with the pseudo-random activation of this high-level control system".
  - Convergence: "these phenomena probably evolved independently from each other, and may represent convergent solutions to shared problems facing complex agents". The abstract has "aspects of two-stage sleep in octopuses may represent convergent features of complex cognition".
  - "Dream" occurs only in the title of reference 41 (Dement & Kleitman 1957). "Conscious" occurs 0 times.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.

## Major Positions

### Convergent solution for complex brains (the authors' reading)
- **Proponents**: Medeiros et al. 2021; Pophale et al. 2023; Becchetti 2025.
- **Core claim**: two-stage sleep arose independently in cephalopods and vertebrates (and possibly arthropods). It may answer "shared problems facing complex agents".
- **Grade**: "probably" and "may", in the authors' words. The independence claim depends on single-state sleep in other molluscs (Medeiros's "if extended" condition).
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral. Convergence on an offline-processing architecture is a fact about brains. It says nothing about whether the offline states are experienced.

### Sensorimotor maintenance (deflationary)
- **Proponents**: Blumberg et al. 2026; Pophale's first hypothesis ("offline refinement of skin pattern control").
- **Core claim**: the phasic events (twitches, skin-pattern flashes, eye movements) calibrate and maintain motor systems. Twitching may have evolved several times for this.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral. This reading needs no experience at all, and it is the default the Map should state beside any experiential reading.

### Reactivation or replay (dream-adjacent)
- **Proponents**: Pophale's second hypothesis. The OIST release's "they could be doing something similar to dreaming". Medeiros and Ribeiro in press (NPR 2021).
- **Core claim**: active sleep reactivates waking neural activity, perhaps for consolidation.
- **Grade**: a hypothesis. Neither paper tests it, and "dreaming" appears in press copy only, hedged ("We currently don't know which of these explanations, if any, could be correct", Reiter).
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral. Replay is a neural process. Whether it is experienced is exactly what the data cannot reach.

### Active sleep and the evolution of consciousness (hypothesis)
- **Proponents**: van De Poll & van Swinderen 2021.
- **Core claim**: active sleep is widespread and may serve predictive processing, with "implications for the evolution of subjective awareness".
- **Grade**: a proposal. On the Map's evidential-status scale, at most a live hypothesis for any consciousness link.
- **Relation to site tenets**: compatible with interface and production readings alike.

## Key Debates

### Homology or analogy?
- **Sides**: Medeiros ("analogous rather than homologous"); Pophale ("probably evolved independently"); Blumberg 2026 ("seem homologous", unargued).
- **Core disagreement**: whether a shared ancestral sleep-state mechanism exists below the level of brain anatomy. One 2026 review argues that sleep's molecular layers predate neurons (Fraigne, Torontali & Pressey 2026, *Front. Neurosci.* 20: 1886828, VERIFIED (abstract)). That would leave room for deep homology of the *drive* even where the *architecture* converged.
- **Current state**: open. The safe page wording is "probably convergent".

### Do octopuses dream?
- **Sides**: press speculation, hedged (Ribeiro; the OIST release; Schnell 2021, "At this stage, however, it is just speculation". Schnell is also a co-author of the LSE/Defra review); a single-animal preprint (Ramos et al. 2023); deflationary sensorimotor readings (Blumberg 2026).
- **Core disagreement**: whether wake-like neural activity plus waking-like skin patterns licenses an experiential reading. Schnell gives the obstacle: "We cannot confirm whether octopuses dream, because they can't verbally report their dreams."
- **Current state**: unresolved and currently unresolvable by the methods in use. The evidential-status discipline names "octopuses dream" as one of its own examples of a label that must say what the catalogue has.

### Is sleep architecture a consciousness marker?
- **Sides**: the Cambridge Declaration (2012) counted sleep circuitry and avian REM. The LSE/Defra criteria (2021) and the New York Declaration (2024) do not use sleep.
- **Core disagreement**: whether a brain-state architecture shared by conscious mammals is evidence of consciousness elsewhere. It is spreading to jumping spiders and, in preprint, butterflies, which makes it a weaker discriminator, not a stronger one.
- **Current state**: current frameworks do not use it. The Map should not either.

## Which Way It Cuts (graded) {#grade}

1. **The architecture finding** (two states, ultradian, raised arousal threshold, rapid reversibility, homeostatic rebound in *O. laqueus*, wake-like LFP): strongly supported as a behavioural and physiological result. Two independent labs and two species agree, cuttlefish data concur, and there is an unpublished field replication. The samples are small (4 animals; 3–6 animals per behavioural assay; nine head-fixed animals, one probe insertion each).
2. **Resemblance to vertebrate REM/NREM**: partial and stated by the authors with disanalogies (no brain-wide slow waves in quiet sleep; atonia not comparable). "Resembles", not "is".
3. **Convergent evolution**: "probably" (Pophale) and "strongly suggestive" (Medeiros), with Medeiros's mollusc condition attached.
4. **Function**: open. Motor refinement and replay are both hypotheses. Sensorimotor maintenance (Blumberg) needs no experience.
5. **Dreaming**: not claimed in either paper. Press speculation is hedged by the authors themselves. On the Map's scale it is no higher than a live hypothesis, and arguably speculative, because no method in use can test it.
6. **Consciousness marker**: no. Two of the three frameworks checked do not use sleep. The one that did (Cambridge 2012) used it as substrate evidence alongside much else, and the octopus sleep data postdate it. The finding raises nothing about octopus consciousness above where [animal-consciousness](/topics/animal-consciousness/) already grades it.

**Relation to the tenets.** Each tenet is **compatible, not supported**.
- **Dualism**: physicalist sleep science predicts and explains alternating brain states. The data are brain-state facts and do not discriminate.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction**, **Bidirectional Interaction**, **No Many Worlds**: no bearing.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits**: no bearing. The tempting chain from REM-like sleep to dreaming to consciousness is an argument from analogy. The Map should not run it in either direction.
- **The interface-modes reading** of sleep (`concepts/sleep-and-consciousness` L94: "The interface isn't binary—its components engage independently") could be extended to octopuses only as **speculative integration**. It would need a phenomenal premise the data cannot supply. Do not map quiet and active sleep onto `comparative-consciousness-and-interface-differences`'s coupling modes ("active selection", "minimal presence").
- **Do not conscript authors.** No source here argues for dualism, interface theory or octopus dreaming. van De Poll & van Swinderen is the only consciousness-facing source, and it is a predictive-processing proposal.
