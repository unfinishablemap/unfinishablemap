# Research: Epistemic Self-Trust as a Reply to Epistemic Circularity

## Executive Summary

Epistemic circularity is Alston's name for the fact that no basic source of belief (perception, memory, introspection, reasoning) can be shown reliable without premises drawn from that source. The standard reply is **self-trust**. Since no non-question-begging assurance of any faculty's reliability is available, trusting one's faculties is rational and unavoidable, and the trust is defeasible. The reply has a long history. Reid's seventh first principle (1785) is an early statement. Lehrer (1997) and Foley (2001) give the two main book-length epistemological accounts, Zagzebski (2012) extends it by consistency to trust in others, and Bergmann (2004, 2006, 2021) defends a Reidian commonsense version. Alston himself held that circularity does not block justification. His non-sceptical moral is already on the Map's pages.

The reply **helps** the Map: circularity alone makes no void, because it is universal and compatible with justification. It also licenses a default trust in introspective seemings. It **cuts against** the Map at the same point. Foley makes the trust defeasible by evidence that a faculty errs in a kind of situation (ch. 3). On Bergmann's account, once a doubt is live, reasoning that relies on the doubted source cannot remove it (2004, as reported by the IEP). Schwitzgebel (2008) supplies exactly such evidence for introspection of the general character of experience. He also argues that the two measures Foley uses to calibrate trust, confidence and its depth under reflection, are unreliable in that domain: error persists "even in favorable circumstances of extended reflection" (p. 259), and confidence is inflated by a "lack of corrective feedback" (p. 260). The reply therefore stops at **thin, canonical** reports (that a vivid pain is occurring), which is where Schwitzgebel himself concedes it is hard to go badly wrong. It gives no licence to **thick** reports about the nature of consciousness, its non-physicality or its causal power.

Self-trust is framework-neutral, and it licenses the physicalist's trust in theoretical reasoning as fully as the dualist's trust in introspection. ### Alston, "Epistemic Circularity" (1986)
- **Citation**: *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 47(1): 1–30, doi:10.2307/2107722 (Crossref). Reprinted in Alston, *Epistemic Justification* (Cornell, 1989), pp. 319–349 (per IEP).
- **Status**: **[carry-forward]**. The 2026-11-25 deep review of `voids/necessary-opacity` checked these against a JSTOR scan: "one may still justify, and be justified in, the reliability claim"; the result "puts no limits whatever on the beliefs that can be justified" and "lends no aid or comfort to any of the more familiar forms of skepticism"; "FRJ is a pipe dream" (FRJ = fully reflective justification); and "without a cosmic guarantee of success" (p. 30). Not re-read this run.
- **Key points**: Alston coined the term in this sense. The IEP says Alston was the one "who first used the term in this sense" **[raw, IEP]**. Circularity blocks a non-circular demonstration and fully reflective justification. It does not block justification or knowledge.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Alston is a reliabilist and non-sceptical.

### Alston, *The Reliability of Sense Perception* (Cornell University Press, 1993)
- **Status**: **[via IEP]**. Text not reached; the archive.org copy is access-restricted.
- **Key points (as quoted by the IEP)**: a circular track-record argument "will not do its job unless we are justified in accepting its premises; and that is the case only if sense perception is in fact reliable. This is to offer a stone instead of bread" (1993, 17). The same can be said of "crystal ball gazing", so the argument "does nothing to indicate that the source belongs to the sheep rather that with the goats" (1993, 17; "rather that" is the IEP's text). Alston then defends the *practical* rationality of engaging in firmly established doxastic practices: "there is no appeal beyond the practices we find ourselves firmly committed to, psychologically and socially" (1993, 125).
- **Later change**: per the IEP, Alston "has since rejected this practical validation argument" (*Beyond "Justification"*, Cornell 2005, 240–242) and "settled for a simpler form of Wittgensteinian contextualism". **Do not present practical rationality as Alston's final view.**
- **Why it matters here**: the "stone instead of bread" passage marks the first place the self-trust reply stops. A circular vindication cannot *discriminate* a reliable source from an unreliable one.

### Reid, *Essays on the Intellectual Powers of Man* (Edinburgh: Bell; London: Robinson, 1785)
- **Status**: **[raw]**, archive.org `essaysonintellec00reiduoft`, 1785 first edition. The OCR uses the long s; quotations below are modernised, and the OCR strings were grepped.
- **Key points**: Essay VI, ch. 5, seventh first principle of contingent truths (pp. 591–592): "That the natural faculties, by which we distinguish truth from error, are not fallacious." A proof is impossible, "because, to judge of a demonstration, a man must trust his faculties, and take for granted the very thing in question." A sceptic who withholds assent until the faculties are proved reliable cannot be argued out of this "strong hold": "he must even be left to enjoy his scepticism." The first such principle is "the existence of every thing of which I am conscious".
- **Why it matters**: this is the historical root of the self-trust and commonsense replies (Bergmann's is explicitly Reidian). Reid's consciousness principle concerns the *existence* of present mental operations, which is thin content. It says nothing about their metaphysical nature.

## Major Positions

### Default self-trust with local defeat (Foley; Zagzebski)
- **Core claim**: no faculty can be given a non-question-begging assurance of reliability, so trust is unavoidable and rational. On Foley's account it is proportioned to confidence and its depth under reflection, and it yields to evidence of unreliability in a kind of situation. Zagzebski grounds its rationality in conscientious self-reflection, and Baril reports her conclusion that we must trust our faculties "especially so when we are conscientious in our exercise of them" (48–49). Her account of how self-trust is defeated was not read.
- **Extension**: consistency carries self-trust over to trust in others (Foley ch. 4; Zagzebski ch. 3 on). Disagreement then becomes a conflict inside self-trust.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. It secures the right to start from one's faculties, introspection included, and gives no faculty special immunity.

### Commonsense particularism (Reid; Bergmann)
- **Core claim**: that our faculties are reliable is a first principle, and epistemically circular support for it is acceptable except in specified circumstances. In particular, it cannot defeat an existing doubt.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. Reid was himself a dualist, but the epistemology carries no metaphysics. Schwitzgebel's manuscript argues that Reid's dualism strains his own commitment to common sense.

### Reliabilist acceptance of circularity (Alston 1986; 1993)
- **Core claim**: if a source is in fact reliable, beliefs from it are justified, and a circular track-record argument can transmit justification. What fails is discrimination: the argument works equally for "crystal ball gazing".
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. It leaves open whether introspection is reliable, which the Map's `observation-and-measurement-void` already says (L98).

### Introspective pessimism (Schwitzgebel)
- **Core claim**: introspection of the general character of current experience is "faulty, untrustworthy, and misleading". Confidence is inflated by missing feedback, and errors survive extended reflection. Canonical pains and foveal colours are the exception, and using them "only as one's inference base rigs the game".
- **Relation to tenets**: cuts against thick introspective premises for Dualism and Bidirectional Interaction. It is equally hard on illusionists' and physicalists' introspective claims, since it bears on all metaphysical uses of introspection (crazyism).

## Key Debates

### Does circularity defeat justification? {#does-circularity-defeat}
- **Sides**: Alston, Bergmann and the reliabilists say no. Internalists demanding metajustification say yes (BonJour, cited on `concepts/phenomenal-conservatism`; Fumerton, *Metaepistemology and Skepticism*, 1995, cited by Foley p. 5 n. 2 [meta]). Vogel (2000) and Cohen (2002) argue that externalism makes reliability knowledge too easy to get (bootstrapping).
- **Current state**: ongoing. Even its defenders accept that circular support is dialectically idle against a doubter (IEP; Bergmann 2004 via IEP). The Map can rely on that agreed core without taking a side.

### Can doubting oneself be coherent? {#self-doubt}
- **Sides**: Roush's SEP entry frames the threat of incoherence. Christensen's higher-order-evidence view says a measured downgrade is rational. Foley's p. 7 point is that a sceptic's argument can be a reductio of the faculty's reliability and need not presuppose it.
- **Current state**: self-doubt is widely held to be coherent when it is local and evidence-driven. That is the form the Map's voids should take.

### Does self-trust radiate outward? {#radiation}
- **Sides**: Foley (consistency; "radiates outwards") and Zagzebski (consistency commits to trust in others). Rysiew argues instead for parity, with no priority of self-trust over trust in others. A 2014 paper by Elizabeth Fricker reportedly questions the derivation (Oxford repository; known from a search summary only, not read).
- **Bearing on the Map**: on either reading, other conscientious introspectors' reports carry weight. Persistent disagreement about the nature of experience (SEP §4.2.3; crazyism) is a defeater the Map must weigh as well as report.

## The Key Question: Does Self-Trust License Introspective Reports About One's Own Conscious Nature? {#key-question}

**Short answer.** It licenses a defeasible default for thin reports, which is the evidential base `concepts/phenomenal-conservatism` already secures. It gives no licence to thick reports about the nature of consciousness. The reply stops at five points.

1. **Discrimination.** Self-trust and circular vindication work equally for any source ("crystal ball gazing", Alston 1993 via IEP). They cannot settle a dispute between two parties who each trust their own faculties. An illusionist trusts theoretical reasoning that says introspection misrepresents. A dualist trusts introspection that seems to present something non-physical. Self-trust backs both starting points equally.
2. **Live doubt.** Once there is a reason to doubt a source, circular support cannot remove it (Bergmann 2004 via IEP; IEP's own diagnosis). Introspective re-checking of a doubted introspective claim is that kind of circular support. Non-circular checks (third-person data, cross-observer and cross-tradition convergence) are the only remedy. The Map's introspection-architecture work already aims at such checks.
3. **Calibration.** Foley proportions trust to "confidence" and its "depth" under reflection. Schwitzgebel's evidence attacks both measures for introspection of general phenomenal character. Error persists "even in favorable circumstances of extended reflection" (p. 259), and confidence is inflated by "lack of corrective feedback" (p. 260). Foley's own ch. 3 principle then applies: data can show "we are less than ideally reliable in certain kinds of situations".
4. **Consistency.** Zagzebski's and Foley's extension to others makes other conscientious introspectors' reports count. The documented "intractable disagreement" about imageless thought, emotion and attention (SEP §4.2.3), and the "peer disagreement" ground of crazyism, are then "a conflict within self-trust".
5. **Bias evidence (the interested-party case).** On Foley's model, what defeats is evidence of *unreliability* in a kind of situation. Evidence that stakes tilt self-relevant judgement (`voids/interested-party-void`) is a local defeater where it shows a tilt on that question type. On this model a motive alone is not a defeater. That last step is an inference from Foley's defeat condition, not his words, and it matches the debunking discipline the outer review cites (Street, Vavova; not researched here).

**What survives.** Thin, canonical reports: that a vivid pain or a foveal colour is occurring now. Schwitzgebel grants that it is "hard, seemingly, to go too badly wrong" there, and Reid's consciousness principle concerns only "the existence of every thing of which I am conscious". These are the reports `concepts/phenomenal-conservatism` (L100) calls "exceptionally secure" on the thin reading, where it adds that the seeming "on its own it contributes little toward dualism". The thick content the metaphysical case needs gets no additional support from self-trust. Neither do non-physicality and causal power. The tenet page already says the latter is not introspectible (cited at PC L98).

**What the reply does for the voids.** Circularity alone makes no void. It is universal (Foley: "no non–question-begging assurances" for any inquiry), and it is compatible with justification (Alston). The outer review's "proves too much" objection lands on any void that rests on circularity alone. A consciousness-specific void needs more than the circle. It needs either the absence of a non-circular check (the private object, no cross-observer triangulation) or specific evidence of unreliability (Schwitzgebel; bias evidence). That is the structure `voids/observation-and-measurement-void` L98 and `voids/necessary-opacity` L64 already approximate. `concepts/self-reference-paradox` L78 says it in non-Bergmann vocabulary (fold F2).

**Foley's reductio point and the Map's self-stultification move.** Foley p. 7 rejects arguments that scepticism is "self-referentially incoherent", because the sceptic can run a reductio. The same reply is open to an illusionist facing the Map's [self-stultification](/concepts/self-stultification/) argument. The illusionist can conditionally grant introspection's reliability and argue that it yields evidence of its own unreliability. The self-stultification pages should not present the argument as a refutation that self-trust secures. `concepts/self-stultification` has 16 words of headroom, so this is a **guard for a future deep review**, not a fold.
