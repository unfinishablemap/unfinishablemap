## Executive Summary

**Verdict: worth writing, as a `topics/` article, with the brief in the boundary section below.** Whole-word "neglect" appears in 20 live body files (the source review's "35" is the substring count, which sweeps in "neglected", "immune neglect" and "duration neglect"; the substring count today is 37). Every substantive locus treats neglect as a *fixed* erasure: "Erasure Without Remainder" (`attention-and-the-consciousness-interface` L112), "when the gate jams shut for half of space, that half effectively disappears" (`neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture` L125), "an entire dimension of experience vanishes without leaving a trace" (`voids/attention-and-consciousness` L101). The forty-year literature showing that neglect *fluctuates and remits with the lesion unchanged* has no footprint at all: zero corpus hits for caloric-vestibular remission, prism adaptation, neck vibration, optokinetic stimulation, phasic alerting or cueing; zero for Rossetti, Rubens, Corbetta, Karnath, Mesulam, Bartolomeo-on-neglect; the 47 "Posner" hits are all Posner molecules or Plum and Posner. The network account of neglect that the whole modern field runs on (Mesulam 1981; Posner et al. 1984; Corbetta and Shulman 2002, 2011; Bartolomeo et al. 2007) is likewise absent.

Three findings shape the article. First, neglect is the **largest and best-controlled stable-lesion instance of intact-substrate reversibility** in the clinical wing's catalogue: Rubens (1985) 17 of 18 patients improving under caloric stimulation; Vallar et al. (1993) 20 right-hemisphere patients; Wilkinson et al. (2014) a 52-patient double-blind galvanic-vestibular trial; 65 RCTs in the 2021 Cochrane review. It answers, in numbers the anosognosia case cannot supply, the complaint at `clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence` L141 that the catalogue lacks "a targeted, reversible perturbation" of a channel with experimental control. Second, it is also the instance where a physicalist account **predicts the reversal outright and names the mechanism**: Corbetta et al. (2005) found that recovery "correlates with the restoration and rebalancing of activity" in "structurally intact dorsal and ventral parietal regions", and Bottini et al. (2001) showed with PET that caloric stimulation and neck vibration drive the very perisylvian and temporoparietal areas the lesions spare. At the case level the interface reading adds nothing, and the article must say so in its first paragraph. Third, the reversal literature *splits* in a way the corpus's "attentional access" framing has never registered: most manipulations demonstrably reverse the exploratory and motor bias, and Striemer and Danckert (2010) argue that prisms "have little influence on the pervasive perceptual biases that characterize neglect"; only Robertson et al. (1998) measured awareness itself (a temporal-order judgement) and reversed it. What the interface reading can honestly claim is a *discipline* rather than a datum: the article should sort every reversal by whether it restores behaviour or awareness, and should correct the corpus's "erasure" language, since a syndrome that can be shifted by a warning tone on the *ipsilesional* side is a bias in a competition, not a deletion.

## Key Sources

Grouped by evidence class. Each entry gives the verified metadata (Crossref unless marked), the PMID (Europe PMC), and the raw-abstract content that bears on the article.

### A. Vestibular stimulation (the class Cappa 1987 belongs to)

**Rubens (1985), "Caloric stimulation and unilateral visual neglect"** — *Neurology* 35(7), 1019-1024; https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.35.7.1019; PMID 4010940. Raw abstract: "18 patients with left-sided visual neglect after strokes. Except for one patient with absent vestibulo-ocular responses, all improved during caloric stimulation on the left by cold (LC) or on the right by warm water (RW). Improvement seemed to depend on the facilitation of left lateral gaze and on past-pointing to the left. During LC and RW caloric stimulation, patients worked from left to right instead of their usual right to left." The first series-scale demonstration, and its own mechanism sentence is motor: gaze facilitation. Crossref lists the page as 1019 only; Europe PMC and the printed issue give 1019-1024.

**Cappa, Sterzi, Vallar and Bisiach (1987), "Remission of hemineglect and anosognosia during vestibular stimulation"** — *Neuropsychologia* 25(5), 775-782; https://doi.org/10.1016/0028-3932(87)90115-1; PMID 3501552. Raw abstract, complete: "The effects of vestibular stimulation on extrapersonal and personal neglect and on awareness of disease were investigated in four patients with severe neglect and anosognosia. Neglect phenomena improved in all patients, while an effect on anosognosia was found in two cases. These preliminary findings indicate a possible role of vestibular stimulation on hemispheric activation." **Split by outcome, as the concept note proposes**: the 4/4 neglect result and the authors' "hemispheric activation" mechanism belong here; the 2/4 anosognosia result belongs to the anosognosia article, which should cite this note's reading rather than re-state the abstract.

**Vallar, Bottini, Rusconi and Sterzi (1993), "Exploring somatosensory hemineglect by vestibular stimulation"** — *Brain* 116(1), 71-86; https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/116.1.71; PMID 8453466. Raw abstract: "20 right brain-damaged patients and 11 left brain-damaged patients. After stimulation, right brain-damaged patients showed a temporary partial recovery from left hemianaesthesia or extinction. Conversely, right somatosensory deficits associated with left brain damage were virtually unaffected ... Temporary recovery from somatosensory deficits was independent of the presence of visuo-spatial hemineglect." The hemispheric asymmetry (right-lesion deficits reverse, left-lesion deficits do not) is a constraint any reading must carry: the reversal is not a general "stimulation wakes the channel" effect.

**Bottini, Karnath, Vallar, Sterzi, Frith, Frackowiak and Paulesu (2001), "Cerebral representations for egocentric space: functional-anatomical evidence from caloric vestibular stimulation and neck vibration"** — *Brain* 124(6), 1182-1196; https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/124.6.1182; PMID 11353734 (author list from Europe PMC; Crossref lists Bottini alone). Raw abstract: CVS, neck vibration and optokinetic stimulation "can induce a distortion of egocentric coordinates ... Appropriate forms of this stimulation can also temporarily improve a variety of pathological manifestations of unilateral neglect"; PET in normals shows "somatosensory areas of the perisylvian cortex including the insula and retroinsular cortex, the temporoparietal junction and somatosensory area II receive signals from both sensory channels." This is the physical face of the reversal: the manipulations drive the spared perisylvian and TPJ tissue directly.

**Wilkinson, Zubko, Sakel, Coulton, Higgins and Pullicino (2014), "Galvanic vestibular stimulation in hemi-spatial neglect"** — *Frontiers in Integrative Neuroscience* 8, article 4; https://doi.org/10.3389/fnint.2014.00004; PMID 24523679. Raw abstract: "a double-blind, randomized, dose-response trial ... (n = 52) ... all three treatment arms showed a statistically significant improvement ... on the conventional tests of the Behavioral Inattention Test. More remarkably, this change (mean change = 28%, SD = 18) was still evident 1 month later." The one controlled vestibular trial at series scale; it makes the vestibular class durable rather than transient, at least for the galvanic variant.

**Neighbours for the anosognosia and body-ownership articles, not this one**: Bisiach, Rusconi and Vallar (1991), "Remission of somatoparaphrenic delusion through vestibular stimulation", *Neuropsychologia* 29(10), 1029-1031, https://doi.org/10.1016/0028-3932(91)90066-H, PMID 1762671 ("Transitory remission of the patient's delusional belief was consistently observed during unilateral vestibular activation"); Rode et al. (1992), "Partial remission of hemiplegia and somatoparaphrenia through vestibular stimulation in a case of unilateral neglect", *Cortex* 28(2), 203-208, https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-9452(13)80048-2, PMID 1499306 ("a temporary reduction of the motor deficit and disappearance of the somatoparaphrenic delusion, in addition to the already reported improvement of extrapersonal and personal neglect and anosognosia"). The neglect article should cite these in one sentence as the same manipulation reaching ownership and motor deficits, and hand the analysis over.

### B. Proprioceptive and optokinetic manipulations

**Karnath, Christ and Hartje (1993), "Decrease of contralateral neglect by neck muscle vibration and spatial orientation of trunk midline"** — *Brain* 116(2), 383-396; https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/116.2.383; PMID 8461972. Raw abstract: three patients; neglect "could be reduced by vibrating the left posterior neck muscles as well as by turning the trunk 15 degrees to the left. In contrast, unspecific stimulation on the left side of the patient's body, produced by vibrating the left hand muscles or the proprioceptive signal induced by turning the head 15 degrees to the left, had no compensatory effects." The built-in active control (hand vibration does nothing) makes this the cleanest single-manipulation study in the class: the effect is specific to the head-on-trunk signal that sets the egocentric frame.

**Schindler, Kerkhoff, Karnath, Keller and Goldenberg (2002), "Neck muscle vibration induces lasting recovery in spatial neglect"** — *JNNP* 73(4), 412-419; https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp.73.4.412; PMID 12235310 (authors from Europe PMC). Crossover, two groups of 10; combined vibration and exploration training produced "a specific and lasting reduction in the symptoms of neglect ... evident two months after the completion of treatment", against "only minor therapeutic benefits" from exploration training alone.

**Pizzamiglio, Frasca, Guariglia, Incoccia and Antonucci (1990), "Effect of optokinetic stimulation in patients with visual neglect"** — *Cortex* 26(4), 535-541; https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-9452(13)80303-6; PMID 2081391. Line bisection against a moving background shifts the subjective midpoint in all groups; in neglect patients "a significant reduction of the left bias was present" for leftward motion, "relatively stable in a test-retest presentation".

**Kerkhoff, Reinhart, Ziegler, Artinger, Marquardt and Keller (2013), "Smooth pursuit eye movement training promotes recovery from auditory and visual neglect: a randomized controlled study"** — *Neurorehabilitation and Neural Repair* 27(9), 789-798; https://doi.org/10.1177/1545968313491012; PMID 23797459 (online June 2013, print November 2013). Fifty patients randomised; the pursuit group "showed significant and lasting improvements in all visual measures and normal performance in the auditory midline. Neither visual nor auditory neglect impairments changed significantly after" visual scanning therapy. The optokinetic class's controlled trial.

### C. Prism adaptation (the class with the most trials and the most disputed mechanism)

**Rossetti, Rode, Pisella, Farné, Li, Boisson and Perenin (1998), "Prism adaptation to a rightward optical deviation rehabilitates left hemispatial neglect"** — *Nature* 395(6698), 166-169; https://doi.org/10.1038/25988; PMID 9744273. Raw abstract: "All patients exposed to the optical shift of the visual field to the right were improved on their manual body-midline demonstration and on classical neuropsychological tests. Unlike other physiological manipulations used to improve neglect, this improvement lasted for at least two hours after prism removal ... The positive effect found for both sensorimotor and more cognitive spatial functions suggests that they share or depend on a common level of space representation linked to multisensory integration." Note the authors' own framing: the lesion "reflects the impairment of coordinate transformation", and adaptation "stimulate[s] neural structures responsible for the transformation of sensorimotor coordinates". A sensorimotor mechanism from the start.

**Frassinetti, Angeli, Meneghello, Avanzi and Làdavas (2002), "Long-lasting amelioration of visuospatial neglect by prism adaptation"** — *Brain* 125(3), 608-623; https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awf056; PMID 11872617. Seven patients, twice-daily sessions for two weeks; the study that turned a two-hour effect into a treatment claim, and tested far, near and personal space separately.

**Striemer and Danckert (2010), "Through a prism darkly: re-evaluating prisms and neglect"** — *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 14(7), 308-316; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2010.04.001; PMID 20444640. Raw abstract: "studies that have directly examined the influence of prisms on perceptual biases inherent to neglect have revealed very few beneficial effects. We propose an alternative explanation whereby many of the beneficial effects of prisms arise via the influence of adaptation on circuits in the dorsal visual stream controlling attention and visuomotor behaviors. We further argue that prisms have little influence on the pervasive perceptual biases that characterize neglect." **The pivotal source for the article's behaviour-versus-awareness split.** If they are right, the best-known "reversal of neglect" reverses exploration and not awareness.

**Newport and Schenk (2012), "Prisms and neglect: what have we learned?"** — *Neuropsychologia* 50(6), 1080-1091; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2012.01.023; PMID 22306519. "More than 280 articles have been published in this area"; reviews therapeutic, subtyping and mechanism strands.

**Jacquin-Courtois, O'Shea, Luauté, Pisella, Revol, Mizuno, Rode and Rossetti (2013), "Rehabilitation of spatial neglect by prism adaptation: a peculiar expansion of sensorimotor after-effects to spatial cognition"** — *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 37(4), 594-609; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2013.02.007; PMID 23428624. The Lyon group's own mechanism statement: "prism adaptation expansion to spatial cognition involves a cerebello-cortical network". Read against Striemer and Danckert: the originating group claims expansion into cognition; the critics claim the expansion is mostly visuomotor.

**Pisella, Rode, Farnè, Tilikete and Rossetti (2006), "Prism adaptation in the rehabilitation of patients with visuo-spatial cognitive disorders"** — *Current Opinion in Neurology* 19(6), 534-542; https://doi.org/10.1097/WCO.0b013e328010924b; PMID 17102690. Frames prisms as an "ascending" (bottom-up) rehabilitation strategy against the "descending" approach in which patients "become aware of their problems and gain intentional control of compensatory strategies". Useful vocabulary for the article: the reversal that works best is the one that bypasses the patient's awareness.

**Ten Brink, Visser-Meily, Schut, Kouwenhoven, Eijsackers and Nijboer (2017), "Prism adaptation in rehabilitation? No additional effects of prism adaptation on neglect recovery in the subacute phase poststroke: a randomized controlled trial"** — *Neurorehabilitation and Neural Repair* 31(12), 1017-1028; https://doi.org/10.1177/1545968317744277; PMID 29192535. Seventy patients, prism versus sham adaptation for two weeks, seven time points: "No beneficial effects of PA over SA in the subacute phase poststroke were observed."

**Vilimovsky, Chen, Hoidekrova, Petioky and Harsa (2021), "Prism adaptation treatment to address spatial neglect in an intensive rehabilitation program: a randomized pilot and feasibility trial"** — *PLoS ONE* 16(1), e0245425; https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0245425; PMID 33481828 (Europe PMC). Thirty-four randomised, sham as active control: "we found no difference between the two groups in the degree of improvement".

**Zhu, Quesque, Nishida, Jacquin-Courtois, Luauté, Chabanat, Rode and Rossetti (2025), "Significant immediate and limited long-term benefit of prism adaptation on spatial neglect: a systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs"** — *Annals of Physical and Rehabilitation Medicine* 68(7), 102022; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rehab.2025.102022; PMID 40972271; first published 18 September 2025 (Europe PMC). Raw abstract: "Only 10 RCTs (356 participants) could be included ... Immediate PA effects on SN showed a significant improvement for CBS ... (WMD = -2.13, 95% CI -3.93 to 0.33; P = 0.020), but non-significance for conventional BIT ... Long-term benefit was not significant for CBS". The originating group's own 2025 verdict, and the title says it: immediate benefit, limited long-term benefit. (The reported CI for the CBS effect spans zero as printed in the abstract; the article should quote the figure as printed and not repair it.)

**Feng, Fong, Zhang, Chen, Chen and Luo (2026), "Effects of prism adaptation on left and right spatial neglect in patients with subacute stroke: a multi-centred randomized controlled trial"** — *Neurorehabilitation and Neural Repair*, online 25 August 2026, article 15459683261477085; https://doi.org/10.1177/15459683261477085; PMID 42638520 (Europe PMC; no volume or pages yet). Forty-eight completers across three sites, sham glasses as control: "The gain scores for all outcomes in the PA group were significantly higher than that in the control group ... more benefits can be seen for LSN." A positive sham-controlled trial, small, published after the 2025 meta-analysis closed; cite as the most recent datum and grade it as one trial.

**Bowen, Hazelton, Pollock and Lincoln (2013), "Cognitive rehabilitation for spatial neglect following stroke"** — *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews*, CD003586.pub3; https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003586.pub3; PMID 23813503. Twenty-three RCTs, 628 participants: "a statistically significant effect in favour of cognitive rehabilitation compared with control, for immediate effects on standardised neglect assessments ... However, sensitivity analyses including only studies of high methodological quality removed evidence of a significant effect."

**Longley, Hazelton, Heal, Pollock, Woodward-Nutt, Mitchell, Pobric, Vail and Bowen (2021), "Non-pharmacological interventions for spatial neglect or inattention following stroke and other non-progressive brain injury"** — *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews* 2021(7), CD003586.pub4; https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003586.pub4; PMID 34196963. Sixty-five RCTs, 1,951 participants: "Only two studies were judged to be entirely at low risk of bias, and all were small ... We found no definitive (phase 3) clinical trials"; for prism adaptation, "evidence is very uncertain about the effects ... based on measures of persisting functional ability in ADL (2 studies, 39 participants)". The grading anchor for the whole treatment literature.

### D. Alerting, cueing and pharmacology (the manipulations that act on arousal or voluntary orienting rather than on the spatial frame)

**Riddoch and Humphreys (1983), "The effect of cueing on unilateral neglect"** — *Neuropsychologia* 21(6), 589-599; https://doi.org/10.1016/0028-3932(83)90056-8; PMID 6664478. Raw abstract: five patients; "a marked decrease in neglect when subjects were cued and forced to report stimuli in their neglected field. This occurred even when there was a competing stimulus in the non-neglected field. However, in the absence of forced report requirements, patients oriented to stimuli in the non-neglected field. The results are interpreted as a failure of patients with unilateral neglect to orient automatically to the side of space contralateral to the lesion, though processes governing the conscious orienting of attention are intact." **The earliest statement of the exogenous-impaired, endogenous-intact asymmetry**, and the transient remission "with attentional cueing" the driver asked about. Note it reverses under *instruction*, which is the middle row of the Map's willed-attention table (`attention-as-interface` L137-143), not the willed row.

**Posner, Walker, Friedrich and Rafal (1984), "Effects of parietal injury on covert orienting of attention"** — *Journal of Neuroscience* 4(7), 1863-1874; https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.04-07-01863.1984; PMID 6737043. "Damage to the parietal lobe produces a deficit in the disengage operation when the target is contralateral to the lesion ... The effects of brain injury on disengagement of attention seem to be unique to the parietal lobe." The disengage-deficit account: the patient cannot let go of the right, not cannot see the left.

**Robertson, Tegnér, Tham, Lo and Nimmo-Smith (1995), "Sustained attention training for unilateral neglect: theoretical and rehabilitation implications"** — *Journal of Clinical and Experimental Neuropsychology* 17(3), 416-430; https://doi.org/10.1080/01688639508405133; PMID 7650103. Eight chronic patients trained to self-alert; "statistically significant improvements in unilateral neglect as well in sustained attention were found following onset of sustained attention training, without corresponding improvements in control measures." A *self-administered* alerting reversal: the patient's own instruction to themself lifts the spatial deficit.

**Robertson, Mattingley, Rorden and Driver (1998), "Phasic alerting of neglect patients overcomes their spatial deficit in visual awareness"** — *Nature* 395(6698), 169-172; https://doi.org/10.1038/25993; PMID 9744274 (the paper immediately following Rossetti et al. in the same issue). Raw abstract: patients "became aware of left events half a second later than right events on average. This spatial imbalance in the time course of visual awareness was corrected when a warning sound alerted the patients phasically. Even a warning sound on the right accelerated the perception of left visual events in this way. Nonspatial phasic alerting can thus overcome disabling spatial biases in perceptual awareness after brain injury." **The only study in this note whose dependent measure is awareness itself** (a temporal-order judgement) rather than a cancellation, bisection or pointing score. It is also the study that most directly refutes "erasure": the left is not absent, it is late.

**Malhotra, Parton, Greenwood and Husain (2006), "Noradrenergic modulation of space exploration in visual neglect"** — *Annals of Neurology* 59(1), 186-190; https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.20701; PMID 16261567 (online December 2005). Guanfacine "improves leftward space exploration in selected right-hemisphere patients with neglect. The positive effects of guanfacine were associated with extended ability to maintain attention on task." The pharmacological class, again acting on sustained attention.

**Husain and Rorden (2003), "Non-spatially lateralized mechanisms in hemispatial neglect"** — *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 4(1), 26-36; https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn1005; PMID 12511859 (no abstract on Europe PMC). Already cited in `voids/attention-and-consciousness`; the review that consolidates the alerting and sustained-attention findings into a two-component (spatial plus non-spatial) account.

### E. Residual processing of the neglected side (what the corpus already knows, with its primary sources restored)

**Volpe, LeDoux and Gazzaniga (1979), "Information processing of visual stimuli in an 'extinguished' field"** — *Nature* 282(5740), 722-724; https://doi.org/10.1038/282722a0; PMID 514351 (no abstract on Europe PMC; title-level verification only). The first implicit-processing report in extinction, a decade before Marshall and Halligan.

**Marshall and Halligan (1988), "Blindsight and insight in visuo-spatial neglect"** — *Nature* 336(6201), 766-767; https://doi.org/10.1038/336766a0; PMID 3205302. Raw abstract confirms the corpus's use: P.S. "judged that the drawings were identical; yet when asked to select which house she would prefer to live in, she reliably chose the house that was not burning." Also confirms "P.S. has a left homonymous hemianopia. Nonetheless, her neglect persists despite free movement of the head and eyes and is thus not a direct consequence of sensory loss" (a detail the corpus omits and which matters, since the corpus contrasts neglect with blindsight as "intact visual systems").

**Berti and Rizzolatti (1992), "Visual processing without awareness: evidence from unilateral neglect"** — *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience* 4(4), 345-351; https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.1992.4.4.345; PMID 23968128. Category-level priming from the neglected field "even when they deny the stimulus presence in the affected field".

**Làdavas, Paladini and Cubelli (1993), "Implicit associative priming in a patient with left visual neglect"** — *Neuropsychologia* 31(12), 1307-1320; https://doi.org/10.1016/0028-3932(93)90100-E; PMID 8127429. Associative word priming from the neglected field; "When obliged to orient attention to the left side of a computer screen, he was not able to detect the presence of, to read aloud, or to judge the lexical status and semantic content of left-sided stimuli." Notable because *forced orienting did not help this patient*, against Riddoch and Humphreys; the article should not generalise the cueing reversal to every patient.

**Mattingley, Davis and Driver (1997), "Preattentive filling-in of visual surfaces in parietal extinction"** — *Science* 275(5300), 671-674; https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5300.671; PMID 9005854. Extinction "was less severe when bilateral stimuli formed a common surface, even if this required visual filling-in to yield illusory Kanizsa figures".

**Driver and Mattingley (1998), "Parietal neglect and visual awareness"** — *Nature Neuroscience* 1(1), 17-22; https://doi.org/10.1038/217; PMID 10195103. The four-point summary: loss of awareness "is characteristically not strictly retinotopic"; segmentation preserved; "extensive unconscious processing takes place"; "parietal damage affects initial stages of motor planning as well as perception".

**Rees, Wojciulik, Clarke, Husain, Frith and Driver (2000), "Unconscious activation of visual cortex in the damaged right hemisphere of a parietal patient with extinction"** — *Brain* 123(8), 1624-1633; https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/123.8.1624; PMID 10908192 (authors from Europe PMC; Crossref lists Rees alone). Event-related fMRI in one patient: "activation of visual cortex contralateral to the extinguished left stimulus on these trials ... in both striate and early extrastriate" areas.

**Vuilleumier, Sagiv, Hazeltine, Poldrack, Swick, Rafal and Gabrieli (2001), "Neural fate of seen and unseen faces in visuospatial neglect: a combined event-related functional MRI and event-related potential study"** — *PNAS* 98(6), 3495-3500; https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.051436898; PMID 11248106. "left faces that were not perceived still activated right V1 and inferior temporal cortex ... with preserved face-specific negative potentials at 170 ms. When left faces were perceived, the same stimuli produced greater activity in a distributed network ... effective connectivity between visual, parietal, and frontal areas increased during perception." Within one patient, the seen/unseen contrast is a fronto-parietal coupling contrast; this is the neglect analogue of the workspace ignition finding and the article should say so.

**Driver and Vuilleumier (2001), "Perceptual awareness and its loss in unilateral neglect and extinction"** — *Cognition* 79(1-2), 39-88; https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0277(00)00124-4; PMID 11164023 (authors from Europe PMC; Crossref lists Driver alone). The review the corpus cites as if it were the primary imaging study. Confirms "such areas can remain structurally intact in neglect, with lesions typically centred on the right inferior parietal lobe."

### F. Extinction versus neglect

**Vossel, Eschenbeck, Weiss, Weidner, Saliger, Karbe and Fink (2011), "Visual extinction in relation to visuospatial neglect after right-hemispheric stroke: quantitative assessment and statistical lesion-symptom mapping"** — *JNNP* 82(8), 862-868; https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp.2010.224261; PMID 21242287. Fifty-six patients: "Extinction and neglect co-occurred in a subset of patients but were also observed independently from each other, thereby constituting a double dissociation. Lesions within the right inferior parietal cortex were significantly associated with the severity of visual extinction. Visuospatial neglect was related to damage of fronto-parietal brain regions."

**de Haan, Karnath and Driver (2012), "Mechanisms and anatomy of unilateral extinction after brain injury"** — *Neuropsychologia* 50(6), 1045-1053; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2012.02.015; PMID 22608081. "extinction, which reflects a specific deficit in the simultaneous processing of multiple briefly presented targets, should be distinguished from the failure to actively explore and serially detect targets amongst distractors in contralesional space commonly known as spatial neglect ... Prototypical extinction is instead best seen as the result of a pathologically biased competition between multiple target representations for pathologically limited attentional resources." The corpus's kaleidoscope table already separates them; this is the citation it lacks, and it supplies the "biased competition" vocabulary the article should adopt in place of "erasure".

**Karnath and Rorden (2012), "The anatomy of spatial neglect"** — *Neuropsychologia* 50(6), 1010-1017; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.06.027; PMID 21756924. Core syndrome ("biased gaze direction and search - with no awareness of these symptoms") after perisylvian damage; bisection bias more posterior; "extinction is associated with damage to the temporo-parietal junction."

### G. The attentional-network accounts (the rivals that predict the reversals)

**Heilman and Van Den Abell (1980), "Right hemisphere dominance for attention: the mechanism underlying hemispheric asymmetries of inattention (neglect)"** — *Neurology* 30(3), 327-330; https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.30.3.327; PMID 7189037. EEG desynchronisation in 12 normals: "the right parietal lobe desynchronized equally after right or left stimuli. These findings support the hypothesis that the right hemisphere is dominant for attention." The hemispheric-asymmetry premise behind the left-neglect preponderance. Crossref gives page 327 only; the article runs 327-330.

**Mesulam (1981), "A cortical network for directed attention and unilateral neglect"** — *Annals of Neurology* 10(4), 309-325; https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.410100402; PMID 7032417. "Unilateral neglect reflects a disturbance in the spatial distribution of directed attention ... four cerebral regions provide an integrated network ... a posterior parietal component provides an internal sensory map ... a limbic component in the cingulate gyrus regulates the spatial distribution of motivational valence; a frontal component coordinates the motor programs for exploration ... and a reticular component provides the underlying level of arousal and vigilance." The founding network statement; the reticular component is where the alerting reversals live.

**Bisiach and Luzzatti (1978), "Unilateral neglect of representational space"** — *Cortex* 14(1), 129-133; https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-9452(78)80016-1; PMID 16295118. Two patients describing the Piazza del Duomo from memory: "Left-sided details were largely omitted in the descriptions." Neglect of *imagined* space; the deficit is in the representation or its exploration, not in input. A datum against any reading on which neglect is a failure to *receive* the left.

**Vallar and Perani (1986), "The anatomy of unilateral neglect after right-hemisphere stroke lesions: a clinical/CT-scan correlation study in man"** — *Neuropsychologia* 24(5), 609-622; https://doi.org/10.1016/0028-3932(86)90001-1; PMID 3785649. 110 patients: "The inferior parietal lobule appears to be the area most frequently involved"; the source of the corpus's "typically parietal".

**Karnath, Ferber and Himmelbach (2001), "Spatial awareness is a function of the temporal not the posterior parietal lobe"** — *Nature* 411(6840), 950-953; https://doi.org/10.1038/35082075; PMID 11418859. "the superior temporal cortex is the neural substrate of spatial neglect in humans, as it is in monkeys."

**Mort, Malhotra, Mannan, Rorden, Pambakian, Kennard and Husain (2003), "The anatomy of visual neglect"** — *Brain* 126(9), 1986-1997; https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awg200; PMID 12821519 (authors from Europe PMC; Crossref lists Mort alone). Thirty-five patients: "the critical area involved in all neglect patients was the angular gyrus of the inferior parietal lobe ... our findings challenge the recent influential proposal that lesions of this area [STG] are critically associated with neglect."

**Bartolomeo and Chokron (2002), "Orienting of attention in left unilateral neglect"** — *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 26(2), 217-234; https://doi.org/10.1016/S0149-7634(01)00065-3; PMID 11856560. "a basic mechanism leading to left neglect behavior is an impaired exogenous orienting toward left-sided targets. In contrast, endogenous processes seem to be relatively preserved, if slowed"; implications "for the mechanisms of visual consciousness are explored." The review-level statement of the asymmetry Riddoch and Humphreys found.

**Corbetta and Shulman (2002), "Control of goal-directed and stimulus-driven attention in the brain"** — *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 3(3), 201-215; https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn755; PMID 11994752. The dorsal/ventral network model: the ventral system "is largely lateralized to the right hemisphere ... works as a 'circuit breaker' for the dorsal system ... both are disrupted in unilateral spatial neglect."

**Corbetta, Kincade, Lewis, Snyder and Sapir (2005), "Neural basis and recovery of spatial attention deficits in spatial neglect"** — *Nature Neuroscience* 8(11), 1603-1610; https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1574; PMID 16234807. Raw abstract: "spatial attention deficits in neglect (rightward bias and reorienting) after right frontal damage correlate with abnormal activation of structurally intact dorsal and ventral parietal regions ... recovery of these attention deficits correlates with the restoration and rebalancing of activity within these regions. These results support a model of recovery based on the re-weighting of activity within a distributed neuronal architecture, and they show that behavioral deficits depend not only on structural changes at the locus of injury, but also on physiological changes in distant but functionally related brain areas." **The physicalist prediction of intact-substrate reversibility, stated in the physicalist's own words, with a functional marker.**

**He, Snyder, Vincent, Epstein, Shulman and Corbetta (2007), "Breakdown of functional connectivity in frontoparietal networks underlies behavioral deficits in spatial neglect"** — *Neuron* 53(6), 905-918; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2007.02.013; PMID 17359924. "In the structurally intact dorsal network, interhemispheric connectivity in posterior parietal cortex was acutely disrupted but fully recovered. This acute disruption ... strongly correlated with impaired attentional processing across subjects." The neglect analogue of Kikuchi/Marsh in the amnesia case: the deficit and its recovery track a *physiological* state of spared tissue.

**Corbetta, Patel and Shulman (2008), "The reorienting system of the human brain: from environment to theory of mind"** — *Neuron* 58(3), 306-324; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2008.04.017; PMID 18466742. Ties the ventral network to "inputs ... from the locus coeruleus/norepinephrine system", which is the bridge from the network account to the alerting and guanfacine reversals.

**Corbetta and Shulman (2011), "Spatial neglect and attention networks"** — *Annual Review of Neuroscience* 34, 569-599; https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-neuro-061010-113731; PMID 21692662. "We argue that neglect is better explained by the dysfunction of distributed cortical networks for the control of attention than by structural damage of specific brain regions ... Structural damage in ventral cortex also induces physiological abnormalities of task-evoked activity and functional connectivity in a dorsal frontoparietal network that controls spatial attention." The consolidated account the article must engage as its named rival.

**Thiebaut de Schotten, Urbanski, Duffau, Volle, Lévy, Dubois and Bartolomeo (2005), "Direct evidence for a parietal-frontal pathway subserving spatial awareness in humans"** — *Science* 309(5744), 2226-2228; https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1116251; PMID 16195465. Intraoperative stimulation: "the strongest shifts occurred with subcortical stimulation. Fiber tracking identified the stimulated site as a section of the superior occipitofrontal fasciculus." A *reversible* induction of neglect-like bias in awake patients, the mirror image of the remission studies; the article can use it to show the sign of the perturbation does not matter to the network account.

**Bartolomeo, Thiebaut de Schotten and Doricchi (2007), "Left unilateral neglect as a disconnection syndrome"** — *Cerebral Cortex* 17(11), 2479-2490; https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhl181; PMID 17272263. "neglect would not result from the dysfunction of a single cortical region but from the disruption of large networks made up of distant cortical regions." This sits inside the Map's own `disconnection-neuroscience` vocabulary and should be linked there.

**Bartolomeo (2007), "Visual neglect"** — *Current Opinion in Neurology* 20(4), 381-386; https://doi.org/10.1097/WCO.0b013e32816aa3a3; PMID 17620870. "neglect raises important issues concerning the brain mechanisms of consciousness, perception and attention."

**Bartolomeo (2014), *Attention Disorders After Right Brain Damage: Living in Halved Worlds*** — Springer; https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4471-5649-9 (Crossref: book record, 2014; no Europe PMC entry). Title-level verification only; the monograph-length treatment if the article's author needs one. A second edition exists but was not verified here.

**Halligan, Fink, Marshall and Vallar (2003), "Spatial cognition: evidence from visual neglect"** — *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 7(3), 125-133; https://doi.org/10.1016/S1364-6613(03)00032-9; PMID 12639694. "neglect is a protean disorder whose symptoms can selectively affect different sensory modalities, cognitive processes, spatial domains and coordinate systems." The heterogeneity warning the article's calibration section needs.

**Liu, Bayle, Spagna, Sitt, Bourgeois, Lehongre, Fernandez-Vidal, Adam, Lambrecq, Navarro, Seidel Malkinson and Bartolomeo (2023), "Fronto-parietal networks shape human conscious report through attention gain and reorienting"** — *Communications Biology* 6, article 730; https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-023-05108-2; PMID 37454150. Intracerebral recordings, 727 contacts in 13 epilepsy patients, near-threshold detection with attentional cues: "attention-enhanced conscious report accompanied sustained right-hemisphere fronto-temporal activity in networks connected by the superior longitudinal fasciculus (SLF) II-III ... attentional reorienting affected conscious report through early, sustained activity in a right-hemisphere network (SLF III) ... distinct, hemisphere-asymmetric fronto-parietal networks support attentional gain and reorienting in shaping human conscious experience." The currency anchor: the Bartolomeo group now maps the same right-lateralised SLF networks that neglect lesions disconnect onto conscious report in intact brains.

## Major Positions

### The interface reading (the Map's)
- **Core claim** (as the corpus states it): attention is the gate through which consciousness selects among processed content; neglect breaks the gate for half of space.
- **What the reversal data do to it**: they do not falsify it and do not support it. A transient remission with the lesion unchanged is what the reading expects of an intact-substrate case, but it is also what every rival expects. The reading gains only if it says something the rivals do not, and the one place it can is the *awareness/behaviour* split: if consciousness selects through willed attention (`attention-as-interface` L137-143), then manipulations acting on the exogenous frame (prisms, vestibular, neck vibration) should restore *exploration* more reliably than *awareness*, while manipulations acting on alertness or endogenous orienting should touch awareness. Striemer and Danckert (2010) and Robertson et al. (1998) are consistent with that pattern. It is also exactly what the network account predicts (the dorsal system that carries endogenous orienting is structurally spared), so the pattern is a consistency point and not a discriminator. The article should present it as such.
- **Relation to tenets**: reads under Tenet 1 (attention is not consciousness; `attention-as-interface` §"Attention and Consciousness Are Dissociable") and Tenet 3 (the willed-orienting reversals in Riddoch and Humphreys and Robertson et al. 1995 are the patient's own instruction restoring access without tissue change); Tenet 5 supplies the only argumentative work, that "erasure" was the parsimonious story and is false.

### The attentional-network account (Mesulam 1981; Posner et al. 1984; Corbetta and Shulman 2002, 2011; He et al. 2007)
- **Core claim**: neglect is a physiological imbalance across a distributed, right-dominant attention network, with structural damage in ventral regions inducing dysfunction in structurally intact dorsal regions.
- **Reversibility prediction**: total and mechanistic. Anything that re-weights activity across the spared network (vestibular and proprioceptive input to the TPJ and insula, Bottini 2001; alerting via locus coeruleus, Corbetta 2008; guanfacine on prefrontal cortex, Malhotra 2006; spontaneous recovery, Corbetta 2005) reverses neglect to the degree that it restores balance. He et al. (2007) give the functional marker.
- **Domain**: all of neglect. It is the field's consensus and the article's named rival.

### The disconnection account (Thiebaut de Schotten et al. 2005; Bartolomeo et al. 2007; Karnath and Rorden 2012)
- **Core claim**: white-matter disconnection of parieto-frontal pathways (SLF II-III, superior occipitofrontal fasciculus) is the crucial antecedent; cortical-locus disputes (Karnath 2001 versus Mort 2003) dissolve into pathway anatomy.
- **Reversibility prediction**: the same as the network account, with a sharper statement of what is *not* reversible: the tract is gone, so remission is always compensation across intact routes and never restoration of the lesioned one. This is a useful constraint for the article's calibration: no remission study claims the lesion's own function returned.

### The sensorimotor / egocentric-frame account (Rubens 1985; Karnath 1993; Rossetti 1998; Jacquin-Courtois 2013; Striemer and Danckert 2010)
- **Core claim**: the vestibular, proprioceptive, optokinetic and prism manipulations act on the coordinate transformation that sets the egocentric midline; their effect on neglect is a shift of the frame, and on the critics' reading, largely of visuomotor behaviour rather than perception.
- **Reversibility prediction**: transient and frame-bound for the passive manipulations; longer for prisms, but disputed (Zhu 2025: immediate yes, long-term limited; Cochrane 2021: very uncertain).
- **Why it matters here**: it is the account on which most "reversals of attentional access" are not reversals of *access* at all.

### The two-component (spatial plus non-spatial) account (Robertson et al. 1995, 1998; Husain and Rorden 2003; Malhotra 2006)
- **Core claim**: a non-lateralised deficit in sustained attention and alertness amplifies the lateralised spatial bias; raising arousal shrinks the bias.
- **Reversibility prediction**: any alerting manipulation, including the patient's own, reverses neglect transiently; and this is the one class where the dependent measure has been awareness itself (Robertson 1998).

## The Discriminative Question (short, as instructed)

Transient remission of neglect on a stable lesion is an intact-substrate reversal in the concept note's sense (no substrate change between impaired and remitted state). Every account above predicts it; the network account predicts it with a mechanism and a marker (Corbetta 2005; He 2007), and Bottini et al. (2001) show the remission manipulations driving exactly the spared tissue that account names. The interface reading therefore adds **nothing at the case level**, and the article should open by saying so, then claim only three things:

1. **A correction to the Map's own description.** Neglect is a biased competition with a measurable latency cost (Robertson 1998: half a second), not an erasure; it reverses under six independent manipulations, under instruction (Riddoch and Humphreys 1983), and under the patient's own self-alerting (Robertson 1995). The corpus's "erasure without remainder", "jams shut" and "vanishes without a trace" are wrong as descriptions and should be retired in the hosts as headroom allows (only `neurological-dissociations` has any).
2. **A discipline for the whole clinical wing.** Reversal of behaviour and reversal of awareness come apart (Striemer and Danckert 2010), and only one study in forty years measured the latter. The wing's other reversibility cases (conversion, dissociative amnesia, anosognosia) mostly measure behaviour too; the neglect literature is where the distinction has been argued out, and the concept page should import it.
3. **One consistency point, labelled as such.** The manipulations that reach awareness act on alertness and endogenous orienting; the ones that reach the frame act on exploration. That is what a willed-attention interface would look like, and it is what a spared dorsal network looks like. The article states both and stops.

What would move the case off neutral: a manipulation that restores *awareness* (temporal-order or detection-threshold measures) without any measurable re-weighting of spared-network activity, or a reversal whose order across patients tracks a channel architecture the network account has to add per case (the `memory-channel-interface-evidence` move). Neither exists in the sources read.

## Positioning Against the Five Clinical Standards

1. **Replication status**: the transient vestibular effect is replicated across groups and decades (Rubens 1985; Cappa 1987; Vallar 1993; Wilkinson 2014 as a 52-patient RCT). Prism adaptation has ten sham-or-conventional-controlled RCTs (Zhu 2025) with a positive immediate and null long-term picture, two clearly null sham-controlled trials (Ten Brink 2017; Vilimovsky 2021) and one recent positive one (Feng 2026). Alerting: two small studies (n = 8 and the 1998 Nature series). This is the best-replicated reversal class in the wing.
2. **Control adequacy**: Karnath et al. (1993) has a within-study active control (hand vibration null); Ten Brink, Vilimovsky and Feng are sham-controlled; Wilkinson is double-blind. Far better than the FND or amnesia classes, where the concept note found one active control in total.
3. **Interpretive range**: five accounts fit every reversal; the range narrows by nothing at the case level. Honest grade: the reversals are *established*; the interface reading of them is *live hypothesis, tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating*.
4. **Convergence across methods**: the finding converges across vestibular, proprioceptive, optokinetic, prism, alerting and pharmacological routes and across bisection, cancellation, midline and temporal-order measures. Route-indifference of this breadth is itself what the network account predicts and the interface reading merely tolerates.
5. **Honest calibration**: "compatible with" for the reading; "established, heterogeneous, mostly behavioural" for the data (Halligan 2003; Longley 2021).
