## Executive Summary

Tressoldi and Storm's Stage 2 registered report is real, is in *F1000Research* (volume 10, article 234), and reached version 4 on 2024-05-24 with open peer-review status "2 approved, 1 not approved". The abstract's headline (pooled effect about .08, four bias tests passed, no decline) is accurate to the paper, but the paper's own tables are much weaker than the abstract reads: I² = 63.8%, a median study effect of .017 with an interval spanning zero, median power .088, and an effect for unselected participants whose interval includes zero. The authors themselves concede that a moderate publication bias "may account for the observed effect size". The critique literature is substantial and unresolved (§Key Sources). The registered report answers the publication-bias and decline critiques with modern estimators and leaves the study-quality, randomisation, heterogeneity and prospective-replication critiques standing: it is a preregistered analysis of a retrospective pool, and it calls for a meta-analysis of preregistered studies.

On replication: no adversarial multi-site ganzfeld test with a published confirmatory result was found (control-pair search described under §Gaps). A six-site international study closed data collection in 2026 and has released data and a methods-paper archive that by its own statement contains no confirmatory psi test. The best prospective datum found favours the claim weakly: a preregistered single-laboratory Edinburgh study of 240 selected participants reported a 30% hit rate against 25% chance (p = .043, one-tailed), in a doctoral thesis. For the Map a positive ganzfeld result would be a breach of the content-confined interface, so nothing here supports the Map in either direction.

### Tressoldi & Storm (2024, v4) — Stage 2 Registered Report

- **Type**: Registered-report meta-analysis, open post-publication peer review
- **Metadata (Crossref)**: Tressoldi, P. E. & Storm, L.; *F1000Research* 10:234. Version DOIs .1 (2021-03-24, "awaiting peer review"), .2 (2023-02-09), .3 (2024-02-08), .4 (2024-05-24). Stage 1 protocol: *F1000Research* 9:826, DOI 10.12688/f1000research.24868.3; its referee reports (Utts, Schmidt) were not read.
- **Peer review**: v2 "2 approved with reservations, 1 not approved"; v4 "2 approved, 1 not approved". Referees: M.-A. Amorim (Paris-Saclay; approved v4), D. Radin (Institute of Noetic Sciences; approved v3), P. Orepic (Geneva, neuroscience; "reject" on v2 and v3). Both approving referees list psi research among their expertise; Orepic states "I am not familiar with the literature nor the methods in parapsychology". Report the dissent with both facts.
- **Key points**:
  - Database: "our final database comprises 78 studies, for a total of 113 effect sizes carried out by 46 different principal investigators". The Discussion calls these "113 studies"; the unit is effect sizes.
  - Pooled effect (Table 2): frequentist .074 (.03–.12), Bayesian .084 (.05–.12), τ² .03, I² 63.8 (49.8–69.9). "The level of heterogeneity is medium-large as expected by the influence of the moderators."
  - Central tendency: "Given this heterogeneity level, the values of the effect size median = .017 (-.025 - .06) and mode -.01 (-.13 - .10), are uninformative."
  - Trend: meta-regression on year "show a slope estimate of .0012 (95%CIs: -.002 - .005; p = .53)".
  - Bias tests (Table 3): p-uniform* .12 (.04–.21), 3PSM .15 (.05–.24), RoBMA .074 (.00–.12). Sensitivity analysis: to shift the estimate to .01, "affirmative results would need to be at least 4 fold more likely to be published than nonaffirmative results". Amorim's v3 report had an earlier "at least 20 fold" corrected to 4.
  - Moderators (Table 4): selected participants .13 (.06–.20), non-selected .04 (−.01–.09); task Type 1 .12 (−.08–.33), Type 2 .04 (−.04–.13), Type 3 (telepathy design) .08 (.03–.14); peer-review level 1 .073, level 2 .076. "Selected participants show an almost three-fold increase in the effect size with respect to the non-selected participants."
  - Power: "The median statistical power related to the observed overall effect size is .088." The paper prints "only 30 (22.5%) of the studies reported statistically significant results"; reported as printed (30 is 26.5% of 113).
  - Authors' own limits: "although the meta-analysis is not immune to publication bias (given that a moderate level of bias favoring significant results in the published literature may account for the observed effect size), the meta-analysis results appear relatively robust against the potential impact of this bias". On QRPs: "These practices were difficult to detect after the publication of the study". Closing: "We hope to update the evidence related to anomalous perception in a Ganzfeld environment with a meta-analysis of preregistered studies in the near future."
  - Abstract-level conclusion, which is stronger than the limits just quoted: "The available evidence does not seem to be contaminated by publication bias or questionable research practices."
  - Version sensitivity: v1 and v2 abstracts give .088 (.04–.13), v3/v4 "approximately .08 (.04 -.12)"; Amorim's v3 report notes the estimate "changed from .099 (.05-.14) to .08 (.04 -.12)" after a change of denominator from participants to trials.
  - Dissent: "I am also not convinced that the problem of publication bias is solved."; "Another major issue that makes the results unconvincing is the inclusion of studies that are not properly peer-reviewed."; on v3, "The authors did not address my concerns. I would suggest not approving this manuscript."

### Milton & Wiseman (1999) and the Storm–Ertel exchange (2001)
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10414223/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11393304/ ; reply abstract via OpenAlex, https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1037/0033-2909.127.3.434
- **Type**: abstracts only
- **Key points**:
  - Milton & Wiseman pooled "30 ganzfeld ESP studies from 7 independent laboratories adhering to the same stringent methodological guidelines that C. Honorton followed". "The studies failed to confirm his main effect of participants scoring above chance on the ESP task, Stouffer z = 0.70, p = .24, one-tailed; M effect size (z/N1/2) = 0.013, SD = 0.23."
  - "The authors conclude that the ganzfeld technique does not at present offer a replicable method for producing ESP in the laboratory."
  - Storm & Ertel answered by aggregating older and newer studies: "a database comprising 79 ganzfeld-autoganzfeld studies (ES = 0.138, Stouffer Z = 5.66, p = 7.78 x 10(-9))".
  - Milton & Wiseman's reply: "they ignored the well-documented and widely recognized methodological problems in the early studies, which make it impossible to interpret the results as evidence of extrasensory perception".

## Key Debates

### Decline or no decline?
- **Sides**: Milton & Wiseman's post-guideline set (0.013) and the Map's Walach et al. (2014) citation say decline; the registered report's meta-regression (slope .0012, p = .53) says none.
- **Core disagreement**: which studies count. A flat slope across a pool of mixed designs does not contradict a drop within the guideline-compliant subset.
- **Current state**: unresolved. The registered report's median of .017 against a mean of .074 shows the pooled figure is carried by a minority of effect sizes.

### Does the registered report answer the earlier critiques?
- **Answered in part**: publication bias (four estimators, with the authors' own concession and a four-fold sensitivity bound); decline (cumulative analysis and meta-regression); fixed-effect pooling (random-effects models).
- **Not answered**: Rouder's randomisation split (no manual-versus-automatic moderator is reported); Hyman's and Milton & Wiseman's study-quality objection (the only quality moderator is peer-review level, which the dissenting referee turns against the paper); heterogeneity (I² 63.8%, attributed to moderators); prospective replication (deferred to a future meta-analysis of preregistered studies).
- **Structural limit**: registration fixed the analysis plan for a database the authors had meta-analysed before. It cannot retro-register the 113 effect sizes.

## Bearing on the Map's Position

The firewall scores a positive ganzfeld result as a breach: telepathic reception is content with no local neural pre-image (Constraint 3). Nothing here reaches the wager's bar of "replicated psi that is large, lawlike or content-importing", because the replication on offer is retrospective aggregation plus one marginal single-laboratory prospective study. Nothing here confirms the Map either: a null would leave the interface as unsupported as before. The one live exposure is the six-site study's confirmatory outcome, which the Map should report when it appears, in whichever direction.
