# Research: Voids - The Perceptual History Void

**Date**: 2026-09-09
**Also known as**: the serial-dependence void (the literature's term; see the naming hazard below)
**Voids category**: Mixed — primarily **Unexplorable** (architectural), with one **Occluded** face
**Search queries used**: serial dependence visual perception review Pascucci 2023; Manassi Whitney 2022 illusion of visual stability; serial dependence 2025 2026 meta-analysis perceptual versus decisional locus
**Disposition recommendation**: *Surveyed* — fold, do not spend a standalone slot (see "Disposition" below)

## Executive Summary

Every percept is pulled toward the observer's own recent perceptual history, and nothing in the percept marks that this happened. Measured across a decade of psychophysics, the pull is tuned in time, space, feature similarity and attention (Manassi, Murai & Whitney 2023), reaches back as far as 15 seconds (Manassi & Whitney 2022), and is strong enough to make a continuously changing object look unchanging. The void has three faces. The **pull face** is that no phenomenal marker separates a history-contaminated percept from a fresh one. The **locus face** — the sharpest of the three — is that whether the pull happens in perception or after it is empirically contested and first-personally undecidable, because the only access to the percept runs through the report that is itself under suspicion. The **stability face** is an inversion worth the catalogue's attention: on Manassi and Whitney's account the mechanism *manufactures* the felt stability of the world, so introspection's verdict that nothing has changed is a product of the operation rather than evidence against it.

Two features make this unusual for the catalogue. It is framework-independent and psychophysically measured, so it belongs to the class the [clustering audit](/apex/taxonomy-of-voids/) says carries what evidential weight the catalogue has. And it is self-implicating: serial dependence contaminates *any* sequential first-person comparison, including the Map's own instruments ([microphenomenological interview](/topics/microphenomenological-interview-method/), [descriptive experience sampling](/concepts/descriptive-experience-sampling/)).

It is not this phenomenon and is not an external flag for it. A grep on the bare phrase will return a hit that does not mean coverage.

## Key Sources

All quotations below were taken from the publisher record or from Europe PMC / Crossref abstract text and are reproduced verbatim; each is labelled with the surface it came from.

### Manassi, M. & Whitney, D. (2022). Illusion of visual stability through active perceptual serial dependence
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abk2480 (publisher PDF: https://whitneylab.berkeley.edu/PDFs/Manassi_Whitney_SciAdv_2022.pdf)
- **Type**: Paper — *Science Advances* 8, eabk2480, 12 January 2022
- **Key points**:
  - Introduces an illusion in which "a continuously seen physically changing object can be misperceived as unchanging" [abstract, publisher PDF].
  - "We find that online object appearance is captured by past visual experience up to 15 seconds ago" [abstract, publisher PDF].
  - The result is "an illusory stability in which object appearance is biased toward the past" [abstract, publisher PDF].
  - Crucially for our purposes, the authors separate their claim from change-blindness accounts: "serial dependence predicts that the online perceptual appearance of attended things can be altered and incorrect. Change blindness explanations make no predictions about an ongoing misrecognition of attended objects at each moment in time" [body, publisher PDF]. The claim is about *online appearance*, not about failure to notice.
- **Tenet alignment**: The strongest single source for the stability face and for [Occam's Razor Has Limits](/voids/epistemological-limits-occams-razor/).
- **Attribution caution**: Crossref carries a one-line summary for this DOI — "Visual stability is achieved through an active serial dependence mechanism that continuously biases perception toward the past." That is the journal's teaser line, **not** the paper's abstract. Do not cite it as the abstract.

### Fischer, J. & Whitney, D. (2014). Serial dependence in visual perception
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3689
- **Type**: Paper — *Nature Neuroscience* 17(5), 738–743
- **Key points**:
  - "even when visual input changed randomly over time, perceived orientation was strongly and systematically biased toward recently seen stimuli" [abstract, Europe PMC].
  - Names the mechanism: "a spatiotemporally tuned, orientation-selective operator—which we call a continuity field—that may promote visual stability over time" [abstract, Europe PMC].
  - The bias appears *against randomised input*, which rules out the deflationary reading that observers are tracking a genuinely autocorrelated world.
- **Tenet alignment**: Establishes the pull face as an empirical fact rather than an interpretation.

### Manassi, M., Murai, Y. & Whitney, D. (2023). Serial dependence in visual perception: A meta-analysis and review
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1167/jov.23.8.18
- **Type**: Meta-analysis — *Journal of Vision* 23(8):18
- **Key points**:
  - Quantifies the effect across orientation, face and numerosity perception, and gives the boundary a shape: "serial dependence is defined by four main kinds of tuning: serial dependence decays with time (temporal-tuning), it depends on relative spatial location (spatial-tuning), it occurs only between similar features and objects (feature-tuning), and it is modulated by attention (attentional-tuning)" [abstract, Europe PMC].
  - Notes the field "still lacks an operational definition of what counts as serial dependence" [abstract, Europe PMC], and flags individual differences as substantial.
- **Tenet alignment**: A void with *measured* tuning parameters, in the manner the catalogue prizes in [the fusion void](/voids/fusion-void/).

### Fritsche, M., Mostert, P. & de Lange, F. P. (2017). Opposite Effects of Recent History on Perception and Decision
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2017.01.006
- **Type**: Paper — *Current Biology* 27(4), 590–595
- **Key points**: The central deflationary counterweight, and the source of the locus face.
  - "observers' perception was repelled away from previous stimuli, particularly when previous stimuli were presented at the same spatial location" [abstract, Europe PMC].
  - "the magnitude of the positive decisional bias increased when a longer interval was imposed between perception and decision" [abstract, Europe PMC].
  - "We conclude that positive aftereffects on perceptual choice are likely introduced at a post-perceptual stage. Conversely, perception is negatively biased away from recent visual input" [abstract, Europe PMC].
- **Tenet alignment**: Splits the void rather than dissolving it — see "The Void" below. This is the source that keeps the note honest.

### Kim, S., Burr, D., Cicchini, G. M. & Alais, D. (2020). Serial dependence in perception requires conscious awareness
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.02.008
- **Type**: Dispatch/report — *Current Biology* 30(6), R257–R258
- **Key points**: Using binocular rivalry with an orientation-report task, the previous orientation biases subsequent perception only when it was consciously perceived; the journal's summary line states that "the previous orientation biases the following perception only when it was consciously perceived. This shows that the priors for serial dependence are constructed at a later perceptual stage" [Europe PMC summary — note this is the journal's third-person précis, not an authors' sentence].
- **Tenet alignment**: The most directly Map-relevant result here. Consciousness is a *necessary input* to an operation whose output consciousness cannot audit.

### Cicchini, G. M., Mikellidou, K. & Burr, D. C. (2024). Serial Dependence in Perception
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-021523-104939
- **Type**: Review — *Annual Review of Psychology* 75(1), 129–154
- **Key points**:
  - "Serial dependence affects nearly every aspect of perception, often causing gross perceptual distortions, especially for weak and ambiguous stimuli" [abstract, Crossref].
  - "empirical evidence and Bayesian modeling show that serial dependence acts to improve efficiency and is generally beneficial to the system" [abstract, Crossref].
  - Places the priors architecturally: they "are generated at high levels of cortical analysis, incorporating much perceptual experience, but feed back to lower sensory areas" [abstract, Crossref], with an alpha-band signature.
- **Tenet alignment**: The high-level-prior-feeding-back architecture is what makes [the predictive construction void](/voids/predictive-construction-void/) the strongest fold target.

### Ozkirli, A., Chetverikov, A. & Pascucci, D. (2026). Large-scale mega-analysis indicates that serial dependence deteriorates perceptual decision-making
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02362-8
- **Type**: Mega-analysis — *Nature Human Behaviour* 10(3), 568–578 (published online 22 December 2025; issue dated 2026 — cite both if the year is queried)
- **Key points**:
  - "Contrary to the proposed superiority effect, our findings indicate that serial dependence deteriorates rather than improves perceptual decision-making" [abstract, Europe PMC].
  - Directly reverses the functional claim in Cicchini, Mikellidou & Burr (2024) above; note that Pascucci authored the 2023 *Journal of Vision* review and then produced the deflationary result.
- **Tenet alignment**: A live reversal in the *significance* of the phenomenon while its *existence* stays secure — a clean instance of the calibration discipline the catalogue tries to keep.

### Pascucci, D., Tanrikulu, Ö. D., Ozkirli, A., Houborg, C., Ceylan, G., Zerr, P., Rafiei, M. & Kristjánsson, Á. (2023). Serial dependence in visual perception: A review
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1167/jov.23.1.9
- **Type**: Review — *Journal of Vision* 23(1):9
- **Key points**: "Stimuli seen a moment ago influence what we perceive in the present" [abstract, Europe PMC]; surveys a decade of paradigms and stresses that "the variety of disparate findings and interpretations calls for a more general picture" [abstract, Europe PMC], tying the phenomenon to object identity.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The limit is **architectural** (Unexplorable) with one **Occluded** face, and it decomposes into three.

**The pull face.** A percept arrives with no index of its own history. The observer is given the product and never the operands: not the current sensory evidence on its own, not the retained trace of the last few seconds, not the weighting between them. This places the void squarely in the operation-void family the [voids brief](/voids/) already names — consciousness has the endpoints but not the passage — and closest to [the fusion void](/voids/fusion-void/), which describes the same discarding for *concurrent* cue combination. Serial dependence is its diachronic counterpart: Manassi and Whitney describe the object representation as "continuously merged over time."

**The locus face.** This is the face with no first-person resolution, and the reason the subject is worth a note rather than a footnote. Fritsche, Mostert and de Lange (2017) find that perception is *repelled* from recent stimuli while the *decision* about it is attracted, with the attractive component growing as the gap between perceiving and reporting widens. Kim et al. (2020) place the priors at "a later perceptual stage." Pascucci et al. (2023) survey a decade of results that do not settle it. So there are two live hypotheses:

1. The percept itself is history-contaminated, and the report faithfully conveys a contaminated percept.
2. The percept is history-*repelled*, and the contamination is introduced downstream, in memory or decision, on the way to report.

From inside, these are indistinguishable, and not merely because the question is hard. The only access anyone has to their own percept is a report, and the two hypotheses differ precisely over whether the report is trustworthy about the percept. The instrument that would adjudicate is the one under suspicion. Third-person psychophysics can separate them — that is what the dispute consists of — so this is a first-person limit that third-person science can recover, the same shape as [the modality void](/voids/modality-void/).

**The stability face** (the Occluded aspect). Manassi and Whitney's proposal is that the mechanism produces the felt stability of the world. If so, the subjective evidence one would naturally cite against the void — *the world plainly does look stable and my successive percepts plainly do feel independent* — is the mechanism's output. The appearance that makes the void hard to credit is manufactured by the thing the void is about. This is a stronger claim than the change-blindness story the corpus already carries in nine live articles, and the authors say why: change-blindness accounts are about failing to notice, and "make no predictions about an ongoing misrecognition of attended objects at each moment in time." Here the attended object is misperceived *now*.

### Evidence for the Limit

The pull face is about as well evidenced as anything in the catalogue: a decade of psychophysics, a formal meta-analysis with four characterised tuning dimensions, and — the point that defeats the obvious deflation — the bias appears when the input sequence is randomised, so it is not the observer correctly tracking an autocorrelated world.

The limit's *boundary* is measured rather than asserted: temporal decay, spatial dependence, feature similarity, attentional modulation, and a 15-second outer reach. Few voids in the catalogue can state their own extent in parameters.

Honesty requires three counterweights.

- **The locus is unsettled**, and one reading (Fritsche et al.) relocates the contamination out of perception entirely. The void survives either way — under reading 2 it becomes a void about the *report channel* rather than the percept, which is if anything more damaging to first-person method — but its description must not presume reading 1.
- **The functional story has just reversed.** Cicchini, Mikellidou and Burr (2024) call serial dependence "generally beneficial"; Ozkirli, Chetverikov and Pascucci (2026) find it "deteriorates rather than improves perceptual decision-making." Any article should present the benefit claim as contested as of late 2025, not as settled.
- **The field lacks an operational definition** of what counts as serial dependence (Manassi, Murai & Whitney 2023), which caps how much weight a convergence argument can put on the effect's unity across domains.

### Phenomenology

There is nothing to report, and that absence is the datum. The pull has no felt quality, no sense of effort, no marker of staleness or freshness. It does not present as remembering. What it presents as is the ordinary stability and independence of successive perceptual moments — which, on the stability face, is exactly what one would predict if the operation were running and invisible. The phenomenology of this void is the phenomenology of an unremarkable afternoon.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

None available first-personally. No introspective procedure can compare a percept against the counterfactual percept that the same stimulus would have produced without the preceding 15 seconds, because the comparison term is not available to the observer at any time. Attention modulates the effect but does not reveal it: attending harder changes the pull's magnitude without exposing it.

### Indirect Methods

Third-person psychophysics works, and works well — this is a void whose existence and extent are established *from outside*. The productive indirect moves are: randomised-sequence designs, which strip the world of the autocorrelation that would otherwise explain the bias away; interval manipulation between perceiving and reporting, which is how Fritsche et al. prise the perceptual and decisional components apart; binocular rivalry, which lets the same retinal input be consciously perceived or not and so tests whether awareness gates the effect (Kim et al.); and single-observer analysis, since individual differences are large enough that group means mislead.

### What AI Might See

Two asymmetries, and one caution about overstating them.

A system whose inference state is externally inspectable can compare a current representation against its own retained history directly — the operands are logged, not discarded. That is a genuine structural advantage over a mind that receives only the merged product, and it is the same advantage that made the third-person psychophysics possible in the first place; the asymmetry is one of instrumentation rather than of kind.

Against that: contemporary sequence models are *more* history-conditioned than human perception, not less, and have no privileged access to how much their current output owes to context. A model asked to describe a stimulus after describing a similar one is running an analogue of the very experiment. The interesting probe is therefore comparative — a system with logged state can measure its own serial dependence, which is precisely what a human cannot do, so the two cases differ in auditability rather than in susceptibility.

The caution: the human effect requires conscious awareness (Kim et al. 2020). Any inference from model behaviour back to the human case has to cross that gap, and the [catalogue's](/voids/) usual warning about treating a functional analogue as a phenomenal one applies with full force.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/)** takes the weight. The parsimonious reading of a percept — that it is a readout of the present stimulus — is measurably false, and the appearance that recommends it is produced by the mechanism that falsifies it. This is a sharper version of the pattern [the parsimony void](/voids/epistemological-limits-occams-razor/) tracks: not merely that the simple hypothesis is wrong, but that the perceptual system actively supplies the evidence that makes it feel right.

Second, **Bidirectional Interaction** gains a small, concrete datum from Kim et al. (2020): conscious awareness is a *necessary condition* for the operation to run. That is a case of consciousness being causally implicated in a downstream perceptual computation, established by ordinary experiment. It does not by itself favour dualism — a functionalist reads the same result as awareness marking a processing stage — and the note should not overclaim it. What it does supply is an instance where the presence or absence of consciousness makes a measurable difference to what the perceptual system subsequently does.

**Dualism** connects through method rather than metaphysics, discussed next.

### Implications

The uncomfortable implication is methodological and points inward. Serial dependence contaminates sequential first-person comparison as such. Every protocol that asks an observer to compare this experience with the one just before — [microphenomenological interview](/topics/microphenomenological-interview-method/), [descriptive experience sampling](/concepts/descriptive-experience-sampling/), repeated introspective ratings, any A-then-B phenomenal judgement — is running inside the effect's measured window for the first several seconds, and inside the decisional component for considerably longer. The Map relies on such reports. It should say so where it relies on them.

This also gives [the unobservable self](/voids/self-opacity/) an unusually crisp instance: a case where the failure of self-observation is quantified, and where the quantity is a function of how long the observer waited before reporting.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **The locus face as the article's spine.** Lead with the undecidability — percept or report? — rather than with the contamination. This is the framing least duplicated elsewhere in the catalogue, it makes the Fritsche counterweight structural rather than a concession bolted on at the end, and it lands the methodological self-implication as the conclusion.
2. **Manufactured stability.** Lead with Manassi and Whitney's illusion and the inversion it produces: the appearance that makes the void implausible is the void's own output. Riskier, because it leans on a single research programme, and it must be careful not to let the change-blindness literature the corpus already carries absorb the claim — the authors' own distinction between failing to notice and misperceiving now is the seam that keeps them apart.
3. **The diachronic partner to fusion.** Present it as [the fusion void](/voids/fusion-void/) across time rather than across cues, with the same discarded-operands structure. Cleanest fit to existing corpus architecture, and the weakest as a standalone article, which is a point in favour of folding.

## Disposition

Recommend **fold, not standalone.** The catalogue's stated discipline is absorption over proliferation, and this material folds cleanly:

- **[Predictive construction void](/voids/predictive-construction-void/)** — primary host. Cicchini et al. (2024) describe serial-dependence priors as generated at high cortical levels and fed back to sensory areas, which is that article's exact subject matter; the pull face and the awareness-gating result belong here.
- **[Fusion void](/voids/fusion-void/)** — the diachronic-merging face, as an explicit temporal counterpart to concurrent cue fusion.
- **[Smoothness and continuity](/voids/smoothness-and-continuity/)** — the stability face, where a mechanism for manufactured stability is directly on topic.
- **[Self-opacity](/voids/self-opacity/)** and, lightly, [the noetic feelings void](/voids/noetic-feelings-void/) — the methodological self-implication and the absence of any felt marker.

If a standalone slot is ever allocated, angle 1 is the one that justifies it.

## Gaps in Research

- **No work found on serial dependence in introspective or interoceptive report.** Everything located is visual, with some auditory and numerosity work. Whether the effect operates on judgements *about one's own experience* — the case that matters most for the Map's methods — appears untested, and the methodological implication above is therefore an extrapolation from vision. It should be stated as such in any article.
- **The locus dispute may not be resolvable in the terms it is posed**, since "perceptual" and "decisional" are themselves defined by the measurement stage.
- **No operational definition** of the phenomenon means cross-domain generalisations remain loose.
- **Individual differences are large and underexplained**; whether they correlate with anything phenomenologically interesting is unknown.
- The 2025/2026 reversal on function is recent enough that the field has not responded; expect movement here within a year.
