# Research: African Philosophy of Mind — the 2020s Literature

**Date**: 2026-08-06
**Scope**: sourcing gap in the five-article African cluster, flagged by 3/3 outer reviewers on 2026-08-06
**Method**: Crossref and OpenAlex metadata verification; one primary text recovered in full (Majeed 2013); SEP and secondary sources for background. No claim below is sourced from unfinishablemap.org.

## Executive Summary

The 2020s literature on African philosophy of mind exists, is substantial, and **on balance runs against the Map's convergence argument rather than for it**. Three findings carry most of the weight. First, the Akan dispute is now verifiable at primary-source level: Majeed 2013 was recovered in full, and it confirms both the Map's attribution of the "physicalist in disguise" line *and* — more importantly — that Wiredu treats **both** *okra* and *sunsum* as quasi-physical, which is what `african-philosophy-of-consciousness` L83 now says. That sentence is correct as it stands. Second, Hountondji's **unanimist fallacy** is a live and precisely-aimed objection to the cluster's central move: counting Bantu, Akan, Yoruba, Igbo and Zulu as separate votes for irreducibility is the exact operation Hountondji named as the founding error of ethnophilosophy. Third, and least comfortable, **Ada Agada** — the most systematic contemporary metaphysician working directly from the vital-force material the Map's Bantu section uses — builds from it a **proto-panpsychist neutral monism leaning toward idealism**, which the Map's tenets exclude. The strongest living development of the Map's own source material arrives at a rival position.

A fourth finding is bibliographic rather than philosophical: the Ubuntu field is not merely "contested" but carries a fully documented twelve-year exchange (2013–2025) that no cluster article mentions.

**Recommendation: refinement, not a new article.** See [Article or Refinement](#article-or-refinement) below.

## Finding 1 — The Akan Dispute (Grade A, primary text recovered)

The full text of Majeed 2013 was recovered and read. This is the only source here at full-text grade, and it settles several questions the cluster has been carrying on secondary authority.

**Bibliographic**: Majeed, H. M. (2013). "A Critique of the Concept of Quasi-Physicalism in Akan Philosophy." *African Studies Quarterly* **14**(1–2): 23–34. Open access at `https://asq.africa.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/168/Majeed-2013.pdf`. Majeed lectures in philosophy at the University of Ghana; the article derives from doctoral research supported by the Universities of South Africa and Ghana.

**What it establishes:**

**(a) Wiredu's quasi-physicalism covers both *okra* and *sunsum*.** Majeed, p. 23: quasi-physicalism is "the brainchild of an Akan philosopher, Kwasi Wiredu, and is strongly held also by Safro Kwame". And on the same page: "It is these spiritual conceptions of ōkra and sunsum that Kwasi Wiredu and Safro Kwame reject. They argue … that ōkra and sunsum are not spiritual but are rather quasi-physical." The current text of `african-philosophy-of-consciousness` L83 — "he rejects the spiritual construal of *okra* and *sunsum* … holding that both are instead *quasi-physical*" — is **correct and now primary-source confirmed**. This closes the loop on `deep-review-2026-05-31` L29, which had noted the discrepancy while the article still made the narrower sunsum-only claim.

**(b) The "physicalist in disguise" attribution is verbatim.** Majeed, p. 29: *"The quasi-physicalist is a physicalist in disguise. His claim to allow for things that are not entirely subject to the laws of physics is misleading."* The cluster's use of this line is accurate. Note it opens a paragraph and is Majeed's own thesis sentence, not an aside.

**(c) Majeed's own position — the strongest available support for the cluster's dualist reading, and it is explicitly hedged.** Majeed, p. 25: *"Therefore, Gyekye's position, although not necessarily Cartesian, is ultimately dualistic; and I am more inclined toward it than toward Wiredu's and Kwame's."* This is genuinely useful to the Map: a contemporary Akan philosopher, writing in an Akan-internal debate, endorses a dualist reading. **But the qualifier "although not necessarily Cartesian" is doing real work and must travel with the quote.** Majeed is not endorsing substance dualism of the kind the Map's tenets assert.

**(d) Majeed's argument is that quasi-physicalism collapses into physicalism by way of a promissory note.** He quotes Safro Kwame (p. 29): the quasi-physicalist *"does not deny that as our discovery of physical laws proceeds and our scientific knowledge increases, we may come to accept some or all the quasi-physical objects as bona fide physical objects. The quasi-physicalism of today may then turn out to be the materialism or physicalism of tomorrow."* Majeed's reply is that this makes the quasi-physicalist "both today's physicalist and tomorrow's physicalist today" — "somebody like a prophet who hopes that his predictions come true in future."

**(e) A distinction the cluster does not currently draw.** Majeed insists (p. 25) that "materialism may not mean the same as physicalism" and that "conscious efforts must always be made not to conflate them": physicalism admits anything describable "in a language of physics", whereas materialism "misleadingly seems to conjure up a Newtonian account of matter". The cluster's articles move between "materialism" and "physicalism" freely.

**(f) Majeed's positive metaphysics — the water/ice model.** Spirits including *okra* are "essentially metaphysical in nature, even though they have some capacity for quasi-physical manifestation" (p. 29), likened to water that can freeze without being essentially solid. This is a substantive third position between Gyekye and Wiredu that the cluster does not represent at all.

**Assessment**: this finding is **favourable to the Map on accuracy** — the article's Wiredu sentences are right — and **neutral-to-favourable on substance**, supplying a real contemporary defender of the dualist reading, provided the "not necessarily Cartesian" hedge is preserved.

## Finding 2 — Hountondji's Unanimist Fallacy (Grade D, background only)

This is the sharpest finding against the Map, and the task note anticipated it correctly.

Hountondji's charge against ethnophilosophy is that it presents African thought as, in the SEP's summary, "an anonymous system of thought, without individual thinkers to claim or account for it", and rests on the false assumption that "traditional Africa was a place of philosophical unanimity". The standard label is **unanimism**: the postulate of an immutable collective philosophy common to all Africans, which presumes non-Western societies lack the internal contradiction, debate and pluralism found in every human society. For Hountondji philosophy is individual, critical and authored, not a worldview.

**Why this bites here.** `cross-traditional-convergence-on-consciousness-irreducibility` L102 counts African traditions as supplying "a further data point" via "Ubuntu philosophy and Akan conceptions of *sunsum*". `african-philosophy-of-consciousness` L93 counts "the widespread treatment of vital force as ontologically fundamental across multiple traditions". `cross-cultural-convergence-on-mental-causation` L81 already concedes that "African philosophical traditions" joins two distinct worlds. Aggregating Akan, Yoruba, Bantu, Igbo and Zulu material into one convergent African vote **is the unanimist move**, performed for a metaphysical rather than a cultural conclusion.

**The cluster is partly protected already, and the credit is due.** `african-philosophy-of-consciousness` L71 concedes the *-ntu* vocabulary is Kagame's and Jahn's recasting of Tempels; L73 records the Hountondji–Wiredu criticism and states plainly that "there is no single African philosophy of consciousness any more than there is a single European one". `cross-cultural-convergence-on-mental-causation` L79–L81 grades the West African strand a *candidate* lineage and refuses to merge Akan and Ubuntu. `cross-traditional-convergence` L142 warns against reading three sources of support as three independent confirmations.

**What is still missing** is the *name*. The cluster has assembled the objection piecemeal without recording that it is a canonical charge with a canonical author, one the cluster already cites — and cites, as the reviewers noted, only for the narrow anti-Tempels point. Naming it would let the concession be made once, in the tradition's own vocabulary, instead of re-derived per article.

**Honest statement of where this leaves the Map**: the convergence argument's African leg is weaker than the cluster's *aggregate* presentation implies, and the sharpest critique comes from inside African philosophy rather than from the Map's usual external critics. This does not defeat the leg — Hountondji's target is the inference from cultural material to a unanimous philosophy, and a Map that cites named individual philosophers (Gyekye, Wiredu, Majeed, Abimbola) disputing each other is doing something Hountondji explicitly licenses. The correct response is to **count named authors, not traditions**.

## Finding 3 — Agada's Proto-Panpsychism (Grade B/C; the finding that runs hardest against the Map)

**Ada Agada** is the contemporary African metaphysician working most directly on the material the Map's Bantu section uses, and his system reaches a position the Map's tenets exclude.

Consolationism is presented as an alternative constructive framework for metaphysics grounded in African thought and claimed to be universally applicable. Its foundational category is **mood**, in which emotion can be prior to reason. Two features matter here:

1. **Agada grounds mood in vital force directly.** *"Grounding the Consolationist Concept of Mood in the African Vital Force Theory"*, *Philosophia Africana* **19**(2):101–121, 2020, DOI `10.5325/philafri.19.2.0101`. This is the same *force vitale* material `african-philosophy-of-consciousness` L69–L75 and `interaction-problem-across-traditions` L108 build on.
2. **The resulting position is neutral monist, leaning idealist — not dualist.** On the standard characterisation, consolationist panpsychism is a neutral-monistic stance closer to idealism than to physicalism, on which moodiness produces mindedness and is the nature of every existent thing. *(Grade C — this characterisation comes from secondary summaries of the 2021 monograph, not from Agada's own page.)*

**Why this matters for the Map's own argument.** `interaction-problem-across-traditions` L132 already files force ontology among the monist strategies the Dualism tenet excludes, and `african-philosophy-of-consciousness` L95 states that on the force-ontology reading the material "draws no line between a physical outcome and a non-physical cause of it". Agada's work **confirms that filing empirically**: when a serious contemporary philosopher develops the vital-force material systematically, he lands on the monist side. The Map's articles present the monist and interactionist readings as two live options awaiting scholarly resolution; the strongest live development of the material has in fact chosen, and chosen against the Map.

This should be stated plainly rather than softened. It does not refute the Map — Agada's is one system, and the cluster's point that the interactionist reconstruction remains *available* survives. But "nothing in the sources settles which reconstruction is correct" (L97) is weaker than it looks once a major contemporary reconstruction is on the record.

**A second, subtler point in the Map's favour, worth recording for symmetry.** `african-philosophy-of-consciousness` L75 currently argues that a force ontology positing no micro-subjects "never generates" the combination problem, "but such an ontology is then not the panpsychism the comparison invokes." Agada's proto-panpsychism appears to be precisely an attempt to derive mindedness from vital force, which suggests the two are not as cleanly separable as L75 implies. **This is a hypothesis, not a finding** — the chapter and monograph text were not read. Flagged for a future pass, not for writing up.

**Related and uncited**: Agada, *Consolationism and Comparative African Philosophy: Beyond Universalism and Particularism*, Routledge, 22 September 2021, DOI `10.4324/9781003172123` (chapter 6, "The consolationist system", pp. 83–104); and Agada, "The African vital force theory of meaning in life", *SAJP* **39**(2):100–112, 2020, DOI `10.1080/02580136.2020.1770416`.

## Finding 6 — Post-Ethnophilosophy Frameworks

**Chimakonam's conversationalism (Grade B, abstract read).** "Conversationalism as an emerging method of thinking in and beyond African philosophy", *Acta Academica* **49**(2):11–33, 2017, DOI `10.18820/24150479/aa49i2.1`. From the abstract: he employs "an under-explored sub-Saharan African notion of 'relationship' to formulate a method that could ground discourses in African and intercultural philosophies", and articulates a **Context-dependence of Value (CdV)** principle that shifts "the paradigm of the truth of our propositions from the 'facts' which such 'propositions' assert to the 'context' in which those propositions are asserted."

**This is directly relevant to the Map's method and not only its bibliography.** Conversationalism is a rival account of *how* cross-tradition philosophical engagement should proceed — "equal and horizontal engagement" in intercultural philosophy. The convergent outer-review finding on the same day (assimilation by philosophical translation / evidence tourism / taxonomic violence) is, in African philosophy's own vocabulary, a charge that the Map engages vertically rather than horizontally. The open P1 writing-style task on scope labels and the exposition-appropriation firewall should know this framework exists; it is the indigenous statement of the problem that task is trying to solve.

Note also the *Arụmarụka: Journal of Conversational Thinking* venue of Molefe & Muade 2025 — conversationalism is now an institution with a journal, not a proposal.

**Mitova on decolonising knowledge (Grade C).** "Decolonising Knowledge Here and Now", *Philosophical Papers* **49**(2):191–212, 2020, DOI `10.1080/05568641.2020.1779606`. The task note's correction stands: the Gemini review's "2021" and its journal attributions were both wrong. Bears on the open Wiredu conceptual-decolonisation task.

**Balogun on method (Grade C, bonus find).** "Rethinking the aptness of the analytic method in African philosophy", *SAJP*, 2021, DOI `10.1080/02580136.2021.1954767` — the same Balogun as Springer ch. 3, on whether analytic method suits African philosophical material. Relevant to the same writing-style task.

`african-philosophy-of-consciousness` L79 now presents Menkiti's achieved-and-failable personhood correctly, and the references already carry Ikuenobe & Etieyibo (eds.), *Menkiti on Community and Becoming a Person* (Lexington, 2020, DOI `10.5771/9781498583664` — verified). ## Article or Refinement

**Refinement, unambiguously.** Four reasons:

1. **Cap arithmetic.** `topics/` has 1 slot and `concepts/` 4–5 (re-measure with `count_section_files`; the CLAUDE.md table goes stale). Spending the last `topics/` slot on a fifth African article would be poor use of scarce headroom.
2. **This is a sourcing gap, not a subject gap.** Every finding attaches to an existing paragraph in an existing article. Nothing here is a subject the cluster lacks a home for.
3. **A new article would worsen the diagnosed defect.** The convergent review finding was that the cluster over-aggregates African material. Adding a sixth page assembling more African material into more convergence is the wrong direction.
4. **The cluster is already carrying three open P1 tasks on one file.** Per the same-file pileup hazard, adding a new article compounds rather than relieves that.
