## Executive Summary

Walter Veit's **pathological complexity thesis** (PCT) states that "The function of consciousness is to enable the agent to respond to pathological complexity" [raw, Veit 2023b p. 175; Veit 2024]. "Pathological complexity" is his name for "the economic trade-off problem of having to deal with a complex body with high degrees of freedom" [raw, Veit 2023b abstract]. On the thesis, a Cambrian "computational explosion" in action options made a **common currency** for comparing actions worth having, and hedonic valence was that currency. Evaluative experience is therefore the first dimension of consciousness, and the sensory, self, temporal and unity dimensions were built on it later. The **term** is Veit's coinage ("that I shall dub", Veit 2022a). The **thesis form** is modelled on Godfrey-Smith's (1996) environmental complexity thesis, and the **pleasure-as-common-currency** idea comes from Michel Cabanac (1992), as Veit himself says.

Veit is a physicalist. He says he has "described myself as an illusionist elsewhere" [raw, Veit 2025b], and he answers the causal question by identity: "I do not mean that their phenomenality exerts causal power in addition to their function. They are identical." [raw, Veit 2025b]. He rejects epiphenomenalism on evolutionary grounds. He calls the PCT "in principle compatible" with dualism, but he regards developing dualism as "little more than games of chmess" [raw, Veit 2025b]. The Map can use him as an account of the **function and timing** of valenced experience and as a naturalist ally **against epiphenomenalism**. It cannot enlist him for dualism or for interaction.

The most useful material for the Map is the critical literature, more than the thesis itself. Three critics who are themselves naturalists (de Weerd 2025, Fischer & Barrett 2025, Pober 2025) argue that the evolutionary evidence supports the evolution of a **valence system**, not of **conscious** valence. Veit concedes that this "weakens my case" [raw, Veit 2026]. Frankish (2025), an illusionist, argues from the other side that if hedonic valence is irreducibly subjective, "the hard problem remains as hard as ever and the explanatory gap as wide" [raw, as quoted by Veit 2025b]. Both lines converge on the claim the Map already defends on `topics/consciousness-and-the-major-evolutionary-transitions`: a functional evolutionary account traces the outer side of the arrival of experience and has no instrument for the arrival itself. Veit also calls his Cambrian event "a major transition in evaluative agency" [raw, Veit 2023b]. He is therefore a second precedent, beside Ginsburg and Jablonka, for the "consciousness as a major transition" label that the transitions page discusses.

The PCT's core claim, hedonic valence as the common currency for action selection, is also the naturalist counterpart of the Map's own **[P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1)** (felt valence as the currency of conscious selection). The critics' objection is the published, evolutionary form of the value-blind horn that [the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection](/topics/the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection/) constructs.

## Key Sources

### Veit (2023b), "Complexity and the Evolution of Consciousness", *Biological Theory* 18(3): 175–190 (online 14 September 2022)
- **Type**: target article, with commentaries by Jablonka & Ginsburg and by Spurrett and a reply (Veit 2023c) in the same issue
- **Key points**:
  - The thesis, set as a boxed statement: "The function of consciousness is to enable the agent to respond to pathological complexity." [raw]
  - Definition: "This is the economic trade-off problem of having to deal with a complex body with high degrees of freedom, what I call 'pathological complexity.'" [raw, abstract]
  - Consciousness is "an adaptive solution to a design problem that led to the extinction of complex multicellular animal life following the Avalon explosion and that was subsequently solved during the Cambrian explosion" [raw, abstract]. On the Ediacaran: "The reason that I suspect the Avalon explosion 'failed' is because these organisms did not come up with a design solution to pay off this complex investment into behavioral flexibility." [raw]. Note the "I suspect": this is a conjecture, not a finding. The Avalon explosion itself is Shen et al. (2008).
  - The transition label: the Cambrian change "led to a major transition in evaluative agency or, as I shall call it, the evolution of Benthamite creatures" [raw]. "Benthamite creatures" is Veit's coinage, offered as a better term than Dennett's (1995) "Skinnerian creatures" for organisms capable of reinforcement learning [summary].
  - Genealogy: "Godfrey-Smith's (1996a) environmental complexity thesis, which inspired my own thesis" [raw]. Veit quotes the ECT: "The function of cognition is to enable the agent to deal with environmental complexity" (1996a, p. 3) [raw, as quoted by Veit]. The "common currency" idea is credited to behavioural ecology (McFarland & Sibly 1975; McNamara & Houston 1986) and to Cabanac, "the old utilitarian Benthamite idea that animals have a proximate common currency in the form of the hedonic experience of pleasure and pain" [raw]. Veit pushes Cabanac's early-Amniota origin back to the Cambrian [summary].
  - On the hard problem (Conclusion, p. 188): "the 'hard problem' of why things feel a certain way does not appear to be as much of a challenge within a hedonic framework. Things feel a certain way because they have to feel that way to be functional." [raw]. Also "This approach substantially narrows the explanatory gap" [raw].
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral on Bidirectional Interaction, since it gives experience an action-selection function. Conflicts with Dualism through its identity background. The p. 188 claim is where the Map disagrees (see [Key Debates](#key-debates)).

### Veit (2024), "The role of consciousness in adaptive behaviour: A philosophy for the science of animal consciousness", *Adaptive Behavior* 33(3): 169–174 (online 18 October 2024)
- **Type**: précis opening a two-part special issue (CC-BY)
- **Key points**:
  - Synonyms and disambiguation: "This should not be confused with complexity related to pathogens" [raw]. "I also use the term 'life-history complexity' and 'teleonomic complexity' interchangeably with 'pathological complexity'." [raw]. The "pathological" comes from his evolutionary account of health and pathology as deviation from the fitness-optimal life history [summary].
  - No single function: "Ironically, perhaps, I reject the idea that there is a specific function to consciousness." [raw]. The PCT is meant as a general ecological framework, not a one-function theory.
  - The mechanism: the Cambrian explosion in pathological complexity "led to the need for a common currency for decision-making: hedonic evaluation" [raw].
  - Evaluation first: chapter 3 argues "that evaluative experience (involving negative and positive feelings) is the best candidate for the origin of consciousness" [raw]. Later dimensions are derived from it: "Sensory experience is simply the gradual enrichment of this basic capacity" [raw].
  - Credit to Cabanac: "Inspired by earlier work by the neuroscientist Michel Cabanac, who also argued that pleasure constitutes both a 'common currency' for decision-making and the origins of consciousness" [raw].
  - Against epiphenomenalism: "Consciousness is too complex, too well-fitted to our environments, and too decision-relevant as that epiphenomenalism could be true, that is, the dualist view that consciousness is merely a byproduct of the brain's physical processes and never their cause." [raw; "as that" sic; a page number has been removed from mid-sentence]. Also "The complex neural architecture of consciousness is not cheap and would 'quickly' be down-sized and removed if it wasn't for its benefits" [raw].
  - Partial retreat on the gap: "However, the explanatory gap is narrower when it comes to evaluative experience" ... "The felt aspect of these processes constitutes its function, that is, to matter to the organism. Nevertheless, this does not eliminate the explanatory gap entirely." [raw, two sentences of one paragraph]
  - Falsifier: if life-history complexity runs against his predictions (animals judged highly conscious turn out low in life-history complexity, and vice versa), "this would be a significant blow to the thesis" [raw]. The comparative measurement paper was announced but not found (see Gaps).
- **Tenet alignment**: Aligns with the Map's anti-epiphenomenalism premise. Note that Veit calls epiphenomenalism "the dualist view". That conflates one dualism with dualism as such. Interactionist dualism agrees with Veit against epiphenomenalism, and the article writer can say so.

## Major Positions

### The pathological complexity thesis (Veit)
- **Core claim**: hedonic valence evolved at or near the Cambrian as a common currency for action selection in animals whose new appendages and body plans multiplied their degrees of freedom. Its presence then unlocked further body-plan diversification. Consciousness began as evaluative experience, and other dimensions enriched it later and to varying degrees across lineages.
- **Key arguments**: trade-off computation becomes unmanageable without a common currency (behavioural ecology, McFarland & Sibly 1975; robotics); the Avalon explosion "failed" for lack of one (conjecture); evaluation is the dimension whose adaptive benefit is least mysterious; costly neural architecture would be pruned if it were idle.
- **Relation to site tenets**: The function and timing claims are compatible with the Map's interactionist dualism. The background identity claim is not. The Map can accept that *evaluative experience came first and was selected for its action-selection role* while rejecting the further claim that *it is identical to the valence system's function*.

### Learning-complexity / UAL (Ginsburg & Jablonka)
- **Core claim**: unlimited associative learning marks minimal consciousness and helped drive the Cambrian explosion. Already covered on the host page. The PCT and UAL are rival accounts of what drove the Cambrian explosion, and both are functional.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Already engaged on the host page.

### The nonconscious-valence objection (de Weerd; Fischer & Barrett; Pober)
- **Core claim**: the evidence supports the evolution of an evaluative or valence system, but cannot tell whether that system was conscious, so the PCT dates the valence system and not consciousness.
- **Relation to site tenets**: This is the objection from inside naturalism that the Map's phenomenal-transition thesis predicts, and it is also the published form of the steelman page's "Evolutionary Clincher". The Map takes the epistemic half (the fitness record cannot discriminate) and refuses the metaphysical half that Pober is read as drawing (conscious valence does nothing beyond what nonconscious valence does), because that half is epiphenomenalism. It is also the value-blind horn of the [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) fork, which the Map holds open at low credence.

### Illusionist functionalism (Frankish)
- **Core claim**: drop "feel" talk. Evolutionary explanation works only for functional properties, and anything irreducibly subjective "will slip the net of evolutionary explanation".
- **Relation to site tenets**: opposes Dualism. His dilemma nevertheless isolates exactly where the PCT's narrowing of the hard problem comes from (see next section).

## Key Debates

### Does the PCT narrow the hard problem?
- **Sides**: Veit (2023b, p. 188): yes, "Things feel a certain way because they have to feel that way to be functional." Veit (2024): narrower, but it "does not eliminate the explanatory gap entirely". Frankish (2025): only if valence is not irreducibly subjective; otherwise "the hard problem remains as hard as ever". Veit (2025b) answers by identity: "They are identical."
- **The Map's reading (note's analysis)**: the narrowing comes from the identity thesis, not from the evolutionary evidence. Granted the identity, the felt aspect "constitutes its function" by definition. Without it, the evolutionary story explains why a valence *system* with that function was selected and leaves open why it feels like anything. Frankish and the Map agree on this conditional and disagree only about which horn to take. So the Map can say, in Veit's own terms, that the PCT accounts for the function and the timing of valenced experience and leaves the hard problem where it was.
- **Current state**: ongoing. Veit calls the metaphysics "premature".

### Can evolutionary evidence date *conscious* valence?
- **Sides**: de Weerd, Fischer & Barrett and Pober say no; Veit concedes this "weakens my case" but denies it refutes the thesis unless "all of the functions of hedonic valence could be achieved without it".
- **The Map's reading**: this is the host page's blindness thesis in miniature. The host already says that a conscious causal contribution "shows up there indistinguishably from a purely functional cause producing the same behaviour". The guard is that indistinguishability in the fitness record is an evidential limit, not a showing of idleness. Veit's reply, that the functions belong "directly to phenomenological experiences themselves", fits the Map's interactionism as well as his identity view. The evidence does not choose between them.
- **Current state**: open; the special issues closed in 2025–2026.

### Frankish's challenge to the Map's own evolutionary argument
- Frankish: "Anything irreducibly subjective will slip the net of evolutionary explanation." The sentence cuts against the Map as well as Veit, because the Map holds both that valence is irreducibly subjective and that it is selectable for its effects (see [evolutionary-case-for-mental-causation](/topics/evolutionary-case-for-mental-causation/)).
- **The answer the Map can give (note's analysis, to be stated as the Map's view and not attributed to anyone)**: selection acts on effects, and a property can be selected for its effects without its nature being exhausted by them. An evolutionary explanation then explains why a property with those effects was retained and elaborated, and leaves its intrinsic character unexplained. That is the Map's position anyway: an evolutionary function alongside a hard problem that remains open. What "slips the net" is the intrinsic character, not the causal contribution. If the writer uses the Frankish sentence, this reply must accompany it.

## Adjudicating the reviewer's summary {#adjudicating-the-reviewers-summary}

The Gemini reviewer (2026-11-13 §6) said the Map's evolution pages ignore Veit, and that "Veit (2023) and the modern bio-evaluative consensus demonstrate exactly how physical, biological complexity drives the elaboration of sentience without violating physical closure". Against the raw texts:

Veit concedes the gap is not eliminated (2024), that unconscious valence "weakens my case" (2026), and that the PCT is compatible with dualism (2025b). His Avalon diagnosis is introduced with "I suspect". His comparative life-history test was announced but not reported in anything reached.
- **Not supported**: "modern bio-evaluative consensus". The thesis is actively contested, by de Weerd, Fischer & Barrett, Pober, Suzuki and Frankish, within two special issues on the book.
- **Mixed**: "gradually ... entirely independent of ... sudden ... thresholds". Veit is a gradualist about phenomenological complexity, but he dates the origin to a Cambrian "computational explosion" and calls it "a major transition in evaluative agency". He is not a continuity theorist set against transition events. His account has the same shape as Ginsburg and Jablonka's (a Cambrian transition), with a different driver.

## Connection to Tenets

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction).** The PCT is a developed naturalist account that gives experience a definite job: being the common currency in which competing actions are compared, since evaluation is where "consciousness becomes discharged in action" [raw, Veit 2023b]. That is the kind of role the Map's Bidirectional Interaction tenet needs consciousness to have, and Veit's anti-epiphenomenalist arguments (decision-relevance, costly architecture) back the tenet's negative premise. It is also the naturalist counterpart of the Map's own position **[P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1)** ([value-in-selection](/positions/value-in-selection/)): "felt valence is the currency in which conscious selection among underdetermined outcomes is denominated". The currency idea predates both. It is Cabanac's (1992), and Veit carried it back to the Cambrian. Within the value-blind / value-sensitive fork that [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) records, Veit holds the value-sensitive side in identity form. His critics (Pober, Fischer & Barrett, de Weerd) give the published, evolutionary version of the value-blind horn that [the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection](/topics/the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection/) constructs under "The Evolutionary Clincher". According to Veit (2026), Pober draws on the same Winkielman–Berridge research on unconscious affect that the steelman page cites: the subliminal happy- and angry-face drinking experiments (Winkielman et al. 2005) and Berridge & Winkielman (2003). The steelman page cites Winkielman & Berridge (2004). Two limits apply. First, Veit's causal role is identity-based ("They are identical"), so he supports *mental causation*, not *interaction*. Second, Veit's "action selection" is the behavioural-ecology and robotics notion, a neural process of choosing among motor options. Do not equate it with the Map's selection among quantum outcomes, and do not suggest that Veit's Cambrian common currency is a quantum interface. The Map may say that the PCT specifies what a conscious causal contribution would be *for*, and that the Map supplies a different account of *how* it is made.

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism).** Veit is a physicalist and should be named as one. The Map's engagement is conditional. Veit himself grants that the PCT is compatible with dualism. Its claim to narrow the hard problem depends on the identity thesis, not on the evolutionary evidence. Without the identity, the thesis explains when and why a valence system with that function evolved, and "the hard problem remains as hard as ever" (Frankish's words, from an illusionist). The naturalist critics' point, that the evidence dates valence "simpliciter—not conscious valence", is the evidential gap that Dualism predicts. **Never concede epiphenomenalism**: the Map accepts that the fitness record cannot separate conscious from nonconscious valence, and holds that conscious valence is nonetheless efficacious.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits).** Veit's preference for identity over dualism is a parsimony choice backed by a promissory claim: "The purported difficulties for naturalist materialism or physicalism are, in fact, being steadily resolved". The evolutionary evidence does not deliver that choice. As Fischer & Barrett show, the same record fits a nonconscious valence system, so the data underdetermine the identity. Tenet 5 says parsimony cannot settle this, in either direction. The Map should not claim the evolutionary record favours dualism any more than Veit can claim it favours identity.

**No Many Worlds; Minimal Quantum Interaction.** No direct bearing. Do not force a connection.
