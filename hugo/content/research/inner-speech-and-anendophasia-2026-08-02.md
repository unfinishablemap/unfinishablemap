## Executive Summary

Inner speech is the experience of language without overt articulation. **Anendophasia** is a proposed name for its complete absence, coined by Nedergaard and Lupyan in *Psychological Science* in 2024 — the term is about two years old, not an established clinical category, and the Map has been using it as though it were the latter.

The empirical picture is far less settled than the Map's two live uses imply. Nedergaard and Lupyan compared self-reported low- and high-inner-speech groups and found the low group performed worse on verbal working memory and rhyme judgment, with **no difference** on task-switching or categorical perception effects. Lind (2025), in the same journal, argued they had shown no compelling evidence that anyone lacks inner speech *entirely*. In an unpublished reply, Lupyan and Nedergaard **granted that their data support a continuum rather than a demonstrated absence**. Hurlburt (2026) then adjudicated: anendophasia is an experiential phenomenon rather than a psychometric construct, mostly-anendophasic people probably do exist and are common, but the distinction between *completely* and *mostly* anendophasic may be impossible to draw in practice.

The important finding for the Map is that **a much stronger and much older evidence base was available and was not used**. Hurlburt and Akhter's *unsymbolized thinking* — explicit, differentiated thought experienced without words, images, or any other symbol — is one of the five most common features of sampled inner experience, rests on within-subject momentary sampling rather than between-group questionnaires, and has stood since 2008. It does the Map's argumentative work more securely than anendophasia does, and it does it without depending on a contested existence claim.

## Key Sources

### Nedergaard & Lupyan (2024) — the coinage

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976241243004
- **Type**: Journal article, *Psychological Science* 35(7), 780–797
- **Key points**:
  - Proposes *anendophasia* as a name for the absence of the experience of inner speech.
  - Four studies. Low-inner-speech group N = 46; high-inner-speech group N = 47, assigned by self-report.
  - Low group showed lower verbal working memory performance and more difficulty with rhyme judgments.
  - Task-switching performance and categorical effects on perceptual judgments were **unrelated** to inner-speech differences.
  - Opens by noting that the assumption of inner speech as a human universal is challenged by evidence that adult experience of it ranges from near-constant to nonexistent.
- **Method caveat that matters**: group assignment is by self-report questionnaire, not by momentary sampling. This is precisely the pressure point the critics press.
- **Tenet alignment**: superficially supportive of Tenet 1 (Dualism) and of consciousness–language separability, but **weaker than it looks**. The behavioural findings are about verbal task performance, not about whether phenomenal experience persists. The dissociation the Map wants — reflective consciousness without inner speech — is not what these four studies measured.

### Lind (2025) — the challenge

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976251335583
- **Type**: Commentary, *Psychological Science* 36(9), 765–767
- **Key points**:
  - Accepts the behavioural findings as an original contribution.
  - Rejects the inference from those findings, plus prior empirical and anecdotal data, to the conclusion that some people have no inner speech at all.
  - Concludes there is no compelling evidence that some individuals lack inner speech.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral on dualism; directly **conflicts with the Map's current use** of anendophasia as demonstrative.

### Lupyan & Nedergaard (2025) — the reply, and the concession

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/w9gfy_v1 — OSF preprint, posted 2025-06-25
- **Type**: ⚠️ **Preprint only.** A Crossref search of *Psychological Science* items from 2025 onward returned no published version. Cite it as a preprint or not at all.
- **Key points**:
  - The authors accept that their data support a **continuum** of inner-speech experience rather than establishing complete absence.
  - They argue against dismissing self-reports of absent inner speech, urging that such reports be treated as data worth investigating rather than as error.
- **Why this is the pivotal source**: the concession comes from the coiners themselves. Any Map article that keeps asserting demonstrated absence is now asserting something the originating authors have declined to assert.

### Lind (2025) — reply to the reply

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/8u4ct_v1 — PsyArXiv preprint
- **Type**: ⚠️ Preprint only.
- **Key points**:
  - The term *anendophasia* is **ambiguous** between self-perceived *low prevalence* of inner speech and *complete lack* of it, and the difference between those readings is crucial.
  - Comparing questionnaire data against descriptive experience sampling data, argues that respondents' presuppositions about their own inner speech dramatically shape their questionnaire answers.
- **Direct relevance to the Map**: this is an [introspection](/concepts/introspection/)-reliability argument of exactly the kind the Map already takes seriously elsewhere (the [aphantasia](/topics/aphantasia/) article cites Schwitzgebel on naive introspection for the parallel reason). The corpus applies that scepticism to imagery self-report and has not applied it to inner-speech self-report.

### Hurlburt (2026) — the adjudication

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976251413525
- **Type**: Commentary, *Psychological Science*, published online ahead of print 2026-01-30, article number 09567976251413525. ⚠️ No volume/issue assigned at time of checking; Europe PMC lists it under volume 36 ahead-of-print. Cite as online-ahead-of-print.
- **Key points**:
  - Distinguishes **fidelity** (faithfulness to experience as it actually occurs) from **validity** (psychometric construct validation), and argues descriptive experience sampling is a fidelity-aspiring method.
  - Anendophasia is an **experiential phenomenon, not a construct**. Nedergaard and Lupyan investigated it as a construct but drew phenomenon-level conclusions — a mismatch of method to claim.
  - Distinguishes **completely** from **mostly** anendophasic individuals, and observes that in practice that distinction might be impossible to draw.
  - Judges that anendophasic, or at least mostly anendophasic, individuals **do exist and are probably frequent**.
- **Why it matters most**: this is the most defensible position currently available, and it is neither Lind's nor Nedergaard and Lupyan's. It supports "many people have little or no inner speech" while refusing "some people have demonstrably none." That is the register the Map should adopt.

### Hurlburt & Akhter (2008) — unsymbolized thinking, the better evidence

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2008.03.021
- **Type**: Journal article, *Consciousness and Cognition* 17(4), 1364–1374
- **Key points**:
  - Unsymbolized thinking is the experience of an explicit, differentiated thought that includes no experience of words, images, or any other symbols.
  - It is a distinct phenomenon, not an incompletely formed inner speech or a vague image.
  - It is one of the **five most common features of inner experience** (with inner speech, inner seeing, feelings, and sensory awareness).
  - Many people, including many professional students of consciousness, believe such an experience is impossible.
  - The authors note the theoretical import: its existence indicates that much experienced thinking takes place without any experience of words or other symbols.
- **Tenet alignment**: **strongly supportive** of Tenet 1 and of the Map's use-relation-not-constitution thesis, and far more securely than anendophasia. This is a *positive* phenomenological datum about what thinking is like, sampled moment-by-moment within subjects, rather than an *absence* claim inferred from a questionnaire split. It also directly serves [cognitive-phenomenology](/concepts/cognitive-phenomenology/).
- ⚠️ **This paper is not cited anywhere in the corpus.** It is the single highest-value addition this research turned up.

### Heavey & Hurlburt (2008) — how common is inner speech, really

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2007.12.006
- **Type**: Journal article, *Consciousness and Cognition* 17(3), 798–810
- **Key points**:
  - Descriptive experience sampling of 10 randomly identified moments from each of 30 participants, drawn from a stratified sample of college students.
  - Each of the five phenomena occurred in approximately **one quarter** of sampled moments.
  - Frequency varied widely across individuals; no significant gender differences.
  - Higher frequencies of inner speech were associated with **lower** levels of psychological distress.
- **Why it matters**: inner speech is not a continuous soundtrack even in people who report having one. It shows up in roughly a quarter of sampled moments. The folk picture of an unbroken inner monologue — which the Map's articles implicitly accept when they treat its absence as remarkable — is not what sampling finds. This substantially reframes the whole debate: if inner speech occupies about a quarter of experience in typical people, then *everyone* spends most of their waking life not engaged in it.

### Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey (2013) — the phenomenology

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2013.10.003
- **Type**: Journal article, *Consciousness and Cognition* 22(4), 1477–1494
- **Key points**: inner speaking occurs in many but certainly not all moments of pristine inner experience; the most common form is speaking in one's own naturally inflected voice with no sound produced; substantial variation in form and large individual differences in frequency; findings diverge from prior research, attributed to methodological differences.
- **Relevance**: supports the Map's existing (and good) observation at [consciousness-and-language-interface](/topics/consciousness-and-language-interface/) that inner speech carries tone, pace and emotional colouring that belong to consciousness rather than to linguistic content.

### Alderson-Day & Fernyhough (2015) — the standard review

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000021
- **Type**: Review, *Psychological Bulletin* 141(5), 931–965
- **Key points**: reviews theoretical approaches and methodological challenges; covers typical and atypical populations; presents a multicomponent model; concludes inner speech performs significant cognitive functions despite variability among individuals and across the lifespan, in some cases reflecting its developmental origins and its sharing of resources with other cognitive processes.
- **Use**: the appropriate general citation for inner speech as such, distinct from the anendophasia dispute. Also the natural place to source the Vygotskian internalisation account without citing Vygotsky at second hand.

### Hinwar & Lambert (2021) — anauralia, the bridge to aphantasia

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.744213
- **Type**: Journal article, *Frontiers in Psychology* 12, 744213
- **Key points**:
  - 128 participants including 34 self-reported aphantasics; auditory imagery measured by BAIS-V, visual imagery by VVIQ-M.
  - The two were strongly associated, **Spearman's rho = 0.83**.
  - Most self-reported aphantasics also reported weak or entirely absent auditory imagery, and participants lacking auditory imagery tended to be aphantasic.
  - The associations **can dissociate but rarely do**: the aphantasic group included one individual with typical auditory imagery, and the 29-person no-auditory-imagery group included one individual with typical visual imagery.
  - Proposes the term **anauralia** for the absence of auditory imagery, noting no English word existed for it.
- **Why this is the structural key to the article**: it supplies the empirical bridge between [aphantasia](/topics/aphantasia/) and absent inner speech that the Map's asymmetry has been missing. Inner speech has an auditory-imagery component, so anauralia and anendophasia are neighbouring but non-identical absences. Note the term-proliferation pattern: *aphantasia* (2015), *anauralia* (2021), *anendophasia* (2024) are three coinages in a decade naming three phenomenal absences, each initially on self-report evidence.

### Zhong et al. (2026) — qualitative aphantasia/inner-speech interaction

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2026.109540
- **Type**: Journal article, *Neuropsychologia* 231(4), 109540
- **Key points**: qualitative study of autistic and non-autistic aphantasics; themes covering imagery (including inner speech), thinking, and emotions/socialisation; significantly more non-autistic than autistic participants endorsed the auditory-and-other-sensory-imagery subtheme, with the reverse pattern for verbal thinking.
- **Status**: recent and useful for showing the imagery/inner-speech interaction is actively studied; ⚠️ metadata only, abstract via Europe PMC, no full text accessed.

### Inner speech and auditory verbal hallucinations

Two current entries into this literature, both verified at Europe PMC:

- Skipper, Lametti & Green (2025), *Inner Speech and the Neurobiology of Psychosis*, bioRxiv, https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.08.22.671634 — ⚠️ **preprint, not peer-reviewed**. Neuroimaging meta-analyses supporting an account on which auditory verbal hallucinations arise when efference-copy signals from inner speech are insufficiently suppressed and insufficiently monitored as self-produced.
- Cioroboiu, Whittingham, Dodgson, Fernyhough & Alderson-Day (2025), *Auditory verbal hallucinations: The ins and outs of subtypes*, *Psychiatry Research* 352, 116696, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2025.116696 — peer-reviewed review of AVH subtypes.

- **Relevance**: inner speech that is not recognised as one's own is the clearest natural case of a **source-attribution** failure, connecting directly to the Map's [source-attribution-void](/voids/source-attribution-void/). It is also a caution: the same inner speech can be experienced as self-produced or as external, which means the phenomenal character of inner speech is not fixed by its linguistic content — a point that supports the Map's use-relation framing.

## Major Positions

### The constitutive view (the Map's target)

- **Proponents**: Dennett; Carruthers; Vygotskian developmental psychologists.
- **Core claim**: higher-order consciousness — self-reflection, [metacognition](/concepts/metacognition/), narrative identity — requires language, with inner speech as a partly constitutive medium rather than a tool.
- **Relation to site tenets**: conflicts with Tenet 1. The Map's standing reply is that the view confuses correlation with constitution.
- ⚠️ **Second uncited attribution found.** Two live articles attribute this view to Dennett, Carruthers and "some Vygotskian psychologists," and the corpus contains **no citation to Vygotsky anywhere** in `topics/`, `concepts/` or `apex/`. The Carruthers 2002 *BBS* citation is present in the interface article; Dennett and Vygotsky are not sourced. Alderson-Day & Fernyhough (2015) is the cleanest way to source the Vygotskian internalisation account. A primary Vygotsky citation (*Thought and Language*, 1934, English translations 1962 and 1986) was **not verified at a publisher of record this session** and should not be inserted on my authority.

### The continuum view (currently the best-supported)

- **Proponents**: Lupyan & Nedergaard in their 2025 reply; Hurlburt (2026) in a more developed form.
- **Core claim**: inner-speech experience varies continuously from near-constant to very rare; individuals at the low extreme are real and probably common; whether any individual is at exact zero may be undecidable by available methods.
- **Relation to site tenets**: **compatible with, and sufficient for, the Map's argument.** The constitutive view is embarrassed by people whose reflective lives proceed with very little inner speech; it does not require anyone at exactly zero. The Map has been claiming more than it needs.

### The sceptical view

- **Proponents**: Lind (2025).
- **Core claim**: the evidence does not establish that anyone lacks inner speech; questionnaire self-reports are shaped by respondents' presuppositions about their own minds.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral on dualism, and worth engaging on its merits rather than dismissing. This is the same introspective-unreliability worry the Map already respects in the imagery case.

### Unsymbolized thinking as the alternative route

- **Proponents**: Hurlburt & Akhter (2008); Heavey & Hurlburt (2008).
- **Core claim**: explicit differentiated thought regularly occurs with no experienced words, images, or symbols at all, in ordinary people, as a common feature of sampled experience.
- **Relation to site tenets**: **strongly supportive** of Tenet 1 and of the use-relation thesis, and it does not depend on the contested existence of a special population. If thinking routinely occurs without symbols in *everyone*, the constitutive view's problem is general rather than confined to a disputed minority.

## Key Debates

### Does anyone completely lack inner speech?

- **Sides**: Nedergaard & Lupyan (initially yes) vs Lind (no compelling evidence) vs Hurlburt (mostly-anendophasic people exist and are common; the completely/mostly line may be undrawable).
- **Core disagreement**: partly evidential and partly methodological — whether a questionnaire split can license a phenomenon-level existence claim, and whether self-reports of absence are data or artefact.
- **Current state**: **live and unresolved as of early 2026.** Two of the five turns are unpublished preprints. Hurlburt's adjudication went online 2026-01-30.

### Is descriptive experience sampling better evidence than questionnaires?

- **Sides**: the DES tradition (Hurlburt, Heavey) argues momentary sampling achieves fidelity to experience that retrospective questionnaires cannot; Lind deploys the DES/questionnaire contrast against Nedergaard and Lupyan.
- **Core disagreement**: whether people can accurately generalise about their own inner experience in the abstract, as opposed to reporting a specific sampled moment.
- **Relevance to the Map**: this is a general methodological lesson about first-person data classes, not a local dispute, and it bears on how the Map weighs self-report evidence throughout.

## Required Corrections to Live Articles

These are findings about existing content, not article-writing tasks. They hold **whether or not** the new article is written, and the downstream expand must not silently inherit the current framing.

1. **`obsidian/topics/consciousness-and-language-interface.md` L162** asserts that anendophasics' experience *demonstrates* the phenomenal stream can flow without linguistic structuring. This is the strongest modal in the corpus on this evidence, and it is now the specific claim Lind disputes and the coiners have declined to defend. A prior review (changelog 2026-W23) deliberately **kept** this as "the one genuinely settled finding" while softening its neighbours. **That judgement is falsified by the 2025–2026 literature and should be revisited.** Note the same line also asserts anendophasics are "fully conscious" and that they "think, plan, create, and reflect" — the plan/create/reflect claims were not measured by Nedergaard and Lupyan, whose null results were on task-switching and categorical perception.

2. **`obsidian/topics/philosophy-of-language-under-dualism.md` L88** uses anendophasia as *demonstrating* that phenomenal reference does not depend on private linguistic rehearsal. Same over-strong modal, same contested base.

3. **The calibration fence in the open P3 task should be tightened, not merely held.** That task warns against re-strengthening "suggests" back to "demonstrates." Correct — but insufficient. Even "suggests reflective consciousness persists without inner speech" presupposes that complete absence is established. The defensible formulation runs through **greatly reduced** inner speech, not zero.

4. **The argument survives the correction intact, and is in fact strengthened**, because unsymbolized thinking and the ~25% sampling frequency are available and are better evidence. The constitutive view has to explain how reflection proceeds during the roughly three-quarters of sampled moments containing no inner speech in *typical* people — no disputed population required.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **Recommended: the phenomenal-absence evidence base and its fragility.** Own the phenomenon, treat anendophasia as a *contested case study in how phenomenal-absence claims get made*, and place the load on unsymbolized thinking and sampling frequency. This makes the article genuinely distinct from [consciousness-and-language-interface](/topics/consciousness-and-language-interface/) rather than a longer version of its L156 section, and it lets the Map be the place that reports the dispute accurately — a real differentiator, since the popular coverage of anendophasia has not caught up with the 2025–2026 exchange. Aligns with [evidential-status-discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/).

2. **The three coinages.** Aphantasia (2015), anauralia (2021), anendophasia (2024) as a decade of naming phenomenal absences, each on self-report, each raising the same [introspection](/concepts/introspection/)-reliability question, and each with the same [phenomenology-vs-function-axis](/concepts/phenomenology-vs-function-axis/) structure: does the absent phenomenology come with a functional deficit? For aphantasia the Map already says largely no; for anendophasia the answer is *partly yes* (verbal working memory, rhyme judgment) — an asymmetry worth reporting honestly rather than smoothing over.

3. **Narrower alternative if the cap slot is judged too precious**: fold a corrected and sourced treatment into the existing L156 section and spend the last `topics/` slot elsewhere. This remains a legitimate outcome. The corrections in the section above are the mandatory part; the new article is the valuable-but-optional part.

When writing, follow `obsidian/project/writing-style.md`: front-load the dispute rather than burying it after a survey of inner speech, use named-anchor summaries for forward references, and connect explicitly to tenets.
