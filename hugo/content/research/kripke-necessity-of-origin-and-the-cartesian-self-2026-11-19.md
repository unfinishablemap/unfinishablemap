## Executive Summary

In the last footnote of *Naming and Necessity* (n. 77, p. 155), Kripke suggests that his own origin essentialism tells against the Cartesian picture. If the self were a clearly conceived, independent spiritual substance, it would have no reason to be tied to one particular sperm and egg. Yet it is "hard to imagine me coming from a sperm and egg different from my actual origins", which "seems to me to indicate that we have no such clear conception of a soul or self". Kripke's wording is tentative ("I would tend to argue the other way"), and his target is narrow: the *Cartesian* picture, and in particular the clarity of the self-conception that Descartes's separability argument needs. The argument also rests on a contested premise. Kripke's own 1980 preface says the footnote-56 argument for the necessity of origin "now seems to me to have problems", the best later defences are stated for *material* things and their *material* origins, and Robertson (1998) and Mackie (2006) find the case for the thesis wanting. A dualist who holds subjects to be primitive has three replies. (A) Deny that subjects have their origins essentially, as Swinburne's "thisness" does, at the price of bare cross-world identities (Forbes's objection) and of explaining away Kripke's datum. (B) Accept origin essentialism for a subject that comes from one organism, at the price of conceding Kripke's real target and of importing origin vagueness into subject identity. (C) Turn the argument round with Madell: bodies tolerate vague origins, subjects seemingly do not, so the subject is not the body. **The Map's analysis:** the footnote leaves Tenet 1 (irreducibility) untouched and does not reach Tenet 4's branch argument, since both successors share one origin. It does press the Map's [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/) page, which currently endorses reply A ("What if I had been you?") while individuating subjects "by its causal history", which is reply B's materials. **Verdict: fold** into [kripke-a-posteriori-necessity-argument](/concepts/kripke-a-posteriori-necessity-argument/), with a zero-word pipe from [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/).

### Kripke, *Naming and Necessity*, Lecture III opening of the mind-body discussion (pp. 144–155 span)
- **Read via**: the same Colorado excerpt.
- **Key point**: Kripke says the materialist cannot accept Descartes's premise and deny his conclusion: "a philosopher who wishes to refute the Cartesian conclusion must refute the Cartesian premise, and the latter task is not trivial." (str.count = 1; page not pinned, since the excerpt is unpaginated. Do not give a page number.)
- **Why it matters (Map's analysis)**: n. 77 reads as Kripke's own hint at how the Cartesian *premise* might be undercut. The origin intuition is offered as evidence that our conception of the self is not clear enough to license "I could exist without this body". So the footnote attacks the epistemic step of Descartes's argument. It does not offer a positive proof that the self is material, and Kripke does not claim one.

### Atkins, Correia & Robertson Ishii, "Essential vs. Accidental Properties", SEP (substantive revision 2026-08-21), main entry §1.4 and supplement

- **Reply**: Cameron, R. & Roca, S. (2006), "Rohrbaugh and deRosset on the Necessity of Origin", *Mind* 115(458): 361–366, doi:10.1093/mind/fzl361. Abstract (OpenAlex): "we object that their argument is question-begging".

## Major Positions

### Origin essentialism (Kripke; defended by Salmon, Forbes, McGinn; Rohrbaugh & deRosset)
- **Core claim**: an organism or artefact could not have had a radically different origin. For persons, that means not a "totally different sperm and egg".
- **Key arguments**: intuition (the Queen); sufficiency/expansion arguments (Kripke n. 56, Salmon, Forbes); the branching conception (McGinn, the two Mackies); production and locality of prevention (Rohrbaugh & deRosset 2004; deRosset 2009).
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral as stated, since every defence is about material things. It bears on the Map only if extended to the subject.

### Kripke's anti-Cartesian extension (n. 77)
- **Core claim**: the origin intuition about *me* is evidence that we lack a clear conception of the self as an independent spiritual substance.
- **Status**: tentative, a footnote, offered against Descartes's premise, and paired in the same footnote with Kripke's anti-materialism and his "wide open" verdict.
- **Relation to site tenets**: no conflict with Tenet 1. It conflicts with a Cartesian-substance reading of the subject and with haecceitist role-swaps.

### Haecceitist dualism (Swinburne; Map's [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/) page; cf. Mackie's non-dualist bare identities)
- **Core claim**: a subject's identity across possibilities is a primitive "thisness", independent of its mental properties and bodily connections.
- **Relation to site tenets**: close to the Map's stated view (haecceity L91, L157). The cost is Forbes's grounding objection.

### The Madell–Robinson inversion
- **Core claim**: origin counterfactuals admit degree for bodies but apparently not for subjects, so the subject is simple and not the body.
- **Relation to site tenets**: supportive of Tenet 1 at the substance level. It depends on the determinacy intuition that Parfitians reject.

### Kripke on the person and the body (Lecture III; "The First Person" 2011)
- **Core claim**: a person is not identical to his body (the corpse case), but that non-identity is "not particularly deep" and compatible with broadly materialist views.
- **Relation to site tenets**: a calibration point. Kripke supplies no positive dualism at any level.

## Key Debates

### Does n. 77 refute the Cartesian self?
- **Sides**: Kripke (tentatively yes, as an undercutting consideration); Burgess (reports Kripke as "denies"); McGinn (endorses the anti-Cartesian reading, extends it against solipsism); haecceitist dualists such as Swinburne (implicitly no, since thisness is independent of bodily connections).
- **Core disagreement**: whether the intuition that "I could not have come from other gametes" concerns the *subject* or the *organism* the name "I" also picks out.
- **Current state**: no published dualist reply to n. 77 *by name* was found (searched for explicitly; an absence in what was retrieved, not proof of none).

### Is the necessity of origin true?
- **Sides**: Kripke, Salmon, Forbes, McGinn, Rohrbaugh & deRosset (yes, in tolerant forms) against Robertson 1998, Mackie 2006, Kahn 2025 (no good argument / false for gametes).
- **Core disagreement**: sufficiency principles versus recycling and generality; tolerance versus S4/S5 transitivity.
- **Current state**: open. Kripke himself in 1980 judged his own n. 56 argument to "have problems".

### Can a non-physical subject have its origin essentially?
- **Sides**: no defence read here addresses it. Kripke's question ("why should it have to have any necessary connection...?") presumes not, for a Cartesian soul. Emergent and causal-history views (Hasker as a lead; the Map's process haecceitism) supply candidate grounds.
- **Current state**: unaddressed in the literature reached. **This is the note's main gap and the fold's original contribution, labelled as the Map's.**

## Potential Article Angles

A standalone article is not warranted: topics and concepts are both at 370/370 (re-measured 20:05Z with `count_section_files`), and the material is a natural continuation of the Kripke page's "What Kripke Does Not Conclude" subsection. The fold angle:

1. **The argument, stated at Kripke's strength.** Reconstruct n. 77: (P1) it is hard to imagine me from different gametes; (P2) a clearly conceived, independent spiritual self would have no necessary tie to particular gametes; (C) so we lack a clear conception of such a self. The target is the clarity premise of Descartes's argument, which Kripke had said a refuter of Descartes must attack.
2. **What it rests on.** P1 is an intuition, and Kripke's one argument for origin necessity is one he flagged in 1980 as having "problems". The defences are framed for material things, and the thesis is contested.
3. **Three replies and their costs** (the Map's analysis, set out below).
4. **Where the Map stands.** The footnote leaves Tenet 1 alone and does not reach Tenet 4's branch argument. It obliges the Map to say whether its haecceity is origin-bound.

### The Map's analysis: three replies and what each costs

*(Everything in this subsection is the Map's analysis, not a reported position, unless a source is named.)*

**A. Deny origin essentialism for subjects (haecceitist route).** The subject's identity across possibilities is primitive, so I could have come from other gametes, and Kripke's datum tracks the organism that "I" is embodied as. Swinburne's thisness and film argument state this position. Mackie shows that bare identities are defensible for ordinary individuals on general grounds. *Costs*: (i) Forbes's objection that ungrounded trans-world identities are unintelligible. The Map already accepts a version of this cost on [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/) (the "Explanatory emptiness" and "Modal concerns" objections). (ii) The datum has to be explained away, and Kripke anticipates the charge of question-begging and reverses it ("I would tend to argue the other way"). The result is a standoff of intuitions, not a win. (iii) Chisholm-style role-swap worlds must be admitted. *Fit*: matches haecceity L157 ("What if I had been you?" seems meaningful).

**B. Accept origin essentialism for subjects via the subject's origin.** A non-physical subject that comes into being with, or from, one organism can have that origin essentially. This answers Kripke's "why should it have to have any necessary connection...?" by pointing to the subject's coming-to-be. Emergent dualism is the obvious home (Hasker, a lead only). So is the Map's own process haecceitism, if "individuated by its causal history" is read across worlds. Production-based necessity (Rohrbaugh & deRosset; deRosset 2009) would have to be *extended* to non-material products, an extension nobody read here makes. *Costs*: (i) it concedes Kripke's actual conclusion, the rejection of the *independent, subsistent* Cartesian soul. That costs the Map little, since its subject is "substance-leaning, not necessarily full Cartesian substance" ([where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) L44). (ii) The subject's cross-world identity is then partly fixed by physical facts about gametes, which sits awkwardly with the Dualism tenet's claim that physical facts do not fix which subject exists (haecceity L187). (iii) Origins are tolerant and vague (SEP's tolerance problem), so subject identity would inherit the vagueness, and that is the result Madell's argument says makes no sense for subjects. To avoid it, B must add a sharp, primitive fact about which near-origin variants would have been *me*, at which point it has become "origin necessary, haecceity sufficient", a hybrid that pays A's cost as well.

**C. Turn the argument round (Madell; SEP §4.3).** Kripke treats the origin intuition as evidence against a clear self-conception. Madell treats the *difference* between bodily and subjective origin counterfactuals as evidence that the subject is simple. Bodies admit degree; "would I have existed?" seems to demand yes or no. *Costs*: (i) it rests on the determinacy intuition, which Parfitians deny (SEP's answer (ii), "no fact of the matter"). (ii) It proves at most simplicity, and the step from simplicity to immateriality is SEP's further inference. (iii) It sits better with A than with B, because a sharp answer for subjects needs something besides vague origins to make it sharp.

**Bearing on the tenets.**
- *Tenet 1 (Dualism)*: unaffected. The p. 155 text to which n. 77 is attached says Kripke suspects "the present considerations tell heavily against the usual forms of materialism", and the footnote's own first half concedes only that materialists have arguments he cannot yet answer. n. 77 addresses the *substance* picture, while Tenet 1's claim is irreducibility.
- *Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds; indexical identity matters)*: not directly reached. Origin essentialism is a cross-world thesis, and the branch question is intra-world and diachronic. Both post-branch successors share one origin, so origin individuation cannot pick either, which is the same symmetry [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/) L171 records for causal-history individuation. The persisting-subject posit ([P-SC3](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc3)) carries Tenet 4 either way. The indirect pressure is on the *haecceity* the tenet presupposes: if subjects' identities were grounded in origin (B, or Forbes's grounding constraint), the "further fact" would be partly physical. That weakens the haecceitist premise's dualist flavour without removing the fact.
- *Tenet 5 (Occam's limits)*: the origin intuition is the kind of parsimony-friendly datum, "identity grounded in qualitative or physical facts", that Tenet 5 tells the Map not to treat as decisive. That is a reason for caution, not a refutation of Kripke.

**The Map's internal decision the fold should expose (not settle).** [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/) currently holds both (a) that the "What if I had been you?" question is meaningful (L157, reply A) and (b) that "an experiential process is individuated by its causal history" (L137 item 1; L123), which, read across worlds, is reply B. The page's own hedge at L167 ("Causal history provides a frame for distinctness without reducing the distinctness to the frame") reads (b) as intra-world. If that reading is intended, the Map is on route A, and the fold should say so and state A's costs.

When writing, follow `obsidian/project/writing-style.md`: front-load the verdict, keep Kripke's tentativeness, and keep the Map's analysis labelled as the Map's.
