# Research: Voids - The Taboo Void

**Date**: 2026-09-14
**Search queries used**: "Tetlock Thinking the unthinkable sacred values taboo cognitions"; "Tetlock Kristel Elson Green Lerner 2000 psychology of the unthinkable"; "Baron Spranca 1997 protected values quantity insensitivity"; "Ginges Atran Medin Shikaki 2007 sacred bounds backfire"; "Berns 2012 price of your soul sacred values fMRI"; "Frankfurt Rationality and the Unthinkable volitional necessity Williams Moral Incapacity"; "sacred values taboo trade-offs neuroscience 2023-2025"; "Raz constitutive incommensurability friendship money"; "large language models sacred values taboo trade-offs"; "Outraged AI large language models fairness enforcement"; "Duc Hanselmann Boesiger Tanner 2013 trade-off type matters"
**Voids category**: Occluded (self-maintained *and* socially enforced), with an Unexplorable residue

## Executive Summary

Investigated the class of thoughts a mind can *form* but refuses to *weigh*: taboo trade-offs between sacred and secular values, forbidden base rates, and heretical counterfactuals. Three decades of experimental work (Tetlock; Baron & Spranca; Ginges & Atran; Hanselmann & Tanner) establish a distinctive signature that separates this territory from merely unpleasant or difficult thought: taboo trade-offs are rated *easier* than routine ones, not harder; outrage targets the *proposer* rather than the proposal; and the mere passage of the thought through consciousness — even when rejected — produces a felt contamination that the subject discharges through "moral cleansing". Neuroimaging finds the same non-compensatory pattern (rule-retrieval regions engaged, cost-calculation regions *disengaged*). Philosophically, Frankfurt, Williams and Raz argue that the incapacity is constitutive rather than defective: refusing the comparison is part of what it is to hold the value at all. That yields the void's peculiar structure — a dark region whose illumination would change the mind doing the illuminating. Tetlock's own reframing data show the *content* is thinkable under redescription, so what is occluded is not a proposition but a comparison operation under a specific description. The void is framework-independent: physicalists and dualists alike must acknowledge it, which makes it useful for the catalogue's circularity audit.

## Key Sources

### Tetlock (2003), "Thinking the unthinkable: sacred values and taboo cognitions"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/s1364-6613(03)00135-9 (open copy: https://gwern.net/doc/philosophy/ethics/2003-tetlock.pdf)
- **Type**: Review paper, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 7(7): 320–324
- **Key points**:
  - Definition (verbatim, verified against the PDF): sacred values are "those values that a moral community treats as possessing transcendental significance that precludes comparisons, trade-offs, or indeed any mingling with secular values."
  - Taxonomy of trade-offs: *routine* (secular vs secular — "the sort of mental operation one performs every time one strolls into a supermarket"), *tragic* (sacred vs sacred — "honor versus life, the stuff of classical Greek tragedies"), *taboo* (sacred vs secular).
  - The Sacred Value Protection Model (SVPM) predicts **moral outrage** toward proposers, **moral cleansing** after exposure, and — its distinctive claim — a **mere contemplation effect**: "it is not necessary to commit a counter-normative act: it is sufficient for counter-normative thoughts to flicker briefly through consciousness before rejecting them. That pre-rejection interval, during which one's natural first reaction to propositions is to consent [Gilbert 1991], can produce a subjective sense – however unjustified – that one has been contaminated."
  - Duration of deliberation is itself sanctioned: "The longer observers believe that a decision-maker has contemplated an indecent proposal, the harsher their assessments of that person's character, even if that person ultimately makes the 'right' choice and affirms the sacred value."
  - The mere-contemplation effect "takes opposite functional forms for taboo and tragic trade-offs": a hospital administrator choosing between two boys' lives is *praised* for agonising; one choosing between a boy's life and $1 million is condemned for taking any time at all.
  - Fragility: "Rhetorical framings of choices as taboo or tragic can shift almost as rapidly as Gestalt figure–ground relations." A 72% majority accepted diverting $100 million from a zero-risk toxic-waste cleanup when the surplus was earmarked for "saving lives in other ways". Tetlock's own hedge: "the policy a community proclaims towards a sacred value represents an expressed, not a revealed, preference"; some sacred values may be "merely pseudo-sacred".
  - Gaze-aversion: "without pressure to confront secular–sacred contradictions, people will be motivated to look away and be easily distracted by rhetorical smokescreens."
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — both simple models (infinite weight; ordinary preference in disguise) fail. See below.
- **Quote**: "They treat the mere thought of trading off sacred values against secular ones (such as money) as transparently outrageous – in effect, taboo."

### Tetlock, Kristel, Elson, Green & Lerner (2000), "The psychology of the unthinkable"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1037//0022-3514.78.5.853
- **Type**: Empirical paper (five experiments), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 78(5): 853–870
- **Key points** (from the abstract, via Europe PMC):
  - Experiments 1–2: taboo trade-offs that monetised sacred values elicited moral outrage and cleansing.
  - Experiments 3–4 ("forbidden base rates"): "racial egalitarians were least likely to use, and angriest at those who did use, race-tainted base rates and … egalitarians who inadvertently used such base rates tried to reaffirm their fair-mindedness."
  - Experiment 5 ("heretical counterfactuals"): Christian fundamentalists "were most likely to reject heretical counterfactuals that applied everyday causal schemata to Biblical narratives and to engage in moral cleansing after merely contemplating such possibilities."
  - The authors' own limit: the SVPM "must draw on cross-cultural taxonomies of relational schemata to specify normative boundaries on thought" — the model predicts *that* boundaries exist, not *where*.
- **Tenet alignment**: Three distinct cognitive operations (valuation, statistical inference, counterfactual reasoning) show the same occlusion signature, suggesting the block is at the level of the *relation* between a proposition and an identity, not of a content type.
- **Quote**: "Five studies explored cognitive, affective, and behavioral responses to proscribed forms of social cognition."

### Baron & Spranca (1997), "Protected Values"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1006/obhd.1997.2690
- **Type**: Empirical paper (five studies), *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 70(1): 1–16
- **Key points**:
  - Protected values "resist trade-offs with other values, particularly economic values" and "arise from deontological rules concerning action. People are concerned about their participation in transactions rather than just with the consequences that result."
  - Five correlated properties: trade-off resistance, **quantity insensitivity**, agent relativity, moral obligation, and "anger at the thought of making trade-offs, and … denial of the need for trade-offs through wishful thinking."
  - Robustness: "These correlations were found even when the subjects could not tell the experimenters which values they were responding to, so they cannot be ascribed entirely to subjects' desire to express commitment."
- **Tenet alignment**: Quantity insensitivity is the behavioural fingerprint of a comparison that is not being performed — the void's operational marker.
- **Quote**: "protected values, defined as those that display trade-off resistance, will also tend to display quantity insensitivity, agent relativity, and moral obligation."

### Hanselmann & Tanner (2008), "Taboos and conflicts in decision making"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/s1930297500000164 (open: https://sjdm.org/~baron/journal/bb5/bb5.html)
- **Type**: Empirical paper (two experiments, N = 84 and 130), *Judgment and Decision Making* 3(1): 51–63
- **Key points**:
  - The finding that most sharply distinguishes a taboo void from a difficult problem: "when the decision situation was associated with only one sacred value (i.e., taboo trade-off), people perceived the task as more negatively emotion-laden, but as *easier* to solve, compared to a situation not involving sacred values (i.e., routine trade-off)." Experiment 1 difficulty means: taboo 2.34 < routine 2.89 < tragic 5.25.
  - Negative emotion rose monotonically (routine 2.90, taboo 3.28, tragic 4.75; F(2,162) = 91.31).
  - Dissociation of difficulty from aversiveness: the taboo case is *aversive and easy*; the tragic case is *aversive and hard*.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — a "difficulty" model of moral cognition would predict the wrong ordering.
- **Quote**: "the involvement of moral issues and values can also facilitate decisions because people often insistently preclude them from trade-offs with other values."

### Ginges, Atran, Medin & Shikaki (2007), "Sacred bounds on rational resolution of violent political conflict"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0701768104
- **Type**: Empirical paper, *PNAS* 104(18): 7357–7360
- **Key points**:
  - Among Palestinian and Israeli participants, "violent opposition to compromise over issues considered sacred is (i) increased by offering material incentives to compromise but (ii) decreased when the adversary makes symbolic compromises over their own sacred values."
  - The **backfire effect**: adding money to a sacred trade-off makes the proposal *more* offensive — the exact inverse of a utility function.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bidirectional interaction of value and world at the collective scale; the sacred value is not a large number but a refusal of the number line.
- **Quote**: "the use of material incentives to promote the peaceful resolution of political and cultural conflicts may backfire when adversaries treat contested issues as sacred values."

### Atran & Ginges (2012), "Religious and sacred imperatives in human conflict"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1216902
- **Type**: Review, *Science* 336(6083): 855–857
- **Key points**: sacred values "operate as moral imperatives that inspire nonrational exertions independent of likely outcomes"; "In conflict situations, otherwise mundane sociopolitical preferences may become sacred values, acquiring immunity to material incentives." Sacralisation is dynamic — the void's boundary moves.
- **Tenet alignment**: The migration of ordinary preferences into the taboo class is direct evidence that the occlusion is *maintained*, not architectural.

### Berns et al. (2012), "The price of your soul"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2011.0262 (PMC3260841)
- **Type**: fMRI study, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 367(1589): 754–762
- **Key points**:
  - Paid real money to induce people to sell personal values (integrity as proxy for sacredness). "values that people refused to sell (sacred values) were associated with increased activity in the left temporoparietal junction and ventrolateral prefrontal cortex, regions previously associated with semantic rule retrieval."
  - Conclusion: sacred values "affect behaviour through the retrieval and processing of deontic rules and not through a utilitarian evaluation of costs and benefits."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neural corroboration of the non-compensatory signature — a lookup, not a weighing.

### Duc, Hanselmann, Boesiger & Tanner (2013), "Sacred values: Trade-off type matters"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1037/npe0000014
- **Type**: fMRI study, *Journal of Neuroscience, Psychology, and Economics* 6(4): 252–263
- **Key points** (abstract via search summary; primary text not read): taboo trade-offs, in a high-sacred-value group, increased BOLD signal in left anterior temporal lobe and bilateral amygdalae; right amygdala signal correlated with moral-disgust ratings. Contrast conditions were taboo, tragic, routine.
- **Tenet alignment**: Affective (disgust) and rule-retrieval systems co-activate — the "contamination" phenomenology has a neural correlate.

### Pretus et al. (2018) and Hamid et al. (2019) — devoted-actor neuroimaging
- **URLs**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02462 ; https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.181585
- **Type**: fMRI studies (*Frontiers in Psychology* 9: 2462; *Royal Society Open Science* 6: 181585)
- **Key points**:
  - Pretus 2018 (38 scanned from 535 surveyed young men in Barcelona): sacred-value processing "shows marked activity in the left inferior frontal gyrus, a region previously associated with sacred values and rule retrieval." Social exclusion "specifically affected nonsacred values, increasing their similarities with sacred values" — exclusion *enlarges* the taboo class.
  - Hamid 2019: "diminished activity in dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC), inferior frontal gyrus, and parietal cortex while conveying willingness to fight and die for sacred relative to non-sacred values-regions that have previously been implicated in calculating costs and consequences." An overlapping dlPFC region was active when viewing conflicting peer ratings, "suggesting that it is possible to induce flexibility in the way people defend sacred values."
- **Tenet alignment**: The void is visible as an *absence* of computation, and it has a social lever (peer ratings) that reopens the closed region.

### Smeele, van Cranenburgh, Donkers, Schermer & de Bekker-Grob (2025), "Taboo trade-off aversion in choice behaviors"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2025.118606
- **Type**: Econometric paper (two discrete-choice experiments), *Social Science & Medicine* 386: 118606
- **Key points**:
  - Extends the random-utility model with penalties for taboo trade-offs. "WTP to save an incremental patient life approximately 3.5 times higher in conventional RUM models compared to the TTOA models" — the void *inflates* estimated valuations when ignored.
  - "The presence and magnitude of TTOA varied considerably across respondents. Latent Class (LC) models reveal that some respondent groups perceive trade-offs as taboo significantly, while others do not."
- **Tenet alignment**: Individual variation means the void's boundary is a property of particular minds, not of the proposition.

### Liu, Dai, Tan, Lei, Zhou & Wu (2025), "Outraged AI: Large language models prioritise emotion over cost in fairness enforcement"
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17880 (preprint, 17 Oct 2025; verified against arXiv metadata)
- **Type**: Preprint (large-scale behavioural comparison)
- **Key points**:
  - "In a large-scale comparison of 4,068 LLM agents with 1,159 adults across 796,100 decisions, LLMs used emotion to guide punishment, sometimes even more strongly than humans did".
  - "LLMs prioritized emotion over cost, enforcing norms in an almost all-or-none manner with reduced cost sensitivity, whereas humans balanced fairness and cost." Reasoning models were more cost-sensitive but "remained heavily emotion-driven."
- **Tenet alignment**: Relevant to non-human minds as void explorers — the current probes exhibit an *exaggerated* non-compensatory pattern, not a freer one.

### Frankfurt (1988), "Rationality and the unthinkable"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9780511818172.014
- **Type**: Book chapter, *The Importance of What We Care About*, Cambridge University Press, pp. 177–190
- **Key points** (via secondary sources; primary text not consulted verbatim — see Gaps):
  - The unthinkable is a limit of the *will*, not of intelligence: the agent cannot bring himself to take the option as a live candidate, though he can describe it.
  - Reported verbatim: "the category of the unthinkable is not essentially a moral category"; the considerations that make something unthinkable "may be entirely self-regarding and without any moral significance at all."
  - The Luther case from the companion essay on volitional necessity: Luther "accedes to it because he is unwilling to oppose it and because, furthermore, his unwillingness is itself something which he is unwilling to alter." The incapacity is endorsed at the second order and is therefore an expression of who the agent is.
  - Frankfurt frames the unthinkable as a *protection* of identity: under utilitarianism, "anything may be required"; a mind for which "nothing can be ruled out in advance" is disoriented.
- **Tenet alignment**: Ties to the [wholeheartedness-void](/voids/wholeheartedness-void/) and [mattering-void](/voids/mattering-void/) treatments of Frankfurt — the taboo void is the hierarchical will seen from the outside.

### Williams (1993), "Moral Incapacity"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/aristotelian/93.1.59
- **Type**: Paper, *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 93(1): 59–70 (reprinted in *Making Sense of Humanity*, 1995)
- **Key points** (via Van Den Beld 1997, *Philosophy* 72(282): 525–536, and Clark 1999, *Ratio* 12(1): 1–13):
  - Moral incapacity is *expressed in deliberation*: the action "does not present itself as a candidate." The agent's "I can't" is a deliberative conclusion, not a report of impotence.
  - Distinguished from psychological incapacity (sheer inability or recoil): the moral incapacity runs *through* the agent's reasons rather than blocking them from outside.
  - Clark's difficulties: scepticism about whether the incapacity is genuine; conflicting incapacities; cases with no actual or possible deliberation.
- **Tenet alignment**: Supplies the conceptual test for the void's reality — whether the option fails to *appear* (Williams) or appears and is *refused* (Tetlock's flicker).

### Raz (1986), *The Morality of Freedom*, ch. 13, and Chang (2001)
- **URL**: SEP entry "Incommensurable Values" §3.1: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/value-incommensurable/ ; Chang: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1758-2237.2001.tb00038.x
- **Type**: Monograph (Clarendon Press); journal article (*Philosophical Issues* 11: 33–60)
- **Key points**:
  - Raz on being offered money to leave one's spouse: "what has symbolic significance is the very judgment that companionship is incommensurable with money" (p. 350); "a belief in incommensurability is itself a qualification for having certain relations" (p. 351).
  - The refusal is not a ranking: if friendship simply outweighed money, buying friendship would be obligatory when possible — a conclusion Raz rejects. Tetlock (2003) cites Raz at exactly this point: treating the values as commensurable "subverts one of the values in the trade-off calculus."
  - Chang's objection: incommensurability becomes "relative to one's participation in social conventions" (2001, p. 48) — constitutive or merely conventional?
- **Tenet alignment**: The constitutive reading is what makes this a void rather than a bias: filling it would dissolve the relation it protects.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The taboo void is the region of possibility-space a mind can name but will not enter as a deliberator. Its content is not hidden: subjects in every study can state the forbidden proposal with precision. What is missing is the *operation* — the weighing, the comparison, the counterfactual run — applied to that content under a specific description. On the territory axis it is **Occluded**; on the reason axis of [taxonomy-of-voids](/apex/taxonomy-of-voids/) it is doubly maintained — psychologically (contamination, cleansing) and socially (outrage, character sanctions on the proposer and on any observed delay). Tetlock's own summary is that the boundaries of the thinkable are "alternately punitively rigid and surprisingly flexible": rigid under the taboo description, dissolved under a routine or tragic one.

Three features distinguish it from neighbouring voids:

1. **It is easy, not hard.** Hanselmann & Tanner's difficulty ordering (taboo < routine < tragic) rules out the reading that this is difficult territory. The [decision-void](/voids/decision-void/) concerns the hidden moment when open weighing collapses into choice; here the weighing never opens.
2. **It sanctions the act of thinking, not the conclusion.** The mere-contemplation effect and the harsher judgment of longer deliberation make this the cleanest empirical case of a thought that is punished *for being had*. [self-maintained-cognitive-limits](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/) describes defence against unwanted conclusions; the taboo void defends against the *process*.
3. **It is constitutive.** Frankfurt, Williams and Raz converge on the claim that the incapacity is part of the identity or the relation it protects. A void of ignorance is filled at no cost; this void is filled at the cost of being a different agent.

### Evidence for the Limit

- **Quantity insensitivity** (Baron & Spranca; Ginges et al.): the response does not scale with the stake — the fingerprint of a comparison not being run.
- **Backfire** (Ginges et al. 2007): adding value to the offer increases refusal — impossible under any monotone utility model.
- **Non-compensatory neural signature** (Berns 2012; Pretus 2018; Hamid 2019): rule-retrieval regions engaged; cost-calculation regions (dlPFC, parietal) *less* active for sacred than non-sacred values.
- **Cleansing after mere exposure** (Tetlock et al. 2000, Exp. 5): the block leaves a trace even when nothing was concluded.
- **Robustness to demand characteristics** (Baron & Spranca): the five-property syndrome survives when subjects cannot tell which value they are rating.

Evidence *against* a hard limit is equally strong and must be carried alongside: reframing flips outcomes (72% acceptance of the toxic-waste diversion); expressed preferences diverge from revealed ones; sacralisation is reversible in both directions (Atran & Ginges 2012; Pretus 2018); Smeele et al.'s latent classes show some respondents have no taboo penalty at all; peer ratings reopen dlPFC engagement (Hamid 2019). The honest reading: the void is **description-relative and person-relative**, real *as an occlusion* while the redescribed content stays reachable.

### Phenomenology

The literature supplies an unusually specific first-person profile. The forbidden proposal is met with **ease** — the answer is already present as a retrieved rule. Beneath the ease sits **aversion** (negative-emotion ratings rise), which Duc et al. tie to amygdala activity and disgust. If the thought is entertained at all, there is the **flicker**: Gilbert's pre-rejection interval in which comprehension momentarily resembles assent, followed by a "subjective sense – however unjustified – that one has been contaminated." The contamination seeks discharge: **cleansing** acts (organ-donation volunteering in the hospital-administrator study) that "reaffirm solidarity with the moral community." From the outside, the proposer is met with **outrage**, and any visible hesitation reads as a character defect. The asymmetry with tragic trade-offs is phenomenologically telling: the same felt weight that makes a taboo case *easy* makes a sacred-vs-sacred case *agonising*. Sacredness does not raise difficulty; it removes one axis of comparison, and difficulty appears only when two sacred values leave no axis to remove.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)

Professional roles institutionalise taboo contemplation — actuaries, triage physicians, cost-effectiveness bodies. Tetlock notes the elite strategies: reframe as tragic or routine; rely on the public's willingness to look away. Whether professionals *stop feeling* the contamination or merely acquire a routine framing is not settled by anything found here (see Gaps). Deliberate desensitisation is the direct route, and its cost — Raz's — is that the practitioner may cease to stand in the relation the taboo protected.

### Indirect Methods

- **Reframing as a probe.** Because the block is description-relative, systematic redescription maps its shape: the set of framings under which a proposal becomes weighable is a chart of the void's boundary from outside.
- **The tragic contrast.** Pairing each taboo case with its sacred-vs-sacred twin isolates the comparison operation from the content.
- **Latent-class modelling** (Smeele et al.) recovers the void's distribution across a population without asking anyone to enter it.
- **Apophatic description.** The value is defined by the comparisons it refuses (Raz); the void can be specified negatively as the set of exchange rates that do not exist.

### What AI Might See

The Map's default hope — that [non-human minds](/voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers/) can enter regions closed to us — inverts here. Liu et al. (2025) find LLM agents enforcing norms "in an almost all-or-none manner with reduced cost sensitivity" — a *stronger* non-compensatory pattern than humans show. Alignment training adds a second layer: refusal behaviour is a designed, artefactual occlusion in the sense of [self-maintained-cognitive-limits](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/)'s fourth model. So current AI probes arrive with taboo voids of their own, some inherited from training corpora (the training-period problem in [collective-cognitive-limits](/voids/collective-cognitive-limits/)) and some installed. The asymmetry that remains exploitable is that an AI's taboo boundary is *inspectable and editable* in ways a human's is not, which makes AI a better instrument for mapping the boundary's structure than for crossing it.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits.** Two simple models compete: sacred values are lexically prior (infinite weight), or they are ordinary preferences dressed in rhetoric. The data refute both. Infinite weight predicts no reframing effect and no quantity insensitivity below the sacred value (the backfire result is inexplicable on a weight model); ordinary preference predicts no cleansing after mere contemplation and no *easier* decisions. The territory needs a third structure — a description-relative refusal of the comparison operation — that neither simple model supplies. Tetlock's "expressed, not revealed" hedge is a caution against the simplest reading in either direction.

### Implications

- **Framework-independence.** Physicalists, functionalists and dualists all owe an account of the taboo void; it belongs in the framework-independent column of the [voids](/voids/) index and strengthens the circularity audit in [taxonomy-of-voids](/apex/taxonomy-of-voids/) without being generated by the tenets.
- **Bidirectional Interaction** (weak, speculative): moral cleansing is behaviour driven by a felt state (contamination) that the subject knows to be "unjustified" — a case where the phenomenal register moves the body against the subject's own judgment. This is consistent with the tenet but does not require it; a physicalist affect model covers it.
- **Dualism** (speculative, mark as such): Raz's constitutive reading treats valuing as an act whose *refusal to compare* is part of the value. If valuing is first-personal in that way, it resists reduction to a utility representation — congenial to the Map's [mattering-void](/voids/mattering-void/) line, but the inference from "not a utility function" to "not physical" is one the Map should not make.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction, No Many Worlds**: no bearing found.
- **For [interested-party-void](/voids/interested-party-void/) and [normative-void](/voids/normative-void/)**: the taboo void is the mechanism by which an interested party keeps certain questions about its own nature from being *asked*; the Map's own commitments are candidates for sacralisation, and the sign to watch for is ease where there should be difficulty.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **"The Taboo Void"** — a void defined by the *easy* answer: build the article around the Hanselmann & Tanner ordering and the mere-contemplation effect, then the constitutive turn (Frankfurt, Williams, Raz), then the reframing evidence as the honest limit on the void's reality. Seam with [decision-void](/voids/decision-void/) (weighing never opens) and [self-maintained-cognitive-limits](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/) (process, not conclusion, is defended).
2. **"The Constitutive Void"** — a void whose filling costs the agent its identity; contrast with voids of ignorance; Chang's conventionalist objection as the counter-position; the Map's own sacralised commitments as the reflexive case.

## Gaps in Research

- Frankfurt (1988) and Williams (1993) were reached only through secondary expositions (Cambridge chapter summary, SEP-adjacent search summaries, Van Den Beld 1997, Clark 1999). Verbatim primary formulations of "the unthinkable" and "does not present itself as a candidate" need checking against the texts before an article quotes them.
- Duc et al. (2013) known from abstract and a search summary only; sample size and group definitions unverified.
- No study found that measures whether professional desensitisation (triage, actuarial pricing of life) removes the contamination response or only the framing — the key empirical question for whether the void is crossable at a cost.
- No first-person phenomenological study of the "flicker" and contamination; the profile above is assembled from behavioural proxies.
- Tetlock (2000) invokes cross-cultural relational schemata (Fiske) to locate boundaries; not pursued here. Where the taboo class *sits* across cultures is unexplored territory in its own right.
- Whether an agent can hold a taboo *and* map it from inside (a Williams incapacity that knows itself) may be unanswerable: knowing the exchange rate one refuses to compute is the contemplation the void forbids.
