The reason to build it is not completeness. Cotard is the one clinical case whose report *content* is the denial of the reporter's own existence, which makes it the sharpest available test of a commitment the Map already registers in prose: "Every reported case is reported from somewhere" ([self-and-self-consciousness](/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/#constitutive-as-kind-not-as-degree), mirrored in [self-opacity](/voids/self-opacity/)). A concept page can therefore do three things no live article does: state the pair, run the "reported from somewhere" test against the hardest case, and record the 2016–2023 debate over whether the seed experience is affective, depersonalisation-like, or interoceptive.

## How the Capgras Article Frames the Channel (and what it says about Cotard)

The Capgras article does not use the phrase "self-model channel"; the review's phrase is a gloss. Its own frame is the **affective-familiarity channel**: "the felt 'mineness' or warmth that normally accompanies the sight of a loved one is severed while perceptual identification of the face stays intact." Its scope discipline is explicit and should be inherited verbatim: "**Only Factor 1 is the Map's territory.** ... Factor 2, the belief-evaluation deficit that converts anomaly into fixed delusion, is a reasoning pathology the Map takes no position on." And its calibration ceiling: Capgras is "*consistent with* interface architecture and explicitly not ... a demonstration that the channel is non-physical."

It says nothing about Cotard. Its "disowning taxonomy" runs act / limb / person ([anarchic hand](/topics/anarchic-hand-and-action-ownership/), alien hand, Capgras). Cotard extends the series by one term — the *self* disowned, or more precisely reported absent — which is the natural hook for the concept page and keeps it from re-treating the Capgras mechanism.

The "self-model ascending channel" phrase the review reached for lives in [neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture](/topics/neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture/) §"Self-Model Ascending — Anosognosia": "a self-model ascending channel that delivers the system's own status to awareness. When this channel is damaged, consciousness receives a coherent but false report about the body's capabilities." Cotard is the same channel reporting the system's *existence* rather than its *capacities*. The anosognosia expand-topic task owns the capability/reversal side; see the boundary section.

## Key Sources

### Young, Robertson, Hellawell, de Pauw and Pentland (1992), "Cotard delusion after brain injury"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/S003329170003823X — *Psychological Medicine* 22(3), 799–804. PMID 1410102. (Crossref + Europe PMC.)
- **Type**: single case, neuropsychological.
- **Key points** (abstract, raw): "A right-handed young man with contusions affecting temporo-parietal areas of the right cerebral hemisphere and some bilateral frontal lobe damage became convinced that he was dead (the Cotard delusion), and experienced difficulties in recognizing familiar faces, buildings and places, as well as feelings of derealization." "We suggest that these impairments contributed to his Cotard delusion by heightening feelings of unreality, and that the underlying pathophysiology and neuropsychology of the Cotard delusion may be related to other problems involving delusional misidentification."
- **Why it matters**: the first paper to tie Cotard to the Capgras-style face-processing story, and it already names *derealisation* as the felt seed, which is where the 2016–2022 debate ends up.

### Wright, Young and Hellawell (1993), "Sequential Cotard and Capgras delusions"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8260.1993.tb01065.x — *British Journal of Clinical Psychology* 32(3), 345–349. PMID 8251965. (Crossref + Europe PMC.)
- **Type**: single case (KH).
- **Key points** (abstract, raw): "The Cotard delusion occurred when KH was depressed and the Capgras delusion arose in the context of persecutory delusions. We suggest that the Cotard and Capgras delusions reflect different interpretations of similar anomalous experiences, and that the persecutory delusions and suspiciousness that are often noted in Capgras cases contribute to the patients' mistaking a change in themselves for a change in others ('they are impostors'), whereas people who are depressed exaggerate the negative effects of the same change whilst correctly attributing it to themselves ('I am dead')."
- **Why it matters**: the mirror claim in its cleanest form, in one patient, sequentially. Note the phrase "correctly attributing it to themselves" — on this account the Cotard patient's attribution is *right* and the content is wrong.

### Young, Leafhead and Szulecka (1994), "The Capgras and Cotard delusions"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1159/000284874 — *Psychopathology* 27, 226–231. PMID 7846242. (Europe PMC gives 1994; Crossref's record carries a 2010 "issued" date, a Karger back-catalogue artefact — cite 1994. Neither index returns volume/issue; commonly cited as 27(3–5).)
- **Type**: review/theory.
- **Key points** (abstract, raw): "both delusions reflect an interaction of impairments at two levels. One set of contributory factors involves perceptual impairment, or anomalous perceptual experience. The other factors lead to an incorrect interpretation of this, for which we offer an explanation in terms of attribution theory. Although the Capgras and Cotard delusions are phenomenally distinct, they may therefore represent attempts to make sense of fundamentally similar experiences."
- **Attribution check for the brief**: the "Young and Leafhead (1990s)" lead is confirmed, but the mirror is a three-paper sequence — Young et al. 1992 (case), Wright et al. 1993 (sequential case), Young, Leafhead & Szulecka 1994 (theory) — with the extended case series in Young & Leafhead (1996). Do not cite "Young & Leafhead" for the 1992 or 1993 findings.

### Gerrans (2000), "Refining the Explanation of Cotard's Delusion"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0017.00125 — *Mind & Language* 15(1), 111–122. (Crossref, with abstract.)
- **Key points** (abstract, raw): restates the Young account — "Cotard patients typically exhibit a style of internal attribution associated with depression, while Capgras patients exhibit the external attribution style more typical of paranoia" — then proposes three modifications: "the nature of the affective deficit in Cotard cases may be more global than in Capgras cases, resulting from the diffuse effects of the neurochemical substrate of depression"; "It is unsurprising that persons whose global affective responses were suppressed would explain their lack of response by saying that they had no bodily existence"; and "in Cotard cases the delusion is produced by a reasoning deficit, rather than attributional style."

- **Key points** (abstract, raw): "A case report of a 19-year old male with autoimmune encephalitis and Cotard syndrome, in whom Positron Emission Tomography (18F-FDG-PET) scans were performed before and after successful treatment with electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)". ### Billon (2016), "Making Sense of the Cotard Syndrome: Insights from the Study of Depersonalisation"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/mila.12110 — *Mind & Language* 31(3), 356–391. (Crossref, with abstract.)
- **Key points** (abstract, raw): "Patients suffering from the Cotard syndrome can deny being alive, having guts, thinking or even existing. ### Davies and Coltheart (2022), "Cotard delusion, emotional experience and depersonalisation"

## Major Positions

### 1. Affective-recognition seed plus attribution style (Young et al. 1992; Wright et al. 1993; Young, Leafhead & Szulecka 1994; Young & Leafhead 1996; McKay & Cipolotti 2007)
- **Core claim**: Cotard and Capgras share a seed (loss of the affective response to familiar stimuli); depressed/internalising patients read the change as a change in *themselves* ("I am dead"), suspicious/externalising patients as a change in *others* ("they are impostors").
- **Status**: the original mirror; still the textbook pairing. Its weak joint is the seed. Young et al. 1992 already named derealisation rather than face-affect as the proximate feeling; Davies & Coltheart 2022 find the face-affect/flatness seed contradicted by the case record.

### 2. Two-factor (Davies, Coltheart, Langdon & Breen 2001; Davies & Coltheart 2022; Restrepo-Martínez et al. 2019 for imaging)
- **Core claim**: Factor 1 = an anomalous experience (now: depersonalisation/derealisation-type experience, insular/interoceptive on the imaging reading); Factor 2 = a belief-evaluation deficit (right prefrontal) that lets "I am dead" be adopted and retained.
- **Prediction the brief asked for**: patients with Factor 1 alone should report the *as-if* form ("it is as if I were dead") with insight preserved — which is the depersonalisation clinical picture; patients with both should drop the "as if". Remission should track Factor 2 more than Factor 1 — which is what the single pre/post PET case shows (prefrontal normalised, insula not, delusion gone).
- **Relation to tenets**: the Capgras article's rule transfers unchanged — Factor 1 is the Map's territory, Factor 2 is not.

### 3. One-factor / face-value (Gerrans 2002 for the pair; Billon 2016 for Cotard)
- **Core claim**: distinguish the phenomenologies properly and no reasoning deficit is needed; Cotard "simply consist[s] in taking these anomalous experiences at face value" (Billon). The experiences lack subjective, present and actual character; a report that faithfully describes such an experience *is* "I do not exist, nothing is happening, the world is not real."
- **Why the Map cannot ignore it**: on this reading the existence report is *accurate to the experience*, and the pathology lies in the experience, not in the belief-fixing. That is closer to the Map's channel vocabulary than the two-factor reading is — it makes Cotard purely a Factor-1 phenomenon — but it inherits the objection that DP patients have the same experiences and do not form the belief.

### 4. Interoceptive active inference / dyshomeostasis (Gerrans 2022)
- **Core claim**: the seed is bodily dysregulation, not depersonalisation; the self is a hierarchical homeostatic model; Cotard and DP are different breakdowns of self-modelling.
- **Why it matters for the boundary**: this is the strongest *self-model-only* account on offer, and it is the one that would treat the "existence-report" as a top-level inference from bottom-level bodily-prediction failure. It also breaks the DP→Cotard continuum the other three assume.

### 5. Nosological deflation (Berrios & Luque 1995a, 1995b; Debruyne et al. 2009)
- **Core claim**: Cotard is a syndrome (or a symptom cluster within depression), not a disease; only about 69% of historical cases even include existence-denial; the contemporary habit of equating "Cotard" with "believes they are dead" and hunting a brain locus for it is a category error the conceptual history warns against.
- **Relation to tenets**: Tenet 5 material against the Map's own tidiness. A concept page titled after the "existence report" must say up front that it is selecting the Type-I / existence-denial subset of a broader délire des négations.

## The Analytic Question, Kept Short

**What does an existence report made by a manifestly present subject show about the relation between a self-model and the subject that has it?**

1. **The performative datum is real and both sides own it.** The patient who says "I do not exist" argues, resists, worries, and (per Davies & Coltheart 2022) feels anxiety, fear and guilt. Every reported case is reported from somewhere, and Cotard is the case where the content of the report denies the somewhere. This *confirms* the register's "constitutive as kind, not degree" claim in its hardest instance: what fails is the report, not the position. But the physicalist self-model theorist grants all of this — a system can misrepresent its own existence exactly as it can misrepresent its own arm — so the datum is **neutral** between "a subject receiving a corrupted report" and "a self-model misrepresenting itself to no one further."

2. **The report is not a transparent readout.** Berrios & Luque's 55% co-occurrence of immortality delusions with 69% existence-denial means the channel's output is internally inconsistent in over half the corpus. That is what one expects from a *constructed* status report rather than a monitored fact, which is the anosognosia point ([neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture](/topics/neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture/): "consciousness gets a coherent fiction rather than degraded truth") extended one level up. Again **neutral**: both readings say the self-model is a construction.

3. **Where the readings could come apart.** A pure self-model account (Metzinger-style, or Gerrans's homeostatic hierarchy) predicts that whatever the model's existence/presence component says, *there is nothing else* whose status could differ from it — the subject just is the model's content. The Map's interface reading predicts the model's existence-report can fail while for-me-ness is *attenuated but never eliminated* — the falsifying scenario is "phenomenal life continuing without any residue of for-me-ness, no position from which the lack is registered" ([self-and-self-consciousness](/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/)). The clinical record fits the second description: DP and Cotard reports are all made from a residual position. But that record is also compatible with the first, because a model that reports non-existence is still a running model, and "residual position" is exactly how a running self-model presents. **The Map should say plainly that Cotard does not discriminate** between the two readings at the significance tier; it tightens the *architecture-tier* claim only — the existence/aliveness dimension of the self-model is a separable component, dissociable from ongoing emotional and cognitive life ([architecture-vs-significance-two-tier-discount](/project/architecture-vs-significance-two-tier-discount/) applies verbatim).

4. **What the interface/filter reading predicts that a pure self-model account does not** — the honest answer is: nothing about the *report*. The prediction that separates them is the one the Capgras article already makes for Factor 1 and cannot cash: that the missing datum is a missing *conscious contribution* rather than a missing functional annotation. Cotard adds one modest asymmetry worth stating: on the interface reading, the existence-report failure should be *content-general* (the same subject can lose felt existence for body, self, world and time — Billon's three characters — because one receiving position is being under-served), whereas a strictly modular self-model account has no reason to expect the four contents to co-vary. Berrios & Luque's co-occurrence rates (body 86%, existence 69%) are consistent with co-variation but the historical corpus cannot test it. Flag this as a *prediction awaiting data*, not as evidence.

5. **The depersonalisation continuum is contested, not given.** Billon 2016 and Davies & Coltheart 2022 treat DP as the sub-delusional form of the same experience; Gerrans 2022 denies it. The concept page should present the continuum as the majority reading and Gerrans as the standing objection, and should not build its architecture on the continuum being true — the "self-model channel" framing survives either way, since both camps agree the failure is in self-modelling.
