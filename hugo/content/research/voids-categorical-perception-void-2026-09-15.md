# Research: Voids - The Categorical Perception Void

**Date**: 2026-09-15
**Search queries used**: "McMurray 2022 myth of categorical perception"; "Pérez-Gay Juárez Harnad 2019 category learning alters perception"; "perceptual narrowing Werker Tees 1984"; "Whorfian categorical perception color lateralized Gilbert 2006 Winawer 2007"; "Toscano McMurray 2010 N1 voice onset time continuous"; "categorical perception cognitive penetration Goldstone Hendrickson 2010"; "Schouten Gerrits van Hessen 2003 end of categorical perception"; "Bonnasse-Gahot Nadal categorical perception groundwork deep learning"; "Etcoff Magee 1992 categorical perception facial expressions"; "Russian blues limits Cognition 2020"; "Burns Ward 1978 musical intervals"; "Logan Lively Pisoni 1991 Japanese /r/ /l/ training"; "Witzel Gegenfurtner 2011 lateralized category effect"; "Gerrits Schouten 2004 discrimination task"
**Voids category**: Mixed — Occluded (the compression is an active operation) with an Unexplorable face that deflates under measurement

## Executive Summary

Where the world offers a continuum — voice-onset time, hue, a morph from one facial expression to another, the width of a musical interval — experience arrives already carved: /ba/ or /pa/, blue or green, anger or disgust, a minor or a major third. Categorical perception (CP) is the finding that equal physical steps are discriminated better across a category boundary than within one, so the interior of each category is perceptually compressed and the region between categories is stretched. The void has three faces. The **between face**: the within-category continuum is delivered to consciousness at reduced resolution, and the reduction is the mind's own doing. The **origin face**: consciousness cannot tell from inside whether a perceived joint tracks a discontinuity in the world or one installed by learning, language, or development — an installed boundary feels exactly like a discovered one. The **residue face**: the continuum survives beneath the category (the auditory N1 tracks voice-onset time linearly and independently of the category the listener reports) but the default deliverable is the verdict, never the gradient. The calibration result is the note's most useful finding: the strong textbook claim that listeners *cannot hear* within-category differences has been rejected by the speech-perception field itself as a task artefact (McMurray 2022), while the weak claim — warping plus a category-first default readout — is robust across speech, colour, faces, music, and trained laboratory categories. The void is therefore real but narrower than its reputation: a limit on *access by default*, not a wall.

## Key Sources

### McMurray (2022) — "The myth of categorical perception"
- **URL**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9803395/
- **Type**: Paper (*Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* 152(6): 3819–3842; doi:10.1121/10.0016614)
- **Key points**:
  - CP is defined empirically as a relationship between phoneme identification and discrimination; because discrimination tasks were assumed not to require categorisation, a categorical discrimination function was read as evidence that listeners perceive speech *solely* in terms of linguistic categories.
  - Fifty years of discrimination tasks, priming, visual-world eye-tracking, and ERP work reject the strongest forms of CP and provide little strong evidence for any form of it within speech perception.
  - The original standard was explicit: McMurray quotes Liberman et al. (1961) — "…perception … is essentially categorical in that *S can hear no differences among the stimuli beyond those that are revealed by the phoneme labels*." That standard was never properly supported.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — the simple "categories all the way down" model won on elegance and task convenience, and survived for decades on measurement artefact.
- **Quote**: "Categorical perception (CP) is likely the single finding from speech perception with the biggest impact on cognitive science. However, within speech perception, it is widely known to be an artifact of task demands." And: "The underlying representation of the continuous space—revealed by continuous ratings and neurophysiological measures—appears linear."

### Toscano, McMurray, Dennhardt & Luck (2010) — continuous encoding beneath graded categorisation
- **URL**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3523688/
- **Type**: Paper (*Psychological Science* 21(10): 1532–1540; doi:10.1177/0956797610384142)
- **Key points**:
  - ERP oddball design with spoken words varying in voice-onset time (VOT). The auditory N1 (~100 ms) tracked VOT in a one-to-one, linear fashion, independent of the category the listener assigned; within-category VOT differences were still present at the post-perceptual P3 (~450 ms).
  - The N1 shows a one-to-one correspondence with VOT even when participants indicate that stimuli belong to different phonological categories.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism (see below) — the encoding a listener's nervous system performs and the content the listener reports come apart cleanly, in a direction that leaves the continuum on the sub-personal side.
- **Quote**: "Thus, at perceptual levels, acoustic information is encoded continuously, independent of phonological information. Further, at phonological levels, fine-grained acoustic differences are preserved along with category information."

### Pérez-Gay Juárez, Sicotte, Thériault & Harnad (2019) — learned CP and its neural correlates
- **URL**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6897555/
- **Type**: Paper (*PLoS ONE* 14(12): e0226000; doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0226000)
- **Key points**:
  - Subjects learned to sort multi-featured visual textures into two categories by trial and error with feedback. Successful learners showed significant between-category separation and, to a lesser extent, within-category compression in pairwise dissimilarity judgments after learning versus before.
  - The late parietal positivity (usually read as decisional) increased and the occipital N1 amplitude (usually read as perceptual) decreased; the size of the perceptual component correlated with between-category separation.
  - A neural-net model for category learning and feature filtering predicted the separation and compression in advance.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — a boundary manufactured in an afternoon by feedback alone is subsequently perceived as a joint in the stimulus.
- **Quote**: "Learned Categorical Perception (CP) occurs when the members of different categories come to look more dissimilar ("between-category separation") and/or members of the same category come to look more similar ("within-category compression") after a new category has been learned." And, on the naming-bias objection: "it is the process of learning to distinguish the categories that influences their similarity, rather than just the name."

### Werker & Tees (1984); Werker & Hensch (2015) — perceptual narrowing
- **URL**: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0163638384800223 ; https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-psych-010814-015104
- **Type**: Papers (*Infant Behavior and Development* 7(1): 49–63; *Annual Review of Psychology* 66: 173–196)
- **Key points**:
  - English-learning infants discriminate Hindi and Nthlakampx (Salish) consonant contrasts at 6–8 months and largely lose the ability by 10–12 months, while infants learning those languages keep it. Cited over 4,000 times; the 2024–2025 forty-year retrospectives treat the finding as settled.
  - Werker & Hensch place the narrowing inside a critical-period model: excitatory/inhibitory balance opens the plasticity window, molecular brakes close it.
  - Kuhl (1991) adds the *perceptual magnet effect*: discrimination is worse around good exemplars of a phonetic category than around poor ones, in human adults and infants but not in monkeys — the category's centre pulls neighbours toward it.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — the adult's clean phonemic joints are the residue of a pruning the adult cannot remember and cannot undo by attention alone.
- **Quote**: (paraphrase from the retrospective literature) in the latter half of the first year, infants become less able to distinguish phonetic contrasts absent from the ambient language.

### Winawer et al. (2007); Gilbert, Regier, Kay & Ivry (2006); Witzel & Gegenfurtner (2011); Martinovic, Paramei & MacInnes (2020) — the colour face and its deflation
- **URL**: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0701644104 ; https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0509868103 ; https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2121056 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010027720301001
- **Type**: Papers (*PNAS* 104: 7780–7785; *PNAS* 103: 489–494; *Journal of Vision* 11(12): 16; *Cognition* 201: 104281)
- **Key points**:
  - Russian obligatorily distinguishes *goluboy* (lighter blue) from *siniy* (darker blue); English does not. Winawer et al. found Russian speakers faster to discriminate two blues that straddle the *siniy/goluboy* border than two from the same category, and the advantage was eliminated by a verbal but not a spatial dual task.
  - Gilbert et al. reported the category advantage in the right visual field (left hemisphere) only — the "lateralised Whorf" result.
  - Witzel & Gegenfurtner ran ten versions of the two original experiments on 230 observers with controlled colour rendering and naming-derived categories: category effects on reaction time were present but *not* lateralised.
  - Martinovic et al. found a boundary speed advantage for *goluboj/zelënyj* (blue/green) but not for *sinij/goluboj*, and the *sinij/goluboj* boundary itself shifted with the lightness distribution of the stimulus set — a lighter context moved the boundary toward lighter shades.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits, twice over — the elegant "language carves colour" story over-reached, and the boundary that does exist moves with context, so its felt fixity is not evidence of a world-joint.
- **Quote**: (paraphrase) the original Winawer abstract: the category advantage was eliminated by a verbal, but not a spatial, dual task.

### Etcoff & Magee (1992); Fugate (2013) — faces
- **URL**: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/001002779290002Y ; https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073912451350
- **Type**: Papers (*Cognition* 44(3): 227–240; *Emotion Review* 5(1): 84–89)
- **Key points**:
  - Computer-generated drawings stepped by constant physical amounts from one expression to another (or to neutral). Faces within an expression category were discriminated more poorly than faces in different categories differing by the same physical amount.
  - Fugate's review: whether the effect is purely structural (differences among the faces) or requires perceiver-side conceptual knowledge, including the emotion words present in the paradigm, remains disputed; a 2024 registered report tests anger/disgust CP across cultures.
- **Tenet alignment**: Origin face — the joint between "angry" and "disgusted" may be partly lexical, and the perceiver cannot audit which part.

### Burns & Ward (1978); Gerrits & Schouten (2004); Schouten, Gerrits & van Hessen (2003) — task dependence
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/670543/ ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03194885 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167639302000948
- **Type**: Papers (*JASA* 63(2): 456–468; *Perception & Psychophysics* 66(3): 363–376; *Speech Communication* 41(1): 71–80)
- **Key points**:
  - Trained musicians perceive melodic intervals (minor third → major third → perfect fourth) categorically under standard identification/discrimination procedures; under an adaptive variable-step discrimination procedure the categorical signature, present initially, largely disappeared once subjects reached asymptotic performance.
  - Gerrits & Schouten: vowel continua showed no CP at all in a four-interval discrimination task (discrimination not predictable from classification), while a 2IFC task on the same stimuli produced the classic categorical pattern. Schouten et al. locate the decisive variable in the degree of response bias the discrimination task invites.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — the void's apparent size is a function of the instrument.
- **Quote**: (near-verbatim from the Burns & Ward abstract) evidence of categorical perception, "although present initially, largely disappeared after subjects had reached asymptotic performance."

### Burnston (2021) — "Perceptual Learning, Categorical Perception, and Cognitive Permeation"
- **URL**: https://philarchive.org/archive/BURPLC
- **Type**: Paper (*Dialectica* 75(1): 25–58; doi:10.48106/dial.v75.i1.03)
- **Key points**:
  - Proponents of cognitive penetration argue from CP plus perceptual learning that beliefs permeate perception in the course of learning categories (the "diachronic" thesis). Burnston argues this is false: to guide learning, beliefs must describe contents subjects can already perceive, so they cannot specify the ability actually learned.
  - Perceptual category learning develops and modifies a "morphspace" — differentiating and accentuating dimensions of perceptual difference — without cognitive influence on the learning operation itself.
- **Tenet alignment**: Origin face — the installed boundary is opaque from *both* directions: not only can the perceiver not see whether it was installed, the perceiver's own prior beliefs cannot have specified what was installed.
- **Quote**: "I argue that this "diachronic" penetration thesis is false." And: "I argue that a better approach recognizes that we can have sophisticated causal precursors to perceptual learning, but that the learning process itself must operate outside of cognitive influence."

### Bonnasse-Gahot & Nadal (2022); Cacioli (2026 preprint) — CP in artificial networks
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05549 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28258
- **Type**: Paper (*Neural Computation* 34(2): 437–475; doi:10.1162/neco_a_01454) and an unreviewed single-author preprint (submitted 30 March 2026, revised 24 April 2026)
- **Key points**:
  - Bonnasse-Gahot & Nadal combine mutual- and Fisher-information analysis with simulations: category learning induces categorical perception in shallow and deep networks, with expansion of representational space near category boundaries and contraction far from them, stronger at deeper layers.
  - Cacioli reports representational-similarity evidence that six LLMs from five architecture families warp their hidden-state geometry at digit-count boundaries (10, 100), with a CP-additive model (log-distance plus boundary boost) beating a continuous model at every primary layer; absent at non-boundary controls and absent for hot/cold where there is no tokenisation discontinuity. Two signatures: "classic CP" (Gemma, Qwen), where the model both categorises explicitly and warps, and "structural CP" (Llama, Mistral, Phi), "where geometry warps at the boundary but models cannot report the category distinction."
- **Tenet alignment**: What AI might see — see below. The Cacioli result must be carried as a preprint, not a finding.

### Goldstone & Hendrickson (2010) — reference definition
- **URL**: https://wires.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/wcs.26
- **Type**: Review (*WIREs Cognitive Science* 1(1): 69–78)
- **Key points**: CP is revealed when discrimination between things is better when they belong to different categories, controlling for physical difference. Open questions the review poses: innate versus learned categories; how early in processing categories act; the relation between ongoing linguistic processing and CP.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Three faces, one operation.

**The between face (compressed interior).** Equal physical steps are not equal experiential steps. Two voice-onset times of 20 and 30 ms both sound like /b/ and are hard to tell apart; 30 and 40 ms straddle the /b/–/p/ boundary and are easy. The same holds for hue near a basic-colour-term border, for morphs between expressions, and — in trained musicians — for interval width. The continuum is not absent from experience, but its interior is delivered at lower resolution than its edges, and the down-sampling is performed by the perceiver.

**The origin face (world-joint or installed joint?).** Some perceived boundaries plausibly track discontinuities that matter (voicing is produced by a discrete laryngeal gesture). Others are demonstrably installed: a two-category texture space learned by feedback in a single session (Pérez-Gay Juárez et al. 2019), a lexical blue/blue border that English speakers do not carry (Winawer et al. 2007), an interval scale only musicians possess (Burns & Ward 1978), a phonemic inventory pruned by twelve months of age (Werker & Tees 1984). The decisive point is phenomenological: nothing in the experience of a boundary marks it as installed. An adult English speaker hears /r/ and /l/ as two sounds; an adult Japanese speaker hears one; each hears a joint, and neither can inspect its provenance. Burnston's argument sharpens this from the other side — even the learner's own beliefs during category learning could not have specified the perceptual ability acquired, so the boundary's origin is not recoverable by remembering what one intended to learn.

**The residue face (the gradient that is kept but not delivered).** Toscano et al. (2010) is the key result: the N1 encodes VOT linearly and independently of the category the listener reports, and within-category detail persists at the P3. The continuum is *in the system* roughly 100 ms after stimulus onset. What consciousness receives by default is the category. This places the void in the operation-void family the catalogue already recognises — [fusion](/voids/fusion-void/) (the verdict, never the component estimates), [inference](/voids/inference-void/), [transit](/voids/transit-void/) — but with a distinct shape: fusion combines several estimates into one; categorisation quantises one dimension into few bins, and keeps the un-quantised copy where the readout does not look.

### Evidence for the Limit — and the calibration that shrinks it

The evidence that *some* warping occurs is broad and replicated across five domains (speech, colour, faces, music, laboratory textures) and across behavioural and electrophysiological measures. That is the robust core.

The evidence that the continuum is *inaccessible* has collapsed, and the collapse should be reported as prominently as the core:

- McMurray (2022) states that within speech perception CP "is widely known to be an artifact of task demands", and that the strongest forms have been rejected by fifty years of converging methods. The original Liberman standard — that a subject "can hear no differences among the stimuli beyond those that are revealed by the phoneme labels" — was never met.
- Gerrits & Schouten (2004): the same vowel continuum yields no CP in a four-interval task and full CP in a 2IFC task. The void's measured size depends on how the question is asked.
- Burns & Ward (1978): the musicians' categorical signature "largely disappeared" under an adaptive procedure once performance stabilised.
- Witzel & Gegenfurtner (2011) with 230 observers: colour category effects exist but are not lateralised, removing the neatest link between the colour effect and language processing. Martinovic et al. (2020) found no *sinij/goluboj* boundary advantage and a boundary that drifts with the stimulus distribution.

The pattern matches what the [palette-extension note](/voids/palette-extension-void/) found for a different limit: a void that splits into two questions with opposite fates. *Can consciousness reach the within-category continuum?* Yes — by continuous rating, by the right discrimination task, by training. *Does it reach it by default, and can it tell whether the joints it receives were discovered or installed?* No, and no. The void that survives is a void of default access and of provenance, not of capacity.

### Phenomenology

Approaching this edge feels like nothing, which is the point. A Japanese-speaking adult listening to "right" and "light" does not experience a blur where a distinction should be; the two words simply sound alike. An English speaker listening to Hindi retroflex and dental stops hears one consonant. Neither perceiver is aware of a gap. The occlusion has no felt texture — it presents as the world being simple.

There is one place the void does acquire a texture: during category learning. Logan, Lively & Pisoni (1991) trained Japanese listeners on English /r/–/l/ with high-variability natural tokens across fifteen sessions; identification improved and the improvement held at three months. Reports from such training describe an interval in which the two sounds are known to differ and yet are not heard to differ — the joint is *believed* before it is *perceived*. That interval is the only first-person window onto the operation, and it closes as the category installs. The expertise-side counterpart is Siegel & Siegel's 1977 "Musicians can't tell sharp from flat": six musicians with relative pitch identified in-tune intervals at over 95% accuracy yet could not reliably tell sharp from flat within a category, and afterwards judged 63% of the stimuli to have been in tune when only 23% were. Acquiring the category costs the within-category continuum, as [the expertise void](/voids/expertise-and-its-occlusion/) predicts — and the musicians' post-hoc estimate shows the loss is invisible to the one who suffers it.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

Three exist, and all three work — which is the deflationary finding. (1) Task choice: four-interval and adaptive discrimination procedures recover within-category sensitivity that 2IFC hides. (2) Continuous rating: asking "how /b/-like is this?" rather than "/b/ or /p/?" yields graded responses tracking the acoustics (McMurray's synthesis). (3) Retraining: high-variability phonetic training re-opens a narrowed contrast in adults, incompletely but durably. The continuum is not sealed; it is simply not the default deliverable.

### Indirect Methods

The origin face has no direct method. Whether a felt joint is world-given or installed is inferred only third-personally: by cross-linguistic comparison (Russian versus English blues), by developmental comparison (6-month versus 12-month infants), by expertise comparison (musicians versus non-musicians), or by installing a boundary in the laboratory and watching the ERP shift (Pérez-Gay Juárez et al. 2019). Each method compares the perceiver to a differently carved perceiver. None is available from inside a single perceiver's experience. The context-drift result (Martinovic et al. 2020: the *sinij/goluboj* boundary moves with the lightness distribution of the set) is a usable indirect probe: a boundary that moves with the sample is not a joint in the world.

### What AI Might See

Two asymmetries, one in each direction.

*Against the human.* For a human perceiver the warp is inferable only through psychophysics and averaged ERPs. For a network the warp is directly measurable: representational-similarity analysis can read the compression and expansion off the hidden states layer by layer (Bonnasse-Gahot & Nadal 2022). An artificial mind's categorical joints are, in principle, fully auditable by an outside probe — the origin face is open for inspection in a way it is not for us.

*Against the machine.* Cacioli's (2026, preprint) "structural CP" dissociation — models whose geometry warps at a digit-count boundary but which cannot report the distinction — is, if it replicates, a clean instance of a mind carved along a joint it cannot describe. That is the human origin face reproduced in a system where it can be checked, and it suggests the human case may likewise contain boundaries that shape discrimination without ever surfacing as reportable categories. The preprint is single-authored and unreviewed; the section should carry it as a hypothesis-generator, not evidence.

The residue face has a machine analogue too: a classifier's logits keep graded information its argmax discards. Whether any current system has a "default readout" in the sense that consciousness does — a channel that delivers the argmax and withholds the logits from the same process that acts on them — is not a question the literature found here addresses.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)**, in two registers. First, about the science: "categories all the way down" was the simpler model and it held the field for decades on task artefact (McMurray 2022); the lateralised-Whorf story was elegant and did not survive 230 observers. Second, about the perceiver: a category *is* a parsimony operation — many stimuli, one label — and the simplicity of the perceived joint is not evidence that the world has a joint there. The colour boundary that moves with the stimulus distribution is the demonstration.

### Implications

**Dualism.** Toscano et al. (2010) shows the nervous system's early encoding (linear, category-independent) and the content the subject reports (categorical) coming apart. The Map should record both readings honestly. On the Map's reading, this is a case where the phenomenal deliverable is not a transparent readout of the functional encoding, which is the general shape [the surplus](/voids/the-surplus-void/) and [phenomenal-quality](/voids/phenomenal-quality-void/) discussions rely on. On the physicalist reading it is the opposite: it identifies consciousness with a late, post-N1 categorical stage and explains the void as nothing more than where the readout sits in the pipeline. The data do not adjudicate; they do fix the structure both sides must explain.

**Bidirectional Interaction.** The verbal-dual-task result (Winawer et al. 2007: a concurrent verbal load abolishes the category advantage; a spatial load does not) shows the online warping depends on what the perceiver is concurrently doing with language. That is a modulation of the operation by ongoing cognition, not evidence of non-physical influence, and the note should not be read as claiming more.

**Minimal Quantum Interaction; No Many Worlds.** No bearing found. The note records this rather than stretching.

**Relation to existing voids.** The conjunction with [the vagueness void](/voids/vagueness-void/) is instructive and, as far as this survey found, unremarked in the catalogue: concepts are fuzzy at their edges (the Sorites) while percepts are sharp at theirs (CP). The same mind that cannot say where "heap" ends hears a hard edge between /b/ and /p/ where the acoustics are continuous. The two voids are complementary failures of edge-representation in opposite directions. [The resolution void](/voids/resolution-void/) supplies the compression framing; [the language-thought boundary](/voids/language-thought-boundary/) and the [translation note](/research/voids-translation-void-2026-05-09/) supply the lexical-carving face; [infant-consciousness](/voids/infant-consciousness/) and the [developmental-closure note](/research/voids-developmental-cognitive-closure-2026-02-04/) already carry Werker & Tees, so the narrowing material should cross-reference rather than duplicate.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **Standalone: "The Categorical Perception Void."** Three faces (between, origin, residue) with the deflation built into the structure rather than appended: the strong "cannot hear" claim retired in the lede, the surviving void named as one of default access and provenance. Its distinctive contribution to the catalogue would be the vagueness/CP conjunction — a mind that is vague where it should be sharp and sharp where the world is continuous — plus the first AI case in the catalogue where the origin face is externally auditable (the LLM "structural CP" dissociation, flagged as preprint).
2. **Fold, if the operator prefers absorption.** Between and residue faces into [resolution-void](/voids/resolution-void/) (as a named quantisation mechanism beside the bit-rate compression); origin face into [language-thought-boundary](/voids/language-thought-boundary/) (Whorfian colour, with the Witzel and Martinovic deflations) and [expertise-and-its-occlusion](/voids/expertise-and-its-occlusion/) (musicians; the training window as the only first-person view of installation); the machine asymmetry into [non-human-minds-as-void-explorers](/voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers/).

## Gaps in Research

- No study located asks the phenomenological question directly: when a listener accesses within-category detail via continuous rating, is the difference *experienced* as a difference in the sound, or only judged? The behavioural literature does not separate these.
- The Pérez-Gay Juárez N1 decrease is interpreted as perceptual; the decisional-versus-perceptual reading of learned CP is still contested (Fugate 2013 for faces), and this note does not resolve it.
- The Whorfian colour face is fragile: the lateralisation failed to replicate at scale, and the Russian-blue advantage failed at one of two boundaries. Any article must carry the effect as real-but-modest and must not lean on the lateralised version.
- Cacioli (2026) is a single-author preprint. The "structural CP" dissociation is the most interesting AI result found and the least secure.
- Whether trained phoneticians and musicians who have re-learned to hear within-category differences regain the continuum *phenomenally* or only behaviourally is not addressed in the sources found — the same question the [palette-extension](/voids/palette-extension-void/) verification face left open.
- Not searched: CP for time intervals, for numerosity, for haptic textures; and any literature on whether categorical warping can be *un*-installed in adults rather than overlaid.
