The core question, whether a note's identity is *heard as a quality* or only *recognised by its name*, is **Unexplorable** on present methods: there is no agreed criterion, and the people who have the ability cannot describe it. Around that core sits an **Unexplored** margin (nobody has collected structured introspective reports from adults trained into the ability) and two **Occluded** faces, in the natural rather than deliberate sense of [three-kinds-of-void](/voids/three-kinds-of-void/): a pitch reference shapes what is heard while being absent from what is heard, and it shows up only when one listener is compared with another or with an earlier self.

## Executive Summary

Almost nobody can name a note heard on its own. Absolute pitch (AP), the ability to say "that was an F♯" without a reference tone, occurs in "approximately 1 out of 10,000 people in Western culture" (Hedger et al. 2013, citing Bachem 1955 and Takeuchi & Hulse 1993) [raw]. The research record puts a void inside this rarity, and it has four faces.

1. **The latent face: memory without a quality.** Listeners without AP still store absolute pitch. Untrained adults pick out the original pitch level of familiar TV theme music from versions shifted by one or two semitones. Schellenberg & Trehub (2003) conclude that "ordinary listeners retain fine-grained information about pitch level over extended periods" and that adults' "reportedly poor memory for pitch is likely to be a by-product of their inability to name isolated pitches" [abstract]. Ordinary listeners carry the information but hear no note-identity. The effect is smaller than first reported: a six-lab replication of Levitin (1994) found 25% of singers hit the original pitch at least once, against Levitin's 44% (Frieler et al. 2013) [abstract].
2. **The template face: a hidden absolute deciding a relation.** In Deutsch's tritone paradox, the same pair of tones is heard as rising by some listeners and as falling by others, and each hears it plainly. Deutsch reads the result as showing that "the large majority of us exhibit a form of absolute pitch in making judgments of these patterns" [raw]. On her account an acquired pitch-class template, linked to the speech of one's community, decides what counts as up. She also states the void outright: "Musical discourse is not precise or accurate enough for such perceptual differences to become apparent through normal communication, and it is only in the laboratory that we can develop a clear idea of what the listener really perceives" [raw]. The template account is **contested**. Repp (1994) found "no within-group correlation" with voice range [abstract]. Some cross-language comparisons failed [secondary, Malek & Sperschneider 2018]. Prior context alone "almost fully determines the perceived direction" of such ambiguous pairs (Chambers et al. 2017) [raw].
3. **The report face: a quality that cannot be described.** Those who have AP cannot say what it is like. Miyazaki, who has studied AP for decades, writes that "it is not known exactly what it is like to experience pitch chroma", because non-possessors cannot recognise chroma and "there are no convincing reports on the nature of pitch chromas from the very few absolute pitch possessors" [raw]. Asked directly, his participants "gave no informative answers except that many of them told that individual tones are always associated with the spoken sol-fa syllables" [raw]. He concludes that, apart from the attached syllables, "absolute pitch chromas seem to be only weak qualia" [raw].
4. **The calibration face: an absolute standard that moves with its input.** Thirteen adults with AP heard Brahms's First Symphony detuned flat by about 2 cents a minute until it sat 33 cents low. "No participant reported any detection of the pitch shift when questioned after the experiment" [raw]. Afterwards, flat notes sounded more in tune and in-tune notes less (Hedger et al. 2013) [raw]. Pitch-naming in AP also drifts sharp with age (Athos et al. 2007) [abstract]. The possessor's only reference is the category itself, and the category is what moves.

**Why the void stays dark.** Adults *can* be trained to name all twelve pitches at AP-level accuracy: "14% of the participants (6 out of 43)" did so after 12 to 40 hours (Wong et al. 2020) [raw]. That would be the natural experiment, but the trainers themselves concede that "there has not been an agreed upon way to ascertain whether an individual perceives every pitch in an absolute manner or not", so whether the trainees acquired "genuine AP" "remains an untestable hypothesis" [raw]. What separates a heard chroma from a fast, accurate label is the very thing nobody knows how to test.

**Calibration for the Map.** The void is neutral between dualism and physicalism. A physicalist at full strength says there is no further fact: AP is pitch memory plus labelling, "C-ness" is the functional profile of a category, and the "weak qualia" report is what that predicts (see the physicalist section below). The dualist and the physicalist describe the same blank. What the void adds to the catalogue is unusual: a dimension of experience where (i) the information is demonstrably present in nearly everyone, (ii) the quality's existence is disputed among those said to have it, and (iii) a trainable route in exists whose result cannot be certified. No tenet is upgraded by it.

## Key Sources

### Deutsch (1991), "The Tritone Paradox: An Influence of Language on Music Perception", *Music Perception* 8(4): 335–347, doi:10.2307/40285517 [raw: JSTOR scan, full text saved and converted]
- **Design**: Shepard tones ("well-defined in terms of pitch class ... but ... poorly defined in terms of height" [raw]) presented as tritone pairs. Two groups: 24 who grew up in California, 12 who grew up in southern England. Peak pitch classes fell in the earlier study's half of the circle for "Twenty-one of the 24 Californian subjects" but only three of the 12 English subjects (p < .001, Fisher exact) [raw].
- **Key quotes**: "listeners disagree substantially as to whether a given tone pair forms an ascending or a descending pattern" [raw]; the findings indicate "that the same, culturally acquired representation of pitch classes influences both speech production and perception of this musical pattern" [raw]; in the parent population "no correlate with musical training was obtained" [raw]; "although absolute pitch is generally considered a rare faculty, the large majority of us exhibit a form of absolute pitch in making judgments of these patterns, in that we hear notes as higher or as lower depending essentially on their pitch classes" [raw; the sentence runs across pp. 345–346 and was verified in two halves either side of the page header]; the closing quote above on musical discourse and the laboratory [raw].
- **Caution**: small groups; one laboratory's paradigm; selected listeners (no more than six errors out of 48 on a sinusoidal pretest).
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits, provisionally. The simplest description of ordinary hearing (relations only) would hide an absolute anchor, *if* the template account holds.

### Deutsch (1986), "A Musical Paradox", *Music Perception* 3(3): 275–280, doi:10.2307/40285337 [abstract]
- "the pattern in any given key is heard as ascending by some listeners but as descending by others" [abstract]. This is the first report of the paradox.

### Deutsch, North & Ray (1990), *Music Perception* 7(4): 371–384, doi:10.2307/40285473 [abstract]; Deutsch, Henthorn & Dolson (2004), *Music Perception* 21(3): 357–372, doi:10.1525/mp.2004.21.3.357 [abstract]; Ragozzine & Deutsch (1994), *Music Perception* 12(2): 213–225, doi:10.2307/40285652 [abstract]
- 1990: a correlation "between perception of the tritone paradox and the range of fundamental frequencies of the listener's speaking voice" (paraphrased from the abstract). 2004: perception "can be heavily influenced by speech heard early in life, even for listeners who do not speak their first language fluently" [abstract]. 1994: "regional differences in perception of the tritone paradox do indeed exist within the United States" (Mahoning/Trumbull counties, Ohio, plus a familial-background effect) [abstract].

### Repp (1994), "The Tritone Paradox and the Pitch Range of the Speaking Voice: A Dubious Connection", *Music Perception* 12(2): 227–255, doi:10.2307/40285653 [abstract]; Deutsch (1994), "Reply to Repp", *Music Perception* 12(2): 257–263, doi:10.2307/40285654 [abstract]
- Repp: with Dutch, British and American listeners, "There was no within-group correlation of perceptual results with individual differences in voice range" [abstract]. Envelope effects were large enough to "casts doubt on the notion of stable individual pitch templates" [abstract, verbatim including the grammatical slip]. A British–American difference appeared on one of two tests.
- Deutsch's reply calls the findings "uninterpretable in relation to" hers, on grounds of population, envelope and voice-range method (paraphrase of abstract). **The dispute is live and unresolved in the sources read.**

### Malek & Sperschneider (2018), "Aftereffects of Spectrally Similar and Dissimilar Spectral Motion Adaptors in the Tritone Paradox", *Frontiers in Psychology* 9: 677, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00677 [raw: full text PDF]
- A review paragraph useful for calibration. It counts replications of the language link (a Canadian sample, Dawe et al. 1998; samples outside the US, Chalikia and colleagues) and also reports that "some studies failed to reveal systematic differences in subjectively highest Shepard tones for different linguistic backgrounds" [raw], naming Krüger & Lukas (2002, German vs Japanese) and Repp (1994). It sets Deutsch's template account against a "psycho-acoustic approach", Terhardt's (1991) virtual-pitch theory and Malek (2013), which explains the patterns by the pitch-height ambiguity of the tones themselves [secondary]. Its own experiment found contrastive aftereffects from rising or falling adaptor scales.

### Chambers, Akram, Adam, Pelofi, Sahani, Shamma & Pressnitzer (2017), "Prior context in audition informs binding and shapes simple features", *Nature Communications* 8: 15027, doi:10.1038/ncomms15027 [raw: UCL Discovery first page, abstract]
- With ambiguous Shepard-type pairs, "presenting context tones before the ambiguous pair almost fully determines the perceived direction of shift" [raw]. A short-term account competes with or adds to a long-term template.

### Levitin (1994), "Absolute memory for musical pitch: Evidence from the production of learned melodies", *Perception & Psychophysics* 56: 414–423, doi:10.3758/bf03206733 [metadata; the Cogprints copy was bot-walled]; Frieler, Fischinger, Schlemmer, Lothwesen, Jakubowski & Müllensiefen (2013), *Musicae Scientiae* 17(3): 334–349, doi:10.1177/1029864913493802 [abstract]
- Levitin's result, as reported by the replication: "Forty-four percent of participants sang the correct pitch on at least one of two trials, and 12% were correct on both trials" [abstract, Frieler et al.].
- Replication across six labs (N = 277): "Twenty-five percent of the participants sang the exact pitch of at least one of the two chosen songs and 4% hit the right pitches for both songs"; "the estimated overall effect using meta-analysis techniques was significantly smaller than Levitin's original result" [abstract]. Levitin's commentary (doi:10.1177/1029864913490633) [metadata + abstract opening] welcomed the replication.

### Schellenberg & Trehub (2003), "Good Pitch Memory Is Widespread", *Psychological Science* 14: 262–266, doi:10.1111/1467-9280.03432 [abstract]
- Unselected college students heard 5-second excerpts of familiar TV soundtracks at original pitch or shifted by 1 or 2 semitones and "successfully identified the original pitch levels"; controls with unfamiliar recordings could not [abstract, paraphrase plus the two quotes in the summary].

### Kim & Knösche (2017), "On the Perceptual Subprocess of Absolute Pitch", *Frontiers in Neuroscience* 11: 557, doi:10.3389/fnins.2017.00557 [raw: full text PDF]
- Separates a perceptual subprocess ("processing auditory input to extract a pitch chroma") from an associative one ("linking an auditory representation of pitch chroma with a verbal/non-verbal label") [raw]. It reports two models of the perceptual stage: absolute pitch categorisation, unique to possessors, versus absolute pitch memory, "rooted in a common phenomenon ... only with heightened efficiency" [raw].
- Quotes Levitin & Rogers (2005): "AP consists of 'pitch memory,' which is widespread in the population, and 'pitch labeling,' which is possessed exclusively by persons with AP" [secondary: verified in Kim & Knösche; Levitin & Rogers, *TICS* 9(1): 26–33, doi:10.1016/j.tics.2004.11.007, is metadata only].
- Two calibrations against overreading the latent face: "the accuracy of APM in non-musicians seems to be still far lower compared to that in musicians with AP", and the singing studies "do not reveal how precise APM in non-AP population is" [raw].

### Miyazaki (2019), "Pitch Chromas and Scale-Degree Qualia: Reconsidering Absolute Pitch and Relative Pitch", Acoustical Society of Japan, Hearing Research Meeting materials, Vol. 49 No. 8, H-2019-109 [raw: author's PDF, Niigata University]
- Defines chroma as "tone quality that recurs at intervals of octaves", in "Pitch chroma, a term coined by Bachem (1937, 1950)" [raw; the coinage attribution is Miyazaki's and was not checked against Bachem]. He insists that absolute-pitch chroma "is not a universal perceptual attribute everyone can perceive, but an idiosyncratic one limited to persons having absolute pitch" [raw].
- Contrasts it with **scale-degree qualia**, the felt character of a tone's place in a key, a term he credits to Huron (2006) "borrowing from philosophers". These, unlike chroma, show "a remarkable level of agreement" across listeners [raw, Miyazaki reporting Huron and Arthur 2018].
- The report-face quotes in the summary [raw]. A pianist reported that piano tones "sound as accompanied by spoken syllables, 'do, re, mi'" [raw], which Miyazaki calls "strange" because the tones have no spectral similarity with the syllables.
- Cross-cultural result (abstract): Japanese music students had "an advantage in pitch chroma identification but had a disadvantage in scale-degree identification" [raw].
- **Status**: a conference-proceedings paper, not peer-reviewed in the journal sense. It is the only source this run found that addresses what chroma is like, and it comes from a leading AP researcher.

### Miyazaki (1993), "Absolute Pitch as an Inability: Identification of Musical Intervals in a Tonal Context", *Music Perception* 11(1): 55–71, doi:10.2307/40285599 [abstract]
- "some absolute pitch listeners are relatively poor in identifying pitch relations in tonal contexts and sometimes tend to stick to absolute pitch even in a task that needs relative pitch" [abstract]. They were worse in F♯ major and an out-of-tune E major than in C major. Possessing the absolute can cost the relational.

### Takeuchi & Hulse (1993), "Absolute pitch", *Psychological Bulletin* 113(2): 345–361, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.113.2.345 [abstract]
- "AP possessors' memory for pitches is mediated by verbal pitch names; they do not have superior memory for pitches per se" [abstract]. Early-learning theory: learnable "up to about age 6", after which a "developmental shift from perceiving individual features to perceiving relations" makes AP "difficult or impossible to acquire" [abstract].

### Hedger, Heald & Nusbaum (2013), "Absolute Pitch May Not Be So Absolute", *Psychological Science* 24(8): 1496–1502, doi:10.1177/0956797612473310 [raw: author PDF]
- Thirteen participants verified at the highest AP level on the UCSF online test. Brahms Symphony No. 1 was detuned over the first movement (about 15 minutes) at "approximately 2 cents per minute", then held 33 cents flat for about 30 minutes [raw]. "No participant reported any detection of the pitch shift when questioned after the experiment" [raw]. In Experiment 2 (14 participants), "No participants commented on the detuned nature of the pieces" [raw]. The intonation shift generalised to unheard notes but not to an unheard timbre [raw, paraphrase].
- Conclusion: "the apparent stability of AP categories is conferred not by early experience but rather by the cultural norms adopted for tuning music" [raw].
- **Caution**: the dependent measure is intonation ratings on a 1–3 scale, and the shift was modest (flat notes 2.08 → 2.31) [raw]. The categories moved; they did not move by 33 cents.

### Athos et al. (2007), "Dichotomy and perceptual distortions in absolute pitch ability", *PNAS* 104(37): 14795–14800, doi:10.1073/pnas.0703868104 [abstract]; Vernon (1977), "Absolute pitch: A case study", *British Journal of Psychology* 68(4): 485–489, doi:10.1111/j.2044-8295.1977.tb01619.x [abstract]
- Athos: web survey of 2,213, a bimodal distribution, a "perceptual magnet" at A, and "a gradual decline in pitch-naming accuracy with age, characterized by a perceptual shift in the 'sharp' direction" [abstract; the inner quotation marks are the abstract's].
- Vernon, a psychologist writing of himself: AP from about 17 to 52; "He then started to hear all music one semitone too high, and now at the age of 71 it is heard a full tone above the true pitch" [abstract]. He located the change in the cochlea rather than in memory [abstract, paraphrase]. He found the shift because he knew what was being played, an external check.

### Wong, Lui, Yip & Wong (2020), "Is it impossible to acquire absolute pitch in adulthood?", *Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics* 82: 1407–1430, doi:10.3758/s13414-019-01869-3 [raw: publisher PDF]
- Three experiments, 12–40 hours of gamified training: "14% of the participants (6 out of 43) were able to name twelve pitches at 90% or above accuracy" [raw].
- The section "Did the training lead to 'genuine AP'?" concedes that trainees may have used internally generated references ("quasi-AP"). It states that "to date there has not been an agreed upon way to ascertain whether an individual perceives every pitch in an absolute manner or not" and that the assumption of genuine AP "remains an untestable hypothesis" [raw]. A grep of the full text for `subjective`, `phenomen`, `qualia`, `chroma`, `feel` and `introspect` found **no** collection of trainees' reports of what changed. That is the unexplored margin.

### Deutsch, Henthorn, Marvin & Xu (2006), *JASA* 119(2): 719–722, doi:10.1121/1.2151799 [abstract]; Gervain et al. (2013), "Valproate reopens critical-period learning of absolute pitch", *Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience* 7: 102, doi:10.3389/fnsys.2013.00102 [abstract]
- Deutsch 2006: prevalence "far greater among the Chinese than the U.S. students for each level of age of onset", and "The findings suggest that the potential for acquiring absolute pitch may be universal" [abstract].
- Gervain 2013: "adult men who took valproate (VPA) (a HDAC inhibitor) learned to identify pitch significantly better than those taking placebo" [abstract]. One trial, men only; the sample size was not read this run. Treat as a lead for the reopenability of the closure, not a result.

### Di Stefano & Spence (2024), "Should absolute pitch be considered as a unique kind of absolute sensory judgment in humans?", *Cognition* 249: 105805, doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2024.105805 [abstract]
- PRISMA systematic review comparing AP with synaesthesia, eidetic memory and colour perception. It concludes that "absolute pitch does indeed appear to constitute a unique kind of absolute sensory judgment in humans" [abstract]. This bears on the obvious analogy: AP possessors are often said to hear notes the way everyone sees colours, and the review argues the analogy fails.

### Bermudez & Zatorre (2009), *Music Perception* 27(2): 89–101, doi:10.1525/mp.2009.27.2.89 [abstract]
- A computerised test on 51 musicians showed "a range of performance, from perfect to random, including a substantial number of intermediate levels of proficiency" [abstract, paraphrase]. Against Athos's bimodality, the boundary of the ability is itself graded in this sample.

### Kim, Salamon, Li & Bello (2018), "CREPE: A Convolutional Representation for Pitch Estimation", ICASSP 2018: 161–165, doi:10.1109/icassp.2018.8461329 [abstract]
- A deep network "that operates directly on the time-domain waveform" to estimate fundamental frequency [abstract]. Used below only as an instance of machine pitch tracking.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Pitch has two components that music psychology has long separated: **height** (continuous, low to high) and **chroma** (circular, the C-ness that recurs at each octave). Ordinary listeners hear height and relations. They hear a melody as the same melody in any key, and they hear a tonic as stable and a leading tone as pulling, the scale-degree qualia that Miyazaki reports listeners agree on. What they do not hear, on the standard account, is the chroma of a lone tone. The void is the conjunction of four facts about that missing dimension.

- It is **represented** in nearly everyone (the latent face), and on one reading it may even be **used** in perception (the template face).
- Where it is **accessible** (AP), its **phenomenal character cannot be reported** beyond the label it travels with (the report face).
- Its **reference is unanchored from inside**: when the standard moves, the possessor cannot see it move (the calibration face).
- **No criterion** separates hearing chroma from rapidly retrieving a name (Wong et al.'s "untestable hypothesis").

Each fact alone is a known finding. Together they form a dark region with a definite shape: a dimension whose information is present, whose use is debated, and whose *quality*, the thing that would make it part of experience rather than of processing, cannot be established by those who lack it, those who have it, or those who train it.

### Evidence for the Limit (genuine boundary versus difficult territory)

- **The information is there, the quality is not reported.** The latent face's evidence is robust in direction but smaller than first reported: 44% falls to 25% in the replication, with large lab-to-lab variation. Kim & Knösche warn that non-AP pitch memory is "still far lower" in accuracy. So the claim is "ordinary listeners hold *some* absolute pitch information without note-identity", not "everyone secretly has absolute pitch".
- **Possessors cannot say what it is like.** Miyazaki's report is the clearest datum. It is one researcher's long experience, so it is a qualitative report about reports. Takeuchi & Hulse's finding that possessors' pitch memory "is mediated by verbal pitch names" points the same way. The ability is hard to separate from the name even in memory tasks.
- **No criterion exists.** Wong et al.'s concession is the strongest evidence that the boundary is principled on present methods. They had reasons to claim their trainees acquired "genuine" AP and declined to, because the field has no test for it. Response-time thresholds were considered and judged "elusive" [raw].
- **The boundary is not fixed.** Training reached AP-level naming in 14% of adults. Prevalence varies with tone language and onset age. Valproate may reopen the window (a lead). This is evidence *against* reading the void as unexplorable for adults. The ability can be entered. What cannot be certified is what was entered.

### Phenomenology

From the non-possessor's side, the approach to the edge is a blank with no felt edge. A single sustained tone sounds like *a* note, never *this* note. Nothing in the experience signals a missing dimension, which is [inventory-blindness](/concepts/inventory-blindness/) in its plainest form. Yet the same listener notices when a familiar recording is played a semitone off, without being able to say what changed (the Schellenberg & Trehub design). The trace is felt as *wrongness* without being heard as *identity*.

From the possessor's side, the reported phenomenology is a name that arrives with the tone: "do, re, mi" heard as if spoken with the piano note, plus an inability to switch it off. Possessors have reported that transposed music sounds wrong (Miyazaki 1993 shows the cost behaviourally). What possessors do *not* deliver is a description of the chroma itself. Miyazaki's phrase "only weak qualia" is the honest summary.

The tritone-paradox listener has a third phenomenology: a percept that is completely obvious (this pair goes *up*) and is reversed for a neighbour, with nothing in either percept marking it as listener-relative. Deutsch's remark that ordinary musical talk is too imprecise for the difference to show is the phenomenology of a void that only comparison reveals.

## What a Physicalist Account Predicts (calibration, at full strength)

The strongest physicalist reading dissolves the void into psychophysics and memory.

1. **There is no chroma quale over and above the category.** AP is long-term pitch memory with narrow categories plus a label mapping, the two-component model in Levitin & Rogers' form. "Hearing C-ness" just *is* the automatic activation of the C category and its label. Miyazaki's finding that possessors can report only the syllable is the *prediction*: there is nothing else to report. The "untestable hypothesis" is untestable because it has no content beyond performance.
2. **The latent face is ordinary implicit memory.** Recognition without recall or labelling is everywhere in memory research. Absolute pitch traces in non-possessors are weak, noisy categories that fail the naming threshold, as Kim & Knösche's accuracy data show.
3. **The template face is either a learned prior or a psychoacoustic artefact.** On Deutsch's account a statistical prior over pitch classes, learned from speech, sets the pitch-class circle's orientation. On Terhardt's or Malek's it is spectral-envelope and virtual-pitch ambiguity. Chambers et al. show recent context can dominate either. None of these needs anything unobservable, and the listener-relativity is the same kind of fact as dialect-dependent vowel categories.
4. **The calibration face is expected adaptive plasticity.** Categories that track their input statistics will drift with input. Hedger et al.'s own conclusion is that tuning norms, not early experience, keep them stable.
5. **The prediction that would embarrass this reading.** If trained adults who meet AP accuracy consistently reported, unprompted, a new sensory character that possessors recognised as their own, *and* that report dissociated from response speed and accuracy, the functional reading would owe an account of a reportable residue. No such data exist.

**Verdict.** At full strength the physicalist reading explains every datum in this note. What it explains is the ability; what it denies is a further fact. The void then sits exactly where the [access/phenomenal](/concepts/access-consciousness/) dispute sits, with one advantage over the usual cases: here the access side is *measured* (naming accuracy, memory precision, drift rates), so the remaining question is cleanly isolated. A dualist reading gains a well-instrumented case of the dispute and no evidence for its side.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

- **Adult training with structured introspection.** Wong et al.'s protocol already produces AP-level naming in some adults. Adding pre-registered experience sampling before, during and after training, asking whether single tones acquire a character apart from a name, would give the first first-person data from people who crossed the boundary. This is [palette-extension-void](/voids/palette-extension-void/)'s "report-category migration" design applied to a case where the endpoint is measurable.
- **Dissociation tests.** Look for a chroma effect that *cannot* be mediated by the label: for example, interference from tone chroma on a non-musical task in possessors who cannot verbalise during it, or chroma-similarity judgements under articulatory suppression. A residue that survives label suppression would favour the categorisation model over the label model.

### Indirect Methods

- **The tritone paradox as an assay.** Whatever its origin, the paradox measures an orientation the listener cannot introspect. Mapping one's own orientation, and a partner's, is one of the few ways to *see* an absolute pitch reference one cannot hear.
- **Drift probes.** Hedger et al.'s design measures how far a possessor's standard moves with exposure. Repeated over months, it would chart how anchored the "absolute" really is, and whether possessors ever notice.
- **Cross-cultural comparison.** Tone-language prevalence (Deutsch 2006), the Japanese chroma-for-scale-degree trade-off (Miyazaki 2019) and dialect effects on the paradox make pitch reference a variable that differs between communities. The void can be triangulated by populations rather than by introspection.

### What AI Might See

Machine pitch trackers estimate fundamental frequency in absolute units by construction (CREPE "operates directly on the time-domain waveform" [abstract]). For such a system absolute pitch is the default, and transposition-invariance, the thing every human listener has for free, must be engineered or learned. The asymmetry is inverted. This cannot answer the human question, since nothing suggests a pitch tracker hears anything. It does separate *having the absolute information* from *having a heard quality* in an extreme form: a system with perfect absolute pitch and no question of chroma qualia. A language model asked what C-ness is like can only recombine possessors' reports, and the report face says those reports are nearly empty. AI here offers no special access, only a clean contrast case for [non-human-minds-as-void-explorers](/voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers/).

## Whether the Void Is Real

Three verdicts are possible:

- **Merely unexplored.** The core question would be answered by the direct methods above, so the darkness reflects the absence of a study, not a principled limit. Possible. Wong et al.'s concession is about the *field's* current tools.
- **Unexplorable in principle.** If phenomenal character outruns any functional measure, then no performance test can tell a heard chroma from a fast label. The question then has the shape of the inverted-spectrum problem, restricted to a single dimension.
- **No void (deflation).** If the physicalist reading is right, there is no further fact and the void is a pseudo-question.

The evidence supports the first verdict for the *margin* and leaves the second and third open for the core. The template face is the most fragile: if the psychoacoustic account defeats the template account, the "hidden absolute deciding a relation" becomes "stimulus ambiguity resolved by context", and that face should be dropped from any article.

## Four-Criteria Calibration ([apophatic-cartography-four-criteria](/concepts/apophatic-cartography-four-criteria/))

One grader and no pre-registered rubric; these grades are judgement.

- **Signature specificity, moderate-to-strong.** The breakdown pattern is distinctive: information present without quality (latent), quality present without description (report), a reference invisible in its percept (template, contested), and a standard that moves unseen (calibration).
- **Structured persistence, moderate.** The perceptual-versus-labelling question is old in the field (Ward and Burns 1982 per Kim & Knösche; Siegel 1974) and is restated as open in 2017, 2019 and 2020 [raw]. Wong et al. ran the obvious dissolving experiment, and it did not dissolve the question.
- **Cross-observer convergence, moderate.** Independent laboratories (San Diego, Chicago, Niigata, Hong Kong, Leipzig, Toronto, six European replication labs) converge on the latent and calibration faces. The template face is one laboratory's account against several dissents.
- **Framework independence: high on the findings, low on interpretive weight.** Nothing here depends on the Map's tenets. The physicalist explains every finding, and the void's residue is the access/phenomenal question, which the dualist cannot claim as evidence.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet: Occam's Razor Has Limits

The simplest model of ordinary hearing, "humans hear pitch relationally", is wrong as a model of what is *stored* (the latent face). If Deutsch is right, it is also wrong about what is *used* (the template face). And the simplest model of AP, "possessors hear notes as everyone hears colours", is disputed by the systematic review (Di Stefano & Spence 2024). Both simplifications hide structure. The lesson is a modest one: simplicity misleads about which dimensions a percept carries. Here, unusually, parsimony may also be the *right* answer for the core question, since the label model is simpler and explains the report face. The tenet licenses suspicion of the relational simplification, not confidence in a chroma quale.

### Dualism

The void gives the [qualia](/concepts/qualia/) debate a well-instrumented test case. The functional side (memory, labelling, drift) is measurable, so the phenomenal question is isolated more cleanly than in most cases. It does **not** supply evidence for dualism. A physicalist predicts the "weak qualia" report. If future structured-introspection data from trained adults showed a reportable residue dissociated from performance, that would bear on the dispute. That is a conditional, not a finding.

### Bidirectional Interaction

Adult AP acquisition is attention-driven perceptual learning. The Map's attention-as-interface reading ([consciousness-as-perceptual-architect](/topics/consciousness-as-perceptual-architect/)) can describe it, and so can ordinary plasticity. No bearing beyond that.

### Minimal Quantum Interaction

No bearing.

### No Many Worlds

No direct bearing. The template face concerns listener-relativity of a percept within one world, a between-minds fact, not an indexical one.

### Implications

The chroma void is a calibration exhibit for the catalogue. It is a void with a working entrance (training), a measured trace (pitch memory, drift), a living dispute (template versus psychoacoustics), and a core that the entrance cannot illuminate. It strengthens the catalogue's discipline more than its metaphysics. It also gives [palette-extension-void](/voids/palette-extension-void/)'s certification problem a second, more measurable case.

## Gaps in Research

- **No structured first-person data from trained adults.** This is the decisive gap, and the full-text grep of Wong et al. found none. A literature search for introspective reports in other adult AP training studies was not run.
- **Template account status.** Post-2018 tests of the speech-template hypothesis with individual-differences measures were not found this run, though one 2010s neural study was seen only in a search listing. The Krüger & Lukas failed replication was not read.
- **Levitin & Rogers (2005) and Levitin (1994) full texts** were not obtained; both are cited through abstracts or secondary quotation.
- **Possessor testimony beyond Miyazaki** (Sacks; first-person accounts by musicians) was not surveyed. Miyazaki's "no informative answers" is one researcher's experience and should be presented as such.
- **Philosophical literature.** No philosophy-of-perception paper on AP chroma as a quality was found. Raffman's work on musical ineffability (1993) is the likely nearest neighbour but was not read. The note's philosophical framing (access/phenomenal) is the researcher's, not a cited author's.
- **Possibly unanswerable**: whether a fast, automatic, accurate category with an attached name *differs* from a heard quality. If it does not, the void dissolves. If it does, no current method can show it.
