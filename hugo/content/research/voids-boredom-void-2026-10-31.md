## Executive Summary

Boredom is the commonest place where consciousness reports on its own engagement, and the report is structured so that its owner cannot read what it is reporting. The best-supported psychological definition treats boredom as "the aversive experience of wanting, but being unable, to engage in satisfying activity" (Eastwood et al. 2012) [abstract]. Four faces of the void follow. (1) **Objectless signal**: boredom demands a change of engagement without supplying a target. On the opportunity-cost model it "signals the value of exploration" (Agrawal et al. 2022) [abstract], and the value of exploring cannot name what exploring will find. Tolstoy put the same shape in one phrase, "a desire for desires" (1877). (2) **Projection**: the verdict arrives as a property of the object ("this is boring"). Yet the attentional definition builds in attributing the cause "to the environment" (Eastwood et al. 2012), and the MAC model traces boredom to two independent mismatches, of attention and of meaning, with opposite attentional causes (understimulation and overstimulation) that produce the same verdict (Westgate & Wilson 2018). (3) **Self-supply and forecast**: untrained minds struggle to occupy themselves with thought for 6–15 minutes, and in one study 12 of 18 men gave themselves at least one electric shock during a 15-minute thinking period (Wilson et al. 2014; replicated across 11 countries by Buttrick et al. 2019). People also predict that just thinking will be less engaging than it turns out to be, and they avoid it on that forecast (Hatano et al. 2022). (4) **Examination**: under the attentional definition, engaged inquiry into a bout of boredom supplies the engagement whose absence defines the bout, so the state does not survive sustained first-person study intact. Under the MAC meaning component this inference weakens. A fifth, subsidiary face is boredom's drag on felt duration. It belongs with the [temporal-void](/voids/temporal-void/) material. Pascal (1670) anticipated faces 2 and 3: weariness arises "even without any cause", and diversion "principally hinders us from reflecting upon ourselves". The tenet relations are mostly *compatible*. The one firm point is against epiphenomenalism: boredom's felt aversiveness is tightly calibrated to the value of disengaging, which is James's (1890) fit argument in a new case.

## Key Sources

### Eastwood, Frischen, Fenske & Smilek (2012), "The Unengaged Mind: Defining Boredom in Terms of Attention", *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 7(5): 482–495, doi:10.1177/1745691612456044 (PMID 26168505)
- Synthesises psychodynamic, existential, arousal and cognitive theories. They argue boredom is "universally conceptualized as 'the aversive experience of wanting, but being unable, to engage in satisfying activity.'" [abstract]. The "universally" is their synthesis claim, not an independent finding.
- Their definition has three parts: (a) failure to engage attention with internal or external information "required for participating in satisfying activity"; (b) the subject "are focused on the fact that we are not able to engage attention and participate in satisfying activity"; (c) the subject "attribute[s] the cause of our aversive state to the environment" [abstract; the bracketed inflection is mine].
- **Use**: anchors faces 1, 2 and 4. Part (c) is a *definitional* component, so the article must not present it as an empirical finding of misattribution.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral. The authors are cognitive psychologists. Do not enlist them.

### Westgate & Wilson (2018), "Boring thoughts and bored minds: The MAC model of boredom and cognitive engagement", *Psychological Review* 125(5): 689–713, doi:10.1037/rev0000097
- Defines boredom as "an affective indicator of unsuccessful attentional engagement in valued goal-congruent activity" [abstract].
- The four novel predictions they report support for: "Deficits in attention and meaning each produce boredom independently of the other"; "there are different profiles of boredom that result from specific deficits in attention and meaning"; "boredom results from two types of attentional deficits, understimulation and overstimulation"; and the model explains "when and why people become bored with their own thoughts" [abstract].
- **Use**: face 2 (one verdict, several causes, including opposite attentional ones) and the scoping of face 4. *Caution*: they report "different profiles", so the article must not claim the causes are experientially indistinguishable. Whether people can identify the operative deficit from the feel alone is the Unexplored margin; no test was found.

### Agrawal, Mattar, Cohen & Daw (2022), "The temporal dynamics of opportunity costs: A normative account of cognitive fatigue and boredom", *Psychological Review* 129(3): 564–585, doi:10.1037/rev0000309
- They set out "to flesh out previous suggestions that feelings associated with subjective effort, like cognitive fatigue and boredom, are the phenomenological counterparts of these opportunity cost measures"; "Fatigue reflects the value of offline computation (internal to the organism) to improve future decisions, while boredom signals the value of exploration (external in the world)." [abstract]
- **Originator credit**: by their own account they are formalising "previous suggestions". The opportunity-cost reading of *effort* is Kurzban et al. (2013), below. Present Agrawal et al. as the formalisation for boredom, not as the origin of the idea.
- **Use**: face 1. Exploration value is value assigned to options not yet sampled, so a signal of it cannot specify its target. If the target were specified, choosing it would be exploitation. This is an in-principle claim *on this model*, argued from the definition of exploration. It does not hold on every theory.

### Kurzban, Duckworth, Kable & Myers (2013), "An opportunity cost model of subjective effort and task performance", *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 36(6): 661–679, doi:10.1017/S0140525X12003196
- "the phenomenology of effort can be understood as the felt output of these cost/benefit computations" [abstract].
- Whether the BBS target article itself discusses boredom was **not verified** (full text not read). Cite it for effort only.

### Bench & Lench (2013), "On the Function of Boredom", *Behavioral Sciences* 3(3): 459–472, doi:10.3390/bs3030459; Elpidorou (2014), "The bright side of boredom", *Frontiers in Psychology* 5: 1245, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01245; Elpidorou (2018), "The good of boredom", *Philosophical Psychology* 31(3): 323–351, doi:10.1080/09515089.2017.1346240
- Bench & Lench: "boredom motivates pursuit of new goals when the previous goal is no longer beneficial" [abstract].
- Elpidorou 2014: "It acts as a regulatory state that keeps one in line with one's projects." [raw, Frontiers full text]
- Elpidorou 2018: boredom is "a regulatory psychological state that has the capacity to promote our well-being" [abstract].
- **Originator credit**: these papers are the functional accounts this note verified. Priority among functional accounts was not established, so do not call any of them "the" originator.
- **Use**: they refute the old note's "no adaptive function" claim. They also sharpen face 1: a functional signal whose function is to *send you elsewhere* need not, and on the exploration reading cannot, say where.

### Wilson, Reinhard, Westgate, Gilbert, Ellerbeck, Hahn, Brown & Shaked (2014), "Just think: The challenges of the disengaged mind", *Science* 345(6192): 75–77, doi:10.1126/science.1250830 (PMC4330241)
- "In 11 studies, we found that participants typically did not enjoy spending 6 to 15 minutes in a room by themselves with nothing to do but think" [abstract].
- Shock study: "67% of men (12 of 18) gave themselves at least one shock during the thinking period", compared with "25% of women (6 of 24" [raw]. The counts include only participants who had said they would pay to avoid the shock [raw]. **The N is small; the article should give the counts, not just the percentages.**
- Pooled across six studies, 57.5% reported it was difficult to concentrate and "their mind wandered (89.0% responded at or above the midpoint of the scale), even though there was nothing competing for their attention" [raw].
- "Without such training, people prefer doing to thinking, even if what they are doing is so unpleasant that they would normally pay to avoid it. The untutored mind does not like to be alone with itself." [raw]. The "training" refers to meditation and similar techniques in the preceding sentence.
- The paper does **not** cite Pascal: `grep -c Pascal` on the full text including references returns 0. Its test of Pascal's fr. 131 situation is therefore at least formally independent of him.

### Buttrick, Choi, Wilson, Oishi, Boker, Gilbert, Alper et al. (2019), "Cross-cultural consistency and relativity in the enjoyment of thinking versus doing", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 117(5): e71–e83, doi:10.1037/pspp0000198
- 2,557 participants from 12 sites in 11 countries; "The results were consistent in every country" [abstract].
- Country differences in enjoyment of thinking were accounted for by individual differences. Four correlated positively: "need for cognition, openness to experience, meditation experience, and initial positive affect". One correlated negatively: "(reported phone usage)" [abstract].
- **Use**: face 3 is robust across cultures *and* trainable. That bounds any absence claim: the limit belongs to the untrained default, not to minds as such.

### Hatano, Ogulmus, Shigemasu & Murayama (2022), "Thinking about thinking: People underestimate how enjoyable and engaging just waiting is", *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 151(12): 3213–3229, doi:10.1037/xge0001255
- N = 259 university students, six experiments: "participants' predicted enjoyment and engagement for the waiting task were significantly less than what they actually experienced" [abstract].
- Participants chose to avoid the waiting task in favour of checking internet news, "despite their experiences not being statistically different" [abstract].
- The authors conclude: "an inherent difficulty in accurately appreciating how engaging just thinking can be" [abstract].
- **Use**: face 3, forecast arm. *Map inference, flag as such*: avoidance driven by the forecast prevents the experience that would correct the forecast, so the mispredicted boredom is self-sealing. Hatano et al. do not state this loop. Link [affective-forecasting-gap](/concepts/affective-forecasting-gap/) and [self-maintained-cognitive-limits](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/).
- *Reconciling with Wilson*: Wilson et al. compare thinking with *doing*, and doing wins. Hatano et al. compare *forecast* with *experience*, and experience beats the forecast. Both can hold.

### Chin, Markey, Bhargava, Kassam & Loewenstein (2017), "Bored in the USA: Experience sampling and boredom in everyday life", *Emotion* 17(2): 359–368, doi:10.1037/emo0000232
- 1.1 million reports from 3,867 adults: "Subjects report boredom in 2.8% of the 30-min sampling periods", and 63% reported boredom at least once in 10 days [abstract].
- The findings are "consistent with cognitive accounts that cast boredom as emerging from situations in which engagement is difficult, and are less consistent with accounts that exclusively associate boredom with low arousal or with situations lacking in meaning" [abstract].
- **Use**: scoping. Brief in-the-moment probes measure boredom well, so the void lies elsewhere than measurement. It also counts against the simple low-stimulation account (see the Occam's Razor discussion).

### Danckert & Merrifield (2018), "Boredom, sustained attention and the default mode network", *Experimental Brain Research* 236(9): 2507–2518, doi:10.1007/s00221-016-4617-5 (epub 2016-03-15)
- They read boredom as "a failure to engage executive control networks when faced with a monotonous task" [abstract]. See the audit row above: this paper does **not** support the old note's "boredom IS the default state of consciousness".

### Time: Danckert & Allman (2005), *Brain and Cognition* 59(3): 236–245, doi:10.1016/j.bandc.2005.07.002; Zakay (2014), "Psychological time as information: the case of boredom", *Frontiers in Psychology* 5: 917, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00917; Witowska, Schmidt & Wittmann (2020), *Acta Psychologica* 205: 103061, doi:10.1016/j.actpsy.2020.103061
- Danckert & Allman, on *trait* boredom proneness: "high-boredom-prone individuals demonstrated higher error values when estimating time" [abstract].
- Zakay: "Boredom is accompanied by a slowing of the felt pace of the flow of time", and the slowing "is a signal which, similarly to pain, is aimed at alerting the executive system" [abstract]. This is a proposal, not a measured mechanism.
- Witowska et al., a real waiting situation of 7.5 minutes with 99 participants: "Boredom was associated with the feeling of time passing more slowly and more frequent thoughts about time." Waiting is unpleasant "since oneself becomes the focus of awareness" [abstract].
- **Use**: the subsidiary duration face, and Eastwood's part (b): the bored mind is already attending to itself and to time.

### Slaby (2010), "The Other Side of Existence: Heidegger on Boredom", in Flach & Söffner (eds.), *Habitus in Habitat II: Other Sides of Cognition*, Peter Lang, pp. 101–120 (venue and pages from one secondary bibliography; text read from the author's proofs PDF)
- Heidegger's three forms (*Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics*, 1929–30 lectures): "becoming bored by something", "being bored with something", and "profound boredom", expressed as "it is boring for one" [via Slaby].
- The two structural moments are "being left empty" (*Leergelassenheit*) and "being held in limbo" (*Hingehaltenheit*) [via Slaby].

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

**Face 1: the objectless signal.** Boredom is a demand without a destination. Eastwood's formula has two halves, *wanting* to engage and being *unable* to, and the state lives in the gap between them. Under the opportunity-cost model the gap is principled. What boredom tracks is the value of exploring, and exploration is defined by not yet knowing what it will find. A signal that named its target would be recommending exploitation. So on this model the objectlessness is a property of what the signal is about, not a flaw in introspection. Tolstoy's Vronsky shows the lived consequence: a mind that receives "a desire for desires" fills the empty slot with "every passing caprice, taking it for a desire and an object." The stand-in objects are the mind's own construction, presented as wants. This is face 1 shading into face 2.

**Face 2: the projection.** The verdict comes as a predicate of the object: *this* lecture, *this* queue, *this* book is boring. Every model consulted locates boredom in a relation instead. Eastwood et al. place it in failed attentional engagement plus an attribution of the cause to the environment. Westgate & Wilson place it in two independent mismatches, attention against resources and activity against goals, and the attentional mismatch runs in both directions: understimulation and overstimulation each produce boredom. Agrawal et al. place it in the value of the alternatives. So one felt verdict covers causes as opposite as *too easy* and *too hard*, and both of those can be causes inside the subject, as when someone is bored "with their own thoughts". Pascal had the observation 350 years earlier: weariness arises "even without any cause for weariness", and the least thing relieves it. **Scope**: Williams's point stands. Boredom is sometimes the fitting response to an object that really is tedious. The void claim is that the felt verdict does not carry, on its face, the information needed to tell the fitting case from the projected one, or the attentional cause from the meaning cause. Westgate & Wilson's "different profiles" show that the causes leave traces. Whether those traces are introspectively legible is the untested question.

**Face 3: self-supply and forecast.** Left with only its own resources, the untrained mind does poorly: most of Wilson's participants found concentration difficult and reported their minds wandering "even though there was nothing competing for their attention". In the shock study, 12 of 18 men and 6 of 24 women chose a shock they had said they would pay to avoid. The effect holds in 11 countries. The mind also mispredicts its own capacity, and in the pessimistic direction: Hatano et al. found experienced engagement consistently above predicted engagement, and the prediction drove avoidance. The Map's inference, not the authors', is that the forecast keeps itself in place, because acting on it removes the evidence that would correct it. This is Pascal's diversion thesis given a mechanism. **Scope**: meditation experience and need for cognition predict more enjoyment of thinking (Buttrick et al.), and Wilson et al. name training as the exception. The limit belongs to the *untutored* default.

**Face 4: the examination.** Eastwood's part (b) says the bored mind is already attending to its own non-engagement, and Witowska et al. start from the observation that waiting is typically described as unpleasant "since oneself becomes the focus of awareness" (a framing premise of their study, not one of its results). So boredom does not block self-attention. What it blocks is *engaged* self-study. If a subject takes up her boredom as an object of absorbed inquiry, she has met condition (a), engaged attention with internal information in a satisfying activity, and by the attentional definition the bout is over. The thing studied is then the memory of boredom, or a new state of curiosity about it. Heidegger's method fits this: he sets out to *awaken* the attunement rather than inspect it. **Scope**: the inference is definition-relative. Under the MAC model a person can be fully attentive and still bored, if the activity has no value for her (a meaning deficit), so engaged examination of *meaning-deficit* boredom may leave it intact. The face is therefore Unexplorable for attention-deficit boredom and open for meaning-deficit boredom. Brief probes such as experience sampling measure boredom without abolishing it. What cannot be had is a long, engaged first-person reading of the same bout. This should be stated, not inflated into the claim that boredom cannot be studied.

**Subsidiary: the duration face.** Boredom lengthens felt time and raises the frequency of time-thoughts (Witowska et al.), and Zakay proposes that the slowing is itself the signal. *Langeweile* means "long while". This is data for the tempo note's claim that experience gives interval judgments, not rate readings. In an article it should take one paragraph, linking [temporal-void](/voids/temporal-void/).

**Boundary: profound boredom.** Heidegger's third form has no object at all ("it is boring for one"), and in it the world "ceases to offer anything of significance" (Slaby's gloss). That is [mattering-void](/voids/mattering-void/) and [mood-void](/voids/mood-void/) territory. The article should name it as the point where the boredom void meets the medium-status voids, and link rather than re-argue.

### Evidence for the Limit

- **Structural (face 1)**: argued from the definition of exploration value, valid on the opportunity-cost model only.
- **Experimental (faces 2–3)**: the MAC predictions (independence of the two deficits; under- and overstimulation), the thinking paradigm and its 11-country replication, and the forecast–experience gap with forecast-driven avoidance.
- **Field (scoping)**: the experience-sampling base rate (2.8% of half-hour periods) and its fit with cognitive rather than low-arousal accounts.
- **Historical convergence**: Pascal (1670), Tolstoy (1877), Heidegger (1929–30) and Pessoa describe the same shape before any of the experimental work: wanting without an object, relief by trivia, and avoidance of the self. See the four-criteria section for how much weight this convergence can bear.
- **What is NOT evidence**: the old note's DMN claim and its "no adaptive function" claim. Both were wrong.

### Phenomenology

The felt texture as the sources give it: restlessness with no direction (Eastwood's wanting without engaging); time thickening and turning noticeable (Witowska; Zakay); self-salience (Witowska; Eastwood (b)); the urge to pick up anything at all, which Vronsky's "every passing caprice" captures and which phone use plausibly stands in for today (Buttrick's negative predictor is correlational only); and, at the limit, Heidegger's depersonalised "no one", for whom nothing offers itself. A first-person exercise for the article: notice boredom and ask what you want instead. Usually the answer is *something else*, and the question itself starts to dissolve the state, which is faces 1 and 4 in miniature.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods
- **Experience sampling** captures boredom in vivo with minimal disturbance (Chin et al.).
- **Induction paradigms**: monotonous video and vigilance tasks (Danckert & Merrifield), and the thinking period (Wilson et al.).
- **Forecast-versus-experience designs** expose the self-model's error directly (Hatano et al.).
- **Manipulating MAC components separately** (attention load against goal value) and then asking subjects which they think is operating would test the Unexplored margin of face 2. No such study was found.

### Indirect Methods
- **Computational modelling** (Agrawal et al.) states what the signal is *about*, which no introspective report can.
- **Literary and phenomenological description** (Tolstoy, Pessoa, Heidegger) maps the objectless shape from the outside of experimental vocabulary.
- **Contemplative training** changes the default (Buttrick's meditation moderator). Trained practitioners are the natural probe of whether engaged examination dissolves boredom or merely re-describes it. That bears on face 4, and the current evidence is correlational.

### What AI Might See
- Artificial agents get boredom-analogues *by design*. Schmidhuber's intrinsic-reward agents and curiosity-driven RL carry an explicit disengage-and-explore signal whose target specification (prediction error, compression progress) is written down. For the designer the signal is transparent, which reverses the human case: for humans the signal is felt and its specification is hidden. The void therefore concerns *first-person access* to the signal's grounds, not whether the function can be specified at all. The article should make that concession plainly, since it is what a physicalist will press.
- The noisy-TV problem shows that a pure novelty signal can be captured by noise. That is the RL counterpart of Westgate & Wilson's meaning component: a boredom signal needs a value term that prediction error lacks. Human boredom has both terms and cannot report which one fired. The machine case has both terms written in code and nothing that feels.
- Current language models are trained on human writing about boredom, so their descriptions are inherited and give no independent purchase (see [non-human-minds-as-void-explorers](/voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers/) on contamination).

## Four-Criteria Calibration ([apophatic-cartography-four-criteria](/concepts/apophatic-cartography-four-criteria/))

One grader and no pre-registered rubric, so these grades are judgment, not proof.

- **Signature specificity, moderate-strong.** The signature is a disengage verdict that names no target and arrives as a property of the object. It differs from mirth (a hidden cause of a positive verdict), from mattering (a transparent medium) and from relevance (an uninspectable filter). Of the [compound-failure-signatures](/voids/compound-failure-signatures/) it falls closest to *construction mistaken for observation*.
- **Structured persistence, moderate-strong.** The objectless-want shape survives translation from Pascal's moral psychology, Tolstoy's fiction and Heidegger's existential analytic into attentional (Eastwood), dual-component (MAC) and normative-computational (Agrawal) vocabularies, and none of them dissolves it. The opportunity-cost model explains it rather than removing it.
- **Cross-observer convergence, moderate.** The traditions differ (moral, literary, phenomenological, experimental, computational), but the European humanist sources share a lineage, and Eastwood's "universally conceptualized" is one review's synthesis, which partly *constructs* the convergence it reports. Wilson et al.'s not citing Pascal is a small point in favour of independence.
- **Framework independence: high on the architectural finding, low on interpretive significance.** The objectless signal is *predicted* by a physicalist computational model, so it is a *because-prediction* anchor in [common-cause-null](/project/common-cause-null/) terms. That makes the void robust and gives it little weight for dualism. Nothing here discriminates the Map's metaphysics from its rivals.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits.** The simplest account, that boredom is low stimulation leading to a bad feeling, fails on three counts. Thinking is always available, yet unrewarding for the untrained (Wilson et al.). *Over*stimulation also produces boredom (Westgate & Wilson). Field data fit difficulty of engagement better than low arousal (Chin et al.). The apparently simple state has at least two independent causes and a computational content about unsampled options. This is ordinary scientific complication, and the article should not dress it up as anything stranger.

### Other Tenets

- **Bidirectional Interaction: compatible, with one firm point.** The felt aversiveness of boredom is calibrated to the value of disengaging: Agrawal et al. count boredom among the "phenomenological counterparts" of opportunity-cost measures, and Zakay proposes that the time-slowing is a pain-like alert. James's fit argument applies in this new case. If the feeling were causally idle, its close match to what it would be useful to feel would be unexplained. **The Map does not concede that the feeling is idle.** The point bears against *epiphenomenalism* only. An identity theorist also treats the feeling as efficacious, as the computation itself, so boredom does not discriminate interactionism from physicalism. Link [the-epiphenomenalist-threat](/topics/the-epiphenomenalist-threat/) and [attention-as-interface](/concepts/attention-as-interface/): boredom is the felt cost at the selection interface.
- **Dualism: compatible, no discriminator.** Boredom has a phenomenal character, as all affect does. Nothing in this material makes that character harder for physicalism than any other felt state. The old note's dualism paragraph should not be carried forward.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction: no bearing.** Say so in one line, or omit.
- **No Many Worlds: no bearing.** Omit.

### Implications

The boredom void is a clean, everyday instance of a pattern the catalogue already holds: a felt verdict that steers behaviour while hiding its grounds from the one who feels it. Its distinctive contribution is that the hidden ground is, on the best model, information about *what has not been encountered*. That puts a structural limit on boredom's introspective legibility, independent of how good introspection is. The second contribution is the self-sealing forecast. The mind underestimates its own capacity for engagement and acts on the underestimate, so it rarely meets the evidence. This connects the boredom void to [self-maintained-cognitive-limits](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/), and Pascal's diversion thesis is its oldest statement.

## Gaps in Research

- **Introspective legibility of MAC causes**: no study was found that asks bored subjects to identify whether the deficit is attention or meaning and then checks them against manipulated conditions. This is the Unexplored margin, and the article should state it as open.
- **Eastwood et al. 2012 full text** was not read (paywall), so whether they cite Tolstoy or Fenichel is unverified. Their "universally conceptualized" is a synthesis claim.
- **Kurzban et al. 2013 full text** was not read, so whether it discusses boredom is unverified.
- **Elpidorou's RoPP "functional theory" (2022/2023) and *The Anatomy of Boredom* (2025)** are read at metadata level only.
- **Fenichel 1934/1951, Cage 1961 and Kierkegaard 1843** are leads only (see above). Williams 1973 is read only via IEP, and its publication details were not checked.
- **Contemplative evidence on face 4** is correlational: mindfulness correlates negatively with boredom proneness in several small studies seen in search results, none of which were read. Nothing directly tests whether engaged examination of a boredom bout dissolves it.
- **Wilson shock study**: small N (18 men, 24 women). Later critiques and re-analyses were not searched.
- **Heidegger's FCM** was not read. Everything comes via Slaby, and Slaby's venue rests on one secondary bibliography.
- **Possibly unanswerable**: if the opportunity-cost model is right, the target of boredom is unspecifiable in principle. This is still a model-relative claim, and a theory on which boredom carries implicit object-information (for example, unconscious goal representations) would weaken it.
