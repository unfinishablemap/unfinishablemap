## Executive Summary

Look at a cat behind a picket fence and you see one cat, not slices of one. The parts behind the slats are there for you: they "have a definite shape", and yet they are "obviously not experienced in quite the same way as directly visible object regions" (Ekroll, Sayim & Wagemans 2017) [raw]. Vision science has called this *amodal completion* since Michotte (Nanay 2018: "Albert Michotte, who introduced the term") [raw]. The literature shows a void with four faces.

1. **The disowning face.** The percept marks completed regions as unseen, and the subject's model of their own vision credits it with less than it delivers. Ekroll et al. (2017): "We have a compelling impression of not being able to see hidden things, but the phenomena of amodal perception suggest that we actually do, at least in a functional sense" [raw]. The completion survives the knowledge that it is false. A hollow shell still looks like a solid ball when one knows it is a shell, and touching the inside of the shell makes one's finger *feel shorter* (Ekroll et al. 2016) [abstract]. Tricks built on completion stay unsolved on repeated viewing, whereas tricks built on attentional misdirection get solved (Ekroll et al. 2018) [abstract].
2. **The mode face.** Introspection cannot settle what kind of state the presence is. Philosophers have offered sensorimotor expectation (Noë 2004), mental imagery (Nanay 2010, 2018), a mix that includes visual representation and belief (Briscoe 2011), and the "empty pointing ahead" of Husserl's back side (Husserl 2001). Nanay's audiences split over whether they "see" the long horse [raw]. Part of the dispute turns out to be verbal (Nanay 2018 concedes this) [raw]. The residue is a question about representational format, which third-person work treats as open (Thielen et al. 2019) [abstract].
3. **The determinacy face.** First-person reports disagree about whether hidden parts are present determinately ("a definite shape", Ekroll et al.) or indeterminately ("rather indeterminate", Husserl). The fragments as they were before completion are reachable only briefly (Rauschenberger & Yantis 2001) [abstract].
4. **The absence face.** Occluders can also produce a felt *emptiness* that goes against knowledge: "Although high-level knowledge makes the viewer expect the middle finger to be there behind the banana, it is still experienced as being curiously absent" (Ekroll et al. 2017) [raw]. The visual system excludes candidates as well as supplying them.

**Calibration.** A physicalist account predicts all four faces. Predictive processing gives presence as the counterfactual richness of a generative model (Seth 2014) [abstract], and machine vision computes hidden extents explicitly (Li & Malik 2016; Zhu et al. 2017) [raw]. The void is real as a first-person limit, it is explorable from outside, and it gives no evidence for dualism. Its value to the catalogue is structural. Most perceptual voids record overclaiming ([plenitude-void](/voids/plenitude-void/)) or a report gap ([categorical-perception-void](/voids/categorical-perception-void/)'s kept gradient). This one records a percept that actively disclaims its own contribution, and it can sit in the same scene as plenitude's overclaim.

**Recommended action: article**, slug `amodal-void`. The fold assessment is below. No host has room for the four faces.

### Ekroll, Sayim & Wagemans (2017), "The Other Side of Magic: The Psychology of Perceiving Hidden Things", *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 12(1): 91–106, doi:10.1177/1745691616654676
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616654676 (full text read from the PDF at gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/illusion-of-depth/2017-ekroll.pdf)
- **Type**: Review/theory article
- **Key points**:
  - Amodal completion is the core mechanism behind a class of magic tricks that depend on "automatic perceptual and cognitive processes" rather than on the magician's skill.
  - The phenomenology: the hidden parts are "really there" although they "may, in actual fact, very well be absent". If the occluder were removed and gaps were revealed, "one would be thoroughly surprised, although one must admit that this is logically possible" [raw].
  - **The disowning claim**: "In this case, though, one's immediate phenomenology conjures up the misleading impression that the visual system does much less than it actually does" [raw]. This is the reverse of the inattentional-blindness case, where people believe they see more than they do.
  - "the experience of hidden things is much more strongly determined by automatic perceptual and cognitive heuristics beyond our conscious control than we intuitively believe" [raw].
  - **Impenetrability, with a concession**: "the illusory experience of a complete ball persists even when one knows it is actually just a semispherical shell" [raw]. The authors cite Kanizsa (1979, 1985), Michotte et al. (1964/1991) and Pylyshyn (1999) for the view that completion is cognitively impenetrable. They then concede: "Some effects of learning and knowledge on mental processing of occluded objects have been documented … but whether these effects are part of what should be called amodal perception proper is open to discussion" [raw]. Their own comparative claim is graded: "Amodal perception is clearly less cognitively penetrable than attention" [raw]. **The article must not upgrade this to "impenetrable".**
  - **Amodal absence**: "amodal absence means that an abstract set of possible objects that could, in principle, be hiding behind the occluder, is actively excluded by the perceptual system" [raw]. It is "positively different from not having any particular perceptual experience at all" [raw]. Whereas the completed letters give a positive, specific percept, amodal absence gives "a negative and unspecific perception" [raw]. The evidence offered consists of figure demonstrations (a "bubbled" occluder over a cluttered desk; a banana before a hand), not controlled experiments.
  - The fragment cost: "It is difficult to see the visible parts of objects in their own right" once organisation has occurred [raw].
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits (the folk self-model of vision errs in a direction the simple story does not predict). It is neutral on dualism.

### Nanay (2018), "The Importance of Amodal Completion in Everyday Perception", *i-Perception* 9(4): 2041669518788887, doi:10.1177/2041669518788887 (PMC6083800, CC-BY)
- **URL**: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6083800/
- **Type**: Theory article (full text read)
- **Key points**:
  - Definition: "Amodal completion is the representation of those parts of the perceived object that we get no sensory stimulation from" [raw].
  - He separates three accounts (perception proper; "beliefs or some other postperceptual mental states"; mental imagery) and states: "My claim is that amodal completion is a form of mental imagery" [raw]. He means imagery in the psychologist's sense, perceptual processing without corresponding stimulation, not active imagining.
  - **The verbal-dispute concession.** He quotes Ekroll et al. (2016, p. 3), whose body text says that experience of hidden backsides "is sometimes based on genuine perceptual representations rather than mere cognitive guesswork or imagery" [raw, as quoted by Nanay]. He replies: "If we understood mental imagery this way then amodal completion is clearly not imagery. If we understood mental imagery as perceptual processing not triggered by corresponding sensory stimulation in the relevant sense modality, then amodal completion is clearly mental imagery" [raw]. Part of the mode dispute is therefore terminological, and the article must say so.
  - **Introspective variability.** He is "skeptical" of phenomenology-based ways of drawing the modal/amodal distinction. "One reason for skepticism is the significant interpersonal variations in amodal completion", and "I frequently give talks where I show the horse illusion to the audience, and each time the audience is divided whether they "see" a long horse" [raw]. He adds that "Michotte himself was not always crystal clear about the nature of this phenomenological difference" [raw]. The audience split is an informal report, not a study.
  - His conclusion: "everyday perception is a mixture between sensory stimulation-driven perception and mental imagery" [raw].
- **Tenet alignment**: It bears on [perceptual-reality-monitoring-void](/voids/perceptual-reality-monitoring-void/). If Nanay is right, every ordinary percept carries a standing imagery component that never trips the "imagined" verdict.

### Briscoe (2011), "Mental imagery and the varieties of amodal perception", *Pacific Philosophical Quarterly* 92(2): 153–173, doi:10.1111/j.1468-0114.2011.01393.x
- **URL**: https://philarchive.org/rec/BRIMIA (full text blocked; abstract read via OpenAlex)
- **Key points** [abstract]: "The problem of amodal perception is the problem of how we represent features of perceived objects that are occluded or otherwise hidden from us." He describes Nanay as holding that we perceive occluded features "by imaginatively projecting them into the relevant regions of visual egocentric space". His own claim: "amodal perception is not a single, unitary capacity", characterised "not only by mental imagery, as Nanay suggests, but also by genuinely visual representations as well as beliefs". He closes on "the role of object-directed bodily action in conferring a sense of unseen presence".

### Ekroll, Sayim, Van der Hallen & Wagemans (2016), "Illusory Visual Completion of an Object's Invisible Backside Can Make Your Finger Feel Shorter", *Current Biology* 26(8): 1029–1033, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2016.02.001 (PMID 27040774)
- **Key points** [abstract]: The shell illusion "persists even when observers touch the inside of the shell with their own finger"; "the finger feels shorter, as if to make space for the purely illusory volume of the visually completed ball"; and this supports the idea that "our experience of the hidden backsides of objects is shaped by genuine perceptual representations rather than mere cognitive guesswork or imagery".
- **Scope**: The body text, as Nanay quotes it, says "*sometimes* based on". The abstract's "is shaped by" is broader than the paper's own sentence, and the article should carry the hedged form. Only the abstract was read; sample size and procedure are unverified.

### Ekroll, Sayim & Wagemans (2013), "Against Better Knowledge: The Magical Force of Amodal Volume Completion", *i-Perception* 4(8): 511–515, doi:10.1068/i0622sas
- [abstract]: spectators "do not merely entertain the intellectual belief that the balls are all solid, but rather automatically and immediately perceive them as such". The evidence is described as "observations", so this is a demonstration paper.

### Ekroll, De Bruyckere, Vanwezemael & Wagemans (2018), "Never Repeat the Same Trick Twice—Unless it is Cognitively Impenetrable", *i-Perception* 9(6), doi:10.1177/2041669518816711 (PMID 30627415)
- [abstract]: four misdirection tricks against four completion tricks, each shown one to three times. Solution rates for misdirection "increased much more with repeated viewing than those for tricks based on amodal completion, which remained very low throughout". This is the best controlled evidence for the knowledge-resistance claim. N and the effect sizes were not read.

### Sekuler & Palmer (1992), "Perception of partly occluded objects: A microgenetic analysis", *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 121(1): 95–111, doi:10.1037/0096-3445.121.1.95
- [abstract]: "For prime durations > 200 ms, a partly occluded object produces priming effects equivalent to its complete interpretation". The complete interpretation develops over time, "possibly proceeding through a preliminary mosaic interpretation". This is objective (non-introspective) evidence that the completion happens.

### Rauschenberger & Yantis (2001), "Masking unveils pre-amodal completion representation in visual search", *Nature* 410(6826): 369–372, doi:10.1038/35066577 (PMID 11268213)
- [abstract]: "The completion of a partially occluded object takes about 200 ms". Pre-completion information exists, and "visual search does have access to pre-completion representations, but only for a limited time that depends on the size of the occluded region". The fragment-as-fragment is reachable, briefly, and then gone. This supports face 3.

### Comparative: Regolin & Vallortigara (1995), *Perception & Psychophysics* 57(7): 971–976, doi:10.3758/bf03205456; Sekuler, Lee & Shettleworth (1996), "Pigeons Do Not Complete Partly Occluded Figures", *Perception* 25(9): 1109–1120, doi:10.1068/p251109; Nagasaka, Lazareva & Wasserman (2007), "Prior experience affects amodal completion in pigeons", *Perception & Psychophysics* 69(4): 596–605, doi:10.3758/bf03193917
- [abstract] Imprinted chicks chose complete or occluded versions over fragments: "Newborn chicks thus appear to behave as if they could experience amodal completion." Note the authors' "as if".
- [abstract] Sekuler et al. report that "Monkeys and mice also seem to complete two-dimensional representations of partly occluded objects", and conclude that "pigeons do not complete partly occluded figures".
- [abstract] Nagasaka et al. find that training shifts pigeons' errors toward the complete foil: "experience with 2-D images may facilitate amodal completion in pigeons, perhaps via perceptual learning". A search summary (not read) reports that later pigeon results are mixed (Qadri & Cook 2015, lead only).
- **Bearing**: A visual system can lack the presence of the hidden, and may acquire it. This is a lead for the other-minds side of the void ([voids-between-minds](/voids/voids-between-minds/)): whether a pigeon looking at a fence sees slices has no first-person answer available to us.

### Seth (2014), "A predictive processing theory of sensorimotor contingencies: Explaining the puzzle of perceptual presence and its absence in synesthesia", *Cognitive Neuroscience* 5(2): 97–118, doi:10.1080/17588928.2013.877880 (PMID 24446823)
- [abstract]: Generative models with "counterfactually-rich" elements encode how input would change under possible actions, "with counterfactual richness determining the degree of perceptual presence associated with a stimulus". Synaesthetic concurrents' models "are hypothesized to be counterfactually poor". The theory "may also lead to a new view of the (in)determinacy of normal perception".
- **Bearing**: This is the physicalist account of presence, and it predicts face 3's determinacy variation. The commentaries in the same issue (Di Paolo 2014 and others; PubMed lists ten) were not read for content.

### Thielen, Bosch, van Leeuwen, van Gerven & van Lier (2019), "Neuroimaging Findings on Amodal Completion: A Review", *i-Perception* 10(2): 2041669519840047, doi:10.1177/2041669519840047; Thielen et al. (2024), "Amodal completion across the brain: The impact of structure and knowledge", *Journal of Vision* 24(6): 10, doi:10.1167/jov.24.6.10
- [abstract, 2019]: "The involvement of low-level visual areas such as V1 and V2 is not yet clear". Higher areas (LOC, FFA) "seem invariant to occlusion". The review discusses "whether the invisible parts of the occluded object are represented as if they were visible, contrary to a high-level representation". **The format question that introspection cannot settle is open third-personally too, but it is being worked on.**
- [abstract, 2024]: Structure and knowledge cues affect completion in LOC, and "predominantly impacts higher-level visual processing, with less pronounced effects on the early visual cortex". Knowledge does reach the process at a higher level. This is consistent with Ekroll et al.'s concession and limits any "impenetrable" claim.

### Loo & Buchsbaum (2026), "Fragile recurrent processing in Aphantasia: Evidence from visual pattern completion", *Consciousness and Cognition* 143: 104100, doi:10.1016/j.concog.2026.104100 (PMID 42492460)
- [abstract]: 31 self-identified aphantasic participants and 41 controls categorised briefly shown, partly occluded objects. The masking cost was larger in aphantasia (β = −0.167, p = 0.039 across SOAs; p = 0.015 at the 25 ms SOA), but "The larger masking cost reflected better unmasked performance in aphantasia that masking eliminated, not worse masked performance", and the findings are "consistent with recurrent processing in aphantasia being preserved but more easily disrupted by subsequent visual interference".
- **Bearing**: Completion survives in people who report no voluntary imagery. That fits Nanay's non-voluntary sense of imagery and does not refute it. It is one study with self-identified participants and modest p-values, and it measured categorisation, not felt presence. If Nanay is right, aphantasics have a form of imagery they cannot introspect *as* imagery, which is a direct link to [imagery-void](/voids/imagery-void/)'s presence face. That conditional is this note's inference, not a finding.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Stated once: **a seen object comes with parts that are present to the subject but unseen, and the subject can tell neither how much vision is doing there nor what kind of state that presence is.** The faces differ in modal status.

- **Face 1, disowning (empirical, robust).** The percept files completed regions under "not seen", and the subject's self-model ("I see only what reaches my eye; the rest I infer") credits vision with too little. The completion nonetheless drives shape priming (Sekuler & Palmer), visual search (Rauschenberger & Yantis), felt body size (Ekroll et al. 2016) and trick-solving (Ekroll et al. 2018). Knowledge that the completion is false leaves it in place, at least largely. Knowledge does reach it at higher levels (Thielen et al. 2024), and Ekroll et al. (2017) themselves say only "less cognitively penetrable than attention". This is a natural occlusion. The signature is a felt *disclaimer*: what the system is doing is marked as not being done.
- **Face 2, mode (first-person unexplorable; third-person partly verbal, partly open).** Noë's expectations, Nanay's imagery, Briscoe's mixture and Husserl's empty intentions are all offered as descriptions of the same phenomenology, and introspection cannot choose among them. The evidence for that is the informal audience split, Michotte's unclarity, and a century of phenomenological description that has not converged. Nanay (2018) shows that the imagery/perception quarrel partly dissolves once the term is fixed. **What survives is a format question**: are hidden parts represented "as if they were visible" or only at a high level (Thielen et al. 2019)? That question is answerable from outside and is not answered from inside.
- **Face 3, determinacy (contested, partly empirical).** Simple completions present a definite shape (Ekroll et al.). Volumetric backsides present "rather indeterminate" content that becomes determinate after exploration (Husserl). Seth's theory predicts that counterfactual richness varies with the object and the action repertoire. The fragment before completion is accessible only for a short window. The subject cannot read off how determinate the hidden content is, and asking for a drawing produces new, determinate content (Zhu et al.'s annotators agree), so the question being probed changes in the asking. That last step echoes [mutation-void](/voids/mutation-void/), but here the transformation is a format conversion (presence into depiction) rather than a change brought about by attention.
- **Face 4, absence (demonstration-level evidence).** The system also produces positive non-presence. Behind a narrow occluder a whole class of candidates is "actively excluded", against high-level expectation. A void that supplies felt absence as well as felt presence is a counterpart to [erasure-void](/voids/erasure-void/), where losses produce *no* signal. Here the visual system produces a signal of absence where there may be presence. The evidence is figures, not experiments, and the article must say so.

### Evidence for the Limit

What makes this a genuine boundary rather than mere difficulty is a double dissociation between functional and phenomenal access. Third-person measures show the completion working (priming at >200 ms, search costs, finger length, solution rates that stay low). First-person access, meanwhile, fails in two ways: it disowns the work (face 1) and cannot classify it (face 2). The failure persists under expertise. Magicians "learn to overcome their natural intuitions" (Ekroll et al. 2017, about the performer's fear of being caught, so a different intuition [raw]), but nothing reported shows observers learning to *see* completions as their own contributions. **The limit is narrow**: it concerns introspective access to the mode and amount of completion, not the existence of completion, which is well measured.

**What is merely difficult**: the format question (face 2 residue) and the knowledge-sensitivity question are being worked on with neuroimaging and could be resolved. The article must keep these out of the "unexplorable" column.

### Phenomenology

Approaching the edge feels like looking at something one cannot quite look *at*. The hidden region is felt as full and shaped yet flagged as unavailable, and attending to it gives one neither more of it nor a view of it. The quickest probe is Ekroll's: imagine removing the occluder and finding gaps. The felt surprise in prospect is the marker that the presence was doing work one had not credited. A second probe is the shell. Knowing it is hollow does not make it look hollow. A third is the banana and the hand: knowing a finger is there does not make it look present.

### Distinctions from Neighbouring Voids

- **[plenitude-void](/voids/plenitude-void/)** records an overclaim: the scene feels richly seen beyond what can be reported. Noë groups unattended detail and occluded parts as one problem of perceptual presence. Ekroll et al. supply the asymmetry that grouping hides: for detail, phenomenology claims more than vision delivers; for occluded parts, it claims less. **The note's own suggestion**: one glance at a cluttered room can carry both errors at once, an overclaim about the periphery and an underclaim about the backs of things. That would make the pair a two-directional exhibit for the [taxonomy](/apex/taxonomy-of-voids/).
- **[predictive-construction-void](/voids/predictive-construction-void/)** says one cannot read off the construction/reception ratio. Here the construction is *flagged* (the hidden part is felt as unseen), so the boundary is visible. What is invisible is how much work the flagged part does, and what kind of state it is.
- **[perceptual-reality-monitoring-void](/voids/perceptual-reality-monitoring-void/)** concerns the real/imagined verdict. If Nanay is right, every ordinary percept carries an imagery component that is felt as real and never tagged as imagined. That gives the discriminator a standing case it never flags.
- **[causal-impression-void](/voids/causal-impression-void/)** is a sibling stamp. Vision stamps "cause" on some sequences, and it stamps "there, behind" on some occlusions. Both stamps resist knowledge and both are measurable from outside.
- **[categorical-perception-void](/voids/categorical-perception-void/)**'s kept gradient is implicit use without report. The amodal case adds active disowning.
- **[molyneux-void](/voids/molyneux-void/)** and Briscoe: object-directed action as a source of unseen presence, and Ekroll's touch result, are cross-modal links. The article should cite and not develop them.
- **[selective-correction-and-reconstruction-paradox](/concepts/selective-correction-and-reconstruction-paradox/)** covers blind-spot filling, which is *modal*: the filled region looks seen. Amodal completion is the third case that page lacks, a region filled and marked unseen.

## What a Physicalist Account Predicts (calibration, required by the brief)

A physicalist predicts this void in detail. A visual system built to act on objects needs representations of whole objects from partial input, so it computes hidden extents. Relatability (Kellman & Shipley, via Ekroll) and recurrent feedback (Loo & Buchsbaum; Thielen et al.) are candidate mechanisms. On predictive processing, presence is the counterfactual richness of the model (Seth). The output carries an "occluded" flag because the system must also track what is not currently sampled, so the occluded flag is functional and not a gap in the system. Introspection receives that output and lacks a format tag, because nothing downstream needs one. The subject's folk theory of vision says that seeing requires light from the thing, so the subject disowns the completion. This account predicts each observed feature: underclaiming, knowledge-resistance with higher-level leakage, interpersonal variation, species variation and trainability, and determinacy that varies with counterfactual richness. Engineered vision systems compute hidden extents too, and they show that the computation needs no extra ingredient.

**So the existence of the void is not evidence for dualism.** Under [the common-cause null](/project/common-cause-null/) it is a *because-prediction* anchor: a void that a rival framework predicts by construction. What the dualist can say is limited to a reading. Husserl's description, on which perception means beyond what it receives, is congenial to a view on which intentionality is not reducible. The predictive-processing description says the same thing in model language, so the void does not discriminate between the two.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods
There are none that reach the mode or amount question. Attending to the hidden region yields no more of it. Removing the occluder answers a different question (what was actually there), not what was present before.

### Indirect Methods
- **Behavioural dissociation**: priming (Sekuler & Palmer), visual search with masking (Rauschenberger & Yantis), solution rates on repeated tricks (Ekroll et al. 2018), and bodily illusions (Ekroll et al. 2016). Each measures the completion's functional role without asking the subject.
- **Neural format tests**: repetition suppression and decoding in early visual cortex against LOC (Thielen et al. 2019, 2024). This is the route that can settle the format residue of face 2.
- **Comparative**: chicks, pigeons before and after training, monkeys and mice. These probe whether presence is a fixed capacity.
- **Individual differences**: aphantasia (Loo & Buchsbaum). Nanay's audience split points to a cheap unexplored test: a systematic survey of whether people report "seeing" a completion, correlated with an objective completion measure. No such study was found. This is the void's Unexplored margin.
- **Externalisation**: drawing or annotating completions (Zhu et al.). Agreement shows that the content can be externalised, but it does not show that the drawing matches what was present before drawing.
- **Phenomenological analysis**: Husserl's horizon analysis. Nanay's interpersonal-variation point limits it.

### What AI Might See
- An amodal segmentation network outputs the visible mask and the amodal mask *separately*, so its completion is an inspectable, determinate output, the partition that human introspection lacks (Li & Malik; Zhu et al.). Machine vision therefore has the opposite profile: completion without presence, and fully legible.
- Standard classifiers may lack automatic completion altogether (Shakya & Tripathi, workshop poster), which puts them alongside untrained pigeons.
- A language model writing this note has no visual completion of its own. Its "back of the tomato" is descriptive, so by construction it sits on Briscoe's *belief* branch. It can state all four accounts of the mode face and has no instance to test them on. This echoes the causal-impression note's observation that an AI has no Michotte module.

## Four-Criteria Calibration ([apophatic-cartography-four-criteria](/concepts/apophatic-cartography-four-criteria/))

One grader and no pre-registered rubric, so these grades are judgment, not proof.

- **Signature specificity, strong.** A felt disclaimer over a functionally active completion, together with a felt absence that goes against knowledge, is recognisable from a description of the breakdown alone. In the [compound-failure-signatures](/voids/compound-failure-signatures/) table it combines *systematic illusion* ("consistent misperception that resists correction") with an inversion of *construction mistaken for observation*: here observation is mistaken for non-observation.
- **Structured persistence, moderate-strong.** The disowning shape survives Husserl's intentional analysis, Michotte and Kanizsa's experimental phenomenology, Noë's enactivism, Nanay's imagery account, predictive processing, and magic practice. None of them makes the subject credit the completion. The mode face is weaker, because its persistence is partly a persistence of terminology (Nanay's concession).
- **Cross-observer convergence, moderate.** Phenomenologists, vision scientists, magicians and machine-vision annotators reach the presence-but-unseen description independently. The disowning claim itself, however, comes mainly from one laboratory (Ekroll, Wagemans and colleagues, 2013–2018), so the article must not present it as a field consensus.
- **Framework independence: high on the architectural finding, low on interpretive significance.** Physicalist models predict the void. It is robust and carries little weight for dualism.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits.** The simplest self-model of vision, "I see what reaches my eye and infer the rest", is wrong, and it is wrong toward modesty, the direction the usual "the mind overreaches" story does not predict. The triad perceive/imagine/believe may also be too coarse for the phenomenon (Briscoe: "not a single, unitary capacity"). This is ordinary scientific complication, and the article should not dress it up as anything stranger.

### Other Tenets
- **Dualism: compatible, no discriminator.** The Husserlian reading is available, and predictive processing restates it. State the parity in one sentence.
- **Bidirectional Interaction: compatible, one modest point.** Unseen presence is tied to action: Noë's expectations about movement, Briscoe's "object-directed bodily action", and the change in felt finger length. The felt presence is therefore not causally idle. This bears against epiphenomenalism only, since an identity theorist also treats it as efficacious.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction: no bearing.** Omit.
- **No Many Worlds: no bearing.** It is tempting to note that amodal absence excludes "an abstract set of possible objects" and commits to one completion. That is a fact about representation and has no ontological bearing (compare [predictive-construction-void](/voids/predictive-construction-void/)'s caution). Omit, or keep it to one cautionary clause.

### Implications

The amodal void adds a direction to the catalogue's perceptual voids. A percept can misreport its own extent by disclaiming work it does, as well as by claiming detail it lacks. The two errors can share one glance, which counts against any single global verdict on whether introspection over- or under-reads perception: the error is local and has a sign. The mode face adds a calibration exhibit. A first-person dispute that looked like an unexplorable limit turned out to be partly verbal once a theorist fixed the term, and the remaining format question moved to neuroimaging. That is the deflation pattern the catalogue has recorded before (olfactory naming, the palette-extension capacity face), now applied to a classificatory question rather than a capacity.
