## Executive Summary

The Map named this test itself, at `topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self` L120: semanticisation "studied phenomenologically rather than functionally — tracking whether felt pastness fades by degrees as an episodic memory becomes semantic — is the sharpest available test" of its constitutive reading of autonoesis. The article adds that this one is "closer to live than a hypothetical". That last judgement is correct, and understated. **The test is being run, and on its categorical limb the field has already largely returned the answer the Map identified as adverse.**

Four findings, in descending order of consequence for the Map:

1. **The episodic/semantic boundary is now widely held to be graded, and the leading arguments for that are explicitly built on semanticisation.** Gentry & Buckner (2024) argue from "transitional gradation" that the distinction should be *replaced* with a "dynamic life cycle of memory"; Renoult, Irish, Moscovitch & Rugg (2019) conclude the two are "inextricably intertwined"; Rubin (2022) and Addis & Szpunar (2024) both replace the dichotomy with multidimensional spaces. This is not a fringe position.

2. **But that literature grades the *category*, not the *quale*.** Every one of those arguments individuates memory kinds by content, structure, function, and neural substrate. None of them measures whether felt pastness fades continuously. The Map's falsifier is specifically phenomenological, and on that specific limb the decisive measurement — longitudinal within-memory tracking of re-experiencing as a given episode semanticises — appears **not to have been published**. A targeted search for it returned nothing on point.

3. **The strongest phenomenal evidence available points the other way, toward discreteness.** Severely deficient autobiographical memory (Palombo, Alain, Söderlund, Khuu & Levine 2015) describes healthy, high-functioning adults whose autobiographical *content* is intact while first-person re-experiencing is, by report and by fMRI/ERP biomarker, absent — "their past is experienced in the absence of recollection". That is content/manner independence in its cleanest available form, and it is the Map's own argument handed to it by the clinical literature.

4. **The Map's L118/L120 dichotomy has no slot for the argument actually being made.** The article sorts semanticisation findings into *functionalist* (absorbed by the relocation reply) and *phenomenological* (survives as a falsifier). Gentry & Buckner's argument is neither: it is a metaphysical argument about kind-individuation under transitional gradation. It cannot be absorbed by relocation, because it does not claim to explain the phenomenology; and it does not trigger the phenomenological falsifier, because it does not measure the phenomenology. It attacks a third thing — whether "autonoetic" and "noetic" name distinct kinds at all. The article needs a third category or it will mis-file the most cited work in this area.

There is also a **structural finding that undercuts the test's own logic** and belongs in any article written from this note: Sekeres et al. (2016) showed that details lost from an episodic memory over seven days can be *reinstated* by a brief reminder — "loss of details is not always permanent, and may reflect both a storage and retrieval deficit". If fading is partly an access failure rather than a storage transformation, then a smoothly graded *report* profile is compatible with an ungraded *phenomenal property*: the grading would live in retrieval accessibility, not in the quality retrieved. The Map's dichotomy — graded phenomenology (bad for the Map) versus discrete phenomenology (good) — omits this third reading, which is the one the empirical literature most supports.

## Key Sources

Grouped by what they do to the Map's position.

### Sources that press the graded reading

**Gentry, H. & Buckner, C. (2024). "Transitional gradation and the distinction between episodic and semantic memory."** *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 379(1913): 20230407. DOI `10.1098/rstb.2023.0407`.
- The single most on-point paper found. Abstract (verbatim, via Europe PMC): "we explore various arguments against the traditional distinction between episodic and semantic memory based on the metaphysical phenomenon of transitional gradation… We review two kinds of arguments — from the gradual semanticization of episodic memories as they are consolidated, and from the composition of episodic memories during storage and recall from semantic memories… we recommend replacing the distinction with a dynamic life cycle of memory in which a variety of transitional forms will proliferate."
- The argument form matters: transitional gradation is a *metaphysical* criterion on natural kinds (A and B grade into each other along their characteristic properties, so they are not two kinds). It also explicitly forecloses the escape route of "distinct underlying memory structures".
- **Tenet relation**: conflicts with the Map's constitutive reading if autonoesis is among the "characteristic properties" that grade. Neutral if it is not — and the abstract does not say. This is exactly where the missing full text bites.
- ⚠️ **Same author as the Map's existing citation.** `topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self` cites "Gentry (2023)" for the self-attentional source-monitoring steelman. That citation was checked and **is sound**: Hunter Gentry, "Special Attention to the Self: a Mechanistic Model of Patient RB's Lost Feeling of Ownership", *Review of Philosophy and Psychology* 14(1): 57–85, print 2023, online 2021, DOI `10.1007/s13164-021-00574-1`. So the Map already cites this author as a friendly source on ownership while omitting his 2024 paper attacking the distinction the Map builds on. Worth citing both.

**Renoult, L., Irish, M., Moscovitch, M. & Rugg, M. D. (2019). "From Knowing to Remembering: The Semantic–Episodic Distinction."** *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 23(12): 1041–1057. DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2019.09.008`.
- Verbatim: "the frontiers between perception and knowledge and between episodic and semantic memory are not as clear cut as previously thought… episodic and semantic memory are inextricably intertwined, yet retain a measure of distinctiveness, despite the fact that their neural correlates demonstrate considerable overlap."
- Note "**yet retain a measure of distinctiveness**". This is a continuum claim with a residue clause, which is more hospitable to the Map than the headline suggests. Precede it with Renoult et al. (2012), "Personal semantics: at the crossroads of semantic and episodic memory", *TiCS* 16(11), DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2012.09.003`, which named the hybrid category.

**Rubin, D. C. (2022). "A conceptual space for episodic and semantic memory."** *Memory & Cognition* 50(3): 464–477. DOI `10.3758/s13421-021-01148-3`.
- Replaces the hierarchy with a three-dimensional space (implicit–explicit, self-reference, scene). "Unlike a hierarchy, which has properties specific to each category, the dimensions have properties that extend throughout the conceptual space."
- **Tenet relation**: the *self-reference* dimension is where the Map's mineness limb would have to live, and Rubin treats it as continuous. Directly relevant to the mineness/pastness confidence split recorded in the topic article on 2026-08-04.

**Addis, D. R. & Szpunar, K. K. (2024). "Beyond the episodic–semantic continuum: the multidimensional model of mental representations."** *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 379(1913): 20230408. DOI `10.1098/rstb.2023.0408`.
- Goes past the continuum: asks "whether episodic representations are distinct from other types of explicit representations, including whether MTT as a neurocognitive capacity is uniquely episodic", and proposes the Multidimensional Model of Mental Representations. Relevant to the Map's mental-time-travel material, which currently treats MTT as episodic-proprietary.

**De Brigard, F. (2024). "Episodic memory without autonoetic consciousness."** *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 379(1913): 20230410. DOI `10.1098/rstb.2023.0410`.
- Already engaged in the topic article as of 2026-08-04. Verified here: authorship (sole author), venue, and abstract match the engagement. Verbatim: "the construct of autonoetic consciousness lacks validity and… even if it was valid, it would still not be necessary for episodic memory", ending with "a functional/computational characterization of episodic memory in which its characteristic phenomenology is a contingent feature of the retrieval process".
- Note the three papers above sit in the **same theme issue** — *Elements of episodic memory: lessons from 40 years of research*, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B vol. 379 issue 1913. An article should cite the issue as a convergence, not treat De Brigard as a lone dissenter. That framing correction is itself a finding.

### Sources that press the discrete reading

**Palombo, D. J., Alain, C., Söderlund, H., Khuu, W. & Levine, B. (2015). "Severely deficient autobiographical memory (SDAM) in healthy adults: A new mnemonic syndrome."** *Neuropsychologia* 72: 105–118. DOI `10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2015.04.012`.
- Three healthy, high-functioning adults with lifelong "selective inability to vividly recollect personally experienced events from a first-person perspective", corroborated by "absence of fMRI and event-related potential biomarkers associated with naturalistic and laboratory episodic recollection". "Learning and memory were otherwise intact, as long as these tasks could be accomplished by non-episodic processes… these individuals function normally in day-to-day life, even though their past is experienced in the absence of recollection."
- **Tenet relation: supports Dualism's explanandum and Occam's-limits.** This is semanticisation's endpoint present from birth, dissociated from content, with a neural signature. It is the strongest single item found for the Map's side, and the Map does not currently cite it — a search of `obsidian/` found no SDAM material.
- Caution against over-reading: SDAM is a *trait*, not an observed transition, so it shows the two states are separable without showing the passage between them is a jump. It also cuts both ways — De Brigard could read it as episodic memory functioning without autonoesis, which is precisely his thesis.

**Barry, D. N., Chadwick, M. J. & Maguire, E. A. (2018). "Nonmonotonic recruitment of ventromedial prefrontal cortex during remote memory recall."** *PLoS Biology* 16(7): e2005479. DOI `10.1371/journal.pbio.2005479`.
- Longitudinal fMRI: vmPFC recruitment "rising and falling around an initial peak of 8–12 months, before reengaging for older 2- and 5-year-old memories", replicated in two independent memory sets and again at 8-month follow-up. "The temporal engagement of vmPFC in memory retrieval seems to be nonmonotonic… unaccounted for by current theories."
- **This bears directly on a Map claim.** `topics/consciousness-and-memory` L137 describes a smooth trajectory — vivid within hours, thinning over days, schematising over months, near-complete semantic absorption over decades. Barry et al. is evidence that the underlying consolidation trajectory is *not* monotone. If the substrate is nonmonotonic, a smooth phenomenal decay curve is an assumption the Map is making, not a finding it inherits.

**Sekeres, M. J., Bonasia, K., St-Laurent, M., Pishdadian, S., Winocur, G., Grady, C. & Moscovitch, M. (2016). "Recovering and preventing loss of detailed memory: differential rates of forgetting for detail types in episodic memory."** *Learning & Memory* 23(2): 72–82. DOI `10.1101/lm.039057.115`.
- Over 7 days, peripheral details were lost while central/gist details "showed significantly less loss"; brief reminders "reinstated memory for peripheral details, suggesting that loss of details is not always permanent, and may reflect both a storage and retrieval deficit"; early retrieval prevented loss of both.
- Two uses. First, **differential forgetting rates by detail type** is a partial delivery of the Map's *other* surviving falsifier — the decomposition test — from the content side. Second, **reinstatement by cueing** supplies the third reading described in the summary, and gives the Map a principled reply that is not the relocation move: graded reportability need not imply a graded quale if the fading is in access.

### Sources on measurement — the test's own instrument

**Zaman, A., Setton, R., Catmur, C. & Russell, C. (2024). "What is autonoetic consciousness? Examining what underlies subjective experience in memory and future thinking."** *Cognition* 253: 105934. DOI `10.1016/j.cognition.2024.105934`.
- N = 342 across two studies; exploratory factor analysis of subjective ratings over autobiographical memories, future events, and an encoded video. "In contrast to work that emphasizes the distinction between Remember and Know as being key to autonoetic consciousness, Re-experiencing, and Pre-experiencing for future events, were consistently identified as core markers", alongside Mental Time Travel; and, "for the first time", the associated imagery features — "vivid, visual imagery from a first-person perspective".
- **The most consequential measurement paper found, and its verdict is split.** Against De Brigard's construct-validity limb: autonoesis *has* a recoverable latent structure, so it is not nothing. Against the Map: that structure is multi-factor, and the Remember/Know joint — which `concepts/anoetic-noetic-autonoetic-consciousness` and `concepts/episodic-memory` both lean on as the demonstration that phenomenal character varies independently of content — is demoted from the core. Any article built on this note should not cite Zaman as a clean win for either side.

**Cooper, R. A. & Ritchey, M. (2022). "Patterns of episodic content and specificity predicting subjective memory vividness."** *Memory & Cognition* 50(8): 1629–1643. DOI `10.3758/s13421-022-01291-5`.
- "A strong correspondence between memory vividness and memory for gist information… a smaller, additive benefit of remembering specific perceptual details", plus individual differences: "one cluster of participants used perceptual detail to inform memory vividness whereas another cluster was more driven by gist information."
- **Adverse to the Map on the decomposition falsifier, and precisely in its terms.** The article's decomposition test asks whether reports track separately manipulable components with no residual once they are controlled. Cooper & Ritchey show vividness ratings mapping onto objective attributes with idiosyncratic weighting. It stops short of the falsifier — nobody controlled the components and looked for a residual — but it is the same experiment one step earlier.

**Folville, A., Simons, J. S., D'Argembeau, A. & Bastin, C. (2022). "I remember it like it was yesterday: Age-related differences in the subjective experience of remembering."** *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 29(4): 1223–1245. DOI `10.3758/s13423-021-02048-y`.
- Older adults "overestimate the intensity of their vividness ratings relative to young adults, and… rely on retrieved memory details to a lesser extent to judge vividness". Concluding line, verbatim: "the subjective experience of remembering is more than the reactivation of memory content."
- **Cuts both ways, sharply.** That closing sentence is close to a statement of the Map's thesis from inside mainstream cognitive psychology, and is quotable. But the mechanism offered for the dissociation is criterion shift and scale interpretation — that is, the subjective measure the Map's proposed test depends on is demonstrably miscalibrated across groups. A phenomenological test of graded fading run on self-report inherits this problem.

**Coane, J. H., Umanath, S., Cimenian, T. & Chang, K. (2022). "Using the phenomenology of memory for recent events to bridge the gap between episodic and semantic memory."** *Memory & Cognition* 50(3): 495–511. DOI `10.3758/s13421-021-01193-y`.
- Public events over 12 years, rated with a modified remember/know paradigm with *no instructions on term usage*. "Remembered and known items were equal in terms of subjective strength. Know responses were highly accurate, and more so than remember responses… Participants' own definitions of remember, know, and just familiar revealed that knowing is associated with retrieval from semantic memory, whereas remembering and just familiarity are more associated with event/episodic memory."
- Closest thing found to semanticisation studied phenomenologically over real time, and it is naturalistic rather than longitudinal. Its finding that *unprompted* participant definitions align with the theoretical categories is a small point in favour of the joint being real; its finding that subjective strength does not differ between the two is a point against strength-based deflation.

**Moulin, C. J. A., Carreras, F. & Barzykowski, K. (2023). "The phenomenology of autobiographical retrieval."** *WIREs Cognitive Science* 14(3): e1638. DOI `10.1002/wcs.1638`.
- Distinguishes "the phenomenology of the retrieval *process*, as opposed to the products of retrieval themselves", and grounds the former in fluency-derived familiarity interpreted metacognitively. Sits with Perrin, Michaelian & Sant'Anna (2020), which the Map already cites, and sharpens it: the process/product distinction is one the Map's articles do not currently draw, and felt pastness may be a product-level property while the metacognitive deflation targets process.

**Høffding, S., Martiny, K. M. & Roepstorff, A. (2021). "Can we trust the phenomenological interview? Metaphysical, epistemological, and methodological objections."** *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences*. DOI `10.1007/s11097-021-09744-z`. With **Heimann, K. et al. (2022), "The lived experience of remembering a 'good' interview: Micro-phenomenology applied to itself."** *Phenom. Cogn. Sci.* DOI `10.1007/s11097-022-09844-4`.
- The methodological pair. Micro-phenomenology is a live, published instrument and has been pointed at remembering — but not, on this search, at semanticisation. **This is the concrete shape of the gap**: the Map's "sharpest available test" is not merely unrun, it is unrun with an instrument that exists and whose reliability is itself under active dispute.

## Major Positions

### Transitional gradation / continuum (Gentry & Buckner; Renoult et al.; Rubin; Addis & Szpunar)
- **Core claim**: episodic and semantic memory are not two kinds; the transitional forms produced by semanticisation and by semantic composition of episodic recall proliferate too densely to sustain a joint.
- **Relation to tenets**: adverse to Dualism *only if* the graded properties include the phenomenal ones. As argued, they are stated in content/structure/function terms. The honest Map position is that this literature has not yet reached the phenomenal limb, and that saying so is a live commitment rather than an evasion — the difference from the relocation reply being that this one is *checkable* and could be settled against the Map next year.

### Functional/computational eliminativism about autonoesis (De Brigard 2024; Cheng 2024)
- Cheng, S. (2024), "Distinct mechanisms and functions of episodic memory", *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 379(1913): 20230411, DOI `10.1098/rstb.2023.0411`, is the *counterweight within the same issue*: it defends episodic memory as "a mechanically and functionally distinct" subsystem via a "scenario construction framework" on which traces carry the gist and retrieval enriches it semantically. Useful to the Map as evidence that the issue is not unanimous — but note Cheng defends distinctness on **mechanistic** grounds while leaving phenomenology out, so it is not a friendly witness for the constitutive reading.
- **Relation to tenets**: De Brigard conflicts with the Map's constitutive limb, is compatible with the necessity limb the topic article already conceded. Cheng is neutral-to-helpful structurally, unhelpful phenomenally.

### Content/manner independence (Tulving; Klein; SDAM literature)
- Tulving, E. (1985), "Memory and consciousness", *Canadian Psychology* 26(1): 1–12, DOI `10.1037/h0080017` — verified at Crossref; this is the paper De Brigard's abstract names as the origin of the necessity claim.
- **Relation to tenets**: supports Dualism as explanandum, Occam's-limits directly. The `concepts/episodic-memory` and `concepts/semantic-memory` tenet sections were recalibrated on 2026-08-04 to "compatible but not supporting" on No Many Worlds; nothing found here disturbs that, and nothing found here supplies No-Many-Worlds support either.

## Key Debates

### Does felt pastness fade continuously?
- **Sides**: the continuum literature predicts yes but has not measured it; the SDAM and remember/know dissociation literature suggests the phenomenal property can be wholly absent with content intact.
- **Current state**: **unresolved and, on the specific measurement, unattempted.** The searches run here found no longitudinal within-memory phenomenological tracking study. This is the single most useful thing an article can report, and it should be reported as a *negative search result at a stated ceiling*, not as a claim that no such study exists.

### Is the remember/know paradigm a valid instrument for this?
- **Sides**: Wixted & Mickes (2010), "A continuous dual-process model of remember/know judgments", *Psychological Review*, DOI `10.1037/a0020874` (411 citations), treats R/K as reflecting a continuous strength dimension with a decision criterion; Yonelinas' dual-process tradition treats recollection as threshold-like. Zaman et al. (2024) sidesteps both by demoting R/K from the core of autonoesis.
- **Why it matters to the Map**: `concepts/episodic-memory`, `concepts/semantic-memory` and `concepts/anoetic-noetic-autonoetic-consciousness` all cite the remember/know distinction as demonstrating that identical content can be accessed with or without experiential character. If R/K is a criterion placement on a continuum, that demonstration is weaker than the three articles currently present it as being. Not addressed in any of them. **This is a live, checkable exposure across three concept articles, and it is independent of the falsifier question.**

### Storage transformation or retrieval access?
- Sekeres et al. (2016) reinstatement, Barry et al. (2018) nonmonotonicity, and the Proustian involuntary-recall phenomenon the Map already discusses at `consciousness-and-memory` L137 all point the same way: what fades may be reachability. Largely undiscussed as a distinct option in the philosophy-of-memory literature surveyed, and absent from the Map. Best candidate for an original contribution.

## Gaps in Research

**Verification ceiling — read this before citing anything above.** Every source here was verified to **metadata plus publisher abstract**, via Crossref, OpenAlex and Europe PMC. **No full text was retrieved for any source.** Specifically:

- **The central interpretive claim of this note — that the continuum literature grades the category but not the quale — is inferred from abstracts.** Gentry & Buckner's full text may well discuss phenomenal character; the abstract's "characteristic properties" is not specified. Treat this as the note's most likely error and check it first.
- Author lists were taken from OpenAlex/Europe PMC records, not from title pages.
- **Year discrepancies**: OpenAlex reports online-first years, Europe PMC print years. Moulin et al. is OpenAlex 2022 / print 2023; Folville et al. is 2021 / 2022; Rubin is 2021 / 2022; Coane et al. is 2021 / 2022; Cooper & Ritchey is 2022 in both. **Print years are used throughout this note.** A downstream article must pick one convention and not mix them.

- PubMed was not usable (reCAPTCHA to WebFetch, per today's sibling runs). PhilPapers was not queried.
