## Executive Summary

The question this research covers: when an intention drives an effector through an engineered signal path — a decoder, a classifier, a robotic limb, a speech synthesiser — which causal paths preserve the agent's authorship of the outcome and which are deviant? A small but well-developed literature now treats brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) as the place where the classic deviant-causal-chain problem stops being an armchair puzzle. The mainstream positions split four ways: (1) Yashin (2025) argues BCIs *invalidate* several classic anti-deviance strategies (sensitivity, well-functioning) and force a reliability strategy supplemented by an explicit analysis of *system boundaries* — a demand that never arises for biological effectors; (2) Buller (2020) argues present BCI-mediated behaviour *fails* the conditions of intentional physical action on both causal and volitionist theories, chiefly because the proximate cause of the outcome is the interface's computation, not the intention; (3) Rainey, Maslen and Savulescu (2020) defend continuity — BCI-mediated action should on the whole be treated as conventional action, with residual differences confined to controllability and foreseeability; (4) Steinert, Bublitz, Jox and Friedrich (2019) map the terrain with a three-way control taxonomy (executory, guidance, veto) and ten "peculiarities," including the finding that predictive decoders can initiate movements *without* any executory command. The empirical sense-of-agency literature (Evans et al. 2015; Serino et al. 2022) shows that felt authorship of BCI actions is constructed from feedback and can be wrong — first-person experience cannot settle which route the causation actually took. No mainstream treatment locates the non-deviance condition where the Map's selection-only interactionism must locate it: at the trying→outcome-selection junction, upstream of the entire engineered channel. That relocation yields a distinctive position with concrete consequences (reliability is neither necessary nor sufficient for authorship; predictive BCIs produce non-actions however reliable they are) and is not covered by any source found.

## Key Sources

### Causal Deviance in Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs): A Challenge for the Philosophy of Action (Yashin 2025)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.3390/philosophies10020037
- **Type**: Paper (*Philosophies* 10(2): 37, 2025; open access). Author: Artem S. Yashin.
- **Access note**: MDPI blocked direct retrieval; worked from OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar abstract plus a structured full-text extraction via reader proxy. Paraphrases below are medium-high confidence; **verify any verbatim quote against the PDF before using it in an article**.
- **Key points**:
  - Three constructed cases: (1) *interface with artifacts* — user intends a command; an EEG artifact from an accidental blink causes the classifier to output the *correct* command despite misclassification: intention causes outcome, control absent; (2) *deviant computation* — counterbalancing errors inside the BCI produce correct operation; (3) *perfect interface plus randomizing module* — an external disruptor malfunctions, letting correct execution through.
  - Thesis: the existence of BCI-mediated action *invalidates* certain anti-deviance strategies. Sensitivity strategies fail because discrete BCI operations make outcomes insensitive to minor variations in intention content; well-functioning analyses (Enç, Roloff) fail because a BCI's functions come from purposeful design, not evolution; Searle-style immediate-causation conditions are satisfied in Case 1 yet deviance persists.
  - Positive proposal: reliability strategies, treating the human and BCI as a unified system — but this *requires* an explicit account of where the system's boundary sits (is the randomizing module inside or outside the action-supporting architecture?). For bodily movement this boundary question never arises; for engineered effectors it is unavoidable.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral diagnostic. The system-boundary demand is the mainstream analogue of the Map's interface-boundary question, posed at the wrong (downstream) junction from the Map's perspective — which is exactly the contrast an article can exploit.

### Doing Things with Thoughts: Brain-Computer Interfaces and Disembodied Agency (Steinert, Bublitz, Jox & Friedrich 2019)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-018-0308-4 (*Philosophy & Technology* 32(3): 457-482; open access; full text retrieved and quotes grep-verified)
- **Type**: Paper
- **Key points**:
  - Ten "peculiarities" of BCI-mediated events. Most relevant here: BCI actions are *non-basic* actions performed by a basic *mental* action (motor imagery or selective attention); motor-intentions (M-intentions in Pacherie's taxonomy) are typically absent; "events mediated by passive BCIs are no actions"; predictive designs "may initiate movements by predictive interpretation of brain signals," so users lose executory control precisely as engineering improves — "engineering BCI systems that work best for users, especially patients, may work well precisely by undermining features such as initiation that are central to concepts of action."
  - Control taxonomy: *executory* (initiation via a go-command), *guidance* (influence over unfolding execution — largely absent in goal-selection designs: "in BCI designs with goal selection, users lack any guidance control"), *veto* (stopping power — often the only control left). They propose executory OR guidance control suffices for action.
  - Uses Schlosser's (2010) formal rendering of basic deviance and the nervousness case to frame the control question.
  - Libet point, congenial to the Map: because unconscious motor preparation cannot drive the device (neuromuscular output is not used), "BCI-movements thus preserve the causal efficacy of a conscious command (such as mental imagery). In this regard, BCI actions may come closer than ordinary actions to the ideal of consciously initiated action."
  - Memorable framing from the abstract: "A motionless body, the epitome of inaction, might be acting."
  - Legal wing: the "willed bodily movement" act requirement excludes BCI outputs; they propose recognising "quasi-bodily devices."
- **Tenet alignment**: The Libet-immunity point aligns strikingly with Tenets 2-3: the BCI channel bypasses exactly the unconscious preparatory machinery that Libet-style arguments use against conscious causation, making BCI action a *cleaner* observational window onto the volitional junction than natural action.

### Brain-Computer Interfaces and the Translation of Thought into Action (Buller 2020)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s12152-020-09433-9 (*Neuroethics* 14(2): 155-165)
- **Type**: Paper
- **Key points** (from abstract, retrieved verbatim):
  - "This paper argues that present BCI-mediated behavior fails to meet the conditions of intentional physical action as proposed by causal and non-causal theories of action."
  - Against the causal theory: "the proximate cause of action in present BCI-mediated behavior is not the person's intention, and the behavior fails to meet the conditions of reliability, sensitivity and difference-making."
  - Against the volitionist rescue: BCI behaviour counts as action "if we can equate imagining movement with trying to move" — and Buller argues the novelty, limited functionality, and impoverished sensory feedback of present BCIs *challenge* that equation.
- **Tenet alignment**: Direct challenge the Map must answer. The Map's action theory makes *trying* the basic act (see [philosophy-of-action-under-dualism](/topics/philosophy-of-action-under-dualism/)); Buller's imagery≠trying argument is the sharpest published objection to treating decoded motor imagery as the agent's volition. Conflicts with no tenet but pressures the Map's application of them.

### When Thinking is Doing: Responsibility for BCI-Mediated Action (Rainey, Maslen & Savulescu 2020)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1080/21507740.2019.1704918 (*AJOB Neuroscience* 11(1): 46-58; abstract retrieved verbatim via PubMed)
- **Type**: Paper (target article with seven open peer commentaries)
- **Key points**:
  - "Whilst BCI-mediated action ought to be, on the whole, treated as conventional action, law and policy ought to be amended to accommodate BCI action by broadening the definition of action as 'willed bodily movement.'"
  - The residual differences "relate to control. Specifically, to limits in both controllability of BCIs via neural states, and in foreseeability of outcomes from such actions." In specific cases BCI action merits different ethical evaluation.
  - Commentary of note: Lim & Duman (2020), "The Continuity of BCI-Mediated and Conventional Action" (*AJOB Neuroscience* 11(1): 59-61), pressing the continuity line harder.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral-to-congenial. The continuity thesis matches the Map's downstream-amplification reading (the decoder is metaphysically on a par with muscle and bone); the controllability/foreseeability residue is what the Map would re-describe as channel noise, not authorship failure.

### Brain-Computer Interface Tool Use and the Contemplation Conundrum (Mehta 2025)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/oons/kvaf002 (*Oxford Open Neuroscience*, 2025; open access)
- **Type**: Paper. Author: Dvija Mehta.
- **Key points**:
  - The "contemplation conundrum": a BCI user (modelled on Neuralink participant P1 playing chess) *contemplates* several moves; contemplation and intended motor imagery generate overlapping neural patterns, and no known neural marker distinguishes imagining-as-trying from imagining-as-considering. The decoder may enact a merely contemplated option — a *voluntary yet unintentional* output.
  - Formalises BCI action as intention ⇒ motor imagery ⇒ device effect, and locates the deviance risk in the extra imagery step: alternative imagery states may be decoded.
  - Uses Mele's content and guidance conditions; argues implementational control is compromised when layered mental contents cannot be neurally disambiguated.
- **Tenet alignment**: Strongly relevant to Tenet 2. The conundrum is a *reading-junction* deviance: the channel attributes authorship to neural activity the agent never enacted as a volition. The Map's selection criterion (below) gives a principled answer where purely neural criteria cannot, precisely because on the Map's account trying is not identical to any imagery pattern the decoder could read.

### Did I Do That? Brain-Computer Interfacing and the Sense of Agency (Haselager 2013)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11023-012-9298-7 (*Minds and Machines* 23: 405-418)
- **Type**: Paper
- **Key points**: When BCIs are coupled with intelligent devices that share control, behavioural control can switch *implicitly* from user to device; users may then be genuinely uncertain "about being the agent of the act." An early statement of the shared-control authorship problem.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral; feeds the [agency-void](/voids/agency-void/) connection — the felt sense of agency does not track the actual causal route.

### Visual Feedback Dominates the Sense of Agency for Brain-Machine Actions (Evans, Gale, Schurger & Blanke 2015)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0130019 (*PLoS ONE* 10(6): e0130019)
- **Type**: Empirical paper
- **Key points**: Bodily and BMI actions rely on common sensorimotor-integration mechanisms for agency judgments, but in the absence of overt movement and proprioception, visual feedback dominates the sense of agency "however erroneous the visual feedback may be." Users claim authorship of outcomes their brain did not produce when the display says so.
- **Tenet alignment**: Supports the Map's honesty about the verification circularity ([agency-void](/voids/agency-void/)): first-person experience cannot certify non-deviance of the route.

### Sense of Agency for Intracortical Brain-Machine Interfaces (Serino et al. 2022)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-021-01233-2 (*Nature Human Behaviour* 6(4): 565-578; abstract retrieved verbatim via PubMed)
- **Type**: Empirical paper (tetraplegic participant, intracortical BMI)
- **Key points**: "By experimentally manipulating the coherence between decoded motor commands and sensory feedback ... we provide evidence that primary motor cortex processes sensory feedback, sensorimotor conflicts and subjective states of actions generated via the brain-machine interface. Neural signals processing the sense of agency affected the proficiency of the brain-machine interface." M1 carries not just motor commands but agency-monitoring signals, and SoA level feeds back into decoder performance.
- **Tenet alignment**: Congenial to the interface programme: the authorship-monitoring machinery is *neural and brain-side*, exactly where the Map says the interface sits; the engineered channel contributes no agency signal of its own.

### Speech Neuroprostheses with Language-Model Priors (Willett et al. 2023; Metzger et al. 2023)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06377-x (*Nature* 620: 1031-1036); https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06443-4 (*Nature* 620: 1037-1046)
- **Type**: Empirical papers
- **Key points**: State-of-the-art speech BCIs decode attempted speech at 62+ words per minute with large vocabularies; crucially, decoding is constrained by language models that select probable word sequences given noisy neural evidence. Part of the *content* of the produced utterance is contributed by a statistical prior over what people typically say — the sharpest current case of the channel co-authoring the outcome.
- **Tenet alignment**: Raises the deviance question at the level of content rather than route: when the LM "corrects" the decoded phoneme stream, which words are the agent's? A concrete, current hook no philosophy source found has yet treated in the deviance idiom.

### Action (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, 2023)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/action/
- **Type**: Encyclopedia entry (rewritten 2023 by Juan S. Piñeros Glasscock & Sergio Tenenbaum)
- **Key points**: Canonical taxonomy: *primary/basic* deviance (Davidson's climber — the movement is not even an action) vs *secondary/consequential* deviance (the bank-robber/wild-pigs style case — an action occurs but the outcome is not intentional under the description). Repair strategies catalogued: proximate-cause (Mele), sensitivity/differential explanation (Peacocke, Bishop), guidance during execution (Frankfurt; Setiya's continued-desire gloss), plan-following for non-basic action (Mele & Moser), and empirically informed approaches.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral scaffolding. Note the basic/consequential distinction matters for BCIs: engineered-channel deviance is structurally *secondary* deviance for the Map (the basic act — the trying — occurred; the question is whether the outcome is the agent's under its description), whereas the mainstream debate often treats it as primary.

## Major Positions

### Reliability-plus-system-boundaries (Yashin)
- **Proponents**: Artem S. Yashin (2025).
- **Core claim**: BCI cases show sensitivity and well-functioning strategies fail; only reliability strategies survive, and they must be supplemented by an explicit specification of the human-machine system's boundary before "reliable path" is even well-defined.
- **Key arguments**: The three constructed cases (artifact-driven correct output; internally counterbalanced errors; malfunctioning external disruptor) differ in deviance verdict *only* in where the malfunction sits relative to the system boundary.
- **Relation to site tenets**: The boundary demand is the mainstream shadow of the Map's interface question. But the Map draws the authorship-fixing boundary at the mind-brain junction (Tenets 2-3), not around the engineered channel: on the Map's view Yashin's boundary fixes *success and intentionality-under-a-description*, not *whether the agent acted*.

### BCI-skepticism about action (Buller)
- **Proponents**: Tom Buller (2020); partial kinship with Mehta (2025).
- **Core claim**: Present BCI-mediated behaviour is not intentional physical action: the proximate cause is machine computation, and reliability/sensitivity/difference-making fail; the volitionist rescue requires an unearned equation of imagining with trying.
- **Key arguments**: Novelty, low functionality, and impoverished feedback of current systems.
- **Relation to site tenets**: The imagery≠trying challenge is the one the Map must answer head-on, since the Map's basic act *is* the trying. The Map's likely reply: what the decoder reads (imagery) is not the volition itself but a neural pattern whose production was itself volitionally selected — the agent performs a genuine basic mental act, and Buller's gap re-opens only if trying is identified with the readable pattern, which the Map denies.

### Continuity (Rainey, Maslen & Savulescu; Lim & Duman)
- **Proponents**: Rainey, Maslen, Savulescu (2020); Lim & Duman (2020).
- **Core claim**: BCI-mediated action is on the whole conventional action; the differences are graded matters of controllability and foreseeability, best handled by broadening "willed bodily movement," not by denying action status.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Congenial. Selection-only interactionism predicts continuity: once the volitional selection has occurred at the neural interface, everything downstream — motor neurons, muscle, or decoder — is ordinary physical causation ([brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary](/topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary/) already argues the decoder sits *inside* the boundary, reading signals consciousness was already producing).

### Control-taxonomy pluralism (Steinert et al.)
- **Proponents**: Steinert, Bublitz, Jox, Friedrich (2019); Haselager (2013) as precursor on shared control.
- **Core claim**: Action status tracks forms of control (executory, guidance, veto); different BCI designs preserve different subsets; predictive and passive designs fall outside action altogether.
- **Relation to site tenets**: The executory go-command — "a conscious and effortful mental act" — is the phenomenon Tenet 3 predicts and the Occam tenet protects (the effortful, attention-demanding, anaesthesia-abolished character of BCI control is what a no-selection story leaves unexplained; the BCI article already runs this argument).

### Voluntary-but-unintentional outputs (Mehta)
- **Proponents**: Dvija Mehta (2025).
- **Core claim**: Because decoders cannot distinguish contemplated from intended imagery, BCIs can produce outputs that are voluntary yet unintentional — a category mainstream action theory struggles to place.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Grist for the Map: the missing discriminator is exactly what the Map posits — the volitional selection event. A framework in which some neural patterns are *enacted* (selected by the subject) and others merely *occur* has the resources to say which decoder outputs are actions. A physicalist reading has to find a neural marker; Mehta reports there is none known.

## Key Debates

### Where does the non-deviance condition bite?
- **Sides**: Along the whole intention→outcome path (Yashin, Buller: the engineered segment can itself be deviant) vs at the initiation junction only, with downstream deviations reclassified as execution noise (continuity theorists; the Map's variant places the junction at trying→outcome-selection).
- **Core disagreement**: Whether the machine segment is part of the *action's constitution* or part of the action's *effects*.
- **Current state**: Open; Yashin's system-boundary argument is the most precise statement that the question cannot be dodged for engineered effectors.

### Is decoded motor imagery a trying?
- **Sides**: Buller (no — the equation is unearned); Steinert et al. (BCI use is non-basic action grounded in a basic mental action); Mehta (the imagery layer is precisely where control leaks out).
- **Current state**: Ongoing; no party has a criterion for imagery-as-volition vs imagery-as-mere-representation. The Map has one to offer (selection vs occurrence), which is the article's opening.

### Does reliability confer authorship?
- **Sides**: Reliability strategists (Yashin) vs control theorists (Steinert et al.: a reliable predictive decoder that bypasses the go-command *undermines* agency; Frankfurt's legacy: guidance during execution matters, not the statistics of the channel).
- **Core disagreement**: Predictive BCIs are the crux — maximally reliable, minimally commanded.
- **Current state**: Live, sharpened by engineering trends (shared control, LM priors); Steinert et al.'s "paradoxical effect" observation is the cleanest statement.

### Can the sense of agency settle the causal route?
- **Sides**: Empirical consensus says no (Evans et al. 2015: visual feedback dominates however erroneous; Serino et al. 2022: SoA is an M1-processed construct; Haselager 2013: implicit control-switching breeds genuine uncertainty; choice-blindness parallels in [authorship-of-action-divergence](/topics/authorship-of-action-divergence/)).
- **Current state**: Settled in the negative for phenomenology-as-verification; this is the empirical face of the Map's [agency-void](/voids/agency-void/) verification circularity.

## Potential Article Angles

Based on this research, an article (target: `topics/`, one slot contingent — the expand-topic chain waits for a freed slot) could:

1. **"Deviant Causation in BCI-Mediated Action" — the Map's relocation thesis.** Mainstream treatments ask whether the engineered path from intention to outcome is deviant. Under [selection-only interactionism](/topics/overdetermination-dissolution-under-selection-only-interactionism/) the authorship-fixing junction sits upstream of every path any decoder can touch: the trying selects which neural outcome becomes actual ([consciousness-selecting-neural-patterns](/concepts/consciousness-selecting-neural-patterns/)), and everything downstream — motoneurons or microelectrodes — is amplification. Consequences to develop: (a) engineered-channel deviance is *secondary* deviance (misfired outcome of a genuine act), never primary; (b) reliability of the channel bears on intentionality-under-a-description, not on whether the agent acted, so reliability is neither necessary nor sufficient for authorship — against Yashin; (c) predictive/passive decoding yields non-actions *however reliable*, because no selection event stands at the origin — with Steinert et al., against pure reliability strategies; (d) Yashin's system-boundary demand is answered rather than faced: the only boundary that fixes authorship is the mind-brain interface, and it is not engineerable. Tenets 2 and 3 carry the argument; the honest residue is the [agency-void](/voids/agency-void/) point that the agent cannot verify the route from inside (Evans, Serino supply the empirical face).
2. **Answering Buller within the article** (needed for tenet honesty): the Map must either defend imagery-as-enacted-trying or concede that current motor-imagery BCIs chain the action through a *further* basic mental act. Prefer the second, Steinert-friendly line: the basic act is the volitional production of the imagery; deviance at the decoding junction (Mehta's conundrum) is then the channel misattributing authorship to unenacted neural activity — which the Map, uniquely, has the vocabulary to state.
3. **Cross-review targets on publication**: [brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary](/topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary/) (add the authorship question its boundary discussion lacks; the "What BCIs Cannot Do" section should gain a deviance clause), [philosophy-of-action-under-dualism](/topics/philosophy-of-action-under-dualism/) (its one-sentence BCI mention should point at the new article), [anarchic-hand-and-action-ownership](/topics/anarchic-hand-and-action-ownership/) (biological analogue of channel-initiated non-actions).
