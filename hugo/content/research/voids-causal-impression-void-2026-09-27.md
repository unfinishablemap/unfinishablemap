# Research: Voids - The Causal Impression Void

**Date**: 2026-09-27
**Search queries used**: "Rolfs Dambacher Cavanagh 2013 visual adaptation of the perception of causality Current Biology"; "Scholl Tremoulet 2000 perceptual causality and animacy Trends in Cognitive Sciences Michotte"; "Hume Enquiry section VII necessary connection influence of volition power will body"; "Anscombe Causality and Determination 1971 scrape push wet carry perceive causation"; "Leslie Keeble 1987 Do six-month-old infants perceive causality"; "Maine de Biran feeling of effort immediate experience of causality reply to Hume"; "Galen Strawson The Secret Connexion 1989 sceptical realism"; "Haggard Clark Kalogeras 2002 voluntary action and conscious awareness"; "Siegel Contents of Visual Experience causal perception"; "Scholl Nakayama 2002 causal capture"; "Russell 1913 On the Notion of Cause relic of a bygone age"
**Voids category**: Mixed. Unexplorable at the core (the relation of causation itself never appears in experience, only a constructed impression of it), with an Occluded layer (the impression is vivid, automatic and early, so it feels like seeing causation and hides the gap it papers over).

## Executive Summary

We never perceive causation itself. We perceive sequences, and the visual system adds a fast, automatic, stimulus-driven *impression* of causation on top of them. Hume's claim that "one event follows another; but we never can observe any tye between them" (EHU 7.26) now has a psychophysical counterpart. The causal impression studied since Michotte can be adapted like colour or motion (Rolfs, Dambacher & Cavanagh 2013; Kominsky & Scholl 2020). It can be captured by nearby context (Scholl & Nakayama 2002). It can be set *after* the putative impact (Choi & Scholl 2006). It is present by about six months of age (Leslie & Keeble 1987). The impression is therefore a perceptual construct: it can be present without causation and, presumably, absent where causation occurs. What it points toward (the "secret connexion" of Strawson's reading of Hume) stays outside experience. This matters for the Map because the same void covers mental causation. The epiphenomenalist's complaint that we never *observe* consciousness causing anything is only half the picture: we never observe *physical* causation either. Hume himself drew this parity (EHU 7.21), and it undercuts any argument that treats mental causation as uniquely unobservable.

## Key Sources

### Hume, *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*, Section VII ("Of the Idea of Necessary Connexion") (1748)
- **URL**: https://davidhume.org/texts/e/7
- **Type**: Primary text (quotes below verified verbatim by grep against the davidhume.org text)
- **Key points**:
  - External bodies: we observe succession, never the connecting power. "One event follows another; but we never can observe any tye between them. They seem *conjoined*, but never *connected*." (EHU 7.26)
  - Volition: we are conscious *that* the body follows the will, but not *how*. "The motion of our body follows upon the command of our will. Of this we are every moment conscious. But the means, by which this is effected; the energy, by which the will performs so extraordinary an operation; of this we are so far from being immediately conscious, that it must for ever escape our most diligent enquiry." (EHU 7.10)
  - The paralysis/lost-limb case (EHU 7.13): "A man, suddenly struck with a palsy in the leg or arm, or who had newly lost those members, frequently endeavours, at first, to move them... Here he is as much conscious of power to command such limbs, as a man in perfect health is conscious of power to actuate any member which remains in its natural state and condition." The feeling of power is the same whether or not the effect follows, so it cannot be a perception of the connection.
  - Effort (the *nisus* footnote): Hume grants that "It is this nisus or strong endeavour, of which we are conscious, that is the original impression from which this idea is copied" is the popular view, but replies that "This sentiment of an endeavour to overcome resistance has no known connexion with any event: What follows it, we know by experience; but could not know it à priori."
  - **Parity**, in Hume's report of the occasionalists: "as we are totally ignorant of the power, on which depends the mutual operation of bodies, we are no less ignorant of that power, on which depends the operation of mind on body, or of body on mind; nor are we able, either from our senses or consciousness, to assign the ultimate principle in one case, more than in the other." (Paragraph number not verified; it is in Part I, in the exposition of occasionalism around EHU 7.21. Attribute it as Hume *reporting* "philosophers advancing still in their enquiries", not as his own endorsed conclusion.)
- **Tenet alignment**: Bidirectional Interaction (parity: mind→body is no more opaque than body→body); Occam's Razor Has Limits (a regularity theory is the "simplest" reading of what we can observe, but that simplicity reflects the limit of observation, not the nature of causation).

### Strawson, G. (1989), *The Secret Connexion: Causation, Realism, and David Hume*
- **URL**: https://philpapers.org/rec/STRTSC-2 (revised edition 2014: https://philpapers.org/rec/STRTSC-5)
- **Type**: Book (Oxford: Clarendon Press)
- **Key points**:
  - Against the standard "regularity theorist" Hume: Hume believed in causal influence but held that we cannot know its nature. Regular succession is all we can *know* of causation, not all there *is*.
  - "Sceptical realism": the causal power is real, and its nature lies beyond cognitive reach.
- **Tenet alignment**: This is the void in its cleanest form: a real relation with no representational foothold. Compare the Map's [intrinsic-nature-void](/voids/intrinsic-nature-void/) (structure known, intrinsic nature not).
- **Caveat**: The "New Hume" reading is contested (e.g. by Winkler, Beebee). The void does not depend on getting Hume right exegetically; it depends only on the epistemic claim.

### Michotte, A. (1946/1963), *The Perception of Causality* (Eng. trans. Methuen, 1963)
- **Type**: Book (classic experimental phenomenology)
- **Key points**: In the "launching effect", disc A moves to B and stops, and B moves off at once. Observers do not merely see two motions; they see A *make* B move. Small changes in timing (a delay of roughly 100+ ms) or spatial gap destroy the impression. Michotte argued the causal impression is a perceptual given, not an inference.
- **Note**: Cited from secondary sources (Scholl & Tremoulet 2000; Scholl & Nakayama 2002); the primary text was not consulted. Do not quote Michotte directly without checking.

### Scholl, B. J. & Tremoulet, P. D. (2000), "Perceptual causality and animacy"
- **URL**: https://perception.yale.edu/papers/00-Scholl-Tremoulet-TICS.pdf
- **Type**: Review, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 4(8): 299–309
- **Key points**: Simple moving-shape displays produce impressions of causality and animacy that are fast, automatic, and stimulus-driven, i.e. perceptual rather than cognitive. The visual system appears to *recover* causal structure as it recovers depth or colour.
- **Tenet alignment**: The causal impression comes from a module built for fitness, not for metaphysical accuracy. The "evolved for the savannah" point from [voids](/voids/) applies to one of our most basic categories.

### Scholl, B. J. & Nakayama, K. (2002), "Causal capture: Contextual effects on the perception of collision events"
- **URL**: https://perception.yale.edu/papers/02-Scholl-Nakayama-PsychSci.pdf (DOI 10.1111/1467-9280.00487)
- **Type**: Paper, *Psychological Science* 13(6): 493–498
- **Key points**: A full-overlap event that is normally seen as a noncausal "pass" is "captured" and "irresistibly seen as causal" when a distinct launch occurs nearby. The context need be present only 50 ms around the "impact"; 200 ms of asynchrony destroys the capture.
- **Quote** (abstract, verified from PDF): "When a distinct launch event occurs nearby, however, the test event is 'captured': It too is now irresistibly seen as causal."
- **Void significance**: The impression of causation can be manufactured for an event whose kinematics are unchanged. Physically there is no collision in either display (they are pixels), so the impression tracks cues, not causation.

### Choi, H. & Scholl, B. J. (2006), "Perceiving causality after the fact: Postdiction in the temporal dynamics of causal perception"
- **URL**: https://perception.yale.edu/papers/06-Choi-Scholl-Perception.pdf (DOI 10.1068/p5462)
- **Type**: Paper, *Perception* 35: 385–399
- **Key points**: Visual events occurring *after* the moment of potential impact can determine whether a collision is perceived.
- **Quote** (abstract, verified from PDF): "our conscious perception of the world is not an instantaneous moment-by-moment construction, but rather is formed by integrating information presented within short temporal windows, so that new information which is obtained can influence the immediate past in our conscious awareness."
- **Void significance**: What looks like *seeing* the cause produce the effect is assembled retrospectively. The experienced order ("A hit B, so B moved") is written after the fact.

### Rolfs, M., Dambacher, M. & Cavanagh, P. (2013), "Visual adaptation of the perception of causality"
- **URL**: https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(12)01490-X (DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2012.12.017)
- **Type**: Paper, *Current Biology* 23(3): 250–254
- **Key points**: After repeated exposure to launches, ambiguous events look more like passes (a negative aftereffect). The aftereffect is retinotopic: per the ScienceDaily report, "the after-effects moved when the eyes moved, just as the green after-image from adapting to red moves when we move our eyes." Rolfs: "causality can be computed at an early level in the visual system."
- **Note**: Abstract not retrieved (OpenAlex had none); the retinotopy detail and the Rolfs quote come from the ScienceDaily press release (https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/01/130110121028.htm). Verify against the paper before quoting in an article.

### Kominsky, J. F. & Scholl, B. J. (2020), "Retinotopic adaptation reveals distinct categories of causal perception"
- **Type**: Paper, *Cognition* 203: 104339 (DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2020.104339; metadata verified via Crossref; abstract not retrieved)
- **Key points (from title/metadata only)**: Different causal categories (e.g. launching vs. entraining/pushing) adapt separately. If so, "causation" in perception is several feature detectors, not one percept of one relation.

### Ohl, S. & Rolfs, M. (2024), "Visual routines for detecting causal interactions are tuned to motion direction"
- **URL**: https://elifesciences.org/articles/93454 (eLife; abstract verified via OpenAlex)
- **Key points**: Adaptation to launches does not transfer to unadapted launch *directions* but does transfer across speeds and colours; "adaptation was contingent on the causal impression in launches as demonstrated by a lack of adaptation in non-causal control events." The authors conclude that there are "specialized visual routines that are tuned to a launch's motion direction."
- **Void significance**: The best current evidence says the causal impression is a direction-tuned visual feature. What we call "seeing causation" is a detector firing on a kinematic template.

### Leslie, A. M. & Keeble, S. (1987), "Do six-month-old infants perceive causality?"
- **URL**: https://ruccs.rutgers.edu/images/archive/personal-alan-leslie/publications/Leslie%20&%20Keeble%201987.pdf
- **Type**: Paper, *Cognition* 25: 265–288
- **Key points**: 27-week-old infants dishabituate more to the reversal of a direct launch than to the reversal of a delayed (noncausal) event, even though the spatiotemporal change is identical. The causal impression predates language and learned regularity.
- **Void significance**: This works against Hume's account of the *origin* of the idea (custom from constant conjunction), but it does not help with the *access* problem. An innate impression is still an impression. It shows the category is built in, not that it reaches its object.

### Anscombe, G. E. M. (1971), *Causality and Determination* (inaugural lecture, Cambridge UP)
- **URL**: https://philpapers.org/rec/ANSCAD
- **Type**: Lecture/monograph
- **Key points**: The main counter-position. Causation is perceived: causal verbs ("scrape, push, wet, carry, eat, burn, knock over...", per secondary summaries) belong to observation language. She defends singularism: causation holds between particulars, independent of regularities elsewhere.
- **Note**: The verb list is from secondary sources (Gijsbers, PhilSci-Archive 19754); check the primary text before quoting.
- **Void significance**: Anscombe marks where the void is *contested*. If "seeing the knife cut the bread" is perception of causation, the void shrinks. The psychophysics suggests a reply: the "seeing" in such cases can be produced without the relation (capture, postdiction, adaptation), so it cannot simply *be* awareness of the relation. Siegel's view (below) offers a middle path.

### Siegel, S. (2010), *The Contents of Visual Experience* (OUP); also "The visual experience of causation" (*Philosophical Quarterly*, 2009)
- **URL**: https://philpapers.org/rec/SIETVE
- **Key points**: On the "Rich Content View", visual experience *represents* causal relations, as argued by the method of phenomenal contrast.
- **Void significance**: Representing causation and acquainting us with it are different things. Siegel can be right that causation is *in the content* of experience while the void persists, because content can be non-veridical and says nothing about the relation's nature.

### Maine de Biran, and Sinclair, M. (2020), "On effort and causal power: Maine de Biran's critique of Hume revisited"
- **URL**: DOI 10.1080/09608788.2020.1777933, *British Journal for the History of Philosophy* (abstract verified via OpenAlex)
- **Key points**: Biran argues that "awareness of the power of the will in effort, understood as the relation of will to resistance, is the fundamental fact of all consciousness." Sinclair revisits this in light of Hume's effort footnote. He concludes that Biran cannot answer Hume's separate charge against free will as a power removed from causal determination, and points to the later French spiritualist tradition for a fuller reply.
- **Tenet alignment**: This is the Map's most promising route *into* the void: the one place where a causal relation might be lived from the inside, not seen from outside. It connects to [phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/). The palsy case (EHU 7.13) is the standing objection: effort feels the same whether or not the limb moves.

### Haggard, P., Clark, S. & Kalogeras, J. (2002), "Voluntary action and conscious awareness"
- **URL**: https://www.nature.com/articles/nn827 (DOI 10.1038/nn827), *Nature Neuroscience* 5: 382–385
- **Key points**: The perceived times of voluntary actions and their consequences are drawn together ("intentional binding"). TMS-induced involuntary movements reverse the effect.
- **Void significance**: Even the felt timing of the agent's own causal episodes is shaped by whether the event is taken to be self-caused. First-person causal experience is also constructed; the constructing just happens inside the loop.
- **Caveat**: Whether intentional binding measures *agency* or causal belief more generally is disputed in the later literature (e.g. binding also occurs for observed/non-self causation). Treat it as evidence that causal *belief* shapes experienced time, not as a pure agency marker.

### Russell, B. (1913), "On the Notion of Cause"
- **Type**: Paper, *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 13: 1–26 (reprinted in *Mysticism and Logic*, 1917)
- **Quote** (widely reproduced; not verified against a page image): "The law of causality, I believe, like much that passes muster among philosophers, is a relic of a bygone age, surviving, like the monarchy, only because it is erroneously supposed to do no harm."
- **Void significance**: A second-order twist. Fundamental physics describes functional dependencies and states no "causes", so the concept may lack a clean object at the fundamental level. The void then has two floors: we do not perceive causation, and the physical theory that is supposed to replace perception does not contain it either.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Three layers:

1. **Perceptual**: Experience delivers an impression of causation, generated early in vision by cue-driven, adaptable, direction-tuned routines. The impression comes apart from the relation in both directions. Capture and postdiction show it appearing without any change in the relevant kinematics. Presumably it is also missing in the many real causal processes that lack launch-like kinematics (a magnet, a hormone, a radio signal).
2. **Introspective**: In one's own agency, as Hume's palsy case shows, the felt power of the will is identical whether or not the effect follows. Intentional binding shows that the felt timing of self-caused sequences is itself adjusted by the attribution.
3. **Conceptual/theoretical**: Nothing in the evidence tells us what causation *is* over and above counterfactual dependence or regularity. Physics does not settle it (Russell); the regularity view may be the shape of our ignorance, not a discovery (Strawson).

This makes it largely **Unexplorable**: no improvement in sensory acuity or introspective care would put the relation itself in view, because every channel we could use delivers content *about* a relation, never the relation. It is **Occluded** in a secondary way: the launching impression is so compelling ("irresistibly seen as causal") that ordinary cognition does not register a gap at all.

### Evidence for the Limit
- The impression can be induced (capture), reversed (adaptation), and set retrospectively (postdiction) while the physical facts stay constant, so it is not a transparent registration of those facts.
- The palsy argument shows the first-person route has the same structure: a feeling of power, silent on whether power is exercised.
- Two centuries of philosophical dispute (Hume vs. Biran, Anscombe vs. regularity theorists, powers vs. Humean supervenience) have not produced agreement on what causation is. This is consistent with a genuine limit, though it is not proof of one. Long dispute can also be ordinary difficulty.

### Phenomenology
Approaching the edge feels like looking harder at a collision and finding only more motion. The "push" is vivid at normal viewing and dissolves under scrutiny, much like semantic satiation of a word. In agency, attending to the "act of will" yields a sense of effort and readiness but no discernible passage of influence from will to limb. There is the intention, then the movement, and between them nothing that shows up as an item.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods
Largely unavailable. The strongest candidate is Biran's lived effort against resistance. If there is any first-person acquaintance with causal power, it is there. Hume's palsy case is the standing counterexample, and a Biranian must argue that the paralytic's effort *is* a causal exercise (on neural or motor systems) that fails further downstream. That reply keeps a causal acquaintance but relocates it somewhere we cannot introspectively confirm.

### Indirect Methods
- **Dissociation mapping**: Catalogue where the impression and the relation come apart (capture, postdiction, adaptation, causal illusions in magic, Wegner-style I-spy paradigms). The shape of the misfires reveals the template the impression uses, i.e. the shape of the void's edge.
- **Counterfactual/interventionist proxies** (Woodward-style): Replace "seeing causation" with "what would change under intervention". This is powerful, but it swaps the relation for a surrogate we can test; it does not open the void.
- **Parity reasoning**: Use the symmetry of ignorance (body→body vs. mind→body) to neutralise arguments that trade on the invisibility of mental causation specifically.

### What AI Might See
- An AI system has no Michotte module unless one is trained into it. Its "causal" judgments come from statistical dependence in text or data. It therefore sits on the Humean side of the void by construction: it can learn constant conjunction, and it can learn interventionist structure where it can act, but it lacks the vivid impression that makes humans feel they see causes. This asymmetry is useful. An AI can point out where human causal confidence outruns the evidence, because it is not fooled by the launching percept.
- Conversely, an AI that acts (runs experiments, calls tools) gets interventionist access without felt effort. Whether it has anything like Biran's "relation of will to resistance" is exactly the question the Map's dualism leaves open. Absent consciousness, it would have causal *efficacy* with no causal *impression*: the mirror image of the paralytic, who has the impression and lacks the efficacy.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet
**Bidirectional Interaction**, via the parity argument. The standard epiphenomenalist and closure-based pressure includes the intuition that mental causation is mysterious because we never see the mind *push* anything. This void shows the intuition proves too much: we never see *anything* push anything. Physical causation comes to us as regular succession plus a constructed impression. Mental causation comes as regular succession plus a felt effort, and that is no worse epistemically. Hume drew the parity himself, in his account of the occasionalists. The Map can use this to say that the [causal-interface](/voids/causal-interface/) void is one instance of a general causal-impression void, not a special embarrassment for interactionism.

### Implications
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits**: The regularity theory is the "parsimonious" reading of causal evidence only because the evidence can contain nothing but regularity. Parsimony here tracks the limit of access, not the structure of the world. This is a textbook case of the fifth tenet.
- **Dualism**: The Biranian thread suggests that if there is anywhere the causal relation is given from the inside, it is in conscious effort. That would make consciousness epistemically privileged with respect to causation, the one place the "secret connexion" might be lived, not merely inferred. This is speculative, and Hume's palsy case must be answered, not ignored.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction**: If physical causation at the fundamental level is not a "push" at all (Russell), then the Map's proposal of biased outcome selection is less exotic than it looks. It asks for a kind of influence that no causal picture actually displays anyway. Handle this with care: absence of a push in physics does not show that selection-style influence is present.
- **Caution against over-reach**: The parity argument neutralises one *intuitive* objection to mental causation. It does not answer the causal-closure or conservation arguments, which rest on physical theory, not on what we can observe. Articles should not present the parity point as a general defence of interactionism.

Based on this research, a voids article could:
1. **"The Causal Impression Void"**: We see motions and a visual system that stamps "cause" on some of them; causation itself never appears. Structure it as three floors (perceptual, introspective, theoretical). ## Gaps in Research

- Michotte's primary text was not consulted; all Michotte claims are via Scholl's reviews.
- Rolfs et al. 2013 and Kominsky & Scholl 2020 abstracts were not retrieved; details come from a press release and metadata. Verify before quoting.
- Anscombe's causal-verb list is from secondary sources.
- The critical literature on intentional binding (whether it measures agency, causality, or neither) was not surveyed. Needed before an article leans on it.
- The contemporary powers-metaphysics literature (Mumford & Anjum, Molnar) and its epistemology of powers was not covered; it may claim that powers are knowable in ways that would narrow the void.
- Biran's own texts were not consulted; the Biran position is via Sinclair's abstract and encyclopedia summaries.
- Fundamentally unanswerable (if the void is real): what causal connection *consists in*, as opposed to the conditions under which we report it.
