## Executive Summary

Starting with Radin et al. (2012), the Institute of Noetic Sciences (IONS) group reported that participants who alternately attended toward and away from a laser double-slit apparatus shifted its interference pattern. The 2012 paper reports z = −4.36 over 250 sessions, and the 2016 online study reports 5.72 sigma over 2,985 sessions with computer-run controls null. Every confirmatory or independently analysed leg since then is null. Tremblay's independent re-analysis of the 2016 dataset was republished in 2021 after a statistical error came to light, and the corrected version finds no significant shift. Walleczek & von Stillfried's report (2019) on a commissioned, pre-specified replication found no effect in the predicted conditions and a nominal false positive in its no-observer sham arm. The original group agrees that the planned analysis was null and disputes the sham result (Radin et al. 2020, 2021). Guerrer's independent preregistered formal sessions were null, though this has only ever appeared as a preprint. Radin's own 2025 study failed its three preregistered pooled hypotheses. The positive claims that remain come from analyses adopted after planned tests failed: bidirectional variance metrics, and exploratory spectral or trend measures.

For the Map the paradigm is a clean firewall case. Its design is a randomized, intention-conditioned contrast, which is the statistical shape of the test the corridor leaves open (horn (a) of `apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy`). It aims that contrast at an external optical system, however, so brain-locality predicts a null whether or not the interface exists. The nulls therefore confirm nothing. A replicated directional effect would cut against [P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2) and brain-locality, not support the tenets. The open question that `research/born-rule-violation-brain-interface-empirical-status-2026-04-23` (L327) left on record also needs a correction: it treated these studies as the closest attempt to probe Born statistics "inside a brain-coupled quantum system", but they never probe a brain-internal quantum system (see [Correction to the Open Question on Record](#correction)).

### Precursor: Ibison & Jeffers (1998) [PR]
- **Metadata**: Ibison, M., & Jeffers, S. (1998). A double-slit diffraction experiment to investigate claims of consciousness-related anomalies. *Journal of Scientific Exploration*, 12(4), 543–550. There is no Crossref DOI. Title, volume and pages agree across the reference lists of Radin et al. (2020) and Radin & Delorme (2022), but the JSE PDF could not be retrieved (404).
- **Key points**: the experiment was run at York University and Princeton (PEAR) on the same apparatus. Tremblay's verified characterization is "contradictory and inconclusive results". An aggregator page reproduces the abstract with Z = −0.481 (York) and Z = 1.654 (Princeton), but that is **not verified at JSE**, so do not cite the figures.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral (external apparatus).

### Radin et al. (2012), "Six experiments" [PR]
- **Metadata**: Radin, D., Michel, L., Galdamez, K., Wendland, P., Rickenbach, R., & Delorme, A. (2012). Consciousness and the double-slit interference pattern: Six experiments. *Physics Essays*, 25(2), 157–171. https://doi.org/10.4006/0836-1398-25.2.157 (Crossref).
- **Key points** (OpenAlex abstract): the predicted measure was the ratio of double-slit to single-slit spectral power, expected to fall during attention-toward epochs. 137 people contributed 250 sessions, z = −4.36, p = 6×10⁻⁶. 250 no-observer controls gave z = 0.43, p = 0.67. Meditation experience, EEG markers and openness/absorption correlated in the predicted directions.
- **Quote** (verified, OpenAlex abstract): "The results appear to be consistent with a consciousness-related interpretation of the quantum measurement problem."
- **Design note**: per Walleczek & von Stillfried (2019, verified), real-time feedback "was included in most, but not all, experiments", and only one of the six used a confirmatory design (their Table 3 note).

### Radin, Michel, Johnston & Delorme (2013) [PR]
- **Metadata**: *Physics Essays*, 26(4), 553–566. https://doi.org/10.4006/0836-1398-26.4.553 (Crossref).
- **Key points** (OpenAlex abstract): the paper reports three experiments. (1) A lab study of 50 sessions at 3 m, effect size −0.73 ± 0.14, p = 2.4×10⁻⁷, with 100 controls null. (2) An internet study with 685 people and 2,089 sessions, effect size −0.09 ± 0.02, p = 2.6×10⁻⁶, with 2,303 controls null and no distance correlation from 1 to 18,000 km. (3) A new apparatus with 20 sessions, p = 0.006.
- **Quote** (verified, OpenAlex abstract; "principle" sic): a Fraunhofer model "suggested that the distribution of light between the two slits and the horizontal stability of the laser beam were the principle components of the optical system that were perturbed". **For the Map**: the authors' own model places the perturbation in beam pointing and slit illumination, which are classical-optics variables. It does not place it in which-path information.

### Radin, Michel & Delorme (2016), online fringe-visibility study [PR]
- **Metadata**: Psychophysical modulation of fringe visibility in a distant double-slit optical system. *Physics Essays*, 29(1), 14–22. https://doi.org/10.4006/0836-1398-29.1.014 (Crossref).
- **Key points** (OpenAlex abstract): 1,479 people from 77 countries ran 2,985 sessions in 2013–2014, and the computer ran 5,738 control sessions. Human sessions "deviated from a null effect by 5.72 sigma" and controls by −0.17 sigma. The authors concluded "the mind of the observer is an inextricable part of the measurement process". Per Radin & Delorme (2022, verified), "In 2014, the feedback was inadvertently reversed", which inverted the line-to-visibility mapping. The data are open at https://osf.io/ywktp/.

### Tremblay (2019; republished 2021) [PR], independent re-analysis of the 2016 data
- **Metadata**: Tremblay, N. (2019). Independent re-analysis of alleged mind-matter interaction in double-slit experimental data. *PLOS ONE*, 14(2), e0211511. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0211511. Correction notice: *PLOS ONE*, 16(6), e0253686 (17 June 2021), https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0253686. Both are on Crossref, the correction is linked as a `correction` relation, and the full text is at PMC6366771.
- **Original 2019 abstract** (verified, S1 File of the notice): "we confirm the existence of significant although small anomalies in the direction predicted by the mind-matter interaction hypothesis", but "we also report significant although even smaller anomalies in the control dataset". It concluded the dataset "does not provide strong evidence of mind-matter interaction".
- **Republished version** (verified, PMC): the original test "contains an erroneous trimming procedure leading to uncontrolled false positives". Visibility shifts appear "in the direction expected", but they "are not deemed significant (p > 0.05)", and the dataset "does not contain evidence of mind-matter interaction". Year by year, the 2013 human sessions shift negative, the 2014 human sessions shift positive, and the 2013 controls shift positive. All are non-significant after correction. The directions follow the accidentally reversed feedback mapping, but at a level the re-analysis judges insignificant. Tremblay estimates that the original test understated the p-value by about five orders of magnitude (~10⁻⁸ reported against ~10⁻³ in his most favourable scenarios).
- **Notice** (verified): the trimming flaw "lead to uncontrolled false positives and thus underestimated p-values". It was identified by von Stillfried & Walleczek, and reviewers "confirmed that the main conclusions of the article are upheld". **Guard**: cite the republished version. Do not cite the 2019 "significant anomalies" sentence as current.

### Walleczek & von Stillfried (2019) [PR], the commissioned AMP replication
- **Metadata**: Walleczek, J., & von Stillfried, N. (2019). False-positive effect in the Radin double-slit experiment on observer consciousness as determined with the advanced meta-experimental protocol. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 10, 1891. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01891 (Crossref; PMC6714546). It was funded by the Fetzer Franklin Fund, and the reviewers were H. Walach and M. Maier.
- **Design** (verified): the study was "commissioned to be performed by the same investigator who carried out also the original Radin DS-experiment", with Radin running it at IONS. It comprised 250 true and 250 paired sham sessions of 20 trials each, 10,000 trials in total. The analysis was pre-specified, blinded, and run from Radin's own Matlab script. Notably, "none of the experiments in the commissioned replication study included psychological feedback".
- **Results** (verified): "the replication study was unable to confirm the original results". The match between predicted and actual true-positives was 0 of 2. The sham category X_S/O_S matched the predicted category and direction, at p = 0.021 (σ = −2.02; N = 1,250), with a 0.0159% effect size against the claimed 0.001%. The authors conclude that "skepticism should replace optimism concerning the radical claim that an anomalous quantum consciousness effect has been observed in a controlled laboratory setting".
- **Methodological point**: the earlier "robot" controls were not strictly paired with human sessions. The AMP sham reproduces the exact randomized trial sequence without a subject. This is the transferable lesson for any future conditioned test.

### The commissioned-study exchange [COM, PP]
- **Radin, Wahbeh, Michel & Delorme (2020)** [COM]. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 11, 726. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00726 (Crossref; PMC7174750). Verified points: it calls the study "an unpublished experiment our group conducted in 2012–2013" and says "we did not publish the results of that experiment at Walleczek's request". It argues for multiplicity correction across eight comparisons, after which "none of the eight tests were significant". It says that a variance comparison in the original script "remained significant after FDR adjustment". It counts 11 of 28 double-slit experiments significant, binomial p < 10⁻⁷, and acknowledges that "Some of the 28 experiments were exploratory".
- **Walleczek & von Stillfried (2020)** [COM]. Response…. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 11, 596125. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.596125 (Crossref; PMC7744610). Verified points: the chi-square test "was not pre-specified in advance" and sat in a later post hoc script version, which the authors call HARKing. They reply that each of the eight predictions was tested on its own dedicated dataset, so no random-multiplicity correction applies. They summarise that "Tremblay reported significant false-positives in control groups and Guerrer found significant effects with post-hoc analyses only".
- **Preprints in the exchange** [PP] (Crossref/OSF metadata only): Radin et al. (2019), PsyArXiv 10.31234/osf.io/9csgu. Walleczek & von Stillfried (2020), PsyArXiv 10.31234/osf.io/a2vkn. von Stillfried & Walleczek (2019), OSF project nyq5x, "Sham-experiments reveal a statistical error…".
- **Adjudication for the Map**: both sides agree that the true experiment's planned analysis was null. What they dispute is whether the sham p = 0.021 is a demonstrated artifact or a multiplicity-uncorrected fluke, and whether the variance test was planned. The Map needs neither dispute settled. The sham result is **contested** and should not be described as a proven artifact. The null is agreed.

### Radin, Wahbeh, Michel & Delorme (2021) [PR], the group's own report of the commissioned study
- **Metadata**: Psychophysical interactions with a double-slit interference pattern: Exploratory evidence of a causal influence. *Physics Essays*, 34(1), 79–88. https://doi.org/10.4006/0836-1398-34.1.79 (Crossref, publisher-deposited abstract).
- **Quotes** (verified): "Based on the planned analysis, no evidence for a psychophysical effect was found." The exploratory spectral metric gave 3.4 sigma, and 7 of 22 fringes exceeded 2.3 sigma after adjustment. The paper's closing caveat: "Future studies using the same protocols and analytical methods will be required to determine if these exploratory results are idiosyncratic or reflect a genuine psychophysical influence."

### Guerrer (2019) [PP], independent preregistered replication
- **Metadata**: Guerrer, G. Consciousness-related interactions in a double-slit optical system. OSF Preprints, https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/zsgwp. It was first posted 25 August 2017 and the project was modified in April 2019; the OSF project is qdkvx. It is conventionally cited as 2019. **It exists, but only as a preprint.** A Crossref author search found no journal version, so call it a preprint and do not call it peer-reviewed.
- **Quotes** (verified, OSF abstract): "In total, 240 sessions contributed by 171 volunteers were recorded." The first 160 sessions were exploratory and analysis variables were then "pre-registered for the subsequent 80 formal sessions". "The formal experiments, based on a directional hypothesis, were not statistically significant." A post hoc bidirectional meta-analysis gave 2.75 sigma, and the controls were uniformly non-significant. The author's own conclusion: "While the pre-registered analysis did not support the existence of the investigated phenomenon, the post hoc findings warrant further investigation to formally test the bi-directional hypothesis."
- **Design note**: participants intended "an increase in the (real-time feedback-informed) amount of light diffracted through a specific single slit". That is a which-slit intention with feedback.

### Radin & Delorme (2022) [PR], variance re-analysis of the 2016 data
- **Metadata**: Psychophysical effects on an interference pattern in a double-slit optical system: An exploratory analysis of variance. *Journal of Anomalous Experience and Cognition*, 2(2), 362–388. https://doi.org/10.31156/jaex.24054. Crossref and the publisher agree, except that Crossref's title omits the subtitle.
- **Quotes** (verified, publisher PDF): the authors say "we revisited the original directional hypothesis" in light of Guerrer, Tremblay and the commissioned study, and they record that Guerrer, "post-hoc he found a significant bidirectional effect". Their method was developed on the 2013 data and applied to the 2014 data: "z = 4.16, p = .00002" for 2013 and "z = 3.14, p = .0008" for 2014. Their stated limitation: "The primary limitation in this reanalysis is that it is unknown if the same analytical approach could successfully detect results in a similarly designed experiment." They claim that "revising the original hypothesis from directional to bidirectional reverses Tremblay’s conclusion".
- **Calibration**: the held-out-year check is a real strength. The metric was still chosen after the directional tests failed, on a dataset whose directional test had already been examined.

### Radin (2025) [PR], preregistered home-apparatus study
- **Metadata**: Observer influence on quantum interference: Testing the von Neumann‐Wigner consciousness-collapse theory. *Physics Essays*, 38(1), 64–80. https://doi.org/10.4006/0836-1398-38.1.64 (Crossref abstract).
- **Quote** (verified): "With all data combined the results did not support three preregistered hypotheses". It reports an outward- versus inward-focus subgroup difference (p = 0.008) and an exploratory progressive decline in observed versus unobserved regions (p = 5.9×10⁻¹⁴), with no-observer controls non-significant. Forty-seven attention-experienced participants used custom apparatus with feedback, environmental sensors and real-time encryption. The abstract names no registry, and the IONS page carries no registry link, so the registration should be located before it is cited as preregistered by ID.

### Adjacent: Guerrer, Walleczek, von Stillfried, Kffuri & Moll (2026) [PP]
- **Metadata**: Investigating the claim of anomalous psychophysical interactions using a rigorous metascientific framework. PsyArXiv, https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/wkjdu_v1 (1 April 2026). This is an **RNG** study, not a double-slit study.

Brain-locality still predicts its null.

## Major Positions

### Proponent reading (Radin, Delorme, Wahbeh, Michel)
- **Core claim**: focused attention or intention perturbs the interference pattern. The effect is small, unstable within a session, and better detected as variance than as a mean shift.
- **Key arguments**: the 2012–2016 positives, the human-versus-robot asymmetry, and the claim that 11 of 28 experiments were significant. They argue that the commissioned study's sham p needs multiplicity correction, and they cite a held-out-year variance replication.
- **Relation to tenets**: proponents frame the work as a test of von Neumann–Wigner collapse, while Radin & Delorme (2022) align it with dual-aspect monism. Neither framing is the Map's. A confirmed effect would be external, observer-conditioned psychokinesis on an optical apparatus, which falls on the disconfirming side of the firewall.

### Systematic-error reading (Walleczek, von Stillfried; Tremblay; Guerrer's formal result)
- **Core claim**: under pre-specified or corrected analysis no effect survives. The apparatus can produce sham positives at the claimed magnitude, so positive replications cannot be read without counterfactual sham controls.
- **Relation to tenets**: this reading is fully compatible with the Map, and so is the no-interface world. It is evidentially inert between them.

### Measurement-problem framing (Ibison & Jeffers; Tremblay's introduction)
- **Core claim**: attention as a weak which-path measurement should "very slightly attenuate the interference pattern". Tremblay (verified): "if it really acts as a weak measurement of the which-way information, should very slightly attenuate the interference pattern".
- **Map-side observation**: the participant never receives which-path information. The attention is directed at the apparatus or at a feedback display. On orthodox and consciousness-causes-collapse readings alike, a loss of visibility needs which-path correlation, which attention to a display does not supply. The paradigm therefore tests intention-driven external psychokinesis on an optical random source more than it tests the measurement problem. This is a Map-side inference, not a source claim.

## Key Debates

### Does the sham false positive show a systematic artifact?
- **Sides**: Walleczek & von Stillfried say yes, because a dedicated, predicted test with no subject reached p = 0.021. Radin et al. say no, because across eight comparisons with FDR nothing is significant, and a variance test survived.
- **Current state**: unresolved. Each side accuses the other of departing from the protocol: Radin et al. say the multiplicity correction is needed, and Walleczek & von Stillfried call Radin's variance test HARKing. Both agree that the planned analysis of the true experiment was null.

### Mean shift or variance shift?
- **Sides**: Radin & Delorme (2022) and Guerrer's post hoc analysis favour a bidirectional variance metric, which the AMP authors regard as post hoc.
- **Current state**: no preregistered confirmatory test of the variance hypothesis has been found, and Guerrer's own abstract calls for one.

## Calibration Verdict {#calibration}

Measured against the brief's discipline and the firewall's L51–L79 logic:

1. **Unconditioned aggregate.** The corridor predicts nothing visible in any unconditioned test. The paradigm is not an unconditioned test, so that prediction is not engaged.
2. **What the design actually is.** It is a randomized within-session contrast between instructed attend and relax epochs. In the notation of `apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy` it compares P(O | do(C), X) across C, which is the horn (a) shape. The locus O is an external photon-detection record, not a brain-internal transition.
3. **Brain-locality.** Under `concepts/brain-interface-boundary` (the four locality constraints; falsifier 1, external PK), the paradigm is out of scope, exactly as the Map already holds for Maier et al. (2018) (firewall L51, tenets L75, [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3) update 2026-09-27). A null is predicted on either horn of the trilemma, so **the nulls (Tremblay corrected, the AMP true-experiment, Guerrer formal, Radin 2021 planned, Radin 2025 pooled) do not confirm the Map**. They are equally expected if there is no interface.
4. **A positive result would count against the Map.** A replicated, preregistered, sham-controlled directional shift in visibility conditioned on instructed intention would be an observer-conditioned deviation in a publicly conditionable context at an external system. That contradicts [P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2)'s wording ("Born-exact in every publicly conditionable context"), brain-locality, and the firewall's third category (lawlike ensemble effects). It would be evidence **against** the Map's Born-preserving, brain-local interface. It would favour some other mind-matter hypothesis, not the tenets. The 2013 authors' own model, which locates the perturbation in beam pointing and slit illumination, makes the effect a classical-optics anomaly before it is a quantum one.
5. **The variance claims are the tempting case.** A variance shift with no mean shift resembles a conditioned deviation that cancels in the aggregate, which is horn (c) at session grain. Reading that resemblance as the interface's footprint would be the possibility-to-probability slippage the firewall (L73) forbids. Horn (c) also carries its own debt, a balancing law the Map has not stated. The metric was adopted post hoc, so it earns nothing either way until it passes a preregistered confirmatory test.
6. **Which conditioned version would bear on [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3).** None of these studies does under the Map's current scope. The version that would is the paradigm's design transplanted inward: randomized do(C) instructions, a single preregistered directional metric, AMP-style paired sham sessions, encrypted blinded data, and as the target a quantum-indeterminate transition inside the subject's own neural processing that the corridor posits selection acts on. No such observable yet exists (`topics/brain-internal-born-rule-testing`, `topics/sorkin-delta-brain-internal-analogues`). The paradigm's methodological record (sham pairing, held-out validation, the trimming failure) is what transfers.
7. **Scope caveat: feedback designs.** Most of these studies (most of 2012, 2016, Guerrer, 2025) had the photon record drive real-time feedback that the participant perceives; the commissioned AMP study had none. The brain-locality exemption assumes the external record is already actualized before it reaches the participant. The Map's post-decoherence account gives objective reduction that job outside interface-grade brains (`concepts/post-decoherence-selection` L3, L96; `concepts/prebiotic-collapse`), but its collapse-priority debt is open. Whether collapse models actualize a low-mass detector record before perception is a known question in that literature (leads with Crossref-verified metadata, content **not** re-read this session: Albert & Vaidman 1989, *Phys. Lett. A* 139, 1–4, doi:10.1016/0375-9601(89)90595-1; Aicardi, Borsellino, Ghirardi & Grassi 1991, *Found. Phys. Lett.* 4, 109–128, doi:10.1007/BF00666047). If the record were unreduced when the participant registered the feedback, selection over the participant's perceptual branch would co-select the correlated record, and feedback designs would fall inside the corridor's reach as horn (a) tests. On that reading the preregistered feedback nulls (Guerrer formal, Radin 2025 pooled) would press weakly toward horn (b), the epiphenomenal side, and so count *against* the Map's answer to [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3), not for it. This is a Map-side inference for `/positions-evolve` or `concepts/brain-interface-boundary` to audit. It is not a finding to publish on the firewall page.
8. **Psi results are not support.** None of this record supports any tenet, and the fold must not say or imply otherwise.

## Correction to the Open Question on Record {#correction}

`research/born-rule-violation-brain-interface-empirical-status-2026-04-23` L327 calls "Radin's double-slit-observation studies (2013-2016)" the closest historical attempt to "probe the Born distribution inside a brain-coupled quantum system". Three corrections apply. The series begins in 2012, with the 1998 precursor before it. The quantum system is an external laser interferometer coupled to the brain only through classical feedback, so it is not brain-internal. The methodology and preregistration review that note asked for is supplied here: there was no preregistration before Guerrer and the 2025 study, and the confirmatory legs are null.
