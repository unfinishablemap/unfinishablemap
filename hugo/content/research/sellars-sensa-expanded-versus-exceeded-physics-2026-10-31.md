## Executive Summary

The question was whether the line the Sellars page draws at L65 ("The Map differs from Sellars only on whether keeping them requires expanding physics or exceeding it") marks a real difference. **It does not mark one where it says.** Sellars' late answer to the grain problem, in the third Carus Lecture, "Is Consciousness Physical?" (1981), recasts sensa as absolute sensory processes (σ-ings). He says they are "physical₁ but not physical₂", physical "in the richer sense of playing a genuine causal role in the behavior of sentient organisms" (¶126). A note in the same lecture glosses physical₁ as "any which belong in the causal order" (n. 15). On that gloss an interacting consciousness of the Map's kind is also physical₁ and not physical₂. Sellars himself called the difference between emergent ("wholistic") materialism and substantial dualism "purely ontological" (¶87), and called the wholistic materialists "metaphysical cousins of interactionistic dualists" (n. 22). Feigl (1958) had already observed that interactionism and Meehl–Sellars emergence alike "would in any case entail a breach in physical₂ determinism".

Sellars' view is **not a dualism by another name**. He presents the process ontology as freeing its adopters from "this last refuge of metaphysical dualism" (¶119), and he openly declares a second, irreducible kind of process, so the Map's [disguised-property-dualism](/concepts/disguised-property-dualism/) diagnostic does not fit. On the "no fundamental mentality" (NFM) reading the verdict turns on the word "mental". Sellars calls σ-ings physical "in the weak sense of not being mental (i.e., conceptual), for they lack intentionality" (¶126). If phenomenal quality counts as mental, primitive σ-ings are fundamental mentality, and NFM classes his late view as non-physicalist, as [hempels-dilemma](/topics/hempels-dilemma/) says it classes emergentism. What genuinely separates the Map from Sellars is two commitments the word "physical" does not settle. (1) The **subject**: a person is "a bundle of absolute processes" (¶125), where the Map's agency cluster posits a persisting subject. (2) **Conceptual consciousness**: his affirmative answer is confined to sensory consciousness, and he leaves open that conceptual acts "might be complex physical₂ states" (¶91). On causal efficacy they **agree**: Sellars rejects epiphenomenalism (¶126).

## Answers to the Research Questions

### 1. What exactly did Sellars hold about sensa?

- **1956, Meehl & Sellars, "The Concept of Emergence"** (*Minnesota Studies* I: 239–252). This paper is where the terms first appear in print, and their aim is narrow: "Our aim is not to defend an emergentist picture of the world, but rather to criticize an argument which, if successful, would make this picture indefensible". The argument is Pepper's (1926), that emergents must be epiphenomenal. Their candidate emergents are "raw feels", which they suppose emergent "though not epiphenomenal". They work in the living brain, where "The flow of electrons at the synaptic interface 'breaks the laws.' But it is not lawless". The definitions are at p. 252:
  - **Physical₁**: "an event or entity is physical₁ if it belongs in the space-time network."
  - **Physical₂**: "an event or entity is physical₂ if it is definable in terms of theoretical primitives adequate to describe completely the actual states though not necessarily the potentialities of the universe before the appearance of life."
  - The emergentist account "denies that the latter are physical₂. But this in no way involves the denial that they are physical₁." They close: "Whether or not there are any emergents in the sense we have sought to clarify is an empirical question." **In 1956 Sellars did not endorse emergent raw feels.** Feigl's 1958 description is accurate: "a theory of emergence, such as the one suggested, though not definitely endorsed, by Meehl and Sellars".
- **1965, "The Identity Approach to the Mind-Body Problem"** (*Review of Metaphysics* 18: 430–51, per the ditext bibliography). Sense-impression universals would be exemplified by "a manifold of logical subjects which might be called ... sensa". This is an early use of "sensa", so the Sellars page's "His late metaphysics turned to sensa" is loose. The late work *recasts* sensa as absolute processes; it does not introduce them.
- **1971, "Science, Sense Impressions, and Sensa: A Reply to Cornman"** (*Review of Metaphysics* 24(3), March 1971: 391–447). **Not read.** The bibliographic details were confirmed only at the ditext bibliography and in the Rosenberg 2007 abstract (title and year 1971). PhilPapers reportedly dates it 1970, and the Review of Metaphysics publisher page is behind Cloudflare. It answers Cornman, "Sellars, Scientific Realism, and Sensa", *Review of Metaphysics* 23 (1969–70): 417–51 (SEP bibliography; not read). Treat its content as unverified.
- **1981, Carus Lectures, "Foundations for a Metaphysics of Pure Process"** (*The Monist* 64(1): 3–90; lectures given 1977–78). Lecture I, "The Lever of Archimedes", pp. 3–36, doi:10.5840/monist19816412. Lecture II, "Naturalism and Process", pp. 37–65, doi:10.5840/monist19816416. Lecture III, "Is Consciousness Physical?", pp. 66–90, doi:10.5840/monist19816415. All titles and pages are Crossref-verified. Text was read in Andrew Chrucky's transcription at ditext. The body is at `carus3.html` with notes at `ncarus3.html`; **`carus.html`, `carus300.html` and the `-con` page are framesets**. The transcription was cross-checked against the SEP's verbatim quotation of III ¶47 and matched. Paragraph numbers are Sellars' own. The transcription renders Greek in Symbol font, so `s-ings` = **σ-ings**, `f2-ings` = **φ₂-ings** and `y` = **ψ**.
  - III ¶4: the title question concerns **sensory** consciousness only, "the sort of consciousness we have simply by virtue of feeling a pain or sensing a cube-of-pinkly". ¶2 stresses "the radical difference between sensory states and conceptual states".
  - III ¶¶75–110 run through the options. Substantial Dualism (¶75) and Reductive Materialism (¶79) come first; the latter is "absurd" at this stage (¶80). Next are Emergent or Wholistic Materialism (¶84) and Epiphenomenalism (¶88). ¶87: "Clearly, the difference between this position and the dualistic position presented above is purely ontological. The basic objects of the latter include both micro-physical particles and sensoria (or, if you prefer, minds). The ontology of Wholistic Materialism includes only micro-physical particles." ¶110: "The diagram is the same; only the ontology is different." The diagram there is the epiphenomenalist-form law-schema both camps historically adopted (¶109), so do not generalise ¶110 into "dualism and emergentism are nomologically equivalent".
  - III n. 22: "The wholistic materialists referred to in effect are metaphysical cousins of interactionistic dualists. The latter reify the functional correlation of variables of radically different kinds (mental and material) by assigning them as states to different substances (minds, bodies)."
  - III ¶¶111–125 give the process turn. Objects are "patterns of" absolute processes (¶112). The domain includes φ₂-ings and σ-ings, the latter being "the transposition of sensa into the framework of absolute process" (¶115). Accepting the ontology frees one "from this last refuge of metaphysical dualism" (¶119). Sellars says an ideal successor theory might constitute neurons in the visual cortex "that they had σ-ings as ingredients" (¶124). "A person would be a bundle of absolute processes, both σ-ings and φ₂-ings" (¶125).
  - III ¶126, the key passage: "σ-ings would be physical, not only in the weak sense of not being mental (i.e., conceptual), for they lack intentionality, but in the richer sense of playing a genuine causal role in the behavior of sentient organisms. They would, as I have used the terms, be physical₁ but not physical₂. Not being epiphenomenal they would conform to a basic metaphysical intuition to be is to make a difference." The transcription has an unmatched "(" before "σ-ings" and a footnote marker 25 after "conceptual),". Note 25: "After all, 'physical' functions traditionally as a contrastive term." Note 26 cites Plato, *Sophist* 248C.
  - III ¶127: "the answer to the question which gives this essay its title is affirmative with respect to sensory consciousness. As for the further question, Is conceptual consciousness physical? a whole new dialectic must be followed."
  - III ¶91: "I am not coping in this essay with the mind-body problem. As far as anything I have said is concerned, conceptual acts might be complex physical₂ states of a highly organized system of micro-physical particles."
  - III n. 15 gives the 1981 definitions. Physical₂ features are those "definable in terms of attributes exemplified in the world before the appearance of sentient organisms, i.e., attributes necessary and sufficient to describe and explain the behavior of 'merely material' things." "Physical₁ features on the other hand, are any which belong in the causal order. I introduced this terminology in discussions of the mind-body problem in the Minnesota Center for Philosophy of Science." **The definitions drift**: 1956 has "before the appearance of life" and "space-time network"; 1981 has "before the appearance of sentient organisms" and "causal order".
- **1982, "Sensa or Sensings: Reflections on the Ontology of Perception"** (*Philosophical Studies* 41(1): 83–111, doi:10.1007/bf00353525; Crossref-verified; not read). III n. 16 cites it as forthcoming in *Essays in Honor of James Cornman* (ed. Lehrer). It is the sensa-versus-sensings sequel.
- **SEP (deVries 2024, §5.2 "The Sensory")**, verified raw: "They are, in the terminology Sellars developed with his colleague Paul Meehl, physical1 but not physical2". Also: "science will postulate a distinctive set of entities, sensa, to be the final embodiment of the sensible qualities, physical1 but not physical2. Sellars is a physicalist about color, not because he thinks colors can be reduced to more basic properties and relations of physical objects, but because physics will have to expand to accommodate the sensible qualities, color included, as primitive entities." And §2 (the manifest-image section): Sellars' third alternative treats manifest objects as appearances of "a reality constituted by systems of imperceptible particles or something even more basic, such as absolute processes (see FMPP)".

### 2. Would his expanded physics count as physicalist under the object-based and via-negativa conceptions?

- **Object-based.** The SEP (Stoljar 2026, §4.1) names "Meehl and Sellars 1956 and Feigl 1967" as "the best examples of philosophers who operate with the object-conception of the physical". The Map's own analysis follows; the SEP does not say this. Physical₂ is the paradigm-anchored notion: what suffices for the pre-life universe. Read that way it is close to the object-based conception with pre-life matter as the paradigm. **On it, Sellars' own sensa are non-physical**, because they are not physical₂. Only the wide physical₁ makes them physical.
- **Theory-based.** The SEP says the theory-based conception "bears some relation to the notion of physical1 discussed in Feigl 1967". **Feigl's physical₁ is not Meehl–Sellars'.** Feigl (1958: 377) glosses it as "practically synonymous with 'scientific', i.e., with being an essential part of the coherent and adequate descriptive and explanatory account of the spatio-temporal-causal world". This is the second horn: whatever completed science must posit.
- **Via negativa / NFM.** Sellars himself uses a contrastive, negative sense: σ-ings are physical "in the weak sense of not being mental (i.e., conceptual)", and "'physical' functions traditionally as a contrastive term" (n. 25). He passes his own via negativa **only because he reserves "mental" for the conceptual/intentional.** If phenomenal quality is mental, as the Map's usage and the Montero framing on [hempels-dilemma](/topics/hempels-dilemma/) assume, σ-ings are fundamental (basic absolute processes, not patterns of φ₂-ings) and mental. NFM then classes Sellars' late view as non-physicalist. **Non-physicalist ≠ dualist**: it is a process monism with an irreducible second kind of basic process, found only in sentient contexts (¶121). The nearest Map neighbours are emergentism and process monism. Do not say Sellars anticipated or originated the via negativa. The point is only that he uses a negative sense of "physical"; no priority claim was checked.

### 3. Is the Map's "exceeding" distinct from Sellars' "expanding" in any way that matters?

- **At the level of physics, no.** Feigl 1958: 378: "Mental states or raw feels, be they regarded as states of an interacting substantial mind (or soul) or as values of emergent scientific variables, would in any case entail a breach in physical₂ determinism." (OCR prints "physicaI2".) Sellars 1981 ¶87 makes the difference "purely ontological". By the n. 15 gloss, any consciousness that belongs in the causal order is physical₁. The Map's [Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction) places consciousness there. **This inference is the note's own.** Sellars does not discuss the Map's view, and the 1956 "space-time network" wording might exclude a non-spatial mind; the 1981 wording does not.
- So the L65 sentence, read as a claim about physics, collapses into the second horn of [hempels-dilemma](/topics/hempels-dilemma/) (the "admitting fundamental mentality" charge). "Physical₁" is wide enough to include an interacting mind, so it cannot keep physicalism opposed to dualism.
- **At the level of ontology, yes, but not where L65 puts it.** The substantive differences are these.
  1. **Subject vs bundle.** Sellars: "A person would be a bundle of absolute processes" (¶125). His n. 24 faults Hume's bundle theory only for leaving out bodily states, since "the unity of the self ... requires the inclusion of bodily states". The Map's agency cluster deploys a persisting, numerically determinate subject (Posit One, [background-commitments](/tenets/background-commitments/); `tenets.md` "Practical ontology of the agency cluster"). Sellars n. 22 points the same way: interactionist dualists "reify the functional correlation ... by assigning them as states to different substances".
  2. **Conceptual consciousness.** Sellars' expansion admits only *sensory* quality as primitive. Conceptual acts "might be complex physical₂ states" (¶91), and normativity is accommodated "not as a basic, ontologically independent feature of the world" (SEP §2, verified raw). The Map's [argument-from-reason](/topics/argument-from-reason/) contests exactly this ("grasping logical relationships cannot be a purely physical achievement").
  3. **Optional, for the agency cluster only.** Meehl–Sellars raw feels are lawful: the electron flow "breaks the laws" of the pre-emergent function, "But it is not lawless". Late σ-ings enter psychophysical "uniformities" (¶121). The Map's agency cluster wants agent-causal selection. Feigl (1958: 386) separates interactionism with a self from interactionism "without a self" that "would require 'spontaneously' arising mental states". Do not put this in the main fold: it imports the substance-leaning sub-reading, which `tenets.md` warns against leaking.
- **Agreement, to state plainly.** Sellars rejects epiphenomenalism for sensory consciousness. The point of Meehl & Sellars 1956 is that emergents need not be epiphenomenal, and Sellars 1981 ¶126 has "to be is to make a difference". This is a resource for the Map's anti-epiphenomenalist pages. The Map concedes nothing here.

## Originator and Disambiguation Checks

- **physical₁/physical₂: Meehl & Sellars 1956 (p. 252).** Sellars says so himself: "I introduced this terminology in discussions of the mind-body problem in the Minnesota Center for Philosophy of Science" (1981 III n. 15). The SEP has "the terminology Sellars developed with his colleague Paul Meehl". Feigl 1958: 377 has "In accord[ance] with the terminology of Meehl and Sellars (221)" (OCR hyphenates "accord-ance"). **Feigl adopted and redefined the terms; he did not coin them.** Credit the pair to "Meehl and Sellars (1956)", not to Sellars alone and not to Feigl.
- **"Absolute processes": C. D. Broad.** Carus II ¶50: "Broad introduces the concept of what he calls 'absolute processes'". II n. 9 cites *Examination of McTaggart's Philosophy*, vol. I (1933), pp. 141–66. Sellars developed the concept; he did not coin it.
- **"reddings"**: Carus II ¶100 credits the word to John Wisdom ("a word coined by John Wisdom in the early 30s").
- **"to be is to make a difference"**: Sellars' phrase. He grounds it in Plato, *Sophist* 248C (III n. 26), the source of the "Eleatic principle". Do not attribute the slogan to Plato.
- **Sellars ≠ Sellars.** Roy Wood Sellars, Wilfrid's father, was an "evolutionary naturalist" in the double-aspect/identity line. He published his own "Is Consciousness Physical?" in *Journal of Philosophy* 19(25) (1922): 690–694, identified from the PDC URL and JSTOR listing only, not opened. That is the same title as Wilfrid's Lecture III. Any "Sellars' naturalism" or "Is Consciousness Physical?" citation must name **Wilfrid**, and a writer must not cite the 1922 paper by mistake. O'Shea, "Critical Direct Realism? New Realism, Roy Wood Sellars, and Wilfrid Sellars", *Topoi* 43(1) (2024): 135–145, doi:10.1007/s11245-023-09958-7 (Crossref only), treats the relation.
- **Object-based conception.** The SEP credits Meehl & Sellars and Feigl as "the best examples". Stoljar named the theory/object distinction ([hempels-dilemma](/topics/hempels-dilemma/) already records this correctly).
- **Not to be cited.** A "Notre Dame Lectures 1969–1986" PDF at Brandom's Pitt course site calls itself "The Bootleg Version". It is a transcription of recorded lectures. It was read for orientation only and is **not** citable; nothing above rests on it.

**Passage** (~360 words):

> **Expanding or exceeding physics.** Sellars' own vocabulary blurs the line between expanding physics and exceeding it. Meehl and Sellars (1956) introduced the terms: an entity is physical₁ "if it belongs in the space-time network", and physical₂ if definable in primitives adequate to describe the universe "before the appearance of life". In Carus Lecture III, "Is Consciousness Physical?", sensa become absolute sensory processes, σ-ings. They are physical "in the richer sense of playing a genuine causal role in the behavior of sentient organisms", and they "would, as I have used the terms, be physical₁ but not physical₂" (Sellars 1981, ¶126). A note in the lecture glosses physical₁ features as "any which belong in the causal order" (n. 15). On that gloss a consciousness that acts on the brain, as [Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction) requires, would also be physical₁ but not physical₂; the inference is the Map's. Sellars himself called the difference between emergent materialism and substantial dualism "purely ontological", a matter of which basic objects exist (¶87). His physical₁ has the defect [hempels-dilemma](/topics/hempels-dilemma/) finds in future-physics definitions: wide enough to include an interacting mind, it cannot keep physicalism opposed to dualism.
>
> The "no fundamental mentality" constraint turns on what counts as mental. Sellars calls σ-ings physical "in the weak sense of not being mental (i.e., conceptual), for they lack intentionality" (¶126). If phenomenal quality counts as mental, as in the Map's usage, primitive σ-ings are fundamental mentality, and the constraint classes his late view as non-physicalist, as it does emergentism. Non-physicalist is not dualist. Sellars offers the process ontology as freedom from "this last refuge of metaphysical dualism" (¶119). Since he names the second kind openly and claims no reduction, the [disguised-property-dualism](/concepts/disguised-property-dualism/) diagnostic does not fit.
>
> Two commitments divide the Map from Sellars, not the label "physical". He makes a person a bundle of absolute processes (¶125), where the Map's agency cluster posits a [persisting subject](/tenets/background-commitments/). And his affirmative answer covers sensory consciousness only: he leaves open that conceptual acts "might be complex physical₂ states" (¶91), which the Map's [argument-from-reason](/topics/argument-from-reason/) contests. On causal efficacy they agree: σ-ings are not epiphenomenal, and so conform to the intuition "to be is to make a difference" (¶126).

## Optional Secondary (host: `topics/hempels-dilemma`, §The Object-Based Conception, L69)

Host length: analyze_length 2,717 against topics hard 4,000. Append after OLD "The object-based conception itself is older; the SEP cites Meehl and Sellars, and Feigl, as its best examples." (~75 words):

## Guards for the Writer

- **Do not call Sellars a dualist, and do not enlist him for dualism.** He calls the process ontology freedom from "this last refuge of metaphysical dualism" (¶119). Landy reconstructs him as rejecting substance dualism. The NFM point is a classification ("non-physicalist"), not a conversion; keep "Non-physicalist is not dualist".

## Relation to Site Tenets

- **Dualism (Tenet 1).** The finding sharpens the open question [hempels-dilemma](/topics/hempels-dilemma/) leaves. On its theory-relative wording ("cannot be fully explained by physics alone"), Tenet 1 cannot separate the Map from Sellars on sensory quality, because physical₁ absorbs both. On the Montero/NFM restatement, Sellars' σ-ings pass the test for non-physicalism *for sensory consciousness*. The Map's distinctiveness must then be stated as a claim about the subject and about conceptual/rational consciousness. This is a reason, not a proof, to prefer the restatement.
- **Bidirectional Interaction (Tenet 3).** This is congenial: Meehl & Sellars 1956 is a formal defence of non-epiphenomenal emergents against Pepper, and Sellars 1981 makes σ-ings causally efficacious. The two papers are leads for [epiphenomenalism](/concepts/epiphenomenalism/) and [the-epiphenomenalist-threat](/topics/the-epiphenomenalist-threat/) (Pepper 1926 and Meehl–Sellars have 0 live hits).
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction (Tenet 2).** Not engaged. Sellars locates σ-ings as "ingredients" of neurons with no quantum locus. Pepper's 1926 dilemma, as Meehl & Sellars quote it, assumed "there are no chance occurrences". That is a possible lead for the Map's selection-at-indeterminacy pages, but the words are **Pepper's, quoted in CE**, not Meehl & Sellars'.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits (Tenet 5).** Feigl's 1958 scepticism about emergence rests on parsimony: "the rule of parsimony (Ockham's razor, or Newton's first regula philosophandi) warns us not to multiply entities". He promised to "cheerfully accept this enrichment" if research showed the need. This is a clean historical instance of the parsimony stance Tenet 5 contests, and it is conditional, not dogmatic.
- **No Many Worlds (Tenet 4).** Not engaged.
