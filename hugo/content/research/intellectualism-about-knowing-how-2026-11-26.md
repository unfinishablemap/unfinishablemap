## Executive Summary

Ryle (1949, ch. 2) split *knowing how* from *knowing that* and attacked the "intellectualist legend", the view that intelligent action is guided by prior consideration of propositions, with a regress (pp. 30–32). Stanley and Williamson (2001) revived intellectualism. They argue that Ryle's regress equivocates on "action", that his ability analysis is "demonstrably false", and that "Knowledge-how is simply a species of knowledge-that", known under a "practical mode of presentation". Their paper ends by applying this to **Lewis's ability hypothesis**: Lewis's account "is incorrect", and the ability reply "in fact entails that Mary acquires propositional knowledge". That bears directly on the knowledge argument, and no Map page mentions it. **The verdict is not a win for the Map.** Cath (2008 thesis; 2009 *Noûs*) shows that the ability reply survives in modified form. Mary comes to know an *old* proposition under a *new*, practical mode. That makes it an old-fact/new-mode reply, so it falls under the phenomenal-concept family the Map already engages, where Chalmers's New Fact Thesis is the standard objection. **Grade: compatible.** Intellectualism gives no evidence for dualism. If true, it removes the ability hypothesis as an *independent* physicalist reply. It also hurts the Map slightly: it supplies a non-mind-body precedent for special modes of presentation, which weakens the Map's "ad hoc" objection to the phenomenal concepts strategy. Topics and concepts are both full (370/370 by `count_section_files`), so the verdict is **fold**. ## Key Sources

### Ryle, *The Concept of Mind* (1949), ch. 2 "Knowing How and Knowing That"
- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/gilbert-ryle-concept-of-mind
- **Type**: Book (primary). OCR slips corrected only where noted.
- **Key points** (all grepped):
  - Intelligence-epithets impute "not the knowledge, or ignorance, of this or that truth, but the ability, or inability, to do certain sorts of things" (p. 27; OCR "orignorance").
  - The anti-Cartesian frame: the assumption that theorising is the mind's primary activity, and a private one, "remains one of the main supports of the dogma of the ghost in the machine" (p. 27).
  - Asymmetries: "we never speak of a person believing or opining how" (p. 28).
  - **Ryle names the target** "the intellectualist legend" (p. 29). "Champions of this legend are apt to try to reassimilate knowing how to knowing that by arguing that intelligent performance involves the observance of rules, or the application of criteria" (p. 29). On the legend, the agent "must preach to himself before he can practise" (p. 29; OCR "dictates.sHe").
  - **The ch. 2 regress**: "The crucial objection to the intellectualist legend is this. The consideration of propositions is itself an operation the execution of which can be more or less intelligent, less or more stupid. But if, for any operation to be intelligently executed, a prior theoretical operation had first to be performed and performed intelligently, it would be a logical impossibility for anyone ever to break into the circle" (p. 30). "The regress is infinite, and this reduces to absurdity the theory that for an operation to be intelligent it must be steered by a prior intellectual operation" (pp. 31–32; OCR "that-for").
  - Conclusion: "'Intelligent' cannot be defined in terms of 'intellectual' or 'knowing how' in terms of 'knowing that'" (p. 32). "When I do something intelligently, i.e. thinking what I am doing, I am doing one thing and not two" (p. 32).
  - **§7 "Intelligent Capacities versus Habits"** (p. 42). Ryle draws the habit/skill line that `topics/philosophy-of-habit-under-dualism` draws without crediting him. "Habits are one sort, but not the only sort, of second nature". "It is of the essence of merely habitual practices that one performance is a replica of its predecessors. It is of the essence of intelligent practices that one performance is modified by its predecessors". "We build up habits by drill, but we build up intelligent capacities by training" (all p. 42).
  - "Knowing how, then, is a disposition, but not a single-track disposition like a reflex or a habit" (p. 46).
- **Tenet alignment**: Ryle's frame conflicts with Tenet 1 in spirit, since the intellectualist legend is filed under the ghost. The know-how thesis itself is neutral between dualism and physicalism.

### NAMING TRAP: Ryle has two regresses
- **Ch. 2 regress (this note)**: intelligent action cannot require prior contemplation of a proposition (pp. 30–32). Its target is *intellectualism about know-how*. It is what "Ryle's regress" usually means in epistemology and on the SEP.
- **Ch. 3 regress (the sibling note `ryles-regress-against-volitions-2026-11-26`)**: are volitions voluntary? (p. 67). Its target is *volitionism*. It is folded into `topics/philosophy-of-action-under-dualism` L71, which already says the ch. 3 argument is "distinct from his regress about knowing how" (count 1).
- They share a form, an infinite regress of inner acts, but not a target or a reply. Ginet 1975 and S&W 2001 answer ch. 2. McCann, Ginet 1990 and the agent-causal reply answer ch. 3. **Do not cite Ginet 1975 for the volitions regress, or the Map's agent-causal regress-stopper for the know-how regress.**

### Stanley & Williamson, "Knowing How" (2001)
- **URL**: https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:1fcf732b-731b-47e6-943d-aaf34905c02a (manuscript). Published *J. Phil.* 98(8): 411–444, DOI 10.2307/2678403 (Crossref; ORA metadata agrees).
- **Type**: Paper. Quotations come from the manuscript and are page-free unless they are cross-checked to print.
- **Key points**:
  - The thesis: "Knowledge-how is simply a species of knowledge-that."
  - **Against the ch. 2 regress.** They reconstruct it as two premises. Premise 1: if one Fs, one employs knowledge how to F. Premise 2: employing knowledge that p means contemplating p. Premise 1 is true only of *intentional* actions (digesting, or winning a lottery, is not). Premise 2 is false of intentional contemplation, following **Ginet 1975**: one manifests knowledge of how to open a door "by performing that operation quite automatically" (Ginet quoted by S&W). No uniform reading makes both premises true, so "the argument is unsound".
  - **Against Ryle's positive account**: know-how without ability (the ski instructor; the pianist who has lost her arms), so "Ryle's own positive account of knowledge-how is demonstrably false".
  - **Positive account**: "Hannah knows how to ride a bicycle" is true iff, for some contextually relevant way w, Hannah knows that w is a way for her to ride a bicycle, under "what we call a practical mode of presentation". They concede that characterizing such a mode is, as for the first-person mode, "quite a substantial philosophical task".
  - **Footnote 18** (print p. 429, as quoted by Cath) is the Mary-shaped case: "If someone entertained a way of riding a bicycle by possessing a complete physiological description of it, that might also give them de re knowledge of that way, though not under a practical mode of presentation."
  - **§IV on Lewis.** Lewis's account "is incorrect". For x, knowing how to imagine red "amounts to knowing a proposition of the form 'w is a way for x to imagine red', entertained under a guise involving a practical mode of presentation of a way" (print p. 442). They close off the pure-ability fallback, on which Mary gains only abilities and no knowledge, on two grounds. Imagining is an intentional action and so an employment of know-how. And it is "absurd" to say pre-release Mary already knew how. Hence the ability account "in fact entails that Mary acquires propositional knowledge upon leaving her black and white room."
  - **Precursors they credit** (fn. 1 and in text): Ginet (1975, pp. 6–9), Brown (1970), Hintikka (1975). The word "intellectualism" in their paper occurs only inside a quotation from Craig (1990).
- **Author stance**: The paper takes **no position on physicalism**. It rejects one *formulation* of one physicalist reply. **Do not enlist S&W as allies of dualism.** The Map's 2026-10-22 modal-epistemology note found no published stance on dualism by Williamson.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.

### Cath, *A Practical Guide to Intellectualism* (ANU thesis, 2008), ch. 2; = "The Ability Hypothesis and the New Knowledge-How", *Noûs* 43(1) (2009): 137–156
- **URL**: https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/8fe98a0e-a98c-4682-98ab-e46149cf6ea7/content (thesis). Noûs DOI 10.1111/j.1468-0068.2008.01699.x.
- **Key points** (thesis, grepped):
  - Abstract: "the ability hypothesis can be restated using the intellectualist view of knowledge-how."
  - **AH***. Before her release Mary knows that w is a way for her to imagine red under a theoretical (or demonstrative) mode, but not under a practical one. After release she knows it practically. So she gains knowledge-how without knowledge of any new proposition.
  - **The disambiguation.** NEG1: "Upon release Mary does not gain knowledge of any new proposition". NEG2: "Upon release Mary does not come to be in any new state of propositional knowledge". Any ability hypothesis must affirm NEG1. AH* affirms NEG1 and denies NEG2. S&W's §IV refutes only NEG2.
  - "Clearly, AH* is a version of this old-fact/new-mode form of reply to KA." "the gap between the old-fact/new-mode reply and the ability hypothesis reply is not as large as it is typically portrayed as being."
  - **The objection AH* inherits**: Chalmers's New Fact Thesis (Objection B). Cath replies that it fails for indexical cases, or must be restricted to non-indexical cases, which leaves room for AH*.
  - In ch. 3 Cath argues *against* intellectualism, for "a new view of knowledge-how that is distinct from both the standard intellectualist and Rylean views" (abstract).
- **Author stance**: AH* is a **physicalist** reply. Cath's conclusion favours neither dualism nor physicalism.

### Lewis, "What Experience Teaches" (1988)
- **URL**: https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/247198
- **Key points** (grepped; pages unverified):
  - "The Ability Hypothesis says that knowing what an experience is like just is the possession of these abilities to remember, imagine, and recognize. It isn't the possession of any kind of information, ordinary or peculiar. It isn't knowing that certain possibilities aren't actualized. It isn't knowing-that. It's knowing-how."

### Chalmers, "Phenomenal Concepts and the Knowledge Argument" (2004)
- **URL**: https://consc.net/papers/knowledge.html (in Ludlow, Nagasawa & Stoljar (eds.), *There's Something About Mary*, MIT Press)
- **Key points** (grepped): "New Fact Thesis: In non-indexical cases, whenever one gains new knowledge of an old fact, one simultaneously gains knowledge of some new fact." Chalmers credits versions of it to Lockwood 1989, Chalmers 1996 and Thau 2002. He assumes phenomenal realism, which "excludes views on which Mary merely gains a new ability" but "is compatible with views on which Mary gains knowledge of an old fact in a new way."
- **Coiner**: the label is Chalmers's (2004). The idea has precursors (Lockwood 1989). Do not credit Chalmers with originating the idea.

### Noë, "Against intellectualism" (2005)
- **URL**: DOI 10.1093/analys/65.4.278 (*Analysis* 65(4): 278–290)
- **Key points** (grepped):
  - "Intellectualism was and is a response to Behaviourism. It is becoming increasingly clear that it fares no better than Behaviourism in accounting for our mental nature" (p. 278).
  - The S&W account "doesn't eliminate the distinction, or give anyone committed to it a reason to give it up; it merely relocates it" (p. 287).

## Major Positions

### Rylean anti-intellectualism (ability or dispositional)
- **Proponents**: Ryle 1946, 1949; Lewis 1988 for the knowledge-argument use; neo-Ryleans (Setiya, Constantin, Wiggins, Löwenstein, per SEP).
- **Core claim**: knowing how is a competence displayed in conduct, not knowledge of a proposition.
- **Relation to tenets**: Ryle's version belongs to the anti-Cartesian programme. The Map already concedes its core (`logical-behaviourism` L46, L84).

### Propositional intellectualism
- **Proponents**: Stanley & Williamson 2001, 2017; Stanley 2011. Precursors: Ginet 1975, Brown 1970, Hintikka 1975.
- **Core claim**: to know how to F is to know, of some way w, that w is a way to F, under a practical mode.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral on mind–body. Its practical modes are a second precedent, beside first-person modes, for "special" modes of presentation posited on grounds unrelated to the mind–body problem.

### Non-propositional and revisionary positions
- **Proponents**: Bengson & Moffett 2011 (objectualist intellectualism); Cath 2008/2011 (a third view); Fridland 2014 (control is needed for skill and is not fully propositional, per SEP).
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral.

## Key Debates

### Does Ryle's ch. 2 regress refute intellectualism?
- **Sides**: Ryle, Craig 1990, Noë 2005 (a regress "in the vicinity") against Ginet 1975 and S&W 2001.
- **Core disagreement**: whether employing knowledge-that is itself an intentional act that needs know-how.
- **State**: open. The SEP surveys the contemplation and employment versions (Cath 2013; Fantl 2011).
- **Map stake**: low. The Map's interactionism does not require each intelligent act to be preceded by contemplation, and the Ginet reply is available to it as well.

### Does intellectualism defeat the ability hypothesis?
- **Sides**: S&W 2001 say yes. Cath 2008/2009 says only its classic formulation (NEG2); it survives as AH*. Nemirow 2007 defends the ability hypothesis (abstract only, so it is not known whether he answers S&W).
- **State**: Cath's point stands unrebutted in what was read here. The SEP repeats it.
- **Map stake**: this is the one place the topic touches a tenet (see below).

### Are practical modes of presentation legitimate?
- **Sides**: S&W, Pavese 2015b in favour; Schiffer 2002, Koethe 2002, Noë 2005, Glick 2015 against (per SEP; only Noë read).
- **Map stake**: indirect. If practical modes are rejected, intellectualism weakens and the classic ability hypothesis regains its independence. If they are accepted, the ability reply joins the phenomenal-concept family.

- **Grade on the ladder: compatible.** It is not suggestive, because it raises no probability of dualism; it only reshapes a reply. It is not discriminating, because both physicalism and dualism survive either verdict on intellectualism.

Yuri Cath (2009) restates the reply so that it survives. Mary comes to know, under a practical mode, a proposition she already knew under another. That makes it an old-fact/new-mode reply, the family to which Loar's strategy, next, belongs, and it meets that family's standard objection, Chalmers's "New Fact Thesis": outside indexical cases, new knowledge of an old fact brings knowledge of some new fact (Chalmers 2004). Intellectualism therefore gives the Map no evidence for dualism; if true, it removes the ability hypothesis as an independent reply, leaving it to stand or fall with the mode-of-presentation dispute.
