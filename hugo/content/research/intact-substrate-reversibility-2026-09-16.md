# Research: Intact-Substrate Reversibility as the Clinical Wing's Discriminating Signature

**Date**: 2026-09-16
**Search queries used**: one WebSearch (`"metarepresentational" inhibition dissociative amnesia conversion higher-order withholds content global workspace`); Crossref REST lookups for 23 DOIs; Europe PMC REST lookups for 21 records (raw abstracts). OpenAlex was not needed: Crossref and Europe PMC answered every metadata and abstract query, so no OpenAlex budget was spent.

## Executive Summary

The clinical wing leans on one recurring datum: a capacity or an access relation that was lost comes back with no change in the tissue between the impaired and the recovered state. Three live articles invoke it under different names and at different grades, and three queued articles (anosognosia, hemispatial neglect, metacognitive access versus retention) will need it. No concept page names it. The subject is worth a short `concepts/` page, and the research turned up two corrections the page should carry. First, the physicalist rival the wing calls "metarepresentational inhibition" is not a Kinderman/Bentall proposal (they are cited in the corpus for theory-of-mind attribution work, `voids/recursion-void` L165); the named source is Tytgat and Panagiotou (2026), a higher-order-theory model whose abstract states outright that its inhibitory mechanisms "can reverse the suppression given the appropriate conditions." Second, the wing's claim at `clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence` L98 that retrieval suppression predicts "the same reversibility" as the interface reading overstates the rival: Anderson's own primary texts predict durable trace weakening that "resists incentives for accurate recall," so retrieval suppression predicts *reversal with residue*, and Harrison et al. (2017) — fugue patients recovering "to normal scores for facts and near-normal scores for events" — sits between the two predictions rather than confirming either. The one inference the signature licenses on its own is the exclusion of substrate-loss readings for that case; it does not discriminate an interface fault from an intra-cerebral gating state, and every rival surveyed here predicts reversal as a matter of course.

## What the Three Live Articles Currently Claim

Verbatim loci, so the concept page can say what it consolidates and where the articles diverge.

### `topics/clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence` (four "reversib-" uses, L96, L98 x2, L102)

- L96: "Unlike organic amnesia from hippocampal damage, dissociative amnesia is often reported to be reversible, sometimes abruptly ... But reversibility does not by itself establish intact storage, accuracy, or non-reconstructive retrieval."
- L98: "This pattern, *if it holds*, distinguishes interface disconnection from substrate damage. ... *metarepresentational inhibition*, where a higher-order process withholds retrieved content from the global broadcast, and ordinary retrieval-suppression (Anderson and Hanslmayr's motivated-forgetting work) both predict the same reversibility the interface reading treats as its signature."
- L102: "recovery is reconnection, not reconstruction: when the dissociative barrier lifts, autobiographical memories typically return as a body rather than being relearned piecemeal ... The inference is not forced—metarepresentational inhibition predicts the same reversibility—but the reconnection signature respects functional architecture rather than scrambling it."
- L64 and L137-141 carry the phrase "intact-substrate" (the only live body file that does) and frame the contest as "sophisticated production reading" versus "filter reading" on an intact substrate.

What this article takes the signature to license: a **two-way** contrast, interface disconnection versus substrate damage, with the intra-brain rivals conceded in a subordinate clause. That framing is the one the concept page should not inherit (see the boundary section below).

### `topics/conversion-disorder-as-consciousness-side-fault` (one "reversib-" use, L125, a link annotation only)

The article never applies "reversible" to its own evidence; its word is *recoverable*. L69: "In organic paralysis from a motor-pathway lesion, the limb does not move under sedation or hypnosis either — the substrate is damaged, and no change in the subject's conscious state can restore a capacity the hardware no longer supports. In conversion paralysis it can move, because the hardware is intact and the fault lies in the awake subject's access to it." L71: "reading it as localising the fault to a consciousness-to-brain *interface* rather than to an intra-cerebral inhibitory process is a further step it does not compel — a purely intra-cranial account (below) appears to predict the same sedation and hypnosis findings." L117 grades the signature: "intact motor substrate, capacity recoverable under sedation or hypnosis, deficit indexed to the awake subject's directed volition — is among the more suggestive clinical patterns."

What this article takes the signature to license: a **three-way** contrast (hardware lost / intra-cerebral gating / interface) with the honest concession that the second and third are not separated by the recovery data. This is the framing the concept page should adopt.

### `topics/terminal-lucidity-and-filter-transmission-theory` (one "reversib-" use, L84, applied to the *damage*)

L84: "Cortical atrophy is substantial and irreversible. On a strict production model, these patients lack the neural substrate for complex cognition. Yet Batthyány and Greyson's dataset found that more than 80% showed complete remission." L86: "the simplest production models predict a monotonic relationship between neural substrate and cognitive capacity ... it does not *establish* that consciousness is non-produced, but it raises the explanatory cost a production model must pay." L112: the dataset "does not adjudicate between filter/transmission framing and a neurological reading on which residual network reserve, paradoxical disinhibition, or an end-of-life neurochemical surge briefly restores access to spared circuitry." L138: "residual inhibitory structures collapse without yet destroying the minimal circuitry needed for brief coherent output."

What this article takes the signature to license: the constraint-not-establishment reading, applied to a **degraded** rather than an intact substrate. Its version of the signature is the strongest in principle (recovery on a substrate that is documented as lost) and the weakest in documentation (retrospective, no in-episode recording).

### Where the three disagree

1. **Two-way versus three-way contrast.** Clinical-dissociation L98 says the pattern "distinguishes interface disconnection from substrate damage"; conversion L71 and terminal lucidity L112 say the same class of pattern leaves an intra-brain gating reading standing. The concept page should state the three-way form once and clinical-dissociation L98 should be re-pointed at it.
2. **What "intact" means.** Clinical-dissociation uses it anatomically (L64: "no lesion, no atrophy, no pharmacological blockade"); terminal lucidity applies the same inferential move to a substrate that is anything but intact. The reconciling reading is that the signature is about the *delta*, not the *level*: no substrate change between the impaired and the recovered state. On that reading a stable lesion (neglect, anosognosia) and a degraded cortex (terminal lucidity) both qualify, and the concept page needs the three substrate levels named.
3. **Whether retrieval suppression is an equal predictor.** Only clinical-dissociation makes the claim, and the primary texts do not support "the same" (next section).

### Rulebook and positions register

`topics/clinical-evidence-quality-standards-consciousness-research` never uses "reversib-" or "intact"; its five standards (L100-116: replication status, control adequacy, interpretive range, convergence across methods, honest calibration) are the grading instrument the concept page should apply, and L118 states the governing rule the page must repeat: "even methodologically high-quality clinical evidence typically underdetermines the dualism-versus-physicalism question."

`obsidian/positions/` contains no entry grading reversibility *evidence*. The only "reversib-" hits are in `positions/moral-status.md` (L52, L63, L66-67) and its calibration history, where "reversible coma" and "irreversible loss" are read modally for the status verdict. That is a different sense (whether a capacity can in principle return to *this* subject) from the clinical wing's (whether it did return with no tissue change), and the concept page should say so in one sentence to keep `positions-evolve` from conflating them.

## Key Sources

### Edwards, Adams, Brown, Pareés and Friston (2012), "A Bayesian account of 'hysteria'"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/aws129 (Crossref: *Brain* 135(11), 3495-3512; Europe PMC PMID 22641838)
- **Type**: Theoretical paper
- **Key points** (raw abstract): "the primary failure is the (autonomous) emergence of a percept or belief that is held with undue certainty (precision) following top-down attentional modulation of synaptic gain. This belief can constitute a sensory percept (or its absence) or induce movement (or its absence)." The account claims to accommodate "(i) their induction and maintenance by attention; (ii) their modification by expectation, prior experience and cultural beliefs and (iii) their involuntary and symptomatic nature."
- **Reversibility prediction**: explicit and central. A symptom is a precision-weighted prior, so anything that revises the prior or withdraws attention from the body part reverses the symptom with no tissue change: sedation, hypnosis, distraction, suggestion, demonstration, placebo. Reversal is the account's *home* datum rather than an embarrassment.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral on Tenet 1; the account is physicalist by construction and predicts every reversal class the conversion article cites.

### Van den Bergh, Witthöft, Petersen and Brown (2017), "Symptoms and the body: Taking the inferential leap"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2017.01.015 (Crossref: *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 74, 185-203; PMID 28108416)
- **Type**: Review
- **Key points** (raw abstract): "We describe the conditions under which a close correspondence between the experience of symptoms and objective physiology might be expected, and when they are likely to diverge."
- **Use**: the generalisation of Edwards et al. from motor symptoms to functional somatic symptoms; the predictive-coding rival's broadest statement.

### Hallett, Aybek, Dworetzky, McWhirter, Staab and Stone (2022), "Functional neurological disorder: new subtypes and shared mechanisms"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(21)00422-1 (Europe PMC: *Lancet Neurology* 21(6), 537-550; PMID 35430029)
- **Type**: Review (currency anchor)
- **Key points** (raw abstract): diagnosis rests on "genuinely experienced symptoms and signs that show variability within the same task and between different tasks over time"; pathophysiology "includes overactivity of the limbic system, the development of an internal symptom model as part of a predictive coding framework, and dysfunction of brain networks that gives movement the sense of voluntariness."
- **Use**: shows that within-task variability — reversibility at the timescale of seconds — is now a *diagnostic criterion* for FND in the field's consensus review, which is the strongest possible statement that the physicalist mainstream owns the signature.

### Oakley (1999), "Hypnosis and Conversion Hysteria: A Unifying Model"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1080/135468099395954 (Crossref: *Cognitive Neuropsychiatry* 4(3), 243-265; PMID 16571508)
- **Type**: Theoretical paper
- **Key points** (raw abstract): "a central executive structure, similar to the notion of a supervisory attentional system, acting outside self-awareness but at a late stage of information processing which can be directly influenced from both internal and external sources to produce the relevant phenomena"; proposes that "conversion disorder, pain disorder, and the dissociation disorders appear to be linked by a common mechanism."
- **Reversibility prediction**: implicit but strong: a late-stage executive gate that responds to "the manipulation of motivational factors, expectancy, and social influence" reopens under the same influences. This is the executive-inhibition rival the driver named; note it is Oakley alone in 1999, with Halligan a co-author on the 2000 hypnotic-paralysis imaging (already cited in the conversion article).

### Bell, Oakley, Halligan and Deeley (2011), "Dissociation in hysteria and hypnosis: evidence from cognitive neuroscience"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp.2009.199158 (Crossref: *JNNP* 82(3), 332-339, online 2010; PMID 20884677)
- **Type**: Review
- **Key points** (raw abstract): "'symptom' presentation, whether clinically diagnosed or simulated using hypnosis, is associated with increases in prefrontal cortex activity suggesting that intervention by the executive system in both automatic and voluntary cognitive processing is common to both hysteria and hypnosis"; "the field still lacks well controlled systematically designed studies."
- **Use**: the executive-inhibition rival's own calibration statement, which matches the wing's.

### Tytgat and Panagiotou (2026), "Deeper into dissociative Amnesia: A perspective from Higher-Order theories of consciousness"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2026.103998 (Crossref: *Consciousness and Cognition* 139, article 103998, issued March 2026; PMID 41570374)
- **Type**: Theoretical review
- **Key points** (raw abstract): "We propose a theoretical model, the metarepresentational system of memory suppression which learns, through self-organizing processes, to downregulate or block access to a negatively colored autobiographical content ... a functional disconnect between first-order representations, located in the neocortex and hippocampus and higher-order representations, the metarepresentations, located in the dorsal anterior cingulate cortex (dACC) and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) which evaluate and regulate conscious access. Additionally, we discuss that the inhibitory mechanisms in the metarepresentational system are flexible and dynamic and can reverse the suppression given the appropriate conditions, as seen in actual dissociative amnesia cases. Although empirical evidence is needed to support our theory ..."
- **Reversibility prediction**: explicit; reversal is built into the model and cited as its fit to the case literature.
- **Attribution note**: this is the paper the wing's phrase "metarepresentational inhibition, where a higher-order process withholds retrieved content from the global broadcast" describes. It is a higher-order-theory model, not a Kinderman/Bentall one. The clinical-dissociation article (last modified 2026-08-19) uses the phrase without a citation; the concept page should supply this one. The authors themselves flag that the model is untested.

### Anderson and Green (2001), "Suppressing unwanted memories by executive control"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/35066572 (Crossref: *Nature* 410(6826), 366-369; PMID 11268212)
- **Type**: Experimental paper
- **Key points** (raw abstract): "When people encounter cues that remind them of an unwanted memory and they consistently try to prevent awareness of it, the later recall of the rejected memory becomes more difficult. The forgetting increases with the number of times the memory is avoided, resists incentives for accurate recall and is caused by processes that suppress the memory itself."
- **Reversibility prediction**: the laboratory effect is *not* freely reversible: it "resists incentives for accurate recall" and is dose-dependent. Retrieval suppression therefore predicts reversal with residue, not the wholesale "return as a body" of clinical-dissociation L102.

### Anderson and Hanslmayr (2014), "Neural mechanisms of motivated forgetting"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2014.03.002 (Crossref: *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 18(6), 279-292; PMID 24747000)
- **Type**: Review (the one the wing cites)
- **Key points** (raw abstract): suppression "is achieved by inhibitory control processes mediated by the lateral prefrontal cortex. These mechanisms interact with neural structures that represent experiences in memory, disrupting traces that support retention. Thus, mechanisms engaged to regulate momentary awareness introduce lasting biases in which experiences remain accessible."
- **Reversibility prediction**: "disrupting traces that support retention" and "lasting biases" are the opposite of a clean gate. This is the text the wing cites for "the same reversibility," and it does not say that.

### Anderson and Hulbert (2021), "Active Forgetting: Adaptation of Memory by Prefrontal Control"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-072720-094140 (Crossref: *Annual Review of Psychology* 72, 1-36; PMID 32928060)
- **Type**: Review
- **Key points** (raw abstract): prefrontal control processes "not only induce forgetting of specific memories but also can suppress the operation of mnemonic processes more broadly, triggering windows of anterograde and retrograde amnesia in healthy people."
- **Use**: the bridge from item-level suppression to the generalised amnesias the clinical cases show; this is what licenses applying the model to fugue.

### Kikuchi et al. (2010), "Memory Repression: Brain Mechanisms underlying Dissociative Amnesia"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2009.21212 (Crossref: *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience* 22(3), 602-613; PMID 19301997)
- **Type**: Case-series fMRI (n = 2)
- **Key points** (raw abstract): "Dissociative amnesia usually follows a stressful event and cannot be attributable to explicit brain damage. It is thought to reflect a reversible deficit in memory retrieval probably due to memory repression ... increased activity in the pFC and decreased activity in the hippocampus in both patients. After treatment for retrograde amnesia, the altered pattern of brain activation disappeared in one patient whose retrograde memories were recovered, whereas it remained unchanged in the other patient whose retrograde memories were not recovered."
- **Use**: the cleanest intact-substrate reversal with a before/after functional marker. The reversal coincides with the disappearance of a prefrontal-hippocampal inhibitory pattern, which is exactly what the intra-brain rivals predict and what the interface reading has to accommodate as the physical face of the reconnection.

### Marsh, Apšvalka, Kikuchi, Abe, Kawaguchi, Kopelman and Anderson (2024), "Prefrontally mediated inhibition of memory systems in dissociative amnesia"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291724003040 (Crossref: *Psychological Medicine* 54(16), 4779-4787, issued December 2024, online 8 January 2025; PMID 39773554; PMC11779556)
- **Type**: Re-analysis of the Kikuchi cases with effective connectivity
- **Key points** (raw abstract): "substantial functional alignment between the inhibitory regions engaged during laboratory-based retrieval suppression tasks, and those engaged when patients failed to recognize their current colleagues ... Dynamic causal modeling confirmed the hypothesized modulatory relationship between the raDLPFC and the hippocampus. This pattern was no longer evident following memory recovery in the first patient, but persisted in the second patient who remained amnesic."
- **Reversibility prediction**: this is Anderson's own group applying retrieval suppression to the clinical case, and here the model *does* predict reversal: recovery is the inhibitory modulation switching off. Read together with Anderson and Green (2001), the retrieval-suppression rival predicts a reversible gate *plus* a residual trace cost proportional to suppression history. That conjunction is testable (below).

### Harrison et al. (2017), "Psychogenic amnesia: syndromes, outcome, and patterns of retrograde amnesia"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awx186 (Crossref: *Brain* 140(9), 2498-2510; PMID 29050391)
- **Type**: Case series (n = 53 psychogenic, 21 neurological, 14 controls)
- **Key points** (raw abstract): "After 3-6 months, the fugue patients had improved to normal scores for facts and near-normal scores for events. By contrast, the two focal retrograde amnesia groups showed less improvement and continued to show a reversed temporal gradient. In conclusion, the outcome in psychogenic amnesia, particularly those characterized by fugue, is better than generally supposed. Findings are interpreted in terms of Markowitsch's and Kopelman's models of psychogenic amnesia, and with respect to Anderson's neuroimaging findings in memory inhibition."
- **Use**: the best outcome data on spontaneous-class reversal. "Normal for facts, near-normal for events" is between the two predictions: closer to wholesale return than the laboratory suppression effect would suggest, but not the clean "as a body" return either, and the focal-retrograde groups reversed only partly. The concept page should quote this rather than Staniloiu and Markowitsch's "sometimes abruptly."

## Major Positions

### The interface reading (the Map's)
- **Core claim**: a capacity returning with no substrate change locates the fault in the coupling between consciousness and an intact channel; the substrate was never the missing part.
- **Relation to tenets**: reads naturally under Tenet 1 and Tenet 3 and is the clinical face of the `filter-vs-interface-distinction`. Under Tenet 2 a reversal that requires no tissue change is the kind of event a selection-only channel can produce, since selection leaves structure alone; that is a consistency point, not evidence.
- **What it predicts about reversal**: content returns whole (clinical-dissociation L102), recovery order need not mirror degradation order (`memory-channel-interface-evidence` L126-136), and recovery should respect channel architecture rather than scramble it.

### Predictive-processing / Bayesian FND (Edwards et al. 2012; Van den Bergh et al. 2017; Hallett et al. 2022)
- **Core claim**: the symptom is an over-precise prior the system fulfils; reversal is prior revision.
- **Reversibility prediction**: total and central. Predicts, further, that reversal tracks *belief-relevant* manipulations regardless of physical route (Garcin 2017: root stimulation equals cortical stimulation; Poole 2010: suggestion predicts recovery) and that symptoms vary within a task (Hallett 2022). The interface reading makes no such route-indifference prediction on its own.
- **Domain**: functional motor, sensory and somatic symptoms. Not built for dissociative amnesia (though Brown 2004, already in the wing, bridges) and silent on terminal lucidity.

### Executive-inhibition / late-stage gating (Oakley 1999; Bell et al. 2011)
- **Core claim**: a supervisory-attention-like executive structure outside self-awareness withholds or redirects processing; conversion and dissociation share the mechanism.
- **Reversibility prediction**: implicit and complete; the gate answers to "expectancy, and social influence," so hypnosis, suggestion and sedation reopen it. Its own authors grade the evidence as lacking "well controlled systematically designed studies."
- **Domain**: both conversion and dissociation.

### Metarepresentational inhibition (Tytgat and Panagiotou 2026)
- **Core claim**: higher-order representations in dACC/DLPFC that "evaluate and regulate conscious access" learn to block first-order autobiographical content.
- **Reversibility prediction**: explicit: mechanisms "can reverse the suppression given the appropriate conditions." Predicts *retention* (first-order traces intact) and *gated access*, hence wholesale return, the same as the interface reading. Untested by the authors' own statement.
- **Domain**: selective dissociative amnesia; extends in principle to depersonalisation via the same higher-order apparatus.

### Retrieval suppression (Anderson and Green 2001; Anderson and Hanslmayr 2014; Anderson and Hulbert 2021; Marsh et al. 2024)
- **Core claim**: lateral prefrontal control down-regulates hippocampal retrieval; in the clinical case the raDLPFC-hippocampus modulation is the amnesia.
- **Reversibility prediction**: **split**. The clinical application (Marsh 2024) predicts reversal when the modulation lifts. The laboratory model predicts a residual cost: forgetting that "resists incentives for accurate recall" and "lasting biases." So the composite prediction is a reopened gate with graded, history-dependent damage to the suppressed traces. This is the one rival whose prediction *differs* from the interface reading's, and it differs in a measurable direction.
- **Domain**: amnesia only.

### Network reserve / paradoxical disinhibition (Mashour et al. 2019)
- **Core claim**: spared circuitry briefly returns to function ("network-level return").
- **Reversibility prediction**: brief, trigger-linked, incomplete return, which is what Tollock et al. 2025 (already in the wing) reported for most episodes.
- **Domain**: terminal and paradoxical lucidity only.

## The Discriminative Question

Which accounts predict intact-substrate reversibility equally well? For a **single reversal**, all of them: Bayesian FND, executive gating and metarepresentational inhibition each predict it outright, retrieval suppression predicts it in its clinical form, and network reserve predicts it for lucidity. The signature therefore rules out exactly one thing, the *substrate-loss* reading of that case (the "hard drive is damaged" model of clinical-dissociation L98, the "monotonic" model of terminal lucidity L86). That is a real exclusion, since it is the default lay and much of the older clinical reading, and it is the whole of what the signature licenses on its own.

Where the accounts come apart, and so where any interface-favouring residue would have to be found, is at the **pattern** level, not the case level:

1. **Post-recovery fidelity.** Retrieval suppression predicts residual, history-graded weakening of the once-suppressed content relative to never-suppressed content; the interface reading and metarepresentational inhibition predict none. Harrison 2017 ("normal scores for facts and near-normal scores for events") is the nearest existing datum and does not settle it. A within-subject comparison of recovered-period versus spared-period autobiographical fidelity would.
2. **Route indifference.** The Bayesian account predicts reversal by any belief-relevant manipulation irrespective of physical route (confirmed for TMS versus root stimulation, Garcin 2017; suggestion in drug interviews, Poole 2010). The interface reading is compatible with this but does not predict it, so every route-indifference result is a point to the rival on the interpretive-range standard.
3. **Direction asymmetry and channel ordering.** These are the corpus's actual candidates for a prediction the rivals do not make without per-case additions (`memory-channel-interface-evidence` L136: production "can recover the asymmetry, but only by adding a direction-specific mechanism per case"). They belong to that article, not to the concept page; the concept page should point at them and claim nothing further.
4. **Functional-marker covariation.** Kikuchi 2010 and Marsh 2024 show the reversal coinciding with the disappearance of a prefrontal-hippocampal inhibitory pattern. Every intra-brain rival predicts a marker of this kind; the interface reading must treat it as the physical face of reconnection. If every reversal in every class turns out to carry a reversing intra-brain control signature, the interface reading survives only as a redescription, which is the honest statement of its exposure.

## Evidence Classes and Which Rivals Struggle

| Class | Exemplars | Substrate level | Rival that owns it | Rival that struggles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pharmacological / TMS / vestibular remission | Stone 2014 (propofol, 5/11); Garcin 2013, 2017 (TMS, RMS); Cappa 1987 (vestibular); Kikuchi 2010 and Marsh 2024 (treatment recovery with fMRI marker) | normal (FND, amnesia) or stable lesion (neglect, anosognosia) | Bayesian FND and executive gating, since Garcin 2017 shows the route does not matter | none; retrieval suppression is silent on motor cases but not embarrassed |
| Spontaneous remission | Harrison 2017 (fugue to normal/near-normal at 3-6 months; focal RA partial) | normal | metarepresentational inhibition and clinical retrieval suppression | laboratory retrieval suppression, if fidelity proves complete; interface reading and metarepresentational inhibition, if a residue is found |
| Placebo, nocebo, suggestion, demonstration | Poole 2010 (suggestion predicts recovery); Pollak 2014 mechanism (c); Rommelfanger 2013 (opinion); Fotopoulou 2009 (video self-observation, permanent) | normal or stable lesion | Bayesian FND, by construction | interface reading has no account of *why* demonstration works that the rival does not already give |
| Terminal / paradoxical lucidity | Batthyány and Greyson 2021; Mashour 2019; Tollock 2025 | degraded | network reserve for brief, trigger-linked episodes | network reserve for the rare complete, autopsy-verified tail; none of the FND or amnesia rivals reach this class at all |

The one class where a physicalist rival visibly strains is the last, and only in its rare tail, and the strain is against *documentation* the wing already grades as retrospective. No rival struggles with the first three classes. The concept page should say this plainly.

## Positioning Against the Five Clinical Standards

1. **Replication status**: the pharmacological class is one uncontrolled series (n = 11); the TMS class has two studies from one group (n = 24, n = 33) and a systematic review finding no placebo-controlled trial; the fMRI reversal is n = 2 reanalysed once; the vestibular datum is n = 4 from 1987. Only the spontaneous class (n = 53) has a series-scale outcome measure. "Suggestive" is the ceiling.
2. **Control adequacy**: the only active control in the whole set is Garcin 2017's root stimulation, and it removed the *physical-modulation* reading rather than supporting any interface one. No sham-controlled reversal exists in any class.
3. **Interpretive range**: at least four frameworks fit every single reversal; the range narrows by one (substrate loss excluded) and no further.
4. **Convergence across methods**: the *finding* converges across four evidence classes and three substrate levels, which is genuine and is what makes the signature worth naming. Convergence of a finding is not convergence on a reading; the rivals converge on it too.
5. **Honest calibration**: "compatible with" for any single case; "suggestive" only for the pattern-level claims, which live in `memory-channel-interface-evidence`. The concept page should carry the grade in its first paragraph so the citing articles can inherit it.

- Terminal lucidity still lacks in-episode EEG or imaging (already recorded in the wing).
