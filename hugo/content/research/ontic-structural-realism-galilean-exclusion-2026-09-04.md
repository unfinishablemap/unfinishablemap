# Research: Ontic Structural Realism and the Galilean Exclusion

**Date**: 2026-09-04
**Commissioned by**: `/harvest-research-subjects`, from the Medium Priority finding in `reviews/optimistic-2026-09-03-explanatory-limit-wing.md`
**Search queries used** (10): OSR Ladyman French relations-without-relata; structural realism + hard problem + "structure and dynamics" Chalmers; OSR + qualia/phenomenal objection; Alter "structure and dynamics argument" Noûs 2016; Ladyman & Ross *Every Thing Must Go* intrinsic natures; structural realism + Galileo primary/secondary qualities; Chalmers 2013 "all the way down"; Esfeld & Lam moderate SR; Stoljar structural/non-structural objection; OSR vs dualism mind-body

## Executive Summary

The reviewer's framing — that ontic structural realism (OSR) is "the one live position that agrees with Galileo's exclusion and then denies there was ever anything excluded" — is philosophically right but bibliographically wrong, and the difference matters more than the framing does. **No one holds the composite position.** OSR is a thesis about the ontology of *physics*; it is silent on consciousness to a degree that is measurable. The Stanford Encyclopedia entry on Structural Realism — written by James Ladyman, the man who named OSR, substantively revised 18 May 2023, roughly 170,000 characters — contains **zero** occurrences of "consciousness", "conscious", or "experience", and exactly one of "qualia": a citation of Unger (1979) for the view that *qualia are the non-structural components of reality*. The canonical OSR survey's only mention of qualia is in the mouth of someone asserting the Map's premise. Symmetrically, the SEP entry on Russellian Monism (Alter and Pereboom, rev. 4 July 2023) uses "consciousness" 96 times and contains **zero** occurrences of "French", "Esfeld", or "relations without relata".

So the rival the wing does not meet is a rival that does not yet exist in print. The two components must be assembled: OSR about physics (Ladyman and Ross 2007) plus structuralism about phenomenal character (Loorits 2014; Fink, Kob and Lyre 2021; Lyre 2022). Both are live and defensible; the people who hold one mostly do not hold the other, and the phenomenal structuralists **explicitly decline** the ontic reading. Lyre opens his 2022 programme by disowning the association: *"One might be inclined to ask whether neurophenomenal structuralism is a version of structural realism. The short answer is: no."* Loorits, whose paper is titled "Structural qualia: a solution to the hard problem of consciousness", classifies OSR as a minority radicalism he does not share. The Map cannot *meet* this rival; it would have to *build* it — and constructing an opponent then defeating it is the failure mode the corpus's `the-steelman-for-*` genre exists to avoid, so the construction has to be labelled as construction on the page.

Second finding: the Map has already conceded this defeater twice, in one clause each, without argument. `concepts/physical-completeness` states that "Ontic structural realism (Ladyman, French) holds that structure is all there is" and that "The Map sides with the epistemic camp" — a declaration, not a case — while devoting a full paragraph to Maudlin, the opponent on the *other* flank. `concepts/intrinsic-nature` lists as the **first** thing that would undermine the Map's use of intrinsic nature "a defensible structural realism showed that relations require no relata and structure no occupant, dissolving quiddities altogether", and discharges it with four words: "remains contested". Chalmers, whose structure-and-dynamics reasoning the Map leans on, is more generous to the opponent than the Map is: he grants these are "respectable" views and that his own objection is only that they "*seem* to yield a world devoid of substance or qualities".

Third finding: this bites the Map's *route*, not its *conclusion* — it removes `physical-completeness`'s ontological-remainder argument, not Dualism. Developed under "Calibration" below, with the recommendation and cap position.

### Structural Realism (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy) — **[raw-verified]**
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/structural-realism/
- **Author / date**: James Ladyman. First published 14 Nov 2007; substantive revision 18 May 2023. Copyright line raw-verified: "Copyright © 2023 by James Ladyman".
- **Type**: Encyclopedia (the canonical survey, by OSR's originator)
- **Key points** (all verbatim):
  - ESR is "the view that scientific theories tell us only about the form or structure of the unobservable world and not about its nature."
  - OSR was introduced by Ladyman (1998) as "a metaphysical position according to which, since the continuity in scientific change is of 'form or structure', the success of science should be accounted for in terms of the representation of modal relations among phenomena, not in terms of continuity of reference to objects." Worrall (1989) is its ancestor: "the continuity is one of form or structure, not of content."
  - The relations-without-relata objection: "there cannot be relations without relata. This objection has been made by various philosophers including Cao (2003b), Dorato (2000), Psillos (2001, 2006a), Busch (2003), Morganti (2004) and Chakravartty (1998, 2003) who says: 'one cannot intelligibly subscribe to the reality of relations unless one is also committed to the fact that some things are related.'"
  - Newman's problem: "The basic problem is that structure is not sufficient to uniquely pick out any relations in the world... only cardinality questions are open to discovery!"
  - The single qualia mention: "Peter Unger (1979) also argues that our knowledge of the world is purely structural and that qualia are the non-structural components of reality."
- **Keyword census (raw grep of the fetched HTML, ~170,502 chars of extracted text)**: consciousness 0 · conscious 0 · experience 0 · qualia 1 · phenomenal 2 · Galile 2 · relations-without-relata 4 · Esfeld 18 · French 46. Both "Galile" hits are Galilean *transformations* and Castellani's "Galilean Particles" — nothing about Galileo's exclusion.
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with Dualism if eliminative OSR is true and generalises to the mental. Neutral as stated, because it never addresses the mental.

### Russellian Monism (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy) — **[raw-verified]**
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/
- **Authors / date**: Torin Alter and Derk Pereboom. First published 3 Jul 2019; substantive revision 4 Jul 2023.
- **Key points** (all verbatim):
  - The structuralist conception of physics the Map shares: "physics describes the world in terms of its spatiotemporal structure and dynamics (changes within that structure) and says nothing about what, if anything, underlies that structure and dynamics."
  - The pivot sentence — the exact logical space OSR occupies, and the space the Map's articles do not currently defend against: "One might reject realism about quiddities even if one accepts structuralism about physics."
  - It quotes Chalmers and then, in its own parenthesis, supplies the OSR referent: "As Chalmers (2013: 254) writes, 'There are respectable structuralist or dispostionalist views of physics on which physics involves just structure or dispositions all the way down' (e.g., Ladyman and Ross, with Spurrett and Collier 2007; Ney 2015; cf.   - Stoljar's standing objection is to the *distinction itself*: the entry cites "Stoljar 2006, 2009, 2014, 2015" for "other objections concerning the Russellian monist's structural/non-structural distinction", with responses from Pereboom and Alter.

- **Citation**: Loorits, K. (2014). *Frontiers in Psychology*, 5: 237. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00237. (Crossref-verified: author, title, container, volume, article, date 2014-03-18.)

## Major Positions

### Eliminative Ontic Structural Realism (Ladyman; Ladyman and Ross; French)
Relational structure is ontologically fundamental; individual objects with intrinsic natures are not. In Loorits's raw-verified gloss: "pieces of relata and elements of structures in theories of natural sciences are merely heuristic devices with no fundamental ontological status." Motivated by continuity of structure (not content) across theory change, underdetermination of object-individuation by quantum statistics, and naturalism about metaphysics.

**Relation to site tenets**: the sharpest available denial of the Map's picture, and it denies it in an unfamiliar way. Standard materialism says the excluded qualities *reduce to* structure, and the Map answers with the form/content argument in `concepts/reductionism`. OSR says something else: there is no categorical residue anywhere, in matter or in mind, so nothing was excluded and nothing needs reducing. The Map's parsimony-by-omission charge — sharpened in `topics/primary-secondary-quality-boundary` as "simplicity through stipulation" — does not obviously land, because OSR is not omitting a remainder it should have counted. It denies the remainder was ever a datum.

### Moderate / Non-eliminative OSR (Esfeld and Lam)
Objects exist as relata but have no intrinsic identity; relations and objects sit on the same ontological footing. **Worse for the Map than eliminative OSR, not better**: the Map's likeliest rhetorical move is the relations-without-relata objection, and moderate OSR is built precisely to absorb it while keeping the result the Map cares about — no quiddities. An article spending its counter-argument budget there will have refuted the version nobody needs to hold.

### Structuralism about Phenomenal Character (Loorits; Fink, Kob and Lyre)
Qualia are compositional and relationally individuated. Loorits adds the debunking step: what presents itself as monadic is a complex pattern "that misperceives some of its own highly complex structural properties as monadic and qualitative."

**Relation to site tenets**: the strongest form of the rival, because it explains the *appearance* of a residue rather than denying the datum. Introspection presents redness as simple; introspection is unreliable about complexity; so the simplicity that generates the hard problem is an introspective artefact. The Map should meet this rather than the bare assertion that structure is all there is. Note the cost the position pays: Lyre's programme yields rejection of inverted qualia and holism about experience, both substantive and both testable via similarity ratings and representational geometry.

### Epistemic Structural Realism (Worrall; Russell; Stanciu for consciousness)
Structure is all we can *know*; something more exists beneath it. The Map's own position, declared in `concepts/physical-completeness` ("The Map sides with the epistemic camp but goes further"). What the corpus lacks is an argument for the choice — and ESR carries Newman's problem as its standing burden, never acknowledged.

### The Anti-structuralist Flank (Maudlin; Stoljar)
Maudlin holds that physics attributes intrinsic natures directly, so no residue is left for physics to miss; `physical-completeness` engages him at length and concedes that "the Map's case rests on the structuralist reading being correct rather than on having refuted Maudlin on his own terms." Stoljar attacks the structural/non-structural *distinction itself* (SEP cites Stoljar 2006, 2009, 2014, 2015) — the more corrosive line, because the Map's position and its OSR rival both presuppose the distinction is well-formed.

## Key Debates

### Does the exclusion have a residue?
- **Sides**: the Map, Chalmers, Russellian monists, ESR — yes, and it is where consciousness lives. Eliminative OSR — no; the "residue" is an artefact of pre-scientific object-thinking.
- **Core disagreement**: whether Galileo's mathematical skeleton is a *filtered view of* the world or *is* the world.
- **Current state**: **not actually staged** (see the keyword censuses above). The nearest thing to a joining is Chalmers's one-paragraph concession that quiddity-free views are "respectable", plus SEP's parenthetical supply of Ladyman and Ross as their referent. This absence is the opportunity and also the reason the Map must not claim to be reporting a debate.

### Galileo: eliminated or relocated?
- SEP's "Primary and Secondary Qualities in Early Modern Philosophy" (first published 1 Jun 2022; **[publisher-read]**, author not captured) reports Galileo saying that from the point of view of the subject in which they seem to inhere "[these things] are nothing but empty names" and that if one removes the animal "all these qualities are … annihilated" (Galileo 1623 [2008: 185]), and reads him as *relocating* rather than eliminating: secondary qualities are "not in bodies that cause them but internal to sentient bodies."
- **Why this matters for the Map**: `concepts/galilean-exclusion` says "Galileo did not *discover* that secondary qualities are unreal. He *decided* to exclude them from physics." The relocation reading supports the second sentence but complicates the first — Galileo did assert their unreality *in the object*, and "empty names" is stronger than "set aside". The Map's claim survives, since relocation into the perceiver is itself a claim that there is a residue living somewhere else. The distinction is worth drawing because it separates Galileo from OSR cleanly: **Galileo relocates the residue, OSR abolishes it.** That is sharper and more accurate than the reviewer's "agrees with Galileo and denies anything was excluded".

### Relations without relata
- **Sides**: Cao, Dorato, Psillos, Busch, Morganti and Chakravartty against; French and Ladyman for; Esfeld and Lam sidestepping.
- **Current state**: live but well-defended. See the moderate-OSR note above — this objection is the obvious counter-move and the weakest one.

### Newman's problem, and whether it cuts both ways
If structure is not sufficient to pick out relations in the world — "only cardinality questions are open to discovery" — then a structure-only ontology is either trivial or must smuggle in something non-structural to individuate. The most promising pressure point available, and one the corpus has never used. Lyre's programme is the concrete case: it must "motivate a solution of the Newman problem of the brain in terms of spatiotemporal relations" before it can begin, and privileging spatiotemporal relations is a non-structural input, honestly declared.

**It cuts both ways, and the Map must say so.** The trivialisation worry was raised against Russell's *epistemic* structuralism, which is the Map's own position; Demopoulos and Friedman concluded that "Russell's realism collapses into a version of phenomenalism or strict empiricism after all". Deploying Newman against OSR while the Map's own ESR commitment sits exposed to it would be an unearned asymmetry — the pattern the corpus files under coherence inflation.

### The unfolding argument as an internal tension
`concepts/the-unfolding-argument-against-causal-structure-theories-of-consciousness` already holds relevant machinery. If ontology is exhausted by structure, the structuralist owes an account of why two systems with identical input-output structure could differ in consciousness — or must accept that they cannot. A pre-existing Map asset rather than a new argument, and the cheapest available bridge between the wing and the interface cluster.

## Potential Article Angles

**Cap position, measured this dispatch** via `tools.evolution.state.count_section_files` (not quoted from the CLAUDE.md table, which is stale): topics **324**/360, concepts **323**/360, voids 99/115, positions 17/80. Real headroom in both candidate sections; no cap pressure.

**Recommendation: a standalone `concepts/` article, plus one mandatory small edit elsewhere.** The fold option is genuine and is set out below; my judgement is that it does less than the corpus needs, but a reviewer who disagrees has a defensible position and the evidence for both is laid out here rather than in a conclusion.

1. **`concepts/ontic-structural-realism` — the recommended shape.** Scope it as the dialectic, not a survey (SEP surveys better; link it rather than compete). The article's job: state what OSR claims, state that it is the position on which the exclusion excluded nothing, then do what the corpus has not done — argue for ESR over OSR instead of announcing the choice. It must open by conceding that **the composite rival is constructed**: OSR proponents are silent on consciousness (keyword census as evidence), phenomenal structuralists decline the ontic reading (Lyre's disavowal quoted), and the Map is assembling an opponent because the assembly is the strongest thing in the vicinity. Load the counter-argument on Newman and on the what-is-it-like-to-be-a-structure line; use relations-without-relata only as the objection the moderate view already absorbs. Target ~2,200–2,500 words, under the `concepts/` soft threshold.

2. **`topics/the-steelman-for-ontic-structural-realism` — the alternative framing.** The corpus's established genre for meeting a rival at full strength, and the honest label for an article that must construct its opponent. Weaker on findability, stronger on calibration. `topics/` has 36 slots.

3. **The fold, if no new article is wanted.** Two cheap edits, both closing the reviewer's specific complaint:
   - `concepts/galilean-exclusion`, §"Not a Discovery but a Decision" — two or three sentences naming OSR as the position that accepts the exclusion and denies the residue, drawing the contrast established above (Galileo *relocates* the qualities into the sentient body; OSR *abolishes* them). The section currently reads as though the only available opponent is the materialist who thinks the residue reduces.
   - `concepts/physical-completeness`, the paragraph beginning "Russell's insight has been developed by structural realists" — promote "The Map sides with the epistemic camp" from declaration to argument, citing Stanciu (2021) as an uncited ally. The article gives Maudlin a full paragraph on the anti-structuralist flank; the pro-structuralist flank gets one sentence, and it is the flank that would dissolve the article's thesis rather than merely deny it.

**One correction is owed whichever route is taken.** `concepts/intrinsic-nature` discharges "a defensible structural realism showed that relations require no relata and structure no occupant, dissolving quiddities altogether" with the words "structural realism without relata remains contested". Chalmers calls these views "respectable" and says only that they "seem" to yield a world devoid of qualities. The Map should not be more confident against this rival than the philosopher whose argument it borrows.

**Calibration the downstream expand must preserve.** OSR is a defeater for the Map's *route*, not its *thesis*. It establishes that physical entities lack intrinsic natures, not that experiences do. The Map does not require matter to have quiddities, only that consciousness not be exhausted by structure — the claim Loorits and FKL attack, not the claim Ladyman attacks. An article treating OSR as an attack on Dualism will have mis-aimed; one treating it as an attack on `physical-completeness`'s ontological-remainder argument will have aimed correctly, and must say plainly which support is being removed.

**Tenet hook worth developing (novel as far as this search reached).** If ontology is exhausted by structure, then *which* structure-instance is mine is not a fact about the world — structure is abstract and multiply realisable, experience particular and indexical. That runs into Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds; indexical identity matters), the Map's standing refusal to let indexical facts dissolve into third-person description. No source found here runs this argument, so present it as the Map's own move, with corresponding caution about its strength.

Follow `obsidian/project/writing-style.md`: named-anchor summary for the forward reference to Newman, background only where framed for the dualist perspective (skip the pessimistic-meta-induction history), explicit tenet alignment, and the constructed-rival concession in the lead rather than buried.
