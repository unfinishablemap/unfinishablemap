## Executive Summary

Pylyshyn's visual indexes (FINSTs) are a hypothesised early-vision mechanism that picks out and tracks about four or five objects "without being categorized or conceptualized" (2001 abstract, **[verbatim]**). Pylyshyn explicitly likens indexing to demonstrative reference, and speculates that "one might say that we can have demonstrative thoughts" through it (2001, p. 154, **[verbatim]**).

**Correction to the task framing:** Pylyshyn's claim is *preconceptual*, not *unconscious*. His 2001 paper never mentions consciousness: "conscious" occurs once, in a reference-list title, and "aware" never. By 2001 he had also stopped calling indexing "preattentive", because tracking "may require a great deal of effort and attention" (p. 149, **[verbatim]**). In standard multiple-object tracking (MOT) the tracked objects are visible and consciously seen. The "without consciousness" half of the rival case therefore has to come from elsewhere:
- spatial attention without awareness in blindsight (Kentridge et al. 1999);
- object-based attention to objects that remain invisible (Norman et al. 2013, 2015);
- residual grouping and surface completion in neglect and extinction (Driver & Vuilleumier 2001; Mattingley et al. 1997);
- object-file correspondences that can diverge from the conscious percept (Mitroff et al. 2005).

Against these, object-file *updating* was "entirely contingent on conscious perception" in one cueing paradigm (Lamy et al. 2015), a result later partly qualified (Schoeberl et al. 2018). **No study the Map has found has observers track MOT targets they are unaware of** (scope below).

Campbell's thesis is narrower than "consciousness is needed to pick things out". His sea-of-faces case grants a subject who points reliably at the referent while consciously attending to nobody, and says that "there is an ordinary sense in which H does not know who S means" (2002, p. 9, as quoted in Williams 2013, **[verbatim]**). Campbell 2007 grants non-conscious perception of objects and argues that conscious perception has "a different causal role" (abstract, **[verbatim]**). So sub-personal indexing does not refute Campbell outright. It shows that *selection and tracking* — reference in the sub-personal sense — run without conscious attention. The open question is whether *knowledge of reference* is a further achievement that needs consciousness, or an epistemic gloss that sub-personal selection plus availability can supply (Barkasi 2019; Berger, Nanay & Quilty-Dunn 2018).

On the calibration: indexing that works without consciousness counts against consciousness being necessary for reference **in the sub-personal sense**. It leaves Campbell's personal-level thesis contested, not refuted. It gives nothing to dualism either way. The cost to the Map is that the one survivor of its scope–phenomenality trade-off is weaker, and the article should say so plainly.

### Pylyshyn & Storm (1988), *Spatial Vision* 3(3): 179–197, doi:10.1163/156856888X00122
- **Abstract (OpenAlex), verified:**
  - "subjects are able to track a subset of up to 5 objects in a field of 10 identical randomly-moving objects" **[verbatim]**
  - A serial scanning algorithm would score about 40%, yet "subjects still manage to do the task with 87% accuracy" **[verbatim]**.
  - The FINST model "posits a primitive identity maintenance mechanism that indexes and tracks a limited number of visual objects in parallel" **[verbatim]**.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral. This is the founding MOT paper, and it is about parallel tracking, not consciousness.

- **Note**: in 1989 FINSTs index *features*. By 2001 they index *objects* or *proto-objects*. Do not flatten the two.

### Lamy, Alon, Carmel & Shalev (2015; online 2014), "The Role of Conscious Perception in Attentional Capture and Object-File Updating", *Psychological Science* 26(1): 48–57, doi:10.1177/0956797614556777

## Major Positions

### Consciousness is needed for demonstrative reference (Campbell; Smithies)
- **Proponents**: Campbell 2002, 2007; Smithies 2011. Evans also, per Smithies's abstract.
- **Core claim**: Campbell holds that conscious attention provides *knowledge of the reference* of a perceptual demonstrative. Sub-personal selection without it leaves the subject not knowing which thing is meant (sea of faces). Smithies makes the role epistemic: consciousness enables immediately justified beliefs about the object.
- **What Campbell concedes**: non-conscious perception of objects exists (2007), and reliable sub-personal latching and pointing are possible (p. 9 via Williams).
- **Relation to site tenets**: consciousness-dependence for one kind of thought. Both authors are non-dualists. Campbell is a relationalist, as `naturalist-relationalism` notes.

### Preconceptual indexing is demonstrative-like reference (Pylyshyn; Fodor & Pylyshyn)
- **Core claim**: early vision binds a small number of indexes to objects without encoding their properties. The relation "functions exactly like a demonstrative" (p. 138). Concepts are grounded there, and a reference-only semantics can build on it (Fodor & Pylyshyn).
- **What it does not claim**: that indexing is unconscious, or that the subject lacks experience of the indexed objects. Since 2001 it does not even claim indexing is "preattentive".
- **Relation to site tenets**: a sub-personal, non-phenomenal route to reference-like relations. It counts against a consciousness condition on reference in that sense. If its own physicalist framing were extended to personal-level thought it would be a rival, but Pylyshyn does not argue that extension, and the article must not argue it for him.

### Object files ground nonconceptual demonstrative reference, inside awareness (Raftopoulos & Müller)
- **Core claim**: perceptual demonstrative reference is fixed causally by nonconceptual content; object-file segmentation individuates the object of perceptual awareness.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral. It routes reference through sub-personal machinery while keeping the object in awareness.

### Layered and spatial views (Clark; Campbell on location; Cohen against)
- **Core claim**: Clark puts a location-based feature-placing layer beneath proto-object indices. Campbell makes experienced location critical to selection, and defends Robertson's thesis against Pylyshyn (2007). Cohen argues that sensory individuals are objects, not places.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral. This is a structural dispute inside the theory of perceptual reference.

### Reference requires attribution (Burge 2022; Quilty-Dunn & Green, flexibly)
- **Core claim**: no perceptual reference without some accurate perceptual attribution. This rejects pure property-free indexing.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral on consciousness. Burge separately rejects consciousness as a condition on perceptual representation (2010, per Begby).

### Experience is not the only route, and justification need not be conscious (Barkasi; Berger, Nanay & Quilty-Dunn)
- **Core claim**: attention sets targets sub-doxastically (Barkasi), and unconscious perceptual states can justify (Berger et al.).
- **Relation to site tenets**: these remove the "only" from Campbell and Smithies. If they succeed, the phenomenal survivor of the Map's trade-off fails.

## Key Debates

### 1. Which "reference"? {#which-reference}
- **Sides**: Pylyshyn's reference is a sub-personal binding that "ceases to exist when the referent … is no longer in view". Campbell's is *knowledge* of reference, a personal-level state that explains verification and action.
- **Core disagreement**: whether a reliable sub-personal latching, of the kind H displays in the sea-of-faces case, is reference in the sense that matters for thought.
- **Current state**: open. The sea-of-faces case is an appeal to "common sense" (p. 9). Williams 2013 argues that Campbell's own arguments "are not entirely successful (largely because of his reliance on his notion of 'knowledge of reference')" **[verbatim, Williams's abstract]**. Tamburini (2025, *PPR* 111(2): 590–606) argues that "knowledge of reference is not necessary for referential understanding" **[verbatim, abstract]**.

### 2. Objects first or places first? {#objects-or-places}
- **Sides**: Pylyshyn and Cohen (objects) against Campbell and Robertson (spatial awareness), with Clark holding a layered view.
- **Core disagreement**: whether selection runs through experienced location or through location-transparent object indexes.
- **Current state**: open. Campbell 2007 says the burden of evidence favours spatial awareness. French 2018 questions the Bálint's-syndrome evidence on which part of that dispute turns.

### 3. Is indexing attentional, and is it conscious? {#attention-consciousness}
- **Sides**: Pylyshyn ("automatic and preconceptual", p. 149; "non-attentional" in 2007) against the split-attention reading (Cavanagh & Alvarez 2005, as Pylyshyn 2007 reports it). Ayare & Srivastava 2024 model MOT without indexes at all.
- **Core disagreement**: these are three separate questions. Is indexing conceptual? Is it attentional? Is it conscious? Pylyshyn answers only the first firmly ("no").
- **Current state**: the consciousness question is not addressed by Pylyshyn and is not directly tested for tracking (see [Absence and Exclusivity Claims](#absence)).

### 4. Does object-level selection run without awareness? {#selection-without-awareness}
- **For**: Kentridge et al. 1999 (spatial, blindsight); Norman et al. 2013, 2015 (object-based, normal observers, masked objects); Driver & Vuilleumier 2001 and Mattingley et al. 1997 (grouping and surfaces in neglect and extinction); Mitroff et al. 2005 (object-file correspondence diverging from the percept).
- **Against, or bounding**: Lamy et al. 2015 (object-file updating contingent on awareness in their paradigm), qualified by Schoeberl et al. 2018. Mole 2008 (spatial versus object attention), partly answered by Norman et al.
- **Current state**: selection and some object-level processing without awareness are well supported. Object-file *updating* without awareness is contested. Tracking without awareness is untested, as far as these searches reached.

### 5. Is a phenomenal condition needed for justification? {#justification}
- **Sides**: Smithies against Berger, Nanay & Quilty-Dunn.
- **Current state**: ongoing.

## The Weighing: What the Evidence Licenses {#weighing}

For the article's central section, stated as the Map's synthesis, not any cited author's:

1. **Established at source**: early vision individuates and tracks a few objects without describing them (Pylyshyn & Storm 1988; Pylyshyn 2001). Pylyshyn calls the relation demonstrative-like.
2. **Established at source**: object-level attentional selection, grouping and surface completion can run without awareness of the selected objects (Norman et al. 2013, 2015; Mattingley et al. 1997; Driver & Vuilleumier 2001). The object-file system's correspondences can diverge from the conscious percept (Mitroff et al. 2005).
3. **Not established**: that FINST indexing or MOT tracking runs without awareness of the tracked objects. In MOT the objects are seen. Pylyshyn makes no such claim.
4. **Counter-evidence**: some object-file updating is awareness-dependent (Lamy et al. 2015), within limits (Schoeberl et al. 2018).
5. **Campbell's thesis survives the vision science as a thesis about knowledge**: it grants the sub-personal latching. The vision science moves the dispute; it does not settle it. What remains is whether "knowledge of reference" names a real further achievement that needs consciousness (Campbell, Smithies) or a gloss that sub-personal selection plus availability supplies (Barkasi, Berger et al.).
6. **For the Map's trade-off** (`singular-thought-and-acquaintance#trade-off`): the perceptual-demonstrative survivor is now *contested on two fronts*. One is empirical (selection without awareness); the other conceptual (is knowledge of reference needed?). The trade-off's verdict should move from "plausibly survives" toward "survives only on Campbell's or Smithies's contested notion". The Map's other route, consciousness entering through the *thinker* on moderate phenomenal intentionality theory, is untouched by any of this.

## Relation to the Map's Tenets

- **Dualism**: no support either way. If Campbell is right, the result is consciousness-dependence for one kind of knowledge, which physicalists accept. If the indexing rivals are right, a phenomenal condition on reference loses its best case. That is a cost to the Map's narrow claim, not a refutation of dualism, which never rested on it.
- **Bidirectional Interaction**: Campbell's "Space and Action" abstract gives conscious attention the role of identifying "the target of the action for the benefit of the underlying brain systems". This is suggestive, but Campbell is no interactionist. The sea-of-faces case shows pointing proceeding *without* conscious attention, which cuts against any reading of this literature as evidence that consciousness is needed for action. Say both.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits**: the preconceptual mechanism is the simpler account of perceptual reference. The tenet licenses doubt that simplicity settles the personal-level question. It does not license dismissing a well-evidenced sub-personal mechanism. Its empirical standing is itself contested (Ayare & Srivastava 2024), which is a reason for care, not a reason to discount it.
- **No Many Worlds; Minimal Quantum Interaction**: not engaged. Do not force a connection.

## Guards for the Article Writer (Binding) {#guards}

**Calibration (from the task):**
- Preconceptual indexing that works without consciousness counts **against** the necessity of consciousness for reference. Scope it: against necessity for **sub-personal** reference (selection and tracking). Against Campbell's **knowledge-of-reference** thesis it is pressure, not refutation, because Campbell grants the sub-personal latching.
- **Do not overclaim Campbell.** His thesis is that conscious attention *provides knowledge of the reference* of a demonstrative (verified, book and ch. "What Is Knowledge of Reference?" abstracts). He does not say non-conscious vision cannot select objects. He grants non-conscious perception (2007) and reliable unconscious pointing (sea of faces, p. 9 via Williams).
- **Weigh the contrast; do not enlist it for dualism.** Neither outcome supports dualism. Say so in the lede and in Relation to Site Perspective.

**Author stances:**
- **Pylyshyn**: a computationalist and no dualist (Fodor & Pylyshyn's reference-only semantics aims at "the naturalistic account of content that cognitive science lacks" **[verbatim, publisher's description]**). His claim is *preconceptual*. **Do not write that Pylyshyn says indexing is unconscious or pre-attentive.** He dropped "preattentive" in 2001 (p. 149). The 2007 Scholarpedia "non-attentional" is paired with "automatic", and says nothing about awareness.
- **Campbell**: a relationalist (`naturalist-relationalism`). His selection model is location-based, via feature integration (chapter abstracts). Since H is imaginary, never call the sea-of-faces case a study or patient.
- **Smithies**: the role is epistemic. Do not describe his metaphysics.
- **Clark**: a pluralist about layers, not an opponent of indexes.
- **Raftopoulos and Müller**: allies of object-file grounding, *within* perceptual awareness.
- **Burge**: rejects a consciousness condition on perceptual representation (per Begby, on Evans), **and** rejects attribution-free perceptual reference (2022). Do not quote Burge on blindsight or unconscious perception.
- **Matthen**: motion-guiding vision supports demonstratives and contributes to the experience of seeing. He is not an "unconscious reference" theorist.
- **Barkasi; Berger, Nanay and Quilty-Dunn**: they deny the *only* and the *special epistemic role* respectively. They do not deny that consciousness often plays these roles.
