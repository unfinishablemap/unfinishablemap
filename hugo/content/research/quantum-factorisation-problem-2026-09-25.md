## Executive Summary

Entanglement, separability and "the subsystem" are defined only relative to a **tensor product structure** (TPS) on Hilbert space, and Hilbert space does not come with one. Zanardi (2001) and Zanardi, Lidar & Lloyd (2004) make this explicit: the partition into subsystems is fixed by which observables and interactions are operationally accessible, so "the associated notion of quantum entanglement" is "relative and observable-induced". Tegmark (2015) named the physicists' version the **quantum factorization problem** and argued that it is tied to what an observer is. The physics literature since then (quantum mereology: Carroll & Singh, Kabernik–Pollack–Singh, Cotler et al., Loizeau & Sels, Zanardi et al. 2024) works on picking a *preferred* TPS from the Hamiltonian, and whether that can be done uniquely is actively disputed (Stoica vs. Soulas et al.). **None of that work picks out a subject's boundary.** Even a unique local TPS gives only the elementary degrees of freedom. It does not say which collection of them makes up one mind, and it does not rule out nested or overlapping candidates. That leftover is the quantum form of IIT's **exclusion** problem and of the **boundary problem**. Any claim that entanglement unifies or individuates a subject owes an account at **three separable levels**, explained below: the fine-grained factorisation, the subject's boundary, and exclusion of nested candidates. The Map's quantum-binding cluster currently states at most the first, and only in one sentence ([quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity](/topics/quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity/) L98). The problem also touches the Map's **preferred mechanism**, not only its binding speculations, because post-decoherence selection relies on einselection, and einselection presupposes a system/environment split.

## The Problem, Stated in Three Levels (the article should use this decomposition)

The review's §1.1 runs three demands together ("which decomposition is privileged, where the subject's boundary lies, and why one subject rather than nested ones"). They have different literatures and different status, and the article should keep them apart:

1. **Level 1 — fine-grained factorisation (the TPS).** Which tensor factorisation of the total Hilbert space counts as "the" subsystems at all? *Literature*: Zanardi 2001; Zanardi, Lidar & Lloyd 2004; Tegmark 2015; Cotler et al. 2019; Carroll & Singh 2021; Kabernik, Pollack & Singh 2020; Loizeau & Sels 2024; Zanardi et al. 2024; Stoica 2022; Soulas et al. 2025. *Status*: contested. The best positive results (Cotler et al.) give generic uniqueness of a *local* TPS **given that a local presentation exists**; Stoica argues that anything fixed by *H* and |ψ⟩ alone is either non-unique or physically idle; Soulas et al. claim to reconcile the two in favour of uniqueness.
2. **Level 2 — the subject's boundary (the coarse cut).** Given the elementary factors, which *collection* of them is the one entangled whole that is a subject? Brain? A microtubule network? Brain plus the environment it is already decohering into? *Literature*: IIT's exclusion postulate (classical analogue); Gómez-Emilsson & Percy 2023 (boundary problem, already in corpus); Carroll & Singh 2021 handles only one **system/environment bipartition** chosen for quasi-classicality, which is the observer's *object*, not the observer. *Status*: no quantum-specific solution located.
3. **Level 3 — exclusion / nesting (the combination face).** Entangled subsystems of an entangled whole are themselves candidate wholes, and so are supersystems. Why exactly *one* subject and not a nested hierarchy? *Literature*: IIT 4.0's exclusion via "maximal existence"; Chalmers's subject-combination problem; Gambini & Pullin 2025 (entanglement *as* the subject-summing solution, from panprotopsychism); Ismael & Schaffer 2020 (quantum holism pushed to its limit gives priority monism, the cosmopsychist drift). *Status*: open.

Why the split matters for the Map. Level 1 is a problem for *everyone* who uses quantum mechanics, including physicalist Everettians (it is the Stoica/Carroll dispute). Level 2 and Level 3 are the Map's *own* debt, and they are the quantum form of [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2)'s **pairing** gap. The article should not present Level 1's difficulty as if it were special to consciousness theories. It should also not let Level 1 results (even a clean Soulas-style uniqueness theorem) pass as a solution to Levels 2–3.

### Zanardi, Lidar & Lloyd (2004), "Quantum Tensor Product Structures are Observable Induced"
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0308043 · doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.92.060402
- **Type**: Paper (PRL 92, 060402)
- **Key points**: The partition into subsystems "is dictated by the set of operationally accessible interactions and measurements"; entanglement is therefore relative. This is the paper [quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity](/topics/quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity/) already cites (ref. 1, L98, L232).
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral, and a sharp constraint on the binding cluster. **Observer-relativity is a specific hazard for a dualist**: if the TPS is fixed by what is accessible *to an observer*, then using entanglement to *constitute* the observer is circular. That circularity is the article's central point.
- **Quote** (abstract, grep-verified from the raw arXiv `citation_abstract`): "It is argued that the partition of a quantum system into subsystems is dictated by the set of operationally accessible interactions and measurements. The emergence of a multi-partite tensor product structure of the state-space and the associated notion of quantum entanglement are then relative and observable-induced."
- ⚠️ Title punctuation: arXiv writes "observable-induced" (hyphen); the PRL/Crossref title is "Quantum Tensor Product Structures are Observable Induced" (no hyphen). The corpus's ref. list uses the unhyphenated form, which matches the publisher of record.

### Tegmark (2015), "Consciousness as a State of Matter"
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.1219 · doi:10.1016/j.chaos.2015.03.014 (metadata verified in the 2026-09-11 note)
- **Type**: Paper (*Chaos, Solitons & Fractals* 76, 238–270)
- **Key points** (all grep-verified in `pdftotext` of arXiv:1401.1219):
  - Coins the term. The problem is "why do conscious observers like us perceive the particular Hilbert space factorization corresponding to classical space (rather than Fourier space, say), and more generally, why do we perceive the world around us as a dynamic hierarchy of objects that are strongly integrated and relatively independent?" He adds: "This fundamental problem has received almost no attention in the literature."
  - It **reverses the usual direction**: "it is not so much that quantum mechanics is relevant to the brain, as the other way around. Specifically, consciousness is relevant to solving an open problem at the very heart of quantum mechanics: the quantum factorization problem."
  - **The "cruelest cut"**: find the factorisation "decomposing H into parts with the smallest interaction Hamiltonian H3 possible", then recurse. Tegmark reports that this "fails catastrophically when confronted with observation": the maximally independent decomposition lies in the energy eigenbasis, which gives the **Quantum Zeno Paradox** ("if we decompose our universe into maximally independent objects, then all change grinds to a halt").
  - **The quantum integration paradox**: on his quantum generalisation of integrated information, "no matter how large a quantum system we create, its state can never contain more than about a quarter of a bit of integrated information!" (Φ ≈ 0.2516 for the optimum). His conclusion: "the integration principle must be supplemented by at least one additional principle."
- **Tenet alignment**: Mixed. His programme is physicalist ("perceptronium") and conflicts with Tenet 1. But his *diagnosis* helps the Map: integration plus independence do not by themselves pick out the observer, and "a closer examination of consciousness may be required" to solve the factorisation problem. The quarter-bit bound is **directly adverse to state-based entanglement-binding**: if integration measured on the quantum *state* is capped at ~0.25 bits for any system size, "entanglement of the state = unity of experience" cannot deliver the richness of experience. **Caveat for the article**: this is Tegmark's own state-based measure, not IIT's Φ and not a no-go on every quantum unity notion. [entanglement-binding-hypothesis](/concepts/entanglement-binding-hypothesis/) has already moved binding into the *interaction Hamiltonian* (following Georgiev), and Tegmark's Hamiltonian-side analysis (the cruelest cut) is the relevant test for that version.
- **Quote**: "This 'quantum factorization problem' appears intimately related to the nature of an observer."
- **Corpus status**: cited by zero articles (the 2026-09-11 note measured 0 corpus-wide). The corpus's Tegmark citations are the 2000 decoherence paper.

### Carroll & Singh (2021), "Quantum Mereology"
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12938 · doi:10.1103/PhysRevA.103.022213 (verified 2026-09-11)
- **Key points** (abstract, raw arXiv): a preferred **bipartite** system/environment split "can be defined by looking for subsystems that exhibit quasi-classical behavior": pointer states robust against environmental monitoring and localised around classical trajectories; "an in-principle algorithm … minimizing a combination of entanglement growth and internal spreading of the system."

### Kabernik, Pollack & Singh (2020), "Quantum State Reduction: Generalized Bipartitions from Algebras of Observables"
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12851 · doi:10.1103/PhysRevA.101.032303 (Crossref: PRA 101(3), 032303, 2020-03-05)

## The Map's Own Exposure (the question the 2026-09-11 note left open)

1. **The binding cluster (direct exposure).** [quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity](/topics/quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity/) L98 states the Level-1 point in one sentence, cites ZLL 2004, and routes the rest to [subject-census](/positions/subject-census/). [entanglement-binding-hypothesis](/concepts/entanglement-binding-hypothesis/) L84–86 answers a *Georgiev-shaped* separability objection by moving binding into the interaction Hamiltonian, which **presupposes** a TPS in which "subsystems" and their "interaction Hamiltonian" are defined. The move is legitimate, but it silently assumes Level 1 is settled. Tegmark's cruelest-cut result is relevant here: *minimising* the interaction term across a cut is the most natural way to pick subsystems from *H*, and it gives maximally non-interacting parts, the opposite of what binding needs. That does not refute the hypothesis. It shows that the natural physics criterion for "subsystem" points away from it.
2. **The preferred mechanism (indirect but real exposure).** [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1) (post-decoherence selection) makes consciousness select among einselected outcomes. Einselection and pointer states are defined relative to a system/environment split, which is exactly what Carroll & Singh (2021) try to *derive*, and Stoica (2022) names "various proposals based on decoherence" among the views his no-go covers. A grep of [post-decoherence-selection-programme](/apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme/) for `factori` / `tensor` found **zero** hits, so the apex does not book this. **This is the highest-value finding for the downstream article**: the factorisation debt belongs to the whole interface programme, not just to the speculative binding pages, and the article should say so rather than treat it as a problem only for the Map's more speculative wing.
3. **A possible dualist move, and its circularity.** Tegmark's inversion ("consciousness is relevant to solving … the quantum factorization problem") suggests a dualist option. The *pairing* of a non-physical subject with a physical system could be what privileges a factorisation, just as ZLL make the TPS relative to accessible observables. On that reading the subject's accessible "observables" fix its own system. **But** (a) this is circular if the same factorisation is then used to *individuate* the subject, (b) it collides with [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2)'s **census-circularity** gap, since a pairing that defines the subsystem cannot be tested by subsystem-level signatures, and (c) it is a new, unwritten posit. The article may *name* this route as a candidate direction. It must not present it as a solution, and it must say that it turns the factorisation debt into the pairing-law debt already booked at [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2) rather than discharging it.
4. **Level 3 and Tenet 4.** The Map's indexical argument against MWI ("why am *I* this branch?") needs a determinate "I". If subject boundaries are TPS-relative, a many-worlder can reply that the Map's own subjects are no more privileged than branches. The article should note this parity risk. It is the same parity the 2026-09-11 note flagged as "Reading A".

## Major Positions

### Observable-relativity (Zanardi, Lidar, Lloyd; Kabernik, Pollack, Singh)
- **Core claim**: subsystems, and therefore entanglement, are relative to an algebra of accessible observables.
- **Relation to tenets**: Compatible with dualism, but it rules out treating entanglement as an *intrinsic, observer-independent* unity-maker. That is the version the binding cluster needs.

### Hamiltonian-selection / quantum mereology (Carroll & Singh; Cotler et al.; Loizeau & Sels; Soulas et al.; Zanardi et al. 2024)
- **Core claim**: a preferred TPS can be picked out from *H* (and |ψ⟩) by locality, quasi-classicality, minimal scrambling or spectral structure.
- **Relation to tenets**: Neutral on Tenet 1. It works at Level 1 (and the object-level bipartition) only. If it succeeds, it helps the Map's einselection dependence. It does not settle subject boundaries.

### No-go / non-uniqueness (Stoica 2022, 2025)
- **Core claim**: physically relevant structure fixed by *H* and |ψ⟩ alone is non-unique. Stoica 2025 extends the argument to observers and concludes that intrinsic natures are needed.
- **Relation to tenets**: Supports Tenet 1's anti-structuralism. It *also* undercuts entanglement-structure as subject-individuator, and it threatens the Map's decoherence-based mechanism. It cuts in both directions.

### Consciousness-first factorisation (Tegmark 2015, physicalist)
- **Core claim**: the properties that make something an observer (integration, independence, dynamics, utility) should fix the factorisation.
- **Relation to tenets**: The mechanism is physicalist, but the direction of explanation (observer before factorisation) fits the Map. His own failures (the quarter-bit bound, the Quantum Zeno Paradox) are usable evidence.

### Entanglement-as-subject (Gambini & Pullin; the Map's own binding cluster)
- **Core claim**: entanglement dissolves the parts' individuality, and so answers subject-summing.
- **Relation to tenets**: Gambini & Pullin are panprotopsychist. The Map's version is dualist. Both owe Levels 1–3, and neither is shown (on abstracts) to pay them.

### Exclusion by maximum (IIT)
- **Core claim**: of overlapping candidates, only the maximum-φ substrate is conscious.
- **Relation to tenets**: Conflicts with Tenet 1 (identity theory). It is the only worked Level-2/3 rule in the literature and so the benchmark any Map pairing law must match or beat. It presupposes units.

## Key Debates

### Can a Hamiltonian fix a unique TPS?
- **Sides**: Cotler et al. and Soulas et al. (yes, generically / with state) vs. Stoica (not if physically relevant).
- **Current state**: open. Soulas et al. (Dec 2025) is a preprint. No published Carroll reply to Stoica was located (per the 2026-09-11 note).

### Does entanglement solve or worsen the combination problem?
- **Sides**: Gambini & Pullin (solves subject-summing); Ismael & Schaffer's holism and the Map's own L98 (it drifts toward one cosmic whole); Georgiev (factorisation means split minds).
- **Current state**: open. No one located addresses the TPS-relativity of the entanglement being invoked.

### Is the factorisation problem a problem *for* consciousness theories or *solved by* them?
- **Sides**: the ChatGPT review (a debt owed by entanglement-based unity claims) vs. Tegmark 2015 and Stoica 2025 (observers are what fix it).
- **Current state**: both readings are live. The article should present both and state that the Map cannot help itself to the second reading without a pairing law.
