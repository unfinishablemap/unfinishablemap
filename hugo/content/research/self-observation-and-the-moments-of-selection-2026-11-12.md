## Headline Findings (read first)

1. **The strong form of the premise fails.** Nothing read supports the claim that self-observation can locate individual selection events in time. Three independent results block it: the timescale gap (finding 4), the reconstructed character of introspected decision time (finding 6), and the verification limit the Map already holds as [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4), which William James stated in 1890 (finding 8).
2. **A weaker form survives and is worth an article.** Self-observation does sort decisions into kinds: arbitrary picking, effortless settling, effortful resolution, withholding. Those kinds can serve as *conditioning variables*. That is the only job [P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2) and [P-Q9](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q9) leave for them, and it is a job, since "only conditionals on the conscious state itself are free" and a conscious state has to be classified before anything can be conditioned on it.
3. **Measurement theory gives the observer's self-knowledge a role and no clock.** London and Bauer's "faculty of introspection" is what entitles the observer to "cut the chain of statistical correlations". Von Neumann's boundary between observed and observer is "arbitrary to a very large extent" and ends at the observer's 'abstract "ego"'. Wigner's friend had the outcome "already decided in his mind". None of the three says when.
4. **The timescale mismatch is ten to nineteen orders of magnitude, and Tegmark works a decision example himself.** His decoherence times are 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻²⁰ s against dynamical times of 10⁻³ to 10⁻¹ s. His own worked case is "a single thought or snap decision where the outcome feels unpredictable to us": the superposition of the two decisions decoheres "already during their process of formation" and leaves "a statistical mixture". Physics therefore offers no decision-sized quantum moment. If selection has a decision-sized grain, the grain comes from how the alternatives are coarse-grained, and the Map has no model that fixes that ([P-Q10](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q10)).
5. **The finest grain claimed for experience is about 30 ms; the claims are contested.** Pöppel reports 30–40 ms "system states" and a 2–3 s integration window. VanRullen reports several perceptual rhythms (about 10 Hz and 7 Hz). Herzog and colleagues put conscious updates "hundreds of milliseconds" after the stimulus. Fekete and colleagues reply that the same data fit continuous perception. On any of these figures experience could register only aggregates of physical events.
6. **The introspected time of a decision is largely inferred after the fact.** Banks and Isham shifted the reported decision time by delaying a feedback beep. Lau and colleagues shifted it with magnetic stimulation applied after the movement. Matsuhashi and Hallett's probe method put the intention 1.42 s before movement, about a second earlier than Libet's recall method.
7. **Where neuroscience finds noise-driven timing is where experience reports the least authorship.** Schurger's accumulator model makes the time of threshold crossing depend on "spontaneous subthreshold fluctuations", in a task of arbitrary self-paced movement. Maoz and colleagues found the readiness potential "strikingly absent" for deliberate decisions, which they say are "generally not driven by random fluctuations". Khalighinejad and colleagues found neural variability *falling* before self-initiated action. Kane and the phenomenology of effort point the other way, at torn, effortful choices. The two maps do not coincide, and the article must say so.
8. **James supplies the phenomenology and the limit together.** The express fiat arises only when an inhibiting idea must be neutralised; "the immense majority of human decisions are decisions without effort"; and "the question of free-will is insoluble on strictly psychologic grounds".
9. **The Abhidhamma lead needs correcting.** In Bodhi's edition the *javana* stage is where "wholesome or unwholesome cittas originate". Volition (*cetanā*) is one of seven universal factors present in every moment of consciousness, so it is not "generated in" javana. The cognitive series is a commentarial theory in which "billions of mind-moments" pass in an eyeblink. It is a doctrine about moral weight and no first-person timing report.
10. **Kane makes exactly the correspondence claim, as a postulate.** He writes that the felt "uncertainty and inner tension" of a self-forming choice "is thus reflected in the indeterminacy of our neural processes themselves". No empirical test of that correspondence was found. His 2024 book drops the doubled effort; Palmer, following Haji, finds the luck problem again in the single-effort revision.
11. **The deflationary rival is strengthened by a formal result.** Breuer (1995) proves that no observer can distinguish all states of a system containing itself, "irrespective of whether this system is a classical or a quantum mechanical one and irrespective of whether the time evolution is deterministic or stochastic". Self-opacity is then expected of any embedded observer and carries no information about indeterminism. That is Planck's "no eye can see itself" in theorem form. The link to Planck is this note's inference; Breuer's abstract does not discuss free will.

## Executive Summary

The question was whether self-observation can locate where and when post-decoherence selection would occur in a conscious life, and whether those moments coincide with choice. Read at source, the literature answers the first part no and the second part "not where expected". Measurement theory from von Neumann to London and Bauer gives the observer's self-knowledge a role in ending the chain of correlations and says nothing about timing. Formal work on self-measurement (Breuer) shows a limit that holds for classical and deterministic systems too, so self-opacity signals nothing about indeterminism. Decoherence in neural tissue is ten to nineteen orders of magnitude faster than any decision or any claimed grain of experience, and the introspected time of deciding is itself reconstructed from later cues. The neuroscience that does find noise-driven timing finds it in arbitrary, unreasoned movements, while deliberate choices show no readiness potential and are described as value-driven. Felt effort, in James and in Kane, sits with the deliberate, conflicted cases. What self-observation can supply is a typology of decision situations, and under [P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2) a typology of conscious states is what a test of the Map's selection hypothesis would have to condition on. The recommended article is therefore about self-observation as a way of saying *which conditionals to test*, with the failure of the stronger reading stated up front.

## Key Sources

### London & Bauer, *La théorie de l'observation en mécanique quantique* (1939); English translation in Wheeler & Zurek (eds.), *Quantum Theory and Measurement* (Princeton, 1983), pp. 217–259

- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/quantumtheorymea0000john (lending-restricted; full-text search only)
- **Type**: Monograph (Hermann, Paris; Actualités scientifiques et industrielles 775), translated
- **Stance**: Physicists writing on measurement. They give consciousness an explicit role. Whether that role is causal is disputed: Steven French reads them through Husserl as describing a change of the observer's relation to the object, not a mental force acting on matter (French 2023, *A Phenomenological Approach to Quantum Mechanics*, OUP; Crossref-verified, not read). Do not present them as holding that a mind pushes a wavefunction.
- **The passage** [raw-snippet], rebuilt from overlapping snippets; "[…]" marks formulae or words no snippet returned:
  - "The observer has a completely different impression. For him it is only the object x and the apparatus y that belong to the external world, to what he calls "objectivity." By contrast he has with himself relations of a very special character. He possesses a characteristic and quite familiar faculty which we can call the "faculty of introspection." He can keep track from moment to moment of his own state. By virtue of this "immanent knowledge" he attributes to himself the right to create his own objectivity—that is, to cut the chain of statistical correlations summarized in […]"
  - "Accordingly, we will label this creative action as "making objective." By it the observer establishes his own framework of objectivity and acquires a new piece of information about the object in question."
  - "Thus it is not a mysterious interaction between the apparatus and the object that produces a new ψ for the system during the measurement. It is only the consciousness of an "I" who can separate himself from the former function […] and, by virtue of his observation, set up a new objectivity in attributing to the object henceforward a new function […]"
  - "We note the essential role played by the consciousness of the observer in this transition"
- **French fragment** [raw-snippet], from the editors' footnote: "dispose d'une faculté caractéristique et bien familière, que nous pouvons appeler la 'faculté d'introspection': il peut se rendre compte de manière immédiate". The continuation was not recovered.
- **Page**: the passage is standardly cited to p. 252 of the Wheeler–Zurek volume. The scan search returns no page numbers, so that page is **unconfirmed this run**.
- **Bearing**: self-knowledge is what licenses the observer to end the chain. No privileged *moment* is identified. "From moment to moment" is a translation; see Dropped Leads.
- **Tenet alignment**: congenial to Tenet 3 on a causal reading, neutral on a phenomenological one. Silent on Tenet 4.

### von Neumann, *Mathematical Foundations of Quantum Mechanics* (1932; tr. R. T. Beyer, Princeton, 1955), ch. VI

- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/mathematicalfoun0000vonn (search only)
- **Stance**: Mathematician. He states psycho-physical parallelism and argues the placement of the cut is conventional. He does not say consciousness causes collapse.
- **Key points** [raw-snippet]:
  - "and this is perceived by the observer. That is, we must always divide the world into two parts, the one being the observed"
  - "The boundary between the two is arbitrary to a very large extent."
  - "boundary can be pushed arbitrarily deeply into the interior of the body of the actual observer is the content of the principle of the psycho-physical parallelism"
  - The three-part division ends: "II his retina, nerve tracts and brain, III his abstract "ego.""
  - "subjective perception leads us into the intellectual inner life of the individual, which is extra-observational by its very nature"
- **Page**: standardly pp. 418–421 of the Beyer translation; **unconfirmed this run**.
- **Bearing**: the movable cut is the classical statement that the formalism does not fix *where* the chain ends. By the same token it does not fix *when*. An article that says "selection happens at moment t" is adding something von Neumann's analysis leaves open by design.

### Wigner, "Remarks on the Mind-Body Question", in I. J. Good (ed.), *The Scientist Speculates* (Heinemann, 1961)

- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/scientistspecula0000good (search only)
- **Stance**: Held in 1961 that consciousness has a special role. Tegmark records that he later withdrew: "Wigner changed his mind and gave up this idea" [raw, Tegmark 2000, footnote 1] after the first decoherence paper.
- **Key points** [raw-snippet]:
  - "It follows that the being with a consciousness must have a different role in quantum mechanics than the inanimate measuring device"
  - "the impression which one gains at an interaction may, and in general does, modify the probabilities with which one gains"
  - On the friend: "the question whether he did or did not see the flash was already decided in his"; the snippet ends there, and the sentence is standardly completed "mind, before I asked him".
- **Bearing**: Wigner's argument uses the friend's own testimony, which is self-observation in the plainest sense, to say the outcome was fixed before the outside observer asked. It fixes an upper bound ("before I asked") and no moment.
- **Tenet alignment**: aligns with Tenet 3 as of 1961; Wigner's later retreat should be stated wherever he is cited.

### Albert, "On quantum-mechanical automata" (1983) and "A Quantum-Mechanical Automaton" (1987)

- **Metadata** (Crossref): *Physics Letters A* 98(5–6): 249–252, DOI 10.1016/0375-9601(83)90863-0; *Philosophy of Science* 54(4): 577–585, DOI 10.1086/289406.
- **Stance**: Philosopher of physics; no dualist commitment.
- **1987 abstract** [raw]: "A Quantum-Mechanical automaton, equipped with mechanisms for the measurement and the recording and the prediction of certain physical properties of the world, is described. It is inquired what sort of empirical description such an automaton would produce of itself. It turns out that this description would be a very novel one, one such as was never imagined in the conventional discussions of measurement."
- **1983** [secondary, via Yurov, arXiv:quant-ph/0301004, unrefereed]: Albert showed "there are some combination of facts that can in principle be predicted by an […] automaton only about itself".
- **Bearing**: on the secondary report, self-measurement can give an automaton predictive access no outside observer has. That is a claim about *what* can be known, with no timing content. Treat as a lead until the 1983 paper is read.

### Breuer, "The Impossibility of Accurate State Self-Measurements" (1995)

- **Metadata** (Crossref): *Philosophy of Science* 62(2): 197–214, DOI 10.1086/289852.
- **Stance**: Philosopher of physics. The result is interpretation-neutral.
- **Abstract** [raw]: "It is shown that it is impossible for an observer to distinguish all present states of a system in which he or she is contained, irrespective of whether this system is a classical or a quantum mechanical one and irrespective of whether the time evolution is deterministic or stochastic. As a corollary, this implies that it is impossible for an observer to measure the EPR-correlations between himself or herself and an outside system."
- **Bearing**: the limit on self-measurement is structural and indifferent to determinism. Two consequences. First, an observer cannot fully read off its own state, so introspection cannot be a complete instrument on any physics. Second, since the limit holds under determinism, running into it tells the observer nothing about whether its dynamics are open. This is the formal partner of Planck and MacKay.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral. It supports [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4)'s verification limit from an independent direction and gives no support to Tenets 2 or 3.

### Bong et al., "A strong no-go theorem on the Wigner's friend paradox" (2020); Wiseman, Cavalcanti & Rieffel (2023); Kent (2023)

- **Metadata** (Crossref): *Nature Physics* 16(12): 1199–1205, DOI 10.1038/s41567-020-0990-x; *Quantum* 7: 1112, DOI 10.22331/q-2023-09-14-1112; Kent, arXiv:2302.12707.
- **Key points**:
  - Abstract [raw]: "we prove that if quantum evolution is controllable on the scale of an observer, then one of 'No-Superdeterminism', 'Locality' or 'Absoluteness of Observed Events'—that every observed event exists absolutely, not relatively—must be false".
  - Assumption 1 [raw]: "An observed event is a real single event, and not relative to anything or anyone."
  - The experiment is "a proof-of-principle experiment where a photon's path is deemed an observer" [raw].
  - Wiseman et al. give a version that "takes seriously the idea that a system's having thoughts is a sufficient condition for it to be an observer" [raw], and it needs human-level artificial intelligence on a large quantum computer.
  - Kent's comment argues the motivations hold only on "replacing "thoughtfulness" by "consciousness" or "conscious thoughtfulness"" [raw, abstract].
- **Bearing**: [post-decoherence-selection](/concepts/post-decoherence-selection/) already has a section on local friendliness. For this article the theorem matters in one way. It tests whether an observer's observed events are absolute when *another* agent can reverse the observer's evolution. It is silent on when an observed event becomes definite for the observer. The concept page already records the Map's response: it keeps Absoluteness of Observed Events and denies that a laboratory containing a conscious observer can be recohered from outside. Nothing here changes that.

### Tegmark, "Importance of quantum decoherence in brain processes" (2000)

- **Metadata** (Crossref): *Physical Review E* 61(4): 4194–4206, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194. Read as arXiv:quant-ph/9907009v2.
- **Stance**: Physicist; argues the brain is classical for cognitive purposes. Favours an Everettian picture elsewhere. An opponent of quantum-consciousness proposals.
- **Key points** [raw]:
  - "We find that the decoherence timescales (∼ 10−13 − 10−20 seconds) are typically much shorter than the relevant dynamical timescales (∼ 10−3 − 10−1 seconds), both for regular neuron firing and for kink-like polarization excitations in microtubules."
  - "the degrees of freedom of the human brain that relate to cognitive processes should be thought of as a classical rather than quantum system"
  - He splits the world into subject, object and environment, and then treats a decision: "let us assume that this can happen even at the level of a single thought or snap decision where the outcome feels unpredictable to us". The subject "makes a snap decision on whether to smile" or frown.
  - "subject decoherence is so fast that such superpositions decohere already during their process of formation. Therefore we are never even close to being able to perceive superpositions of different perceptions."
  - Figure 5 caption: "The final result is a statistical mixture of the two states".
- **Bearing**: this is the most useful single source for the article. Tegmark grants, for illustration and "follow[ing] Stapp", that a snap decision could begin as a superposition of two outcomes. He then shows that decoherence turns it into a mixture before it forms. The mixture is exactly the state [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1) says selection acts on. So Tegmark's own example is a picture of post-decoherence selection *minus the selector*: two decohered decision-outcomes, weights one half each, and no account in the physics of which one the subject finds herself in. He takes that as showing nothing quantum remains. The Map takes the same mixture as the place where selection would act. The disagreement is over the mixture's interpretation, and both sides accept the timescales.
- **What it does to the premise**: the decohering events happen 10¹⁰ to 10¹⁹ times faster than a decision unfolds. No decision-sized "moment of decoherence" exists for experience to find.
- **Tenet alignment**: conflicts with Tenets 2 and 3 as he intends it; his numbers are the reason the Map prefers post-decoherence selection ([P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1)).

### Schurger, Sitt & Dehaene, "An accumulator model for spontaneous neural activity prior to self-initiated movement" (2012)

- **Metadata** (Crossref): *PNAS* 109(42): E2904–E2913, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1210467109.
- **Stance**: Neuroscientists. The model is classical; "noise" is autocorrelated neural fluctuation. They make no quantum claim and none should be attributed to them.
- **Key points** [raw]:
  - "the precise moment at which the decision threshold is crossed leading to movement is largely determined by spontaneous subthreshold fluctuations in neuronal activity"
  - The threshold crossing is "the "neural decision to move now"", and: "This event is conceptually distinct from the conscious decision to move, which refers to the feeling of an urge or intention to move that may or may not play a causal role."
  - "it sets into motion a cascade that is ballistic, but not deterministic"
  - The neural decision "might come very late in the time course of the RP", near "an abrupt increase in excitability in primary motor cortex ∼100 ms before the onset of muscle flexion".
  - "Although our study is not concerned with the subjective urge to move".
- **Bearing**: Schurger supplies the best physically specified candidate for a decision-sized event: a threshold crossing, late, timed by fluctuations. He separates it explicitly from the conscious decision and declines to say whether the latter is causal. The task is arbitrary self-paced movement with a weak imperative.

### Maoz, Yaffe, Koch & Mudrik, "Neural precursors of decisions that matter—an ERP study of deliberate and arbitrary choice" (2019)

A reader who wants selection to coincide with weighty choice gets no help here: on Maoz's account weighty choices are the ones values settle. ### The inferred time of deciding: Banks & Isham (2009); Lau, Rogers & Passingham (2007); Matsuhashi & Hallett (2008)

- **Metadata** (PubMed): *Psychological Science* 20(1): 17–21, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02254.x; *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience* 19(1): 81–90, DOI 10.1162/jocn.2007.19.1.81; *European Journal of Neuroscience* 28(11): 2344–2351, DOI 10.1111/j.1460-9568.2008.06525.x.

- **URL**: https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin26.htm (FULL chapter)

- **Key points** [raw], with pages from the embedded markers:

## Major Positions

### The observer's self-knowledge ends the chain (London & Bauer; Wigner 1961)
- **Core claim**: the observer's immediate knowledge of her own state is what warrants assigning the object a new state.
- **Relation to tenets**: nearest ancestor of Tenet 3. It supplies no timing and no account of selection among decohered alternatives, which postdates it.

### The cut is movable (von Neumann)
- **Core claim**: the formalism does not fix where observed ends and observer begins.
- **Relation to tenets**: permits the Map's placement and equally permits any other.

### Self-measurement is limited on any physics (Breuer; Planck; MacKay)
- **Core claim**: an observer cannot fully determine the state of a system that contains it, whether the dynamics are deterministic or not.
- **Relation to tenets**: the deflationary rival. It predicts felt openness and self-opacity without indeterminism. It conflicts with any *use of phenomenology as evidence* for Tenets 2 and 3 and is consistent with the tenets themselves.

### The brain is classical at decision grain (Tegmark)
- **Core claim**: neural superpositions decohere before they form; a snap decision yields a statistical mixture.
- **Relation to tenets**: the Map accepts the timescales ([P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1)) and reads the mixture as improper and in need of selection.

### Decision timing is noise-plus-threshold in arbitrary action and value-driven in deliberate action (Schurger; Maoz)
- **Core claim**: the readiness potential reflects accumulated fluctuations where nothing is at stake and is absent where something is.
- **Relation to tenets**: supports [P-A3](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a3). Cuts against placing selection at weighty choices on the strength of neural noise.

### The time of deciding is reconstructed (Banks & Isham; Lau et al.)
- **Core claim**: reports of when one decided depend on post-action information.
- **Relation to tenets**: supports [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4) empirically. Removes introspective timing as an instrument.

### Effort marks the open choice (James, as phenomenology; Kane, as metaphysics)
- **Core claim**: a minority of decisions involve felt effort, and (Kane only) that effort corresponds to neural indeterminacy.
- **Relation to tenets**: James's description is usable. Kane's correspondence is unevidenced, and his event-causal framework is the rival [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2) rejects.

## Where the Disagreement Actually Lies

1. **How many selections does a decision contain?** On Tegmark's numbers, between 10¹⁰ and 10¹⁹ decoherence times elapse per decision-relevant neural process. Either selection occurs at something like that rate, in which case no selection is individually experienced and experience registers at most an aggregate; or selection occurs once per coarse-grained alternative (a firing pattern, a threshold crossing), in which case something has to say what counts as an alternative. Quantum Darwinism's single branch index, from the 2026-09-10 note, makes the second reading *available*: fixing one label fixes all records. Nothing found makes it *required*. The Map has no model that decides ([P-Q10](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q10)). The article should state this as an open fork and should not choose a horn by fiat.
2. **Is the candidate moment the one physics points to or the one experience points to?** The physically specified candidate is a late threshold crossing timed by fluctuations in arbitrary action. The experientially salient candidate is effortful resolution in deliberate action. Schurger separates the first from the conscious decision. Maoz says the second is driven by values. They are different moments in different kinds of decision.
3. **Does felt openness discriminate anything?** Planck, MacKay and Breuer say an embedded observer would find its own choice open under determinism. James says the sense of the alternative is present even in effortless volition. So openness is everywhere and discriminates nothing. Effort is not everywhere, and the 2026-10-29 note found the deflationary account silent on it. Whether ordinary computational accounts of effort (conflict monitoring, opportunity cost) fill that silence was **not researched here** and is a gap.
4. **Can the instrument be read at all?** Reported decision time is inferred (finding 6), concurrent report slows the process (Fox), and naive introspection of process is poor while trained retrospective access is better (Petitmengin). So self-observation is more trustworthy about *what kind* of decision occurred than about *when* it occurred.

### The central tension, laid out

[P-A6](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a6) says where contact is made: at the trying. [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4) says the agent cannot certify from the inside that contact was efficacious. [P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2) says nothing publicly conditionable will show a deviation. [P-Q9](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q9) says the remaining exposure is in conditionals on intention, task or subject, and in "introspective fine structure". Taken together:

- Self-observation **cannot verify** that a selection occurred at a trying. [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4) forbids it in principle, and the timing studies show the report is not even a reliable clock.
- Self-observation **can classify** tryings: effortful or not, deliberate or arbitrary, completed or withheld. James's chapter is such a classification, and Maoz's experiment shows one such contrast (deliberate against arbitrary) has a neural signature.
- A classification is what [P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2)'s "conditionals on the conscious state" need. A test of the form [P-Q9](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q9) describes, residual dependence of outcome on a preregistered intention covariate given the measured physical state, requires the covariate to be defined, and first-person categories are candidate definitions.
- This does **not** turn phenomenology into evidence. The categories tell an experimenter where to look. A null result at a well-defined category would count against the Map ([P-Q9](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q9)'s shift condition says so). The article can therefore present self-observation as increasing the Map's exposure.

One unresolved point should be stated plainly. [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4) says introspection cannot certify efficacy. It does not say introspective *categories* are accurate. Banks and Isham, Johansson and Schwitzgebel give reasons to doubt the categories too, at least in untrained subjects. If the categories are unreliable, the conditioning variable is noisy and the test loses power. The article should say that the proposal inherits introspection's unreliability, and that Petitmengin's result is the reason to think training can reduce it.

### How the self-observation angle stands against Planck and MacKay

The 2026-10-29 note concluded that felt openness "cannot discriminate" between the determinist and indeterminist horns, and that the Map's one discriminator is "statistical rather than phenomenological". This note agrees and adds four things.

1. Breuer gives the rival a theorem that is explicitly indifferent to determinism.
2. James already conceded that the sense of alternative possibility attends effortless volition, which he took to be determined.
3. The timing studies show that the *when* of deciding is reconstructed, which is what a standpoint account would lead one to expect.
4. The one place the rival was silent, effort, is where Kane puts indeterminacy and where the evidence found is thin or adverse (Maoz: value-driven; Khalighinejad: less noise).

So the self-observation angle does not answer Planck and MacKay. It accepts their account of openness and relocates the Map's claim: the phenomenology supplies conditioning variables for the statistical discriminator and carries no evidential weight of its own.

## Assess-First Verdict

**The premise is weak in its strong form and sound in a weaker one. Write the article around the weaker one and state the failure of the stronger in the first paragraph.**

Strong form: "self-observation can find the moments at which post-decoherence selection occurs, and they coincide with choice." Not supported. The physical events are too fast by ten or more orders of magnitude. Introspected decision time is inferred. Efficacy cannot be certified from inside ([P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4)). Felt openness is predicted without indeterminism. The moments where noise-driven timing is evidenced are arbitrary movements, and the moments experience marks as most one's own are deliberate ones.

Weak form: "self-observation classifies decision situations, and that classification names the conditionals a test of selection would have to use." Supported as a programme. It is consistent with [P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2), [P-Q9](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q9), [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4) and [P-A6](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a6), it uses James, Maoz and Petitmengin without overreading them, and it exposes the Map to a possible null.

Recommended framing, in order:

1. Answer first: self-observation cannot time selection and cannot confirm it; it can say which kinds of conscious episode a selection hypothesis should be tested on.
2. The measurement-theory background as *history of a role without a clock* (von Neumann, London and Bauer, Wigner), with Breuer as the limit.
3. Tegmark's snap decision as the cleanest picture of what decoherence leaves, and the timescale gap stated with its numbers.
4. The fork over how many selections a decision contains, left open.
5. The two maps that do not coincide (Schurger and Maoz against James and Kane), stated as a finding.
6. The reliability of the instrument (Banks and Isham, Lau, Fox, Petitmengin).
7. Planck, MacKay and Breuer answered on their own terms: conceded on openness.
8. What could be tested, with the null stated as a risk.
9. Relation to Site Perspective across all five tenets.

Suggested title: keep "Self-Observation and the Moments of Selection". A more exact alternative is "Can Self-Observation Find the Moments of Selection?".

## Binding Guards for the Article Writer

1. **Never say introspection locates, detects, times or confirms a selection.** Say it classifies episodes. [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4) is binding.
2. **Do not present phenomenology as evidence for any tenet.** Felt openness in particular is conceded to Planck, MacKay and Breuer.
3. **State the timescale gap with Tegmark's numbers** and say it is unfavourable to the strong premise. Do not soften it with Hagan et al.: their figure concerns microtubules and still falls short.
4. **Tegmark is an opponent.** His snap-decision example may be used as a description of the decohered mixture. He draws the opposite conclusion from it and must be reported as doing so.
5. **Schurger's noise is classical.** Never call it quantum, and never say Schurger locates free will or selection. He says the conscious decision "may or may not play a causal role".
6. **Maoz does not say deliberate decisions are determined.** He says they are driven mainly by the values of the alternatives.
7. **London and Bauer**: quote "cut the chain"; do not write "completes the measurement" as their words; do not rest anything on "from moment to moment"; give p. 252 only after checking it; note French's non-causal reading.
8. **von Neumann** did not say consciousness causes collapse. **Wigner** withdrew his 1961 view.
9. **Albert 1983 and Peres 1984 were not read.** Cite Albert 1987's abstract or omit Albert.
10. **Stroud**: do not quote "psychological moment" as his.
11. **Abhidhamma**: volition is a universal factor; the javana stage is where kammically active cittas occur; the scheme is commentarial; no claim of introspective timing and no free-will doctrine. Do not enlist it.
12. **Kane**: the tension passage is a postulate. He is not a dualist. Use the 2024 single-effort view; the doubled effort is withdrawn. Do not reinstate the contrastive luck reply retracted on 2026-10-25. Kane 2024 was read only through Palmer.
13. **Libet**: he *proposed* the veto; the label "free won't" is not his; the veto is "potentially available". The 1985 abstract's veto result concerns a prearranged time to act.
14. **Brass and Haggard**: "candidate brain area", their word.
15. **Stapp**: "question-choice and quantum-Zeno holding", never "biasing". [P-Q4](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q4) and [P-Q5](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q5) demote Stapp-Zeno and Orch OR. The word "biasing" inside quotations of [P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2) and [P-A6](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a6) is the Map's own usage about its own corridor and may be quoted, not extended to Stapp.
16. **Born statistics**: selection leaves outcome frequencies Born-exact in every publicly conditionable context. Never describe it as shifting frequencies.
17. **Label every mechanism step speculative** ([P-Q10](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q10)), including both horns of the how-many-selections fork.
18. **The Breuer–Planck link and the Kane–Stapp timing resemblance are this note's inferences.** Present them as the Map's observations, not as the authors' claims.
19. **Banks and Isham, Lau, and Matsuhashi and Hallett studied simple spontaneous movements.** Do not generalise the inference effect to deliberate choice without saying it is untested there.
20. **Never concede epiphenomenalism.** The conclusion is that efficacy is not introspectively verifiable and that a statistical test is specifiable. It is not that consciousness does nothing.
21. Re-verify every citation at Crossref or the publisher before publishing; pages marked unconfirmed here must be confirmed or dropped.
