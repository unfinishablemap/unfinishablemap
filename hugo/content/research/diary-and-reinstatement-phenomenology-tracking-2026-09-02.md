## Executive Summary

**The August null was partly a vocabulary artefact.** The decisive measurement's two halves both exist as published paradigms — in literatures the semanticisation vocabulary never touches. What does not exist, on this search either, is their conjunction.

1. **Longitudinal within-memory phenomenology tracking exists, at scale, under "flashbulb memory" vocabulary.** Frinco et al. (2024) review **57 test–retest studies** of memory for public events since 1977. The model design is Talarico & Rubin (2003): the same memory rated on vividness, recollection, and belief at 1, 7, 42, and 224 days. Everyday memories' reported recollection and vividness **declined by degrees over 32 weeks** — a graded report-level fade, the falsifier's adverse direction — while flashbulb memories' reported recollection stayed high **as their consistency declined at the same rate as everyday memories'**: reported phenomenology dissociating from content fidelity, which is the manner/content independence the Map argues from, delivered by the same dataset.

2. **The remember-to-know shift literature is semanticisation tracked by memory-awareness report.** Conway, Gardiner, Perfect, Anderson & Cohen (1997) tracked psychology undergraduates' course knowledge across a term-plus-retest and found remembering giving way to knowing as knowledge schematised; Dewhurst, Conway & Brandt (2009) reproduced the R-to-K shift across repeated tests spanning six months. But the paradigm's report format is **categorical by construction** — each item's R→K transition is a flip of a binary judgment, so a graded within-item fade and a discrete within-item jump produce identical data. The paradigm demonstrates the transition and cannot adjudicate the falsifier.

3. **Diary methods reach the needed timescales but have not carried the needed instrument.** Linton's six-year self-study (1982) is the founding observation — repeated similar events fusing into semantic knowledge — without a phenomenology instrument. Thomsen et al. (2015) — the August note's flagged near-miss, now resolved — rated events in the diary week and measured remembering and **reliving once, at a 3.5-year follow-up**: reliving is an outcome predicted from encoding characteristics, not a within-memory trajectory. Frankenberg et al. (2022) observed **live longitudinal semanticisation over a decade** — specific memories becoming general ones in the same healthy participants, "without cognitive decline" — but measured as narrative category counts from a semi-structured interview, not felt re-experiencing.

4. **The reinstatement-probe half exists and mostly supports the access reading.** Odour cues retrieve the oldest memories — mostly from the first decade of life, maximally semanticised on any account — with the **strongest** "being brought back in time" phenomenology (Willander & Larsson 2006). Involuntary retrieval reaches more specific episodes with more physical reaction and mood impact than voluntary word-cued retrieval of the same person's past (Berntsen & Hall 2004) — so any fade curve built from voluntary verbal recall may be a retrieval-route artefact, exactly as the access reading predicts. Sekeres et al. (2016), already on the page, showed reminders reinstating lost detail within a week.

5. **A new confound the falsifier design must state: measurement changes the object.** Shifting to an observer perspective during one retrieval reduces vividness and the accuracy of subsequent retrievals (Marcotti & St. Jacques 2018); targeted memory reactivation during sleep after imagery rescripting left autobiographical memories less vivid (Translational Psychiatry 2024, flagged below). A repeated-measures within-memory design partially manufactures the trajectory it measures. Talarico & Rubin's structure — one baseline, one follow-up per delay group, between-groups across delays — is the existing solution and should be named in any falsifier-design section.

**Net status change for the page**: the falsifier moves from "decisive measurement not found, at a stated search ceiling" to "each half of the decisive design is published — longitudinal phenomenological tracking under flashbulb/diary vocabularies, reinstatement probes under Proustian/involuntary/reminder vocabularies — and no study conjoins them." The full protocol is now assemblable from published components; the conjunction remains the open falsifier. That is a stronger and more actionable "unrun" than August's.

## Key Sources

### The longitudinal phenomenology track (flashbulb test–retest)

**Talarico, J. M. & Rubin, D. C. (2003). "Confidence, not consistency, characterizes flashbulb memories."** *Psychological Science* 14(5): 455–461. DOI `10.1111/1467-9280.02453`.
- Duke students recorded their memory of learning of the 9/11 attacks and of a recent everyday event on 12 September 2001; retested at 1, 6, or 32 weeks (delays of 1, 7, 42, 224 days from the event).
- Verbatim (Europe PMC catalogue copy): "Consistency for the flashbulb and everyday memories did not differ, in both cases declining over time. However, ratings of vividness, recollection, and belief in the accuracy of memory declined only for everyday memories." And: "Flashbulb memories are not special in their accuracy, as previously claimed, but only in their perceived accuracy."
- **Both limbs of the falsifier's evidence space in one study.** Everyday memories: graded decline of reported recollection over 224 days — adverse direction, access uncontrolled. Flashbulb memories: reported reliving held high while content fidelity decayed normally — phenomenology tracking something other than stored content, friendly to the Map's manner/content independence and to its reading of Folville et al. (2022) already on the page.
- **Tenet alignment**: cuts both ways by design; the dissociation limb supports the Dualism-motivated constitutive reading more directly than the everyday-fade limb undermines it, because the fade limb is exposed to the access confound and the dissociation limb is not.

**Talarico, J. M. & Rubin, D. C. (2007). "Flashbulb memories are special after all; in phenomenology, not accuracy."** *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 21(5): 557–578. DOI `10.1002/acp.1293`.
- Same paradigm, the phenomenology conclusion in the title. Crossref-carried abstract (paraphrase): consistency of flashbulb and everyday memories does not differ; recollection and vividness ratings stay higher for flashbulb memories across delays; belief in accuracy declines only for everyday memories.

**Frinco, R., Muzzulini, B., Tinti, C., Luminet, O. & Schmidt, S. (2024). "A review of longitudinal studies on flashbulb memories. Where we started, are, and are going?"** *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 38(4). DOI `10.1002/acp.4233`.
- Reviews 57 test–retest flashbulb studies since Brown & Kulik (1977); reports "huge methodological heterogeneity" (phrase as carried in the Crossref abstract; verify at Wiley) across target events, populations, and criteria for consistency, vividness, and confidence.
- **Use**: the currency anchor. A 57-study longitudinal literature tracking within-memory phenomenological ratings existed the whole time the semanticisation-vocabulary search was returning null. Scope caution: flashbulb designs track memories for *public-event reception contexts* over months-to-years; none was found extending to the decades-long semanticisation endpoint.

**Hirst, W. et al. (2015). "A ten-year follow-up of a study of memory for the attack of September 11, 2001: Flashbulb memories and memories for flashbulb events."** *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 144(3): 604–623. DOI `10.1037/xge0000055`.
- Surveys at ~1 week, 11, 25, and 119 months. Rapid forgetting of both flashbulb and event memories within the first year; **the forgetting curves then level off**, with no significant further change out to ten years (paraphrase from abstract as surfaced in search results; quote-verify before article use).
- **Use**: the longest within-memory test–retest series found. The plateau is a second non-smooth time course to set beside Barry, Chadwick & Maguire (2018) already on the page — the "smooth decades-long trajectory" in the Map's consolidation prose is an assumption on this evidence too.

### The remember-to-know shift (semanticisation by memory-awareness report)

**Conway, M. A., Gardiner, J. M., Perfect, T. J., Anderson, S. J. & Cohen, G. M. (1997). "Changes in memory awareness during learning: The acquisition of knowledge by psychology undergraduates."** *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 126(4): 393–413. DOI `10.1037/0096-3445.126.4.393`.
- The founding study: course knowledge tracked with remember/know judgments across learning and retest; remembering gives way to knowing as knowledge schematises.
- ⚠️ **No abstract could be verified this session** (Europe PMC: no record; Semantic Scholar: abstract elided by publisher; Bristol repository page: metadata only, and the fetch rendered its pagination as "126, pages 1-21", conflicting with the widely cited 393–413 — resolve at APA PsycNet before citing page numbers). Findings above are described via the secondary literature (Herbert & Burt; Dewhurst et al.), not quoted.

**Dewhurst, S. A., Conway, M. A. & Brandt, K. R. (2009). "Tracking the R-to-K shift: Changes in memory awareness across repeated tests."** *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 23(6): 849–858. DOI `10.1002/acp.1517`.
- Word definitions learned, recognition tests at intervals spanning six months. Verbatim as carried in the Crossref record: "Remember responses dominated recognition on the first test but decreased on subsequent tests, whereas know response increased across successive tests." (The singular "know response" is the record's rendering; grep the Wiley page before quoting.)
- **Design lesson for the falsifier**: R/K is a binary report. A within-item graded fade and a within-item discrete flip generate the same R→K data; the aggregate shift curve grades because proportions grade. The paradigm therefore *demonstrates* semanticisation of memory awareness while being **constitutionally unable to adjudicate graded-vs-discrete within a memory** — which is the falsifier's actual question. Any falsifier-design section should say so; it is also a reply to anyone citing the R-to-K shift as if it settled the phenomenal limb.

**Herbert, D. M. B. & Burt, J. S. (2001). "Memory awareness and schematisation: learning in the university context."** *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 15(6): 617–637 (pages unverified). DOI `10.1002/acp.729`.
- Extends Conway et al. to further course contexts; know responses associate with greater schematisation of knowledge. A 2004 follow-up circulating as "What do students remember? Episodic memories become semantic" **could not have its venue verified this session** — do not cite the 2004 paper until its record is confirmed.

### Diary methods at the needed timescales

**Linton, M. (1982). "Transformations of memory in everyday life."** In U. Neisser (ed.), *Memory Observed: Remembering in Natural Contexts*. San Francisco: W. H. Freeman.
- Six-year self-study, two events recorded daily, systematically retested. The founding diary observation that repeated similar events lose episodic distinctness and fuse into semantic knowledge — semanticisation watched from inside, but narratively; no phenomenology instrument, N = 1, and the chapter is not quote-verifiable online. Cite as historical anchor only.

**Thomsen, D. K., Jensen, T., Holm, T., Olesen, M. H., Schnieber, A. & Tønnesvang, J. (2015). "A 3.5 year diary study: Remembering and life story importance are predicted by different event characteristics."** *Consciousness and Cognition* 36: 180–195. DOI `10.1016/j.concog.2015.06.011`.
- Full abstract obtained verbatim (Europe PMC catalogue copy): "Forty-five participants described and rated two events each week during their first term at university. After 3.5 years, we examined whether event characteristics rated in the diary predicted remembering, reliving, and life story importance at the follow-up. … Approximately 60% of events were remembered … Higher unusualness, rehearsal, and planning predicted whether an event was remembered 3.5 years later."
- **The August note's flagged near-miss, now resolved**: reliving is measured **once**, at follow-up, as an outcome predicted from diary-time event characteristics. Real events, real years, reliving rated — but no within-memory phenomenological trajectory. Close in materials, not in design.

**Frankenberg, C., Knebel, M., Degen, C., Siebert, J. S., Wahl, H.-W. & Schröder, J. (2022). "Autobiographical Memory in Healthy Aging: a Decade-long Longitudinal Study."** *Aging, Neuropsychology, and Cognition* 29(1): 158–179. DOI `10.1080/13825585.2020.1859082`.
- Same participants interviewed across a decade with an established semi-structured autobiographical interview. Verbatim (Europe PMC catalogue copy): "age-related semantization effects with a significantly lower number of specific and thereby a higher number of general AMs exclusively from young-old to old age", in healthy subjects "without cognitive decline".
- **The closest thing found to semanticisation observed longitudinally in the same people** — under "semantization" spelled with a z and aging-cohort vocabulary, which is presumably why August's searches missed it. But the measure is narrative category counts (specific vs general memories), not felt re-experiencing: the content-side transformation confirmed live, the phenomenal side again unmeasured.

**Bonnici, H. M. & Maguire, E. A. (2018). "Two years later — Revisiting autobiographical memory representations in vmPFC and hippocampus."** *Neuropsychologia* 110: 159–169 (volume/pages unverified; DOI `10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2017.05.014`).
- Within-subject longitudinal fMRI on the *same memories*: two-week-old and ten-year-old memories re-examined two years on. Catalogue-copy quotes: "no difference in the detectability of memory representations within vmPFC for the now 2 year old and 12 year old memories"; representation "stablised by, at most, two years later" (spelling as returned — grep the publisher before quoting).
- **Use**: proof that within-memory longitudinal designs are practicable — done neurally, twice (with Barry et al. 2018 already on the page). The phenomenological twin has simply never been run.

### Reinstatement probes (the access-reading evidence)

**Willander, J. & Larsson, M. (2006). "Smell your way back to childhood: Autobiographical odor memory."** *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 13(2): 240–244. DOI `10.3758/BF03193837`.
- Catalogue-copy phrases: "most odor-cued memories were located to the first decade of life"; "odor-evoked memories were associated with stronger feelings of being brought back in time" than word- or picture-cued memories, and "had been thought of less often".
- **The Proustian reinstatement datum in quantitative form**: the oldest, least-rehearsed, on-any-account-most-semanticised memories return with the *strongest* being-brought-back phenomenology when the right sensory route is used. On the transformation reading, decades-old memories should have the least re-experiencing available; on the access reading, this is what success looks like when a rarely-used retrieval route is finally exercised. Directly extends the page's third-reading section, which currently carries the Proust point only as anecdote.

**Berntsen, D. & Hall, N. M. (2004). "The episodic nature of involuntary autobiographical memories."** *Memory & Cognition* 32(5): 789–803. DOI `10.3758/BF03195869`.
- Full abstract obtained verbatim (Europe PMC catalogue copy). Key sentences: "The involuntary memories more frequently referred to specific episodes, came with more physical reaction, had more impact on mood, and dealt with more unusual and less positive events." And: "the way autobiographical memories are sampled greatly affects the findings and … involuntary retrieval more often provides access to memories of specific episodes and associated emotional states."
- **The sampling-method point is the methodological heart of the access reading**: phenomenology observed under voluntary word-cued recall — the retrieval route of nearly every study above — systematically understates the episodic character the same past shows under involuntary cueing. A graded fade curve measured by voluntary recall is compatible with intact re-experiencing reachable by other routes. Berntsen's structured involuntary-memory diary method is itself a published instrument the decisive study could incorporate.

**Marcotti, P. & St. Jacques, P. L. (2018). "Shifting visual perspective during memory retrieval reduces the accuracy of subsequent memories."** *Memory* 26(3): 330–341.
- Retrieving from an observer perspective reduces vividness, and the vividness reduction predicts accuracy loss at subsequent retrieval (paraphrase from search results; quote-verify before use). With St. Jacques, Szpunar & Schacter (2017, *NeuroImage*), establishes that **single retrieval acts durably alter later within-memory phenomenology** — the observer-effect confound for any repeated-measures falsifier design, and incidentally a within-memory *experimental* phenomenology-change literature under "visual perspective" vocabulary (the field-vs-observer shift with memory age, from Nigro & Neisser 1983 onward, is the oldest within-memory phenomenal-change finding of all, though classically cross-sectional).

**Targeted memory reactivation** (control search): TMR studies overwhelmingly track performance, not phenomenology. The exception found: "Targeted memory reactivation during sleep improves emotional memory modulation following imagery rescripting", *Translational Psychiatry* (2024), DOI `10.1038/s41398-024-03192-4` (metadata from URL; unverified) — pre-registered, N = 80, cueing rescripted aversive autobiographical memories during NREM sleep left them less vivid with less distress and arousal (paraphrase). A sleep-reinstatement manipulation that *moves* within-memory phenomenology — the manipulable-probe half of the decisive design, already running in clinical vocabulary.

### Cross-sectional grading with memory age (context)

**Piolino, P., Desgranges, B. & Eustache, F. (2009). "Episodic autobiographical memories over the course of time: Cognitive, neuropsychological and neuroimaging findings."** *Neuropsychologia* 47(11): 2314–2329. DOI `10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2009.01.020`.
- Verbatim (Europe PMC catalogue copy): mental time travel "is the last feature of autobiographical memory to become fully operational in development and the first feature to go in aging and most amnesias." The TEMPau task assesses remember/know with justification across lifetime periods; autonoetic ratings run higher for recent than remote periods.
- ⚠️ A frequently circulating stronger sentence — a "shift away from autonoetic consciousness and towards noetic" with time and repetition — was **not found in the abstract** and is unverified pending full text; do not quote it.
- **Use**: the cross-sectional grading everyone cites. Cross-sectional across memories of different ages, so it cannot separate within-memory fading from cohort/selection effects — the standard design the decisive study exists to improve on. Janssen, Rubin & St. Jacques (2011, *Memory & Cognition*, DOI `10.3758/s13421-010-0003-x`) is the lifespan-distribution version for reliving and vividness ratings specifically.

## What the Decisive Study Now Requires (assembled from published parts)

Every component below is published; the conjunction is not:

1. **Sampling at encoding**: weekly diary with event-characteristic ratings (Thomsen et al. 2015) or Berntsen-style involuntary/voluntary dual sampling (Berntsen & Hall 2004).
2. **Phenomenology instrument**: graded re-experiencing / mental-time-travel ratings, not bare R/K — Zaman et al. (2024, already on the page) supplies the validated factor structure; the AMQ (Talarico & Rubin) and the ART (Berntsen et al. 2019, *Behavior Research Methods* — surfaced, unexamined) are the standing questionnaires. R/K alone is categorical by construction and cannot answer the graded-vs-discrete question (Dewhurst lesson).
3. **Retest schedule**: between-groups single-retest per delay (Talarico & Rubin 2003) out to multi-year delays (Thomsen; Frankenberg), so retrieval history does not manufacture the curve (Marcotti & St. Jacques confound).
4. **Reinstatement probes crossed with standard recall**: odour/music/context cues (Willander & Larsson), reminder reinstatement (Sekeres et al. 2016), involuntary-capture diaries (Berntsen), so a fading report can be classified as transformation (fails to return under every route) or access loss (returns under the right probe). This is the discriminator the page's third-reading section already calls for.
5. **Interview depth where ratings saturate**: micro-phenomenological interview at selected timepoints, with its reliability dispute acknowledged (Høffding et al.; Heimann et al., already on the page).

The adverse outcome for the Map is now specifiable to a design: felt re-experiencing declining by degrees within memories **across probe routes** — graded under voluntary recall *and* under the best reinstatement probes, with the decline surviving access controls. The favourable outcomes are two: discrete drop-offs, or graded voluntary-recall reports with full-strength returns under reinstatement (the access reading confirmed).

## Tenet Alignment Notes

- The dissociation limb (Talarico & Rubin: reliving held while accuracy decayed; Willander & Larsson: oldest memories, strongest return) supports the Dualism-motivated claim that the manner of remembering is not a readout of retained content. Honest counter-reading: a functionalist can model reported reliving as a metacognitive confidence signal detached from content fidelity — the flashbulb dissociation is equally at home there (Talarico & Rubin themselves gloss it as *perceived* accuracy). The dissociation constrains, it does not decide.
- The everyday-memory fade (graded decline over 224 days) is the adverse direction at the report level and should be reported as such on the page, with the access confound stated, not hidden.
- Occam's-limits: the R-to-K shift's categorical report format shows how an instrument can *impose* the answer either way — a binary instrument manufactures discreteness, a Likert instrument manufactures grading. The falsifier's design section should say this against both sides.
- No bearing found on the quantum-interaction tenets or Many-Worlds; none claimed.

## Gaps in Research

- **The conjunction study remains unfound** — longitudinal within-memory phenomenological tracking *with* reinstatement-probe access controls.
