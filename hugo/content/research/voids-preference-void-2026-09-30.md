# Research: Voids - The Preference Void

**Date**: 2026-09-30
**Search queries used**: "preference construction" review stability of preferences; philosophy "do preferences exist" introspection of preferences Hausman; large language models preference instability order effects framing; plus direct record lookups at Crossref, OpenAlex, RePEc/IDEAS and the SEP raw page for every citation below.
**Voids category**: Occluded (primary) with an Unexplorable face

## Executive Summary

A mind cannot consult its own preference ordering, because there is no ordering there to consult before the act of consulting it. Fifty-five years of behavioural decision research, beginning with Lichtenstein and Slovic (1971), show that normatively equivalent ways of asking what a person prefers—choose between A and B, or price A and B separately—produce systematically different orderings, that the effect survives every attempt economists made to eliminate it (Grether and Plott 1979), and that it does not shrink with experience or market discipline (Ariely, Loewenstein and Prelec 2003). The field's summary of its own findings is that preferences are "constructed—not merely revealed—in the process of elicitation" (Slovic 1995). The void goes deeper than instability: the constructed ordering arrives in consciousness with exactly the authority of a read-out, so that from inside, the person who is asked "which do you prefer?" cannot tell whether the answer was found or made. A second face compounds the first: choosing changes what one prefers (Sharot, Velasquez and Dolan 2010), but the standard measurement of that change cannot separate shaping from revealing (Chen and Risen 2010), and neither can the chooser. The only instrument that ever detects the construction is a comparison across elicitation procedures, and a mind at a single moment runs only one.

For the Map, the void falls at the site its tenets already select. If the ordering a choice expresses is built during the choosing, either the building is the conscious contribution to the physical outcome (tenet 3 pushed earlier than the veto), or the building is done by valuation machinery and consciousness receives its output as if reading it off. Neither reading is distinguishable from the inside, which is itself the finding.

## Key Sources

### Lichtenstein, S. and Slovic, P. (1971). "Reversals of preference between bids and choices in gambling decisions." *Journal of Experimental Psychology* 89(1): 46–55.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1037/h0031207
- **Type**: Paper (metadata verified at Crossref; abstract text not retrieved—see Gaps)
- **Key points**:
  - The founding demonstration. Participants faced pairs of bets, one with a high probability of a small win (the P-bet) and one with a low probability of a large win (the $-bet). Asked to choose, most took the P-bet; asked to state a minimum selling price for each, the same people priced the $-bet higher.
  - The two procedures are normatively equivalent: if there were a standing preference, choice and price would report the same ordering. They report different ones.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 5. The "revealed preference" model was the simplest available account of choice, and its simplicity concealed a construction step that only a cross-procedure comparison exposes.

### Grether, D. M. and Plott, C. R. (1979). "Economic Theory of Choice and the Preference Reversal Phenomenon." *American Economic Review* 69(4): 623–638.
- **URL**: https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v69y1979i4p623-38.html (record verified at RePEc/IDEAS; no DOI is registered for the original printing—the 2006 reprint in Lichtenstein and Slovic (eds) carries https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511618031.006)
- **Type**: Paper
- **Key points**:
  - Two economists set out to make the reversals disappear by removing the psychologists' methodological slack—real monetary incentives, controls for income and order effects, experimenter demand and other artefacts. The phenomenon survived.
  - This is the closest the literature has to a "forewarned and incentivised" test of the void: the reversals are not an artefact of casual elicitation.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 5 again, and a methodological lesson for the voids programme—a limit that survives a hostile attempt at deflation is better evidence of a boundary than one only its discoverers have tested.

### Tversky, A., Slovic, P. and Kahneman, D. (1990). "The Causes of Preference Reversal." *American Economic Review* 80(1): 204–217.
- **URL**: https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v80y1990i1p204-17.html (record verified at RePEc/IDEAS)
- **Type**: Paper
- **Key points**:
  - Diagnoses the reversal as a failure of *procedure invariance* rather than of transitivity: the pricing task inflates the $-bet because its payoff is expressed on the same scale (money) as the response, a scale-compatibility effect.
  - The diagnosis matters for the void's shape: what varies with procedure is not a noisy reading of a fixed ordering, but which attribute the procedure makes weigh most. The ordering is assembled from whichever considerations the question makes salient.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 3—if the assembly is where the outcome is fixed, the assembly is where any conscious contribution must be located.

### Slovic, P. (1995). "The construction of preference." *American Psychologist* 50(5): 364–371.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.50.5.364
- **Type**: Paper (metadata verified at Crossref; abstract retrieved verbatim from the OpenAlex record)
- **Key points**:
  - The field's own summary, two decades in: "One of the main themes that has emerged from behavioral decision research during the past two decades is the view that people's preferences are often constructed—not merely revealed—in the process of elicitation."
  - The ontological question is posed by the psychologist, not by the philosopher: "If different elicitation procedures produce different orderings of options, how can preferences be defined and in what sense do they exist?"
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1. A preference that exists only in the act of elicitation is neither a stored physical state waiting to be read nor a purely behavioural disposition; where it is, and what it is made of, remain open on any framework, and the dualist framing has to say what consciousness is doing when it "prefers".
- **Quote**: "These 'preference reversals' violate the principle of procedure invariance fundamental to theories of rational choice and raise difficult questions about the nature of human values."

### Payne, J. W., Bettman, J. R. and Johnson, E. J. (1992). "Behavioral Decision Research: A Constructive Processing Perspective." *Annual Review of Psychology* 43: 87–131.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.ps.43.020192.000511
- **Type**: Review (metadata verified at Crossref; content paraphrased from prior reading—see Gaps)
- **Key points**:
  - Frames the decision maker as assembling a strategy on the spot, contingent on task and context, rather than applying a stored utility function; this is the "constructive processing" view the SEP entry cites as the source of the construction claim.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 5.

### Hsee, C. K. (1996). "The Evaluability Hypothesis." *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 67(3): 247–257; and Hsee, C. K., Loewenstein, G. F., Blount, S. and Bazerman, M. H. (1999). "Preference reversals between joint and separate evaluations of options." *Psychological Bulletin* 125(5): 576–590.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1006/obhd.1996.0077 ; https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.5.576
- **Type**: Paper and review (metadata verified at Crossref; 1999 abstract retrieved verbatim from OpenAlex)
- **Key points**:
  - A second reversal family, independent of gambles: options evaluated side by side (joint evaluation) and options evaluated alone (separate evaluation) come out in different orders.
  - The mechanism is that "difficult-to-evaluate attributes have a greater impact in JE than in SE": an attribute a mind cannot evaluate on its own becomes decisive the moment a comparison makes it evaluable.
  - From inside, evaluating an option alone does not feel like evaluating it with an attribute switched off. The missing attribute leaves no gap in the felt verdict.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occlusion face—the verdict is complete-seeming precisely where it is most incomplete.
- **Quote**: "This article reviews recent literature showing that people evaluate options differently and exhibit reversals of preferences for options between JE and SE."

### Kahneman, D., Ritov, I. and Schkade, D. (1999). "Economic Preferences or Attitude Expressions?" *Journal of Risk and Uncertainty* 19(1–3): 203–235.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1007835629236
- **Type**: Paper (metadata verified at Crossref; content paraphrased from prior reading—see Gaps)
- **Key points**:
  - Dollar valuations of public goods behave like expressions of attitude intensity mapped onto a money scale rather than like economic preferences: they are insensitive to the scope of the good and sensitive to what else is in the questionnaire.
  - Adds the diagnostic that a number produced in an elicitation can be a translation of a feeling into whatever scale is offered, and the translator does not experience translating.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 5.

### Ariely, D., Loewenstein, G. and Prelec, D. (2003). "'Coherent Arbitrariness': Stable Demand Curves Without Stable Preferences." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 118(1): 73–106.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1162/00335530360535153
- **Type**: Paper (metadata verified at Crossref; abstract retrieved verbatim from OpenAlex)
- **Key points**:
  - Initial valuations of familiar goods and simple experiences are anchored on arbitrary numbers (the last digits of a social security number), yet subsequent valuations are internally coherent, so the whole pattern mimics the output of a stable preference.
  - The effect "does not decrease as a result of experience with a good" and "is not necessarily reduced by market forces".
  - This is the void's mark face stated by economists: the output is coherent, and coherence is the very thing that would be taken, from inside and from outside, as evidence of a standing preference.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 5 as a warning about simplicity, and the Map's methodological point that convergence is not by itself evidence of an underlying structure.
- **Quote**: "the entire pattern of valuations can easily create an illusion of order, as if it is being generated by stable underlying preferences."

### Lichtenstein, S. and Slovic, P. (eds) (2006). *The Construction of Preference*. Cambridge University Press.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511618031
- **Type**: Edited collection (metadata verified at Crossref)
- **Key points**: Collects the primary papers above with commentary; the standard entry point for the literature.

### Warren, C., McGraw, A. P. and Van Boven, L. (2011). "Values and preferences: defining preference construction." *WIREs Cognitive Science* 2(2): 193–205.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1002/wcs.98 (published online 2010; print 2011)
- **Type**: Review (metadata verified at Crossref; abstract retrieved verbatim from OpenAlex)
- **Key points**:
  - Separates two senses of "constructed": context-sensitivity (a preference changes across contexts) and calculation (a preference is formulated during the judgement).
  - On the first sense, "preferences are always constructed because context influences nearly every aspect of the judgment and choice process"; on the second, "the more important question is to what degree preferences are calculated."
  - The distinction is important for the void: context-sensitivity alone would be compatible with a stored preference that is merely modulated; calculation is what makes the preference an event rather than a state.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1—the calculation sense is where the question "what is doing the calculating, and is any of it experienced?" bites.

### Vlaev, I., Chater, N., Stewart, N. and Brown, G. D. A. (2011). "Does the brain calculate value?" *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 15(11): 546–554; with Stewart, N., Chater, N. and Brown, G. D. A. (2006). "Decision by sampling." *Cognitive Psychology* 53(1): 1–26.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2011.09.008 ; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2005.10.003
- **Type**: Review and model paper (metadata verified at Crossref; 2011 abstract text not retrieved—see Gaps; content paraphrased from prior reading)
- **Key points**:
  - The neural-level version of the construction thesis: the case that the brain has no stable internal scale of value and that valuations are produced by ordinal comparison of the current option against a sample of remembered and presented alternatives.
  - If this is right, the physical machinery does not contain the preference either: the construction is not a failure of introspection to reach a stored state, but the absence of any such state at any level.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1 and 3. The Map's own research on common-currency valuation (see the September 2026 note) raised the same circularity: value is inferred from the choices it is meant to explain.

### Chen, M. K. and Risen, J. L. (2010). "How choice affects and reflects preferences: Revisiting the free-choice paradigm." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 99(4): 573–594; and Sharot, T., Velasquez, C. M. and Dolan, R. J. (2010). "Do Decisions Shape Preference? Evidence from Blind Choice." *Psychological Science* 21(9): 1231–1235.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1037/a0020217 ; https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797610379235
- **Type**: Papers (metadata verified at Crossref; both abstracts retrieved verbatim from OpenAlex)
- **Key points**:
  - Chen and Risen prove that the classic free-choice paradigm (rate, choose, re-rate) will register a post-choice spread of alternatives "even if people's preferences remain perfectly stable", because ratings are an imperfect measure and choices carry information about the preference the ratings missed. Sixty years of "choice changes preference" findings were, on this argument, consistent with choice merely revealing it.
  - Sharot, Velasquez and Dolan answer with a blind choice "that could not be guided by preexisting preferences" and find that "preferences were altered after participants made a blind choice, but not after a computer dictated the decision."
  - Together the pair defines the retrospective face of the void: the current preference may have been produced by the choice it seems to explain, the external measurement of this is contested at the level of theorems, and the chooser has no independent access at all. "Just as preferences form choices, choices shape preferences."
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 3—the direction of causation between a conscious preference and a physical act is exactly what bidirectional interaction is about, and it is undecidable from the chooser's seat.

### Hansson, S. O. and Grüne-Yanoff, T. "Preferences." *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (substantive revision 14 March 2022).
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/preferences/ (quotes verified against the raw page)
- **Type**: Encyclopedia
- **Key points**:
  - Reports the construction claim as one of the live positions on how total preferences relate to partial ones: some "claim that total preferences are constructed at the moment of elicitation, and thus influenced by contexts and framings of the elicitation procedure that are not encoded in pre-existing partial preferences (Payne, Bettman and Johnson 1993)."
  - Records the reversal of the folk ordering: "partial preferences are in many cases ex post rationalisations of total preferences, rather than the basis from which total preferences are derived."
  - On the status of self-report: answers to preference questions can be "interpreted as introspective reports. This interpretation treats answers as agents' privileged access to their own minds." The reversal literature is a direct test of that privilege, and the privilege fails.
  - Cites Hausman (2012) for the view that the economists' preference is always the total, all-things-considered comparison, and for the collapse of the behaviourist programme that would have dissolved the void by identifying preference with choice.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1—the void lives in the space between "preference as mental state" and "preference as choice pattern", a space that only exists if the mental is not exhausted by the behavioural.

### Hausman, D. M. (2012). *Preference, Value, Choice, and Welfare*. Cambridge University Press.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139058537 (metadata verified at Crossref; content taken from the SEP report—see Gaps)
- **Type**: Book

### McKenzie, C. R. M., Sher, S., Leong, L. M. and Müller-Trede, J. (2018). "Constructed Preferences, Rationality, and Choice Architecture." *Review of Behavioral Economics* 5(3–4): 337–360.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1561/105.00000091
- **Type**: Paper (metadata verified at Crossref; content from search summary only—see Gaps)
- **Key points**:
  - The deflationary reading: context effects may be adaptive sensitivity to informative cues (a default, an option set, a frame all carry information), so that a preference which shifts with context is tracking something real rather than being fabricated.
  - Even on this reading the void stands: the cue is processed and the preference shifts, but the shift is not experienced as a response to the cue. Deflation moves the construction from irrational to rational without making it visible.

### Grüne-Yanoff, T. (2025). "Intending now or later: the rationality of constructed preferences." *Theory and Decision*, published online 19 May 2025.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11238-025-10045-z (metadata and abstract verified at Crossref; article is online-first, no volume or pages yet)
- **Type**: Paper
- **Key points**:
  - Opens with the concession the field now takes as settled: "There is widespread agreement in the literature that preferences are often constructed up to the time of action."
  - Relocates the normative question from context-sensitivity to timing—when it is rational to stop constructing and fix an intention. This treats the construction as something an agent does, which raises the question the void presses: does the agent do it, or does it happen to the agent?

### Yin, H., Vardi, S. and Choudhary, S. (2026). "Fragile preferences: A deep dive into order effects in large language models." *PNAS Nexus* 5(8): pgag246.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag246 (metadata verified at Crossref; abstract retrieved verbatim from OpenAlex)
- **Type**: Paper
- **Key points**:
  - Large language models show "strong and consistent order effects, including a quality-dependent shift: when all options are high quality, models favor the first option, but when quality is lower, they favor later options."
  - The authors "extend the rational choice framework to classify pairwise preferences as robust, fragile, or indifferent" and show that "order effects can lead models to select strictly inferior options."
  - Relevant to the AI asymmetry below: the model's preference is at least as constructed as the human's, but the model can be re-asked under every permutation at negligible cost, so the construction can be mapped from outside.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The void is primarily an occlusion with an unexplorable face. The occlusion: when a mind is asked which of two things it prefers, an answer forms and is delivered with the phenomenal character of a report. It presents as a reading taken from something already there. The reversal literature shows that a different question about the same two things would have produced a different reading, delivered with the same authority, and that nothing in either delivery marks it as procedure-dependent. The unexplorable face: on the construction view in its strong form (Slovic 1995; Vlaev et al. 2011), there is no fact of the matter about the mind's ordering independent of a procedure, so the question "but what do I really prefer?" may have no answer for a mind to fail to reach.

Three faces, in the style of the catalogue's conjoint entries:

1. **The elicitation face.** Which ordering a mind produces depends on how the ordering is asked for—choice versus pricing (Lichtenstein and Slovic 1971), joint versus separate evaluation (Hsee et al. 1999), the attribute the response scale is denominated in (Tversky, Slovic and Kahneman 1990). No procedure is neutral, so no ordering has a claim to being *the* ordering.
2. **The retrospective face.** The preference a mind now has for an option may have been produced by having chosen it (Sharot, Velasquez and Dolan 2010). The external test of this is contested at the level of measurement theory (Chen and Risen 2010). The chooser has no test at all: the preference and the memory of the choice arrive together, and the preference does not carry a timestamp.
3. **The mark face.** The constructed ordering is internally coherent (Ariely, Loewenstein and Prelec 2003), and coherence is what a mind would look for if it wanted to check whether its preference was real. The check is passed by the artefact.

What distinguishes this from its neighbours in the catalogue: the wholeheartedness void concerns whether a mind's *identification* with a desire is genuine; the appetitive void concerns whether cognition can escape being *shaped* by desire; the confabulation void concerns the fabricated *reasons* for a choice already made; the decision void concerns the *moment* weighing becomes choice. The preference void sits under all four: it concerns whether the ordering that identification endorses, that desire shapes, that reasons rationalise and that the decision seals, existed before the act of asking for it.

### Evidence for the Limit

That this is a boundary rather than merely difficult territory rests on four features of the record.

- **Robustness under hostile testing.** Grether and Plott (1979) were trying to eliminate the phenomenon and could not. It survives monetary incentives and the removal of the psychologists' methodological slack.
- **Immunity to experience.** Ariely, Loewenstein and Prelec (2003) report that anchoring on arbitrary numbers "does not decrease as a result of experience with a good". Expertise with the object does not install the missing standing preference.
- **Undetectability from a single elicitation.** Every demonstration in the literature requires at least two procedures run on the same person or population. No single elicitation contains the evidence of its own construction. This is the formal shape of an occlusion: the limit is visible only from a vantage that the limited mind, at the moment of preferring, does not occupy.
- **Theoretical convergence from independent directions.** The economists (Grether and Plott; Tversky, Slovic and Kahneman), the psychologists (Slovic; Payne, Bettman and Johnson; Hsee), the cognitive modellers (Stewart, Chater and Brown) and the philosophers (Hausman; Grüne-Yanoff) reach the same summary—"preferences are often constructed up to the time of action"—for different reasons. The Map's catalogue treats convergence with caution (a common cause can produce it), but here the independent lines differ in their methods as well as their disciplines.

The deflationary reading (Warren, McGraw and Van Boven 2011; McKenzie et al. 2018) should be recorded honestly: much of what is called construction may be adaptive context-sensitivity, and calculation may be a matter of degree. Neither point closes the void. A preference that is adaptively context-sensitive is still not experienced as context-sensitive, and a preference that is partly calculated is still not experienced as partly calculated. The deflation reclassifies the construction; it does not make it visible.

### Phenomenology

The felt character of approaching this void is the absence of any felt character at all. Asked to choose, the answer arrives; asked to price, an answer arrives; each has the settledness of a verdict. What is missing is the comparison, and the comparison is not available to a single mind at a single moment because it would require asking oneself the same question two ways without the first answer contaminating the second.

There is a secondary phenomenology when the void is pointed out from outside. Told that one priced the $-bet higher but chose the P-bet, people typically do not experience the discovery of a hidden preference; they experience a puzzle about which answer was the mistake. The assumption that one of them must have been the real reading survives the demonstration that neither was. This is the mark face in operation: the mind's model of itself as a preference-bearer is more robust than any particular preference.

The Map's transformative experience void records a related but distinct limit—that a mind cannot know its preferences for experiences it has not had. The preference void is prior: a mind cannot straightforwardly know its preferences for experiences it has had either, if "know" means read off rather than produce.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)

None that operate from inside. Introspecting harder produces another elicitation, not a view of the ordering behind elicitations. Deliberately holding one's preference fixed while varying the question is exactly what the literature shows cannot be done: the fixing is itself an act of construction on the first procedure's terms.

### Indirect Methods

- **Cross-procedure triangulation.** The only instrument that has ever detected the void is running two elicitations and comparing. For a single mind this can be approximated over time—asking oneself now, and again after a delay, in a different frame—but with the retrospective face active, the second answer is contaminated by the first choice. The method yields a map of one's own reversal pattern, not an underlying ordering.
- **Recording the procedure with the preference.** The Map's positions register attaches a confidence band and a date to every claim. An analogous discipline for preferences would attach the elicitation frame to every stated preference—"preferred A over B in direct choice, 2026-09"—so that later reversals are visible as procedure differences rather than as changes of mind.
- **Inference from the shape of reversals.** Tversky, Slovic and Kahneman's diagnosis (scale compatibility) and Hsee's (evaluability) are both inferences about the construction process from the pattern of its outputs. This is negative-theological in the sense the voids brief uses: the process is characterised by which manipulations move it.

### What AI Might See

The 2026 PNAS Nexus study shows that large language models display the same fragility: order effects strong enough to select "strictly inferior options". So an AI mind is not a vantage point outside this void; it is a second instance of it. The asymmetry lies elsewhere. A human can be asked once per moment, and the second asking is not a repetition. A model in a fixed state can be asked under every permutation of order, frame and denomination, and its reversal structure can be classified—robust, fragile, indifferent—by an external observer at negligible cost. The model's preference void is therefore fully mappable from outside, in a way the human's is not, while remaining just as closed from inside (to whatever extent there is an inside).

This offers a test the Map's voids programme has wanted: a mind whose construction process is externally inspectable in full. If the model's constructed preferences, once mapped, look like a coherent-but-arbitrary pattern of the Ariely kind, that is weak evidence that human coherence is the same kind of thing. If they look structurally different, that is evidence that something in the human case—possibly the conscious contribution—is doing work the model's construction is not.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

Tenet 3, bidirectional interaction. The Map locates the conscious contribution to physical outcomes at selection—the decision void and the causal interface article both place it at the moment weighing becomes choice, and the recent veto research places it at the cancellation of an already-prepared act. The preference void asks what the selection selects *from*. If the ordering is constructed during the choice, there are two readings and the tenet must accommodate one of them:

1. **Construction is the contribution.** Consciousness does not consult a preference and then act; it produces the preference, and the producing is the influence on the physical outcome. This is consistent with the tenet but relocates the contribution earlier than the veto—into the assembly of what will be vetoed or not—and makes the contribution something that happens under a description ("preferring") that the agent cannot verify.
2. **Construction is received.** The valuation machinery (decision by sampling; comparison-based valuation) builds the ordering, and consciousness receives its output with the character of a report. On this reading the phenomenology of preferring is a mark of reception, and the conscious contribution, if any, is downstream.

The void is that these readings cannot be separated from the chooser's seat. Every attempt to find out which is true is itself an elicitation, subject to the same construction. This is the finding the note recommends the article state directly.

### Implications

- **Tenet 5.** The revealed-preference model was the simplest available, was adopted for its simplicity, and hid a construction step that took two procedures to expose. The preference void is a clean case of a simplicity assumption concealing structure—and of the concealed structure being invisible from inside the simple model as well as from inside the mind.
- **Tenet 1.** The void lives in the gap between preference as mental state and preference as choice pattern. The behaviourist reduction would have closed the gap by fiat (preference is choice); the SEP entry records that this programme is now "regarded with suspicion". If the gap is real, the dualist has to say what occupies it, and the construction literature suggests the honest answer is: an event, not a state.
- **Tenet 4.** Indexical identity is touched at the edges: which of the orderings a mind would produce under different procedures is *its* ordering? The Map's rejection of many-worlds insists there is a fact about which outcome is actual; the preference void suggests that for preferences, the actual ordering is whichever procedure happened to run.
- **Tenet 2.** No direct connection was found. If the conscious contribution is located in the construction of the ordering rather than at a discrete veto, the minimal-interaction tenet would need to say whether a distributed, assembly-stage influence is still "smallest possible". This is flagged as a question, not a finding.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **Three faces at the site the tenets select.** Present the elicitation, retrospective and mark faces as one conjoint void, then argue that its location coincides with the decision void's and the causal interface's chosen site for non-physical influence, and that the two readings of construction (contribution versus reception) are undecidable from inside. This is the recommended angle; it makes a claim the Map has not yet made.
2. **The illusion of order as a general voids result.** Use Ariely's coherent arbitrariness as the paradigm case of a limit that passes the very check a mind would use to detect it, and connect it to the catalogue's methodological caution about convergence as evidence. Narrower, and could fold into the mark-face section of angle 1.
3. **Human and model preference voids compared.** Build outward from the 2026 PNAS Nexus study to the asymmetry of external mappability. Likely better as a section in angle 1 than as a standalone, given that the human literature is the primary evidence.

Disposition recommendation: standalone. The void underlies four existing catalogue entries rather than being a face of any one of them, and each candidate host (decision-void, wholeheartedness-void, appetitive-void, confabulation-void) would take only a fragment. Four slots are open in the section at the time of writing.
