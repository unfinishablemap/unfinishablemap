## Assessment: article-sized, but the reply it was commissioned to house only half works

The task carried a licence to decline. Verdict: **a `concepts/` article is warranted** (slug `epistemic-akrasia`; concepts measured at 347 of a 360 cap by `tools.evolution.state.count_section_files`, so 13 slots). Three findings make it more than a fold:

1. **The charge as worded misfires, and a nearby charge lands.** Epistemic akrasia in the literature is high confidence in *p* together with the judgement that one's evidence does not support that confidence. The Map's register never gives a selector-bearing entry high credence, and the divided-will's finding that the fragmentation data give the selector no support is a statement about one dataset. What does land is narrower: a *moderate* credence sitting on an external-evidence grade of D, with nothing saying where the moderate comes from, and a credence that did not move when [the-divided-will](/topics/the-divided-will/) reported evidence running modestly against one reading of the selector.
2. **The acceptance-not-belief reply fits the tenets and fails for the register.** The tenets are "chosen starting points" used as premises, which is what Cohen and Bratman mean by acceptance. The register defines its credence axis as "the Map's subjective probability the position is true *as a claim*". That is degree of belief by definition. The selector sits on the join: accepted as Tenet 3, believed at *moderate* as [P-A1](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a1) and [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2).
3. **Acceptance relocates the question; it does not close it.** An accepted premise still needs a reason for accepting it, which is the licensing question today's Kant note answers mostly in the negative.

**Division of labour with the planned Kant article.** That article asks what could *license* positing (Kant's conditions, Wizenmann). This one asks what *attitude* the Map takes to a posit and which coherence norms that attitude answers to. They should cross-link and not duplicate: this article cites the Wizenmann result in a sentence and does not re-argue it.

**The fold considered and set aside.** A register entry in [methodology-and-calibration](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/) ("tenets are accepted; positions are believed") is worth having whether or not the article is written, but a register entry cannot carry the literature or the test below. It is listed under Gaps as a lead for `/positions-evolve`.

## Executive Summary

Epistemic akrasia is believing *p* while judging that one's evidence does not support *p*. The contemporary debate grew out of higher-order evidence cases, and the SEP entry reports it as unsettled: level-splitting views allow rational akrasia, Horowitz (2014) argues that this licenses bootstrapping, and several level-bridging principles "allow some instances of epistemic akrasia". The pro-akrasia literature gives the Map no shelter, because it defends agents whose first-order evidence really does support *p* and whose higher-order evidence misleads; the Map's higher-order judgement about its selector is its own considered verdict. The belief/acceptance distinction (Cohen 1989, 1992; Bratman 1992; van Fraassen 1980) offers a different reply: acceptance is a voluntary policy of using a proposition as a premise, and it need not involve believing it. Tested against the Map's own texts, that reply holds for the tenets and the three background posits and fails for the positions register, whose credence axis is a belief report. A hybrid is available and has costs the Map has not yet stated.

## Key Sources

### SEP, "Higher-Order Evidence" (first published 2022; substantive revision 16 September 2026)

- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/higher-order-evidence/ (raw HTML retrieved and grepped; author Sophie Horowitz, per the entry's copyright line)
- **Type**: Encyclopedia. No SEP entry titled "Epistemic Akrasia" was found; this entry is the standard treatment.
- **Key points and quotes**:
  - *What the state is.* In the entry's Hypoxia case, an agent who keeps high confidence in G while taking seriously that she may be impaired "will be in a state of epistemic akrasia: she will have a belief state (her high confidence in G) which she also judges likely to be irrational, or unsupported by her evidence."
  - *Level-splitting.* The view that first-order and higher-order evidence each govern their own level; the entry lists as defenders "Lasonen (2014), Coates (2012), Weatherson (ms–see Other Internet Resources), Williamson (2014), and Wedgwood (2012)". ("Lasonen" is the entry's form of Lasonen-Aarnio.) "An obvious upshot of this view is that epistemic akrasia can be rational".
  - *The cost.* "But admitting rational epistemic akrasia in this case incurs a large intuitive cost." The entry reports Horowitz (2014) as arguing that if akrasia is rational here "then so is bootstrapping": the agent "can use her first-order beliefs to establish a fantastic track record of success, ultimately dismissing the possibility that she was ever rationally impaired."
  - *Exceptions even on Horowitz's view.* "Horowitz argues that epistemic akrasia is rationally permissible when we expect the evidence to be “falsity-guiding”". Several level-bridging principles "take higher-order evidence into account, but also allow some instances of epistemic akrasia."
  - *Steadfast views.* Titelbaum (2015) is quoted: "mistakes about rationality are mistakes of rationality"; the entry says his argument "rests on the assumption that epistemic akrasia is irrational."
  - *State of play.* One of two questions the entry calls central going forward: "are there genuine norms prohibiting epistemic akrasia?"
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Bears on the Map's method, not its metaphysics.

### Cohen, *An Essay on Belief and Acceptance* (1992) and "Belief and Acceptance" (1989)

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198236047.001.0001 (Oxford Scholarship record; Clarendon Press 1992); article in *Mind* 98(391), 367–389, doi 10.1093/mind/xcviii.391.367.
- **Status**: book **not read**; the definition is quoted *as reproduced in* SEP "Faith", which marks an ellipsis and its own added emphasis. The 1989 abstract was read via OpenAlex.
- **Key points**:
  - Definition (Cohen 1992, p. 4, per SEP "Faith"): to accept that p is "to have or adopt a policy of deeming, positing, or postulating that p—i.e. of including that proposition … among one’s premisses for deciding what to do or think in a particular context, whether or not one feels it to be true that p".
  - 1989 abstract: the conflation of the two attitudes has produced "a widespread tendency to ignore the fact that there are many important differences between belief and acceptance".
- **Relevance**: Cohen's own gloss uses the word *positing*. On his account a posit is a paradigm object of acceptance.

### Bratman, "Practical Reasoning and Acceptance in a Context" (1992)

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/101.401.1. *Mind* 101(401), 1–16 per Crossref (SEP "Ethics of Belief" and one bibliography page give 1–15). Reprinted as ch. 2 of *Faces of Intention* (Cambridge 1999).
- **Status**: **abstract only**, as reproduced on a bibliographic page (stafforini.com); article not read.
- **Key points**: "the cognitive attitudes guiding practical reasoning and action go beyond our beliefs and include context-relative acceptance." Practical pressures can make it reasonable to accept a proposition in one context that the agent "reasonably would not (or, indeed, does not) accept" in another. "Such reasonable acceptance need not include all of what one believes; nor need it be limited to what one believes; nor can it be identified with mere supposition or mere pretence."

## Major Positions

- **Anti-akrasia (enkratic) norms.** Proponents per SEP: Horowitz (with exceptions), Titelbaum, Smithies, Feldman 2005. Core claim: first-order confidence and one's judgement of what the evidence supports must line up. For the Map: this is the norm a critic would invoke.
- **Level-splitting.** Proponents per SEP: Lasonen-Aarnio, Coates, Weatherson, Williamson, Wedgwood. Core claim: akrasia can be rational when higher-order evidence misleads. **No help to the Map**: these cases stipulate that the first-order evidence supports *p*. The Map says the opposite of its selector.
- **Two-norm and dilemma views.** Christensen (dilemmas); Lasonen-Aarnio 2020 (success versus good dispositions). For the Map: the dispositional reading is the form of the charge that survives.
- **Belief/acceptance pluralism.** Cohen, Bratman, van Fraassen; per SEP also Stalnaker, Alston, Audi. Core claim: a voluntary, premise-adopting attitude exists beside belief and answers to partly practical norms. For the Map: the candidate reply, tested below.

## Key Debates

- **Can epistemic akrasia be rational?** Open, per the SEP's closing section.
- **Can acceptance do the work asked of it?** SEP "Ethics of Belief" records "some dispute about whether acceptance is able to play the various roles that its advocates intend (Radford 1990, Maher 1990, Moore 1994)". None of those was read.

## The Test (the Map's own reconstruction)

**Step 1. Does the Map meet the definition of the charge?** Three components.

| Component of akrasia | What the Map's texts show | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| High confidence in *p* | [P-A1](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a1) (agent-causal free will): credence *moderate*, grade C. [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2) (authorship needs agent causation): *moderate*, grade D. [P-A6](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a6): *moderate*, D. [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) (closed individualism): *moderate*, D. The register's three *high*-credence grade-D entries ([P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2), [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3), [P-SC1](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc1)) are structural or conditional claims about the framework itself | Not met as stated |
| A judgement that one's *total* evidence does not support that confidence | The divided-will's sentence concerns the fragmentation data. [P-A1](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a1) claims grade C support from elsewhere. For grade-D entries the register says "no independent evidence discriminates" | Met only for the grade-D entries, and only if philosophical argument is not counted as evidence |
| The two held together knowingly | Yes. The register's design goal was to let credence and evidence grade come apart visibly | Met |

So "epistemic akrasia" is the wrong name for most of it. Two residues are real.

- **An unanchored moderate.** Where the grade is D and discriminability is *none*, a moderate credence is a prior. The register never says so, and never says why moderate and not low. A critic need not call that akrasia to call it unexplained.
- **A credence that does not respond.** [the-divided-will](/topics/the-divided-will/) reports that rival architectures predict the fragmentation data, that the selector only accommodates them, and that the data are "modest evidence against" one reading. `positions/agency-and-will` carries no note dated after that article and [P-A1](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a1) stands unmoved. Three coarse bands can absorb a small update honestly, but the register records neither the absorption nor the reasoning. On Lasonen-Aarnio's diagnosis the fault in akrasia is a disposition not to respond to conspicuous reasons, and this is where the Map is exposed.

**Step 2. Does the acceptance-not-belief reply work?**

| Mark of acceptance (source) | Tenets and background posits | Positions register |
|---|---|---|
| Voluntary; adopted as policy (Cohen; SEP "Belief") | Yes: "chosen starting points" (tenets page and background-commitments) | No: bands are reported, with "would shift if" conditions |
| Used as a premise "whether or not one feels it to be true" (Cohen) | Yes: tenets "constrain all content" | Mixed |
| Context-relative (Bratman) | Yes, weakly: the project is the context. The Map has no second context in which it declines the tenets, so the mark cannot be tested | No |
| Belief component limited to empirical adequacy (van Fraassen) | **No.** Tenet 1 says "There exists something beyond the material"; each tenet has a "Rules out" clause; the corpus argues that rivals are false | **No.** Credence is defined as probability "the position is true *as a claim*" |
| Inquiry into the proposition closed for present purposes (SEP "Belief") | Yes inside the automation, which requires alignment | No: shift conditions keep it open |
| Speaks as an explainer (van Fraassen) | Yes, and this mark does not discriminate | Yes, likewise |

Result: the reply is **available for the tenet tier and unavailable for the register tier**. The selector is held under both. Tenet 3's own standing paragraph already speaks the language of acceptance: downward causation is "a posit the interface argument leaves open, not a result it secures". [P-A1](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a1) and [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2) then put a credence on it.

**Step 3. The hybrid and its costs.** The coherent position is that the Map *accepts* its tenets and posits as premises of a research programme and *believes*, to stated degrees, what follows from them and what bears on them. That needs four things said that the corpus does not now say.

1. **Tenet-dependent credences are conditional credences.** A moderate credence in [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2) is best read as credence given the framework. [P-Q11](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q11) already does this ("the credence attaches to the conditional"). Read unconditionally, the band is a belief the acceptance reply cannot cover.
2. **Acceptance moves the burden to practical reason.** One accepts for a purpose. Today's Kant note finds that the Map's need is not a need of reason as such and that Kant concedes Wizenmann's objection for such cases. The acceptance reply therefore inherits that negative result. It answers "are you believing against your evidence?" and leaves "why this premise?" standing.
3. **The permission is hostage to the evidence staying weak.** On the moderate-fideist condition quoted above, acceptance is permitted where strong evidence is lacking either way. The Map meets that for the selector by its own account. It stops meeting it if evidence against accumulates, so the unresponsive band in Step 1 matters under acceptance too.
4. **Accepted premises yield no evidence.** [P-M1](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#p-m1) already holds that a tenet removes a defeater and never upgrades an evidence tier. The bootstrapping worry the SEP reports from Horowitz has a Map-sized analogue: counting the coherence of tenet-derived articles as a track record. [P-M2](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#p-m2)'s convergence discount is the existing guard, and the article should name the connection.

A vocabulary cost follows. A file-level count today found "the Map holds" in 259 files, "the Map accepts" in 61, "the Map posits" in 31 and "the Map believes" in 2 (topics, concepts, positions, tenets, apex, voids; occurrences not classified by sense). "Holds" is neutral between the two attitudes, which is convenient and is also how the question has stayed unasked.

**What the Map cannot say.** It cannot say "we merely accept dualism" while publishing a register of probabilities that its claims are true. It cannot borrow the level-splitters' defence. And it cannot treat van Fraassen as an ally without noting that his accepter is agnostic about exactly the kind of claim Tenet 1 asserts.
