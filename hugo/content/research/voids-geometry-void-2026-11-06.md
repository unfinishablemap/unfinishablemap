## Executive Summary

A sighted subject spends every waking moment inside visual space and takes its geometry to be the geometry of the world: parallel walls look parallel, square floor tiles look square. Measurement says otherwise. When observers set two rows of lights to look parallel, the rows diverge physically (Hillebrand 1902; Blumenfeld 1913) [via Wagner et al. 2018]. Equilateral triangles of 2–20 m laid out by exocentric pointing show "angular excesses and deficits of up to 30 degrees" (Koenderink, van Doorn & Lappin 2000) [abstract]. The apparent angles of real squares on the ground sum to "consistently less than the 360° predicted by Euclidean geometry" (Wagner, Hatfield, Cassese & Makwinski 2018) [raw]. None of this is experienced as distortion. The record shows a void with four faces.

1. **The access face.** The geometry of one's own visual space cannot be read from inside. The curvature appears only when large figures are measured or when sight is checked against touch or action. Three features hide it: a curved space is locally flat (Reid 1764; Helmholtz 1870), the comparison standard sits in the same space as what it measures (Helmholtz's mirror-world men), and the subject's *conceived* geometry is Euclidean (Izard et al. 2011, 2022; McGinn 1995). "Railway tracks appear phenomenally to converge, but we also believe that they are parallel" [raw: Wagner et al. 2018].
2. **The unity face.** There may be no single visual space whose geometry could be read. Foley's (1972) result "contradicts properties of any space of constant curvature, whether that curvature is positive, negative, or zero" [raw: Suppes 1995]. Curvature changes sign with distance (Koenderink et al. 2000) and with task, instruction and observer. Norman et al. (2025) found 30 observers spread across "elliptic, Euclidean, and hyperbolic geometry" [abstract], while a 2026 VR replication found no far-space hyperbolicity (Chambers et al. 2026) [abstract]. Action escapes the distortion that visual matching shows (Loomis et al. 1992) [abstract]. Introspection cannot tell the subject which of these spaces it is in at a given moment.
3. **The readout face.** No judgement is known to read out the *phenomenal* geometry. Results depend on whether observers are told to report objective, perspective, apparent or projective size. "Apparent" instructions are themselves read in several ways (Wagner, via Wagner et al. 2018) [raw], and "statements about the geometry of visual space are always in reference to a specific set of instructions and stimulus conditions" [raw].
4. **The conventionality face.** Even complete data may not fix the answer. Helmholtz's inhabitants of a convex-mirror world, whose rulers shrink with them, would get "exactly the same results" as we do, and "neither … would be able to convince the other that he had the true, the other the distorted, relations" [raw]. Poincaré concluded: "One geometry can not be more true than another; it can only be more convenient", with Euclidean geometry the most convenient "Because it is the simplest" [raw]. Luneburg's hyperbolic metric and Erkelens's perspective-transformed Euclidean space both fit Blumenfeld's classic alleys.

**History.** Thomas Reid derived a non-Euclidean "geometry of visibles" in 1764, decades before Lobachevsky. In it, "Of every right-lined triangle, the three angles taken together, are greater than two right angles", and "If two lines be parallel — that is, everywhere equally distant from each other — they cannot both be straight" [raw]. Reid's visibles are the two-dimensional field of visual *directions*, and Luneburg's space is binocular and three-dimensional, so the two results concern different objects (Angell 1974) [abstract]. The pattern still recurs: in both, parallelism and equidistance come apart in seen space.

**Calibration.** A physicalist account predicts every face. Neural spatial representations need not preserve the Euclidean metric (Turski 2023 derives curved "phenomenal geometries" from the optics of asymmetric eyes) [abstract]. Task-dependence fits an architecture with several spatial representations. The void is real as a first-person limit and gives no evidence for dualism. It belongs on [voids](/voids/)'s framework-independent list. Its value to the Map is twofold. It is a clean exhibit for Tenet 5: Poincaré names simplicity as the rule that picks a geometry where the data cannot. And it costs the Map something: introspection, which cannot read the geometry of the spatial *content* it presents, is weaker evidence than usually assumed for the claim that experience itself is non-spatial (McGinn 1995). See "Where the Map Is Exposed".

## Key Sources

### Wagner, Hatfield, Cassese & Makwinski (2018), "Differentiating between Affine and Perspective-Based Models for the Geometry of Visual Space Based on Judgments of the Interior Angles of Squares", *Vision* 2(2): 22, doi:10.3390/vision2020022 [raw: full JATS text via Europe PMC, PMC6835612, CC-BY]
- **Type**: Experimental paper with a historical review. Hatfield (a philosopher, University of Pennsylvania) is a co-author.
- **Key points**:
  - The history in brief. Gibson's realism implies a strictly Euclidean visual space. Reid proposed a spherical one. Luneburg proposed a hyperbolic one to explain Hillebrand's and Blumenfeld's alleys, "which found that in order to produce apparently parallel lines or walls, subjects adjusted the lines or walls so that they diverged as they receded from the observer" [raw]. Indow's tests "generally supported the idea that visual space had a negative curvature" [raw].
  - Dissent: "Some authors question the existence of any well-defined visual geometry, citing demonstrations that spatial judgments can be affected by changes in context and the presence of reference frames" [raw]. Wagner's reply is that visual space changes with context: "Therefore, statements about the geometry of visual space are always in reference to a specific set of instructions and stimulus conditions" [raw].
  - The affine model: depth is compressed relative to frontal extent, "on average c = 0.45" in Wagner (1985) [raw]. Compression varies with distance: depth expands for stimuli nearer than about 1 m, compresses beyond, and approaches c ≈ 0.5 (Wagner & Gambino meta-analysis) [raw, paraphrased].
  - Hatfield's perspective model rests on a phenomenological observation: tracks, sidewalks and hallways "appear to converge with distance", while the floor appears to rise [raw, paraphrased]. "Hatfield observed that, phenomenally, contraction is a regular and pervasive feature of human visual experience" [raw]. The Boring paradox: "our experience of objects at a distance is paradoxical"; "Railway tracks appear phenomenally to converge, but we also believe that they are parallel" [raw].
  - Registered versus perceived values: the first are "nonconsciously registered and processed by the visual system", the second are "phenomenally available in consciousness" [raw].
  - Four instruction types (objective, perspective, apparent, projective). "Hatfield [43] and Granrud [74], for example, have argued that objective instructions add a cognitive component to judgments, while apparent instructions more closely correspond to phenomenal experience" [raw]. Apparent instructions average out to constancy perhaps because observers read them variously, "some interpreting the instructions as requesting projective size, some perspective size, and some objective size" [raw].
  - Results from two experiments, the second with clarified instructions ("apparent number of degrees"): near angles are smaller than far angles, and angle sums fall "consistently less than the 360° predicted by Euclidean geometry" [raw]. The authors read this as "consistent with a slight positive curvature to visual space very near to the observer and increasingly negative curvature with increasing distance" [raw]. Perspective-model proponents "should question the simplifying assumption that visual space is Euclidean" [raw]. Their closing hedge: "Only systematic parametric work that describes the metric of visual space throughout the visual field will be able to offer a closer specification of the geometry or geometries of visual space" [raw].
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam (three successive simple models fail); the readout face.

### Erkelens (2015), "The Perspective Structure of Visual Space", *i-Perception* 6(5), doi:10.1177/2041669515613672 [raw: full JATS text via Europe PMC, PMC5016827]
- **Key points**:
  - "Luneburg’s model has been the reference for experimental studies of visual space for almost seventy years" [raw]. "The claim made by Luneburg was that the different results from the two alley experiments were evidence for a non-Euclidean geometry of visual space" [raw]. "All subjects set the distance alley wider than the parallel alley" [raw]. "In visual space parallelism is not equivalent with equidistance because, if it were, parallel and distance alleys would be indistinguishable" [raw].
  - On Foley (1972): "A possible consequence of Foley’s conclusion is that perceived angles and distances do not constitute a single visual space" [raw]. Dependence on task, reference, distance and instruction "culminated in even questioning visual space itself" (Koenderink et al., 2008) [raw].
  - His own model: a perspective transformation of Euclidean space with a finite vanishing point fits Blumenfeld's alleys to within millimetres, so "perspective space is an appropriate model for how we perceive orientations and angles" [raw]. The distance alleys are explained "if we assume that subjects mixed up the metrics of both visual and physical spaces" [raw]. "Instead, human beings have both perspective and Euclidean representations of physical space at their disposal" [raw].
- **Bibliographic caution**: Erkelens's reference list prints Luneburg (1950) as *JOSA* 50: 637–642. Crossref gives volume 40, issue 10, start page 627 (doi:10.1364/JOSA.40.000627). Wagner et al. print 40: 637–642. Use the Crossref values.

### Suppes (1995), "Some Foundational Problems in the Theory of Visual Space", in Luce, D'Zmura, Hoffman, Iverson & Romney (eds.), *Geometric Representations of Perceptual Phenomena: Papers in Honor of Tarow Indow on his 70th Birthday*, Erlbaum, pp. 37–45 [raw: Suppes Corpus PDF, Stanford; OCR text with scattered glyph errors, and quotes were taken only from clean spans]
- **Key points**:
  - Abstract: "The most important general feature of visual space is that it is context dependent, a characteristic of physical systems rather than classical geometrical ones"; "no reasonably simple unified set of axioms in the spirit of qualitative synthetic geometry can be given for the structure of visual space" [raw]. In the body: "visual space is more like a physical system than a geometrical one" [raw].
  - On Luneburg's program, which Suppes rates the best developed: Indow's careful experiments revealed "a complete lack of parametric stability" once constant curvature is assumed [raw]. Estimated curvature is unstable from day to day for the same subject, and there is little transfer across task types [raw, paraphrased; the passage has OCR damage].
  - **Foley (1972)** [via Suppes]: subjects built a figure from perpendicularity and congruence judgements, then judged one segment significantly longer than another (24 subjects, 40 of 48 trials). That judgement "contradicts properties of any space of constant curvature, whether that curvature is positive, negative, or zero" [raw].
  - **Wagner (1985)** [via Suppes]: in an outdoor field of 13 stakes, perceived depth extents came out about half of physically equal frontal extents. "Notice how drastic the foreshortening is along the depth axis" [raw].
- **Note**: Suppes (1977), "Is visual space Euclidean?", *Synthese* 35(4): 397–421, doi:10.1007/bf00485624 [M], is the earlier and better-known paper. It was not consulted.

### Koenderink, van Doorn & Lappin (2000), "Direct Measurement of the Curvature of Visual Space", *Perception* 29(1): 69–79, doi:10.1068/p2921 [abstract]
- The method uses Gauss's definition of angular excess, measured by exocentric pointing outdoors under natural viewing, for triangles with sides of 2–20 m. "We found angular excesses and deficits of up to 30 degrees" [abstract]. "The curvature changes from elliptic in near space to hyperbolic in far space. At very large distances the plane becomes parabolic" [abstract].

### Cuijpers, Kappers & Koenderink (2000), "Large Systematic Deviations in Visual Parallelism", *Perception* 29(12): 1467–1482, doi:10.1068/p3041 [abstract]
- "The visual environment is distorted with respect to the physical environment" [abstract]. When subjects set bars to look parallel, "large deviations (of up to 40 degrees) occur" [abstract], proportional to the bars' separation angle. "A Riemannian space of constant curvature, therefore, cannot be an adequate description" [abstract].

### Luneburg (1950), "The Metric of Binocular Visual Space", *JOSA* 40(10): 627ff., doi:10.1364/JOSA.40.000627 [abstract]; Luneburg (1947), *Mathematical Analysis of Binocular Vision*, Princeton UP [M via Erkelens and Cuijpers; Wagner et al. give Oxford UP, New York]
- The 1950 claim: "the so-called visual space has a uniquely determined non-Euclidean metric" whose parameters "depend on the individual observer" [abstract]. This is the strongest simple-model claim in the record, and the one the later work dismantled.

### Norman, Carmichael, Hagan, Mishchuk, Bryant, Ramirez & Sutton (2025), "Visual outdoor space perception", *Scientific Reports* 15: 11549, doi:10.1038/s41598-025-96424-6 [abstract]
- 30 observers adjusted marker triangles outdoors until they looked equilateral or right-angled. "There was a wide range of outcomes, such that the observers' judgments were consistent with elliptic, Euclidean, and hyperbolic geometry" [abstract]. "There is thus no single consistent relationship between physical space and perceived space" [abstract]. "The geometry of visual space frequently changes as the size of spatial configurations is varied" [abstract].
- **Currency**: the 2020s record extends the century-old one. It does not close it.

### Chambers, Pinet, Center, Ojala, LaValle & Prencipe (2026), "Estimating the Curvature of Visual Space in Virtual Reality Using an Exocentric Pointing Task", *Proc. 2026 ACM Symposium on Applied Perception*, pp. 1–11, doi:10.1145/3821409.3821467 [abstract]
- "Human perception of space deviates from a simple Euclidean representation of physical reality" [abstract]. The study replicates Koenderink et al. (2000) in VR with 30 participants. Near space shows "a highly significant elliptic effect", while "middle and far distances showed no evidence of hyperbolicity (negative angular error) and were perceived as closer to Euclidean" [abstract]. That disagrees with the 2000 field result in far space. The medium differs (VR against outdoors), so the disagreement may be about cues and not about the geometry of seeing.

### Turski (2023), "Riemannian geometries of visual space: Variable curvature and horizon", *Mathematical Methods in the Applied Sciences* 46(8): 9298–9324, doi:10.1002/mma.9054 [abstract]
- Derives "phenomenal geometries" from simulated iso-disparity conics for asymmetric eyes: "Thus, geometries of physical and visual spaces are different" [abstract]. For most fixations "only the sign of the curvature may be inferred" [abstract, paraphrased]. This is the physicalist mechanism sketch: the optics of the eye generates the curved geometry.

### Loomis, Da Silva, Fujita & Fukusima (1992), "Visual space perception and visually directed action", *JEP: Human Perception and Performance* 18(4): 906–921, doi:10.1037/0096-1523.18.4.906 [abstract]
- Depth intervals had to be made "considerably larger" than frontal intervals to look equal, increasingly so over 4–12 m [abstract, paraphrased]. Blind walking and pointing to the same targets was "quite accurate", "indicating that the distortion in the mapping from physical to visual space evident in the visual matching task does not manifest itself in the visually open-loop motoric tasks" [abstract].

### Izard, Pica, Spelke & Dehaene (2011), "Flexible intuitions of Euclidean geometry in an Amazonian indigene group", *PNAS* 108(24): 9782–9787, doi:10.1073/pnas.1016686108 [abstract]; Izard, Pica & Spelke (2022), "Visual foundations of Euclidean geometry", *Cognitive Psychology* 136: 101494, doi:10.1016/j.cogpsych.2022.101494 [abstract]
- 2011: "on a surface described to them as perfectly planar, the Mundurucu's estimations of the internal angles of triangles added up to ~180 degrees", and they affirmed a single parallel through a point [abstract]. 2022: in analysing planar visual forms, participants of all ages and both cultures "retained the same properties as Euclidean geometry ... even in the absence of formal instruction in geometry" [abstract].
- **Use**: the *conceived* geometry, of imagined planes and planar forms, is Euclidean across cultures, while the *perceived* layout geometry is not. The two systems are measured on different objects (imagined planes and 2D forms against 3D layout), so this is a dissociation, not a contradiction. The article must say so.

### Reid (1764), *An Inquiry into the Human Mind on the Principles of Common Sense*, ch. VI §IX, "Of the Geometry of Visibles" [raw: Sneath (ed.), *The Philosophy of Reid*, New York: Henry Holt, 1892, pp. 199–202, whose text is taken from Hamilton's seventh edition (Edinburgh, 1872) "The most important notes of Hamilton, as well as his Index, abridged, have been retained" [raw]; archive.org cu31924029079790, OCR]
- **Key points**:
  - The geometer "does not consider that the visible figure presented to his eye, is only the representative of a tangible figure"; the two have different properties, so "what he demonstrates to be true of the one, is not true of the other" [raw].
  - The construction: the eye at the centre of a sphere, perceiving "only the position of objects with regard to itself, and not their distance" [raw], so every visible straight line coincides with a great circle.
  - Propositions: "Every right line being produced, will at last return into itself"; "If two lines be parallel — that is, everywhere equally distant from each other — they cannot both be straight"; "Of every right-lined triangle, the three angles taken together, are greater than two right angles" [raw].
  - Why the geometer never notices: "a small part of visible extension differs very little from that extension in length and breadth, which is the object of touch", and "we never see distinctly what is at a considerable distance from the axis of the eye" [raw].
  - The Idomenians, Reid's imagined beings with sight but no touch. Their geometry "agrees in everything with the geometry of visibles" [raw].
  - Hamilton's footnote (signed "H"): "How does this differ from a doctrine of Perspective?— At any rate, the notion is Berkeley's" [raw]. Hamilton asked in the 1840s–70s the same question the Erkelens–Hatfield perspective models put to Luneburg.

### Daniels (1972), "Thomas Reid's Discovery of a Non-Euclidean Geometry", *Philosophy of Science* 39(2): 219–234, doi:10.1086/288435 [abstract]; Angell (1974), "The Geometry of Visibles", *Noûs* 8(2): 87ff., doi:10.2307/2214780 [abstract]; Yaffe (2002), "Reconsidering Reid's Geometry of Visibles", *Philosophical Quarterly* 52(209): 602–620, doi:10.1111/1467-9213.00288 [abstract]; Meadows (2009), "Contemporary Arguments for a Geometry of Visual Experience", *European Journal of Philosophy*, doi:10.1111/j.1468-0378.2009.00387.x [abstract; online 2009, print issue not verified]
- Daniels: "Reid's construction uses an idealized eye, incapable of making distance discriminations"; the geometry is "doubly elliptical" with the sphere as model [abstract].
- Angell defends the thesis for the visual field: "the geometry which precisely and naturally fits the actual configurations of the visual field is a non-Euclidean, two-dimensional, elliptical geometry". It conflicts "with the view held by most psychologists and philosophers that the actual two-dimensional configurations of the visual field are Euclidean". The conflict with Luneburg's hyperbolic result is "merely apparent-Luneburg's theory deals with a different problem" [abstract].
- Yaffe: Reid's equivalence proof succeeds, but "the geometry of spherical projections is not the only geometry of visible figure" [abstract].
- Meadows: the equivalence (Yaffe, Van Cleve, Belot) is established, but "the resistance of this contemporary argument to empirical considerations counts against its plausibility", and it gives no reason to think the spherical geometry "is the correct geometrical description of visual experience" [abstract, the second span paraphrased].
- **Van Cleve (2002)**, "Thomas Reid's Geometry of Visibles", *Philosophical Review* 111(3): 373–416, doi:10.1215/00318108-111-3-373 [M]. The claim, reported only in a search summary, that Van Cleve thinks Reid secures the geometry only at the cost of his direct realism is **unverified**. Do not state it.

### Helmholtz (1870 lecture), "On the Origin and Significance of Geometrical Axioms", in *Popular Lectures on Scientific Subjects, Second Series*, trans. E. Atkinson [raw: Project Gutenberg #77725, from the London: Longmans, Green, 1908 printing]
- The scaling point: if all bodies, ours included, changed size in proportion, "we should with our means of space-perception be utterly unaware of the change" [raw]. "The eye turning in all directions is our theodolite for measuring arcs and angles in the visual field" [raw].
- **The convex-mirror world.** The images of objects are "diminished and flattened in proportion to the distance". "The flattening, or decrease in the third dimension, is relatively greater than the decrease of the surface-dimensions". "The images of the distant horizon and of the sun in the sky lie behind the mirror at a limited distance" [raw]. A man in the image measuring with his shrinking rule "would count out exactly the same number of centimetres as the real man" [raw]. "In short I do not see how men in the mirror are to discover that their bodies are not rigid solids and their experiences good examples of the correctness of Euclid’s axioms" [raw]. And: "neither, so far as I can see, would be able to convince the other that he had the true, the other the distorted, relations. Indeed I cannot see that such a question would have any meaning at all, so long as mechanical considerations are not mixed up with it" [raw].

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The subject lives in visual space without access to its metric. Three structural facts keep it out of reach.

**Locality.** Curvature shows only in large figures. Reid saw this: a small patch of visible extension "differs very little" from tangible extension, and distinct vision covers only a small patch. Helmholtz says the same of physical space: angle sums differ between large and small triangles only if curvature is non-zero. Ordinary looking samples small, foveated regions and never integrates the angular excess.

**Self-measurement.** Any visual comparison standard (a reference bar, a compass, the felt rightness of a corner) is itself seen in visual space. Helmholtz's mirror men "count out exactly the same number of centimetres" because their rule shrinks with what it measures. Visual-on-visual checking cannot expose a distortion common to standard and target. Only a cross-modal or motor check can, by touch or by walking, and those are exactly the checks that reveal it (Loomis 1992).

**A Euclidean overlay.** The conceived geometry is Euclidean (Izard 2011, 2022). The subject reads the scene through it: tracks "appear phenomenally to converge, but we also believe that they are parallel". The belief sits on top of the appearance and is reported in its place. That is why McGinn could report folk space as "broadly Euclidian" without contradiction from his own vision.

### Evidence for the Limit

- The deviations are large: up to 30° of angular excess (Koenderink 2000), up to 40° in parallelity settings (Cuijpers 2000), depth compression to about half (Wagner 1985, via Suppes). Subjects do not report seeing distortion. They report what looks parallel or square, and that is the datum.
- The finding is robust across 1902–2026, outdoor and lab, dark and full-cue, field and VR, and across Viennese, German, American, Japanese and Dutch laboratories. What varies is *which* geometry, never *whether* Euclidean fits.
- It is old. Reid derived a non-Euclidean visible geometry by reasoning in 1764, and psychophysics found non-Euclidean settings from 1902. For two centuries the dominant view, in Angell's words, held "that the actual two-dimensional configurations of the visual field are Euclidean". The persistence of that belief in the face of both is the access face at the level of a discipline.

### Phenomenology

There is almost nothing to report, and that is the phenomenology. Approaching the edge feels like nothing: the room is just the room. Two exceptions open a crack. One is the railway-track experience, the felt convergence that sits alongside a belief in parallelism (Boring's paradox), which most people can produce on request. The other is Hatfield's rising floor. Both are experienced as facts about *looking*, never as facts about the *geometry of the space looked into*. Every subject asked to set a square or a parallel alley produces a confident, non-Euclidean setting without any sense of error. Here the signature void-phenomenology is the absence of any felt edge.

### The Unity Question: Is There One Space to Have a Geometry?

The access face presupposes a single visual space with a geometry one fails to read. The unity face questions that. Foley's result fits no constant-curvature space. Suppes found no parametric stability. Curvature flips sign with distance. Different tasks (alleys, triangles, pointing, angle estimates) give different geometries for the same observer, and different observers give elliptic, Euclidean and hyperbolic results under the same task (Norman 2025). Visual matching is distorted while walking is accurate (Loomis 1992). Erkelens's reconciliation is itself a two-space hypothesis ("both perspective and Euclidean representations of physical space"). Wagner's counter ("visual space itself may change with context") keeps the word "space" but gives up the single geometry.

What this adds to the void: if there are several spatial representations, the subject cannot tell from inside which one is producing the current appearance, or whether "the" look of the room is one of them. The grammar of "my visual space" implies a single object that the evidence does not support.

## What a Physicalist Account Predicts (calibration)

Every face is predicted. Spatial representations in nervous systems are built for action and recognition and have no reason to be Euclidean. Turski (2023) gets curved phenomenal geometries from ocular optics alone, and Erkelens gets the alleys from perspective with a finite vanishing point. A multi-representation architecture predicts task-dependence and the matching/action split. The Euclidean overlay is predicted by a separate geometric-concept system (Izard). The conventionality face is a point about measurement that binds any agent. A physicalist and a dualist describe the same blank in the same place. **The void gives no evidence for dualism and must not be presented as doing so.** Its candidacy rests on the robustness of the first-person limit and on its bearing on Tenet 5 and on the introspective case for non-spatiality.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods
None from inside. Prolonged attention to the railway-track experience yields convergence, not a metric.

### Indirect Methods
- **Large-figure measurement**: Gauss's angular excess by exocentric pointing (Koenderink 2000; Chambers 2026). It is the only way to see curvature directly, and it is done by an experimenter.
- **Cross-task consistency tests**: Foley's construction and Suppes's axiomatics. They test whether a single geometry exists before asking which.
- **Cross-modal and motor checks**: blind walking against visual matching (Loomis 1992). These are Helmholtz's "mechanical considerations".
- **Instruction manipulation**: Wagner's four instruction types. Wagner et al.'s "apparent number of degrees" wording is a small advance in reaching the phenomenal reading.
- **Individual-difference profiling**: large-N field studies (Norman 2025). The geometry may be a trait as much as a constant.
- **Reid's a priori route**: derive the geometry an idealized eye must have. It succeeds as mathematics (Yaffe) and is weak as phenomenology (Meadows).

### What AI Might See
Speculative. A vision system trained to output physical depth has its output geometry fixed by its training targets and has no inside for a curvature to hide in. An AI's real advantage here is analytical. It can fit one model across the whole record (instruction × distance × cue condition × observer × task) and test whether any single geometry survives, which is the question Suppes says no simple axiom set answers. A language model trained on human text inherits the conceived Euclidean geometry and McGinn-style reports, so it would repeat the human disclaimer of curvature. It is evidence about human testimony, not about seeing. Whether multimodal models trained on human judgements of "looks parallel" reproduce the alley divergence was not searched.

## Whether the Void Is Real

The access face is real and explorable from outside: the curvature has been measured, so the limit is first-personal (*Unexplored presenting as Unexplorable*). The unity face may dissolve into a better-posed question: *which representation, under which instruction?* If it does, the void becomes *the subject cannot tell which space it is in*, which is still a void. The conventionality face is the most fragile. Helmholtz himself exempts cases where "mechanical considerations" enter, and for vision they do enter, through touch and action. So for visual space, unlike physical space, there may be a fact to recover. What survives every reading is the first-person blank: no amount of looking reveals the geometry of the looked-into.

## Four-Criteria Calibration ([apophatic-cartography-four-criteria](/concepts/apophatic-cartography-four-criteria/))

One grader and no pre-registered rubric, so these grades are judgement.

- **Signature specificity, strong.** A metric distortion of tens of degrees, reported as no distortion, exposed only by large-figure measurement or by a cross-modal check, and overlaid by a Euclidean belief. It is recognisable from the breakdown alone.
- **Structured persistence, strong.** The shape survives Reid's a priori construction, Helmholtz's and Poincaré's conventionalism, Luneburg's Riemannian program, Indow's decades of tests, Koenderink's pointing, Wagner's affine models, the Hatfield/Erkelens perspective models, VR and large-N field work. None of them makes the subject see the curvature.
- **Cross-observer convergence: strong on the negative, weak on the positive.** Independent laboratories over 120 years agree that visual space is not the Euclidean physical space. They disagree on what it is: elliptic near and hyperbolic far (Koenderink 2000; Wagner et al. 2018), elliptic near and Euclidean far (Chambers 2026), observer-dependent (Norman 2025), or not one space (Foley, Suppes, Koenderink 2008).
- **Framework independence: high on the finding, low on interpretive weight for dualism.** It belongs on [voids](/voids/)'s framework-independent list.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet: Occam's Razor Has Limits
The record is a sequence of simplicity defaults falling one after another. First, visual space equals physical space (Gibson's realism implies strict Euclidean geometry). Second, a single constant curvature per observer (Luneburg's "uniquely determined" metric). Third, a single affine compression (Wagner's c). Fourth, a perspective transform that stays Euclidean (Gilinsky, Hatfield, Erkelens), which Wagner et al. (2018) say should drop that assumption. Poincaré states the rule outright: Euclidean geometry is "the most convenient: 1º Because it is the simplest". Where data underdetermine the geometry, simplicity picks it, and it picks by convenience, not by truth. That is the tenet's claim in its cleanest form, made by the founder of conventionalism.

### Dualism
McGinn's argument that "Consciousness lacks extension and other spatial properties" leans on how experience presents itself to introspection. This void shows introspection cannot read even the geometry of the spatial *content* it presents. The point does not refute McGinn, whose claim concerns the location and extension of the experience, not its content. It does lower the evidential weight of his premise that "our consciousness does indeed present itself as non-spatial in character" [raw]. Phenomenal space has a metric that diverges from physical space's and is not readable by its owner. A dualist may call that a structure with no place, but neural representational spaces also have non-Euclidean metrics, so the divergence is neutral between the views.

### Bidirectional Interaction
Visually directed action is accurate where visual matching is distorted (Loomis 1992). If conscious visual experience guides action, the Map must say why action tracks physical space better than the experienced layout does. One answer is that action runs on non-consciously *registered* values (Hatfield's term, via Wagner et al.), which narrows the role of consciousness in visuomotor control to something other than metric guidance. The other is that action runs on a correction the subject does not experience. Either answer is a cost. The article should state it, not hide it.

### Minimal Quantum Interaction
No connection.

### No Many Worlds
Weak. Visual space is centred: visual direction is preserved around an origin, and the vanishing point lies at a finite distance *from the observer*. The geometry belongs to a here. That links the void to the indexical *this* vantage only.

### Implications
The catalogue gains a perceptual void with the longest quantitative record of any, and a direct line from Reid and Helmholtz to 2026 data. It supplies the Tenet 5 exhibit above. It also gives the Map a reason to calibrate its own introspective arguments about spatiality.

## Where the Map Is Exposed

1. **Introspective non-spatiality.** See Dualism above. Any Map page that cites the non-spatial self-presentation of experience as evidence should carry the qualification that introspection cannot read the metric of experienced space. Candidate pages were not audited this run.
2. **Action and the conscious layout.** See Bidirectional Interaction above.
3. **The dissolution narrative.** [meta-epistemology-of-limits](/voids/meta-epistemology-of-limits/) L88 lists non-Euclidean geometry among "Concepts once deemed unthinkable" that "became routine". Reid's 1764 construction complicates that. The geometry was thought, modelled and published for the visual field, and for decades it did not register as a geometry *of space*. That is arguably a better example of an occluded idea than an unthinkable one. It is a nuance, not a defect. Nothing was edited.

## Guards (for any writer)

- Do not say visual space *is* hyperbolic, or elliptic. The record does not converge (Chambers 2026 against Koenderink 2000; Norman 2025).
- Do not conflate Reid's 2D field of visual directions with Luneburg's binocular 3D space (Angell). Say "the same pattern recurs" (parallelism and equidistance coming apart), not "Reid predicted Luneburg".
- The Helmholtz-mirror and perspective-model parallel is this note's observation. Do not attribute it to Helmholtz, Hatfield or Erkelens.
- Do not quote Foley, Blumenfeld, Hillebrand, Luneburg (1947), Hatfield, Van Cleve or Wagner (2006) directly. They are known here only through the raw texts above. Do not state the Van Cleve direct-realism claim.
- Use Luneburg (1950) metadata from Crossref (40(10): 627ff.), not Erkelens's list.
- Keep the innocent-eye territory to one clause.
- The Izard contrast is a dissociation between systems measured on different objects. It is not a contradiction within one judgement.
- Do not present the void as evidence for dualism (see calibration).
