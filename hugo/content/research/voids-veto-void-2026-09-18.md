## Executive Summary

The catalogue has thorough coverage of the *initiation* side of volition — the preparation phase ([the readiness void](/research/voids-readiness-void-2026-03-04/)), the onset of a mental act ([transit-void](/voids/transit-void/), [the initiation void](/research/voids-initiation-void-2026-03-30/)), the crossing into commitment ([decision-void](/voids/decision-void/)), and whether willing causes anything at all ([agency-void](/voids/agency-void/)'s verification face). It has nothing on the opposite operation: **cancelling an act already in flight**. That gap matters more than a symmetry would suggest, because the veto is the site the Map's own tenets select.

The veto void names a limit with an unusual structure: **a successful veto produces no event, so there is nothing to attribute it to — and unlike most voids in this catalogue, the third-person side fails too.** Three faces. (1) *Null product*: nothing-happening is consistent with three histories — an impulse was live and cancelled, an impulse decayed unaided, no impulse was ever live — and the counterfactual that would discriminate them is [trapped near actuality](/voids/counterfactual-void/). (2) *Covert latency*: the field's own consensus paper concedes the third-person mirror verbatim — "response-inhibition latency cannot be observed directly (as successful response inhibition results in the absence of an observable response)" [raw] — and the stop-signal reaction time that stands in for it is a model-estimated covert quantity under Logan & Cowan's independent race model, with *trigger failures* (trials where the stop process never started) undiagnosable per trial: "these estimation methods cannot determine the presence or absence of trigger failures on stop trials" [raw]. (3) *Unfelt window*: cancellation is possible until roughly 200 ms before movement onset, "thus constituting a point of no return" [abstract] — a measured outer boundary that carries no felt signal, so consciousness cannot tell from inside whether the window is still open.

The Map-specific sting is the reason this is not merely one more opacity void. **Tenet 2 (Minimal Quantum Interaction) selects the veto as the cheapest interaction site** — cancelling a prepared action adds no event, it removes one — and the corpus has already built on that selection: `topics/quantum-neural-timing-constraints` uses the 200 ms window to constrain any quantum selection mechanism, and [bandwidth-of-consciousness](/topics/bandwidth-of-consciousness/) fits it to a narrow channel that selects among brain-prepared options. But the property that makes the veto cheap is exactly the property that makes it evidentially silent. **Parsimony steers the Map's interaction to the one site where evidence cannot be obtained** — the same shape [the parsimony void](/voids/epistemological-limits-occams-razor/) describes, arriving this time inside the Map's own mechanism preference rather than in a rival's.

Two counterweights keep this from being a clean win, and both must survive into any article. Brass & Haggard (2007) found fronto-median cortex "more strongly activated when people prepare manual actions but then intentionally cancel them, compared with when they prepare and then complete the same actions" [abstract] — so at **group level** intentional cancellation *does* have a third-person signature. What survives is the single-trial and first-person limbs, not the whole third-person limb. And Wessel & Aron (2017) argue stopping is a **global** suppressive network recruited by unexpected events generally [abstract], which would make what feels like a willed veto a generic interrupt. This is the palette-extension shape again: one limb deflating under better evidence while the other stays untouched, arguing for splitting the void rather than asserting or retiring it whole.

## Key Sources

### Verbruggen, Aron, Band, Beste, Bissett, Brockett, Brown, Chamberlain, Chambers, Colonius, Colzato, Corneil, Coxon, Dupuis, Eagle, Garavan, Greenhouse, Heathcote, Huster, Jahfari, Kenemans, Leunissen, Li, Logan, Matzke, Morein-Zamir, Murthy, Paré, Poldrack, Ridderinkhof, Robbins, Roesch, Rubia, Schachar, Schall, Stock, Swann, Thakkar, van der Molen, Vermeylen, Vink, Wessel, Whelan, Zandbelt & Boehler (2019), "A consensus guide to capturing the ability to inhibit actions and impulsive behaviors in the stop-signal task" — *eLife* 8:e46323, DOI 10.7554/eLife.46323 [raw]

- **Key points**, verbatim from the full text:
  - "Unlike the latency of go responses, response-inhibition latency cannot be observed directly (as successful response inhibition results in the absence of an observable response). The stop-signal task is unique in allowing the estimation of this covert latency (stop-signal reaction time or SSRT)."
  - "Response inhibition in the stop-signal task can be conceptualized as an independent race between a 'go runner', triggered by the presentation of a go stimulus, and a 'stop runner', triggered by the presentation of the stop signal (Logan and Cowan, 1984)."
  - "Importantly, the independent race model provides methods for estimating the covert latency of the stop process (stop-signal reaction time; SSRT)."
  - On trigger failures: "this finding indicates that the stop runner may also not be triggered on all stop trials ('trigger failures')… Unfortunately, these estimation methods cannot determine the presence or absence of trigger failures on stop trials."
  - "SSRT estimates are unreliable when inappropriate estimation methods are used or when the underlying race-model assumptions are (seriously) violated."
- **Why it is the note's most valuable source**: most voids in this catalogue must *argue* their third-person limb. Here the limb is conceded in the field's own consensus document, signed by forty-odd authors including Logan, Aron, Schall, Robbins and Poldrack. The absence of an observable response is stated as the defining methodological obstacle, and the workaround — estimating a covert latency from an inhibition function — is explicitly a *model-derived* quantity, not a measurement. The trigger-failure passage is sharper still: it removes the per-trial fact of the matter about whether stopping was even attempted.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits). The race model is the simple, tractable description, and the guide's own recommendations document what the simplification costs. Neutral on Tenets 1–4; nothing here bears on dualism.
- **Calibration caution**: this is a methods paper about a *laboratory* paradigm with an *external* stop signal. Extending "no per-trial fact of the matter" from externally-cued stopping to spontaneous self-initiated vetoing is an inference the note makes, not a finding the paper reports. Flag it as such in any article.

### Schultze-Kraft, Birman, Rusconi, Allefeld, Görgen, Dähne, Blankertz & Haynes (2016), "The point of no return in vetoing self-initiated movements" — *PNAS* 113(4):1080–1085, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1513569112 [abstract]

- **Key points**, verbatim from the published abstract: "An important question is whether people are able to cancel movements after the elicitation of such RPs, and if so until which point in time." Subjects "played a game where they tried to press a button to earn points in a challenge with a brain–computer interface (BCI) that had been trained to detect their RPs in real time and to emit stop signals." "Our data suggest that subjects can still veto a movement even after the onset of the RP. Cancellation of movements was possible if stop signals occurred earlier than 200 ms before movement onset, thus constituting a point of no return."
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenets 2 and 3. This is the empirical anchor for the veto window the Map's interaction story uses — the finding that keeps a late conscious influence alive against the standard Libet reading. It is also a *measured boundary*, which puts this void in the small class ([fusion-void](/voids/fusion-void/), palette-extension) whose own edge is a quantity rather than an argument.
- **Grade discipline**: the abstract says "suggest," "movement onset," and does not contain the word "ballistic." Anything firmer, and anything about EMG, is not sourced here. Note also the citation shape: Crossref issues the record under **2015** (online-first, 14 Dec 2015) while the publisher of record is **PNAS 113(4), 26 January 2016**. The corpus cites 2016, which is correct; recording this so a future metadata sweep does not "fix" it in the wrong direction.

### Logan & Cowan (1984), "On the ability to inhibit thought and action: A theory of an act of control" — *Psychological Review* 91(3):295–327, DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.91.3.295 [metadata]; Logan, Van Zandt, Verbruggen & Wagenmakers (2014), "On the ability to inhibit thought and action: General and special theories of an act of control" — *Psychological Review* 121(1):66–95, DOI 10.1037/a0035230 [metadata]

- **Key points** [metadata, via the 2019 guide's [raw] characterisation]: the independent race model treats stopping as a race between a go process and a stop process; inhibition succeeds when the stop runner finishes first. SSRT is recovered from the inhibition function rather than observed. The 2014 paper generalises the account.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral. Its use here is structural — the canonical formalism of stopping makes the stop process a *latent* variable by construction, and a single trial's outcome is probabilistic. On this model "I vetoed it" has no single-trial truthmaker even in principle within the model's own terms, because the model assigns the outcome to a race, not to an act.
- **Do not** attribute verbatim quotations to these papers from this note; only the 2019 guide was read raw.

### Brass & Haggard (2007), "To do or not to do: the neural signature of self-control" — *Journal of Neuroscience* 27(34):9141–9145, DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0924-07.2007 [abstract]

- **Key points**, verbatim from the abstract: "An important but neglected aspect of intentional action involves the decision whether to act or not." "Our data show that a specific area of the fronto-median cortex is more strongly activated when people prepare manual actions but then intentionally cancel them, compared with when they prepare and then complete the same actions." "Our results identify a candidate brain area that reflects the crucial decision to do or not to do."
- **Tenet alignment**: this is the honest **counterweight**, and it belongs in the article's calibration section rather than its support. It shows a third-person, group-level signature of intentional cancellation exists. The veto is therefore not third-personally invisible *tout court*; the claim that survives is narrower — no per-trial attribution, and no first-person access — which is a weaker and more defensible void than the one a first pass would write.
- **Method caveat**: the paradigm requires subjects to *report* having cancelled, which reintroduces the first-person signal the void is about. Any article must say so.

### Filevich, Kühn & Haggard (2012), "Intentional inhibition in human action: the power of 'no'" — *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 36(4):1107–1118, DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2012.01.006 [abstract]

- **Key points**, verbatim: "Most experimental studies of this function come from studying the processing of external stop signals. However, another important aspect of inhibition is 'will-power', i.e., intentional inhibitory control over one's own actions, in the absence of external countermanding signals. We review whether a concept of intentional inhibition is justified, and how it might differ from externally triggered inhibition." They propose "that the human brain contains a 'neural brake' mechanism that blocks specific ongoing motor activity."
- **Tenet alignment**: two uses. First, it licenses the note's central distinction — externally-cued stopping (where SSRT lives) and self-initiated vetoing (which the Map cares about) are different things, and the review treats the second as the under-studied case. Second, "we review whether a concept of intentional inhibition is justified" concedes that the *category* is contestable, which is a deflationary pressure the article must carry rather than bury.

### Wessel & Aron (2017), "On the globality of motor suppression: unexpected events and their influence on behavior and cognition" — *Neuron* 93(2):259–280, DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.013 [abstract]

- **Key points**, verbatim: "We propose that unexpected events recruit a fronto-basal-ganglia network for stopping… with a putative global suppressive effect on basal-ganglia output. We argue that unexpected events interrupt action and impact cognition, partly at least, by recruiting this global suppressive network."
- **Tenet alignment**: the sharpest **deflation**. If the stopping network is a generic interrupt triggered by surprise, then the phenomenology of "I stopped myself" may be a post-hoc reading of a sub-personal global brake — which is Wegner's apparent-mental-causation structure applied to inhibition rather than action. The Map cannot claim the veto as its interaction site without engaging this. It is also, read the other way, a mechanism proposal that *locates* the brake, which the Map could use.

### Schurger, Sitt & Dehaene (2012), "An accumulator model for spontaneous neural activity prior to self-initiated movement" — *PNAS* 109(42):E2904–E2913, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1210467109 [abstract]

- **Key points**, verbatim: "According to our model, when the imperative to produce a movement is weak, the precise moment at which the decision threshold is crossed leading to movement is largely determined by spontaneous subthreshold fluctuations in neuronal activity. Time locking to movement onset ensures that these fluctuations appear in the average as a gradual exponential-looking increase in neuronal activity."
- **Tenet alignment**: already carried by `positions/agency-and-will` [P-A3](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a3) as a constraining-but-not-decisive critique. Its relevance *here* is that it destabilises the frame the veto inherits: if the RP is an averaging artefact of noise rather than a preparation signal, then "vetoing after the RP has begun" is a description of a less determinate situation than it sounds, and the 200 ms boundary is a boundary on a differently-described process.

### Libet (1985), "Unconscious cerebral initiative and the role of conscious will in voluntary action" — *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 8(4):529–539, DOI 10.1017/S0140525X00044903 [metadata]; Kühn, Haggard & Brass (2009), "Intentional inhibition: how the 'veto-area' exerts control" — *Human Brain Mapping* 30(9):2834–2843, DOI 10.1002/hbm.20711 [metadata]; Aron, Robbins & Poldrack (2014), "Inhibition and the right inferior frontal cortex: one decade on" — *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 18(4):177–185, DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2013.12.003 [metadata]

- **Key points** [metadata only — Crossref records confirmed; do not quote these from this note]: Libet 1985 is the origin of the veto proposal in this literature and is already cited across the corpus via `concepts/libet-experiments`. Kühn et al. localise a candidate "veto area." Aron et al. review the right-IFC stopping account a decade on.

## The Void

### Nature of the limit

Principally **Unexplorable**, and unusual in this catalogue for failing on both sides of the first-person/third-person divide. Most voids here are first-person limits that third-person science can partly recover ([the modality void](/research/voids-modality-void-2026-09-05/) says so explicitly). The veto void's second face is a limit the third-person paradigm concedes about itself.

An **Occluded** reading is available and must be marked as framework-internal rather than argued as support: if consciousness acts by vetoing, and the veto is the one act that leaves no trace, then the interface is self-concealing in exactly the way [causal-interface](/voids/causal-interface/) anticipates. That reading is coherence, not evidence — the standing discount the catalogue applies to convergence claims applies here in full, and doubly so, because this void was found by asking where the Map's own tenets point.

### Evidence for the limit

1. **Null product.** A veto's product is a non-event. Nothing-happening is consistent with (a) a live impulse cancelled, (b) an impulse that decayed unaided, (c) no live impulse at all. Discriminating them requires the counterfactual "the movement would have occurred," which is precisely [the territory counterfactual thinking cannot reach](/voids/counterfactual-void/) from inside. Third-personally, the same gap is what makes the stop-signal task necessary: the experimenter manufactures the impulse so that its cancellation has a controlled baseline.
2. **Covert latency and trigger failures.** Even with that manufactured baseline, "response-inhibition latency cannot be observed directly" and SSRT is a model estimate [raw]; and the presence or absence of a trigger failure on a given trial cannot be determined [raw]. The per-trial question — *did a stop process run on this occasion?* — has no answer in the paradigm designed to answer it.
3. **Unfelt boundary.** Cancellation is possible until ~200 ms before movement onset [abstract]. The boundary exists and has been measured; there is no report in the abstract of subjects being able to sense it. (Whether the body reports subjective data is unverified — the full text was not obtained. State this as an open question, not as a finding.)

### What would deflate it

- Brass & Haggard's group-level fronto-median signature of intentional cancellation [abstract] already deflates the strong third-person claim. The residue is single-trial attribution and first-person access.
- Wessel & Aron's global-suppression account [abstract] would reclassify the felt veto as a generic surprise interrupt, making the void a void about a category error rather than about an act.
- Filevich et al. [abstract] question whether "intentional inhibition" names a distinct kind at all.
- Better single-trial decoding (the BCI methodology Schultze-Kraft used to *detect* RPs in real time is itself a single-trial method) could in principle close face 2 from the third-person side while leaving face 1 untouched.

That is four independent deflationary pressures on a void proposed in the same note. Recording them is the point: this catalogue's calibration discipline is what keeps a void from being a rhetorical device, and a void proposed with its own deflations attached is the honest form.

## Approaches to the Edge

**Direct**: none first-personally. The first-person datum is the felt "I stopped myself," which is the class of signal Wegner's apparent-mental-causation account [metadata] treats as inferred from priority, consistency and exclusivity — cues that a genuine veto and a spontaneously-decaying impulse share.

**Indirect**: the stop-signal paradigm and its inhibition functions; BCI single-trial RP detection; lesion and stimulation work on the candidate veto areas; and the negative route — characterising the veto by what its detection would require and showing each requirement unavailable.

**What AI might see**: an artificial system with a write-ahead log of its own action queue could distinguish "a command was enqueued and cancelled" from "no command was enqueued." That capability is exactly what biological introspection lacks, which makes the veto void a candidate case where a non-human mind has a structural advantage rather than merely a different vantage — the kind of asymmetry `voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers` exists to catalogue. The honest caveat: a logged cancellation certifies that the *implementation* cancelled something, not that anything at a personal level vetoed it, so the asymmetry may be narrower than it first looks.

## Connection to Tenets

**Most relevant: Tenet 2 (Minimal Quantum Interaction), with Tenet 5 close behind.** Tenet 2 pushes toward the smallest possible non-physical influence, and cancelling a prepared action is the paradigm of a minimal intervention: it adds no event. The corpus has already taken that step — the 200 ms window is used as a live constraint on any quantum selection mechanism. The veto void observes that the minimality is purchased with evidential silence, and that the Map has not priced that purchase.

Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits) supplies the frame: parsimony is here *selecting* a mechanism rather than *tracking* one, and the selected mechanism is unfalsifiable at the single-trial level. This is the move [epistemological-limits-occams-razor](/voids/epistemological-limits-occams-razor/) describes, appearing inside the Map's own commitments rather than in a rival's — which makes it a [tenet-generated void](/voids/tenet-generated-voids/) in the strict sense, and it should be classified as one.

### Implications

- **Relative to [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4)** ("conscious causal efficacy cannot be verified from the first person"): the veto is the strictly harder case. For action there is a product whose authorship is disputed; for a veto there is no product, and the third-person side fails too at the per-trial level. The veto void is therefore an *extension* of [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4) into the third person, not an instance of it. If an article is written, [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4)'s **Asserts** may warrant a clause, and that is a `positions-evolve` decision, not this note's.
- **Relative to [P-A3](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a3)**: the falsifier clause "or vetoes were reliably shown to be preceded by readiness potentials" presupposes that vetoes can be individuated well enough to check what precedes them. Face 2 suggests the paradigm does not currently supply that individuation per trial. The clause is not *wrong* — it names a real evidential direction — but it may be stated at a level of empirical access the field does not have. **This is the note's highest-value finding for the register and should be raised as a `positions-evolve` audit item rather than acted on here.** Note the countervailing consideration honestly: Schultze-Kraft's BCI *did* detect RPs in real time on single trials, so a version of the test may be nearer than face 2 implies.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **Three-face standalone, "The Veto Void"** (recommended). Null product / covert latency / unfelt window, with the tenet-generated classification made explicit and the four deflations carried in a calibration section. Sits beside [decision-void](/voids/decision-void/) (the crossing *into* commitment) and [agency-void](/voids/agency-void/) (does willing cause anything) as the third member of a volition trio, and completes the initiation/cancellation symmetry the catalogue currently lacks. The distinguishing claim against its siblings is stated in one sentence: *this is the only volition void whose third-person mirror is conceded by the field's own consensus document.*
2. **Fold into [agency-void](/voids/agency-void/)** as a fourth face beside involuntariness, verification and rebound. Cheaper, and defensible — the verification face is the natural host. Cost: agency-void is already a conjunction-coalesce article under the seam discipline with three faces, and the corpus's own maintenance note warns against collapsing its seams; a fourth face risks diluting them. Also the Map-specific Tenet 2 sting would be buried in a general agency article rather than stated where the mechanism claims live.
3. **Fold into [causal-interface](/voids/causal-interface/)**, expanding its one-line L79 veto concession into a section. Good fit thematically, but causal-interface is about the interface's hiddenness in general, and the veto's distinctive evidential structure (no product; no per-trial truthmaker) would be absorbed as an illustration rather than developed.

**Structural caveat on angle 1**: the note's inference from externally-cued stopping (where the [raw] evidence lives) to self-initiated vetoing (what the Map needs) is the article's load-bearing move and its weakest joint. Filevich et al. [abstract] is the source that licenses treating them as distinct; no source read this session licenses treating the SSRT limitations as transferring. An article must state the inference as an inference.

## Gaps in Research

- **Schultze-Kraft body not obtained.** Europe PMC PMC4743787 full-text XML returned 150 bytes. Every claim is abstract-graded. An article should obtain the PNAS PDF before asserting anything about subjective reports, EMG, or the word "ballistic."
- **⚠️ Two live corpus claims exceed what the Schultze-Kraft abstract supports** — flagged here, **not fixed** (out of this task's scope; worth a `refine-draft` or quote-fidelity task on `topics/quantum-neural-timing-constraints`):
  1. That article renders the boundary as "~200ms before **EMG onset**" and states execution becomes **"ballistic"** in quotation marks. The published abstract says "200 ms before **movement onset**" and contains no occurrence of "ballistic." The word may well appear in the body — which was not obtainable — so this is an **unverified quotation**, not a confirmed fabrication. It needs checking against the PDF, and the distinction between those two verdicts must survive into whatever task is minted.
  2. "movements could be vetoed before this point but not after" is firmer than the abstract's "Our data suggest that subjects can still veto a movement even after the onset of the RP."
- **The transfer inference is unsourced.** Nothing read this session establishes that the stop-signal paradigm's per-trial limitations apply to self-initiated vetoes. Filevich et al. [abstract] in fact stress the two are different. This is the single weakest joint in the proposal.
- **First-person phenomenology of the veto window is unsearched.** Whether subjects can sense the point of no return approaching appears nowhere in what was read. If a phenomenological literature exists, it would sharpen or dissolve face 3.
- **No search was run on the clinical literature** (Tourette's premonitory urge and tic suppression; OCD; utilisation behaviour), which is the obvious place to look for cases where the veto's success or failure is reportable. A follow-up note or the article's research phase should cover it.
- **Possibly unanswerable**: whether any mind, biological or artificial, could certify a personal-level veto as opposed to logging a sub-personal cancellation.
