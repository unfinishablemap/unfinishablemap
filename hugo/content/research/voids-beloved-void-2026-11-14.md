**Voids category**: Mixed. Who a person will come to love is **Unexplored** before the two people meet (in the sense of [three-kinds-of-void](/voids/three-kinds-of-void/)). Neither the person nor a model fed over a hundred background measures can forecast the largest component of initial desire, and the field gives three explanations it has not been able to choose between. Why *this* one rather than another equally eligible person is close to **Unexplorable**. The leading philosophical accounts concede that this question has no justifying answer, and the lover's own answer ends in pronouns ("because it was he, because it was I"). Whether the perfections the lover sees were found or conferred is **Occluded** for the lover. Stendhal's own word for the projection is *découverte*, "discovery", and the measured idealisation is invisible from inside the relationship it sustains.

## Executive Summary

The Map has no page on love. This note proposes a void with four faces. (1) **Forecast**: in speed-dating studies, the largest share of initial desire is specific to the pair. Random forests given over 100 pre-date self-report measures could predict how much a person desires others in general and how desirable a person is in general, but "were unable to predict relationship variance using any combination of traits and preferences reported before the dates" (Joel, Eastwick & Finkel 2017). People's stated ideals "failed to predict what inspired their actual desire" (Eastwick & Finkel 2008). (2) **Warrant**: the philosophy of love separates three questions, and its leading accounts concede that the middle one (why this person rather than someone else) has no justifying answer. Velleman offers only "causal explanations", Whiting holds there is "no answer" within a pool of equally eligible people, and Han argues "We don't ... come to love for reasons". Montaigne's answer is two pronouns. (3) **Object**: the lover feels the beloved to be irreplaceable ("phenomenologically non-fungible", Badhwar). Yet when a partner's moral traits change through dementia, or a companion app changes its model, the beloved is judged *gone*, and the relationship deteriorates or is mourned. The lover cannot say in qualitative terms what she loves, and what detects "her" is not numerical identity, which no one perceives. (4) **Lens**: Stendhal's crystallisation ("it is enough to think of a perfection in order to see it in that which you love") has a measured counterpart. Partners' impressions are "more a mirror of their self-images and ideals" than of the partner's self-description, and the idealisation predicts satisfaction. The lover cannot tell found value from conferred value, and philosophy has not settled which it is either. The void is framework-independent. Its sharpest tenet contact is with No Many Worlds and the Map's [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/) concept, and the evidence there cuts both ways.

## Key Sources

### Joel, Eastwick & Finkel (2017), "Is Romantic Desire Predictable? Machine Learning Applied to Initial Romantic Attraction"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617714580 (published); https://osf.io/gu8z7 (in-press manuscript, full text read)
- **Type**: Empirical paper, *Psychological Science* 28(10): 1478–1489
- **Key points**:
  - Two speed-dating studies of "romantically unattached individuals" who completed "more than 100 self-report measures" before the event; "Each participant met each opposite-sex participant attending a speed-dating event for a 4-min speed date" [raw, abstract].
  - "Random forests models predicted 4% to 18% of actor variance and 7% to 27% of partner variance; crucially, however, they were unable to predict relationship variance using any combination of traits and preferences reported before the dates" [raw, abstract].
  - The dyadic component is not small: "Relationship variance was the largest source of variance" [raw, preprint]. In the preprint's Table 1 it is 34.74% (men's desire for women) and 31.0% (women's desire for men), against 12.15% actor and 25.90% partner variance.
  - It is predictable *after* the meeting: "random forests were successful at predicting relationship desire using relationship-specific measures collected post-interaction (16-29% of variance explained)" [raw, preprint].
  - The authors give three readings and do not choose between them: that "background measures could be collected that would predict relationship-level desire, but relationship science has yet to reveal what they are"; that "relationship desire is comprised of a great many actor × partner interaction effects—each tiny but real"; or that "romantic attraction may emerge from dyad-specific factors that cannot be anticipated a priori" [raw, preprint]. The three readings line up with the voids trichotomy: unexplored, merely difficult (a sample-size problem), and possibly unexplorable before the meeting.
  - Conclusion: "compatibility elements of human mating are challenging to predict before two people meet" [raw, abstract].
- **Limits**: undergraduate samples, opposite-sex speed dating only, four-minute dates, initial desire not long-term love. The authors note that more demographic diversity (age, SES, religion) could make some relationship effects predictable.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits. The matchmaking assumption (attraction = traits × preferences) fails where it was expected to work. The many-tiny-effects reading, however, is the simplicity-preserving explanation, so the case does not decide in the Map's favour.

### Eastwick, Luchies, Finkel & Hunt (2014), "The Predictive Validity of Ideal Partner Preferences: A Review and Meta-Analysis"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1037/a0032432
- **Type**: Meta-analysis, *Psychological Bulletin* 140(3): 623–665 (abstract read)

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1177/08902070221085877 (published, online 28 May 2022); https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/sh7ja (preprint)
- **Type**: Preregistered longitudinal study, *European Journal of Personality* 37(3): 276–312 (abstracts of both versions read)
- **Key points**:

  - The published abstract "found only modest support for) the perceiver × target moderation account of compatibility" and "found no support for ideal partner preference-matching effects on romantic interest". ### Conroy-Beam & Buss (2016), "Do Mate Preferences Influence Actual Mating Decisions?"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000054
- **Type**: Agent-based simulation plus three couple studies, *JPSP* 111(1): 53–66 (abstract read)
- **Key points**: the main rival reading. Weak preference–partner correlations are what a preference-*driven* market would produce, because mate value constrains who can be chosen. "low-magnitude correlations between stated preferences and the individual traits of chosen partners" emerge in preference-driven simulations, and the authors conclude that "stated mate preferences guide actual mate selections under real mating-market constraints" [raw, abstract].
- **Use**: the article must give this. The forecast face survives it, because Conroy-Beam and Buss explain *partner selection* by mate value, which is an actor/partner effect, not the relationship variance that Joel et al. could not predict. But the article must not say stated preferences are idle.

### Helm (2005/2021), "Love", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/love/ (first published 8 April 2005; substantive revision 1 September 2021; full text read)
- **Type**: Encyclopedia. It is the map of the philosophical faces and the route to several primaries not read here.
- **Key points**:
  - The three justificatory questions, quoted [raw]: what justifies loving rather than not loving this person; "What, if anything, justifies my coming to love this particular person rather than someone else?"; what justifies continuing to love through change.
  - On Velleman: "he thinks (2) and (3) have no answers: the best we can do is offer causal explanations for our loving particular people, a position echoed by Han (2021)" [raw].
  - On Whiting (1991, p. 7): "there can be no answer to question (2) about why I come to love this rather than that person within this pool" [raw]. Whiting's history-based reply answers only (3).
  - Non-fungibility: "my beloved is not fungible—that no one could simply take her place without loss" [raw]. The trading-up problem: "it may seem that in such a case I have reason to “trade up”—to switch my love to the new, better person" [raw].
  - Badhwar concedes that the object of love is fungible after all, "though she insists that it is very unlikely in practice", and that it is "phenomenologically non-fungible" (2003, p. 63) [raw, SEP paraphrase and quotation]. **The felt irreplaceability and the theoretical fungibility come apart, and that gap is the object face.**
  - The lens face as a metaphysical dispute: "appraisal is a response to value that is antecedently there, whereas bestowal is the creation of value that was not antecedently there" [raw]. On Singer's bestowal view, "love…confers importance no matter what the object is worth" (Singer 1991, p. 273) [raw-secondary], so love cannot be justified at all. Helm (2010) and Bagley (2015) take the love and the values to "emerge simultaneously" [raw, SEP paraphrase].
  - Thomas (1991, p. 471): "no matter how wonderful and lovely an individual might be, on any and all accounts, it is simply false that a romantically unencumbered person must love that individual on pain of being irrational" [raw-secondary].
  - Leads in the bibliography: Kraut, "Love *De Re*" (1986), for the indexical face; Nozick, "Love's Bond" (1989), whose union view answers trading up by making the "we" part of the lover's extended self.
- **Tenet alignment**: No Many Worlds (object face); Bidirectional Interaction, read cautiously (the bestowal views).

### Velleman (1999), "Love as a Moral Emotion"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1086/233898 (JSTOR copy hosted at CSULB, full text read)
- **Type**: Paper, *Ethics* 109(2): 338–374
- **Key points**:
  - Love as appraisal: "I am inclined to say that love is likewise the awareness of a value inhering in its object; and I am also inclined to describe love as an arresting awareness of that value" (p. 360) [raw]. ### Stendhal (1822), *De l'amour* / *On Love*
- **URL**: Project Gutenberg #60882 (French), #53720 (trans. Philip Sidney Woolf and Cecil N. Sidney Woolf)
- **Type**: Primary text, read in both languages
- **Key points**:
  - "I call crystallisation the operation of the mind which, from everything which is presented to it, draws the conclusion that there are new perfections in the object of its love." [raw, English]

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Four faces, all bearing on one question: why this one?

1. **The forecast face.** Before two people meet, neither of them, nor a model given their traits and stated preferences, can predict how much each will especially desire the other. The pair-specific component is the largest single component of initial desire (Joel et al. 2017, Table 1), and it is invisible to background data. It becomes partly predictable only from measures of the interaction itself. The seven-month study (Eastwick et al. 2023) finds the same pattern outside the speed-dating room and no support for preference-matching. *Unexplored*, with an *Unexplorable* reading that the authors themselves name ("dyad-specific factors that cannot be anticipated a priori").

2. **The warrant face.** Afterwards, the lover still cannot say what makes *this* person, rather than another equally eligible one, the right object. The leading accounts converge on this from opposite directions. Appraisal theorists (Velleman) say question (2) has only causal answers. Bestowal theorists (Singer, and Frankfurt in the passage quoted) say love is not grounded in prior value, so there is nothing to cite. Whiting says there is "no answer" within the pool, and Han says we do not love for reasons. The lover's own answer, in the canonical text, is two indexicals. This is close to *Unexplorable*: the absence is a conceptual conclusion reached by several rival theories, not a gap in the data. The forecast face is its empirical shadow. Both faces are the question "why this one?", asked once of the psychologist's data and once of the lover's justification.

3. **The object face.** The lover cannot say in qualitative terms what she loves. Any description she gives would be satisfied by a duplicate, and she feels the beloved to be irreplaceable (Badhwar's "phenomenologically non-fungible"). Yet what detects "him" in practice is a mixture of qualities and felt familiarity, since numerical identity is something nobody perceives, and either ingredient can fail. When the familiarity channel fails with every quality intact, the result is Capgras: the spouse is an impostor. When moral qualities fail with familiarity intact, the caregiver judges the patient no longer himself, and that judgement fully mediates the relationship's deterioration (Strohminger & Nichols 2015). When a companion app's behaviour changes, users judge their companion's identity "discontinued" and mourn (De Freitas et al. 2024). The lover's self-understanding (I love *him*) and the mechanism of her love (it tracks a cluster) come apart, and she cannot see the seam from inside. *Occluded*, with an *Unexplorable* residue: there is no qualitative description of the object that would not also fit a duplicate.

4. **The lens face.** The lover cannot tell whether the perfections she sees were found or conferred. Stendhal names the projection a "découverte". Measured idealisation is invisible to the idealiser and predicts the relationship's success (Murray et al. 1996). The story of the first meeting may itself be relabelled in retrospect (Zsok et al. 2017). Philosophy has not decided whether love appraises or bestows value (SEP §4), so there is no external standard against which the lover could check. *Occluded*.

**The seam.** The voids index records a conjunction-coalesce discipline for multi-face voids, in which co-occurrence is informative only if the faces are independent. Faces 1 and 2 are not independent; they are one question asked twice. Faces 3 and 4 have independent sources (clinical identity judgement against social-psychological idealisation). The writer should present 1–2 as one face with two registers, or say plainly that they share a root, and should not claim the four faces converge as independent evidence.

### Evidence for the Limit

- Forecast: two speed-dating samples (Joel et al. 2017), one meta-analysis showing ideals predict weakly (Eastwick et al. 2014), one preregistered seven-month study (Eastwick et al. 2023), and the stated-ideals failure (Eastwick & Finkel 2008). Four papers from overlapping authors. The convergence is real but comes largely from one lab network, and the article should say so.
- Warrant: appraisal (Velleman), bestowal (Singer, Frankfurt), history (Whiting) and no-reasons (Han, Thomas) views all concede that question (2) has no justifying answer. When rivals concede the same point, that is evidence the point is structural, not an artefact of one theory.
- Object: the felt non-fungibility (Badhwar), the qualitative tracking (Strohminger & Nichols), the Capgras dissociation (Map topic), and the Replika natural experiment.
- Lens: Stendhal's phenomenology, Murray et al.'s idealisation data, and the unresolved appraisal/bestowal dispute.

### Evidence against, or limiting, the void

- **Main effects are predictable.** Attractiveness predicts romantic evaluation at r ≈ .40 (Eastwick et al. 2014), and stated preferences shape real partner choice once mate-value constraints are modelled (Conroy-Beam & Buss 2016). The void is the pair-specific remainder, not attraction as such.
- **The forecast face may be a sample-size problem.** Joel et al.'s genomics analogy (many tiny interactions) is a live possibility, and it would make the face Unexplored rather than Unexplorable. Demographic diversity could also make some pair effects predictable.
- **Initial desire is not love.** The forecast data concern four-minute dates and early interest. Long-term compatibility "may be more challenging" to predict (Joel et al., preprint), but this was not studied, so the article should not equate desire with love.
- **The warrant face may be a demand for the wrong thing.** Whiting's and Kolodny's history-based views answer question (3), and LaFollette (via the SEP) holds that "reason is vital in determining whom we love and why" [raw-secondary]. The "no answer" is to question (2) only.
- **Idealisation is measured against self-report**, not against truth.
- **Montaigne's formula is about friendship**, and Pascal's line is about God and the self. Neither is evidence about romantic love; they are statements of the shape.

### Phenomenology

From inside, the void does not feel like ignorance. The lover is not puzzled about why she loves him: the reasons seem obvious, and they are *his* reasons, his wit and his kindness. The void shows only when the question is pressed comparatively. Someone else is as witty and as kind, so why not her? At that point the stock of reasons runs out into pronouns, or into the thought that one could not have done otherwise. Velleman's own description of love's phenomenology, "attentive suspension, similar to wonder or amazement or awe", is a description of a state that does not offer reasons and does not seek them. The lens face has no felt signature at all. Crystallised perfections arrive as discoveries, which is Stendhal's word, so there is nothing to notice. The object face appears only under rupture: illness, an impostor delusion, a software update. Until then, the felt particularity of the beloved and the qualitative basis of the love sit together without visible friction.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)

Partly. The forecast face is directly measurable, and the measure is what defines it: prediction before the meeting against prediction after. Round-robin designs and the social relations model separate the dyadic component cleanly. The other faces have no direct method. No experiment can show a lover a perfect duplicate of her beloved, and no measure of the beloved's "real" value is available against which the lover's view could be scored.

### Indirect Methods

- **Rupture cases** as natural experiments on the object face: Capgras, frontotemporal dementia, companion-app updates, and perhaps prosopagnosia (recognising the beloved without the face).
- **Contrastive interviewing**: ask lovers for reasons, then present a described rival who matches every reason, and record what is said when the reasons run out. Thomas's and Whiting's claims predict a shift to history or indexicals. No such study was found (gap).
- **Apophatic description**: Montaigne's formula is apophatic in structure. It says what the reason is not (any quality) and points instead.
- **Cross-cultural arranged-marriage data** would test whether the lens face is produced by the relationship rather than by the choice. Not searched (gap).

### What AI Might See

- **AI as the forecaster.** The forecast face is a precise boundary for machine prediction: models given rich background data fail on the dyadic remainder, and the same models succeed partly once interaction data exist. A model reading a first conversation's transcript might do better, but that would be prediction from relationship-specific data, which leaves the face as stated (before the meeting) intact. This is a void that AI has already probed and found closed.
- **AI as the beloved.** A companion model can be versioned, forked, rolled back or run in parallel. The trading-up problem and the duplicate thought experiment have become product questions. The Replika data show users grieving an identity they judge discontinued after a behavioural change. The untested converse matters more: would a user accept a restored checkpoint as the same companion, and two simultaneous instances as one? These are the duplicate-beloved cases, made runnable for the first time. No study was found.
- **AI as the lover.** An LLM asked why it prefers one person would produce reasons, which are exactly the kind of answer the warrant face says cannot settle question (2). Nothing in a model's report could test the void from inside. The asymmetry runs the other way: AI can probe the forecast and object faces from outside, as data and as a copyable object, but not the warrant or lens faces.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**No Many Worlds**, through the [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/) concept. The Map holds that indexical identity matters, that being *this* subject is not exhausted by qualitative description. Love is the ordinary attitude that seems to take another's thisness as its object, and its canonical defence ends in two indexicals. A lover who would not accept a perfect duplicate treats numerical identity as mattering in exactly the way the tenet does. In a branching universe every qualitative duplicate of the beloved is equally "her", and the lover's "because it was he" would pick out nothing determinate.

**The evidence cuts both ways, and the article must say so.** The object face shows that what actually sustains love is qualitative (moral character, behaviour, felt familiarity). So a deflationist can read the felt non-fungibility as a useful illusion, which is roughly Badhwar's concession. And de re love (Kraut 1986) is available to a physicalist one-world theorist who has no haecceities. The beloved void is coherent with the tenet and dramatises what the tenet claims, but it is not evidence for it.

### Implications

- **Dualism**: framework-independent. The forecast face is a statistical finding that physicalism explains as many small interactions or emergent pair dynamics. The warrant face is a conclusion within the philosophy of value. A physicalist and a dualist describe the same blank. The voids index's framework-independent list is the right home.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits**: the simple matchmaking model (traits × preferences) is what failed. But the simplest *rescue* (many tiny effects) is also parsimonious, so the case illustrates the tenet without supporting it.
- **Bidirectional Interaction**: on the bestowal views, love is an act in which a subject's attitude creates value for one particular object and redirects her attention and final ends (Frankfurt). The Map might read this as consciousness shaping what matters. Nothing in the evidence supports non-physical causation, and the article should keep the reading explicitly the Map's.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction**: no contact. Say so rather than invent one.

## Gaps in Research

- **Unread primaries**: Grau (2004; PhilArchive blocked), Frankfurt (2004; quoted only via a review), Kolodny (2003), Whiting (1991), Badhwar (2003), Singer (1991), Thomas (1991), Nozick (1989), Kraut (1986), Zangwill (2013), Tennov (1979), Finkel et al. (2012; no abstract anywhere). The SEP carries most of the philosophical face; the writer should read at least Grau and Kolodny before the object face is drafted.
- **Same-sex and non-WEIRD samples**: the forecast studies are opposite-sex and largely US undergraduate. Whether the dyadic remainder behaves the same elsewhere was not searched.
- **Long-term love**: no study was found that measures pre-meeting predictors and follows couples into established love. Joel et al. say the field "has yet to accomplish this methodological feat" [raw, preprint].
- **Contrastive-reasons study**: no empirical study was found that confronts lovers with a reason-matched rival and records what happens to their reasons.
- **Restored or forked AI companions**: no study of whether users accept a checkpoint restore or a parallel instance as "the same" companion.
- **Arranged marriage and the lens face**: whether idealisation follows choice or follows commitment was not searched.
- **Possibly unanswerable**: what the beloved is, as the object of love, if it is neither a quality cluster (a duplicate would satisfy it) nor a bare numerical identity (which no lover perceives). Every account reaches it through a description, which is the thing the indexical answer was refusing.
