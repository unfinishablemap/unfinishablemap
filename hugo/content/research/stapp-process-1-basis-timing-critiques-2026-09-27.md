## Executive Summary

The two critiques strike the two halves of Stapp's Process 1, which are *which question* is asked (basis) and *when* it is asked (timing). Read at source, they are closer to each other than the Map's current one-line summaries suggest. **Donald (2003)** makes a *specification* objection. The Zeno effect can drive a state anywhere if someone chooses the right sequence of projections, but Stapp never says who builds those projections, how, or in what basis. They would have to be "defined to the precision of an individual wavefunction", which no biological mechanism can do. Donald also asks why mental choice should stop at the skull, which is a point the Map's `brain-specialness-boundary` does not credit to him. **Georgiev (2015)** makes a *basis dilemma* out of the timing objection. If the mind projects in a basis different from the decoherence pointer basis, its projections can only raise entropy, and the Zeno effect is gone within the decoherence time. If it projects in the same basis, decoherence already does the Zeno work and the mind is "redundant". His argument depends on the assumption that each collapse obeys the Born rule. **Stapp's 2012 reply** accepts that the mind's basis *is* the decoherence (coordinate) basis. He says the mind picks "some particular tiny portion" of the decohered diagonal strip, and that it rolls back the brain's own Schrödinger spreading rather than slowing decoherence. **de Barros (2014)** adds a circularity charge: choosing a basis requires physical apparatus, so the mind would have to act on matter in order to act on matter. He reports a fix due to Kathryn Laskey in which the *brain* supplies a cycling basis and the mind chooses only *whether and when* to observe.

For the Map, the result is that "question-choice" narrows under pressure to **consent/timing choice over a physically supplied basis**. Stapp's own 2000 wording, as Donald quotes it, already says this: "the choices to consent or not consent … to putting to nature the question". The Born-intact question-choice row on `born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface` is the version that Georgiev's theorem targets directly, because the theorem assumes Born-consistent collapses. The row is not refuted, but its "more minimal" status is paid for by leaving the basis unspecified.

## Key Sources

### Donald, M.J. (2003). *On the Work of Henry P. Stapp.* quant-ph/0311158
- **URL**: https://www.mjdquantum.uk/stapp.pdf (arXiv: quant-ph/0311158, November 2003)
- **Type**: Critical review article (author: Cavendish Laboratory, Cambridge; a many-minds theorist)
- **Key points**:
  - *Decoherence premise, shared with Stapp.* A 10⁻⁵ s difference in when a neuron fires is "macroscopic on the picosecond timescale of molecular vibrations" (verbatim), so the branches decohere almost at once into ρ = p₁ρ₁ + p₂ρ₂. As a result, "no conventional physical process within the brain will be able to cause a (generalized) 'collapse' from ρ to either ρ1 or ρ2" (verbatim).
  - *Zeno as a trick with no builder.* Carefully chosen projections can drive the state towards ρ₂ with probability one, but "That dynamics, however, is not the well-understood biologically-evolved dynamics … It is a purely theoretical dynamics which depends on working towards the desired outcome right from the start" (verbatim). Also: "How and when the choice is supposed to result in the construction and action of the projection operators still has to be explained. The projections need to be defined to the precision of an individual wavefunction. Above atomic scales, no biological mechanism can control or even repeat states at this level of precision." (verbatim)
  - *Apparatus demand.* A laboratory Zeno effect needs apparatus (Itano et al. 1990). So "Stapp needs to tell us what apparatus biology has built into our brains which allows us to use the quantum Zeno effect to make choices." Otherwise Stapp has "invented a homunculus with access to a space of projection operators defined on an atomic scale" (both verbatim).
  - *No link between neural representation and projection.* "My basic objection to Stapp's work is that he does not appear to have made any connection between the representation of choices by physical neural processing – or indeed any other cellular process – and their representation as projection operators to be measured." (verbatim)
  - *Boundary question (not credited on the Map).* "Why should they be restricted to changes within a brain? If mental forces can effectively decide the trajectories of atoms or molecules inside a brain, why can they not decide the trajectories of electrons in a laboratory or of prey in the ocean? What determined the point in evolutionary history when brains are supposed to have started to be able to make choices?" (verbatim)
  - *Preferred-basis demand, stated generally.* A theory of collapse "should tell us what possibilities arise – this is 'the preferred basis problem'". With such a theory, "it will no longer be possible to invoke the quantum Zeno effect as a clever trick by which arbitrary sequences of collapses can be used to attain any desired outcome" (verbatim).
  - *Born statistics not shown.* Donald quotes Stapp (1995) as admitting "No attempt is made here to show that the quantum statistical laws will hold for the aspects of the brain's internal dynamics controlled by conscious thoughts". **Secondary.** This is Donald quoting Stapp and has not been checked against Stapp 1995. Donald links it to the energy-conservation claim: average energy is conserved only if "causal effectiveness" gives "the same averages as conventional quantum probabilities".
  - *Stapp's own consent wording.* Donald quotes Stapp (2000b): "there is one element that [is] not governed by any known law of physics, namely the choices to consent or not consent at time t to putting to nature the question associated with the possible experience E(t)". **Secondary**, not checked against Stapp 2000b.
- **Tenet alignment**: Donald's own framework conflicts with Tenet 4 (many-minds) and with Tenet 3. He describes it as "epiphenomenalistic in the sense that a mind does not direct a pattern of observed physical events" (verbatim). His *criticisms* of Stapp do not depend on that framework, and a dualist has to answer them on their own terms.
- **Correction to the Map's gloss**: `brain-specialness-boundary` L67 says Donald "questions whether the required projections have a well-defined basis at all". That is a fair compression of the preferred-basis paragraph, but it leaves out his sharper points. Those are the *precision* point (wavefunction-level projections that biology cannot build), the *representation* gap (no mapping from neural choice to projector), and the *boundary* question, which is the very subject of the page that cites him.

### Georgiev, D.D. (2015). Monte Carlo simulation of quantum Zeno effect in the brain. *IJMPB* 29(7), 1550039
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.4741 · DOI 10.1142/S0217979215500393 (Crossref: sole author Georgiev; vol. 29, issue 07, article 1550039; published 2015-03-02)
- **Type**: Physics paper (simulation + theorem)
- **Key points**:
  - The model is a triple-well electron in an ion-channel voltage sensor. With no decoherence, repeated projective position measurements do produce a Zeno effect. With decoherence in a *different* (energy) pointer basis, the state behaves like a "random telegraph" and the Zeno effect breaks down at t ≈ τ (the decoherence time). Abstract (verbatim): "The simulations unambiguously showed that the quantum Zeno effect breaks down for timescales greater than the brain decoherence time."
  - *Theorem 4*: local projective measurements "cannot decrease the von Neumann entropy of the unconditional density matrix" (verbatim). Georgiev says this generalises the result to any n-level system and answers Stapp's "two-state model" complaint.
  - *Collapse doesn't help.* Averaging Born-weighted collapses gives back the unconditional density matrix. "Both collapse and no collapse approaches are restrained by the quantum informational theorem 4 because, if the probabilities for all collapse outcomes are consistent with the Born rule, one could statistically average over all possible outcomes … to get the very same unconditional density matrix" (verbatim). His lottery analogy: a conditional success such as winning the jackpot does not make a scheme efficacious.
  - **The basis dilemma** (the core point for this task): "If the decoherence pointer basis happens to be the one in which the mind action is intended, then the decoherence alone can achieve the quantum Zeno effect and the mind action will be irrelevant or redundant." And: "there could be no free will if the mind cannot choose the basis in which the projective measurement is performed." (both verbatim) If the mind's basis is mutually unbiased with the pointer basis, probability falls to 1/n straight after the first projection.
  - *Door left open*: a decoherence-free subspace, used by unitaries that "'hide' the initial state" and later restore it. He calls this "admittedly speculative" (verbatim), and the question then becomes why a *subspace* of the brain escapes decoherence.
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with Tenet 3 *as mediated by Stapp's Zeno mechanism*. The theorem is conditional on Born-consistent, brain-local projections. It therefore leaves two routes open: Born-bending models (Tenet 2's minimum-outside-the-corridor row) and post-decoherence selection, which does not rely on repeated projection.

### Georgiev, D.D. (2012). Mind efforts, quantum Zeno effect and environmental decoherence. *NeuroQuantology* 10(3)
- Already cited on the Map (`stapp-quantum-mind`). It is the paper Stapp's 2012 reply answers. The two-level (photon polarisation) entropy argument is summarised in Georgiev 2015 §1. Not re-read here.

### Georgiev, D.D. (2015). No-go theorem for Stapp's quantum Zeno model of mind-brain interaction. *NeuroQuantology* 13(2). DOI 10.14704/nq.2015.13.2.839
- **Abstract read only** (OpenAlex). It restates Theorem 4 with simulations and "answer[s] various issues raised by Stapp in his reply". A search-engine summary attributes to the abstract the phrase "not robust against environmental decoherence if the mind is supposed not to violate the Born rule and to act only locally at the brain", with Psi effects as the only patch. **This wording is not verified verbatim.** It is consistent with the verified IJMPB text above.

### Stapp, H.P. (2012). Reply to a Critic: "Mind Efforts, Quantum Zeno Effect and Environmental Decoherence". *NeuroQuantology* 10(4), 601–605. DOI 10.14704/nq.2012.10.4.619
- **URL**: https://www-physics.lbl.gov/~stapp/Reply.doc (draft; page range from the OpenAlex abstract footer)
- **Key points** (draft text; see verification note above):
  - Stapp accepts that decoherence turns superpositions into mixtures and says his model "does not use interference effects between superposed states". The brain density matrix is "reduced to near-diagonal form in the coordinate-space basis".
  - **He concedes the basis.** Replying to Georgiev's charge that Stapp's model has the environment and the mind measuring in the same basis, he writes: "Yes, the decoherence basis is the coordinate basis, and the projection operator P might be a projection onto a certain particular interval in coordinate space. But this does not make the latter redundant with the former … The action of the environment reduces the nonzero part of the brain density matrix essentially to the long strip along the diagonal, whereas the action of the mind picks out some particular tiny portion of that strip."
  - **Mechanism restated.** Mind does not slow decoherence. It "rolls back the brain-based process of evolution", meaning the Schrödinger spreading of narrow Gaussians away from the interval that the last Process-1 choice picked out. He agrees that Georgiev's claim that Zeno cannot slow decoherence "is certainly correct, but in no way contradicts my model".
  - Freedom glossed minimally: "The adjective “free” means only not determined by any known law or rule."
  - He calls the Process-1 "postulates" "neither novel nor mine", identifying them with the Copenhagen "free choice on the part of the experimenter".
- **What the reply does not answer**: it gives no account of how the mind's "tiny portion" (interval and grain) is specified. That is exactly Donald's question. It also does not say whether projecting onto an interval of a density matrix that is *already* near-diagonal in that basis changes the *unconditional* statistics. That is Georgiev's lottery point: under Born weighting, the mind's effect lives only in conditional histories.

### Stapp, H.P. (2015). Reply to Georgiev: No-Go for Georgiev's No-Go Theorem. *NeuroQuantology* 13(2). DOI 10.14704/nq.2015.13.2.851
- **Abstract read only** (OpenAlex), verbatim: "I give here a simple proof that environmental decoherence does not nullify the quantum Zeno effect." The body was not located, and this version is not on Stapp's LBL directory, whose latest files date from 2016. **This is a gap.** Whether the "simple proof" deals with the unconditional-statistics point or the redundancy horn is unknown.

### de Barros, J.A. (2014). On a Model of Quantum Mechanics and the Mind. arXiv:1404.0714
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.0714 (v2, 16 Apr 2014; "To appear in the Proceedings of the Foundations of Mind Conference, Berkeley, 2014"; no DOI or published version found)
- **Key points**:
  - *Circularity*: a measurement in a given basis needs physical apparatus, "the Stern-Gerlach setup needs to be there; it cannot be produced by the mind" (verbatim). The mind "does not have a preferred pointer basis" and so "we get into a circular argument: to solve the problem of how the mind affects matter, we need to postulate that the mind affects matter" (verbatim).
  - *The Laskey fix* (attributed by de Barros to a private communication from Kathryn Laskey): the *brain* runs a cyclic apparatus whose measurement basis rotates in time. "The mind, in this case, can choose to observe or not the system, and can also choose when to observe the system" (verbatim). de Barros notes the fix "could in principle be observable".
  - He also argues that dynamical decoherence removes von Neumann's original motive for a mind-triggered collapse (supporting remark only).
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral to mildly hostile (de Barros prefers non-quantum alternatives). The Laskey fix is the most useful constructive result here. It matches Stapp's own "consent" wording and the Map's `coupling-modes` timing-control mode.

## Major Positions

### Stapp: Process 1 as free question-choice (later narrowed to interval-plus-timing)
- **Core claim**: the mind chooses which yes/no question is put and when. Nature answers by the Born rule, and rapid repetition Zeno-holds the chosen interval against the brain's own spreading.
- **Under pressure (2012 reply)**: the basis is the decoherence (coordinate) basis. What remains free is which interval, at what grain, and when.
- **Relation to tenets**: this is the Born-intact question-choice row. It is the most minimal under Tenet 2, but its minimality depends on leaving the choice-to-projector map unspecified.

### Donald: specification failure
- **Core claim**: without a characterisation of which projections occur, the Zeno appeal is "a clever trick", and the choice mechanism is a homunculus.
- **Relation to tenets**: the Map's dualism *accepts* a non-physical chooser, so the homunculus charge lands less hard here than on Stapp, who (per Donald) disowned Eccles-style dualism. The precision and representation points still stand for any dualist: *what does the chooser choose among, and how is that set fixed?*

### Georgiev: basis dilemma under Born-consistent collapse
- **Core claim**: either the mind's basis differs from the pointer basis, so its projections raise entropy and do nothing useful, or it matches, so decoherence does the work and the mind is redundant.
- **Relation to tenets**: this is the strongest pressure on the Zeno route under Tenets 2 and 3 together. It leaves post-decoherence selection and Born-bending untouched.

### de Barros/Laskey: basis from brain, timing from mind
- **Core claim**: the only non-circular Process 1 lets physical brain dynamics fix the basis and gives the mind a choice of whether and when to observe.
- **Relation to tenets**: this matches the `coupling-modes` timing-control mode and `concepts/agency-budget`'s question-choice reading. It is minimal and has an in-principle observable signature.

## Key Debates

### Is the mind redundant when it shares the decoherence basis?
- **Sides**: Georgiev (yes, "irrelevant or redundant") vs. Stapp (no, the mind picks a "tiny portion" of the strip).
- **Core disagreement**: whether projections finer than the decoherence grain, onto a density matrix that is near-diagonal in the same basis, change *unconditional* outcome statistics under Born weighting. They can hold a conditional history in place, but that may not be the same thing.
- **Current state**: Unresolved in print as far as could be found. Stapp's 2015 "simple proof" is unread. No worked model on either side quantifies the interval grain against the decoherence width. **This is the crux a Map article should state, not settle.**

### Who specifies the projector?
- **Sides**: Donald and de Barros (someone has to, and neither biology nor a basis-less mind can) vs. Stapp (the free choice is primitive, as in Copenhagen).
- **Current state**: the Laskey fix and Stapp's "consent" wording both move the specification onto the brain. Once that move is made, "question-choice" becomes "timing/consent choice".

### Why only brains?
- **Sides**: Donald ("electrons in a laboratory or … prey in the ocean") vs. the Map's `brain-specialness-boundary` (control-loop integration: representation of alternatives before collapse).
- **Current state**: the Map's page already *answers* Donald's boundary question with its representation criterion, but it credits him only with the basis point. The representation criterion is itself Donald's representation gap run in reverse. The page says the mind can only select among alternatives the brain represents, and Donald says Stapp gives no map from those representations to projectors. The Map's answer is only as good as that map.

## Bearing on the Map's question-choice row (`born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface` ~L165)

1. **Georgiev's theorem targets exactly this row.** His argument assumes collapses are "consistent with the Born rule". The Born-intact question-choice reading is therefore the model the no-go is built against, not a way around it. The row's text, "It faces no Born dilemma", is true, but it faces the *basis dilemma* in its place. The row should say so.
2. **Born-intact does not mean statistically inert.** Choosing which question is asked and when can change the distribution of brain-state histories while every individual answer obeys the Born rule. That is the whole point of the Zeno mechanism. Georgiev's claim is that under decoherence this lever vanishes when judged unconditionally. The row's "fixes what is asked rather than what happens" is therefore too strong on the favourable reading of Stapp, and exactly right on Georgiev's reading. The row should keep the two apart.
3. **Under pressure, "which question" narrows to consent/timing.** This comes from Stapp's own 2012 concession (coordinate basis), his quoted 2000 wording ("consent or not consent"), and the Laskey fix. The row currently glosses Process 1 as "which question is put and when". A more accurate gloss is *when and whether a physically supplied question is put*, with basis choice left as an open specification debt (Donald).
4. **Donald's "Born statistics not shown" remark cuts against calling Stapp's reading cleanly Born-intact.** This rests on his quoted Stapp 1995, which is unverified. The pages edited today (free-will, quantum-measurement-and-consciousness, consciousness-and-probability-interpretation) say that Stapp's own reading is question-choice, Born-intact. That is consistent with Stapp's 2012 reply ("random chance enters only in connection with nature's response"). But the same page also lists "Stapp's quantum Zeno" in the Born-bending row ("shifting selection probabilities away from non-conscious outcomes"). Georgiev's dilemma explains why both placements exist. The mechanism does work only on conditional histories, if it works at all, and whether that amounts to shifting probabilities depends on whether one judges it conditionally or unconditionally. A future edit could turn this apparent double-listing into a stated fork.
