## Executive Summary

Martial, Fritz and five co-authors published "A neuroscientific model of near-death experiences" in *Nature Reviews Neurology* in 2025. It is a narrative review that ends by proposing NEPTUNE, a model in which the energy crisis of cardiac arrest raises cortical excitability and releases a set of neurotransmitters, each assigned to an NDE feature, with psychological predisposition and an evolutionary rationale attached. The acronym is confirmed at source. The model is a synthesis and a research programme, and its authors say so: it is "theoretical at present", no biological signature of an NDE has been found, and the timing of the experience relative to the arrest is undetermined. Most of the mechanistic evidence comes from asphyxiated rats, psychedelic and ketamine analogues, syncope in healthy volunteers, and electrical stimulation or epilepsy. The human dying-brain recordings it cites come from patients who died without being interviewed. Dualist theories are excluded at the start as a matter of method. Five published critiques were found: three correspondences in the same journal (O'Grady and Varghese; Engmann; Parnia and ten co-authors), each answered by an authors' reply; a critique by Greyson and Pehlivanova in *Psychology of Consciousness*; and a letter by Delgado-Ron against the evolutionary leg. No reply by van Lommel was found.

## What is new relative to `concepts/near-death-experiences`

The page's §Materialist Explanations (L112) lists five mechanisms in 57 words with no citation and a one-clause dismissal each. Against that paragraph, the source adds or changes the following.

1. **An integrated rival exists and the page does not cite it.** The five mechanisms are no longer offered separately; NEPTUNE combines them and adds serotonin (5-HT1A for calm, 5-HT2A for hallucination), dopamine (hyper-reality), GABA, noradrenaline and acetylcholine (memory encoding).
2. **The ketamine item changes.** The page has "ketamine-like NMDA blockade (phenomenologically similar but not established as causal)". The review goes further: Jansen's endogenous ketamine-like agent has no evidence behind it, and NMDA antagonism disrupts memory encoding, which sits badly with a vividly recalled experience. The review relocates the ketamine resemblance to glutamate excess under hypoxia.
3. **The page's parenthetical dismissals are partly answered and partly conceded by the authors.** "REM intrusion (… but not coherent narratives)" and "endorphin release (… but not complex narrative structure)" treat each mechanism as a sole cause. NEPTUNE's reply is that no single mechanism is meant to carry the whole experience. The authors still concede that the combination needed is unknown (quotation 7 below).
4. **The page's RED terminology sentence is contested by this group.** L57 says a 2022 consensus "established" Recalled Experience of Death as "the preferred terminology". The Liège group rejects the term in print (2022 and again in 2026; see the Parnia exchange below). The page presents as settled what is one side of a live dispute. This is outside the fold plan and is listed under [Follow-ups not folded](#follow-ups-not-folded).
5. **The page's van Lommel quotation uses "clinically dead"**, and the authors' reply to O'Grady and Varghese objects to exactly that usage in van Lommel et al. (2001). The page already hedges the inference; the terminological objection is new to it.
6. **Nothing here bears on the three corrections in the open P3** from the same review (Reynolds timeline at L106, veridical-hit scoring at L166/L178, Whitehead at L156). This note does not touch them.

### Martial et al. (2025), the review

- **Citation**: Martial, C., Fritz, P., Gosseries, O., Bonhomme, V., Kondziella, D., Nelson, K. & Lejeune, N. (2025). A neuroscientific model of near-death experiences. *Nature Reviews Neurology*, 21(6), 297–311. Published online 31 March 2025. Martial and Fritz contributed equally. PMID 40159547.
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41582-025-01072-z (paywalled). Full text read from the publisher-formatted PDF on ORBi: https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/330124/1/s41582-025-01072-z_Nature.pdf (26 pages, about 15,100 words including references). Abstract also at PubMed.
- **Metadata**: verified at Crossref and PubMed (volume 21, issue 6, pages 297–311, seven authors).
- **Type**: narrative review article with a proposed model. No literature-search criteria are stated (Engmann's reply makes this point; I found none either).
- **Correction**: Author Correction, *Nat Rev Neurol* 21(8), 465, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41582-025-01111-9 (read in full). It changes one word in Box 2: DMT's precursor is tryptophan, where the original said tyrosine. The ORBi PDF is the uncorrected version. Nothing else was corrected.
- **Tenet alignment**: conflicts with Dualism and Bidirectional Interaction by stipulation (see quotation 1). Neutral on the other three.

**The model, in the authors' words**

1. On scope: "We have excluded dualistic theories from our discussion owing to the lack of empirical neuroscientific evidence and the fact that a fundamental tenet of neuroscience asserts that human experience arises from the brain".
2. The name: "Neurophysiological Evolutionary Psychological Theory Understanding Near-death Experience (NEPTUNE; Fig. 3)".
3. The cascade: "NEPTUNE assumes a cascade of concomitant psychological and neurophysiological processes, which might start with systemic hypotension, oxygen deprivation, elevated CO2 levels, disruption of ATP production, and a decreased pH, culminating in cerebral acidosis."
4. The restriction: "For the sake of clarity, the model focuses specifically on the prototypical scenario cardiac arrest".
5. The definition (abstract): NDEs are "episodes of disconnected consciousness".

The assignments, paraphrased from the model section and Fig. 3: energy deprivation leads to raised excitability in mesiotemporal, occipital, insular and temporoparietal–occipital cortex; calm comes from 5-HT1A activity, endorphins and GABA; vivid visual hallucination from 5-HT2A hyperactivation and dopamine; the sense of hyper-reality from dopamine; memory encoding from noradrenaline, acetylcholine and glutamate; REM-intrusion and dissociation propensity act as individual predispositions; dissociation is read as a defence with an evolutionary role, following Peinkhofer et al. (2021) on thanatosis (death-feigning). The 5-HT2A hallucinatory component is described as a possible evolutionary by-product, not an adaptation.

**Gaps and limits stated by the authors**

6. Status: "Our NEPTUNE model, although theoretical at present, provides a foundation for the next research phase, which will entail empirical testing of each mechanism."
7. Open questions: "Despite our efforts to develop a comprehensive model, some questions remain: for example, what combinations of the above-mentioned processes are necessary and/or sufficient to trigger an NDE? Also, although our model might effectively explain certain key NDE features, it does not cover other features, such as precognition."
8. No marker: "Although no biological signature for NDEs has been identified, evidence from both animal and human studies might help us to formulate strong hypotheses."
9. Timing: "the exact timing of NDE occurrence — before, during and/or after (that is, during recovery from) the cardiac arrest — remains open to debate".
10. Veridical perception: "no empirical research to date has used sufficiently rigorous methodology to objectively verify whether these perceptions corresponded to actual external stimuli".
11. Out-of-body experience: of temporoparietal stimulation, "This phenomenology does not precisely match what is recalled during NDEs"; and "to date, no study has been able to fully replicate the intricate OBE phenomenon commonly reported by NDE experiencers".
12. Feature coverage: "The model that we present below effectively addresses the perception-related features that can be linked to one or more neurotransmitter systems or neurophysiological processes. However, the interpretive features tend to involve additional processes".
13. Psychological leg: "Importantly, however, these findings are mostly from retrospective studies"; and "Acknowledging the speculative nature of these hypotheses, our aim is to provide a conceptual framework that integrates existing evidence and highlights testable hypotheses".
14. Evolutionary leg (Box 1): "Evolutionary theories are also difficult to test".
15. Particular mechanisms: "Determining the precise role of glutamate in NDEs remains challenging, but we tentatively suggest that it could be involved in cognitive dissociation." On distressing "void" experiences: "The identification of neurochemical explanations for void NDEs remains challenging."
16. Ketamine: of Jansen's endogenous ketamine-like neurotoxin, "To date, however, no empirical evidence has been found to support the existence of such a molecule."
17. Dying-brain surges (Xu et al. 2023): the findings should "be interpreted cautiously as no interview was possible owing to the imminent death of the patients". The review also reports rodent intracellular work that points away from the surge and suggests "that the slow recovery of brain function following resuscitation could represent a more relevant time window for generating NDEs".
18. AWARE II (their ref. 10, the 2023 in-hospital resuscitation study): methodological limitations "have rendered the findings inconclusive".
19. Brain death: "The absence of brain activity precludes the possibility of consciousness and, hence, NDEs, which are survived events by definition."
20. On dualist theories (Box 1): "dualistic theories lack robust tools for empirical validation, and the few empirical studies that have attempted to test them were not able to exclude alternative non-dualistic hypotheses".

One oddity, recorded and not to be used: the Fig. 3 label in the ORBi PDF reads "increased pO2" under physiological stress, while the body text describes oxygen deprivation and raised CO2. The Author Correction does not mention it. I could not check the publisher's current figure.

### Evidential grade of the model's components

This is my tabulation from the review's text. "Source of evidence" follows what the review itself cites.

| Component | Source of evidence as cited | Measured in dying or resuscitated humans? |
|---|---|---|
| Prevalence (3% after traumatic brain injury, 15% after prolonged ICU stay, 10–23% after cardiac arrest) | Eight prospective studies plus one aortic-surgery study | Yes: survivors interviewed afterwards |
| Blood gas, ATP and acidosis cascade | General physiology of arrest and ischaemia | The physiology is established; its link to the experience is inferred |
| Serotonin surge ("up to 100–200 times" baseline) | Asphyxiated rats (Li et al., their ref. 31) | No |
| Dopamine and noradrenaline surges | Asphyxiated and CO2-asphyxiated rats | No |
| Endorphin release | Dogs undergoing sudden death | No |
| 5-HT2A hallucination and DMT overlap | Psychedelic studies; one placebo-controlled DMT study | No: healthy volunteers |
| Ketamine similarity | Semantic comparison of written reports across 165 substances; sheep EEG | No |
| Temporoparietal contribution to out-of-body experience | Intracranial stimulation and epilepsy cases | No: and the authors say the match is imprecise (quotation 11) |
| REM intrusion | Questionnaire studies of experiencers after the fact | Predisposition only; measured long after the event |
| Delta/theta signature | Vasovagal syncope in healthy volunteers (2024) | No: "NDEs-like" in a harmless faint |
| Terminal gamma surge and connectivity | Chawla et al. 2009; Xu et al. 2023 (two of four patients) | Yes, but no patient survived to report (quotation 17) |
| Dissociation and fantasy proneness | Mostly retrospective trait studies | Measured after the experience |
| Thanatosis as evolutionary origin | Peinkhofer et al. 2021; receptor sequence conservation | Not testable directly, by the authors' account (quotation 14) |

The summary that follows from the table: the one class of direct human recording at the relevant moment (dying-brain EEG) has no reports attached, and the one class of human reports (survivor interviews) has no concurrent recording that the authors accept as conclusive. Everything connecting the two is inference from analogues. The authors do not hide this; quotations 6, 8 and 9 say it. A page should report it as the stated status of a young research programme. It is not a refutation, and a model can be correct while still untested.

### The replies

**(a) O'Grady, G. & Varghese, C. (2025). Limitations of neurocentric models for near-death experiences. *Nat Rev Neurol* 21(8), 461.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41582-025-01117-3. Authors are at the Department of Surgery, University of Auckland. Paywalled; only the publisher's free preview (first two paragraphs, 227 words) and the reference list were read.
- Their thesis, from the preview: the model "omits key phenomenological features that are central to the core NDE experience and overextends certain neurochemical correlates into causal explanations".
- The preview lists the features they mean: veridical out-of-body observations, transitions to other realms, panoramic life review, encounters with deceased relatives, lasting transformation, cross-cultural consistency.
- Their reference list includes Greyson (2007, 2010), van Lommel (2001, 2011), Parnia (2014, 2017, 2022) and Kelly et al., *Irreducible Mind*. The rest of their argument is known to me only through the authors' reply.

**(b) Martial et al. (2025). Reply to (a). *Nat Rev Neurol* 21(8), 462.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41582-025-01119-1. Read in full from ORBi: https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/333595/1/be8392ab-400c-49be-8da2-b8b6c0efbdd4.pdf
- On veridical perception: "no study has yet provided robust evidence of real-world perception that cannot be explained by neurophysiological explanations during unresponsive states".
- A concession with a physicalist reading attached. They "do not dismiss this possibility" and find it plausible "that some perceptual access might persist in these critical states, though not necessarily visual, and not through extracorporeal mechanisms". They tie this to "connected consciousness", covert awareness of the environment during behavioural unresponsiveness, as documented under general anaesthesia.
- On the limits of the field: "Although our understanding of how complex consciousness emerges from brain matter remains incomplete, we are surprised by the resistance to recent neuroscientific models in this field."
- On flat EEG: "We reiterate the inherent limitations of EEG in terms of depth and spatial resolution".
- On "clinical death" in van Lommel et al. (2001): "In near-death cases, the brain that supports consciousness has not died".
- They argue NDEs fit the DSM-5 definition of hallucination and that memory reconstruction shapes the reports.

**(c) Engmann, B. (2025). Perils and pitfalls of near-death experience research. *Nat Rev Neurol* 21(8), 463.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41582-025-01118-2. Author is at the Karl Sudhoff Institute of the History of Medicine and Science, Leipzig. Paywalled; preview (176 words) and reference list read.
- Engmann is a sceptic of NDE research generally, and his critique comes from the opposite side to (a). From the preview: "the attribution of complex psychic phenomena to neurotransmitters is perilous, to say the least".
- He objects that the review states no literature-search criteria and does not define NDE, and that NDE scales exclude most experiences and privilege transcendental content: "This flaw invalidates most NDE studies."
- From the authors' reply, he also questions the model's novelty and the evolutionary account.

**(d) Martial et al. (2025). Reply to (c). *Nat Rev Neurol* 21(8), 464.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41582-025-01120-8. Read in full from ORBi: https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/333596/1/Martial_et_al-2025-Nature_Reviews_Neurology.pdf
- They accept the caution against reduction: "This concern echoes the enduring ‘hard problem of consciousness’". They add: "we argue that proposing neurochemical hypotheses remains an essential step in the iterative process of scientific inquiry".
- On the evolutionary leg, a narrowing: "Our hypothesis suggests a possible but not universal adaptive origin for certain components of NDEs."
- On dualist theories: "these theories fall outside our empirical focus and belong to domains such as religion and philosophy".

**(e) Parnia, S., Patel, J., Kulstad, E., Spiegel, R., Post, S. G., Alilou, S., Bloom, B. M., Leontovich, N., Gonzales, A. M., Keshavarz, T. & Deakin, C. D. (2026). Recalled experiences of death: need for empirical research without prejudice. *Nat Rev Neurol* 22(3), 196–197.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41582-026-01186-y. Published online 11 February 2026. Paywalled; preview (125 words), reference list and acknowledgements read.
- From the preview: "A more accurate term proposed by a consensus of scientists is recalled experiences of death (REDs)".
- From the acknowledgements and the authors' reply: Parnia and colleagues propose functional disinhibition and the activation of dormant neural pathways near death, and they dispute that standard NDE scales and semantic-similarity studies show real overlap with psychedelic states. Their own text on these points was not read.

**(f) Fritz, P., Martial, C. et al. (2026). Reply to (e). *Nat Rev Neurol* 22(3), 198.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41582-026-01187-x. Read in full from ORBi: https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/339352/3/Fritz_et_al-2026-Nature_Reviews_Neurology.pdf
- On the term RED: it "presupposes that the experiencers were dead", and "Because the RED concept relies on scientific inaccuracies, it cannot replace the term NDE".
- On psychedelic overlap: "semantic overlap does not imply shared ontological status".
- On Parnia's dormant pathways: "No empirical evidence from either humans or animals demonstrates the existence of such putative pathways and their activation during physiological crises."
- On what a mechanism shows: "the presence of neurobiological correlates does not diminish their subjective reality".
- The earlier statement of the terminology objection is Martial, C., Gosseries, O., Cassol, H. & Kondziella, D. (2022). Studying death and near-death experiences requires neuroscientific expertise. *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences*, 1517(1), 11–14. https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.14888 (metadata verified at Crossref; text not read).

**(g) Greyson, B. & Pehlivanova, M. (2025). A neuroscientific model of near-death experiences reconsidered. *Psychology of Consciousness: Theory, Research, and Practice*. Advance online publication, 30 October 2025.** https://doi.org/10.1037/cns0000448. Metadata verified at Crossref (no volume or pages assigned; licence CC BY-NC-ND). **Full text not obtained**: the APA site returned a bot challenge on every route tried, and no repository copy was found. What I know of its content is from a press release and is listed under [Unverified leads](#unverified-leads-do-not-quote).

**(h) Delgado-Ron, J. A. (2026). Reappraising the claims of evolutionary continuity between thanatosis and near-death experiences. *Brain Communications*, 8(5), fcag368.** https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcag368. Letter to the Editor, published 30 September 2026. Full text read via PMC efetch (PMC13655614). PMID 42865712.
- Its stated target is Peinkhofer et al. (2021), the paper NEPTUNE's evolutionary leg rests on. PubMed and OpenAlex both list it as citing the 2025 review, and it discusses Martial et al.'s dissociation hypothesis by name. It is a critique of the model's evolutionary foundation, not of the model as a whole.
- Conclusion: "I would argue not only that thanatosis and NDEs are not related (phenomenologically, through common cause, or through their effects), but also that the hypothesis that one constitutes the evolutionary foundation of the other remains highly speculative."
- Evidence used: "WP2 shows that only 14% of NDEs are associated with ‘predator’ attacks (including inanimate objects)"; tonic immobility in humans is linked to later PTSD while NDEs are linked to improved functioning, so "the long-term effects of NDEs and TI are not only different, but the opposite of each other".

## Major Positions

### NEPTUNE (integrative physicalism)
- **Proponents**: Martial, Fritz, Gosseries, Bonhomme, Lejeune (Liège); Kondziella (Copenhagen); Nelson (Kentucky).
- **Core claim**: NDEs are disconnected-consciousness episodes generated by a brain in energy crisis, through a cascade of excitability and transmitter release, shaped by predisposition and by top-down expectation, with a possible adaptive origin for some components.
- **Strength at full statement**: it is the first account that assigns the major pleasant-NDE features to named mechanisms inside one framework, it explains why NDE-like experiences occur without danger (syncope, drugs), it makes each assignment separately testable, and it accommodates reports of accurate perception through residual brain function ("connected consciousness") without leaving physiology.
- **Relation to site tenets**: it assumes the negation of Dualism as a methodological starting point (quotation 1). It does not argue against dualism from its data, and its authors place dualist theories in "religion and philosophy". The Map can report the model accurately and still hold its tenets, since the hard-problem arguments the tenets rest on are independent of NDE physiology, as the page's L114 already says.

### Phenomenological critics (O'Grady and Varghese; Greyson and Pehlivanova)
- **Core claim**: the model explains components and leaves the integrated, lucid, transformative and allegedly veridical character of NDEs unexplained.
- **Relation to site tenets**: sympathetic to a non-production reading. Evidential status is weak where it matters most to the Map: the authors' counter (reply b) that no controlled study has shown perception that neurophysiology cannot explain matches what the Map's own page reports about AWARE.

### RED programme (Parnia and colleagues)
- **Core claim**: the experiences are of death itself, are distinct from psychedelic and other states, and may arise from disinhibition of dormant pathways.
- **Note**: Parnia's own mechanism is also neural. This exchange is a dispute between two brain-based accounts and over terminology. It should not be cited as dualist criticism of NEPTUNE.

### Methodological sceptic (Engmann)
- **Core claim**: the NDE category and its scales are too loose for any neurochemical mapping to be valid.

### Evolutionary critic (Delgado-Ron)
- **Core claim**: thanatosis and NDEs differ in phenomenology, cause and after-effects, and the 2021 evidence for continuity was hypothesis-generating at best.

## Key Debates

### Does similarity of report imply shared mechanism?
- **Sides**: Martial group (semantic similarity across large narrative corpora justifies the psychedelic model) against Parnia and Greyson (the resemblance is superficial).
- **Current state**: ongoing. The authors themselves limit the inference: "semantic overlap does not imply shared ontological status".

### When does the experience happen?
- **Sides**: the review leaves it open (quotation 9) and leans toward the recovery period on rodent evidence (quotation 17). Critics on the phenomenological side take it to be during arrest.
- **Current state**: unresolved; no human data fix the timing. The Map's page already makes this point in its RED paragraph and its illusionism section.

### What counts as death in "near-death"?
- **Sides**: Parnia (cardiac arrest is death that is sometimes reversed) against the Liège group (death is irreversible cessation of brain function, so no experiencer was dead).
- **Current state**: ongoing since 2022.
