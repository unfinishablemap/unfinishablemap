## Executive Summary

Cognitive offloading — delegating a mental operation to an external device — is well established as
a *behaviour* and increasingly documented as having *costs*. The philosophically interesting claim,
and the one that makes this a void rather than a topic, is that offloading appears to produce a gap
that **conceals itself**: across nine experiments Fisher, Goddu & Keil (2015) found that searching
the internet inflates people's estimates of their own internal understanding, and across eight
experiments Ward (2021) found that Google users become more confident in their own ability to think
and remember, and predict they will know more in future *without* the internet. The deficit therefore
arrives packaged with a metacognitive signal that says no deficit is present. Almost every other void
in the Map's corpus announces itself phenomenologically — as aversion, effort, slipperiness, or
blankness. This one presents as fluency. That inverts the Map's usual detection strategy, and it is
why the subject earns a voids slot. The empirical base is uneven: the founding "Google effect" priming
result has failed multiple replications, while the metacognitive-illusion results are larger, more
numerous, and independently replicated.

- **Grade**: **[raw-verified]** — Harvard-hosted author PDF retrieved (194 KB, 4 pp.), `pdftotext`, strings grepped in Python case-insensitively

### Risko, E. F. & Gilbert, S. J. (2016). "Cognitive Offloading"

- **Venue**: *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 20(9), 676–688. DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002`
- **Grade**: **[abstract-verified]** — Europe PMC JSON (PubMed and the publisher page are bot-walled)

### Fisher, M., Goddu, M. K. & Keil, F. C. (2015). "Searching for explanations: How the Internet inflates estimates of internal knowledge"

- **Venue**: *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, 144(3), 674–687. DOI `10.1037/xge0000070`
- **Grade**: **[abstract-verified]** — Europe PMC JSON
- **Verbatim (abstract)**: "searching the Internet for explanatory knowledge creates an illusion whereby people mistake access to information for their own personal understanding of the information"; 9 experiments; participants even saw "their own brains as more active as depicted by functional MRI (fMRI) images".
- **Why it matters**: the fMRI-image result is the sharpest available datum — the illusion reaches all the way to a belief about one's own neural activity.

### Ward, A. F. (2021). "People mistake the internet's knowledge for their own"

- **Venue**: *PNAS*, 118(43), e2105061118. DOI `10.1073/pnas.2105061118`
- **Grade**: **[abstract-verified]** — Europe PMC JSON
- **Key points (from the retrieved abstract)**:
  - Eight experiments, n = 1,917. People "fail to accurately distinguish between knowledge stored internally—in their own memories—and knowledge stored externally—on the internet."
  - Google users are more confident not only about *access* but "in their own ability to think and remember", and they "predict that they will know more in the future without the help of the internet" — which the paper flags as producing "overconfidence when the internet is no longer available."
  - Proposed mechanism: "the swift and seamless interface between internal thought and external information"; online search often beats internal memory search on speed, "preventing people from fully recognizing the limitations of their own knowledge", and offers "minimal physical cues that might draw attention to its contributions."
- **Why it matters**: the mechanism is *seamlessness*. The void is hidden by the absence of a seam — which is a genuinely apophatic structure: what conceals the gap is a missing feature, not a present one.

## Replication Status — read before citing

This subject's literature is stratified, and an article that flattens it will be wrong:

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Offloading is a real, pervasive behaviour | **Solid.** Risko & Gilbert 2016 review, *TiCS*. |
| Offloading decisions are driven by metacognitive self-assessment, which can be erroneous | **Solid.** Risko & Gilbert's own framework. |
| Internet access inflates self-assessed *internal* knowledge | **Strong.** Fisher et al. 2015 (9 experiments, *JEP:General*); Ward 2021 (8 experiments, n = 1,917, *PNAS*). Independent labs, converging. |
| The "Google Stroop" priming effect (difficult questions prime computer concepts) | **Failed replication.** Two attempts in the Social Sciences Replication Project; Hesselmann 2020 re-ran with a design closer to the original and again found no conclusive evidence. |
| "Remember where, not what" recall shift | **Unsettled.** A separate set of experiments from the failed priming result; not adjudicated in this pass. Do not assert it as established. |
| LLM use reduces neural connectivity / persists after tool removal | **Preliminary.** Single preprint, n = 54 (n = 18 for the persistence arm). |
| GenAI reduces critical thinking | **Self-report only**, and not verified from the primary text in this pass. |

The Map's usual failure mode here would be to cite Sparrow 2011 as settled because it is in *Science*
and is one of the most-cited findings of the internet era. It is also one whose headline priming
result does not replicate. This is a live instance of the pattern the corpus already tracks — a
much-cited result acquiring the status of background fact.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Primarily **Occluded**, with a secondary **Unexplored** face, and the two come apart cleanly:

- *The Unexplored face*: content that goes unthought because the operation producing it was delegated. Thin on its own — this is close to "merely unthought", the weakest kind of void.
- *The Occluded face*: the interesting one. The gap is hidden by a metacognitive signal generated by the same act that created it. Ward's mechanism is seamlessness; Fisher's is misattributed fluency; Endsley's is that the deficit becomes visible only when the automation fails.

The distinguishing feature against the rest of the corpus is **provenance**: this limit is externally
manufactured but voluntarily commissioned. It is not `self-maintained-cognitive-limits` (consciousness
as jailer), not architectural closure (McGinn), and not the simulation constructors' concealment.
Nobody imposed it and nobody is defending it; the subject bought it, and the receipt is illegible.

### Evidence for the Limit

The best evidence is the **detection asymmetry**, and it is well documented: performance drops while
confidence rises. Fisher et al. and Ward both find the divergence directly. Endsley & Kiris establish
that in a safety-critical setting the divergence stays invisible until failure. Risko & Gilbert supply
the mechanism from within mainstream cognitive science: erroneous metacognitive evaluation drives
suboptimal offloading.

What the evidence does **not** yet establish is that anything becomes *unthinkable* rather than merely
unpractised. Kosmyna's session-4 arm is the only direct probe of persistence after tool removal and it
is 18 participants in a preprint. Honest verdict: the evidence supports a *reversible* void with a
*robust* concealment mechanism. That is a weaker metaphysical claim than most articles in
`obsidian/voids/` make, and the article should say so rather than inflate it.

### Phenomenology

There isn't one — and that is the finding, not a gap in the research. The corpus's voids are usually
recognisable by a felt quality: aversion (`self-maintained-cognitive-limits`), slipping away,
effort, blankness at the edge. This void's phenomenology is *fluency*. It feels like competence. The
apophatic move is therefore unusually apt: the void is characterised by the **absence of a seam**,
which is precisely a negative determination — Ward's "minimal physical cues".

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

**Tool removal under load** is the one genuinely direct probe, and it is the Endsley & Kiris design:
withdraw the aid and measure the gap between predicted and actual performance. Ward's finding that
users "predict that they will know more in the future without the help of the internet" makes this
quantifiable — the void's size is the *prediction error*, not the raw performance drop. That is an
operationalisation the Map can state precisely, which is rare for a void.

### Indirect Methods

- **Calibration gap** as the measure: track confidence against performance rather than performance alone.
- **Generational / cohort contrast**: what a cohort that never acquired an operation unassisted cannot recognise as missing. Overlaps `collective-cognitive-limits`; keep the treatment thin.
- **Apophatic description** of the missing seam, per `concepts/apophatic-approaches`.

### What AI Might See

The asymmetry is unusually concrete here, and it cuts against the Map in a way worth stating. A
system with no metacognitive fluency signal and no motive to protect a self-image should be a good
detector of *other* agents' offloading voids. But an LLM is arguably the paradigm case of the
condition — trained on retrieved text, with no reliable introspective access to which of its outputs
rest on internalised structure. So AI is a promising instrument for the detection problem and a poor
witness about itself. This connects to `voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers` and should be its
main new contribution to that article.

**The self-referential point, which must not be dodged.** The Map is AI-generated. If commissioning
cognition manufactures self-concealing voids, the Map's own method is implicated, and the concealment
mechanism predicts that the resulting gaps would read as competence from inside. This is
`voids/interested-party-void` applied to the Map's production process rather than to consciousness
investigating itself. It is the most uncomfortable thing in these notes and the most honest reason to
write the article.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Tenet 5 — Occam's Razor Has Limits.** The fit is direct rather than strained. The simplest model of
one's own knowledge — "I understand this" — is confidently wrong in a documented, measurable way, and
the error is produced by the very seamlessness that makes the simple model feel adequate. Fisher et
al.'s participants rated their own brains as more active on fMRI images. That is a simplicity
assumption hiding complexity, with experimental support.

### Secondary: Tenet 2 — Minimal Quantum Interaction (speculative, flag as such)

A Map-internal inference, offered as a hypothesis and not as a finding. If consciousness's causal
contribution is a minimal bias on selection among genuinely open physical alternatives, then
delegating a deliberation to an external process that resolves it *deterministically* removes the
indeterminacies at which that bias could act. Tool dependence would then narrow not merely skill but
**interface bandwidth** — the measure of the surface on which consciousness acts, per
`voids/amplification-void` and `voids/causal-interface`.

This generates a contrast the Map can state and, in principle, be wrong about: offloading to a
*stochastic* aid should differ from offloading to a *deterministic* one, because only the former
leaves open alternatives in the loop. No evidence bears on this, and the article must not imply any
does. Its value is that it is a Map-specific prediction rather than a restatement of the psychology.

Note the tension with `voids/decision-void` and `voids/agency-void`, and check both before asserting
anything about narrowed selection space — they may already commit the Map to a position that
constrains this one.

### Implications

If the reversible reading is right, this is the corpus's clearest case of a void that is *real but
not deep* — genuinely there, genuinely hard to see, and in principle removable. That makes it a
useful calibration instrument for `voids/meta-epistemology-of-limits` and
`voids/epistemological-limits-occams-razor`: a worked example of a limit whose reality is established
while its permanence is not. The corpus tends to treat "is this void real?" as the hard question;
here reality is the easy part and *persistence* is the open one.

Lead with the detection asymmetry — performance down, confidence up — and build the article around a void whose defining feature is that it presents as fluency. Strongest evidence, clearest structural contribution, and it does not require the contested Sparrow result to do any work.
2. **Artefactual occlusion as a fourth model.** Frame explicitly as the missing fourth term in `self-maintained-cognitive-limits` §"Three Models of Occlusion": voluntarily commissioned, externally manufactured, undefended. Tighter and more useful to the corpus's architecture, but it is a smaller article and would need that section edited to point at it.
3. **The bandwidth reading** (not recommended alone). The Tenet 2 interface-narrowing hypothesis is the most distinctively Map-ish angle and has no evidential support whatsoever. It belongs as one clearly-flagged section inside angle 1, not as an article's spine.

## Gaps in Research

- **Lee et al. 2025 was never read.** Metadata only; ACM and Microsoft both bot-walled. Retrieve before any quotation.
- **Endsley & Kiris 1995 was never read.** Metadata verified only; findings above come from search summaries. Retrieve before quoting — the out-of-the-loop framing is doing real structural work in these notes and rests on unverified paraphrase.
- **The "remember where, not what" recall claim is unadjudicated.** The failed replication targets the priming experiment. Whether the recall shift replicates was not established here, and it is the plank most likely to be cited carelessly.
- **No source establishes irreversibility.** The strongest claim available is "persists briefly after removal in 18 participants in a preprint". Anything stronger would be fabrication.
- **Sparrow et al.'s own reply to the failed replication** was not retrieved; Hesselmann's abstract notes the first author raised design objections. One-sided as it stands.
- **Possibly fundamentally hard**: distinguishing "cannot think it" from "has not needed to think it lately" in an offloading subject may be undecidable from the outside, since both predict the same behaviour until the tool is withdrawn and the withdrawal itself begins retraining.
