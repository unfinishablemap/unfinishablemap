## Executive Summary

The "unposed" report is **half right, and right in the half that matters** — but the article's surrounding context now needs repair, and the reason is more interesting than a simple yes or no.

Split the question in two. (1) *Do introspective orderings of felt intensity differences satisfy the algebraic-difference axioms?* This is **emphatically posed, and empirically tested**. Reisenzein and Junge (2024) run exactly the Krantz–Luce–Suppes–Tversky difference-structure axioms — weak ordering plus the sextuple condition, with the stronger quadruple condition in the Debreu / Luce–Suppes axiomatisation — on introspective judgements of the intensity of *emotional experiences*, and report per-emotion adherence rates ranging from 71% to 97% of participants. This is not a perceptual proxy study; the authors explicitly ground it in introspection and in the representational theory of measurement. (2) *Do the stark cross-kind contrasts the phenomenal-contrast method extracts — understanding versus incomprehension, agency versus passivity — satisfy those axioms?* Nothing this run reached poses that. The article's literal sentence therefore **survives**, because it was scoped to the cross-kind case.

The genuinely valuable finding sits between the two. In Reisenzein and Junge's implementation the **relata are stimuli**: "One selects sextuples of stimuli," and the phenomenal magnitude enters as the *latent quantity* the judgements are constructed from, not as an element of the domain. Meanwhile Kleiner and Ludwig (2024) supply, in *Synthese*, the first explicit criterion for when a mathematical structure is a structure *of conscious experience* — condition (D1): "The domains of the structure are sets whose elements correspond to aspects of conscious experiences" — and they close by naming the RTM connection as an **open question their result opens**, not one already answered. **Nobody has combined them.** No axiom test this run reached has been run on a difference structure whose domain satisfies (D1). That is the precise, defensible open question, and it is sharper than the one the article currently poses.

Corroborating the philosophy-side absence: Isaac (2025), the flagship statement of the structuralist research programme in *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences*, contains (grep-verified on the retrieved galley) **zero** occurrences of "scale type", "interval scale", or "ordinal", and one "axiom" — inside a Helmholtz reference title. Lee (2021) raises the scale type of a phenomenal quantity and explicitly declines to settle it.

## Key Sources

### Reisenzein & Junge 2024 — the direct hit
- **Citation**: Reisenzein, R., & Junge, M. (2024). "Measuring the intensity of emotions." *Frontiers in Psychology*, 15, 1437843. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1437843
- **Type**: Theoretical-and-review paper summarising the authors' own study series. Retrieved in full; quotes grep-verified.
- **What it does**: Applies probabilistic nonmetric difference scaling — Ordinal Difference Scaling (ODS) and Maximum Likelihood Difference Scaling (MLDS) — to emotion intensity, and *tests the measurement axioms* rather than assuming them.
- **The axioms, in their words**: "The two main testable axioms of difference structures in the standard axiomatization (Krantz et al., 1971) are the weak ordering axiom, and the axiom of weak monotonicity or the sextuple condition." And: "The sextuple axiom is generally regarded as the central testable axiom of difference structures." Its content: "The sextuple axiom is an ordinal implication of the fact that intervals between numbers are additive." The stronger alternative axiomatisation (they attribute it to Debreu 1958 and Luce and Suppes 1965) replaces it with the quadruple axiom.
- **The relata — decisive for the Map**: "One selects sextuples of stimuli" — the domain is a stimulus set, with the quaternary relation defined by *introspective* comparison of the intensity differences of the feelings those stimuli evoke. The phenomenal magnitude is the latent variable: a scaling model is "a theory about how ... the person's overt judgments are constructed on the basis of—in the case of conscious mental states—her introspective observation of the latent quantity."
- **Introspective, not behavioural**: "These methods are ultimately based on introspection." They argue self-report has epistemic priority over physiological and behavioural indicators for emotion.
- **Results**: "For the six emotions investigated by Junge and Reisenzein (2016), the hypothesis that the participants' ODS scale values adhered to the quadruple axiom could be retained for most participants: amusement 71%; relief 74%; disgust 81%; surprise 88%; pleasantness 97%, and disappointment 97%." Abstract verdict: "the scale values appear to fulfill central measurement-theoretical axioms necessary for interval-level measurement."
- **Contrast with rating scales**: standard emotion rating scales did *not* fare as well — direct ratings deviated significantly from ODS scale values for a majority of participants across all six emotions.
- **Framing**: explicitly realist about quantity, citing Michell (1999, 2005) and Tal (2020), and combining latent-variable psychometrics with "a deductive order of inquiry of testing measurement axioms."
- **Tenet alignment**: Cuts both ways. It **strengthens** the Map's interval rung (a phenomenal-intensity attribute has passed the axiom tests, in an introspective paradigm) and **weakens** the corpus's more sweeping unmeasurability rhetoric. It is fully compatible with the cross-subject impossibility, which it never addresses: every test is within-subject, at "the individual subject level."

### Kleiner & Ludwig 2024 — the relata criterion, and the open question named
- **Citation**: Kleiner, J., & Ludwig, T. (2024). "What is a mathematical structure of conscious experience?" *Synthese*, 203(3), 89, 1–23. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-024-04503-4 (CC BY 4.0; retrieved from the Bamberg institutional repository)
- **Type**: Philosophy/mathematics paper. Retrieved in full; quotes grep-verified.
- **The criterion**: their starting condition (MDC) has two parts — "(D1) The domains of the structure are sets whose elements correspond to aspects of conscious experiences" and "(D2) The axioms of the structure are satisfied." They argue (MDC) is *necessary but not sufficient*, because it lets incompatible structures (e.g. discrete and non-discrete topologies on the same aspect set) both count, and they extend it using **variations** — changes of one experience into another, which "can be induced introspectively ... stimulated in a laboratory by change of stimuli, or studied theoretically."
- **Why this is exactly the Map's tool**: the domain is a set *E* of conscious experiences of a *single* experiencing subject, with aspects *a, b, c* drawn from those experiences. They explicitly handle comparative aspects: it "is usually the case that an experience of relative similarity is an experience of relative similarity of something, for example two color aspects relative to a third color aspect." That is a phenomenal comparative relation with phenomenal relata — the thing a difference structure over experiences would need.
- **The open question, in their closing section**: they ask "whether our result on mathematical structures might open new perspectives on measurements of consciousness (Irvine, 2013), as arguably promised by the Representational Theory of Measurement (Krantz et al., 1971) whenever an axiomatic structure on a target domain is available." Note the tense: this is future work, in the leading 2024 formal treatment.
- **Single-subject by construction**: *E* comprises the experiences "which one experiencing subject can have." This independently corroborates the article's cross-subject rung: the leading formal framework does not even attempt a cross-subject domain.
- **Tenet alignment**: Strongly congenial. It is the formal statement of the Map's own bridge-policing, and its (D1) is a cleaner discriminator than anything the article currently deploys.

## The Core Finding: Three Levels, Not One Question

The article treats "do phenomenal contrasts satisfy difference-structure axioms" as one question. The literature splits it into three, with different answers.

**Level 1 — Within-attribute felt intensity, stimulus relata, introspective relation. POSED, TESTED, LARGELY UPHELD.** Reisenzein and Junge (2024) for six emotions; the MLDS/perception tradition for gloss, slant, and colour differences; Schneider et al. (1974) for loudness intervals if that lead verifies. The empirical relational structure has stimuli as objects; the quaternary relation `ab ≽ cd` is fixed by the subject's introspective comparison of felt intensity differences; the phenomenal magnitude Ψ is the latent quantity the representation delivers. Adherence to the quadruple axiom in the 71–97% band, per participant, per emotion.

**Level 2 — Same attributes, but with experiences (or aspects of experiences) as the relata. NOT POSED.** This is Kleiner and Ludwig's (D1). Their framework can express such a structure — their "relative similarity" aspect is a phenomenal comparative with phenomenal relata — and they name the RTM link as future work. No one this run reached has written down a difference structure satisfying (D1) and asked whether its axioms hold. **This is the sharpest form of the Map's question, and the article does not currently distinguish it from Level 1.**

**Level 3 — Cross-kind stark contrasts (understanding vs incomprehension; agency vs passivity). NOT POSED, and possibly not well-formed.** Every tested case is a *unidimensional intensity* attribute inside a fixed quality: louder, glossier, more amused, more disappointed. The Map's phenomenal-contrast method compares experiences that differ in kind, not degree. The quaternary relation "the difference between understanding and incomprehension is at least that between agency and passivity" may not denote — and the honest first move is to ask whether the weak-ordering axiom (connectedness, in particular) is even satisfiable on such a domain, before asking about the sextuple condition. Bayne, Hohwy and Owen's multidimensionality argument, already cited in the article, is the nearest thing to a negative answer, and it is about global states rather than cross-kind contrasts. Siegel's phenomenal-contrast literature is entirely qualitative; nothing in it touches measurement axioms.

The article's literal sentence is scoped to Level 3, so it stands. Its *context* implies Level 1 is perceptual-only, which is now false.

## Key Debates

### Are the axioms testable on phenomenal relata at all?
- **Sides**: Reisenzein & Junge — yes in practice, given a deductivist test design that selects test cases from the fitted scale values rather than from the raw ordering (they modify the classical RTM test procedure precisely because the classical one is under-powered). Trendler — no, psychological attributes resist the experimental control axiom-testing requires. Student & Read — partially yes, quantifiably, at sufficient N.
- **Core disagreement**: whether a probabilistic latent-variable fit that *checks* an axiom is a test of the axiom or a test of the model. This is the same objection Trendler (2019) presses against the maximum-likelihood conjoint method, and the article already flags it. Reisenzein & Junge do not evade it; they take the latent-variable route deliberately.
- **Bearing on the Map**: the article should not cite Trendler as if unrebutted for phenomenal attributes. The Reisenzein–Junge series is the strongest existing counter-instance, and it is introspective.

### Does a well-defined metric over phenomenal quality space exist?
- **Sides**: Isaac (2025) — convergence between independently derived models is a regulative ideal, imperfect but progressive. MacLeod & Willen (1995), Decock (2006), Barwich (2019) — failures of convergence are evidence against a well-defined metric.
- **Current state**: open, and conducted almost entirely in convergence vocabulary rather than axiom vocabulary. Translating it into the axiom register is itself a contribution the Map could make.
- **Bearing on the Map**: this sceptical line is a natural ally for the corpus's measurement-limit arguments, arrived at by a *third* route (empirical non-convergence) distinct from both the Map's structural argument and Trendler's methodological one. Per the Map's convergence discipline, count it as a distinct route, not as independent confirmation of the same argument.

### Can similarity/difference structures be aligned across subjects?
- **Sides**: the article (and the Map generally) — no spanning empirical relation exists, so no representation theorem can be stated. Isaac (2025) and the Gromov–Wasserstein alignment programme — structural relations can be rendered behaviourally manifest and then compared third-personally.
- **Current state**: live and moving; the alignment work is 2023–2025 and unretrieved here.
- **Bearing on the Map**: this is the sharpest external challenge surfaced by this run, and it lands on the article's strongest claim. The Map's reply is presumably that aligning *discrimination-behaviour* structures is not aligning experiences — the same bridge objection, one level up. But the reply needs to be written, and it needs the papers read first.
