# Research: Structuralism about Phenomenal Character

**Date**: 2026-09-06
**Commissioned by**: the P3 `research-topic` task minted from `reviews/optimistic-2026-09-06-kinds-and-vagueness-wing.md` — five live pages defer to this rival and none treats it.

## Verdict: PROCEED — and the corpus's central concession about this rival is wrong

**PROCEED with the chained `concepts/` article.** Capacity measured live this dispatch with
`tools.evolution.state.count_section_files`: concepts **324**/360, topics 328/360, voids 102/115,
positions 18/80. No cap pressure.

The reason to proceed is stronger than the task supposed, and it is not the reason the task gave.
**Phenomenal structuralism is not a Map-assembled composite.** It is a self-named position with a
named critical literature, and the corpus currently says the opposite on three live pages. Four
independent findings, each raw-verified:

1. **The authors name the position themselves.** Fink, Kob and Lyre (2021), §3: "First, it is the
   location in a structure, not an intrinsic property, that individuates a phenomenal character.
   **This view could be called phenomenal structuralism** and is introduced in subsection 3.1." Their
   conclusion uses the label as a standing noun: "In contrast to qualia intrinsicalism, phenomenal
   structuralism claims that it is this net of relations … that helps us to individuate any single
   experience."
2. **The ontic composite exists in print, defined, under a name.** Kleiner (2025) coins the
   distinction the 2026-09-04 note said had never been drawn: "we might designate the former as
   epistemic phenomenal structural realism (EPSR), and the latter as ontic phenomenal structural
   realism (OPSR). **OPSR says that phenomenal structures are ontologically basic: non-structural
   features of phenomenal character, such as intrinsic qualities, do not in fact exist**; only claims
   of the form (2) can be true. EPSR is the view that all we can know about phenomenal character is
   its structure." He cites Frigg and Votsis (2011) for the philosophy-of-science parallel. This is
   precisely the ESR/OSR distinction transposed to the phenomenal, which `concepts/ontic-structural-realism`
   states no one has performed.
3. **There is a dedicated special issue.** *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences* Vol. 6 (2025),
   "Structuralism in the Science of Consciousness", edited by Sascha Benjamin Fink and Andrew Y. Lee —
   **15 papers**, including three critiques of the position and two on the Newman problem. Full
   contents below.
4. **Two further critiques sit in *Synthese*.** Negro (2025) and Aldé (2026), the first of which
   supplies the taxonomy the Map needs and argues the Map's own side of the case.

**The distinction the task asked to preserve survives intact and gets sharper.** Ontic structural
realism is a defeater for the Map's *route* — it removes `physical-completeness`'s
ontological-remainder argument. Phenomenal structuralism reaches **Tenet 1 directly**, and in Lyre's
version it reaches **Tenet 3** as well, because his programme runs a causal-exclusion argument
against the phenomenal. Those are two different threats and the new article exists to keep them apart.

## Key Sources

### Fink, Kob & Lyre (2021), "A structural constraint on neural correlates of consciousness" — **[raw-verified]**
- **Citation**: *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences*, 2, article 7. DOI `10.33735/phimisci.2021.79`.
  Crossref-verified this dispatch: authors Sascha Benjamin Fink, Lukas Kob, Holger Lyre; published
  2021-07-19; ISSN 2699-0369; publisher Universitätsbibliothek der Ruhr-Universität Bochum. Crossref
  carries no page or article-number field; the article's own running header and Lyre's 2022 reference
  list both give **2, 7**, so the corpus's existing "2: 7" is right.
- **What it actually is, and this matters for the composite question**: an **NCC-methodology paper**.
  Grep counts on the retrieved artefact: "hard problem" **0**, "dualis*" **0**, "Newman" **0**,
  "structural realism" **1** — and that one occurrence is a bibliography entry for Isaac (2014). FKL
  never engage the metaphysics of consciousness at all.
- **The self-naming, verbatim**: "First, it is the location in a structure, not an intrinsic property,
  that individuates a phenomenal character. This view could be called phenomenal structuralism".
- **Their declared position on the metaphysics, verbatim**: after stating the strong reading they write
  "We are sympathetic to this reading, but focus here on a methodological point". Elsewhere: "A broad
  range of philosophers (us included) disagree with this metaphysical position [intrinsicalism] for
  several reasons. But here, we want to focus only on the methodological side."
- **The structural similarity constraint (SSC)**, from the abstract: "Neural activation must preserve
  the structure governing the domain of experiences it is associated with in order to count as that
  domain's NCC proper. Any activation that fails to preserve phenomenal structure fails to be an NCC
  proper." Illustrated on Brouwer and Heeger (2009) on colour decoding.
- **They state the Map's objection themselves, and concede it, verbatim**: "The qualia enthusiast may
  even concede that location within a structure may uniquely identify an experience, but not that this
  makes an experience what it is (Levine, 1995). Instead, qualia are supposed to be something
  additional to this structure. In scientific contexts, it suffices if we can uniquely identify basic
  experiences by their location in a structure. **Beyond this, scientists may be agnostic as to whether
  there is anything that individuates experiences other than their location in a structure.**" This is
  the single most useful sentence in the paper for the Map: the individuation/constitution distinction
  is granted, and the paper's scope is drawn to leave it open.
- **Tenet alignment**: **neutral as written.** FKL's methodological thesis is compatible with Dualism
  by their own declared agnosticism. Their sympathies are not, but sympathies are not arguments and the
  paper does not offer one.

### Loorits (2014), "Structural qualia: a solution to the hard problem of consciousness" — **[raw-verified]**
- **Citation**: *Frontiers in Psychology*, 5, 237. DOI `10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00237`. Received 2014-01-09,
  accepted 2014-03-03.
- **The frame**: the hard problem as a tension among three theses — (1) the objects of natural science
  can be fully analysed in structural terms, (2) consciousness is or has something over and above its
  structure, (3) consciousness is part of the natural world. He accepts 1 and 3 and rejects 2, and
  states the motive plainly: "I will argue that **the threat of dualism can be avoided** and the hard
  problem can be solved by accepting the first and the third theses while rejecting the second one."
- **The thesis, verbatim**: "the apparently non-structural and monadic elements of consciousness, namely
  the qualia, are in fact compositional and have an internal structure." The components are unconscious
  associations, following Crick and Koch (1998) and Koch (2004).
- **The debunking step, verbatim from the abstract**: "consciousness as a whole can be seen as a complex
  neural pattern that misperceives some of its own highly complex structural properties as monadic and
  qualitative." ⚠️ The same abstract sentence two clauses earlier reads "those internal structures can be
  identified **which** certain neural patterns" — a typo in the published source. Quote around it; do not
  silently repair it and do not treat it as evidence of a bad transcription.
- **The stated testable commitment, verbatim**: "**Since I am denying the ideal positive conceivability of
  zombies and inverted qualia**, I will not examine any of such arguments here." He grounds it: "it would
  be logically inconsistent to hold that some fully structural phenomenon could be somehow different in
  two structurally identical cases."
- **He is not an ontic structural realist, verbatim**: "only a relatively small minority of the proponents
  of scientific object structuralism believe that structure and relations are actually all there is. An
  example of that kind of radical structuralist metaphysics is a theory of Ladyman and Ross (2007)."
- **Tenet alignment**: **conflicts with Dualism, by design and by declaration.** The strongest single
  statement of the rival, and the only one of the three that asserts the metaphysics outright.

### Lyre (2022), "Neurophenomenal structuralism" — **[raw-verified]**
- **Citation**: *Neuroscience of Consciousness*, 2022(1), niac012. DOI `10.1093/nc/niac012`. Published
  2022-08-23; CC BY-NC; DFG grant 337619223/RTG2386.
- **What he is doing, verbatim**: "Complementary to the FKL paper, the focus of my present paper is on
  ontology… I go well beyond the FKL paper… the position of neurophenomenal structuralism is independent.
  It should in particular become clear that the position, as I develop it here, must be understood as a
  **rigorous structuralist ontology about phenomenal consciousness that is physicalist and reductionist in
  spirit**."
- **⚠️ The disavowal has been read backwards in the corpus.** Lyre's "The short answer is: no" answers the
  question whether neurophenomenal structuralism is a version of *structural realism*, a doctrine he
  defines as "a moderate version of scientific realism" about the theoretical entities of physics. The
  next thing he says is: "there seems to be a **family resemblance** to neurophenomenal structuralism,
  since our doctrine **denies phenomenal intrinsicality** and considers phenomenal experience as
  individuated by its place in a Q-structure". And his reason for declining "realism about the phenomenal"
  is that he holds something stronger: "we can run an **exclusion argument according to which the
  Q-structure plays no causal role at all**, but all the causal work is taken over by the N-structure.
  Therefore, neurophenomenal structuralism, as I advocate it here, is no realism about the phenomenal (be
  it structural or otherwise), and it is rather **consistent with a reduction of the phenomenal to the
  neural**." Quoting the disavowal as evidence that Lyre softens the threat to Tenet 1 inverts its sense.
- **The Tenet 3 hit, which the corpus has not noticed.** The exclusion argument denies the phenomenal any
  causal role. Tenet 3 asserts that consciousness causally influences the physical world. Lyre's
  programme is therefore a rival on **Tenets 1 and 3 jointly**, and no live page says so.
- **The concession the Map should build on, verbatim.** On the metaphysical possibility of inverted qualia
  construed as quiddities (citing Chalmers 2012, ch. 7.9): "As a metaphysical possibility, I fear, this
  cannot be ruled out. And it certainly cannot be captured by any structuralist conception. **If this is
  the core difficulty about qualia, then the hard problem cannot be solved structurally.**" He then argues
  the possibility is not worth upholding, leaning on Lewis (2009) on quidditistic humility, and closes:
  "what-it's-like character reduces to holistic phenomenal content (which in turn reduces to neural
  vehicle representations). This is at least what neurophenomenal structuralism has on offer to the
  notorious hard problem."
- **How he actually rules out inversion**, in three graded steps: real colour spaces are asymmetric
  (Hardin, Clark, Palmer), so inversion is contingently ruled out; sensory intersections and holism rule
  out nomological inversion of a single modality; metaphysical inversion he concedes.
- **The declared non-structural input**: the abstract's promise of "a solution of the Newman problem of the
  brain in terms of spatiotemporal relations". Privileging spatiotemporal relations is an input from
  outside the structure, honestly flagged.
- **Tenet alignment**: **conflicts with Dualism and with Bidirectional Interaction.** The most complete
  statement of the rival, and the one that concedes most.

### Kleiner (2025), "The Newman problem of consciousness science" — **[raw-verified]**
- **Citation**: *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences*, 6. DOI `10.33735/phimisci.2025.11827`.
  ⚠️ **Do not copy the DOI printed in the PDF's own citation line** — it reads
  `https://doi.org/10.33735/phimisci.2100.1234`, a publisher placeholder. The DOI above is from the
  article page's `citation_doi` meta tag.
- **Why it matters most**: it coins **OPSR / EPSR** for the phenomenal domain (quoted in the verdict
  above), which settles the composite question, and it then argues the Map's chosen lever away.
- **The threat to the Map's Newman lever**: Kleiner argues that Newman "ceases to apply" once structural
  claims are defined carefully. His condition: a claim "the structure of conscious experience is E", where
  E = (C, R), is true iff **(C1)** the elements of C are phenomenal properties of conscious experiences and
  **(C2)** "the relation R as specified by E exists, and there is a corresponding **higher-order phenomenal
  R-property**", where a phenomenal property p is an R-property iff any variation preserving R preserves p.
  "What distinguishes our proposal from previous approaches in the scientific study of consciousness is only
  the inclusion of R-properties in (C2)."
- **The Map's opening, and it is a real one**: the repair buys off Newman by requiring genuine
  *phenomenal* properties as truth-makers for the structural claim, and by denying "relations that induce
  trivial automorphism groups the status of viable objects of a structural claim". A structuralism that
  needs higher-order phenomenal properties to make its own structural claims true has admitted phenomenal
  properties into the ontology. Whether those count as non-structural is the question to press, and it is
  a sharper question than "Newman is a pressure point".
- He also rejects the naturalness solution (Lewis 1983; Frigg and Votsis 2011; endorsed by Chalmers 2022)
  as unstable because "what counts as a natural kind might change as science progresses (Melia & Saatsi,
  2006)".
- **Tenet alignment**: mixed. It supplies the taxonomy the Map wants and blunts the weapon the Map planned.

### Negro (2025), "Three arguments against metaphysical structuralism in consciousness research" — **[raw-verified]**
- **Citation**: *Synthese*, 206(1), article 8. DOI `10.1007/s11229-025-05103-6`. Published 2025-06-19.
  Sole author; Tel Aviv University; CC BY 4.0.
- **The taxonomy the new article should adopt**, from the abstract: "This paper distinguishes three types
  of structuralism in consciousness studies: methodological, epistemological, and metaphysical. I
  critically evaluate metaphysical structuralism, which asserts that the phenomenal character of an
  experience is fully determined by its relational properties. I present three arguments challenging this
  metaphysical claim… I also claim, however, that this critique does not affect the fruitfulness of
  methodological and epistemological structuralism."
- **His restatement of the three senses**, verbatim from the conclusion: "in the first sense, an experience
  has an internal structure; in the second sense, an experience is within an external structure; in the
  third sense, an experience is fully characterised by the external structure. Metaphysical structuralism
  requires this third sense."
- **The three arguments**, each a thought experiment:
  - **Relational Mary** — a Mary who has seen every human-visible colour but orange and has complete
    knowledge of the relational structure of colour experience, including every relation orange bears to
    the others. Framed on inter-subject similarity structure (he floats Kawakita et al. 2025's
    Gromov–Wasserstein metric as the formal specification) and deliberately kept neutral between
    representationalism and sense-data theory.
  - **The novel experience argument** — a system exposed only to red, yellow and green acquires blue; its
    structure changes from R to R′; metaphysical structuralism therefore implies red should now look
    different. "any novel experience should change previously instantiated experiences", which conflicts
    with phenomenological observation.
  - **The lonely experience argument** — structuralism needs at least one relation and so at least two
    experiences, making a single-experience subject metaphysically impossible; but a system with one
    experience of smell seems conceivable. He rules out self-identity as a repair because the relations
    that build quality spaces are difference-sensitive (citing Rosenthal 2010).
- **⚠️ Calibration: Negro is not a dualist ally.** He argues the methodological and epistemological
  versions "can contribute to consciousness science without heavy and counterintuitive metaphysical
  commitments" — which is a case for keeping structuralism, not for the Map's residue.
- **Tenet alignment**: supports the Map's *conclusion* against metaphysical structuralism while rejecting
  the Map's *framing*. Cite him for the taxonomy and the arguments; do not enlist him.

### Sebastián (2025), "The incommensurability problem: A critique of phenomenal structuralism" — **[raw-verified]**
- **Citation**: *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences*, 6. DOI `10.33735/phimisci.2025.11819`. UNAM.
- **The argument**: holism is the pressure point. "even minor differences in experiential capacities lead
  to fundamentally different Q-structures, rendering qualities incommensurable across different systems or
  even across different states within the same system." So inter- and intra-subjective comparison fails,
  "especially pressing in the case of pain", which he flags as having ethical consequences. He adds a
  second edge: "different sets of qualities can indeed yield the same discriminations within a single
  modality", so structure underdetermines quality.
- **⚠️ He is not an intrinsicalist either.** He closes by recommending representationalism, which "might
  preserve the strengths of phenomenal structuralism without its problematic consequences", and calls for
  "further investigation into how qualitative experiences can be rigorously understood **without invoking
  intrinsic properties in mental states**."
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral-to-useful. The incommensurability charge is a live cost the rival pays;
  Sebastián's own remedy is not the Map's.

### Mihálik & Marvan (2025), "Neurophenomenal structuralism as a general theory of consciousness?" — **[raw-verified]**
- **Citation**: *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences*, 6. DOI `10.33735/phimisci.2025.11821`. Institute of
  Philosophy, Czech Academy of Sciences.
- **The target is Fink's extension**, not FKL 2021: whether NPS can explain not only *which* character an
  experience has but *why there is* an experience at all. They call the extended view NPS+ and raise five
  challenges: the unconsciously perceived gist objection (evidence for unconscious Gestalten), Ganzfeld
  experiences (experiences apparently lacking Gestalt structure), the mirroring dilemma (vicious regress of
  incorporating structures versus giving up the methodological promise), the "more of the same" challenge
  (why does adding structure make structure conscious), and the structure-selection challenge (why these
  neural structures and not others).
- **Useful for scoping**: they record that "proponents of NPS disagree on whether their theory is to be
  taken merely as" a character theory or a general one, and cite Fink & Kob 2023, FKL 2021, Kob 2023 and
  Lyre 2022 as the position's statements. The "more of the same" challenge is the structuralist form of the
  Map's own question about why structure should light up at all.

### Lyre (2025), "Structural representation and the Newman problem of the brain and AI" — **[raw-verified]**
- **Citation**: *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences*, 6. DOI `10.33735/phimisci.2025.11855`.
- Lyre's own follow-through on the 2022 promissory note. The abstract states the problem at full strength:
  "The specific 'Newman problem of the brain' is the concern that the brain's operational closure is
  accompanied by a representational closure, where neither the intrinsic nature of the distal causes nor
  the nature of the external relations can be grasped from the inside… If the Newman problem persists, then
  such systems 'know' nothing, the outside world remains completely hidden. This is nothing less than a
  skeptical scenario of the most extreme form. However, I will present a solution… It works by
  'spatio-temporal grounding': spatial and temporal relations are unalteredly transferred from the external
  world to systems of structural representation."
- **Why the Map should cite it**: the 2022 abstract *promised* the Newman solution; this paper *supplies*
  it, and supplies it by stipulating that spatiotemporal relations transfer unaltered. The Map's Newman
  lever must now engage this specific move rather than the promise. Note also the extension to AI, which
  connects to `concepts/the-unfolding-argument-against-causal-structure-theories-of-consciousness`.

### Fink & Lee (2025), "Structuralism in the science of consciousness. Editorial introduction" — **[raw-verified]**
- **Citation**: *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences*, 6. DOI `10.33735/phimisci.2025.12733`. Editors:
  Sascha Benjamin Fink (FAU Erlangen) and Andrew Y. Lee (Toronto).
- **The taxonomy, verbatim**: "'Structuralism', in the most general sense, may be defined as an approach to
  consciousness research where the central aim is to investigate the structures of conscious experiences…
  **methodological structuralists** think that scientific methods give us knowledge only about structural
  features of consciousness, while **ontic structuralists** think that all there is to consciousness is
  structure."
- **The honest limit, verbatim, and the Map should quote it**: "research on the structures of conscious
  experiences is currently at an early stage of development. Relevant articles are scattered across
  different debates and different academic fields." So the position exists and the *field* is young — a
  much more defensible claim than either "the rival does not exist in print" or "the debate is settled".
- **Two lineage anchors from its reference list**: **Chalmers, D. J. (2012). "Phenomenal structuralism." In

## Major Positions

The single most useful structural finding for the downstream article: **this is not one position but
three or four, and Negro's taxonomy is the right way to keep them apart.** Treating them as one is how
the corpus arrived at both its over-claim and its under-claim in the same paragraph.

### Metaphysical / ontic phenomenal structuralism (Loorits; Kleiner's OPSR label)
Phenomenal character is *fully determined* by relational position; non-structural features such as
intrinsic qualities do not exist. **Loorits (2014) is the only one of the three primary authors who
asserts this outright**, and he pairs it with the debunking of apparent monadicity and the denial that
inverted qualia are ideally positively conceivable. Kleiner (2025) names the general form OPSR.
**Relation to site tenets**: the direct rival to Tenet 1. This is the position the new article exists to
meet, and it is also the position Negro, Sebastián and Aldé all attack — so the article meets a rival
that already has critics, and must not pretend to be the first.

### Reductive-physicalist structuralist ontology of the phenomenal (Lyre 2022, 2025)
Q-structure is mirrored homomorphically in N-structure; phenomenal states get content with neural-map
states as vehicles; a causal-exclusion argument leaves Q-structure with no causal role; the whole thing
reduces to the neural. Lyre declines the *structural realism* label while denying phenomenal
intrinsicality, and concedes that quiddistic qualia, if that is the core difficulty, are beyond
structural treatment.
**Relation to site tenets**: conflicts with **Dualism** and with **Bidirectional Interaction** — the
first live position the corpus has met that attacks both at once. Also the most honest opponent
available: it declares its non-structural input (spatiotemporal grounding) and names the case in which
it fails.

### Methodological phenomenal structuralism (FKL 2021; Kob 2023; Fink 2024; Isaac 2025)
Structure is how experiences are individuated *in scientific contexts*, which yields the structural
similarity constraint on NCCs. Explicitly agnostic about whether anything beyond structural position
makes an experience what it is.
**Relation to site tenets**: **compatible with Dualism as stated.** The Map should say so. Conceding the
methodological version costs nothing and makes the refusal of the metaphysical version credible — and
FKL's own agnosticism sentence licenses the split.

### Epistemological structuralism about the phenomenal (Negro's second sense; Kleiner's EPSR)
All we can *know* about phenomenal character is its structure; something more may exist. The exact
counterpart of the Map's own epistemic structural realism about physics, transposed to the phenomenal —
and therefore the position the Map is closest to holding without having noticed. Worth working out
whether the Map's commitments imply EPSR about the phenomenal, given that it holds acquaintance
delivers one non-structural item directly. If acquaintance is genuine, the Map denies EPSR about the
phenomenal while asserting ESR about physics, and owes an account of the asymmetry.

### The quality-space ancestry (Austen Clark 1993, 2000; Rosenthal 2010, 2015, 2025; Gert; O'Brien & Opie)
The thirty-year lineage, and the reason the position is not new. Clark 2000 supplies the debunking claim
in its sharpest form, quoted inside FKL 2021: "When one sees a patch of orange, the experience seems to
involve an intrinsic monadic quale: the quale orange. But this experience is an illusion. The facts in
virtue of which that experience is an experience of orange, and not of some other quality, are all
relational facts." **[metadata only — reached inside FKL, not read]**

## Key Debates

### Is the phenomenal character of an experience constituted by its relational position, or merely identified by it?
- **Sides**: metaphysical structuralists (Loorits; Lyre) — constituted. Levine (1995), and FKL themselves
  reporting the objection — identification does not settle constitution.
- **Current state**: **staged, explicitly, by the proponents.** FKL state the objection, grant that
  scientists "may be agnostic" about it, and bracket it. Negro's three arguments then press the
  constitution claim directly. This is the debate the new article should join, and it is the Map's own
  form/content distinction (`concepts/reductionism`) arriving in a different vocabulary.

### Does holism make qualities incommensurable across subjects and across time?
- **Sides**: Sebastián (2025) — yes, and the pain case makes it ethically serious. FKL (2021) — variability
  in individual structure "is expected and reflects the true nature of experiential qualities", so holism
  about phenomenality escapes the Fodor and Lepore (1992) objections to psychosemantic holism.
- **Current state**: live, joined in print, with FKL's reply already on the record.

### Can a single-experience subject exist? Does a new experience change old ones?
- **Sides**: Negro (2025) — the lonely and novel experience arguments say metaphysical structuralism gets
  both wrong. FKL (2021) — bite the bullet on the first: "even if (metaphysically) all one could ever
  experience were a single color, one could not know which color it was—or that it was a color at all. Our
  method of identification is structural and holistic."
- **Current state**: **directly joined, and the joining is quotable in both directions.** The strongest
  concrete exchange available to the article.

### Does the Newman problem defeat structuralism about the phenomenal?
- **Sides**: Lyre (2022, 2025) — solved by spatiotemporal grounding. Kleiner (2025) — dissolved by defining
  structural claims to require higher-order phenomenal R-properties. Chalmers (2022) and Frigg and Votsis
  (2011) — solved by restricting to natural relations, which Kleiner rejects.
- **Current state**: **contested among structuralists, with two competing repairs published in the same
  issue.** The Map's Newman lever, which `concepts/ontic-structural-realism` L75 treats as its most
  promising objection, is stale as stated and must engage these two repairs. This is the most important
  single revision the downstream article forces.

### Are inverted qualia conceivable?
- **Sides**: Loorits — denies ideal positive conceivability outright. Lyre — concedes metaphysical
  possibility cannot be ruled out, rules out the contingent and nomological cases empirically, and rests
  on Lewisian humility.
- **Current state**: **the two leading proponents disagree**, and the disagreement is exactly at the point
  the Map cares about. Worth stating plainly: the position's strongest advocate for the metaphysics
  (Loorits) has the weaker argument at the crucial step, and the advocate with the developed programme
  (Lyre) concedes the step. `concepts/inverted-qualia` is the natural second home for this.

### Can structuralism explain why there is experience at all, not just which experience?
- **Sides**: Fink (2024) — pushes NPS toward a general theory. Mihálik and Marvan (2025) — five challenges,
  of which the "more of the same" challenge is the sharpest.
- **Current state**: open, and the structuralist form of the Map's own question.

## Potential Article Angles

**Recommended shape: a standalone `concepts/` article, ~2,400 words, on structuralism about phenomenal
character**, scoped as the dialectic rather than a survey. Its job, in order:

1. **Open by naming the position and its holders**, with the FKL self-naming quote and Kleiner's OPSR
   definition. The lead must not repeat the constructed-composite concession — that concession belongs to
   `ontic-structural-realism` and is false here. The article's opening contrast is the reason it exists:
   OSR-about-physics threatens the Map's route; this threatens Tenet 1 directly, and in Lyre's version
   Tenet 3 as well.
2. **Adopt Negro's three-way split** (methodological / epistemological / metaphysical) as the article's
   spine, and **concede the methodological version explicitly**, citing FKL's own agnosticism sentence.
   The Map loses nothing and gains the standing to refuse the metaphysical version.
3. **Put the individuation/constitution distinction at the centre.** FKL state it, grant it and bracket
   it; Levine (1995) is its canonical statement; it is the Map's own form/content argument in another
   vocabulary. Cross-link `concepts/reductionism`.
4. **Report the debate rather than inventing it.** Negro's Relational Mary, novel-experience and
   lonely-experience arguments; Sebastián's incommensurability; Mihálik and Marvan's "more of the same".
   Give FKL's single-colour reply as the counter, because it is on the record and it is a real bullet
   bitten.
5. **Re-aim the Newman lever.** The article must engage Lyre's spatiotemporal grounding (2025) and
   Kleiner's R-property condition (2025), and should press the point that Kleiner's repair needs
   higher-order *phenomenal* properties to make its structural claims true. Flag that Newman presses the
   Map's own epistemic structuralism too — the asymmetry warning from the 2026-09-04 note still applies.
6. **Quote Lyre's concession, in his words and with his qualification** — that if quiddistic qualia are
   the core difficulty then "the hard problem cannot be solved structurally", followed by his reason for
   thinking the possibility is not worth upholding. Presenting the concession without the reply would be
   the over-concession's mirror image.
7. **Note what the position costs and what it predicts**: rejection of inverted qualia, holism about
   experience, and the measurement programme (similarity ratings, representational geometry) that makes
   it empirically live. The Map should say plainly that this rival has testable consequences, which is
   more than most rivals it treats.

**Alternative shape, weaker on findability and stronger on calibration**: `topics/the-steelman-for-phenomenal-structuralism`.
Less apt than for OSR, because there the steelman genre was earning its keep by labelling a construction,
and here there is nothing constructed to label.

**Fold option, if no new article is wanted**: not recommended, because the corrections listed above are
needed either way and the material is too large for a paragraph in
`concepts/ontic-structural-realism` — which is 2655w and already in `soft_warning`.

**Calibration the downstream expand must preserve.**
- **OSR is a defeater for the Map's route, not its thesis; phenomenal structuralism reaches the thesis.**
  Do not blur them. Keeping them apart is the whole reason for a separate page.
- **The field is young and says so.** Quote Fink and Lee's "currently at an early stage of development…
  scattered across different debates" rather than claiming either that the rival is unstaged or that the
  literature is settled.
- **Three critics of this rival are not the Map's allies.** Negro wants methodological structuralism to
  flourish; Sebastián recommends representationalism and explicitly wants qualities understood "without
  invoking intrinsic properties". Cite their arguments, decline their conclusions, and say which is
  which.
- **Do not over-concede.** Lyre's hard-problem concession is scope-limited and he immediately argues the
  scope is empty. The Map's own tell for this failure — *no possible / cannot ever / in principle
  undetectable* — applies here.

Follow `obsidian/project/writing-style.md`: important information first, named-anchor summaries for the
forward reference to Newman, background only where framed for the dualist perspective (skip the
psychophysics history), explicit tenet alignment, and no "This is not X. It is Y." construction.
