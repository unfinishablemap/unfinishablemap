## Headline Findings (read first)

1. **Saad's default is nomic, which is a different kind of default from those in actual causation.** In the actual-causation literature, defaults and normality are drawn from statistical, moral, legal and functional norms. Halpern and Hitchcock accept that their account of actual causation therefore incorporates subjective, context-dependent and value-laden elements, and they place objectivity in the structural equations instead. Saad defines his default by subtracting laws: it is "what results from applying physical laws and not applying laws of phenomenal causation". Its nearest precedent in the literature is the Newtonian "undisturbed evolution" default. Hall offers this case as his example of a default that may, unlike most, be "a purely objective matter" (with a "maybe"), and Maudlin builds his "quasi-Newtonian" laws on it. The harvest's worry was that a fundamental psychophysical law would end up quantifying over an interest-relative status. That happens only if the normality notion is borrowed, and Saad does not borrow it. **Verdict on this axis: (b), a better-grounded nomic default.**
2. **The vacuism threat is narrower than the brief assumed.** Vacuism (Lewis's and Stalnaker's orthodoxy, defended by Williamson) concerns counterfactuals whose antecedents are *metaphysically* impossible. A counterlegal antecedent is vacuous only if the laws are metaphysically necessary (Handfield 2004; Kimpton-Nye 2020). Saad's Organizational Invariance "is restricted to nomically possible worlds" and "is consistent with their [functional zombies'] metaphysical possibility". On Saad's own modal assumptions, then, the default conditional is counterlegal but not counterpossible, and standard semantics gives it a non-trivial value. The threat is real for a **necessitarian variant**. If the psychophysical laws were metaphysically necessary and vacuism held, every effect would fall within every default profile, and Subset Law* would assign every experience to every physical state. That trivialisation is the Map's own inference, not something found in the literature. The known rescues are Handfield's presupposition strategy, Kimpton-Nye's fictionalism, and non-vacuist impossible-worlds semantics (Nolan 1997).
3. **Determinacy depends on how the laws are partitioned, and Saad's own footnote 15 shows the partition is a substantive choice.** Saad classes quantum interactionism's collapse postulate as "a basic psychophysical law rather than as a physical law". On that classification, subtracting the phenomenal laws leaves only unitary evolution, so no Born-weighted outcome distribution remains to serve as the default. The Map's candidate reading of the default as the Born-rule distribution (a Map reading, not Saad's) is therefore well defined only if the law that actualises outcomes is physical. The Map already posits exactly that in [prebiotic-collapse](/concepts/prebiotic-collapse/), where objective reduction supplies the baseline. The fold should state this condition where the identification is argued.
4. **A well-defined default does not certify efficacy, and the Map should say so without conceding epiphenomenalism.** In the default/deviant frameworks where a nomic default is best grounded, an effect is a deviation from the default (Maudlin). Hall suspects that a sharp default/deviant distinction is what enables us to tell preemption from overdetermination. Delegated experiences produce no deviation, because they cause "whatever effects their (default) causal profiles encode" (Saad). Saad secures their efficacy by law (the Delegatory Law) and by a primitivist view of causation: "deep joints in nature underwritten by laws of nature" (n. 25). Difference-making plays no part. This restates the open bias-without-deviation question ([P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3); [prebiotic-collapse](/concepts/prebiotic-collapse/) L128) in the vocabulary of default theory. Today's sibling note on type and token causation reaches the same point, so **no separate fold is planned for it**.

## Executive Summary

Theories of actual causation use "defaults" to break ties that structural equations leave open: early preemption versus short circuit, overdetermination versus preemption, omissions. Those defaults are either normality defaults or dynamical defaults. Normality defaults are statistical, prescriptive or functional (Hitchcock & Knobe 2009; Halpern & Hitchcock 2015). Dynamical defaults describe what a system does when nothing acts on it (Hall 2007; Maudlin 2004; Hitchcock 2007; Menzies). Normality defaults are admitted to be context-relative, and critics argue that defaults of either kind generate "under-constrained unclarities" (Blanchard & Schaffer 2017).

Saad's default causal profile belongs to neither family exactly. It is a **law-subtraction default**: the profile a physical property has when the physical laws are applied and the laws of phenomenal causation are not. That makes it closest to the dynamical family. It also escapes the interest-relativity that would be fatal for a fundamental psychophysical law. Its price is that its antecedent is counterlegal, because Organizational Invariance makes the experience-free functional duplicate nomically impossible.

Counterlegality is harmless under standard semantics if the antecedent is metaphysically possible, which Saad's text allows. It becomes trivialising only under necessitarianism about the psychophysical laws combined with vacuism. The determinacy that remains depends on a principled partition of the laws into physical and phenomenal. Saad's own footnote 15 shows that partition can reclassify collapse, and with it what the default is. Finally, a well-defined default is neutral on efficacy. Delegation's efficacy rests on Saad's law-underwritten primitivism, not on difference-making. That is the open [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3) question again, not a concession.

### Saad, B. (2025). "A dualist theory of experience." *Philosophical Studies* 182(3-4): 939–967
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-025-02290-3 (PMC12062107)
- **Type**: Journal article (open access). Read in full.
- **Key points** (all [raw]):
  - The definition: "Let a default causal profile be one that a property has conditional on the absence of non-physical, phenomenal interference. For physical states, default causal profiles will be what results from applying physical laws and not applying laws of phenomenal causation."
  - The analogy: "The sergeant has this power by default, i.e. in the absence of interference from the major."
  - The default can be chancy (n. 2): "We can think of (forward-looking) causal profiles as mappings from circumstances of instantiation to effects or probability distributions over candidate effects."
  - Modal status: "Organizational Invariance is restricted to nomically possible worlds." It "entails the nomological impossibility of functional zombies … but it is consistent with their metaphysical possibility".
  - Laws of phenomenal causation may be essentialist: "It is open to dualists to construe these laws as either contingent causal laws or as principles that systematically encode essential causal powers of experiential properties."
  - Partial application of laws is native to Saad's framework. He defines a candidate effect as follows: "a state x is a candidate effect of an experience relative to a given law if the other laws do not forbid that experience from causing x when applied to that experience and its circumstances".
  - n. 15, on quantum interactionist dualism, says it "construes the collapse postulate that generates its distinctive observable predictions as a basic psychophysical law rather than as a physical law".
  - n. 25: on a counterfactual theory, distinctions among joint causes, preemptive causes, overdeterminers and epiphenomena might be "merely verbal ones based on an arbitrary decision about which system of weights for cross-world similarity to use in evaluating the relevant counterfactuals".   - Observational Closure is inherited from the default: "provided that physical states do not by default cause observable violations of Closure, no such violations will ensue".
  - No deviation: "sensory experiences rather than those physical states cause in a given circumstance whatever effects their (default) causal profiles encode for that circumstance".
  - n. 55 raises and answers a worry: "one might worry that whether experiences preemptively cause rather than overdetermine their effects is a merely verbal issue". Saad answers with Schaffer's Merlin/Morgana trumping case.
  - Upward Systematicity: "Subset Law* is a simple principle that fixes experiences as a function of physical states."
- **Saad never uses** "counterlegal" (0 hits), "counternomic" or "normality". The classification of the default as counterlegal belongs to the Map and the reviewer.

### Hall, N. (2007). "Structural equations and causation." *Philosophical Studies* 132(1): 109–136
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-006-9057-9 (DASH: http://nrs.harvard.edu/urn-3:HUL.InstRepos:3710361)
- **Type**: Journal article. The posted version was read in full, so cite by section (§6, §8) and not by journal page.
- **Key points** [raw]:
  - "Very roughly: A system's default behavior is the behavior it would exhibit, if nothing acted on it."
  - The test "is explicitly causal, thanks to the word 'acting'". He adds: "At this point, I do not know whether we can provide a fully general test that isn't causal, tacitly or explicitly."
  - "Third, what counts as a default state is not, I think, a purely objective matter. (Well, maybe it is in some cases: e.g., for Newtonian particles.)" and "Context can, within severe constraints, affect what counts as the appropriate default state for some part of the world."

  - "So a comprehensive, illuminating account of the default/deviant distinction is not going to be easy to find."

- **Bearing**: Saad's second formulation of the default carries Hall's "canonical environment" strategy from spatial environments over to *law-sets*: the canonical "environment" is the physical laws alone. Hall's hedged objectivity for the Newtonian case is the closest licence the literature offers. Hall's general verdict (defaults are not "a purely objective matter") is the danger.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral.

### Halpern, J. Y. & Hitchcock, C. (2015). "Graded Causation and Defaults." *BJPS* 66(2): 413–457
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axt050; preprint arXiv:1309.1226
- **Type**: Journal article; preprint read in full. **All quotations are preprint wording.**
- **Key points** [raw, preprint]:
  - "A default is an assumption about what happens, or what is the case, when no additional information is given." Normality has "both a descriptive and a prescriptive dimension". The prescriptive norms they list are moral, legal, institutional policies, and "norms of proper functioning in machines or organisms".
  - The worry is that "the incorporation of norms will render causation: (1) subjective, (2) socially constructed, (3) value-laden, (4) context-dependent, and (5) vague." Their reply: "actual causation is the wrong place to look for objectivity" and "Causal structure, as represented in the equations of a causal model, is objective."
  - They distinguish the dynamical family: "Here, instead of taking the absence of change to be the default, one takes a certain evolution of an 'undisturbed' system to be its default behavior. The classic example is from Newtonian mechanics: the default behavior of a body is to continue in a state of uniform motion." They credit this to Hitchcock (2007), Maudlin (2004) and Menzies (2004, 2007).
  - On constraint: "we do not think that the assignment of norms and defaults is completely unconstrained" and "An assignment of norms and defaults is the sort of thing that can be defended and criticized on rational grounds."
  - On Hitchcock & Knobe [secondary]: "Hitchcock and Knobe [2009] argue that attributions of actual causation typically serve to identify appropriate targets of corrective intervention."
- **Bearing**: this is the central evidence for Headline 1. The authors themselves move objectivity out of normality-laden actual causation and into causal structure, which is the level at which Saad's laws sit.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral.

## Major Positions

### Normality defaults in actual causation (Hitchcock & Knobe 2009; Halpern 2008; Halpern & Hitchcock 2015)
- **Core claim**: judgments of actual causation depend on what is normal (statistically, prescriptively or functionally), and a formal account should include a normality ordering.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral. Imported into a psychophysical law, this notion would make the law interest-relative, against Saad's own Upward Systematicity. The Map must not gloss the default in these terms.

### Dynamical defaults (Hall 2007; Maudlin 2004; Hitchcock 2007; Menzies via SEP)
- **Core claim**: a system's default is what it does when nothing acts on it. For some law-forms (quasi-Newtonian) this is fixed by the laws.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral. This is the family in which Saad's default is best grounded.

### Default scepticism (Blanchard & Schaffer 2017)
- **Core claim**: defaults add under-constrained unclarity; apt-model constraints and cognitive-bias explanations should replace them.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral. It threatens Saad only if the physical/phenomenal partition is modeller-relative.

### Vacuism (Lewis–Stalnaker orthodoxy; Williamson) versus non-vacuism (Nolan 1997; Berto et al. 2018; Tan 2019)
- **Core disagreement**: whether counterfactuals with metaphysically impossible antecedents are all true.
- **Relation to site tenets**: idle for Saad's stated version. It matters only for a necessitarian variant.

### Saad's primitivism about causation (n. 25)
- **Core claim**: "the most basic causal relations are deep joints in nature underwritten by laws of nature", which is how he avoids deflation by similarity weights.
- **Relation to site tenets**: compatible with Bidirectional Interaction. It is the point at which delegation's efficacy is secured without difference-making.

## The Key Question: Is Saad's Default Well Defined? {#key-question}

Seven tests follow. Each states which way it cuts. "(a)" means ill-defined or arbitrary; "(b)" means a legitimate nomic default.

1. **Is it a normality default?** No. Saad's default subtracts laws; it is not a ranking by what is typical or proper. **(b).** The harvest's interest-relativity worry applies only to borrowing the H&H notion, and neither Saad nor any Map page does that (0 normality-vocabulary glosses).
2. **Is a law-defined default objective?** Hall hedges ("maybe it is in some cases: e.g., for Newtonian particles") and H&H separate the "undisturbed system" family. **(b), on one condition**: the total laws must divide into "inertial" physical laws and phenomenal "laws of deviation". Maudlin's warning that "Laws, of course, need not be quasi-Newtonian" applies. Saad's construction builds the division in by formulating two separate laws, so the division is part of the postulate, not something discovered.
3. **Is the definition circular, or causal?** Hall's "nothing acting" test "is explicitly causal". Saad's first wording ("absence of … phenomenal interference") is likewise causal. His second wording ("applying physical laws and not applying laws of phenomenal causation") swaps the causal test for a law-subtraction test. Subset Law* quantifies over the default, and the default is fixed by the physical laws alone. **(b): no circularity.** This is consistent with [delegatory-causation](/concepts/delegatory-causation/) L97, which says the "default" qualifier is what keeps the theory from being circular.
4. **Does counterlegality trivialise it?** No, on Saad's assumptions, since the zombie antecedent is "consistent with their metaphysical possibility" and vacuism concerns only metaphysical impossibility. **(b).** Under necessitarianism about Subset Law* the answer is yes: vacuism makes every effect fall within every default, and Subset Law* assigns every experience to every physical state (the Map's inference). **(a) for that variant**, unless it adopts Handfield, Kimpton-Nye or Nolan. Note that Saad's essentialist option attaches to the laws of *phenomenal causation*. Essentialism about experiences' powers does not make the zombie world impossible, because the experience is absent there. Only necessity of the *distribution* law threatens the default.
5. **Is its evaluation determinate?** If the default is read as a Lewisian counterfactual, its value depends on similarity weights over law-changes, which is the arbitrariness Saad's n. 25 raises and dismisses. If it is read as a computation, Saad's own phrasing ("applying … laws"; candidate effects "when applied to that experience and its circumstances") makes it the output of the physical laws on the actual physical state, and no similarity ordering is needed. Because Observational Closure holds, the physical regularities used are exactly those actually confirmed. **(b) on the computational reading; (a) on a free-weights counterfactual reading.**
6. **Does indeterminism or the collapse classification break it?** Chancy defaults are allowed (n. 2). The partition, however, decides what the default *is* (n. 15). If collapse is psychophysical, the default of a superposed brain state is unitary evolution only, with no Born-weighted outcome distribution. **Conditional.** This bears on the Map's Born-rule reading rather than on Saad's theory, and the Map's [prebiotic-collapse](/concepts/prebiotic-collapse/) posit (a physical objective-reduction baseline) meets the condition.
7. **Does a well-defined default show that experience is efficacious?** No, and it does not show the opposite either. In default/deviant frameworks, effects are deviations (Maudlin), and the preemption/overdetermination contrast may itself depend on a sharp default (Hall's suspicion). Delegated experience produces no deviation by construction. Its efficacy rests on the Delegatory Law and on Saad's primitivism. **Neutral.** The Map should describe this as the price of delegation's authority being something other than difference-making, which is the open [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3) question. It must never be stated as epiphenomenalism. The sibling type/token note reaches the same structural conclusion from probabilistic causation.

**Net verdict.** The default is well defined on Saad's stated assumptions. It is better grounded than the defaults of actual-causation theory because it answers to laws, not to norms. It is determinate, however, only relative to a principled partition of the laws, to the contingency of the psychophysical distribution law (or a non-vacuist semantics), and to a computational reading of "applying" the laws. None of this is evidence that the default exists or that dualism is true. The literature supplies criteria that the default meets or fails, and no support.

## Relation to Site Perspective (graded: compatible at most)

- **[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism): compatible.** The law-subtraction default presupposes distinct physical and phenomenal law families. A dualist posits these, but the literature neither supplies nor supports them.
- **[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction): compatible.** Definedness is neutral on efficacy. Saad secures efficacy by law and primitivism. Never concede epiphenomenalism: the absence of deviation is [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3), which is open, not a refutation.
- **[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction): compatible.** The Born-rule reading of the default needs a physical actualisation law, which the Map posits ([prebiotic-collapse](/concepts/prebiotic-collapse/)). Saad's n. 15 classification shows that a consciousness-collapse variant would lose the reading.
- **[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds): compatible.** Without an actualisation law there is no outcome-distribution default. [delegation-meets-quantum-selection](/topics/delegation-meets-quantum-selection/) L72 already records this dependence.
- **[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits): binds both ways** (`tenets.md` L145). The nomic default must not be preferred for being "simpler" or "more objective", and the normality reading must not be dismissed as unparsimonious. That the default is well defined is no evidence that it exists.
