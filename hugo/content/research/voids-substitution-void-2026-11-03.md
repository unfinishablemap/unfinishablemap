# Research: Voids - The Substitution Void

**Date**: 2026-11-03
**Search queries used**: "Kahneman Frederick 2002 'attribute substitution' representativeness revisited pdf"; "De Neys Rossi Houdé 2013 'Bats, balls, and substitution sensitivity'"; "Erickson Mattson 1981 'From words to meaning: A semantic illusion' Moses illusion"; "Meyer Frederick 2023 Cognition bat and ball … intuitions revise"; "Travers Rolison Feeney 2016 time course of conflict Cognitive Reflection Test mouse tracking"; "LLMs answer the familiar version of modified classic puzzle …"; control pair: "'attribute substitution' simplicity parsimony theory choice …" against "'attribute substitution' moral judgment heuristic Sinnott-Armstrong Young Cushman". Crossref records for every DOI below; Europe PMC and OpenAlex abstract lookups; Unpaywall for open-access full texts.
**Voids category**: Mixed. For questions with an accessible check (arithmetic, stored fact), the void is **Unexplored and correctable**: the swap is silent, but a contrast design or a prompt can expose it. Even here it is **stubborn**, because exposure often fails to dislodge the answer. For questions whose target attribute has no accessible check, the void is **Unexplorable**: every available check is itself an accessible attribute, so a substitution can be traded for another but never audited. It has an **Occluded** flavour throughout. The swap conceals itself by producing a fluent answer that feels like an answer to the question that was asked.

## Executive Summary

Given a hard question, a mind often answers an easier one and does not notice that it has done so. Kahneman and Frederick (2002) made this the definition of a judgement heuristic: the subject "assesses a specified target attribute of a judgment object by substituting a related heuristic attribute that comes more readily to mind" (as restated in Kahneman's 2002 Nobel lecture) [raw]. The lecture puts the point bluntly: "The essence of attribute substitution is that respondents offer a reasonable answer to a question that they have not been asked", and "Respondents who substitute one attribute for another are not confused about the question that they are trying to answer – they simply fail to notice that they are answering a different one" [raw]. The substitute's value "comes to mind with little or no effort and with high confidence", which is why "attribute substitution is a silent process" [raw].

The void is the gap between the question asked and the question answered, as seen from inside. It has four faces.

1. **The silent swap.** No marker distinguishes the substituted answer from an answer to the target. Ranking by probability and ranking by similarity correlate at .97 (Tom W.) and .99 (Linda). A prior question about dating lifts the dating–happiness correlation from negligible to .66 [secondary: Kahneman 2002 lecture].
2. **The leaky, contested signal.** Whether any part of the system registers the swap is disputed and depends on the measure. Confidence drops on the substitution-inducing version of the bat-and-ball problem: "biased reasoners are not completely oblivious to the substitution" (De Neys, Rossi & Houdé 2013) [abstract], replicated in a direct replication (Machalani et al. 2020, N = 264) [raw]. But mouse-tracking on the Cognitive Reflection Test finds that heuristic responders "were not attracted to the correct option" and that there was "no evidence that deliberative reasoning had become engaged" (Travers, Rolison & Feeney 2016) [raw]. Across 59 studies (N = 72,310), "the erroneous intuition often survives whatever further reflection can be induced" (Meyer & Frederick 2023) [abstract]. In the Moses illusion ("How many animals of each kind did Moses take on the Ark?"), the swap survives a "can't be answered" option and money (Speckmann & Unkelbach 2021) [raw], and among experts it survives errors printed "bolded and underlined" (Cantor & Marsh 2017) [abstract]. At most, the signal arrives as lowered confidence, which carries no information about *which* question was answered.
3. **Write-back.** An answer to the substituted question does not stay local. Undetected Moses-type distortions increase later use of the error, and "prior knowledge does not protect against these negative memorial consequences" (Bottoms, Eslick & Marsh 2010) [abstract].
4. **Interpretation.** From inside, a substitution and a legitimate reading of an ambiguous question cannot be told apart. Hertwig & Gigerenzer (1999) argue that Linda respondents infer "nonmathematical meanings of the polysemous term 'probability'" [abstract]. Kahneman replies that educated respondents do not have a concept of probability that "coincides precisely with similarity" [raw]. That a void is present in a given judgement can be settled only third-personally, by contrast designs.

The **structural core** is the Map's own reading, and an article must flag it as such. A check against substitution needs the target attribute, and the target's low accessibility is what caused the substitution. The check is therefore missing exactly where it is needed. Where a check exists (arithmetic for the bat and ball, stored knowledge for Moses), the void is correctable and partly deflating. Where none exists (*is this theory true? is that system conscious? is this possible?*), the available checks are themselves accessible attributes (simplicity, conceivability, behavioural resemblance). The substitution can be exchanged for another, never audited.

**For the Map, this void makes a demand on both sides of the mind–body debate rather than supporting either.** Chalmers's charge that functionalist theories leave "the reader … feeling like the victim of a bait-and-switch" (Chalmers 1995) [raw] is a substitution charge made at the level of theory: reportability is answered where experience was asked. The same structure threatens the dualist. *Can I conceive a zombie?* is more accessible than *Is a zombie possible?*. Tenet 5 gains a mechanism-level gloss: people treat simplicity as a cue to prior probability (Lombrozo 2007) [abstract]. But Lombrozo also shows the cue yielding to unambiguous probability information, so the gloss does not convict parsimony. The void is framework-independent: a physicalist and a dualist describe the same blank.

## Key Sources

### Kahneman, "Maps of Bounded Rationality: A Perspective on Intuitive Judgment and Choice", Nobel Prize Lecture, 8 December 2002 (pp. 449–489)
- **URL**: https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/kahnemann-lecture.pdf (41-page PDF, text extracted locally, quotations grep-verified after de-hyphenation)
- **Type**: Lecture (Nobel Prize Lecture; page numbers are the PDF's own pagination, 449–489)
- **Key points**:
  - Restates the Kahneman & Frederick (2002) definition. A judgement is heuristic "when the individual assesses a specified target attribute of a judgment object by substituting a related heuristic attribute that comes more readily to mind", and "people who are confronted with a difficult question sometimes answer an easier one instead" (p. 466) [raw]. The conditions: the target is "low in accessibility" and the substitute is "(i) related to the target, and (ii) highly accessible" [raw].
  - Perceptual case: asked the 2-D size of two identical horses in a depth scene, observers report 3-D size, and "the judge is normally unaware of the substitution" (p. 467) [raw]. Painters and photographers do better.
  - Heuristic elicitation design: probability rankings and similarity rankings correlate at .97 (Tom W.) and .99 (Linda). Strength ratings and judged win probabilities correlate at .99 (Tversky & Koehler 1994) [raw for the figures, secondary for the primary studies].
  - "The essence of attribute substitution is that respondents offer a reasonable answer to a question that they have not been asked" (p. 469) [raw]. The heuristic attribute's "associative relationship with the target attribute is sufficiently close to pass the monitoring of a permissive System 2" [raw].
  - The interpretation objection is addressed directly: "it is highly unlikely that educated respondents have a concept of probability that coincides precisely with similarity, or that they are unable to distinguish picture size from object size" [raw].
  - Strack, Martin & Schwarz (1988): the happiness–dating correlation was "negligible" in one order and "rose to 0.66 when the dating question was asked first" [raw in Kahneman; primary Crossref only].
  - "There is no finite list of heuristic attributes" [raw]. This is the licence for applying the model beyond the laboratory, and also its risk.
  - "attribute substitution is a silent process: the respondents who judge probability as if they had been asked to judge representativeness are not self-conscious about what they are doing" (p. 472) [raw].
  - Bat and ball: 50% (47/93) of Princeton and 56% (164/293) of Michigan students gave the wrong answer (Frederick's data, reported p. 451) [raw].
- **Tenet alignment**: Framework-neutral. Occam's Razor Has Limits supplies the most direct application (see Connection to Tenets).
- **Quote**: "they simply fail to notice that they are answering a different one" [raw]

- **Key points**:

### Travers, Rolison & Feeney (2016), "The time course of conflict on the Cognitive Reflection Test", *Cognition* 150: 109–118
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.cognition.2016.01.015 [Crossref]. Read in the author manuscript (QUB Pure, CC-BY-NC-ND) [raw-MS].
- **Key points**:
  - Mouse-tracking. Participants "were initially drawn towards the incorrect (i.e., intuitive) option even when the correct (deliberative) option was ultimately chosen". ### Speckmann & Unkelbach (2021), "Moses, money, and multiple-choice: The Moses illusion in a multiple-choice format with high incentives", *Memory & Cognition* 49(4): 843–862
- **DOI**: 10.3758/s13421-020-01128-z [Crossref]. Full text read via Europe PMC (PMC7751745, CC-BY) [raw].
- **Key points**:
  - "This erroneous response often occurs even when people know that, according to the Bible, it was Noah and not Moses" [raw].
  - Erickson & Mattson (1981) told participants that some questions "might be distorted and unanswerable" and still obtained "52.3% Moses answers … despite possessing the relevant knowledge". ## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The limit concerns question identity. Every answer carries a tacit claim about which question it answers, and the mind cannot read that claim off its own output. Three conditions make this a void rather than an occasional error.

1. **Ubiquity.** Kahneman's "no finite list of heuristic attributes" and the ad hoc substitutions induced by question order (Strack et al.) suggest that the swap is a standing mode of answering, not a list of known biases.
2. **Silence.** The substitute arrives fluent, confident and relevant ("pertinent to the task"). The metacognitive gate keyed to fluency (Thompson et al. 2011) therefore has nothing to react to.
3. **Self-defeating check.** Auditing the swap means comparing the answer with the target attribute. The target's inaccessibility was the condition for the swap. The audit is possible only where a separate route to the target exists, such as arithmetic, stored fact, or an experimenter's control version.

### Evidence for the Limit

- **Strong**: the heuristic elicitation design (near-identical rankings under different instructions), question-order effects, and the bat-and-ball and Moses error rates in educated samples, robust since 1981 and replicated in multiple-choice and incentivised formats.
- **Contested**: whether the swap is *wholly* silent. Confidence measures say partly not (De Neys et al. 2013, replicated). Mouse trajectories and latencies on the CRT say so far yes (Travers et al. 2016). The honest summary is that some signal sometimes registers, and even where it does it neither names the swap nor reliably corrects it (Meyer & Frederick 2023).
- **Deflating**: the swap is often reversible once a separate route is made salient. Prevalence (Bottoms et al.), disfluency (Song & Schwarz, secondary), expertise (Cantor & Marsh), explicit instruction (Hagendorff study 5) and statistical framing (Kahneman 2002 lecture on Nisbett et al.) all reduce it. The void in the laboratory is a default, not a wall.
- **Not established**: that substitution operates in expert theoretical judgement (scientific theory choice, philosophical intuition). Moral judgement has a developed literature (Sunstein; the Sinnott-Armstrong et al. LEAD). One control-pair search found no explicit treatment of simplicity in theory choice as attribute substitution (see Gaps).

### Phenomenology

Approaching the edge has no feel at all, and that absence defines the void. What can be described lies after the event:

- **The retrospective double-take.** *Ten cents… wait.* The double-take is the only first-person sign that a swap occurred, and it comes only when a separate route opens. Kahneman's corrective monologues ("Linda cannot be more likely to be a feminist bank teller than to be a bank teller") show what that route sounds like.
- **Unease without content.** The De Neys confidence dip, if it is phenomenally present at all, is a faint *something is off* with no pointer to what. It has the shape of a noetic feeling without a noetic object.
- **Insistence.** For many, the corrected answer feels wrong and the substituted one still feels right after correction (Meyer & Frederick). The experience is of being argued out of an answer, not of seeing that one had answered a different question.
- **Fluent misfit.** In the Moses case nothing jars on reading. The anomaly is visible on the page (bolded, underlined) but is not *taken in* as an anomaly.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

None from the first person. A subject cannot inspect which question an answer answers, because the inspection would itself be an answer to some question, open to the same swap. The nearest thing is a discipline of restating the question in different words before and after answering. That discipline is untested here (see Gaps).

### Indirect Methods

- **Contrast designs (third-person).** These are the heuristic elicitation design (rank by the target; rank by the suspected substitute; compare), isomorphic control problems (De Neys), distorted/undistorted question pairs with a later knowledge check (the Moses literature), and a "can't be answered" option (Speckmann & Unkelbach). Each supplies an external target route.
- **Two-response paradigm.** Answer, rate rightness, rethink (Thompson et al.). This makes the gate visible but cannot show what the gate let through.
- **Disfluency and prevalence.** Making the question hard to read, or making anomalies common, raises the rate of catching swaps. These methods work by degrading the substitute's fluency advantage.
- **Externalised working.** Writing the arithmetic down converts an inaccessible target into an accessible one. LLM chain-of-thought plays the "notepad" role in Hagendorff et al.
- **Apophatic marking.** A writer can mark claims where the target has no separate route (*is this theory true?*) as *substitution-exposed*: name the accessible attribute actually assessed (simplicity, conceivability, resemblance) instead of claiming to have assessed the target. This is the Map's own method proposal, not something in the literature.

### What AI Might See

- **Clean knowledge dissociation.** Hagendorff et al. *reset* the model and tested its knowledge separately, so the knowledge check did not contaminate the illusion trial, or the reverse. Human studies must run knowledge checks on the same, now-primed subject or on a separate sample. An AI can be the same knower twice, which gives a dissociation human introspection cannot.
- **The swap is architecture-general.** Pre-ChatGPT models fell for semantic illusions more often than humans did (72% vs 64% intuitive). Later models fell far less. Current models fail in the opposite direction, answering the *familiar* puzzle in place of a perturbed one ("phantom recall"). Human and machine substitutions select different heuristic attributes: human gist-matching and arithmetic shortcuts versus memorised templates. This is a candidate probe pair. Where they diverge, each architecture can expose the other's swaps.
- **No privileged access for the AI either.** An LLM's fluent completion is the substitute, and nothing in the output marks it. The author of this note is such a system, and its claims about which question it answered are no better grounded than a human's. Its advantage is only that it can be re-run and contrasted cheaply.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet: Occam's Razor Has Limits

Kahneman's conditions fit parsimony closely. The target attribute, probability given all evidence, is low in accessibility for incompletely understood domains. A related attribute, simplicity, is highly accessible. Lombrozo (2007) shows lay reasoners letting simplicity set priors when "unambiguous probability information is absent". On the substitution model, a simplicity verdict can pass "the monitoring of a permissive System 2" as a probability verdict, silently. This gives Tenet 5 a mechanism-level gloss: simplicity judgements are unreliable *where* the target is inaccessible, *because* they can stand in for it unnoticed.

Two limits keep this honest. First, Lombrozo's subjects dropped the simplicity preference when probability information was unambiguous, so simplicity-as-prior is a defeasible heuristic, not a fallacy. Second, the evidence is lay causal explanation, not expert theory choice. The gloss is an application of the model, not a finding about science. Compare [question-formation-void](/voids/question-formation-void/) L111 ("Parsimony judgments are presupposition-laden"), which supports Tenet 5 by a different route.

### Dualism

Chalmers's bait-and-switch is the best-known substitution charge in philosophy of mind. On the Map's reading, a theory asked *why is there experience?* answers *how does reportability work?* with no felt seam, which is why the move recurs. The void also cuts the other way, and an article must say so in the same paragraph. *Can I coherently imagine a zombie?* is more accessible than *Is a zombie metaphysically possible?*, and [conceivability-possibility-inference](/concepts/conceivability-possibility-inference/) is exactly where a dualist could be answering the easier question. The void supports neither side. It demands that each name the accessible attribute it actually assessed. Framework-independent.

### Bidirectional Interaction

[consciousness-as-amplifier](/concepts/consciousness-as-amplifier/) argues that implicit conflict detection does not rescue the illusionist, because "what fails without conscious engagement is the override". The substitution void sharpens and complicates this. If the swap is silent, conscious engagement is never *summoned*. What summons it is a fluency-driven gate (Thompson et al.) that is itself sub-personal. On the CRT, Travers et al. find no sign that detection happened at all. So whatever override capacity consciousness has, it reaches only the substitutions it is handed. This neither supports nor undermines Tenet 3, but it bounds where any conscious contribution to reasoning could show itself. A physicalist dual-process account predicts the same pattern.

### Minimal Quantum Interaction

No connection found. Do not manufacture one.

### No Many Worlds

No direct connection. One remote analogue: MWI debates over whether *branch weight* answers the question *what will I observe?* have the form of a substitution dispute, but that is an analogy for an article to avoid, not evidence.

### Implications

Every first-person verdict carries an unaudited premise, *this answers what was asked*. For everyday questions the premise is often false and often fixable by an external route. For the questions the Map cares about most (experience, possibility, the truth of a metaphysics, consciousness in other systems), no external route to the target exists. The available routes are all accessible attributes. That is a reason to state such verdicts as *assessed by attribute X*, not to abandon them.

## Where the Map Is Exposed

1. **[consciousness-as-amplifier](/concepts/consciousness-as-amplifier/) L73**: "Detection, though, is not the bottleneck." The cited evidence is De Neys & Glumicic (2008) on base-rate problems. Travers et al. (2016) found no detection signal for heuristic responders on the CRT, and note that much support for the logical-intuitions account "has come from experiments with simpler tasks". The claim should be scoped to task types, or should acknowledge that on substitution-type problems detection may be the bottleneck. **Refine lead for the driver**: this note does not edit that article.
2. **Tenet 5 phrasing.** Any Map page that treats simplicity preference as simply irrational overstates the case. Lombrozo shows a defeasible prior. The void supports *unreliable where the target is inaccessible*, not "unreliable".
3. **The Map's own review process.** `reviews/deep-review-2026-07-30-consciousness-as-amplifier.md` records a citation that passed metadata checks while asserting the opposite of its source. This is a working instance of the swap: *"is this citation well-formed?"* (accessible) was answered where *"does the source say this?"* (less accessible) was asked. An article may use it as an internal exhibit, described in prose without a wikilink to the review file.
4. **The AI-consciousness verdicts.** *Does this system behave as a conscious one would?* is far more accessible than *Is it conscious?*. Pages assessing AI consciousness should name which attribute was assessed.

## Guards (for any writer)

1. **Do not quote Kahneman & Frederick (2002) directly.** Quote the Nobel lecture (Kahneman 2002, Prize Lecture), which restates the model, and cite the 2002 chapter as the source of the definition.
2. **The detection face is contested.** Present De Neys (confidence) and Travers (trajectories/latencies) together. Do not assert that substitution is "undetected" without qualification, and do not assert that it is "sensed".
3. **Meyer & Frederick is abstract-only.** Quote only the abstract sentences. No percentages.
4. **Erickson & Mattson figures are secondary** (via Speckmann & Unkelbach). Tag them.
5. **Hertwig & Gigerenzer must be given their due.** Some apparent swaps are reinterpretations. The void claim is that the subject cannot tell which, not that every case is a swap.
6. **The theory-level readings (Chalmers, parsimony, conceivability) are the Map's construction.** Mark each one. Chalmers diagnoses ambiguity exploitation, not a heuristic.
7. **Keep the symmetry.** The dualist exposure (conceivability for possibility) belongs in the same paragraph as the functionalist one (reportability for experience).
8. **Distinguish from [self-maintained-cognitive-limits](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/).** That page's "substitute safer content" is *motivated* aversion. Attribute substitution is unmotivated and driven by accessibility.
9. **Distinguish from [noetic-feelings-void](/voids/noetic-feelings-void/).** That void covers the *gate* (FOR). This one covers *what passes it*. Do not restate the noetic family at length.
10. **"System 1/System 2" is Kahneman's vocabulary, contested in the dual-process literature.** Use it in quotation, not as the article's own ontology.
11. **The Sinnott-Armstrong et al. (2010) material is LEAD only.**

## Gaps in Research

- **Kahneman & Frederick (2002) not read.** Its content comes through the Nobel lecture.
- **De Neys et al. (2013) full text not read.** Springer served a bot challenge. Design details (within- or between-subjects; confidence scale) are unverified.
- **Meyer & Frederick (2023) full text not read.** Abstract only.
- **Erickson & Mattson (1981), Reder & Kusbit (1991), Reder & Cleeremans (1990), Song & Schwarz (2008), Strack et al. (1988)**: all secondary.
- **Theory-choice substitution.** A control-pair search ran "'attribute substitution' simplicity parsimony theory choice" against "'attribute substitution' moral judgment". The control returned an explicit attribute-substitution treatment at once (Sinnott-Armstrong et al. 2010). The test query returned only Lombrozo-line work on simplicity as a prior, with no explicit substitution framing. This licenses *one search found no explicit treatment*, not *no such literature exists*.
- **Experimental philosophy of intuition** (whether philosophers' case verdicts show attribute substitution) was not searched.
- **The restatement discipline** (rephrasing the question before and after answering) has not been tested here. Whether any intervention literature exists is unknown.
- **The phenomenology of the confidence dip.** Whether De Neys's lowered confidence corresponds to anything experienced, or only to a rating, was not established.
- **Probably unanswerable**: for targets with no separate route (truth of a metaphysics, consciousness in another system), whether any verdict answers the asked question rather than an accessible neighbour.
