## Executive Summary

**Verdict: `article`. Target: `concepts/basic-argument-and-constitutive-luck`, 367/370 by `count_section_files`.**

Galen Strawson's Basic Argument has three steps:
1. You do what you do "because of the way you are".
2. To be truly responsible for what you do, you must be truly responsible for the way you are, which would require being *causa sui*.
3. Nothing can be *causa sui*.

Strawson concludes that "true" moral responsibility is impossible whether or not determinism holds. The Map has no page on it. Five live pages call character "self-created" (the apex, `moral-responsibility`, `moral-implications-of-genuine-agency`, `living-with-the-map`, `ethics-under-dualism`). That is the claim the argument targets.

The outer reviewer's parity charge is correct, and Strawson made it himself:
- His third response attacks "a certain picture of the self" that decides independently of its character (1994: 20–21).
- He answers it with "whatever S decides, it decides as it does because of the way it is" (p. 21).
- He extends the demand for self-determination to Kant's "non-temporal noumenal nature" (n. 8).

So the regress reaches a non-physical self exactly as it reaches a brain. The Map's agent-substance is not *causa sui*, and nothing in agent causation makes it so.

The literature's agent-causal reply does not escape the regress. It **rejects the regress's demand** (premise 2):
- On the account Clarke defends (2003: 170–76, SEP-REPORTED), how one is explains the choice without determining it.
- The agent then causes the choice, so no sufficient source lies outside the agent.

That move escapes constitutive luck only by leaving the choice not fixed by the agent's nature. The rollback presses exactly that opening. Levy's "luck pincer" names the junction, and so does Strawson's own two-horned reply to S. **The Basic Argument and the rollback therefore end at one primitive, the agent's exercise of its power**, approached from opposite sides:
- The Basic Argument asks who made the maker.
- The rollback asks why this exercise went this way.

The Map's honest position has five parts:
1. The agent originates its exercises. It did not author its powers.
2. Character is self-*shaped*, not self-*created*.
3. Responsibility is graded and grows over time (O'Connor 2009; Mele 2006).
4. Whether origination without self-creation suffices for desert is exactly what Strawson denies. The Map's tenets do not settle it.
5. Strawson grants that people can change the way they are, "in certain respects" (1994: 7), so the Basic Argument leaves Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction) untouched. What it reaches is desert, and with desert the "ultimate desert" residue that `positions/agency-and-will` [P-A5](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a5) relies on.

### Strawson, G. (1994), "The Impossibility of Moral Responsibility": VERIFIED (full text)
- **Metadata**: *Philosophical Studies* 75(1–2): 5–24, doi 10.1007/bf00989879 (Crossref). Reprinted in Strawson, *Real Materialism and Other Essays* (OUP 2008), pp. 319–333, as "The Impossibility of Ultimate Moral Responsibility" (doi 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199267422.003.0014, Crossref).
- **First statement**: n. 1 says the cumbersome version is "Adapted from G. Strawson, 1986, pp. 28-30", that is, *Freedom and Belief* (Clarendon 1986). The revised edition is OUP 2010, doi 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199247493.001.0001. SEP Free Will cites "1986, ch. 2". VERIFIED in the 1994 note.
- **The name**: "There is an argument, which I will call the Basic Argument, which appears to prove that we cannot be truly or ultimately morally responsible for our actions. According to the Basic Argument, it makes no difference whether determinism is true or false." (p. 5)
- **Short form** (p. 5): "(1) Nothing can be causa sui – nothing can be the cause of itself. (2) In order to be truly morally responsible for one's actions one would have to be causa sui, at least in certain crucial mental respects. (3) Therefore nothing can be truly morally responsible."
- **The regress** (pp. 6–7):
  - Each choice of how to be needs prior "principles of choice" P1, then P2, and so on.
  - "Here we are setting out on a regress that we cannot stop. True self-determination is impossible because it requires the actual completion of an infinite series of choices of principles of choice." (p. 7)
- **The natural form** (p. 7):
  - One is initially the way one is through heredity and early experience.
  - Both the way one is moved to change oneself and one's success in doing so are fixed by how one already is.
  - Indeterminism does not help: "But it is absurd to suppose that indeterministic or random factors, for which one is ex hypothesi in no way responsible, can in themselves contribute in any way to one's being truly morally responsible for how one is."
- **What Strawson concedes** (p. 7). The Map must quote this so it does not overstate the target: "The claim, then, is not that people cannot change the way they are." … "The claim is only that people cannot be supposed to change themselves in such a way as to be or become truly or ultimately morally responsible for the way they are, and hence for their actions."
- **The standard** (p. 9). True moral responsibility "is responsibility of such a kind that, if we have it, then it makes sense, at least, to suppose that it could be just to punish some of us with (eternal) torment in hell and reward others with (eternal) bliss in heaven."
- **The restated argument** (pp. 12–13):
  - "(1) You do what you do, in any situation in which you find yourself, because of the way you are." (p. 12)
  - "(2) To be truly morally responsible for what you do you must be truly responsible for the way you are – at least in certain crucial mental respects." (p. 13)
  - "I take it that (1) is incontrovertible, and that it is (2) that must be resisted." (p. 13)
- **Causa sui** (p. 15): "Nothing can be causa sui in the required way." Nietzsche, *Beyond Good and Evil* §21, is quoted as precursor.
- **Against Kane, the libertarian response** (pp. 17–19):
  - "If my efforts of will shape my character in an admirable way, and in so doing are partly indeterministic in nature, while also being shaped (as Kane grants) by my already existing character, why am I not merely lucky?" (p. 18)
  - Our genes and early influences shape "the general bent and strength of our capacity to make efforts of will" (p. 18).
  - "Nothing can be ultimately causa sui in any respect at all. Even if God can be, we can't be." (p. 19)
  - "Kane appears to accept step (2) of the Basic Argument." (p. 19)
  - The Kane cited is **Kane 1989**, "Two Kinds of Incompatibilism", *PPR* 50(2): 219–254, doi 10.2307/2107958. It is **not** Kane 1996, which postdates the paper.
- **Against the self picture, the third response** (pp. 20–21). This is where the parity point lives.
  - Strawson describes a view that "appeals to a certain picture of the self in order to argue that one can be truly free and morally responsible in spite of the fact that one cannot be held to be ultimately responsible for one's character or personality or motivational structure" (p. 20).
  - "S is imagined to be in some way independent of one's CPM." (p. 21)
  - "The trouble with the picture is obvious. … But whatever S decides, it decides as it does because of the way it is" (p. 21).
  - "To be a source of true or ultimate responsibility, S must be responsible for being the way it is. But this is impossible, for the reasons given in the Basic Argument." (p. 21)
  - The S picture is footnoted "Cf. C.A. Campbell (1957)" (n. 11, p. 23). That is Campbell's "Has the Self 'Free Will'?" in *On Selfhood and Godhood*.
- **The noumenal self** (n. 8, p. 23). Kant "grounds the possibility of our freedom in our possession of an unknowable, non-temporal noumenal nature". Strawson adds that Kant plausibly thinks "radical or ultimate self-determination must take place even in the noumenal realm".

- **His verdict on his own argument** (p. 8): "I think that the Basic Argument is certainly valid in showing that we cannot be morally responsible in the way that many suppose."
- **Tenet alignment**:

  - It is neutral on Tenet 1, since it applies to any self.

## The Argument and What It Targets

Strawson's premise (1) says that action for reasons flows from "how one is, mentally speaking". Premise (2) says that true responsibility for action requires true responsibility for how one is. Since being responsible for how one is would mean having chosen it in the light of prior principles, a regress follows (pp. 6–7).

The argument is indifferent to determinism: indeterministic factors "for which one is ex hypothesi in no way responsible" add nothing (p. 7). That indifference makes it a **source** argument in the [source-versus-leeway-incompatibilism](/concepts/source-versus-leeway-incompatibilism/) taxonomy. It never mentions alternatives, and it presses libertarian sourcehood as hard as compatibilist sourcehood.

The target is narrower than it first looks:
- Strawson grants self-change (p. 7). He denies only that self-change can yield "true or ultimate" responsibility.
- He defines that standard by heaven and hell (p. 9).

A Map page that says "character is partly self-created" is therefore contested only if it uses self-creation to ground **ultimate desert**. The Map's pages do exactly that:
- [moral-responsibility](/concepts/moral-responsibility/) L73: "Agent-causal desert has an irreducible source: people deserve praise and blame because they ultimately originated their actions."
- L75: "retribution becomes intelligible".
- [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/) L155.

### Constitutive luck as the Basic Argument's seed

Nagel's constitutive luck is luck in "the kind of person you are … your inclinations, capacities, and temperament". The Basic Argument generalizes it by regress: every attempt to escape constitutive luck by self-shaping is itself made by an agent with a constitution. Hartman's abstract names this relation directly: Strawson "overgeneralizes from instances of constitutive luck … to all kinds of constitutive luck".

Nagel's own sentence on the vices already holds the Map's concession. A quality "may be the product of earlier choices … But it is largely a matter of constitutive bad fortune."

## Major Positions (Replies to the Basic Argument)

### Compatibilist: deny premise (2)
- **Proponents**: Strawson's own section VI(I), pp. 16–17; Fischer 2006; Coates 2017.
- **Core claim**: responsibility needs no responsibility for how one is.
- **Strawson's answer**: compatibilist responsibility then "famously fails to amount to any sort of true moral responsibility" (p. 17).
- **Relation to the Map**: the Map's [compatibilist-symmetry-challenge](/concepts/compatibilist-symmetry-challenge/) discipline ([P-A5](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a5)) already concedes that compatibilism secures the practice-level phenomena.

### Event-causal self-formation: accept premise (2) and stop the regress at undetermined self-forming actions
- **Proponents**: Kane 1989, 1996, 2000.
- **Core claim**: the regress terminates in SFAs, which prior character does not fully explain.
- **Strawson's answer**: indeterminism cannot make one responsible. The SFA is shaped by existing character, "why am I not merely lucky?" (p. 18). Kane, he says, "appears to accept step (2)" (p. 19). In 2000 Strawson writes that Kane agrees ultimate responsibility "requires a kind of self-creation", and adds "So do I".
- **Relation to the Map**: Kane is the Map's naturalistic rival. His terminus is undetermined effort; the Map's is the agent's settling.

### Agent-causal origination: deny premise (2) for agent-caused choices
- **Proponents**: Clarke 2003: 170–76 and 2005a, SEP-REPORTED; the SEP Free Will framing.
- **Core claim**: how one is explains the choice without determining it, and the agent causes the choice. So no sufficient ultimate source lies outside the agent. The demand to be *causa sui* is replaced by the demand that the agent originate, with no external sufficient source.
- **Critic**: Istvan 2011, SEP-REPORTED.
- **Relation to the Map**: this is the only reply structurally available to the Map. It **does not make the agent causa sui**; it denies that ultimate responsibility needs that.

### Locus and degree: freedom belongs to actions first, and responsibility grows
- **Proponents**: Mele 1995: 221–27; Mele 2006; O'Connor 2009.
- **Core claim**: Strawson misplaces the locus. Responsibility is graded and accumulates as past choices shape how one is.
- **Relation to the Map**: this is the scoping the Map's self-creation passages need. Self-*shaping* that grows responsibility by degrees is defensible; self-*creation* that grounds ultimate desert is what Strawson denies.

### Luck pincer: constitution or present luck, either way no responsibility
- **Proponents**: Levy 2011, chs. 3–4.
- **Core claim**: every morally significant act is constitutively lucky, presently lucky, or both.
- **Relation to the Map**: this is where the Basic Argument meets the rollback. See [where the two arguments meet](#meeting).

### Hard incompatibilism: agent causation would suffice but is not credible
- **Proponents**: Pereboom 2001, 2014.
- **Core claim**: the objection to agent causation is empirical (wild coincidences), not the regress.
- **Relation to the Map**: sceptics split. Strawson says no self escapes; Pereboom says an agent cause would supply the control in theory. The Map must not claim that sceptics as a class concede agent causation, nor that they all reject it.

## The Parity Problem (the Map's Reading) {#parity}

This section is the Map's own analysis. No source above makes it.

The outer review (outer-review-2026-10-25-claude-fable-5-1 L115) says the charge "applies equally to the Map. The agent-substance's powers and character were not self-chosen either." Stated symmetrically, with no exemption for the Map:

1. The Map's agent-substance has a nature. That nature includes its powers to select, its sensitivity to reasons, its attentional capacities and whatever weights it brings to a torn choice. **It did not choose that nature.** If it was created, something else created it; if it is a brute existent, its nature is brute. Either way it is not *causa sui*, and the [agent-causal](/concepts/agent-causation/#the-luck-objection) literature does not claim otherwise.
   - Lowe's agents are "unmoved movers": their volitions are uncaused ([agent-causation](/concepts/agent-causation/) L83). They are uncaused causes **of their choices**, not causes **of themselves**.
   - Strawson's demand concerns the second.
2. Strawson's S reply applies word for word: "whatever S decides, it decides as it does because of the way it is". His n. 8 applies the self-determination demand to a non-temporal noumenal self. **Dualism buys no exemption.** A non-physical subject faces the regress exactly as a brain does.
3. The Map's self-creation claims are therefore true only as self-*shaping*:
   - The agent's exercises reshape its dispositions (Tenet 3, [attentional-economics](/concepts/attentional-economics/)).
   - The power doing the reshaping is constitutive luck.
   - "We're not responsible for our initial characters, but we are responsible for what we make of them" ([moral-responsibility](/concepts/moral-responsibility/) L79) holds for the exercises and fails for the power that makes them.
4. What remains is the Clarke move: origination without self-creation. It is the Map's commitment, **not a result**, and Istvan argues it fails.
5. **Parity in the other direction.** Kane can make the same move from the event-causal side: his SFAs are not fixed by prior character either. On the regress itself, agent causation gains no structural advantage over Kane. Its claimed advantage is that the settler is the agent rather than an undetermined effort. That is the categorical difference the rollback page already found to be "metaphysical (which thing is the causal source)" ([agency-and-will](/positions/agency-and-will/) [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2)), with no counterfactual difference.

**What the Basic Argument does not touch**:
- It does not touch causal efficacy. Strawson grants that people change the way they are (p. 7), so Tenet 3 survives intact.
- The Basic Argument never implies that consciousness is idle. **No epiphenomenalism is conceded or implied.**

What it reaches is the step from efficacy plus origination to **ultimate desert**. That step is the residue [agency-and-will](/positions/agency-and-will/) [P-A5](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a5) lists as "ultimate desert in retributive contexts". If the Basic Argument holds against the agent-causal subject, that residue item does no separating work. [P-A5](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a5)'s "Would shift if" names exactly this trigger ("the named residue (ultimate desert, genuine alternatives) were shown to be illusory"). **Flag it for `/positions-evolve`; do not edit the register from here.**

## How the Basic Argument Differs From the Rollback, and Where They Meet {#meeting}

The comparison is with [rollback-argument-against-agent-causation](/concepts/rollback-argument-against-agent-causation/), built from [luck-and-mind-arguments-against-agent-causation-2026-10-25](/research/luck-and-mind-arguments-against-agent-causation-2026-10-25/).

| | Rollback (van Inwagen 2000) | Basic Argument (Strawson 1986, 1994) |
|---|---|---|
| Kind of luck | Present luck: the same state, different outcomes | Constitutive luck: the agent's nature, unchosen |
| Time-shape | Synchronic, token: why this exercise went A rather than B | Diachronic, historical: who made the maker |
| Needs probabilities? | Yes; it infers ground-floor chances from replay frequencies | No; it is a priori, a regress of choices of principles of choice |
| Determinism | Grants indeterminism and asks if it helps | Indifferent: "makes no difference whether determinism is true or false" |
| Family | Leeway/luck | Source |
| Agent-causal reply ends in | The token primitive: the exercise of the power | Origination without self-creation: the exercise of the power |

**Where they meet.** The agent-causal escape from the Basic Argument requires that how one is "fully explain[s]" the choice "without causally determining it". If the choice were fixed by the agent's nature, constitutive luck would swallow it, which is Strawson's first horn. So the escape opens exactly the gap the rollback presses: given everything about the agent, what settled A rather than B?

Strawson's own S reply is already two-horned. S decides "because of the way it is" or through indeterministic factors that cannot add responsibility (p. 21). Levy's pincer (ch. 4) makes it a theorem-shaped dilemma. The Map's same-day retraction completes the picture: reasons give intelligibility, not contrastive explanation ([free-will](/topics/free-will/) L94; the rollback page's "Where Every Reply Ends").

The two concessions are therefore **one concession seen from two sides**:
- Because reasons do not contrastively explain the settling, the agent's nature does not fix it. That is what lets the agent escape constitutive luck.
- For the same reason, nothing more basic explains why the settling went one way. That is what leaves the rollback's primitive.

The Map can say *who* originates. It has no further answer to *why the originator's settling went this way*, nor to *why the originator is such as to settle at all*. Both questions end at the bare exercise.

## Key Debates

### Does ultimate responsibility require being *causa sui*?
- **Sides**:
  - Yes: Strawson, and Kane as Strawson reads him.
  - No: Clarke, Mele, O'Connor, Hartman, compatibilists.
- **Core disagreement**: whether premise (2) states a requirement of the ordinary concept or an inflated "heaven-and-hell" one. Hartman's dilemma is either implausible for the responsibility we take ourselves to have, or plausible for a kind we never took ourselves to have.
- **Current state**: open. The SEP lists replies and counter-replies (Istvan 2011, Parks 2009).

### Can agent causation immunize the agent?
- **Sides**: Clarke 2005a and SEP Free Will say yes, conditionally. Istvan 2011 says no. Pereboom grants it in theory.
- **Core disagreement**: whether origination by an unchosen agent is enough.
- **Current state**: open. The Map's position is a commitment here, not a result.
