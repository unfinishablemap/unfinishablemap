## What "Collapse" Names

"Collapse" is the harvest's label, taken from the outer reviewers. It is not a term of art with a coiner. Two of the sources use the verb in passing: Weir 2024 ("Otherwise, the distinction between property dualism and substance dualism will collapse" [raw], said of a definitional constraint, not as his thesis) and the Giberman and Kovacs 2026 abstract ("criticized for either collapsing into substance dualism or for offering no distinctive advantage over it" [abstract]). The second sentence is the best short map of the field, because it separates two claims the reviewers ran together:

- **Entailment**: property dualism, properly stated, implies substance dualism (Francescotti, Schneider, Weir, Giberman and Kovacs on some property metaphysics; Sharpe 2011, unpublished).
- **No-advantage**: property dualism is no better off than substance dualism against the standard objections (Lycan; Zimmerman's "no likelier" comparison).

The reviewers' gloss, "the case for property dualism supports substance dualism if it supports anything", fits Weir alone. It overstates Lycan (a comparative-plausibility claim) and Zimmerman (a route through adverbialism and bearer vagueness to a comparative claim), and it is silent on which sense of "substance" is reached.

### Schneider's own conclusion

The 2009 draft states the thesis as a constriction of the options, and closes on a disjunction [all raw, draft; the published wording may differ and was not re-checked]:

- "property dualism, when paired with these theories of substance, leads to substance dualism."
- "If I am correct then there are not two dualist options here but one. You may reject souls and ectoplasm, but if consciousness is rock bottom for you, so too are mental substances."
- "Today I aim for negative progress."
- Conclusion: the property dualist "may now see the challenge as being either to make substance dualism palatable, Cartesian or otherwise, or to reject property dualism altogether."

She also writes "I do not mean to suggest that this notion of such a mental substance is unproblematic" (fn 6, draft). Weir 2024 nonetheless lists her among substance dualism's supporters: it "has also received support from some contemporary philosophers including Schneider (2012) and Zimmerman (2010)" [raw]. On the draft's evidence that is Weir's gloss, and it should not be repeated as her self-description.

### Lycan's own conclusion

Abstract: "This paper argues that substance dualism is no less plausible than property dualism and even has two advantages over it" [raw]. He brackets the truth of either: "I am assuming at least for the sake of discussion that no argument for PD is deductively sound; for my question is that of whether, considered as theories only, SD is less plausible than mere PD" [raw]. He denies that substance dualism carries disembodiability with it: "it is not obvious that SD is committed to the metaphysical possibility of disembodied existence. I contend that there is no entailment" [raw]. That last sentence matters for the Map: it is a non-dualist's statement that a substance view in the persistence sense need not be Weir's independence sense.

Lycan's fn 4 is also the source of a lead the 11-08 note missed: "Another defense of the entailment claim is given by Sharpe (2011)" [raw], cited as K. Sharpe, "Property dualism entails substance dualism", presented at the APA Central Division, Minneapolis, April 2011. A conference paper; no published version located. **Lead only.**

## What This Costs or Gains the Map

The 11-08 "Tenet Evaluation" stands: the literature supports the lean and the Tenet 5 framing, and cannot convert the lean into a commitment. Four additions.

1. **Tenet 1's neutrality has a second, newer pressure.** The tenet text says the commitment "is neutral between substance and property dualism". Giberman and Kovacs argue that what a "mental property" is depends on one's metaphysics of properties, and that the candidates leave property dualism incoherent or unmotivated. If that holds, the neutral clause names one stable option and one that needs a property metaphysics the Map has not chosen. That is a cost to how the Map presents itself, and the Map has no reply on file, because it has no stated view on universals versus tropes.
2. **The live replies are stronger than the 11-08 fold text shows.** That text names Mackie alone on the other side. Yang 2015 (against Schneider), Zimmerman 2022 (against Robinson), and Hershenov 2023 (against Zimmerman's own landing) are all published. A paragraph listing five challengers and one reply would misstate the balance.
3. **The fold text conflicts with `concepts/manyism`.** The manyism page says of the Too-Many-Minds problem that "that Unger, Zimmerman, and Simon have run it as an argument for substance dualism does not make it one the Map can borrow". The 11-08 Fold Plan's second paragraph opens "This literature is a reason the Map leans", with Zimmerman's bearer-vagueness route among the items just listed. Installed as drafted, two pages would disagree about whether the Map uses Zimmerman's vagueness argument. See Binding Guards.
4. **Lycan gives the Map a cheap, honest separation.** His "no entailment" from substance dualism to disembodiability is the sentence that lets the Map hold a persistence-sense lean without inheriting Weir's independence-sense conclusion. It comes from a non-dualist and costs the Map nothing.

Nothing here supports any tenet directly. Every argument is conditional on property dualism, and two of the five reviewers' authors (Lycan, Schneider in the draft) decline to affirm the antecedent.

## Binding Guards for Any Writer

1. **Stances.** Lycan is not a substance dualist and in the 2013 paper does not assert either dualism. Schneider's conclusion (2009 draft) is a disjunction: make substance dualism palatable or reject property dualism. Do not call her a substance dualist, including on Weir's authority. Zimmerman is an emergent dualist, not a Cartesian. Francescotti's stance is not established. Weir rejects property dualism and leaves panpsychism open.
2. **Quote status.** Usable as [raw]: every Lycan 2013 and Weir 2024 quotation in this note and those re-grepped from the 11-08 note; Cucu's review; SEP. Schneider quotations in this note are from the **2009 draft** and must be labelled so, or replaced from the published text. Zimmerman 2010, Mackie 2011, Zimmerman 2022, Hershenov 2023, Giberman and Kovacs 2026, Segal 2025, Spackman 2013: abstract only; no page numbers. Yang 2015: search snippet only; paraphrase, do not quote. Francescotti 2001: one sentence (p. 114), as quoted by Lycan fn 4.
3. **SEP is a list, not a summary.** SEP §2.4 says only that the preference for property dualism "is itself regularly challenged" by seven works (the reviewers dropped Searle 2002 and Robinson 2016). It does not say what any of them argues, and §4.6, despite its title, is Lowe's bundle argument. Never write "as SEP summarises, X argues".
4. **Coiner.** No one coined "collapse". Do not credit Zimmerman or Weir with originating the entailment claim; do not credit Francescotti 2001 as its first statement without the hedge in Correction 3.
5. **Zimmerman's vagueness route is reported, not borrowed.** `concepts/manyism` disclaims it. The only Zimmerman consideration the Map leans on is the adverbial one at `concepts/substance-property-dualism`. If the fold wants the vagueness route as support, `concepts/manyism` must be changed in the same pass, and that is a position change, not a refinement.
6. **Two senses of substance.** SEP §3.1: the independence sense and the unitary-persisting sense "are logically independent". Weir reaches the first; the Map leans in the second; Lycan denies the second entails the first. Any sentence saying an argument "reaches substance dualism" must say which.
7. **Not to be cited**: Sharpe 2011 (unpublished talk), McKee 2025 (title only), Hernández Maturana 2024 as a reply to Weir (target unnamed in the abstract), and a PhilArchive review surfaced by search under an unreliable author name (id MORTMB-8; not fetched).
8. **Robinson and Zimmerman are opponents on vagueness** (Zimmerman 2022). Keep them in separate sentences.
