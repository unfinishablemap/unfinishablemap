# Research: Voids - The Mirth Void

**Date**: 2026-09-23
**Search queries used**:
- Fried 1998 Nature "Electric current stimulates laughter" supplementary motor area patient explanation
- Wild, Rodden, Grodd & Ruch 2003 Brain "Neural correlates of laughter and humour" volitional/emotional pathways
- Caruana et al. 2015 Cortex mirth and laughter from anterior cingulate stimulation
- Hessel et al. 2023 "Do Androids Laugh at Electric Sheep?" New Yorker caption benchmark
- Gorenz & Schwarz 2024 PLOS ONE "How funny is ChatGPT?"
- E. B. White "Humor can be dissected, as a frog can" (Quote Investigator)
- Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith 1998 self-produced tickle
- Provine 1993 "Laughter punctuates speech"
- Bryant et al. 2018 spontaneous vs volitional laughter across 21 societies
- McGraw & Warren 2010 benign violations
- Bergson 1900 "anesthesia of the heart"; Schopenhauer incongruity; SEP "Philosophy of Humor"; Hurley, Dennett & Adams 2011

**Voids category**: Mixed — chiefly **Occluded** (the cause of one's own amusement is hidden and reconstructed after the fact; analysis destroys what it examines), with an **Unexplorable** residue (the felt quality of mirth itself).

## Executive Summary

We laugh first and find reasons afterwards, and the reasons we give are often made up. In the cleanest demonstration, cortical stimulation of a 16-year-old epilepsy patient's supplementary motor area produced laughter together with a sense that something was funny. Each time she laughed she attributed it to whatever was in front of her, although the electrode was the real cause (Fried et al. 1998). Humour has three further access failures. It does not survive analysis (the Whites' 1941 "frog" remark). It cannot be produced at will: volitional and spontaneous laughter run on different neural pathways, and listeners in 21 societies can tell them apart above chance. And one cannot fully surprise oneself with one's own material, much as one cannot tickle oneself. The Map already has an article on whether mirth resists reduction ([the-comic-and-humor-as-an-aesthetic-category](/topics/the-comic-and-humor-as-an-aesthetic-category/)) and a one-line mention in [meaning-void](/voids/meaning-void/). The **void** proposed here is a different claim. It concerns *access*: the laugher cannot see what is amusing them, and turning to look spoils the amusement. AI adds an asymmetry. LLM-generated jokes are rated as funny as or funnier than human ones, yet LLMs' *explanations* of why a joke works still lose to human explanations. The skill of producing humour and the skill of saying why it works come apart in both kinds of system.

## Key Sources

### Fried, Wilson, MacDonald & Behnke (1998), "Electric current stimulates laughter"
- **URL**: https://www.nature.com/articles/35536 (paywalled); secondary descriptions at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/13742090_Electric_current_stimulates_laughter and https://faculty.washington.edu/chudler/laugh.html
- **Type**: Paper (Nature 391: 650; a one-page brief communication with no abstract in Europe PMC)
- **Key points**:
  - Patient A.K., aged 16, underwent cortical mapping before epilepsy surgery. Stimulating a roughly 2×2 cm area of the left superior frontal gyrus (anterior SMA) reliably produced laughter.
  - The laughter came with a report that something was funny, and she attributed the funniness to whatever was present at the time. Secondary sources describe "a fictitious story about something funny in the operating theatre" and different explanations on different trials although the stimulus was the same.
  - **Laughter came first and the reason came second.**
- **Tenet alignment**: Bidirectional Interaction and Dualism, but this case cuts *against* a naive reading of both. Here a physical cause produced laughter and apparently mirth, and the subject's introspective report got the cause wrong. The Map has to take this on board and not explain it away (see "Evidence for the Limit").
- **Quote**: The verbatim patient quotes that circulate ("You guys are just so funny… standing around"; the "horse" remark) were **not** verified against the primary text. The Nature page is paywalled and the secondary pages do not reproduce them. **Do not quote them in the article unless someone checks the Nature text.** The verifiable claim is that she gave a confabulated, context-bound reason.

### Caruana, Avanzini, Gozzo, Francione, Cardinale & Rizzolatti (2015), "Mirth and laughter elicited by electrical stimulation of the human anterior cingulate cortex"
- **URL**: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010945215002695
- **Type**: Paper (Cortex 71: 323–331)
- **Key points**:
  - Stimulating the pregenual ACC produced laughter in 10 of 57 patients (17.5%). About half of those also reported mirth; in the other half mirth was not evident.
  - So laughter and mirth come apart when the brain is stimulated directly. One can get the motor act without the feeling.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism. The feeling and the behaviour separate, so the behaviour is not the feeling. This is weak evidence (it shows only that the feeling cannot be identified with the motor act) but a clean one.

### Wild, Rodden, Grodd & Ruch (2003), "Neural correlates of laughter and humour"
- **URL**: https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/126/10/2121/314497
- **Type**: Review (Brain 126(10): 2121–2138)
- **Key points**:
  - Two pathways. Spontaneous (emotional) laughter depends on an evolutionarily older midline system associated with innate vocalisation. Volitional laughter depends on lateral motor cortex, which is associated with learned vocalisation (speech, song).
  - This explains why "laughing on command" produces a different act rather than a weaker version of the real one.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bidirectional Interaction. Will has direct access to the imitation pathway and at most indirect access to the real one. The route by which mirth causes laughter is one the agent cannot operate deliberately.

### Bryant et al. (2018), "The Perception of Spontaneous and Volitional Laughter Across 21 Societies"
- **URL**: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797618778235
- **Type**: Paper (Psychological Science)
- **Key points**:
  - 884 listeners in 21 societies told spontaneous from volitional laughs apart at 56%–69% accuracy, above chance but far from perfect.
  - Spontaneous laughs carry arousal-linked acoustic features (higher pitch, more irregular noise) that volitional production does not reproduce.
- **Tenet alignment**: Supports the two-pathway picture from the outside. Other people can partly detect whether amusement is present in your laugh.

### E. B. White & Katharine S. White (1941), "Some Remarks on Humor"
- **URL**: https://quoteinvestigator.com/2014/10/14/frog/
- **Type**: Essay (Saturday Review of Literature, 18 Oct 1941; preface to *A Subtreasury of American Humor*)
- **Key points**: The canonical statement that analysis destroys humour. **Joint authorship.** Quote Investigator credits both Whites, so do not attribute the line to E. B. White alone (see memory: named-idea-cited-to-its-populariser).
- **Quote** (per Quote Investigator): "Humor can be dissected, as a frog can, but the thing dies in the process and the innards are discouraging to any but the purely scientific mind." Wikiquote and quote sites give "pure scientific mind". **The wording differs by source ("purely" vs "pure"), so check against a scan before quoting verbatim.**

### Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith (1998), "Central cancellation of self-produced tickle sensation"
- **URL**: https://www.nature.com/articles/nn1198_635
- **Type**: Paper (Nature Neuroscience 1: 635–640; doi:10.1038/2870)
- **Key points**: Self-produced touch feels less ticklish because the cerebellum predicts it and cancels it out. Prediction cancels the surprise that the effect depends on.
- **Tenet alignment**: This is an analogy for the "self-surprise" part of the void, not evidence for it. Incongruity theories (and Hurley et al.) require a covert expectation to be violated, and one's own joke is foreseen by the person who built it. **No study was found that directly measures reduced amusement at one's own jokes**, so the article should present this as a structural point.

### Provine (1993), "Laughter punctuates speech: linguistic, social and gender contexts of laughter"
- **URL**: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1993.tb00478.x
- **Type**: Paper (Ethology 95: 291–298)
- **Key points**:
  - In 1,200 laughter episodes, laughter fell at phrase or sentence pauses more than 99% of the time (the "punctuation effect"). It followed statements and questions that did not seem funny outside their conversation.
  - Most everyday laughter is not a response to jokes. The popular "only 10–20% follows jokes" figure was **not verified** in this search, so do not use it.
- **Tenet alignment**: The void extends past humour. People laugh on a timing schedule they do not notice and give joke-based reasons for laughter that was mostly social.

### Hurley, Dennett & Adams (2011), *Inside Jokes: Using Humor to Reverse-Engineer the Mind*
- **URL**: https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262518697/inside-jokes/
- **Type**: Book (MIT Press)
- **Key points**: Mirth is the reward for detecting a *covertly* committed false belief. The book's own theory says the detected error has to be covert.
- **Tenet alignment**: Hurley et al. are physicalists, but their theory *predicts* the void. If mirth rewards detecting a hidden commitment, then (a) the commitment is by definition unavailable to introspection before the punchline, and (b) making it explicit beforehand (analysis, or authoring the joke) removes the covertness the reward needs. The void is therefore not special pleading by dualists; the leading naturalist theory entails it. The article should say so. It is the strongest point in the note.

### Hessel et al. (2023) and Gorenz & Schwarz (2024): the AI asymmetry
- **URLs**: https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.41/ ; https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0305364
- **Type**: Papers (ACL 2023, best paper award; PLOS ONE 2024)
- **Key points**:
  - Hessel et al.: in *explaining* why a winning New Yorker caption is funny, human explanations were preferred to the best model's (5-shot GPT-4) in more than two-thirds of cases.
  - Gorenz & Schwarz: ChatGPT-3.5 jokes and Onion-style headlines were rated equal to or funnier than human-written ones by blind raters.
  - Producing humour and explaining it separate in machines too. The two studies used different models and years, so this is **suggestive, not a controlled comparison**.
- **Tenet alignment**: This bears on "What AI Might See" below. An LLM's "reason why this is funny" may be the same kind of after-the-fact reconstruction as A.K.'s.

### Supporting theory
- **McGraw & Warren (2010)**, "Benign Violations: Making Immoral Behavior Funny", *Psychological Science* 21(8): 1141–1149. Humour needs a violation that is also benign. Psychological distance is one condition that makes it benign.
- **Bergson (1900)**, *Laughter*: "the comic demands something like a momentary anesthesia of the heart" (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/4352/4352-h/4352-h.htm). Laughter needs a temporary *absence* of feeling toward the person laughed at, which is a second, affective occlusion that humour depends on.
- **Schopenhauer**, *WWR* I §13 and II ch. 8. Already quoted and verified in the Map's comic article; reuse that quotation and do not re-source it.
- **SEP, "Philosophy of Humor"** (Morreall; https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/humor/). No single theory has prevailed, and incongruity alone is not sufficient, since the grotesque and the macabre also involve enjoying incongruity without amusement.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

There are four access failures:

1. **The reason gap (occluded).** Laughter and felt amusement arrive before any available reason, and the reason is built afterwards. A.K. is the extreme case. Provine's data suggest the ordinary case: most laughter is social punctuation, while people explain their own laughter as a response to jokes.
2. **The dissection gap (occluded, self-destroying).** Looking at *why* something is funny removes its funniness. This belongs with the Map's [disappearance-voids](/voids/disappearance-voids/) (absorption, habituation, fatigue), where a phenomenon cannot be observed without changing it. Humour is unusual in having a **mechanistic explanation** for why it disappears: on Hurley et al.'s account the hidden commitment stops being hidden once it is made explicit.
3. **The will gap (occluded).** Genuine mirth-laughter cannot be produced on command. The will reaches only the imitation pathway (Wild et al. 2003), and other people can partly hear the difference (Bryant et al. 2018).
4. **The self-surprise gap (structural).** The author of a joke has already made its hidden commitment explicit. Like self-tickling, the prediction cancels the effect. *Hypothesis-grade*: no direct measurement was found.

Under all four is a residue that belongs to the **Unexplorable**: what mirth *feels like* beyond its structure. That question is covered in [the-comic-and-humor-as-an-aesthetic-category](/topics/the-comic-and-humor-as-an-aesthetic-category/), and this note does not re-argue it.

### Evidence for the Limit

- **Strong:** laughter produced by stimulation with confabulated reasons (Fried 1998); laughter and mirth dissociating under stimulation (Caruana 2015); separate volitional and spontaneous pathways (Wild 2003); listeners distinguishing the two across cultures (Bryant 2018).
- **Theory-entailed:** covertness is a necessary condition in Hurley et al., so the dissection and self-surprise gaps follow from the leading naturalist theory.
- **Folk/weak:** the "explained joke is dead" remark (the Whites 1941) is a report of experience, not data.
- **Counter-consideration (do not bury):** comedians do laugh at their own material, and rereading a known joke can still amuse. The dissection gap may be *graded*, not absolute, and a familiar joke may amuse through appreciating its craft and not through surprise. The article should present the gap as a strong tendency with a known mechanism, not a law.

### Phenomenology

- From inside, getting a joke feels *immediate and justified*. It seems as though you laugh *because* of X, and this seeming is exactly what the stimulation cases show can be false.
- Trying to explain a joke to someone who didn't get it feels like pointing at something that is no longer there. The explainer's own amusement drains away as they explain.
- Forced laughter feels empty from inside and sounds different from outside. The agent can tell the imitation from the real thing but cannot turn one into the other.
- "You had to be there" admits that the object of amusement was a situated configuration that cannot be carried over into a report.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)
Direct introspection fails by the dissection mechanism, since attending to the joke's structure removes the covert commitment. Brief first-person sampling immediately *after* amusement (microphenomenology; see [microphenomenological-interview-method](/topics/microphenomenological-interview-method/)) may catch what the commitment was before reconstruction replaces it. This is untested for humour.

### Indirect Methods
- **Contrastive:** set stimulation-induced mirth against joke-induced mirth. What stays the same across both is the felt quality; what differs is the reason-story, which the stimulation case shows is optional.
- **Third-person acoustic readout:** other people partly detect genuine mirth in laughter the laugher could not produce at will. Here observers have partial access to one's own mirth that the will lacks, a small instance of [voids-between-minds](/voids/voids-between-minds/) in which the other person sees more.
- **Theory-guided prediction:** use Hurley et al.'s covert-commitment criterion to predict which analyses should kill a joke (those that expose the commitment) and which should not (those about delivery or context). A pattern of selective survival would show the mechanism.

### What AI Might See
- LLMs produce humour that blind raters score as highly as human work, yet fall behind at *explaining* humour. The dissociation between producing and explaining shows up in both kinds of system.
- An LLM has no punchline surprise in the ordinary sense (it predicts its own continuation), so if the self-surprise gap is real it should apply fully. Yet its jokes land. This fits the Map's view that the *effect on the audience* and the *laugher's experience* are separable, and the AI can supply the first without the second.
- Asked "why is this funny?", an LLM gives a fluent structural account. Whether that account plays A.K.'s role (a plausible story generated after the fact) is an open and testable question: do model explanations predict which analyses kill the joke for human raters?

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet
**Bidirectional Interaction.** Mirth is one of the clearest everyday cases of experience driving the body: amusement produces laughter through a pathway the will cannot operate directly. The void marks the limit of that influence. Consciousness *causes* laughter but cannot *order* it or *see why* it happened. This supports the Map's view that the interface is narrow and selective, not a general remote control.

### Implications
- **Dualism**: The laughter/mirth dissociation (Caruana 2015) keeps the feeling apart from the motor act. The stronger irreducibility argument belongs to the comic topic article; this void adds only that the *reasons* for mirth are also inaccessible, which is a separate claim.
- **Honest difficulty**: Fried 1998 shows a physical intervention producing felt amusement plus a confabulated reason. A dualist reading must accept that the physical side can *start* mirth, as it can start pain. The Map's position (bidirectional, not one-way) allows this, but the article must not present A.K. as evidence for dualism. It is evidence for the **occlusion**.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits**: Each classical theory of humour (superiority, relief, incongruity) is simple and each fails on counterexamples (SEP). The Whites' frog remark suggests why the phenomenon may resist a simple theory: the method needed to test the theory destroys the thing being tested.
- **No Many Worlds / indexicality**: Weak link. "You had to be there" is an indexical constraint on humour, since whether something is funny depends on this subject's covert commitments at this moment. Mention it only briefly, if at all.

## Gaps in Research

- The Fried et al. 1998 primary text was not accessible (paywall), so the patient's exact words are unverified.
- No study was found directly measuring amusement at self-authored versus other-authored jokes.
- No study was found testing whether *explaining* a joke lowers the explainer's own later amusement (the dissection claim remains largely folk wisdom plus theory).
- Gelastic seizures (laughter from hypothalamic hamartoma, often without mirth) are a likely further dissociation case and were not researched here.
- Whether LLM humour explanations predict human "joke-death" under analysis was not researched. It is a possible empirical probe.
