## Executive Summary

The transparency method is the proposal that one can come to know one's own beliefs, and on some versions one's other mental states, by attending to the world rather than to one's mind. Evans's canonical case: asked whether I think there will be a third world war, I consider whether there will be one. The idea has five monographs (Gallois 1996; Moran 2001; Fernández 2013; Byrne 2018; Boyle 2024) and one book-length critique (Cassam 2014). It divides into an empiricist inferential version (Byrne: the rule *If p, believe that you believe that p*), a non-inferential "bypass" version (Fernández) and an agentialist version (Moran; Boyle's reflectivism). The Map mentions it in a single sentence (phenomenal-authority L144), and that sentence has two defects. It draws the method's limit where its leading defender does not: Byrne says transparency "covers belief and perception" and finds desire the hard case. It also attributes to the method the negative thesis that inward attention is impossible, which belongs to the Harman/Tye transparency of experience.

The Map's real interest lies where the method stops. Byrne extends it to sensation by adopting the perceptual theory of pain and identifying pains with the bodily disturbances perceived. His 2026 chapter draws the consequence that there are hallucinations of pain. That is the precise point of divergence from the Map's acquaintance view of phenomenal character. The method also gives a non-constitutive explanation of Shoemaker's self-blindness result: Byrne calls his account "a version of Shoemaker's 'broad perceptual model'" that vindicates Shoemaker's appeal to rationality. Gallois built his own case for the method on a self-blind believer.

## Key Sources

### Evans, *The Varieties of Reference* (1982), pp. 225–228
- **Type**: Book (posthumous, ed. McDowell)
- **Verified wording (archive.org OCR)**: "in making a self-ascription of belief, one's eyes are, so to speak, or occasionally literally, directed outward—upon the world. If someone asks me 'Do you think there is going to be a third world war?', I must attend, in answering him, to precisely the same outward phenomena as I would attend to if I were answering the question 'Will there be a third world war?' I get myself in a position to answer the question whether I believe that p by putting into operation whatever procedure I have for answering the question whether p." The page is p. 225 per SEP (both entries), Byrne 2005 p. 98 and Byrne 2011.
  - **Wording trap.** The original reads "**in a** position". SEP "Introspection" prints "into the position", Cassam prints "into a position", and the optimistic review copied SEP. Quote the original wording.
- **Also verified (OCR)**: "If a judging subject applies this procedure, then necessarily he will gain knowledge of one of his own mental states: even the most determined sceptic cannot find here a gap in which to insert his knife" (p. 225, as also printed in Byrne 2005, p. 98).
- **Also verified (OCR)**: Evans concentrates "upon the ways we have of knowing what we believe and what we experience", which if got right give "a good model of self-knowledge (or intro- spection) to follow in other cases". Byrne 2011 §5 gives the page as p. 225.
- **Perception (OCR-verified wording; p. 227 per Byrne 2011 §2)**: the subject gains knowledge of his informational states "by re-using precisely those skills of conceptualization that he uses to make judgements about the world". He "goes through exactly the same procedure as he would go through if he were trying to make a judgement about how it is at this place now, but excluding any knowledge he has of an extraneous kind". Byrne 2011 §2 says this epistemological observation "was first clearly made by Evans (1982, p. 227)".
- **Coiner check**: Evans's OCR has **0 hits for "ascent"** and **0 for "transparency"**. "Transparent" occurs only in the reported-speech sense ("a transparent style"). One caveat: a word broken across a line ("trans- parency") would evade the search. Do not credit Evans with either term.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.

### Edgley, *Reason in Theory and Practice* (1969), p. 90, quoted in Moran 2001, pp. 60–61
- **Verified (Moran OCR; Google Books p. 60)**: "Roy Edgley has called this feature the 'transparency' of one's own thinking". Moran then quotes Edgley: "[M]y own present thinking, in contrast to the thinking of others, is transparent in the sense that I cannot distinguish the question 'Do I think that P?' from a question in which there is no essential reference to myself or my belief, namely 'Is it the case that P?'" (1969, p. 90).
- **Moran p. 61 (Google Books)**: "As Edgley points out, the correct answers to the two questions need not be the same." Moran disagrees with Edgley that the two questions are indistinguishable from within the first person.
- Cassam (2014, p. 3, footnote; the OCR footnote markers are garbled, so give no note number) agrees: "It was Edgley's idea to describe this phenomenon as the 'transparency' of one's own present thinking."
- **Use**: Edgley is the earliest named source for the *term*, ahead of Evans. Edgley 1969 has not been opened. Quote him only "as quoted in Moran 2001, p. 61".

### Gallois, *The World Without, the Mind Within* (1996)
- **Verified (OCR)**: names the "doxastic schema" ("P / So, I believe p"). Its inference "does not fit any standard pattern of good inference" (p. 47 per Byrne 2011 §3).
- Gallois's case for the schema begins from a believer who is self-blind in Shoemaker's sense. Without the schema, "as a self-blind believer, I will form a deeply irrational view of my non-doxastic world" (OCR; p. 76 per Byrne 2011 §3).
- Gallois also engages Shoemaker's George and the "evidence principle" directly. Among the verified OCR strings are "a self-blind asserter would not be self-blind" and "George is not self-blind".
- Byrne (2011 §3) argues that Gallois's memory argument "has a false premise": a rational animal that changes its mind need not "recollect" that yesterday p.
- **LEAD**: Gallois's exact verdict on Shoemaker's George argument (pp. 45–51, 66–68 per the index) was not reconstructed.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.

### Moran, *Authority and Estrangement* (2001); Moran 2003
- **Verified (OCR)**: "With respect to the attitude of belief, the claim of transparency 'tells us that the first-person question 'Do I believe P?' is 'transparent' to, answered in the same way as, the outward-directed question as to the truth of P itself" (p. 66 per Byrne 2011 §4).
  - The stray quote mark before "tells" is in the OCR. Byrne's transcription has no inner quotes, so check the page before reproducing the punctuation.
- **Verified (OCR)**: a self-ascription is said to "obey the Transparency Condition" when it is made by consideration of the facts about X (p. 101 per Byrne 2011). "Transparency Condition" is Moran's term.
- **Verified (Google Books p. 64)**: the claims of "Edgley and Evans have their source in the primacy of a deliberative rather than a theoretical stance toward one's own state of mind". Byrne 2011 renders this as "towards one's state of mind", so quote Moran's own wording.
- **Ch. 3.1** is "Wittgenstein and Moore's Paradox" (verified in the OCR contents).
- **Moran 2003** ("Responses to O'Brien and Shoemaker", *EJP* 11(3): 402–419; Crossref). Byrne 2005 p. 95 quotes p. 413: "the claim of Transparency is something of a paradox: how can a question referring to a matter of empirical psychological fact about a particular person be legitimately answered without appeal to the evidence about that person, but rather by appeal to a quite independent body of evidence?"

- **Shoemaker, "Moran on Self-Knowledge"** (*EJP* 11(3): 391–401, 2003; Crossref) is the direct Shoemaker–Moran exchange. **LEAD**: not opened.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Moran's deliberative stance concerns agency, but it is a normative-epistemic account, not a causal one.

### Byrne, "Introspection" (2005), *Philosophical Topics* 33(1): 79–104 (scan read)
- **p. 95**: "BEL If p, believe that you believe that p". Because the antecedent "expresses the content of the mental state that the rule-follower ends up believing she is in, BEL can be called a *transparent* rule."
- **§7.2 "The Puzzle of Transparency" (p. 95)**: Byrne quotes Moran 2003 (above) and writes "This *puzzle of transparency* can be expressed in the terminology of epistemic rules". So the **name** "puzzle of transparency" is Byrne's, the puzzle having been posed by Moran (2003) and, as the 2018 chapter abstract says, by Gallois.
- **p. 98, §7.4 "Shoemaker's Insights Validated"**: "The hypothesis that privileged and peculiar access is explained by our following (or trying to follow) BEL is a version of Shoemaker's 'broad perceptual model.'" Byrne adds that someone might believe that it's raining, "possess the concept of belief, and yet not even have the capacity to follow BEL".
- **p. 98, on Evans's "necessarily ... knowledge" clause**: "Whether or not this is Evans's view, on the present account it is incorrect."
- **p. 99**: "Yet the account is not a version of the inner-sense theory. It is *economical*, like behaviorism... Since this capacity belongs to the department of reasoning, not perceiving, Shoemaker's idea that the source of self-knowledge can be traced to 'rationality' is vindicated, albeit not via his preferred route."
- **p. 99, §8 "Beyond Belief?"**: "It is often pointed out the phenomenon of transparency is quite limited. It covers belief and perception, but what about, to take the obvious example, knowledge of one's *wants* or *desires*?" The **"puzzle of opacity"** (Byrne's term) is introduced here.
- **p. 100**: "at least with respect to belief, the inner-sense theory is partly right. There is an inner mechanism for detecting one's beliefs. But the inner-sense theory is also partly wrong: the mechanism comes with our capacity for reasoning about the external world—there is no inner eye."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Byrne is a physicalist; see the firewall under Byrne 2011.

### Byrne, "Transparency, Belief, Intention" (2011), *Arist. Soc. Supp. Vol.* 85: 201–221 (author's manuscript; cite by section)
- **§1 (firewall)**: the phrase "self-knowledge" "has a suspicious Cartesian flavor. It suggests that one's 'self' is a non-physical entity... However, since the terminology is entrenched, it will not be discarded."
- **§2**: Moore's "sensation of blue" passage "suggest[s] either that there is no such procedure or, if there is, it is not necessary". This concerns inward attention. **The method therefore does not need the impossibility of inward attention.**
- **§3**: the doxastic schema is "strongly self-verifying"; the account claims safety, not a demonstration of knowledge-conduciveness.
- **§5**: Boyle's "Uniformity Assumption" is quoted (Boyle 2009, p. 141). On pain: "I know that I feel a pain in my elbow, not by attending to myself, or my own mind, but by attending to the painful disturbance in my elbow. (My elbow hurts: hence, I feel pain in my elbow.)"
- **§5, the argument from no dissociation**: "If the epistemology of mental states is not broadly uniform, then dissociations are to be expected... Such dissociations do not seem to occur, however." And: "As Shoemaker observes, 'self-blindness' is not an actual condition... The obvious explanation of this fact is that rationality and other epistemic capacities are all that is needed for self-knowledge."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The no-dissociation premise is empirical and meets the Map's clinical pages (see Key Debates).

### Byrne, *Transparency and Self-Knowledge* (2018), OUP (Crossref chapter abstracts only)
- **Book abstract**: one knows that one is in mental state M "by an inference from a worldly or environmental premise". Two problems are named: "the inference seems terrible" and "the idea seems not to generalize. For example, what is the worldly premise corresponding to 'I intend to ϕ,' or 'I feel a pain'?" The book claims to cover "perception, sensation, desire, intention, emotion, memory, imagination, and thought".
- **Ch. 2, "Inner Sense"**: the inner-sense theory "gives no explanation of why Shoemakerian 'self-blindness' does not actually occur".
- **Ch. 4, "The Puzzle of Transparency"**: "André Gallois points out that the inference from 'p' to 'I believe that p' is neither deductively valid nor inductively strong. This is (a version of) the puzzle of transparency." The solutions of Gallois, Moran and Dretske are criticised.
- **Ch. 6, "Perception and Sensation" (pp. 128–155)**: "to have a bodily sensation... is to perceive something occurring in one's body. The chapter argues that this perceptual theory of sensation is correct... the basic transparency account for perception applies."

### Byrne, "Knowing That I Am in Pain" (2026a), in *Self-Knowledge and Knowledge A Priori*, OUP, pp. 191–207 (abstract only; editors not in Crossref)
- **Abstract (Crossref and OpenAlex, verified)**: perceptual theorists "typically add to it the claim that disturbances—the objects of 'pain perception'—are not pains. This is wrong. Once this error is corrected, the perceptual theory of pain leads naturally to a 'transparent' theory of the first-person epistemology of pain... One striking consequence of this theory is that a seemingly self-evident proposition—that there are no hallucinations of pain—is false."
- **Tenet alignment**: this is the method's sharpest **conflict** with the Map's view that phenomenal character is presented. The phenomenal-authority article writes "For consciousness, appearance *is* reality"; Byrne's theory makes pain something one can seem to feel when it is absent.

### Fernández, "Privileged Access Naturalized" (2003) and *Transparent Minds* (2013)
- **2003 abstract (OpenAlex)**: "one may have the very same grounds for both a given belief that p and a higher-order belief about this belief". The account "does not posit any mysterious faculty of introspection or 'inner perception' mechanism".
- **SEP §3.4 (verified)**: Fernández (2013) labels this "the bypass model". The self-ascription is "partly internalist" and "partly externalist" (ibid.: 44). He "limits his account to explaining self-knowledge of beliefs and desires", while "Byrne extends his account to encompass self-knowledge of all of our mental states".
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral (explicitly naturalising).

### Boyle, "Two Kinds of Self-Knowledge" (2009), "Transparent Self-Knowledge" (2011), *Transparency and Reflection* (2024)
- **2009 abstract (OpenAlex)**: a satisfactory account must recognise "an active kind through which we know our own judgments, and a passive kind through which we know our sensations". The former is "intimately connected with the very capacity for rational reflection".
- **2011 abstract**: an "inferential" approach (Byrne) "faces a basic difficulty", which a "reflective" approach avoids. SEP §3.4: the inference from p to *I believe that p* "will not appear reasonable to the thinker".
- **2024 (Crossref abstracts)**: the book names the "Problem of Transparency". Ch. 3 criticises Moran ("making up one's mind"), Byrne ("inference from world to mind") and Peacocke. Ch. 4's reflectivist answer, drawn from Sartre, makes the entitlement to transparency depend on "a form of implicit or 'nonpositional' self-awareness" present in first-order "intention, perception, and belief".
- **The "puzzle of reflection"**: **LEAD only**, not found in any abstract checked. Boyle's 2024 term is the "Problem of Transparency".
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The nonpositional self-awareness is Sartrean, not a posit about substance. It is the transparency literature's own route back to a pre-reflective self-awareness (compare [self-and-self-consciousness](/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/)). Do not enlist it.

### Cassam, *Self-Knowledge for Humans* (2014), OUP (full text)
- **p. 2**: "what I'm going to call the Transparency Method, or TM for short". The capitalised label is Cassam's; SEP Gertler also uses "transparency method" in lower case. **LEAD**: whether the phrase is older.
- **p. 3**: "TM is tailor made for homo philosophicus". **p. 3, footnote**: "I’m not planning to discuss Byrne or Fernandez, whose approaches are different from Moran's."
- **Ch. 9, "Looking Outwards"**, against "Rationalism", read as TM via Finkelstein's "ought rationally" recasting:
  - the **Generality Problem** (p. 103): TM "can only account for a sub-class of intentional self-knowledge";
  - the **Substitution Problem** (p. 104): TM substitutes the harder question "Do the reasons require me to believe that P?" for the easier one;
  - the **Matching Problem** (p. 106): "what you believe isn't always what you ought rationally to believe". Cassam illustrates it with Gertler's belief-perseverance, "a common psychological phenomenon that threatens the method of transparency" (Gertler 2011b: 138).
- **Ch. 9 opening (scope guard)**: that Rationalism "doesn't try to explain how we know our own sensations" is "not an objection to Rationalism", since "it's neither necessary nor possible to account for all self-knowledge in one fell swoop". Cassam's own positive view is inferentialism (chs. 11–12).
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.

### Other objections (from SEP Self-Knowledge §3.4, verified)
- **Shah & Velleman (2005)**: deliberating whether p may produce a new belief rather than reveal an old one. Gertler (2011b) presses the point.
- **Valaris (2011)**: the transition from p to *I believe that p* cannot be used in hypothetical reasoning, so it is not a genuine inference.
- **Barnett (2016, abstract verified)**: "one can be justified in inferring from p that q only if p amounts to strong evidence that q is true", and p "often is not very strong evidence that you believe that p".
- **Extension sceptics**: Ashwell (desire), Samoilova (thought and intention), Tang (partial belief). These were not opened.

## Major Positions

### Empiricist inferential transparency (Byrne; Dretske)
- **Core claim**: self-knowledge comes by inference from a worldly premise (rule BEL), which is self-verifying and safe. It is economical, since no inner sense is needed, and it extends to all mental states.
- **Relation to tenets**: compatible with physicalism, and Byrne is a physicalist. Its extension to sensation requires the perceptual theory of pain plus the identification of pains with bodily disturbances (2026a). The Map's acquaintance view of phenomenal character declines that identification. There the Map and Byrne part.

### Bypass transparency (Fernández)
- **Core claim**: the grounds of a first-order belief directly ground the self-ascription, which is therefore non-inferential. It is limited to belief and desire.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral; it claims nothing about phenomenal states.

### Agentialist and rationalist transparency (Moran; Boyle; Burge and Bilgrami adjacent)
- **Core claim**: we are entitled to answer "Do I believe P?" by deliberating about P, because we are responsible for our attitudes. Self-knowledge of attitudes is "making up one's mind". Boyle splits the field into active knowledge of judgement and passive knowledge of sensation, and in 2024 grounds transparency in nonpositional self-awareness.
- **Relation to tenets**: it fits the Map's interest in agency (the deliberative stance treats the subject as author of her attitudes) without evidencing Bidirectional Interaction. The account is normative and epistemic and makes no causal claim. Boyle's 2009 concession that sensations need a separate, passive account converges with the Map's limit without supporting dualism.

### Expressivist ascent routines (Gordon)
- **Core claim**: ascent routines, plus expressive self-ascription, cover all the attitudes.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral.

### Rivals named for orientation only (not researched here)
- **Inner sense** (Armstrong, Lycan; the self-blindness page). Byrne (2005, p. 100) calls it "partly right" about belief.
- **Inferentialism / parity** (Carruthers; Cassam). The open research task on Carruthers's ISA theory covers this. Note the overlap and do not duplicate it.
- **Acquaintance** (the Map's position for phenomenal states; phenomenal-authority).

## Key Debates

### The puzzle of transparency
- **Sides**: Gallois (rationality argument), Moran (transcendental entitlement), Byrne (self-verification plus safety), Fernández (bypass, non-inferential) and Boyle (reflectivism) offer solutions. Boyle (2011), Valaris (2011), Barnett (2016) and Gertler (2011b) object.
- **Core disagreement**: how a transition from p to *I believe that p*, which is neither deductively valid nor inductively strong, can yield knowledge.
- **Current state**: open. Boyle 2024 and Byrne 2026b are the current statements.

### The extension problem
- **Sides**: Byrne and Gordon (uniform; all states) against Fernández (belief and desire), Boyle 2009 (two kinds) and Cassam (Generality Problem).
- **Core disagreement**: whether the method reaches desire, intention, emotion and sensation. Byrne's route to sensation goes through the perceptual theory of pain.
- **Current state**: open. **The Map's stake is here**; see the Potential Article Angles below.

### Transparency and self-blindness (bridge to [self-blindness](/concepts/self-blindness/))
- **The positions**:
  - Gallois (1996, p. 76) argues from a self-blind believer to the necessity of the doxastic schema.
  - Byrne (2005, §7.4) says his account is "a version of Shoemaker's 'broad perceptual model'" that nonetheless vindicates Shoemaker's appeal to rationality.
  - Byrne (2011 §5) treats the non-occurrence of self-blindness as evidence that the correct theory is economical.
  - Byrne (2018, ch. 2) charges inner sense with giving "no explanation of why Shoemakerian 'self-blindness' does not actually occur".
- **Core point**: transparency offers a third explanation of the George result, beside Shoemaker's constitutive one and the inner-sense theorist's de dicto stipulation. Self-knowledge of belief comes with the capacity to reason about the world, so a rational subject who answers "whether p" thereby has the means to answer "whether I believe that p".
- **Scope guard**: Byrne's claims concern **actual** non-occurrence ("not an actual condition"), not conceptual impossibility. His account keeps the independence condition that Shoemaker's target model has. Byrne (2005, p. 98) allows a subject with the concept of belief but without the capacity to follow BEL.
- **The empirical premise**: Byrne's "Such dissociations do not seem to occur" (2011 §5) meets the Map's clinical record. [alexithymia](/concepts/alexithymia/) (a graded difficulty identifying emotions in subjects with intact general cognition) is the nearest candidate for a domain-specific dissociation. Whether it counts is open: Byrne 2018 ch. 7 covers emotion, and the self-blindness page already rules alexithymia out as total blindness. **Record it as an open question, never as a refutation.**

### What "transparency" means (disambiguation)
- **(a) Edgley/Evans/Moran/Byrne**: the transparency of the question "Do I believe that p?" to "p?". This is the subject of the proposed page.
- **(b) Moore/Harman/Tye**: the transparency or diaphanousness of experience, the negative thesis that one cannot attend to intrinsic features of experience. The Map covers it at [first-order-representationalism](/concepts/first-order-representationalism/) §"The Transparency Argument".
  - SEP §2.3.4 treats (a) and (b) as positive and negative theses of one family: "The positive and negative theses are separable".
  - Byrne 2011 §2 derives (a) from (b) for perception, while noting that inward attention may exist but be "not necessary".
- **(c) Metzinger**: phenomenal transparency of the self-model ([phenomenal-transparency-opacity-spectrum](/concepts/phenomenal-transparency-opacity-spectrum/)), which is a different notion again.
- **(d) Self-intimation**: "Cartesian transparency" (the [self-blindness](/concepts/self-blindness/) page L42; SEP: "a completely different usage... for example in Wright 1998; Bilgrami 2006").
- **(e) The Map's own "transparency problem"** for idealism (the q3-q4 sliding-boundary article). The research note `research/idealist-answers-to-the-transparency-problem-2026-10-11` records it as the Map's coinage.

6. **Where the method stops (#limit), the Map's contribution**: the extension runs belief → perception → sensation. Each step needs more, and the step to pain needs the perceptual theory of pain plus Byrne's identification of pains with disturbances. Its cost, in Byrne's own statement, is that there are hallucinations of pain. Boyle (2009) and Cassam (2014) leave sensation outside the method, and Fernández stops at desire. The Map's position is that the method explains how one comes to know *that* one believes, intends or sees. Phenomenal character is reached only by treating it as a represented or perceived worldly feature, which is the representationalist step the Map declines on [first-order-representationalism](/concepts/first-order-representationalism/). Two consequences follow:
   - (i) self-knowledge of attitudes carries **no** evidential weight for dualism;
   - (ii) the phenomenal case stays where phenomenal-authority's layered account puts it, with weak Revelation only ([revelation-thesis](/concepts/revelation-thesis/)).

### Guards (binding on the article writer; copied here because task fields above Notes are dropped)

1. **Coiners.**
   - Evans did **not** use "transparency" or "ascent routine" (0 OCR hits each).
   - "Ascent routine" is Gordon's; that 1995 is its first use is a LEAD.
   - "Transparent" in this sense is Edgley's (1969, quoted in Moran 2001, p. 61).
   - "Transparency Condition" is Moran's; "doxastic schema" is Gallois's.
   - "Puzzle of transparency" and BEL are Byrne's (2005, p. 95). The puzzle itself was posed by Gallois (1996, p. 47) and Moran (2003, p. 413).
   - "Transparency Method (TM)" as a capitalised label is Cassam's (2014, p. 2).
   - The "puzzle of reflection" is a LEAD and must not be attributed.
2. **Quote wording.**
   - Evans: "I get myself **in a** position…", never SEP's "into the position".
   - Moran p. 64: "toward one's own state of mind", never Byrne's "towards one's state of mind".
   - Byrne 2011 is quoted from the author's manuscript, so cite it by section, not page.
   - Byrne 2018, Byrne 2026a and 2026b, and Boyle 2024 are abstract-only. Paraphrase them, or quote the abstract as abstract.
   - Edgley is quoted only "as quoted in Moran 2001".
3. **Do not misdescribe the limit.**
   - Byrne holds that transparency "covers belief and perception" (2005, p. 99) and extends to sensation (2018, ch. 6; 2026a). The claim that it fails for phenomenal character is **the Map's verdict against his explicit extension**, and must be presented as such.
   - Do not attribute to transparency theorists the claim that inward attention is impossible. That is the negative thesis (Harman/Tye). Byrne 2011 §2 allows that an inward procedure may exist but be "not necessary".
4. **Author-stance firewall.** Do not enlist Evans, Edgley, Gordon, Gallois, Moran, Fernández, Byrne, Boyle or Cassam for dualism or for the Map's subject. Byrne is explicit that "self-knowledge" only *suggests* a non-physical self (2011 §1). Boyle's nonpositional self-awareness is Sartrean phenomenology, not a substance claim.
5. **Self-blindness scoping.**
   - Byrne's claims concern **actual** non-occurrence, and his account is "a version of Shoemaker's 'broad perceptual model'" (2005, p. 98). Do not present him as a constitutivist or as proving Shoemaker's impossibility thesis.
   - Alexithymia goes in as an open question bearing on the no-dissociation premise, never as a counterexample to Byrne.
6. **Cassam scoping.** His Generality, Substitution and Matching Problems target "Rationalism" (Moran via Finkelstein), not Byrne or Fernández (p. 3, footnote). He says explicitly that the sensation limit is "not an objection".
7. **Calibration.**

   - Weak Revelation only. Phrase the Map's claim as "phenomenal character is presented", never as "introspection reveals its essence".
