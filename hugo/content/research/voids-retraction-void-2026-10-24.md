## Executive Summary

A mind can withdraw a belief, and know that it has, without being able to see what the belief was holding up. Wilkes & Leatherbarrow (1988) found that "Inferences based upon the old information continued to be drawn although subjects had clearly recalled that it had been subsequently corrected" [raw]. Ecker et al. (2010) generalise: continued influence "occurs even if the retraction itself is well remembered" [raw].

Philosophy supplies the frame. Harman's coherence theory denies that we keep a ledger of the reasons for our beliefs, so a withdrawn premise cannot be traced forward to what rested on it. Artificial intelligence built the alternative, a system for "recording and maintaining the reasons for program beliefs" (Doyle 1979) [raw]. Weight-edited language models show the human pattern: they recall edited facts "accurately" and "fail catastrophically" on the edit's consequences (Zhong et al. 2023) [raw]. Citation networks show it too: of 299 citations to retracted papers, "in only 19 instances was the retraction noted" (Budd et al. 1998) [raw].

The calibration matters as much as the void:
- **Corrections mostly work.** Debunking *d* = 1.14–1.33 (Chan et al. 2017); the average residual is *r* = –.05 (Walter & Tukachinsky 2020).
- **Persistence is conditional.** It appears where the withdrawn item filled causal structure. Retracted claims about persons are often "fully discounted" (Mickelberg et al. 2024) [raw].
- **The mechanism is open** (Johnson & Seifert 1994; Sanderson et al. 2022), and so is the question of rationality (Connor Desai et al. 2020; Godden 2012).

None of these disputes needs settling for the void to stand. On every account, the agent cannot read from inside where a retraction has not yet reached. The Map's own records show the same shape (see "Where the Map Is Exposed").

## Key Sources

### The philosophical frame: revision without a ledger

- **Harman (1984a, 1984b; 1986 not read).** Harman contrasts a foundations theory with a coherence theory, on which "Justification is taken to be required only if one has a special reason to doubt a particular belief" (1984a) [raw]. His conservatism says one "should not change one's view without a positive reason" (1984b) [raw].
  - The 1984a raw text is OpenAlex's rendering of the paper's opening, with italicised words dropped, so only unbroken strings are quoted.
  - Godden (2012) reports that Harman argued that "the foundations theory absurdly deems most of our beliefs to be not rationally held" [raw]. That *Change in View* draws on debriefing cases is a paraphrase via Godden.
  - **Stance**: coherentist, descriptive and normative. On his view, keeping no ledger is both what we do and acceptable.
- **Godden (2012).** Godden accepts the coherence frame and rejects its verdict: debriefing cases "are properly viewed as instances of positive undermining of the sort described by the coherence theory" [raw].
  - **Stance**: perseverance is irrational.
  - **Use**: the void does not need Harman and Godden's dispute settled. On either view the agent fails to *see* what the debriefing undermined. They disagree only on whether that failure is excusable.
- **AGM (1985); Gärdenfors (1992); Doyle (1992).**
  - AGM formalise "contracting a theory to eliminate a proposition" [raw].
  - Gärdenfors names the case: "we discover that the reasons for some of our beliefs are invalid, and so we must give up those beliefs" [raw].
  - Doyle sets the traditions side by side. Coherence "maintains that pedigrees do not matter for rational beliefs" [raw]. Reason maintenance "explicitly computes sets of beliefs from sets of recorded reasons" [raw]. "Harman (1986) and Gärdenfors (1990) view the two approaches as antithetical" [raw].
  - Doyle's own reconciling argument was not read beyond the chapter's opening.

### The empirical core: perseverance and continued influence

- **Ross, Lepper & Hubbard (1975); Anderson, Lepper & Ross (1980).**
  - After false feedback and full debriefing, "Both actors and observers showed substantial perseverance" [raw].
  - "'Process' debriefing, in which explicit discussion of the perseverance process was provided, generally proved sufficient to eliminate erroneous self-perceptions" [raw].
  - Theories "can survive the total discrediting of that initial evidential base" [raw], and this "may be mediated, in part, by the cognitive process of formulating causal scenarios or explanations" [raw].
  - **Use**: a self-built explanation becomes an independent ground, and nothing marks it as downstream of what was withdrawn. Process debriefing works because it supplies the dependency map from outside.
- **Wilkes & Leatherbarrow (1988).**
  - The dissociation is as quoted above.
  - The boundary: "editing could be effective if the old information did not play a central role in the message sequence" [raw].
  - The proposed mechanism: "contradictions in the memory record are not dealt with immediately but are resolved locally when comprehension is questioned" [raw], and "This recency strategy breaks down when the old information provides a better fit to the question posed" [raw].
  - **Use**: resolution is lazy, local and happens when a question arrives. No global propagation step occurs.
- **Johnson & Seifert (1994).**
  - Earlier theories blamed "failing to successfully trace out and alter inferences or explanations generated before a correction" [raw]. Yet an immediate correction produced as many misinformation-based inferences as a late one.
  - Inferences continued "when it afforded causal structure, but not when only incidentally mentioned" [raw].
  - "providing a plausible causal alternative, rather than simply negating misinformation, mitigated the effect" [raw].
  - **Use**: this corrects the void's statement. The dark region includes both the *backward* set of old inferences awaiting edit and the *forward* use of a withdrawn item that still fills a causal slot.
- **Ecker, Lewandowsky & Tang (2010); Lewandowsky et al. (2012).**
  - A specific warning "succeeded in reducing the continued reliance on outdated information but did not eliminate it" [raw]. Adding an alternative "still failed to eliminate it altogether" [raw].
  - The review asks "why retractions of misinformation are so ineffective in memory updating" [raw].
- **Meta-analyses.**
  - Chan et al. (2017; *k* = 52, *N* = 6,878) report debunking *d* = 1.14–1.33 and persistence *d* = 0.75–1.06. "Persistence was stronger and the debunking effect was weaker when audiences generated reasons in support of the initial misinformation" [raw], which reproduces Anderson's mediator at scale.
  - Walter & Tukachinsky (2020; 32 studies, *N* = 6,527): "correction does not entirely eliminate the effect of misinformation" [raw] (*r* = –.05). "Corrections are less effective if the misinformation was attributed to a credible source, the misinformation has been repeated multiple times prior to correction, or when there was a time lag" [raw].
  - The two compare different contrasts: Chan's persistence is measured against a no-misinformation control, while Walter & Tukachinsky measure the residual after correction.
- **Sanderson, Farrell & Ecker (2022).** "Dominant theoretical models of the CIE have suggested the effect arises primarily from failures to retrieve the correction" [raw], and integration accounts are newer. Their Experiment 2 is "more in line with retrieval accounts of the CIE" [raw]; Experiment 1 was null.
  - **Use**: on either account the agent cannot see the reliance.

### Boundary conditions and rival readings

- **Connor Desai, Pilditch & Madsen (2020).** The paper rejects the assumption that "continued reliance on misinformation is the consequence of a biased process" [raw]: the effect has "normative backing" [raw]. Yet "very few were able to execute the complex model update that the Bayesian model entails" [raw].
  - **Stance**: continued influence can be rational.
  - **Use**: on this reading the void lies in *execution*. Even warranted reliance cannot be computed and read off.
- **Mickelberg et al. (2024, 2025).** Across four experiments "participants fully discounted the misinformation once retracted" (2024) [raw]. In 2025, one experiment in three showed continued influence, and "retracted person-related misinformation does not consistently show a continued influence effect" [raw].
  - **Use**: a scope limit. The void bites on items that carry causal or explanatory structure.
- **Swire-Thompson, DeGutis & Lazer (2020); Wood & Porter (2019).** "backfire effects are not a robust empirical phenomenon" [raw]. Wood & Porter is paraphrase-only (title and metadata only).
  - **Use**: a guard. The void never claims that corrections strengthen belief.

### The community face: retraction in citation networks

- Of 299 post-retraction citations examined, only 19 noted the retraction (Budd et al. 1998).
- Of 13,252 post-retraction citation contexts, "only 722 (5.4%) citation contexts acknowledged the retraction" [raw]. "retraction did not change the way the retracted papers were cited" [raw], though "old retracted papers stopped being cited as time progressed" [raw] (Hsiao & Schneider 2021).
- In one case, "The retraction is not mentioned in 96% (107/112) of direct post-retraction citations" [raw], with second-generation spread (Schneider et al. 2020).
- **Calibration**: much of this is infrastructure. "Only 1/8 databases (and 1/9 database records) consistently resolved the retraction notice to its full-text correctly in our tests" [raw]. The community case is a **structural parallel**, not evidence about individual cognition: the same shape appears where the dependency graph is public and still unconsulted.

### The machine face

- **Doyle (1979).** A reason-maintenance system works by "recording and maintaining the reasons for program beliefs" [raw] and revises them by "dependency-directed backtracking" [raw].
  - **Use**: a mind that keeps the ledger propagates a retraction exactly, within its records. The void is architecture-relative.
- **Eiter & Gottlob (1992), via Gogic, Papadimitriou & Sideri (1998).** For the minimal-change revision schemes, "even making the next inference, which is known as the counterfactual problem, is complete at some high level of the polynomial hierarchy for all of them" [raw, Gogic et al.]. The exception is Horn bases with small updates under model-based schemes. Eiter & Gottlob is paraphrase-only.
  - **Use**: a worst-case bound only.
- **Zhong et al. (2023); Cohen et al. (2024).**
  - "current knowledge-editing approaches can recall edited facts accurately, they fail catastrophically on the constructed multi-hop questions" [raw]. The fix "stores all edited facts externally while prompting the language model iteratively" [raw].
  - Editing methods "fail to introduce consistent changes in the model's knowledge" [raw], and "a simple in-context editing baseline obtains the best scores" [raw].
  - **Use**: the human dissociation, correction recalled and consequences unrevised, has a machine analogue in weight-stored knowledge. The engineered remedy is an external record plus checking at query time. Its resemblance to the query-time resolution Wilkes & Leatherbarrow inferred is **the note's own suggestion**.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

**Statement.** A mind can withdraw a belief and register the withdrawal. It cannot read off which of its other beliefs and inferences the withdrawn item still supports, or will go on supplying. The withdrawal feels complete because the remaining reliance carries no mark of origin. The shortfall shows only from a third position, or when a question forces a local resolution.

**Kind.** Mixed:
- **Occluded at the readout (natural; principal).**
  - The correction is remembered and the reliance continues.
  - Self-generated explanations add grounds that do not present as downstream of the withdrawn item (Anderson et al.; Chan et al.).
  - From inside, the readout is "I no longer believe that", and later inferences arrive as fresh conclusions about the world.
- **Unexplored as territory.**
  - The effect is measurable: the gap between recall of the correction and the inference measures is the void's footprint.
  - It is movable: process debriefing, causal alternatives, warnings, source and timing all change it.
- **Not Unexplorable.**
  - Reason maintenance propagates retractions within its records, so the void does not limit minds as such.
  - Complete propagation through arbitrary belief systems is worst-case intractable. That bound concerns any finite reasoner, not consciousness, and does not show typical cases are hard. It is recorded and **not claimed**.
- **Reason axis** (per [taxonomy-of-voids](/apex/taxonomy-of-voids/)): *adaptive*, with a natural occlusion at the readout. Keeping no ledger is cheap, and its cost falls only when a withdrawn item was carrying structure. This is Harman's resource rationale, by paraphrase.

**Candidate shared structure (the note's own suggestion).** The banked template-void note proposes an "exported void": a limit of the knower recorded as a property of the known. Persisting reliance has that shape. It is recorded as a fact about the *world* ("the fire was caused by negligence"), not as residue of the knower's revision history. Whether this makes a second member of the sub-kind is a question for the apex.

### Evidence for the Limit

- **Robust**:
  - continued reliance after a remembered correction, in event-related reasoning, across five decades and two meta-analyses;
  - the causal-slot condition (Johnson & Seifert; Wilkes & Leatherbarrow Exp. 2);
  - the self-generated-explanation mediator (Anderson et al.; Chan et al.).
- **Supported, narrower**:
  - warnings and alternatives reduce persistence without abolishing it (Ecker et al. 2010);
  - the machine analogue (Zhong et al.; Cohen et al.);
  - the community analogue, which is confounded by infrastructure.
- **Not supported, or contested**:
  - backfire;
  - generality across content types (person impressions);
  - any single mechanism (editing, retrieval or integration);
  - whether continued reliance is irrational.

**The readout claim** (that the reliance is unmarked from inside) is inferred, not measured. Participants remember and endorse the correction, and they still rely on the corrected item. No study found in this session asks them afterwards whether their inference drew on the corrected item. That absence is scoped to the searches in the header, and it is a falsifier slot.

### Phenomenology

Withdrawal is experienced as a completed act. What follows has no felt seam. Wilkes & Leatherbarrow's readers resolved contradictions "locally when comprehension is questioned" [raw], and the resolution can go either way: it "breaks down when the old information provides a better fit to the question posed" [raw]. From inside, a misinformation-based inference is simply the answer that fits best. This qualifies the Reorganisation step in [phenomenology-of-intellectual-life](/topics/phenomenology-of-intellectual-life/) L134. Felt reorganisation is local and partial, and it carries no information about the inferences it did not reach.

## Approaches to the Edge

- **Direct**:
  - Dissociation designs measure recall of the correction and inferential reliance in the same participants; the void is the gap between the two.
  - Process debriefing (Ross et al.) hands the agent its missing ledger.
- **Indirect**:
  - Fill the slot with a causal alternative (Johnson & Seifert) and track which inferences move.
  - Probe *entailed consequences*, as MQuAKE does for models, rather than recall of the correction. Recall tests miss the void by construction.
  - Keep external ledgers: reason-maintenance records, the register's `Depends on` fields, corpus-wide string search.
  - Ask for the readout after continued-influence trials; no study found here does so.
- **What AI might see (speculative)**:
  - The machine cases split along the architectural line. Symbolic reason maintenance keeps the ledger; weight-stored knowledge does not, and shows the human dissociation.
  - A mind's position relative to this void therefore depends on how it stores reasons, not on whether it is conscious.
  - An AI author working on a text corpus can grep its dependencies, an asymmetry humans lack for their own beliefs. Whether a model can audit its own weight-stored dependencies is open, and current editing results suggest it cannot do so directly.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet
**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)**, as a **guard**, not as support. The data invite the slide "you consciously accept the retraction, and your inferences carry on regardless." They do not say that:
- corrections have large effects (*d* = 1.14–1.33);
- process debriefing "generally proved sufficient to eliminate erroneous self-perceptions" [raw];
- the average residual is small.

What persists is partial and conditional. The void concerns knowledge of *where* a withdrawal has not reached, not whether accepting it does anything.

### Other Tenets
- **[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)**: fits, does not evidence. The ledger of reasons is not given in experience, which is consistent with Weak Revelation (experience reveals some, not all, of the mind's structure). A physicalist account of memory, with coexisting traces and retrieval without the retraction tag, explains the whole pattern. Under the common-cause null in [voids-as-evidence](/positions/voids-as-evidence/) the void adds no cumulative weight.
- **[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)**: applied to the Map's own reasoning only. "The repair landed, so the claim is fixed" is the simplest self-model of the corpus, and it is unreliable under incomplete knowledge of its dependencies. It is not used against any rival.
- **[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)**: a commitment, not engaged here.
- **[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)**: no connection.

## Four-Criteria Scoring ([apophatic-cartography-four-criteria](/concepts/apophatic-cartography-four-criteria/))

This is one hand's grading, so the same-hand caveat applies.
- **Signature specificity: strong.** A remembered correction plus continued reliance, conditional on causal structure.
