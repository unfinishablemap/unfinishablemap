## Executive Summary

Every source referred into this pass now has resolved metadata, and two of them change what the article should say rather than merely adding to it. **The Hori microstaging scheme has nine stages**, confirmed against two independent secondary attestations that cite the 1994 chapter directly; the "ten" claim traces to a single 2026 paper that contradicts its own first author's 2023 review. Separately, a **preregistered conceptual replication of Lacaux et al. (2021) found no N1 effect on insight** and published in PLOS Biology in 2025 — the article's current "awaiting replication" hedge is now factually stale, and the replication points at N2 rather than N1. A third find, **Decat et al. (2026) in Cell Reports**, is the strongest new item on the article's own thesis: the same phenomenological state cluster occurs across wake, N1, and N2, which loosens the yoking of phenomenology to sleep stage that the article's staged-disengagement reading assumes. The local-sleep material and the N1-incubation control that this task originally anticipated are already in the article and cited; that ground should not be re-surveyed.

## Answer on the Hori Stage Count: NINE

**The scheme is nine stages.** Recorded with provenance because the disagreement is real and traceable, not a case of two different schemes.

Evidence for nine:

1. **Tanaka, H., Hayashi, M. & Hori, T. (1996)**, *Sleep* 19(9), 731–738 — Hori's own group, the methods paper for the scheme. Abstract examines "nine hypnagogic electroencephalographic (EEG) stages" and names them by index: alpha patterns (stages 1, 2, 3), EEG flattening (stage 4), ripples (stage 5), vertex sharp wave (stages 6, 7, 8), spindles (stage 9). *Access: abstract inspected at Oxford Academic.*
2. **Biabani, N., Birdseye, A., Higgins, S., et al. (2023)**, *J. Thoracic Disease* 15(8), 4530–4543 — systematic review of 139 sleep-onset studies. States the scheme "subdivides standard scoring stages W, NREM 1 and NREM 2 into nine EEG-based sequential stages", citing Hori, Hayashi & Morikawa (1994) as its reference 40. *Access: full text inspected.*
3. The canonical list is stable across independent secondary descriptions: (1) alpha train; (2) alpha intermittent ≥50%; (3) alpha intermittent <50%; (4) EEG flattening; (5) ripples; (6) vertex sharp wave solitary; (7) vertex sharp wave bursts; (8) vertex sharp waves with incomplete spindles; (9) spindles. Stages 1–2 map to wake, 3–8 to Stage 1 sleep, 9 to Stage 2.

Where "ten" comes from:

- **Biabani, N., Birdseye, A., Gnoni, V., et al. (2026)**, *npj Biological Timing and Sleep* 3(1), art. 8, says: "the Hori staging system offers a 10-stage microstructural description of sleep onset based on 4-s EEG epochs" and "Hori microstaging was performed manually in contiguous 4 s mini-epochs using the standard 10-stage system". Both sentences cite Tanaka et al. (1996) and Hori et al. (1994) — the two nine-stage sources. *Access: full text inspected; raw HTML grepped for every occurrence of "Hori" rather than confirmed by prompt.*
- The 2026 mapping is internally shifted, not merely renumbered. It reports H1–H3 as alpha (matches canonical 1–3), then H4–H8 as "flattening and early theta" (canonical 6–8 are vertex sharp waves, not theta), then H9–H10 as "vertex waves and spindle-like activity" (canonical 9 is spindles, and no tenth stage exists). Its analyses run over H4–H10 with canonical progression H4→H10.
- The same first author and the same senior author (Rosenzweig) published the nine-stage description three years earlier. This is one group's internal inconsistency, and the earlier statement is the one that matches Hori's own methods paper.
- A targeted search for any "Hori stage 10" or 10-stage variant in the wider literature returned nothing.

One genuine complication worth recording, since it is probably how re-binning gets loose: **Hayashi, Katoh & Hori (1999)** themselves collapsed the scheme — "the hypnagogic EEGs were classified into **5 stages** according to Hori's modified criteria." Collapsed variants exist and are authored by Hori's group. None of them expands the scheme to ten.

**Caveat on access.** The 1994 chapter itself was not read. APA PsycNet serves a JavaScript shell and returns no body text to a fetcher, and no full-text copy was reachable. The chapter's own metadata is now confirmed to publisher standard via its DOI: **10.1037/10166-014**, Hori, T., Hayashi, M. & Morikawa, T. (1994), "Topographical EEG changes and the hypnagogic experience", in *Sleep Onset: Normal and Abnormal Processes*, pp. 237–253, American Psychological Association. The count therefore rests on two independent secondary sources that cite that chapter directly, plus Hori's own 1996 methods paper, rather than on the chapter's own words.

**Operational consequence.** The article's current wording at the Micro-Awakenings section credits Hori et al. with "a fine-grained EEG microstaging scheme" and gives no number. That wording is safe and can now be upgraded to "nine-stage" without hedging. The reference entry should also gain the DOI. Whichever pass does this should **not** cite Biabani et al. (2026) for the stage count.

Online-first 2022, issue 2023. Cite as 2023 with the issue, or 2022 as online-first, but state one convention and hold it. This is the two-systems shape, not a metadata defect.
- **On its weight**: it is a genuine peer-reviewed Review Article, not an editorial, and it structures itself around Schacter's (1976) six future research directions plus three of its own. But it self-describes as "a brief update" and its centre of gravity is phenomenology, prevalence, and clinical correlates. For *sleep-onset neurophysiology* specifically, the heavier synthesis is **Biabani et al. (2023)**, a systematic review of 139 studies from 1970–2022, and the more current framing review is **Lacaux et al. (2024)**. Calling Ghibellini & Meier "the principal synthesising review" would indeed be inflated.
- **Biabani et al. (2026) must not be cited for general sleep-onset structure.** Confirmed clinical-comparison pilot: narcolepsy type 1 (n=12), iRBD (n=12), NREM parasomnia (n=16), exploratory fibromyalgia (n=8), each with matched controls, 48 pairs total. Controls are drawn from the MASS open-access database, not recruited healthy volunteers — the paper says clinical healthy controls "were not available in sufficient numbers". The authors call it a pilot and a brief communication, and all fibromyalgia findings "hypothesis-generating". Its relevance to the Map is narrower and more interesting than general structure: it measures **ordering violations** at sleep onset (a Cumulative Ordering Index over canonical substage progression) and finds NT1 patients alone show non-zero out-of-order transitions.
- **Lacaux et al. (2024) has four authors, not three.** Lacaux, C., Strauss, M., Bekinschtein, T. A. & **Oudiette, D.** (2024), "Embracing sleep-onset complexity", *Trends in Neurosciences* 47(4), 273–288. Dropping Oudiette would be a citation defect.

## Genuinely New — Ranked by Consequence

### 1. The N1 creativity result failed a preregistered replication

**Löwe, A. T., Petzka, M., Tzegka, M. M. & Schuck, N. W. (2025).** "N2 sleep promotes the occurrence of 'aha' moments in a perceptual insight task." *PLOS Biology* 23, e3003185. DOI `10.1371/journal.pbio.3003185`. Preregistered (OSF), N=90, 20-minute daytime nap, perceptual insight task. *Access: **published full text inspected** (PLOS Biology manuscript XML fetched directly from the publisher and grepped, 2026-08-22, during the refine pass that consumed this note). Supersedes this note's original abstract-level access; the CCN 2024 conference version was also inspected but is no longer the source for any figure.*

Result, verbatim from the **published** paper: "We thus find no evidence that N1 sleep promotes insight as reported by Lacaux et al. [14]" — where reference 14 resolves in its bibliography to Lacaux et al. 2021, *Sci Adv*, confirming the replication targets the paper the article cites. The discussion adds: "the insight ratio of N1 subjects did not differ from subjects of the Wake group, thus providing no support for the hypothesis that N1 sleep fosters insight, contrary to [14]." Group rates: N2 85.7% (24/28), N1 63.6% (14/22), Wake 55.5% (10/18). N1 vs Wake p=0.75; N1 vs N2 p=0.1; **N2 vs Wake p=0.038** (Fisher's exact tests) — all three confirmed present in the published paper, not only the conference version. The published paper additionally reports Bayes factors this note originally lacked: N2>N1 BF=24.71, N2>Wake BF=8.19, N1>Wake BF=1.19, N1>N2 BF=0.04. Both preregistered hypotheses — that N1 would beat Wake, and that N1 would beat N2 — were unsupported. Exploratory: aperiodic 1/f spectral slope predicted insight beyond sleep stage; alpha and spindle power did not.

Why this matters more than an added citation. The article currently calls Lacaux 2021 "a striking single-study result awaiting replication". It is no longer awaiting one. A preregistered conceptual replication ran, and it inverted the stage assignment: deeper sleep, not the boundary state, carried the effect. The article's "dual-mode operation" section leans on N1 being functionally special — "consciousness loosened enough to reach associative connections executive control normally filters out, yet engaged enough to recognise solutions". That reading now has a direct disconfirming result against it and should be re-hedged rather than deleted; the replication used a different task, which is why the authors call it conceptual, and its N1 group was small (n=22).

This is the highest-value item in this note. It is a correction to a live claim, not an expansion.

**Three same-first-author items must be kept apart.** (1) The CCN 2024 conference version; (2) this 2025 *PLOS Biology* paper, 23(6) e3003185, the one to cite; (3) Löwe, Touzo, Muhle-Karbe, Saxe, Summerfield & Schuck (2024), "Abrupt and spontaneous strategy switches emerge in simple regularised neural networks", *PLoS Comput Biol* 20(10) e1012505 — a modelling paper, reference 10 of the 2025 paper, unrelated to the sleep result. Only (2) is cited in the article.

### 2. The same mental states occur at wake, N1, and N2

**Decat, N., Le Coz, A., Sénéchal, J., Scellier-Dekens, I., de Verville, H., Herzog, R., Lejeune, F.-X., Arnulf, I., Andrillon, T. & Oudiette, D. (2026).** "Dream-like mental states can occur during wakefulness." *Cell Reports* 45, 117237. DOI `10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117237`. *Access: abstract inspected via EuropePMC; Crossref metadata confirmed.* The prior pass's inability to find it was index lag, exactly as it guessed.

EEG in 92 participants across two daytime resting periods; 375 mental-content reports scored on bizarreness, fluidity, spontaneity, and wake perception; clustering yields four phenomenological profiles. From the abstract: "Strikingly, all these mental states emerge across wakefulness, N1 sleep, and N2 sleep." EEG features of spectral power, complexity, and connectivity differentiated the mental states **independently of** wake/sleep stage.

This bears directly on the article's core argument and cuts both ways, which is why it is worth the words. Against the article: if a given phenomenological profile appears at wake, N1, and N2 alike, then the article's ordered sequence — attention, then imagery, then agency, then reality-monitoring — is less tightly indexed to sleep stage than the staged-disengagement reading implies, and the "sequence maps the architecture" inference weakens. For the article: the finding that fine-grained brain dynamics rather than stage labels track phenomenology is exactly the kind of decoupling the multi-channel reading predicts, since channels are not supposed to be stage-synchronous. Either way it belongs in "What Would Challenge This View" or the single-mechanism section rather than as decoration.

### 3. Ordering violations are measurable and disorder-specific

From Biabani et al. (2026), used for what it actually tested. The Cumulative Ordering Index quantifies how often the empirical first-arrival order of substages inverts against canonical progression. Controls sat at floor across all four cohorts; narcolepsy type 1 alone showed non-zero inversions, clustered at early boundaries. Inter-rater reliability for the microstaging was substantial (Cohen's κ ≈ 0.74–0.75).

The relevance is methodological. The article's central empirical bet is that the *order* of disengagement carries information. Here is a validated instrument that scores exactly that, plus a demonstration that the order is stable in controls and breaks in one specific disorder. It is a small clinical pilot and cannot be cited for general structure, but it shows the article's key variable is measurable rather than merely phenomenological — and it names a falsification route more concrete than the one the article currently lists.

### 4. Secondary additions

- **Lacaux, C., Andrillon, T., Arnulf, I. & Oudiette, D. (2022).** "Memory loss at sleep onset." *Cerebral Cortex Communications* 3(4), tgac042. DOI `10.1093/texcom/tgac042`. N1-only rest produced ~10% more forgetting than N2; the alpha/theta power ratio correlated negatively with forgetting across both groups. *Access: abstract inspected.* Useful counterweight: the same group that reported N1 as a creative sweet spot also reports N1 as costly for memory. That is a capacity coming apart from another capacity at the same boundary, which is the article's own thesis shape — and it is a needed complication to any suggestion that N1 is simply beneficial.
- **Nielsen (2017)** microdream neurophenomenology, with **Windt (2019)**, niz005, as the published commentary. Nielsen proposes an "oneiragogic spectrum" classification of sleep-onset imagery plus two new imagery types (autosensory, exosensory); Windt argues some microdreams may not meet even minimal dreamhood. This pair is the closest thing in the literature to a taxonomy of hypnagogic phenomenology, and the article has no taxonomy citation beyond Mavromatis (1987). Windt is also a co-author on the Andrillon 2021 paper the article leans on.
- **Konkoly et al. (2026)**, *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2026(1), niaf067 — targeted memory reactivation in lucid dreamers during REM. Not a sleep-onset paper; noted only as the current state of the sleep-and-creative-insight literature, and it belongs to `topics/incubation-effect-and-unconscious-processing` rather than here.
