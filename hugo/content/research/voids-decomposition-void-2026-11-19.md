## Executive Summary

Some structures can be computed by a human but not grasped. The clearest measurement is Halford, Baker, McCredden and Bain (2005): asked to interpret graphed statistical interactions, academics declined sharply from three-way to four-way interactions, and "performance on a five-way interaction was at chance level". Halford's relational complexity theory reads this as a ceiling on the *arity* of a relation that can be held in one act, "a soft limit corresponding to the parallel processing of one quaternary relation", above which a mind must segment the problem or chunk it, and chunking makes "some relational information inaccessible". The same limit has a field-level face. Herbert Simon (1962) argued that our ability to understand complex systems depends on their being nearly decomposable, and he then turned the claim around: "If there are important systems in the world that are complex without being hierarchic, they may to a considerable extent escape our observation and our understanding." Fodor (1983, as quoted in the SEP) said the same of the mind's own central processes: "The more global (e.g., the more isotropic) a cognitive process is, the less anybody understands it." Evolutionary genetics supplies a worked case of the occluded face. The field "has mainly focused on epistasis between pairs of mutations", and Weinreich et al. (2013) report "substantial higher-order epistasis in almost every published fitness landscape". The void is real but narrow. Its size depends on an open empirical question, which is how much of nature is in fact nearly decomposable, and the realist reply that most of it is (Simon's own watchmaker argument; the SEP mechanisms entry; pairwise models that nearly match full-complexity ones on many datasets) is strong.

### Halford, Baker, McCredden & Bain (2005), "How many variables can humans process?"
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15660854/ (DOI 10.1111/j.0956-7976.2005.00782.x)
- **Type**: Empirical paper, *Psychological Science* 16(1): 70–76. **Abstract read at PubMed; full text not obtained.**
- **Key points**:
  - The task was chosen to resist decomposition: in interpreting interactions "all independent variables need to be considered together, so that decomposition into smaller subtasks is constrained, and thus the order of the interaction directly determines conceptual complexity" [PubMed abstract].

### Halford, Cowan & Andrews (2007), "Separating cognitive capacity from knowledge: a new hypothesis"
- **URL**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2613182/ (DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2007.04.001)
- **Type**: Review, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 11(6): 236–242. **Full text read (NIH author manuscript, PMC HTML).**
- **Key points**:

- **URL**: https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~ztatlock/599z-17sp/papers/complexity-simon-62.pdf

- **Key points**:

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The void has three faces, which are one limit seen at different scales.

1. **The grasp ceiling (Unexplorable).** One act of relational thought binds at most about four interrelated variables (Halford et al. 1998; 2005; 2007). A structure whose content *is* the joint dependence of five or more variables, such as a five-way interaction, cannot be held as one object. It can be computed, stored and plotted. It cannot be understood as a whole by a human. The two escape routes both cost something. Segmentation replaces the joint relation with a sequence of lower-order views. Chunking keeps the whole but makes its inner relations "inaccessible".
2. **The silent default (Occluded).** When neither route works, overload "is likely to be handled by default to a simpler representation, usually by ignoring one or more variables" (Halford et al. 2007). The mind keeps a representation that is complete in feel and missing a variable in fact. Sterman's subjects were "insensitive to the feedbacks from their decisions". Whether the dropped variable is felt as missing is unknown (see Gaps).
3. **The visibility filter (Occluded, field scale).** Science understands what it can decompose (Simon 1962; Fodor 1983; Bechtel & Richardson 1993). Simon's "other way round" sentence makes the consequence explicit: non-hierarchic complex systems "may to a considerable extent escape our observation and our understanding". Our picture of nature as nearly decomposable is then partly a fact about nature and partly a fact about which parts of nature reach our picture. Weinreich et al. (2013) is a measured case. Pairwise methods saw pairwise epistasis, and the higher-order kind was found only when a method of higher order was built.

The Map's reading of what unifies them, labelled as the Map's: Halford's own text makes hierarchy a *segmentation strategy* for a capacity-limited mind, and Simon makes hierarchy the *condition* of our understanding nature. Put together, the decomposition we find in the world and the decomposition we are forced to perform on hard problems may be one operation. If so, the field-level filter is the individual ceiling carried into method.

### Evidence for the Limit

- **Signature specificity.** The failure has a specific shape. Accuracy and speed drop between orders three and four, and performance is at chance at order five, on a task built so that the problem cannot be split (Halford et al. 2005). Overload resolves to a representation with a variable missing, and the result is not confusion. In the field, a pairwise analysis misses the higher-order structure and does not register that it has missed anything. None of the neighbouring voids predicts that pattern: quantity, nesting depth, level transition and relevance selection each produce a different one.
- **Structured persistence.** Simon in 1962, Fodor in 1983, Halford from 1998 to 2007, Weinreich in 2013 and the interpretability literature in 2022 describe the same constraint in different vocabularies over six decades. Simon's own concession has not been retracted by the realist reply. The reply counters it with the watchmaker argument and with the sciences' track record, and both of those are themselves evidence gathered through decomposition.
- **Cross-observer convergence.** Four disciplines arrive independently: cognitive psychology (relational complexity), philosophy of science (near-decomposability; decomposition and localization as heuristics), philosophy of mind (Fodor's First Law), and population genetics (higher-order epistasis). Machine learning supplies a fifth by defining "intelligible" at order two (Lou et al. 2013).
- **Framework independence.** High. Halford's account is a binding-capacity account with neural-net models; Simon and Fodor are physicalists; nothing in the limit needs dualism. It is evidence for none of the ontological tenets; its bearing on the epistemic tenet is set out under Connection to Tenets.

What keeps the limit narrow:

- The ceiling is "soft" (Halford et al. 1998), measured in one paradigm with 30 participants (press release), and contested as a parallel-processing claim (Chalmers & McGonigle; Pascual-Leone).
- Much of the world may in fact be nearly decomposable (SEP; Simon's watchmakers), in which case the occluded region is a residue. Pairwise models nearly match full-complexity models "on a number of real datasets" (Lou et al. 2013).
- Experts move the line by chunking (Halford et al. 2007: chunking and segmentation "are important components of expertise"). What they cannot do is chunk without cost. A chunk's inner relations stay inaccessible until it is unpacked.

### Phenomenology

The one direct report is collapse and restart: "Everything fell apart and I had to go back" (press release). The [phenomenology-of-cognitive-capacity](/topics/phenomenology-of-cognitive-capacity/) topic describes the approach to a capacity limit as *saturation*, in which items slip and the mind feels itself running out of room. The decomposition void adds a different texture at the far side of the edge. When the default to a simpler representation succeeds, there is no saturation, because the representation now fits. The Map's suggestion, which is a hypothesis: the most characteristic phenomenology of this void is the *absence* of strain once a variable has been dropped, a problem that suddenly feels tractable because it has silently become a different problem. Simon's drawing example gives the ordinary face: we describe a face outline-first, then features, then details, and the order feels like the structure of faces, not of describing.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)

None for the grasp itself. Five-way joint dependence cannot be brought into a single act by effort. External graphs do not lift the ceiling: the 2005 task supplied them and performance at order five was still at chance.

### Indirect Methods

- **Mark the edge by measurement.** Order-of-interaction tasks fix where the ceiling falls and let the ceiling be compared across expertise, notation and training. This is the most precise void boundary the catalogue would contain, comparable to the fifth-order figure in [recursion-void](/voids/recursion-void/).
- **Detect the silent default.** Give tasks whose correct answer requires all of n variables, and compare confidence with accuracy as n crosses four. A confidence curve that stays flat while accuracy falls would demonstrate the occluded face directly. No such study was found (see Gaps).
- **Build higher-order instruments.** Weinreich et al. found higher-order epistasis by generalising the formalism from pairs to "arbitrary subsets of mutations of all orders". The method is to construct a tool whose output is the residue that lower-order analysis leaves behind. The tool's verdict can be trusted without the user grasping the structure it reports.
- **Use the exit ramps.** Bechtel and Richardson's flowchart records where decomposition and localization fail in practice. Cataloguing those failures maps the void's boundary from the outside.

### What AI Might See

An asymmetry runs in both directions. Statistical learners fit high-order interactions routinely, so in one sense a model "holds" structure no human can. But the interpretability programme exists to decompose models into human-sized parts, and superposition shows the model's own units need not respect any such decomposition (Elhage et al. 2022). The likely result is a *handover* gap. An AI system can carry a five-way dependence and use it, and any explanation it gives a human must be segmented or chunked to order two or four, with the costs Halford names. Machine learning has already built this ceiling into its definition of an intelligible model (Lou et al. 2013). Whether a language model has its own relational-arity ceiling is unknown and testable with Halford's paradigm.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits.** Simon's empty world hypothesis, "most things are only weakly connected with most other things", is a simplicity assumption about the interaction structure of reality, and the decomposition void explains why it would look confirmed whether or not it is true. A mind whose comprehension tops out near four interrelated variables, using methods that find what decomposes, will report a sparse, hierarchic world. That report is partly an observation and partly an instrument effect, and the two cannot be separated from inside. Weinreich et al. show one field where the instrument effect was large and was later corrected. This is a concrete case of the tenet's general claim that simplicity is unreliable when knowledge is incomplete, and here the incompleteness has a measured size.

### Implications

- **Dualism and unity (the Map's analysis).** Halford's proposed mechanism is that related items must be "in the focus of attention concurrently". The ceiling therefore falls on the width of a single unified act of comprehension. The Map treats that unity as a datum ([unity-of-consciousness](/concepts/unity-of-consciousness/), [binding-void](/voids/binding-void/)). The decomposition void gives the unity a measured width, about four bound variables, without telling us what the unity is. A physicalist binding account explains the same width, so the point gives the Map a number to work with and no evidence for dualism.
- **The interface (the Map's analysis, with a guard).** If the consciousness–brain relation were a high-order interaction with no localizable mechanism, Fodor's First Law would predict that it is among the things "anybody" understands least, and the decomposition void would explain why decomposition-based neuroscience fails to find it. **Guard**: this must not be used as an immunising reply ("the interface is invisible because it is non-decomposable"). [what-voids-reveal](/voids/what-voids-reveal/) withdrew that form of reply as unfalsifiable and symmetric. The void explains why a failure to find a localized mechanism is weak evidence in *either* direction. It does not explain the failure in the Map's favour.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction, No Many Worlds**: no relevant bearing found.
- **Bidirectional Interaction**: no direct bearing. The silent default is a case of a mind *changing what it thinks about* without noticing, but it has an ordinary capacity explanation and should not be read as evidence of mental causation.
