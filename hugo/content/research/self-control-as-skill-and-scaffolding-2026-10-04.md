## Executive Summary

Self-control research has moved from inhibition to strategy. Trait self-control predicts success mainly through fewer temptations, habits and early situational strategies, while effortful resistance in the moment predicts goal attainment weakly or not at all. The philosophical skill models of Bermúdez, Sripada and Mylopoulos & Pacherie keep synchronic exertion at the core and treat prevention as what expertise delivers. Kennett and Ainslie supply the older taxonomies and record the costs of rigid control. For the Map, conscious selection moves to moments of policy and override, which [skill-delegation](/concepts/skill-delegation/) already describes, and the inference from felt effort to conscious contribution weakens.

### D. Mylopoulos & Pacherie (2020), "Self-Control as Hybrid Skill" [A]
- They draw on motor-skill research "to highlight important ways in which experts differ from novices in the capacities they deploy".
- They reconcile "a motivational construal of self-control, according to which it involves resisting competing temptations in order to do what one deems best, and an executive construal, in which the emphasis is on overriding “cold” habits that are at odds with what one intends to do."
- Bermúdez [2nd]: theirs is "a hybrid skill (one which merges top-down, reflective processes and spontaneous, automatic processes)".
- **Habit has two roles.** On the executive construal, habit is self-control's *target*. For Ainslie (§E), effortless habit is "a successful result of resolve". Compare [philosophy-of-habit-under-dualism](/topics/philosophy-of-habit-under-dualism/) §Habit Is Not Skill.

### E. Kennett (2001) and Ainslie (2001; 2021): the older taxonomies [S]/[A]

- **Definition** (p. 119): "self-control is the capacity to bring one's actions into line with one's judgements of value when there is such a divergence between judgement and desire".
- **Diachronic** (pp. 134–5): "Suppose Jane knows in advance that come tea-time she will want most to eat a cream cake". "Diachronic self-control is also relatively unproblematic for the agent, since the earlier circumstances of action, in which a strategy is put in place, differ significantly from the later circumstances". (A phrase search false-zeroed; the snippet shows it.)
- **Synchronic** (pp. 136–146): control "over a desire which was present and strongest at the very time at which I undertook the exercise. This deserves to be called synchronic self-control" (p. 146).
  - Attentional techniques: "she may narrow or redirect the focus of her attention" (p. 136). These "become automatic, and they can be very effective" (p. 140).
- **Rigidity** (p. 149): the extreme diachronic agent "structures her life and her character so as to avoid any situation where she will be vulnerable to a loss of control, closes off the possibility of evaluative discovery and revision". Also, "synchronic self-control too can be taken to extremes".
- Kennett & Smith 1996 is [M].

**Ainslie**, *Breakdown of Will* §5.1: "four kinds of tactics an interest can employ to commit future choice" (p. 74).
- **The four tactics**: extrapsychic commitment (p. 74), manipulation of attention (p. 76), preparation of emotion (p. 77), and "the personal rule to behave alike toward all the members of a category" (p. 84). The "fourth tactic, willpower, seems to be at once the strongest and most versatile, but has hitherto been mysterious" (p. 89).
- **Rigidity**: rules "can make large categories of differential reward hinge on decisions of little intrinsic importance; and they may be hard to modify once established" (p. 148). The rules of "anorectics or misers" are too strict, "but their exactness makes them more enforceable than subtler rules that depend on judgment calls" (p. 152).
- ***BBS* 2021** (44: e30) [A]: "Resolve is based on interpretation of a current choice as a test case for a broader set of future choices". "Suppression is inhibiting valuation of (modulating) and/or keeping attention from (filtering) immediate alternatives to a current intention". Reliable preference "is experienced as an effortless habit - a successful result of resolve, not an alternative method of self-control."
- **Sibling seam**: [the-divided-will](/topics/the-divided-will/) uses personal rules only as the way a will emerges without an organ. Here they are one tactic of four, with costs. Resolve cuts across the synchronic/diachronic split: it is exercised at the moment of choice, but it reads that choice as a precedent.

### F. The empirical record supporting a scaffolding reading [A]
- **Trait and automaticity.**
  - de Ridder et al. 2012 (meta-analysis, 102 studies, N = 32,648): associations "significantly stronger for automatic (as compared to controlled) behavior".
  - Galla & Duckworth 2015 (6 studies, N = 2,274): "emerging evidence that self-control is associated with less inhibition in daily life"; habits mediated the effect. Adriaanse et al. 2014 (cross-sectional) agree.
  - Gillebaart & de Ridder 2015: high scorers "do not seem to subjectively experience this conflict as much".
  - Baumeister, Wright & Carreon 2019 (N = 3,327): "People with high trait self-control reported fewer such feelings and events than others". The events were self-restraint, effortful decisions and pushing through unwanted tasks.
- **Resistance against temptation frequency.**
  - Milyavskaya & Inzlicht 2017, a single study: "goal attainment was influenced by experiences of temptations rather than by actively resisting or controlling those temptations". "effortful self-control was consistently unrelated to goal attainment throughout all analyses" (Bayesian).
  - Hofmann et al. 2012 (*JPSP*): desires are "largely unproblematic". The fewer-temptations finding for high scorers is [Lead]; it appears only in Bermúdez's reading.
- **Interpretive reviews.**
  - Inzlicht & Roberts 2024 (opinion): "individuals high in trait self-control do not engage in more state self-control but engage it less".
  - Bermúdez et al. 2024: "overcoming temptation is neither necessary nor sufficient for successful self-control".
  - Fujita 2011: inhibition is "only one of many means".
- **Situational strategies.**
  - Duckworth, Gendler & Gross 2016: they "may be especially effective in preventing undesirable action". Also: "by manipulating our circumstances to advantage, we are often able to minimize the in-the-moment experience of intrapsychic struggle typically associated with exercising self-control".
  - Duckworth et al. 2016: two one-week field experiments favoured situation modification, "partially mediated by decreased feelings of temptation".
  - Davydenko & Peetz 2024 (N = 363): proactive beat reactive, but "The control condition did not differ from the other conditions."
- **Implementation intentions.** Gollwitzer 1999: they "delegate the control of goal-directed responses to anticipated situational cues, which (when actually encountered) elicit these responses automatically". Meta-analyses: 94 tests, d = .65 (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006); 642 tests, ".27 ≤ d ≤ .66" (Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer 2025).
- **Extended will.**
  - Heath & Anderson 2010: "our distributed will and its supportive social scaffolding".
  - Haug 2024: "an excellent ability to use indirect strategies to achieve motivational harmony".

### G. Counterweights and replication status [A]
- **Milyavskaya, Saunders & Inzlicht 2021** (N = 197; 4,462 desires): "many strategies, including inhibition, are similarly effective and that using multiple strategies is especially effective". The authors present this as running against "recent theoretical predictions".
- **Hennecke, Czikmantori & Brandstätter 2019** (N = 264): no strategy accounted for the benefit of trait self-control. Trait and strategy use are "separate routes".
- **Friese et al. 2017** (in [akrasia-and-weakness-of-will](/topics/akrasia-and-weakness-of-will/)): training g = 0.30, or .13–.24 after bias correction.
- **Watts, Duncan & Quan 2018**: the marshmallow correlation was "only half the size of those reported in the original studies and was reduced by two thirds" with controls.
- **The folk concept is not efficacy.** Irving et al. 2022 (§C). Bermúdez, Murray, Chartrand & Barbosa 2023: participants "rated them [intra-psychic strategies] as more effective". Gennara, Peetz & Milyavskaya 2023: "willpower is more central to people’s idea of self-control than strategies". The folk belief runs against the scaffolding data.
- **Not established**: no preregistered multi-site test of the trait-through-fewer-temptations route was found. The experience-sampling data are self-report, the field experiments are short, and no bias-adjusted estimate for implementation intentions was checked.

## Major Positions (RQ1)

**(a) The empirical finding.**

| Claim | Five-tier status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Trait self-control tracks automatic behaviour, habits and fewer effortful-restraint events | *Strongly supported* | Meta-analysis, six studies, N = 3,327 experience sampling |
| Scaffolding is the main causal route | *Live hypothesis* | Opposed by Milyavskaya et al. 2021 and Hennecke et al. 2019 |
| Effortful resistance is causally idle | *Not established* | One study's Bayesian null on self-reported resistance |
| Implementation intentions work | *Strongly supported* | Bias adjustment unchecked |

**(b) The skill models.** In each, "skill" means a skill of *exercising* control. None says scaffolding replaces exertion, except as the endpoint of expertise.

| Model | Skill of what | Synchronic or diachronic | Felt effort | Endpoint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bermúdez 2021 [F] | Guiding the implementation and monitoring of strategies through flexible practical reasoning | Synchronic has "explanatory primacy", diachronic "practical prevalence" | Feedback; Kurzban's cost | Self-effacing |
| Sripada 2021 [A] | "skilled sequences of cognitive control" against response pulses | Synchronic; denies diachronic counts [2nd] | Not in abstract | Common-sense fit |
| Mylopoulos & Pacherie 2020 [A] | A "hybrid skill" modelled on motor expertise | Diachronic focus [2nd] | Not in abstract | Reconciles motivational and executive construals |

**(c) Kennett and (d) Ainslie** are summarised in §E. Neither ranks diachronic over synchronic control, and both record what rigid control costs.

## Key Debates

1. **Does scaffolding do most of the work?** Duckworth, Inzlicht & Roberts and Bermúdez lean yes. Milyavskaya et al. 2021 and Hennecke et al. 2019 complicate the claim. It is open, and its strongest form rests on correlational self-report.
2. **Is diachronic regulation self-control?** Sripada [2nd] and the folk concept say no, and Bermúdez et al. 2023 make it peripheral. Duckworth, Heath & Anderson and Haug say yes. Bermúdez thinks nothing hinges on the label.
3. **Rules or judgement?** Ainslie weighs enforceability against rigidity. Kennett holds that extreme control "closes off the possibility of evaluative discovery", and Bermúdez stresses flexible practical reasoning.
4. **What is felt effort?** One reading makes it a cost signal (Kurzban; Inzlicht; Bermúdez), the other a resource gauge. The resource reading failed preregistered tests, though an effect survives intense manipulations (Dang 2025).

## What This Does to an Effortful Controller (RQ2)

**It undercuts only an organ or effort-paradigm picture.** A selector defined by resistance in the moment would be in trouble. No Map page defines it that way, and [the-divided-will](/topics/the-divided-will/) already rules out "a faculty of general strength". The skill models are one more non-organ account, consistent with that article's verdict. Some pages, though, make felt synchronic effort the strongest evidence of conscious contribution (§Corpus Consistency), so the picture is not untouched.

**It relocates.** Synchronic control becomes the developmental foundation and, for skilled agents, "an emergency measure". Ordinary success moves to moments of policy: choosing situations, forming if-then plans, adopting rules. Each of these selections is made while the temptation is weak. Irving et al.'s folk data place self-control in a diachronic case at initiation and follow-through. That is a fact about the concept, though it matches where the Map would put selection.

**Where the Map's selector sits.** It sits at moments of policy and override, and in handing control to cues. That is [skill-delegation](/concepts/skill-delegation/)'s shape. Its L56 reads "Consciousness selects among alternatives where genuine choice is required and delegates the rest", and Gollwitzer's verb is also "delegate". The picture fits [control-theoretic-will](/concepts/control-theoretic-will/) as well. Its L74 says "The effort required scales with the disturbance magnitude", and situation selection removes a disturbance at its source, which any low-bandwidth controller should prefer.

**Discrimination: none.** Bermúdez's model is physical, Gollwitzer's delegation hands control to automatic processes, and a physical planner predicts the same distribution. By the decorative-falsifier rule (`project/evidential-status-discipline`), the delegation reply absorbs "if self-control turned out to be mostly scaffolding", so the Map cannot list it as a challenge or count it as support. This matches control-theoretic-will L130: "no current prediction separates a non-physical controller from a physical one".

**The cost: effort as evidence.** [phenomenology-of-choice-and-volition](/concepts/phenomenology-of-choice-and-volition/) L94 says "What remains owed is why felt effort attaches to exactly the selections that are contested", and reads that distribution as evidence of conscious contribution. The skill literature answers part of this physically. Felt effort attaches to contested selections because it is control's cost signal, the reading that [mental-effort](/concepts/mental-effort/) L88 already files on the materialist side and that Bermúdez adopts. Contested selections are the ones scaffolding failed to prevent. Felt effort then marks where control is hard, and on Bermúdez's account the most skilled control is the least effortful. The honest scope: felt effort is evidence about where the system meets resistance, and the claim that conscious contribution is largest there is a further one. Vohs et al. 2021 already showed felt effort rising with no reliable change in performance.

**The luck objection (aside).** Moving selection to moments of policy does not answer the luck objection that [the-divided-will](/topics/the-divided-will/) leaves standing. It moves the contrastive question to the moment the resolution is formed. An article must not offer scaffolding as a reply.

**Guards.**
- No view on the Tenet 3 quantifier (actual against capacity), which is referred to the operator.
- No universal claim that consciousness is causally efficacious.
- The bi-aspectual tension stays open. Diachronic control presupposes that the agent who arranges the situation is the agent who benefits from it, while the Map's aspects may need only a subject present at each selection. Ainslie's recursive self-prediction works without literal future selves (*Breakdown* p. 93, per the sibling note), so the literature does not force the persisting reading.
