## Executive Summary

The interpretive sensory-access (ISA) theory is Peter Carruthers's account of self-knowledge, developed in a 2009 *BBS* target article, a 2010 *PPR* paper and *The Opacity of Mind* (2011). One mindreading faculty serves both self and others. It receives only sensory-format input (perception, imagery, inner speech, bodily and affective feeling), together with situational and behavioural cues. So our knowledge of our own propositional attitudes (judgments, decisions, intentions, beliefs, desires) is "swift unconscious self-interpretation" (2009 abstract), not introspection. The theory is explicitly **divided**: it "won't challenge the existence of introspective access to perceptual and imagistic states, nor to emotional feelings and bodily sensations" (2010 abstract). Its corollary is stronger: there are hardly any conscious propositional attitudes, and so no conscious agency (2011, ch. 12 abstract). By 2015, amodal attitudes are never conscious (*The Centered Mind*, abstract).

## Key Sources

### Carruthers, "How we know our own minds: The relationship between mindreading and metacognition" (2009), *BBS* 32(2): 121–138
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X09000545 (Crossref; author-written BBS abstract via OpenAlex)
- **Key points** (verbatim from the abstract):
  - Four accounts of the mindreading–metacognition relation are compared. Three "endorse the existence of introspection for propositional attitudes". The fourth, which he defends, "claims that our knowledge of our own attitudes results from turning our mindreading capacities upon ourselves".
  - The "mindreading is prior" model "predicts introspection for perceptual and quasi-perceptual (e.g., imagistic) mental events while claiming that metacognitive access to our own attitudes always results from swift unconscious self-interpretation".
  - §3: "the commonsense belief in the existence of introspection should be given no weight".
  - §4: "data from childhood development are of no help in resolving this debate". **Guard**: Gopnik's developmental argument is Gopnik's, not part of Carruthers's evidence.
  - §7: "although there are introspectable events within System 2, there are no introspectable attitudes".
  - §8 "examines alleged evidence of 'unsymbolized thinking'". The verdict wording of §8 was not read, so it is a LEAD.
  - §§9–10: the claimed dissociations in schizophrenia and autism.
- **Names**: the abstract does not verify the labels of the other three models. They are a LEAD.
- **Response**: "Mindreading underlies metacognition", *BBS* 32(2): 164–182 (https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x09000831). It restates that "our access to our own propositional attitudes is through interpretation rather than introspection".
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral on dualism; physicalist-built.

### Carruthers, "Introspection: Divided and Partly Eliminated" (2010), *PPR* 80(1): 76–111
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1933-1592.2009.00311.x
- **Key points** (verbatim from the abstract; this is the cleanest scope statement):
  - "there is no such thing as introspective access to judgments and decisions. It won't challenge the existence of introspective access to perceptual and imagistic states, nor to emotional feelings and bodily sensations."
  - Introspection is "divided into two categories: introspection of propositional attitude events, on the one hand, and introspection of broadly perceptual events, on the other". He argues that the former doesn't exist "(or not in the case of judgments and decisions, at least)".
  - "The upshot is a limited form of eliminativism about introspection, in respect of at least two core categories of propositional attitude."
- **Note on scope**: the 2010 elimination is hedged to judgments and decisions "at least". The 2009 and 2011 statements cover attitudes generally. SEP's gloss is "propositional attitudes like belief and desire". The article should state the scope at the attitude level and keep the 2010 hedge visible.

- **Chapter abstracts** (OUP; paraphrase in the article, do not quote as Carruthers's words):
  - **Ch. 2**: the chapter tries to "explain away our common-sense intuitions of mental transparency". It suggests that "an implicit transparency assumption might be an innately channeled property of the human mind". This, not fluency, is the verified source for the medium-status apex sentence (see Integration (C)).
  - **Ch. 3**: names "the interpretive sensory-access (ISA) account" and claims support from "global broadcasting theory, theories of sensory-based working memory, and theories that see the evolution of metarepresentational capacities as driven by the demands of living in complex social groups".
  - **Ch. 4**: critiques three theories that accept the sensory-access component but claim the sensory events give transparent access to attitudes:
    - one on which self-knowledge comes "by looking outward, into the world" (the transparency-method family);
    - one on which it comes by "expressing our attitudes directly into speech" (expressivism);
    - one on which "our beliefs about our own attitudes can constitute, or bring it about, that we possess attitudes" (constitutivism).
    - Which authors he names in the chapter is a LEAD.
  - **Ch. 5, affect**: "the affective components of such attitudes can be globally broadcast". There is "an element of truth" in transparent sensory access to affective attitudes, but "people have very little insight into the sources of their affective feelings".
  - **Ch. 6**: rejects three intermediate accounts:
    - attitude-of-origin **tags** on working-memory contents (compare Rey 2013 below; whether ch. 6 names Rey is a LEAD);
    - a non-sensory working memory;
    - efference-copy access to "active intentions and mental actions".
  - **Ch. 7**: inner-sense theories, including "introspection-sampling evidence of amodal 'unsymbolozed' thinking". The typo is OUP's.
  - **Ch. 10**: inner-sense theories predict dissociations between self- and other-knowledge, and ISA "predicts an absence of dissociations". The chapter examines schizophrenia, autism and **alexithymia**, plus self/other imaging overlap.
  - **Ch. 11**: confabulation "just as predicted", focusing on "the self-perception and dissonance traditions in social psychology". He argues that transparent-access theories can adopt a dual-method account, but none can explain "the patterning in the data".
  - **Ch. 12**: "there exist hardly any conscious propositional attitudes (although there are many other kinds of conscious mental event). As a result, there is no such thing as conscious agency, and our beliefs about moral responsibility may need to be re-examined."
- **Book abstract**: a third-person publisher blurb ("he argues that access to our own thoughts is always interpretive, grounded in perceptual awareness and sensory imagery"). Do not quote it as Carruthers's words.
- **Coiner**: "ISA" is Carruthers's own label (the title of ch. 3).

### Carruthers, *The Centered Mind: What the Science of Working Memory Shows Us About the Nature of Human Thought* (2015), OUP
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198738824.001.0001 (Crossref title and subtitle; OUP abstract)
- **Key points** (abstract; paraphrase or cite as the book's abstract):
  - Conscious thought is "always sensory based", using the working-memory system.
  - When conceptual representations are bound into sensory images, "we experience ourselves as making judgments or arriving at decisions".
  - Amodal attitudes "are never among the contents of this stream of conscious reflection". Beliefs, goals and decisions work "behind the scenes to select the sensory-based imagery that occurs in working memory. They are never themselves conscious."
- **Guard**: 2011 says "hardly any" conscious attitudes, while 2015 says amodal attitudes are "never" conscious. Do not merge the two.

### Carruthers, *Human and Animal Minds: The Consciousness Questions Laid to Rest* (2019), OUP
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198843702.001.0001

## Major Positions

### Interpretive sensory access (Carruthers)
- **Core claim**: one metarepresentational faculty, fed only by globally broadcast sensory-format states and by contextual and behavioural cues, produces all attitude self-ascriptions by swift unconscious interpretation. Sensory, imagistic, bodily and affective-feeling states are introspected; attitudes are not.
- **Key arguments**:
  - Confabulation patterning (ch. 11).
  - The predicted absence of self/other dissociations (ch. 10).
  - Unification with global broadcasting, sensory working memory and the social evolution of mindreading (ch. 3).
  - The commonsense belief in introspection gets "no weight" (2009 §3), and the transparency intuition is explained away (ch. 2).
- **Relation to site tenets**: physicalist in origin. Its access thesis is metaphysically neutral. Its corollary (no conscious attitudes, no conscious agency) collides with [P-TU1](/positions/thought-and-understanding/#p-tu1) and with ordinary readings of Tenet 3, though not with Tenet 3 itself (explained under Key Debates).

### Precursor parity (Gopnik; Nisbett & Wilson; Bem)
- Gopnik 1993 argues for developmental parity and an "illusion of expertise".
- Nisbett & Wilson 1977 hold that processes are inaccessible but attitudes and sensations are knowable with "near certainty" (per SEP).
- Bem's self-perception theory (1967, 1972) is a LEAD here (SEP cites it in §4.2.2).
- **Relation**: ISA is the strongest member of the family. It differs from Nisbett and Wilson in moving attitudes to the interpreted side.

### Self-detection rivals (inner sense: Armstrong, Lycan; Nichols & Stich; Goldman)
- These posit dedicated monitoring. Nichols & Stich claim a double dissociation (per SEP §2.2.1). Goldman's 2006 account is quasi-perceptual and multi-process.
- **Relation**: ISA's ch. 7 and ch. 10 target them. The Map's own view for phenomenal states is acquaintance, a fourth option that is neither inner sense nor interpretation.

### Mixed and modest positions (Frankish; Rey)
- Frankish: interpretive access to unconscious attitudes and introspective access to conscious thoughts, on a two-level view.
- Rey: modest introspectionism by attention to processing tags.
- **Relation**: they show that the confabulation data underdetermine full parity.

### Transparency method (Evans, Moran, Byrne)
- This is covered in `transparency-method-of-self-knowledge`. ISA's ch. 4 rejects the outward-looking route as a source of *transparent* access.
- **The Map need not adjudicate**: on either account, attitude self-knowledge carries no evidential weight for dualism (the transparency page's verdict, L31 and L94).

## Key Debates

### Scope: which states are interpreted? {#scope}
- **Sides**: Carruthers (attitudes interpreted; perceptual, imagistic, bodily and affective feeling introspected) against careless secondary reports that make him an across-the-board interpretivist. Three Map pages make that error.
- **Current state**: settled by the primary texts (2009, 2010 abstracts; SEP §§2.1.2, 4.2.2). The one live nuance is affect. Ch. 5 grants "an element of truth" to transparent sensory access to affective attitudes, but people have "very little insight into the sources" of affective feelings.

### From interpretive self-ascription to no conscious attitudes
- **Sides**:
  - Carruthers: ch. 12; *Centered Mind*; the 2018 interview's "whichever view you adopt" argument from global workspace and higher-order theories.
  - Against: Frankish 2009 (the mixed position); Shepherd 2013 (conscious deciding not refuted).
- **Core disagreement**: whether a state's being self-ascribed by interpretation shows that the state is not conscious. The step needs a theory of what makes a state conscious, and the confabulation evidence does not supply one.
- **The Map's verdict (as the Map's)**: the access thesis can stand while the corollary is resisted. The corollary inherits its premises from functionalist theories of consciousness (global workspace, higher-order), which the Map does not accept as theories of phenomenal consciousness. That is a reason the corollary is not forced on the Map. It does not refute it.

### Unsymbolized thinking
- **Sides**: Carruthers (2009 §8; ch. 7) against Hurlburt & Akhter's report of experienced symbol-free thought. Hurlburt (2009) re-describes the phenomenon as possibly non-symbolically sensory, and so compatible with ISA.
- **Current state**: open. It is the modern form of the Würzburg–Titchener stalemate that `cognitive-phenomenology` already records. The Map should not cite unsymbolized thinking as clean evidence of proprietary cognitive phenomenology: its discoverer allows that it may be sensory.

### Dissociations
- **Sides**: Nichols & Stich (double dissociation in autism and schizophrenia) against Carruthers (ISA predicts none; ch. 10 also examines alexithymia).
- **Current state**: contested. Rimkevičius 2020 says the predictions held up over the decade, and SEP §2.1.2 calls the evidence "contentious".

### ISA as absorption, not witness
- In the medium-status and phenomenal-output clusters, ISA *predicts* the output-available, process-hidden profile from physicalist premises. `phenomenal-output-causal-machinery-dissociation` L177 already concludes: "Carruthers cannot be both a convergent witness and an exponent of the strongest absorption". `cross-architecture-llm-introspection` L106 applies the Map's rule that where a rival predicts what the Map only accommodates, "the evidential vector on that item points toward the rival".
- The new page should state this once, as the citers' common premise.
