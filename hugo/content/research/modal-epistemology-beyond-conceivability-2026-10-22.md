## Executive Summary

Five families of modal epistemology deny that conceiving is the basic route to knowledge of metaphysical possibility:
- **counterfactual**: Williamson 2007, and Hill 2006 in a related form;
- **essentialist**: Lowe 2012, Hale 2013 and Kment 2021, with Goff 2021 as a variant that keeps conceivability as a guide;
- **similarity- and induction-based modal empiricism**: Roca-Royes 2017;
- **dispositional or potentiality-based**: Vetter 2015, 2023;
- **theory-based**: Fischer 2016, 2017, with Biggs and Wilson's abductive variant.

**On the sources read, none of the five supports the zombie argument's step from conceivability to possibility by itself.** Each makes the verdict on zombies depend on something the argument was meant to establish:
- the counterfactual and essentialist routes need knowledge of which facts linking the phenomenal and the physical are constitutive or essential;
- the theory-based route needs a justified theory;
- the dispositional route needs knowledge of actual potentialities.

The most modest empiricist accounts are, in the SEP's words, "either skeptical or silent" about zombies.

**Two authors aim a rival modal epistemology directly at the mind–body case.**
- **Hill (2006)**, a type-B materialist, argues that "Cartesian modal arguments for property dualism presuppose" a priori grasp of the essential natures of qualitative properties, and "that this presupposition fails".
- **Allen (2022)** argues that on powers ontologies "the conception of properties as powerful blocks conceivability arguments unless a rather implausible form of emergence is true".

**Only Goff (2021), a Russellian panpsychist, offers a rival that rescues the step**, and only for conceivers who "grasp the essential nature" of what they conceive. That premise sits close to strong Revelation, which the Map denies.

**The cost for the Map.** The Map's conceivability premise needs modal rationalism of Chalmers's kind, which is one contested position among at least five. On every rival route surveyed, zombie possibility becomes downstream of the disputed metaphysics rather than independent evidence for it. The note names eleven Map pages that lean on the step ([Dependent pages](#dependents)).

**What survives:**
- **The explanatory gap.** Type-B materialists "typically allow that there is an epistemic gap" (Chalmers 2014).
- **Chalmers's 2007 dilemma against the phenomenal-concepts strategy.** On the Map's own report, it assumes "nothing about the relationship between conceivability and possibility" ([type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism](/concepts/type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism/) L101).
- **Symmetry.** The rivals cut both ways: none of them delivers the psychophysical identity unless the identity is first fed in as a constitutive fact.

## Key Sources

### SEP, "The Epistemology of Modality" (Mallozzi, Vaidya & Wallner 2023)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/modality-epistemology/
- **Type**: Encyclopedia (first published 2007; substantive revision 7 July 2023; read in full)
- **Key points**:
  - It classifies accounts by mental capacity: conceivability (§4.1), counterfactual imagination (§4.2), intuition and understanding (§4.3), essence (§5), perception (§6), and induction and abduction (§7).
  - It sorts them as modal rationalism or modal empiricism (§2.3), and as possibility-first or necessity-first.
  - It frames the critical questions for conceivability. The Scope, Navigation, Dependence, Human Capacity and Relevant Depth questions are directly usable for the article.
  - The Dependence Question states the essentialist critique: conceivability exercises "need to be constrained by some crucial information about the (essence of) the entities involved in them, creating the needed epistemic friction for conceivability" **[verbatim]**. The SEP attributes this to Vaidya and Wallner (2021, §2).
  - Modest theories "aim to offer an account of ordinary modal knowledge, which involves what is possible and necessary for couches and cups, say; but they are either skeptical or silent about extraordinary or philosophical cases, such as whether the number 2 could have been the number 3 or whether there could have been philosophical zombies" **[verbatim]**.
  - On the Navigation Question, Chalmers holds that a priori passage from primary to secondary possibility works "in some special cases". "Such cases require that an expression's primary and secondary intensions coincide. Candidate examples include, most notably, the class of phenomenal truths as well as mathematical and analytic truths" **[verbatim]**.
  - On the Relevant Depth Question: "is it enough to imagine a human brain functioning normally while not having any experiences to justify the possibility of a philosophical zombie?" **[verbatim]**
- **Stance**: Neutral survey.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. It is the article's map of the field.
- **Caution**: Its gloss of Chalmers 2002, an ideal reasoner "free of all cognitive limitations" (2002: 148), **does not match Chalmers's own text**. See [Chalmers 2002](#chalmers-2002) below.

### Williamson, *The Philosophy of Philosophy* (2007), ch. 5 {#williamson}
- **DOI**: 10.1002/9780470696675 (Crossref: Wiley, 2007; metadata only, book not read)
- **Type**: Book
- **Key points** (all via SEP §4.2 and Roca-Royes 2011b):
  - The ordinary capacity to handle counterfactuals "carries with it the cognitive capacity to handle metaphysical modality" (2007: 136) **[verbatim, secondary]**.
  - "the epistemology of metaphysically modal thinking is tantamount to a special case of the epistemology of counterfactual thinking" (2007: 158) **[verbatim, secondary]**.
  - Possibility and necessity are given by counterfactual equivalences: □A iff (¬A □→ ⊥); ◇A iff ¬(A □→ ⊥). We test ◇A by developing the supposition A in imagination "as 'realistically' as we can" and checking whether a contradiction follows (SEP's paraphrase of 2007: 142).
  - Constitutive facts are held fixed: "part of the general way we develop counterfactual suppositions is to hold such constitutive facts fixed" (2007: 164, quoted in Roca-Royes 2011b and paraphrased in the SEP) **[verbatim, secondary]**.
  - The resulting knowledge is "armchair". It is neither strictly a priori nor a posteriori, and Williamson holds that the distinction does not "cut at the epistemic joints" (SEP, quoting 2013: 8).
- **Critiques** (SEP §4.2):
  - counterpossibles (Berto et al. 2018);
  - the epistemic fallacy (Jenkins 2008; Casullo 2012b);
  - circularity, because constitutive facts are modal (Boghossian 2011; Tahko 2012; Roca-Royes 2011b);
  - normative constraints: which facts to hold fixed (Roca-Royes 2011b, 2012; Mallozzi 2021e);
  - scope: Lowe 2012 holds that counterfactual knowledge is causal-nomological and may not reach metaphysical modality.
- **Author stance on mind**: **Not established.** No source located states Williamson's view on dualism or applies his account to zombies (see [Absence claims](#absence), item 1). One web summary of a podcast calls him anti-reductionist about physicalism in a strong sense. That is second-hand and unverified, and **must not be cited**.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral in itself. Its bearing on the zombie premise is set out in [Major Positions](#positions).

### Williamson, "Knowing by Imagining" (2016)
- **DOI**: 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198716808.003.0005 (in Kind & Kung, eds., *Knowledge Through Imagination*, OUP, pp. 113–123)
- **Key points** (chapter abstract):
  - Imagination evolved for practical problem-solving, "can I leap across this river, or do I need to take the long walk around?" **[verbatim]**.

### Roca-Royes, "Modal Knowledge and Counterfactual Knowledge" (2011b)
- **Source**: *Logique & Analyse* 216: 537–552; open PDF, read in full.
- **Key points**:
  - Williamson's account (W) "cannot satisfactorily elucidate modal knowledge" (abstract) **[verbatim]**.
  - The core point, which is what the zombie application turns on: "we need to know … that facts about atomic numbers are constitutive matters. This must generalize to any constitutive fact, or we will get extensionally wrong results by over-generating possibilities" (p. 549) **[verbatim]**.
  - It remains "not obvious what are the cognitive mechanisms … by means of which we are able to, more or less reliably, tell apart constitutive and non-constitutive facts" (p. 549) **[verbatim]**.
  - She grants that Williamson escapes outright circularity by appeal to Fine. Her objection is that constitutive knowledge goes unexplained.
- **Stance**: A modal empiricist critic of both conceivability and counterfactual accounts. No position on mind was found.

### Roca-Royes, "Conceivability and De Re Modal Knowledge" (2011a) and "Similarity and Possibility" (2017)
- **DOIs**:
  - 2011a: 10.1111/j.1468-0068.2010.00757.x (*Noûs* 45(1): 22–49)
  - 2017: 10.1007/978-3-319-44309-6_12 (in Fischer & Leon, eds., *Modal Epistemology After Rationalism*, Synthese Library 378, pp. 221–245)
- **Key points**:
  - 2011a abstract: it "presents a dilemma for both epistemic and non‐epistemic versions of conceivability‐based accounts of modal knowledge … In neither case, therefore, do conceivability accounts elucidate de re modal knowledge" **[verbatim]**. Whitespace is normalised; the OpenAlex text runs "lifede remodal" together.
  - 2017 (via SEP §7; not read): de re possibility for concrete entities is known from what actually happened to relevantly similar "epistemic counterparts", plus inductively supported "nomic-like" knowledge. Her example: Twin-Messy broke, so Messy can break.

### Lowe, "What is the Source of Our Knowledge of Modal Truths?" (2012)
- **DOI**: 10.1093/mind/fzs122 (*Mind* 121(484): 919–950; abstract read)
- **Key points**:
  - The conceivability approach "is open to certain familiar and seemingly powerful objections" (abstract) **[verbatim]**.
  - Williamson's account "crucially involves a thesis to the effect that modal truths can be explained in terms of counterfactual truths. In this paper, I query Williamson's account on a number of points, including this thesis" **[verbatim]**.
  - His positive proposal "appeals instead to our capacity to grasp essences, understood in a neo-Aristotelian fashion, according to which essences are expressed by 'real definitions'" **[verbatim]**.
  - "Knowing an entity's essence is simply knowing what that entity is. And at least in the case of some entities, we must be able to know what they are, because otherwise it would be hard to see how we could know anything at all about them" (2012: 944) **[verbatim, secondary]** (SEP §5).
- **Author stance**: Lowe defended **non-Cartesian substance dualism** (Lowe 2006, *Erkenntnis* 65(1): 5–23; *Personal Agency* 2008, already cited on [agent-causation-and-acting-for-reasons](/topics/agent-causation-and-acting-for-reasons/)). **No source located connects his 2012 modal epistemology to a zombie-style or conceivability argument for dualism.** His dualism should not be presented as an output of this epistemology, and his epistemology should not be presented as dualist-friendly.

### Hale, *Necessary Beings* (2013)
- **DOI**: 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199669578.001.0001 (abstract read)
- **Key points**:
  - Facts about absolute necessity and possibility "have their source or basis, not in meanings or concepts nor in facts about alternative 'worlds', but in the natures or essences of things" (abstract) **[verbatim]**.
  - On the SEP's account (§5), some knowledge of essence is a priori, through knowledge of meaning. "knowing what [the word] 'and' means is sufficient for knowledge of the essence of truth-functional conjunction" (2013: 258) **[verbatim, secondary]**.
  - Other knowledge of essence is a posteriori, by a Kripke-style deduction.
- **Stance**: Essentialist. No position on mind was found.

### Kment, "Essence and Modal Knowledge" (2021) and *Modality and Explanatory Reasoning* (2014)
- **DOIs**:
  - 2021: 10.1007/s11229-018-01903-1 (*Synthese* 198(S8): 1957–1979; author's final version read in full)
  - 2014: 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199604685.001.0001
- **Key points**:
  - Knowledge of the "metaphysical laws", essential truths among them, comes by inference to the best explanation and from conceptual competence.
  - Conceiving yields modal knowledge when the appearance of possibility rests on negative knowledge of essences. "The knowledge that is required is of a negative kind and very minimal" **[verbatim]**.
  - His worked example: "I know that there is nothing in the essences of rain and Bundt-cake-hood that precludes the scenario of a Bundt cake's being made while it is raining" **[verbatim]**.
  - He admits only "the roughest idea of what it is to be a Bundt cake" **[verbatim]**. Partial grasp of an essence can therefore suffice in easy cases.
- **Stance**: Essentialist. The paper does not discuss zombies (term absent from the raw text).
- **Lead correction**: The task's "Kment 2017 (SEP-adjacent)" matches no Kment work located. His SEP entry "Varieties of Modality" dates from 2012 (revised 2021) and is about the metaphysics of modality, not its epistemology. Use Kment 2014 or 2021.

### Hill, "Modality, Modal Epistemology, and the Metaphysics of Consciousness" (2006)
- **DOI**: 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199275731.003.0011 (in Nichols, ed., *The Architecture of the Imagination*, OUP, pp. 205–236; chapter abstract read). **Not in the task's lead list. It is the closest rival aimed at the Map's argument.**
- **Key points** (abstract):
  - "Cartesian modal arguments for property dualism presuppose that facts about the essential natures of pain and other qualitative properties can be grasped a priori by merely conceiving of them or imagining them. This chapter argues that this presupposition fails" **[verbatim]**.
  - His theory reduces necessity to the subjunctive conditional: "to say that it is metaphysically necessary that p … is equivalent to saying that p would be the case no matter what else was the case" **[verbatim]**.
  - The account "allows that such claims can, in many cases, be known a priori, but it implies that in many other cases, our grasp of them is a posteriori" **[verbatim]**.
- **Author stance**: Type-B materialist. Chalmers 2002 names "Loar 1997, 1999, and Hill 1998" among the "type-B materialists" who "have bitten this bullet" on strong necessities **[verbatim]**. The Map's [kripke-a-posteriori-necessity-argument](/concepts/kripke-a-posteriori-necessity-argument/) L55 lists Hill on the Type-B line.
- **Earlier paper**: Hill 1997, *Philosophical Studies* 87(1): 61–85, DOI 10.1023/A:1017911200883. Papineau 2007 reports it as attributing to Kripke the view that conceivable distinctness implies real distinctness, unless the commonsense kind comes with "a property that normally guides us in recognizing instances of X, but that is only contingently connected with X" (p. 63, quoted by Papineau) **[verbatim, secondary]**.

### Goff, "Essentialist Modal Rationalism" (2021)
- **DOI**: 10.1007/s11229-019-02109-9 (*Synthese* 198(S8): 2019–2027; abstract read)
- **Key points**: "I will propose a nuanced form of modal rationalism consistent with the Kripke/Putnam cases. The rough idea is that rational coherence entails possibility when you grasp the essential nature of what you're conceiving of" **[verbatim]**.
- **Author stance**: Russellian panpsychist ([revelation-thesis](/concepts/revelation-thesis/) L64). The Map treats his programme as a rival.
- **Bearing**: This is the only rival route that rescues the conceivability step. It does so under a condition, grasp of essential nature, that the Map's denial of strong Revelation withholds ([The cost](#cost), item 5).

## Major Positions {#positions}

Each position below gives three things: the account itself, as sourced; the author's stance, as sourced; and **what it does to the zombie premise**. The last is flagged **(note's inference)** wherever no source makes the application.

### 1. Modal rationalism (Chalmers; Yablo's weaker evidential form)
- **Proponents**:
  - Chalmers 2002, 2010, 2014.
  - Yablo 1993: conceivability gives prima facie, defeasible evidence. "[T]o imagine an X is thereby to enjoy the appearance that an X could exist" (1993: 30) **[verbatim, secondary]**.
  - Goff 2021, in an essentialist form.
- **Core claim**: Ideal positive primary conceivability entails primary possibility. With modal monism, primary possibilities are metaphysical possibilities.
- **Zombie premise**: Delivered, given that zombies are ideally positively conceivable and that phenomenal truths' intensions coincide (the Navigation Question).
- **Relation to the Map**: This is the position the Map's conceivability pages need. It is contested by every account below.

### 2. Counterfactual accounts (Williamson; Hill)
- **Core claim**: Knowledge of possibility and necessity is knowledge of counterfactuals of a special form, acquired by developing suppositions "realistically" while holding constitutive facts fixed. Hill's version: necessity is truth under every subjunctive supposition.
- **Zombie premise (note's inference for Williamson; Hill's own conclusion)**:
  - Under (POS), zombies are possible iff developing the supposition *a physical duplicate of us without consciousness* yields no contradiction.
  - A contradiction follows iff the psychophysical link is among the constitutive facts held fixed. If physicalism is true it is (the identity is constitutive), and the development yields ⊥. If dualism is true it is not.
  - So the method returns whatever verdict one's prior view of the constitutive facts feeds it.
  - Roca-Royes's point carries over: we would need to know which facts are constitutive, "or we will get extensionally wrong results by over-generating possibilities". The zombie case is the one where constitutive status is the thing in dispute.
  - Hill reaches the anti-Cartesian conclusion directly. Dualist modal arguments presuppose a priori grasp of qualitative essences that we lack.
- **Result**: The zombie premise is neutralised as independent evidence. Physicalism is not delivered either.

### 3. Essentialist accounts (Lowe, Hale, Kment, Mallozzi; Goff's variant)
- **Core claim**: Necessity is grounded in essence ("real definition"), and modal knowledge comes from knowledge of essence. Conceiving is legitimate only under "epistemic friction" from what we know of essences (Kment; Vaidya & Wallner).
- **Zombie premise (note's inference, built on Kment's pattern and on Hill and Goff)**:
  - The step needs **negative** essential knowledge: nothing in the essences of the phenomenal properties, the physical properties or the metaphysical laws precludes a physical duplicate without consciousness.
  - Kment shows such negative knowledge can come with only partial grasp in uncontroversial cases (rain and Bundt cakes). In the zombie case, the negative claim that phenomenal essence includes nothing physical is exactly what Type-B denies.
  - Goff closes the gap by requiring grasp of "the essential nature of what you're conceiving of".
  - Hill denies that we have that grasp for qualitative properties.
- **Result**: The zombie premise stands or falls with a claim about how much of phenomenal essence we grasp. [The cost](#cost), item 5, shows why that collides with the Map's Revelation stance.

### 4. Modal empiricism by similarity and induction (Roca-Royes)
- **Core claim**: De re possibilities of concrete things are known from actual histories of relevantly similar epistemic counterparts, plus inductive "nomic-like" knowledge.
- **Zombie premise**: No route. The SEP files such accounts as "skeptical or silent" about zombies, and Sjölin Wirling says empiricist accounts "cannot account for" justification of philosophically interesting modal claims.
- **Result**: Judgement on zombie possibility is withheld. The claim is not refuted; we lack a warrant for it.

### 5. Dispositional and potentiality accounts (Vetter; Allen's application)
- **Core claim**: Possibility is iterated potentiality of actual things. Modal knowledge comes from knowledge of abilities, dispositions and affordances.
- **Zombie premise**: A zombie world is possible iff something actual has an iterated potentiality for it. That is not knowable by conceiving, and on a powers ontology the physical powers may themselves fix consciousness. Allen (2022), on her own account, concludes that powers "block" conceivability arguments unless an implausible emergence holds. Vetter is not known to have addressed zombies.
- **Result**: The step is blocked or relocated to the metaphysics of powers. This touches Map pages that entertain powers metaphysics ([causal-powers](/concepts/causal-powers/), [consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions](/topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions/)).

### 6. Theory-based accounts (Fischer; Biggs & Wilson)
- **Core claim**: Belief in an interesting modal claim is justified only by a justified theory that implies it. Conceiving is "downstream".
- **Zombie premise (note's inference)**: Zombie possibility would be justified only by a justified theory that implies it. The zombie argument then cannot serve as an independent premise for that theory without circularity. This is the structure of the SEP's Priority Question, which uses Berkeley's argument for idealism as its example.
- **Result**: The step is demoted from premise to consequence.

### 7. Papineau's separation of epistemic and metaphysical modality
- **Core claim**: Metaphysical modality has nothing to do with conceivability. Strong necessities are ubiquitous. The physicalist identity is supported by "conclusive evidence" for generalised physicalism from elsewhere.
- **Zombie premise**: Zombies appear possible, even to physicalists, and that appearance is an "intuition of dualism", not evidence of possibility.
- **Result**: Type-B gets a principled epistemology. Its strong necessities are a consequence of that epistemology, not an ad hoc posit.

### 8. Modal scepticism (van Inwagen 1998)
- Already on the Map ([counterfactual-void](/voids/counterfactual-void/) L55). We should withhold judgement on modal claims outside the reliable range. The zombie claim is outside it. Pointer only.

## Key Debates

### Is conceiving the source of modal knowledge, or derivative from another source?
- **Sides**:
  - Rationalists (Chalmers): conceiving, constrained by conceptual structure, is the source.
  - Essentialists (Lowe, Hale, Kment), counterfactual theorists (Williamson) and theory-based empiricists (Fischer): conceiving is derivative, constrained by or downstream from knowledge of essence, constitutive facts or theory.
- **Core disagreement**: The SEP's Dependence Question.
- **Current state**: Open. The SEP presents essentialism's rise as driven partly by "the criticism or incompleteness" of conceivability, counterfactual and understanding-based accounts.

### Does a non-conceivability modal epistemology leave room for any a priori modal knowledge?
- **Answer from the sources**: Yes, on several accounts.
  - Hill 2006 allows a priori knowledge "in many cases".
  - Hale takes some essence knowledge to be a priori through meaning.
  - Lowe grounds modal knowledge in grasp of essence.
  - Williamson's knowledge is "armchair".
- **This contradicts** the Map's L144 claim that rejecting the rationalist route "abandons a priori modal reasoning altogether".

### Is the type-B strong necessity ad hoc?
- **Sides**:
  - Chalmers: there are "no clear examples of strong necessities, and the only candidates are highly tendentious" (2002) **[verbatim]**. He also asks how conceivability could guide us if modality floats free of it: why not "just one metaphysically possible world, or 37?" (2010, §10, as quoted on [primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities](/concepts/primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities/) L87; not re-verified here).
  - Papineau: they are ubiquitous.
  - Hale's essentialism locates the source of necessity in essences, not concepts.
- **Current state**: Open. The Map's [primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities](/concepts/primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities/) already rates the access question "a priori … so it bears on plausibility rather than tier" (L97). That rating should carry over.

### Do powers block the zombie argument?
- **Sides**: Allen 2022 says yes for physicalist powers theorists. The Map's dispositions page notes the zombie-world disanalogy weakens dispositional essentialism's grounding claim (L126).
- **Current state**: Live. Not yet engaged on the Map.

## The Cost for the Map {#cost}

This is the point of the exercise, stated plainly.

1. **The conceivability premise rests on one contested modal epistemology.**
   - Several Map pages present the step as defensible on general grounds:
     - "gives strong reason to trust that phenomenal conceivability tracks genuine possibility" ([modal-structure-of-phenomenal-properties](/topics/modal-structure-of-phenomenal-properties/) L87);
     - "Zombies pass this test" (L85);
     - "Conceivability is evidence of possibility. When we can coherently imagine a scenario without contradiction, that scenario could obtain in some possible world" ([philosophical-zombies](/concepts/philosophical-zombies/) L57).
   - The step actually needs Chalmers-style modal rationalism. On the counterfactual, essentialist, empiricist, dispositional and theory-based routes surveyed here, conceiving zombies does not by itself warrant their possibility.
   - The article must say that the Map's modal arguments inherit the standing of modal rationalism. That standing is contested.
2. **The trilemma's third horn is overstated.**
   - [conceivability-possibility-inference](/concepts/conceivability-possibility-inference/) L142–144 says that rejecting modal rationalism means "abandoning reason as a guide to possibility" and "a radical skepticism that undermines much of philosophy and mathematics".
   - Hill, Hale, Lowe, Kment and Williamson each reject conceiving as the basic route while keeping a priori or armchair modal knowledge. Hill does so for exactly the mind–body case.
   - The physicalist's exit is cheaper than the page says.
3. **Strong necessities are not ad hoc on rival epistemologies.**
   - The Map prices Type-B's strong necessities as a posit Type-B "must" make ([conceivability-possibility-inference](/concepts/conceivability-possibility-inference/) L144; [zombie-master-argument](/concepts/zombie-master-argument/) L102).
   - For Papineau they are ubiquitous. For essentialists, a necessity with no conceptual explanation is unremarkable, because necessity's source is essence, not concepts (Hale).
   - Chalmers's reply survives, but it is the a priori access challenge, which the Map already rates as bearing on plausibility, not tier.
4. **[P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1)'s shared premise is narrower than stated.**
   - [P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1) (arguments-for-dualism L47) says the modal arguments "share the inference from conceivability to possibility". That inference is itself an output of one modal epistemology.
   - If modal rationalism loses to its rivals, the modal cluster's contribution falls toward nothing. The explanatory gap and the unity prong remain.
   - This tightens [P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1)'s existing "fail together" concession. It does not reverse it.
5. **The essentialist routes collide with the Map's Revelation stance.**
   - On the two sources in this family that address the mind case, the step needs grasp of the qualitative property's essential nature: Hill denies we have it, and Goff makes it the condition.
   - Chalmers's own reply holds that "our most fundamental concepts are either transparent concepts or indexical concepts" (2014) **[verbatim]**.
   - The Map holds weak Revelation only: experience reveals "the existence of a quality and part of its character" ([revelation-thesis](/concepts/revelation-thesis/) L36). The Map also says its objection to opaque phenomenal concepts "must not drift into" Goff's transparency (L76).
   - Partial grasp might still supply the needed negative knowledge (Kment's pattern). **That is an open argument the Map has not made**, and the article must not assume it.
   - At least one page currently asserts the strong form outright ([Out-of-scope](#out-of-scope), item 1).
6. **Tenet 5 does not touch these replies.**
   - [tenets](/tenets/) L172 says "the standard physicalist reply to conceivability arguments is a simplicity claim and Tenet 5 blocks it". None of the rival epistemologies here is a simplicity claim. They concern **access**, as the optimistic review that spawned this task notes.
   - Fischer's TEM uses simplicity in theory choice. Fischer himself denies that simplicity is evidence of truth, and Tenet 5 binds only the Map's own reasoning.

### What survives
- **The explanatory gap.** The rivals concern possibility, not a priori entailment, and Type-B grants the epistemic gap (Chalmers 2014).
- **Chalmers's 2007 dilemma against the phenomenal-concepts strategy.** It assumes "nothing about the relationship between conceivability and possibility" ([type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism](/concepts/type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism/) L101, quoting Chalmers). No rival here reaches it.
- **Symmetry.** No surveyed epistemology delivers the identity either. On the counterfactual and essentialist routes, the zombie verdict comes out as whatever the constitutive or essential facts put in. Physicalists rest the identity on other evidence (Papineau's "conclusive evidence for this generalized claim").
- **Chalmers's access challenge.** His question of how conceivability could guide us to a modality that floats free of it remains the rationalist's reply, at the plausibility level ([primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities](/concepts/primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities/) L87, L97).

- **Tenet 5** constrains only the Map's own reasoning. It is never an argument against a rival. The rival epistemologies concern access, not economy. Do not repeat [tenets](/tenets/) L172's "Tenet 5 blocks it" against any of them.
- **Do not overclaim conceivability arguments.** The lede must say plainly that the Map's modal arguments depend on modal rationalism, and that on the rival routes the zombie premise loses its independent force. Name the dependent pages, or at least link [conceivability-possibility-inference](/concepts/conceivability-possibility-inference/), [zombie-master-argument](/concepts/zombie-master-argument/) and [philosophical-zombies](/concepts/philosophical-zombies/).
- **Do not frame any rival account as supporting dualism unless its author does.** Only Goff's essentialist modal rationalism rescues the step, and Goff is a Russellian panpsychist the Map argues against. Do not enlist him.

**Author stances (record them as given here):**
- **Williamson**: no published stance on dualism was located. Do **not** call him a physicalist, a dualist, or "no dualist". Describe his modal epistemology only. Do not extend his 2016 reliability claim to remote philosophical scenarios.
- **Hill**: a Type-B materialist. His 2006 chapter is aimed at "Cartesian modal arguments for property dualism". Say so.
- **Papineau**: a Type-B physicalist. "Intuition of dualism" is his diagnosis of an appearance, not a concession. Quote it only with that framing.
- **Lowe**: a non-Cartesian substance dualist, but **no link located** between his dualism and his 2012 epistemology. Do not present the epistemology as dualist-friendly, and do not present his dualism as conceivability-based.
- **Goff**: a Russellian panpsychist and a rival (as on [revelation-thesis](/concepts/revelation-thesis/) L64).
- **Frankish**: an illusionist. "dualists should not be zombists" is an opponent's moral.
- **Allen**: only the abstract was read. Report her claim about physicalist powers theorists, and do not decide whether the Map's dualism is her "implausible" emergence.
- **Roca-Royes, Vetter, Hale, Kment, Fischer**: no position on mind was located. Present their epistemologies and keep the zombie application labelled as the Map's.
- **Vetter is "ambitious"** on the SEP's classification. Do not file her with the modest accounts that are "skeptical or silent".

**Quote status:**
- **Quote only [verbatim] material.** **[verbatim, secondary]** quotations may be used, attributed as quoted in the SEP or in Roca-Royes 2011b.
- **Never quote the SEP's "free of all cognitive limitations"** as Chalmers's words. Chalmers's own definition is undefeatability "by better reasoning".
- **The SEP's "mind-body dualism" example under TEM is the SEP's illustration.** Do not attribute it to Fischer.
- **Balog 1999's abstract is not the journal's.** Paraphrase only.

**Inference labelling:**
- **Label the note's applications as the Map's own analysis**, for example "applied to the zombie case, the method…". This covers the Williamson application, the Fischer circularity and the essentialist negative-knowledge requirement. No source located makes these applications in these words.
- **The Revelation tension** ([The cost](#cost), item 5) must be stated as **an open question the Map owes an answer to**. It is not a refutation. Kment's partial-grasp pattern is the Map's best available reply, and it has not been made.

**What the article must not claim:**
- that any rival epistemology refutes zombie possibility, or establishes physicalism;
- that rejecting modal rationalism means abandoning a priori modal knowledge, which is the L144 error;
- that modal empiricism shows zombies impossible (it withholds judgement);
- that Williamson, Lowe or Vetter discuss zombies;
- that Tenet 5 answers these accounts;
- that zombies are known to be *ideally* conceivable. Ideal conceivability is justification "undefeatable by better reasoning". The Map can report sustained prima facie positive conceivability and treat ideal conceivability as a defeasible judgement. "Zombies pass this test" ([modal-structure-of-phenomenal-properties](/topics/modal-structure-of-phenomenal-properties/) L85) overstates.

**Wording**: Avoid "This is not X. It is Y." and do not use "load-bearing" as an intensifier.

## Out-of-Scope Observations {#out-of-scope}

1. **Strong Revelation is asserted in [modal-structure-of-phenomenal-properties](/topics/modal-structure-of-phenomenal-properties/) L60–62, against [revelation-thesis](/concepts/revelation-thesis/).**
   - L60 says "Pain has no hidden nature". L62 says "Phenomenal properties are fully present in experience — their nature is exhausted by how they appear", and "given to us in its entirety through acquaintance".
   - That is the **strong** Revelation that [revelation-thesis](/concepts/revelation-thesis/) L36 and L78 say the Map denies, for three independent reasons.
   - A grep for this phrasing found no other live page asserting it. [eliminative-materialism](/topics/eliminative-materialism/) L120 quotes Searle on existence-appearance only, which fits weak Revelation.
   - Suggested: a refine-draft task on modal-structure L60–62, length-neutral, re-scoping to weak Revelation. Kripke's point needs only that pain lacks a *contingent mode of presentation*, which Loar grants (kripke L55).
2. **The SEP misquotes Chalmers 2002.** Its "free of all cognitive limitations" (2002: 148) drops "contingent" and presents Menzies's ideal-reasoner strategy as Chalmers's definition. No Map page quotes the phrase (live grep: 0). Logged here so that no future page imports it.
3. **[tenets](/tenets/) L172's "the standard physicalist reply to conceivability arguments is a simplicity claim and Tenet 5 blocks it".**
   - The rival epistemologies show that the standard Type-B reply also has a modal-epistemological basis, untouched by Tenet 5.
   - Whether "blocks" uses Tenet 5 against a rival is a tenets-level question for a human. This note flags it and proposes no edit.
4. **Lead corrections for the harvest record**:
   - "Kment 2017 (SEP-adjacent)" matches no work located. Use Kment 2014 or 2021; the SEP "Varieties of Modality" entry dates from 2012, revised 2021.
   - The "Lowe 2012" title is confirmed.
   - "Roca-Royes 2017" is confirmed (online 2016).
   - Hill 2006 and Allen 2022, both outside the lead list, are the most relevant finds.

## Gaps in Research

- **Unread full texts**:
  - Williamson 2007, ch. 5: whether it mentions zombies or conceivability arguments in mind.
  - Hill 2006: the argument behind the abstract.
  - Allen 2022: which emergence is excluded.
  - Vetter 2015: alien possibilities and extraordinary claims.
  - Fischer 2017: whether TEM's "mind-independent" scope restriction covers claims about consciousness. Biggs's review reports that TEM targets claims whose truthmakers "aren't grounded in minds". **Whether zombie claims fall inside that scope is unresolved and could matter.**
- **Lowe's dualist writings** were not checked for any use of his modal epistemology.
- **Dohrn 2020** ("Counterfactuals versus conceivability as a guide to modal knowledge", *Philosophical Studies* 177(12): 3637–3659) argues, on a search summary only, that conceivability has an advantage over counterfactual accounts. It might give the Map a reply to item 2 of [The cost](#cost). Metadata verified; text not read.
- **Prelević 2013** (a Belgrade PhD thesis, in Serbian, with a long abstract) defends Chalmers's argument against parity objections and against strong-necessity candidates. A possible source for the rationalist reply. Not read beyond the abstract.
- **Kung 2010** (*PPR* 81(3): 620–663), on stipulation in imagining, underlies O'Conaill 2022. Not read.
- **The partial-grasp reply** to the Revelation tension, Kment's negative-knowledge pattern applied to phenomenal essence, was not searched for in the literature.
