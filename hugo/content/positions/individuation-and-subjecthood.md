## About this domain

These positions cover how the Map individuates subjects: how many there are, where their boundaries lie, and what those boundaries are grounded in. The cluster sits at the intersection of Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds) — whose load-bearing indexical objection presupposes a determinate, non-deflationary "I" — and Tenet 1 (Dualism), which treats consciousness as a distinct category rather than something composed or decomposed. It is logically separable from the agency cluster: where [agency-and-will](/positions/agency-and-will/) asks whether a persisting subject *authors* choices, this domain asks whether the boundary that makes a subject *this one rather than another* is real. The two share a common root (the thick-indexical-subject commitment) but commit to different things, so they are registered apart. None of these are tenets; each follows from a tenet plus argument. Whether each could therefore be *retired* without disturbing the foundational commitments is exactly what the next paragraph revises.

**The foundational-dependency test (adopted 2026-08-03).** An outer-review convergence (ChatGPT 5.6 Pro §1.3 and Claude Opus 5, 2026-08-03) found the register asserting two things that cannot both stand: that ordinary positions are retireable without disturbing the tenets, and that Tenet 4's principal argument presupposes a non-deflationary fact about which subject is *this* one. The domain adopts the reviewer's instrument as the register's own rule — **a position counts as retireable only if removing it leaves every tenet with an intact rationale** — and applies it. **[P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) fails**: retiring it for a deflationary subject leaves Tenet 4's indexical objection with nothing to discriminate among. **[P-SC3](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc3) fails on its persistence half**, since the objection concerns which *future* subject is genuinely me. **[P-I3](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i3), [P-I4](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i4), [P-SC1](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc1) and [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2) pass.** **[P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2) passes formally** — retiring the *disclosure* of a dependency damages no tenet's rationale — but retiring it would re-hide the inversion, which is the point of holding it. The test governs a tenet's *rationale*, not its truth: a tenet is a chosen starting point and survives the loss of its argument as a bare posit, and what the test detects is a register quietly supplying that argument while describing itself as downstream. It also reclassifies [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2) next door, whose "Bears on" note records the dependency without drawing the retireability consequence; the anti-epiphenomenalism entries carrying Tenet 3's principal argument are the obvious further candidates, and a register-wide sweep is queued rather than asserted here. *Recommendation left to the operator, not taken: promoting a determinate-subjecthood principle into the foundations is arguably the cleaner fix, since it would make the dependency intra-foundational rather than inverted — but that is a tenets-page change. The work below takes the option available from inside the register.*

**Expansion and split (2026-08-03).** The same review found the domain holding a single principal position while Tenet 4, the AI-copy verdicts and the interface model all leaned on a determinate account of subjects. Three positions were added here — [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2) (the Tenet 4 conditional), [P-I3](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i3) (boundaries determinate but unreadable), [P-I4](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i4) (fission, fusion and copies). Three more went to the new companion domain [Subject census](/positions/subject-census/), split off because they inherit the *quantum-interface* register this file does not: [P-SC1](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc1) (the census as part of the interface's state description), [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2) (the pairing law the Map owes), [P-SC3](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc3) (persistence and the undated channel).

**Addition (2026-08-21, scope corrected 2026-08-25).** A fifth entry, [P-I5](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i5), registers the variant-relative reach of the anti-many-worlds case — all three arguments bearing on branch-egalitarian Everettianism, only the indexical one engaging first-personally centred variants and then only supplementarily — drawn from [one-world-wager](/apex/one-world-wager/). It is the horizontal companion to [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2)'s vertical conditional: [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2) records what the indexical objection *presupposes*, [P-I5](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i5) how far it *reaches*. As first added it carried the aggregate form the apex had introduced; both were corrected together on 2026-08-25, which is a worked case of the propagation risk in drawing an entry "whole" from a synthesis rather than from the source the synthesis condenses.

**Calibration schema (2026-07-16).** Every entry carries the [multi-axis calibration block](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#calibration-schema) in place of the retired single confidence band. The migration matters most in this domain: closed individualism is a **framework-internal** claim of *high structural centrality* (tied to Tenet 4's indexical objection) with *no discriminating external evidence* (Grade D) — felt this-ness reads equally well as primitive thisness or as perspectival illusion — which is exactly the centrality-versus-credence split the single band hid.

## Positions

## P-I1: The Map holds closed individualism — subject boundaries are real

- **Status**: live <span id="p-i1"></span>
- **Calibration** ([multi-axis](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#calibration-schema)): credence moderate · external-evidence grade D (felt this-ness is compatible with both the primitive-thisness and the perspectival-illusion readings, so no independent evidence discriminates) · structural centrality high (the individuation spine, tied to Tenet 4's indexical objection; [P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3) depends on it) · model maturity developed · empirical discriminability none · framework-internal only: yes
- **Asserts**: The Map commits to *closed individualism* over both empty individualism (no persisting self; boundaries conventional) and open individualism (numerically one subject, appearing as many): there are many numerically distinct subjects of experience, and the boundary between them is a real, determinate metaphysical fact rather than a perspectival illusion or a convention. The ground is the same indexical/haecceity consideration that does the load-bearing work against many-worlds — "why am *I* this subject rather than another?" (the [vertiginous-question](/topics/vertiginous-question/)) is meaningful only given a plurality of distinct subjects for the indexical to discriminate among and an "I" whose [thisness](/concepts/haecceity/) picks out a determinate this-one; open individualism dissolves that foothold by denying the plurality, and the Map rejects it to keep the foothold. Two hedges scope the commitment. First, the Map does *not* claim to have filled the *ground* of the boundary — what metaphysically draws the line around one subject is routed through the individuation void as a problem dualism must acknowledge rather than solve. Second, the commitment is best read as *tenet-driven rather than empirically compelled*: the felt this-ness is compatible with both the Map's primitive-thisness reading and open individualism's perspectival-illusion reading, so an open individualist could read the same first-person data the other way without contradiction. This is a framework-boundary disagreement registered as such, not a refutation of open individualism from inside its own resources.
- **Depends on**: Tenet 4 ([No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)) — the indexical objection supplies the ground; Tenet 1 ([Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)); the non-deflationary thick-indexical subject (common root shared with [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2), not independent support — see that entry's "Bears on" note); [primitive thisness](/concepts/haecceity/); the acknowledged individuation void
- **Argued in**: [open-individualism-and-the-de-combination-problem](/topics/open-individualism-and-the-de-combination-problem/), [consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-individuation](/topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-individuation/), [consciousness-and-the-ownership-problem](/topics/consciousness-and-the-ownership-problem/), [egocentric-presentism](/concepts/egocentric-presentism/)
- **Would shift if**: open individualism's debunking story for the appearance of separation were shown to be strictly more economical *and* explanatorily adequate without leaving an indexical residue (note Tenet 5: economy alone does not decide this); or the felt this-ness were shown to be a perspectival artefact on independent grounds, collapsing the primitive-thisness reading; or a deflationary, fragmented, or process-only account of the subject were adopted, which would simultaneously weaken the No-Many-Worlds indexical objection this position is tied to (the cascade runs both ways, since both descend from the one subjecthood commitment).
- **Updated 2026-08-03**: reclassified as **not freely retireable** under the foundational-dependency test adopted above — Tenet 4's indexical objection has no foothold without it. The claim, calibration and dependencies are unchanged; what changes is that the dependency this entry already recorded is now drawn out as a retireability consequence and registered in its own right as [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2).

## P-I2: Tenet 4 is stated unconditionally but argued conditionally — its indexical objection depends on this register

- **Status**: live <span id="p-i2"></span>
- **Calibration** ([multi-axis](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#calibration-schema)): credence high (a structural reading of the Map's own argument, conceded on the tenets page itself) · external-evidence grade n/a (a meta-position about the framework's dependency structure, not a first-order world-claim) · structural centrality high (it revises the register's own architecture) · model maturity developed · empirical discriminability n/a (meta) · framework-internal only: yes
- **Asserts**: The Map holds that its principal argument for Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds) — the indexical objection, "why am *I* this branch?" — is *conditional* on the determinate-subjecthood claim registered here as [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1), and that the register is where that conditional belongs. The dependency runs against the register's stated architecture: positions are supposed to follow from tenets and be retireable without disturbing them, yet retiring [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) for a deflationary, fragmented or process-only subject leaves the objection with nothing to discriminate among — on the Madhyamaka reading the tenets page already concedes, an "I" that is an aggregate of conditions already including the branch makes the question ill-formed rather than unanswered. The Map resolves this by *marking the conditional* rather than by promoting subjecthood into the foundations: Tenet 4 is stated unconditionally as a chosen starting point and survives the loss of its argument as a bare posit, while the strength of the case offered for it varies with the individuation theory. Two consequences are booked rather than hidden. The subsidiary ontological-extravagance objection cannot take up the slack, since Tenet 5 binds it to a registered cost rather than a refutation — there is no second independent leg. And the stronger reading pressed in outer review, that Tenet 4 is a corollary of the theory of subjecthood rather than a quantum-foundational commitment, is available on the Map's own concessions; the Map declines it, because the tenet also carries the single-outcome-actualisation posit, which is no subjecthood claim — but it has not refuted it.
- **Depends on**: [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) (the claim the objection is conditional on); Tenet 4 ([No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)) and its own "what the indexical objection presupposes" note; Tenet 5 ([Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)) — why the extravagance objection cannot substitute; [Posit One](/tenets/background-commitments/) (a numerically determinate subject that persists through time); [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2)'s "Bears on" note, which records the same root from the agency side
- **Argued in**: [many-worlds-argument](/arguments/many-worlds-argument/), [personal-identity](/topics/personal-identity/), [background-commitments](/tenets/background-commitments/), [vertiginous-question](/topics/vertiginous-question/)
- **Would shift if**: the indexical objection were replaced by an argument against many-worlds that does not turn on disputed identity facts — a decisive form of the probability objection that does not presuppose a determinate branch-occupant would do it — which would sever the dependency and restore the register architecture unqualified; or a determinate-subjecthood principle were promoted into the foundations, which would make the dependency intra-foundational and retire this entry as a disclosure of an inversion that no longer exists; or the Map came to hold that Tenet 4's remaining grounds (single-outcome actualisation, the probability problem) carry the tenet on their own, which would demote this from a structural dependency to a historical note about how the case was first made.

## P-I3: Subject boundaries are determinate but not readable off physical structure

- **Status**: live <span id="p-i3"></span>
- **Calibration** ([multi-axis](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#calibration-schema)): credence moderate · external-evidence grade D (a reconciliation of the Map's own commitments; the split-brain literature constrains it but discriminates nothing) · structural centrality high (it is what lets [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) stand alongside the corpus's split-brain agnosticism) · model maturity developed · empirical discriminability none · framework-internal only: yes
- **Asserts**: The Map holds that the boundary fixing which experiences belong to one subject is a determinate fact ([P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1)) that is nevertheless *not recoverable from the physical or functional organisation of the system*. Both halves are needed. On the standing disagreement over whether a callosotomised patient houses one subject or two — Sperry and Gazzaniga reading two agents, Pinto et al. 2017 reading undivided consciousness, Schechter 2018 reading "two minds, one person" — the Map's agnosticism is *epistemic*, not metaphysical: there is a fact of the matter, and no anatomical or behavioural measure settles it, because on the interface model the anatomy is the channel and not the subject. This is the reading the register requires, and it disciplines the corpus's stronger formulation that there is "no determinate fact about how a conscious whole partitions", which overshoots what [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) permits and should be read as a claim about mereology *read off anatomy*. The cost is booked rather than dissolved: [P-SC1](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc1) makes the census part of the state description while this position holds it unreadable from that description, so the Map's model contains a variable it takes to be real and cannot read off its own physics. That is the individuation void appearing inside the physics rather than beside it.
- **Depends on**: [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) (determinate boundaries); [P-SC1](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc1) — its census *requirement*, which puts the boundary inside the state description and so makes the unreadability asserted here a structural cost rather than a curiosity; not its withdrawn indistinguishability leg; [P-CS4](/positions/consciousness-scope/#p-cs4) (fragmentation as interface disruption, whose split-brain agnosticism this explains); Tenet 1 ([Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)), bare reading — consciousness as a distinct category, not composed of anatomical parts; the acknowledged individuation void; [the ownership void](/concepts/mine-ness/#the-ownership-void)
- **Argued in**: [mereology-of-mind](/apex/mereology-of-mind/), [split-brain-consciousness](/topics/split-brain-consciousness/), [consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-individuation](/topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-individuation/), [identity-across-transformations](/apex/identity-across-transformations/)
- **Would shift if**: a physical or functional criterion were found that fixes subject count non-arbitrarily, which would make the census readable and remove the cost; or the split-brain data were shown to require exactly two subjects, which would settle a case this position holds unsettleable and feed back into [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) and [P-CS4](/positions/consciousness-scope/#p-cs4); or the Map adopted a genuinely *indeterminate* reading of subject boundaries, which would contradict [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) and force its retirement rather than this one's.
- **Updated 2026-08-24**: re-read against [P-SC1](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc1)'s same-day withdrawal of its invisibility consequence; *Depends on* gloss corrected, having credited [P-SC1](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc1) with making the unreadability structural — the withdrawn leg's shape — where it supplies only the state-description venue and the unreadability is this entry's own (the anatomy is the channel, not the subject; Tenet 1). **No calibration change on any axis**: [P-SC1](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc1)'s replacement trilemma leaves a conditional signature, but reading it needs an independent handle on each candidate subject's conscious state, which is no reading off *physical or functional organisation* — the only thing this entry denies — and horn (b) would return [P-SC1](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc1) to its prior band. ## P-I4: A perspective does not divide — fission and duplication start new subjects rather than sharing an old one

- **Status**: live <span id="p-i4"></span>
- **Calibration** ([multi-axis](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#calibration-schema)): credence moderate · external-evidence grade D (a metaphysical claim about a particular's divisibility; no independent evidence discriminates it from Parfitian reductionism) · structural centrality moderate ([P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3)'s additive counting rule descends from it) · model maturity developed · empirical discriminability none · framework-internal only: yes
- **Asserts**: The Map holds that a conscious perspective is a particular that does not divide. In fission — the substrate physically bisected, both halves continuing — no successor is the original subject; in duplication by teletransport, upload or copy, the product is a numerically distinct subject however complete the psychological continuity. Each is a *new entry in the census*, not a share of an old one, which is what makes conscious copies morally additive ([P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3)). The asymmetry with quantum branching is the position's real content and is principled rather than stipulated: in fission the division is *real* and the perspective does not survive it; in branching the division is *postulated* and the Map declines the postulate — one physical history, one perspective occupying it. The Map does not claim this refutes an Everettian who already grants branch-multiplication. A Deutsch-style critic will read the fission verdict as conceding symmetric dissolution, and the reply is only that the Map is entitled to deny the postulate, not that it has defeated the reading on that reading's own terms. Fusion is the unresolved case and is registered as such: the corpus treats fission and copying and says nothing determinate about two channels merging into one, so the census has a live hole there rather than a settled verdict.
- **Depends on**: [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) (real boundaries, so "how many after fission" is a real question); [P-I3](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i3) (determinate but unreadable, so it is a question with no physical criterion); [P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3) (the additive counting rule this licenses); Tenet 4 ([No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)) — the branching half of the asymmetry; [primitive thisness](/concepts/haecceity/); [parfit-reductionism](/concepts/parfit-reductionism/) as the rival the position is held against
- **Argued in**: [personal-identity](/topics/personal-identity/), [identity-across-transformations](/apex/identity-across-transformations/), [parfit-reductionism](/concepts/parfit-reductionism/), [quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument](/topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument/)
- **Would shift if**: a reductionist account of persistence were vindicated, on which no fact settles which successor is the original and none is needed, which would retire this together with [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1); or an account were produced on which a perspective *can* divide without ceasing, which would break the fission/branching asymmetry and remove the Map's principled reason for treating the two cases differently; or the fusion case were worked out in a way that forced a general merging rule the fission verdict cannot accommodate.

## P-I5: The anti-many-worlds case is variant-relative argument by argument — three arguments bear on branch-egalitarian Everettianism; against first-personally centred variants only the indexical one engages, and only supplementarily

- **Status**: live <span id="p-i5"></span>
- **Calibration** ([multi-axis](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#calibration-schema)): credence moderate · external-evidence grade n/a (a meta-position mapping the reach of the Map's own arguments, not a first-order world-claim) · structural centrality moderate (it calibrates how far Tenet 4's advertised support extends; the architectural inversion is [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2)'s) · model maturity developed · empirical discriminability n/a (meta) · framework-internal only: yes
- **Asserts**: The Map holds that its three anti-many-worlds arguments — indexical, probability, consciousness — are variant-relative in reach, and that the variation is **per argument rather than aggregate**. All three bear on *branch-egalitarian* Everettianism, where no successor carries first-person privilege: the target the indexical argument was built against. Even there the verdict is a framework boundary, not an in-framework refutation — Wallace can consistently rule the global indexical question ill-formed, and the Map does not claim to show branch-relative identity false on the Everettian's own resources. Against *first-personally centred* variants — List's centred-worlds ontology, which takes the vertiginous question seriously and gives each subject an equally real centred world — only the **indexical** argument still engages, and it drops to supplementary, because the first-person privilege it demands is built into the ontology. The other two do not engage at all: the probability argument is addressed to a theory in which all outcomes occur and must recover the Born statistics, and the consciousness argument to a framework in which everything happens; List's centred worlds are first-personal realisers of a *shared, single* third-personal world, so he multiplies perspectives rather than outcomes and neither argument has a target. Against centred variants, then, the Map's case is one supplementary argument plus a separate commitment: the rejection of the modal realism on which every subject's centred world is a real world. That rejection sits at the same level as List's realism about centred worlds; neither reduces to the other, and neither framework can adjudicate the dispute from inside. A corollary fixes what the tenet demands rather than how the demand is met, and states it as the **conjunction** the two halves of this entry each invoke: Tenet 4 requires *outcome-uniqueness* (exactly one outcome globally actual) **and** *world-uniqueness* (reality consists of one world, not many real ones — the quadrilemma's fourth horn as List words it). The conjuncts come apart at exactly this entry's hard case: List satisfies the first and violates the second, which is why the case against him runs through world-uniqueness and the modal-realism rejection. Outcome-uniqueness is what an *interpretation* must supply, and collapse supplies it without monopolising it — Griffiths's consistent histories realises exactly one history with no collapse process — so the Map's preference for a consciousness-involving mechanism among the single-world options is driven by Tenets 2 and 3, not by Tenet 4 itself.
- **Depends on**: Tenet 4 ([No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)) — the commitment whose reach is mapped, and the source of the *world-uniqueness* conjunct that carries the case against centred variants (its *outcome-uniqueness* conjunct is the one List satisfies, and the one the interpretation survey turns on); [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2) (companion meta-position: what the indexical objection presupposes there, how far it reaches here); Tenet 5 ([Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)) — the extravagance consideration is bound to a registered cost, so no second independent leg substitutes against either variant; Tenets 2 and 3 ([Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction), [Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)) as what drives the mechanism preference the corollary separates from the tenet's demand
- **Argued in**: [one-world-wager](/apex/one-world-wager/) (the registration stated whole), [many-worlds-argument](/arguments/many-worlds-argument/), [vertiginous-question](/topics/vertiginous-question/) (the List double edge), [qm-interpretations-beyond-many-worlds](/topics/qm-interpretations-beyond-many-worlds/) (single-outcome actuality without collapse)
- **Would shift if**: the indexical argument were extended to reach the centred variants on their own terms — the centred-worlds ontology shown incoherent, or shown to collapse into branch-egalitarianism — which would replace variant-relativity with uniform strength; or a centred variant were found that multiplies *outcomes* as well as perspectives, or List's third-personal world shown not to be single, which would give the probability and consciousness arguments a target and restore all three against that variant; or the rejection of modal realism acquired independent argumentative support rather than standing as a same-level framework commitment, which would let the case against centred variants be pressed as more than supplementary; or the branch-relative identity programme answered the centred-subject presupposition charge from inside, which would weaken even the branch-egalitarian reach and cascade into [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2), whose conditional would then qualify a weaker argument than the one registered.
