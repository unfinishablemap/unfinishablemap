## Executive Summary

The four pages ask one question of four kinds of report: a delusional denial of one's own existence, an insight-retained "as if" report of unreality, introspective acquaintance with phenomenal character, and a neural command-following response from a patient who cannot move. All four give the same answer. The report or marker shows something real about the architecture, and that something is equally well explained by the Map's interface reading and by its physicalist rival. The verdicts are mutually consistent; Revelation and covert consciousness turn out to be the first-person and third-person faces of the same refusal.

The consistency problems sit one step outside the wing. `mine-ness`, the hub both clinical pages cite, still says the Dualism tenet "finds direct support" in separability, while `depersonalisation` L87 says mine-ness holds the "compatible with, not evidence for" tier. "For-me-ness" is also doing two jobs: it names a felt feature that can lapse (Billon; depersonalisation) and a position that cannot (self-and-self-consciousness; Cotard). Both fixes fit inside current headroom. The wing implies two missing subjects: thought insertion, where the Map's kind-claim meets its strongest published counterexample, and Anton's syndrome, the false-positive experience report.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would recognise `revelation-thesis` as the page that takes his side's best weapon seriously enough to put it down. The argument from revelation ("if pain were a brain state and feeling pain revealed pain's essence, feeling pain would reveal a brain state", L37) is stated at full strength. The page then explains why the Map cannot use it: the argument needs the strong thesis, and the strong thesis contradicts three of the Map's own commitments. His own "distinction strategy" appears through Lin's dilemma ("either it fails to genuinely rescue panpsychism, or it can equally be used to rescue physicalism", L65), and none of it is caricatured. The Dualism tenet is left resting where `tenets.md` L55 says it rests, "on the explanatory gap and conceivability" (L79). In the clinical pages he would welcome how cleanly the explanatory gap survives a complete architecture story: `depersonalisation` L79 lists four mechanisms ("corticolimbic inhibition, interoceptive precision failure, impaired transparency, broken type-specific relations") and concludes "Irreducibility is reached by nothing on the table."

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp gets the wing's most disciplined silences. Cotard (L74), depersonalisation (L89) and covert consciousness (L100) each say Tenet 2 has "nothing" or "no bearing", and CMD gives a reason that is easy to miss: the data "associate CMD with preserved rather than widely damaged cortex, so they cannot locate or bound a quantum-level interaction." Where the interface does enter, it enters as a constraint, not a confirmation. `revelation-thesis` L77 shows that strong Revelation would make the selection power "no part of the state's essence", which is Goff's epiphenomenalism pressure turned on the Map's own mechanism. L79 then records that the interface power "is consistent with the Map's epistemology only because the Map holds that what experience reveals is partial, and that holding is a framework commitment, not an independent finding." Linking epistemology to the interface's structural invisibility ([P-Q9](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q9)) without counting either as evidence for the other is the honest version of Stapp's Process-1 programme.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The wing keeps the first person central without letting it decide what it cannot. Nagel's line is in `depersonalisation` L63: a patient asks "But in that case where am I? Who is watching the film?", and the page answers in one sentence: "The question is still being asked by someone." `covert-consciousness` handles the case where no first-person report is available. It refuses to infer absent experience from absent behaviour, and it equally refuses to fill the silence: "That ignorance cuts both ways: it forbids reading a negative scan as absence, and equally forbids asserting any prevalence of hidden awareness" (L62).

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would enjoy how often the wing describes the self as an activity rather than a substance. Cotard's existence report is "what a *constructed* status report looks like rather than a monitored fact" (L64). Knappik's alternative in `depersonalisation` L75 dissolves the global mode-feature into "type-specific self-relation[s]" that "break independently", a relational picture Whitehead would call prehensive. `revelation-thesis` names Mørch's phenomenal-powers view, the closest thing to process metaphysics in the debate, as "the Map's closest competitor on the metaphysics of dispositions" (L77) instead of absorbing it. **Constraint check:** none of this praise moves a tier. No page uses process-friendly coherence to upgrade anything; the wing makes no organism-level evidential-status claim at all. The one place in the cluster where coherence is cashed out as support sits in the hub, `mine-ness` L136 (see [Consistency](#consistency-verdict) below).

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane would take `covert-consciousness` L98 as the most honest statement in the corpus about what clinical data can do for agency: "CMD is strong evidence of cognition proceeding without motor output: a command understood and a task maintained. Whether the phenomenal character of that cognition does causal work is a further question the data do not reach." The deep review's change from "a task selected" to "a task maintained" (Klein withholds intentional agency) is the right kind of restraint. Kane prizes agency, and this page refuses to fake the verification `tenets.md` L93 says introspection cannot supply.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

McGinn gets cognitive closure about our own experience, stated as a commitment rather than a lament. `revelation-thesis` denies strong Revelation first on grounds of self-opacity: "Strong Revelation and self-opacity cannot both be held, and the Map has already chosen" (L77). The admissions of unread sources are specific rather than ritual. Cotard holds its verdict "provisional against the unread Gerrans chapter" (L72). Revelation reports Trogdon "by metadata, its text and abstract being unavailable at every index consulted" (L59). Covert consciousness ends its numbers with "the true figure is unknown" (L52).

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This is the wing's strongest axis. Birch would single out four declined upgrades:

- **Revelation gives up an argument it could have used.** "The Map's position ... is that it holds weak Revelation and denies strong Revelation — and that this choice costs it an argument" (L37). Weak Revelation "is compatible with physicalism and therefore compatible with everything, and it discriminates nothing" (L79). This is the cleanest "tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating" move in the wing, applied to the Map's own epistemology.
- **Depersonalisation names the begged premise.** The route from separable to non-physical "runs through a bridging premise ... That premise is the dualist thesis itself, so the inference would assume what it set out to establish" (L83). The conditional it allows is the honest residue.
- **Covert consciousness binds its proposed test with the Map's own tenet.** The sub-threshold recall test "could embarrass production without confirming the interface", because "the Map's own commitment to minimal interaction forecasts little or no detectable residual" (L92). A page that pre-registers how its own framework would blunt a positive result has internalised the discipline.
- **Cotard keeps a prediction as a prediction.** The co-variation asymmetry is recorded "as a prediction rather than evidence ... Nothing on this page rests on the prediction" (L68), as the creation-wing review noted.

**Persona convergence.** Within the four pages, the Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist praise different passages and never conflict, so the wing has resolved the tension honestly. They do conflict on `mine-ness` L136, the hub both clinical pages cite: Zahavi's "completely irrelational" for-me-ness is called "exactly what dualism predicts" and the tenet "finds direct support". That is coherence counted as support, which is the calibration concern this persona exists to flag. Priority item 1 is the resulting `refine-draft`.

## Consistency Verdict {#consistency-verdict}

**The four verdicts are mutually consistent. Two seams sit at the wing's edge, and one is a stated inconsistency on the wing's own page.**

**(a) Cotard and depersonalisation agree on the significance tier.** Cotard: "the significance-tier claim is untouched" (L66); Tenet 1 "compatible with, at most suggestive for ... Nothing in Cotard discriminates; what it earns is framework-internal weight" (L72). Depersonalisation: "The significance-tier claim is null" (L83); Tenet 1 "compatible with, not evidence for" (L87). Both say no void's independence score rises. "At most suggestive" (Cotard L36, L72) and "not evidence for" differ in wording, not in tier: Cotard's own sentence closes on non-discrimination, and the creation-wing review read "at most suggestive" as a ceiling. No action. On the architecture tier they also agree. Cotard tightens a level-1 claim, that the existence report is separable from ongoing emotional and cognitive life. Depersonalisation holds level 1 as "defensible" and level 2 as "contested".

**(b) Seam 1: depersonalisation asserts a tier that mine-ness does not hold.** Depersonalisation L87 reads "compatible with, not evidence for; the tier [mine-ness](/concepts/mine-ness/) and [cotard-delusion](/concepts/cotard-delusion/) hold." Cotard holds it. `mine-ness` does not. Its Dualism subsection (L136) opens "The Dualism tenet finds direct support in mine-ness's separability and apparent irreducibility" and calls a feature with no functional decomposition "exactly what dualism predicts". A grep of mine-ness's body finds 0 hits for "compatib", and its one "two-tier" hit is a Further Reading pointer (L171) the Relation section does not apply. `git log -S` dates the sentence to the page's creation (2026-05-19), and the 2026-08-22 deep review did not touch it. The creation-wing review asserted the same false presence (L169: "at the tier `mine-ness` and `cotard` already hold"). A reader who follows depersonalisation's link to mine-ness therefore finds the opposite verdict on the same datum. Depersonalisation L73 also reports that the universalist reads mine-ness's own coherence argument (mine-ness L74, "If mine-ness were not separable, DPD would be impossible to describe") as evidence that for-me-ness *survives*, "a standoff". So the hub's "direct support" is contradicted by the very page written to be its evidential home. → Priority item 1.

**(c) Seam 2: "for-me-ness" names a feature that can lapse and a position that cannot.** `self-and-self-consciousness` L148 states the Map's kind-claim: clinical variation "can attenuate, distort, or destabilise — never that it fully dissolves while phenomenal life continues". Its falsifier is "phenomenal life continuing without any residue of for-me-ness, no position from which the lack is registered". Cotard runs that claim ("what fails is the report, not the position", L64) and predicts "for-me-ness is attenuated but never eliminated" (L66). Depersonalisation adopts Billon's reading as "the Map's reading" (L39) and reports that on Billon's scope mineness is "general, if not universal" and can "lapse partially or globally" (L63). The research note confirms this is Billon's position, not a paraphrase drift: "can be partially or globally lacking, which is exactly the separability claim the Map wants" (`research/depersonalisation-2026-09-30` L79). The note's L207 adds that Billon's 2013 thought-insertion paper "is where he argues consciousness does not entail subjectivity", which negates the kind-claim directly. Depersonalisation's universalism paragraph then presents the "the report is hers" argument as the *rival's* reading (L73), the same argument Cotard and self-and-self-consciousness run as the Map's. Depersonalisation never states which side the Map takes on scope, and its body has **0** links to `self-and-self-consciousness` (it appears only in frontmatter).

The materials for the resolution are already on the page. Billon's subject "persists and feels, and what the experience lacks is its subjective *character*" (L61), and the film-watcher "is still being asked by someone" (L63). One sentence can say the Map sides with the universalist about the reporting position and with Billon about the felt feature. This does **not** re-flag the deep review's stability note ("Universalist ... and localist ... objections are framework-level standoffs ... not defects"). The standoff stays a standoff; the sentence only places the Map in it. → Priority item 2.

**(d) Revelation and covert consciousness are coherent, and complementary.** Revelation's acquaintance claim concerns what *having* an experience puts the haver in a position to know. Weak Revelation reveals "existence, some character" (L75) to the subject and licenses nothing about what an observer can know. Covert consciousness's "strong evidence of probable consciousness, reached through a neural marker" (L70) is a third-person inference from a volitional-cognition marker, which reports no phenomenal character at all. The two never compete for the same evidence. They converge in one place: neither exhibits phenomenal causal power. Revelation: "no selection power is revealed" (L77). CMD: "an epiphenomenalist can grant the covert processing and deny only that its phenomenal character contributes" (L98). That is the standing `tenets.md` L93 gives Tenet 3, "not ... a directly introspectible datum", reached from the first-person side and the third-person side. Both pages also place the discriminating evidence elsewhere: the explanatory gap and conceivability (Revelation L79), and the unrun sub-threshold recall test (CMD L92).

**(e) Revelation and the clinical pages.** Revelation's first reason for denying strong Revelation (self-opacity; Lewis's "if we fail to notice structure, there is no structure there to notice", L51) is consistent with the clinical pages. On Billon's reading, depersonalisation is the case where a feature normal experience never notices shows itself by its absence (mine-ness L98: "A feature that pervades every experience yet normally goes unnoticed is by definition *transparent*"). The reading is contested at level 2, so this is an illustration, not evidence. No inconsistency.
