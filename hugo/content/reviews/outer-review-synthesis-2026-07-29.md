## TL;DR

All three reviewers independently found that the Map's epistemology hub carries an external bibliography that stops in 1996 — the cycle's only unanimous finding, and the one the site can act on most cheaply. Two deeper 2-of-3 clusters do more damage: the hub adopts a seemings-internalist epistemology without ever facing externalism or reliabilism, and it never engages predictive processing, the leading physicalist account of the very epistemic access it partitions into two channels. Eleven clusters were identified — eleven convergent, six singleton, three divergent — and three tasks were upgraded P2 → P1.

The cycle's structural lesson is narrower than any single reviewer stated: the hub is not wrong so much as *behind its own children*. Two reviewers reached that diagnosis from opposite ends, and it is the property that explains most of the specific defects below.

## Divergences

Cases where reviewers explicitly contradicted each other. The disagreement is itself signal.

- **Gemini vs. ChatGPT on causal closure.** Both attacked the same sentence and reached opposite conclusions. Gemini argued closure is "a strict operational necessity for the empirical distinguishability" of consciousness theories, citing Kleiner & Hartmann (2023); ChatGPT argued closure is historically and scientifically motivated and formulated Papineau's probabilistic version. Verification found Gemini had **inverted its own source** — Kleiner & Hartmann argue closure is untenable in a scientific context, which makes the paper formal 2020s *support* for the article's stipulation framing. The genuinely hard residual is one no reviewer stated: a minimal-interaction dualism aims at the smallest possible physical difference, which pushes the Map toward exactly the practical indistinguishability those authors treat as a defect.
- **Gemini vs. ChatGPT on the illusionism matrix.** Gemini charged a "persistent strawman" conflating illusionism's denial of phenomenal properties with denial of psychological episodes; verification refuted it, since the article already distinguishes eliminativism and illusionism from reductive phenomenal realism, phenomenal-concept strategies, representationalism and nonreductive physicalism, and warns against mistaking the weaker opponent for the whole field. ChatGPT, reading the same matrix, found a narrower and surviving defect: the matrix works only if "phenomenal data of the relevant kind" is defined as intrinsic nonphysical qualitative properties. The narrower reading is the one that survived.
- **Claude vs. the corpus on the deferral chain.** Claude claimed the reliability question loops between three articles and terminates only in a void. Verification found it terminates in a graded positive account — five named strategies for epistemic traction, with the process/content backbone marked strongly supported. Only the hub's unqualified restatement is defective. ChatGPT's parallel charge was narrower and survived intact. A useful calibration for the proposed deferral-chain checker: it should report *where and at what strength* a chain grounds out, not flag cycles as defects.

## Method Notes

- **Reviewer scope discipline diverged sharply, and it tracked accuracy.** Gemini audited the epistemology *cluster* while the prompt named one article: "phenomenal authority", "Phenomenal Conservatism", "Huemer", "Zanotti", "apodictic certainty", "brute fact" and "*C. elegans*" return zero hits in [concepts/epistemology.md](/concepts/epistemology/). Four of Gemini's five weaknesses were refuted or inverted at corpus level, and it minted no new tasks — its two usable findings were folded into existing ones. Claude, by contrast, repeatedly checked whether the corpus already held the material and framed its gaps as hub-level rather than corpus-level; that discipline is why its findings survived verification at a much higher rate.
- **Correlated error was correctly excluded.** Two Claude findings that would otherwise have looked like strong single-reviewer signal were refuted against the working copy: the tenet numbering is *not* inverted (Claude's own constitutional statement had the indices transposed; the site is right), and the Fitness-Beats-Truth asymmetry is already handled by a dedicated "symmetry objection" section in [concepts/fitness-beats-truth.md](/concepts/fitness-beats-truth/). Neither was counted toward any cluster.
- **Source-quality flags.** Claude cited Wikipedia and Grokipedia as authorities for two substantive claims; neither is usable as a reference of record, and the tasks carry explicit instructions not to propagate that sourcing. Gemini's supporting citations (Corcoran et al., Whyte et al., Dung & Kammerer, "Weber 2026") were not verified at publisher and no task depends on them.
- **Same-file pileup is now acute and was deliberately not worsened.** Four separate refine-draft tasks target [concepts/epistemology.md](/concepts/epistemology/), three of them now P1. They were not merged — each fixes distinct sentences with distinct remedies — but each carries explicit sequencing and locus-boundary instructions, and length must be re-measured between passes: the file is 2074w against a 2500 soft ceiling, and the bibliography task alone adds five references. [topics/phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence.md](/topics/phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence/) is already over its hard ceiling at 4441w, so nothing may be added there without paying for it first.
- **No tasks were deduplicated or deleted this cycle.** Where two reviewers converged, they had converged on the same *property* at different *loci*, so the matching tasks were annotated rather than merged.
