## Reply

# External review: “Eastern Philosophy and Consciousness”

## Overall judgment: major revision

The article is strongest as a catalogue of **possible analogies** between the Map and selected Asian traditions. It is much weaker as historical exegesis, as an argument for cross-traditional convergence, and as empirical support for interactionist dualism.

Its defensible conclusion is roughly this:

> Advaita Vedānta, several Buddhist philosophical systems, and Daoist traditions contain sophisticated alternatives to reductive Western accounts of mind, together with unusually developed first-person investigative practices.

Its current conclusion is substantially stronger: that the traditions converge on consciousness’s fundamental or irreducible status, illuminate all five Map tenets, and permit Buddhist no-self to be reconciled with primitive indexical identity through “process haecceitism.” Those conclusions are not established. The article repeatedly moves between five distinct claims without defending the transitions:

1. Experience is taken seriously.
2. Experience cannot be eliminated from useful discourse.
3. Consciousness cannot be reduced to physical descriptions.
4. Consciousness is a nonphysical or fundamental constituent of reality.
5. Nonphysical consciousness and matter are distinct relata that causally interact.

The Map’s Dualism and Bidirectional Interaction tenets require the stronger end of this sequence: something beyond the material that causally affects physical outcomes. Merely treating experience as real or philosophically important does not supply that ontology. [topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/)

I checked both the live article and the activity record. The rendered changelog was still serving material only through 6 August, but the repository changelog extends through 18 August and records continuing corrections to citation framing, including the distinction between meditation experience correlating with introspective accuracy and meditation training causing it. The neighbouring *Buddhism and Dualism* article was also materially revised after the present article, on 17 August, and is now appreciably more cautious. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

## 1. The traditions do not converge on the same proposition

The article’s central convergence argument is constructed at too coarse a level. “Consciousness is real and significant” is compatible with physicalism, nonreductive physicalism, idealism, neutral monism, phenomenology, Madhyamaka conventionalism, and several forms of illusionism. It therefore cannot by itself support dualism.

Once the shared proposition is made precise, the apparent convergence largely disappears:

- **Advaita** makes consciousness ultimately fundamental but rejects two ultimately real interacting relata.
- **Yogācāra** may privilege consciousness, but thereby removes the independent physical relatum required by the Map.
- **Abhidharma traditions** can treat momentary consciousness-events as ultimately real while rejecting a persisting subject or substance-agent.
- **Madhyamaka** challenges the project of identifying any ultimately grounded constituent, including consciousness.
- **Daoism**, on prominent contemporary interpretations, does not formulate the Western mind–body problem in the first place.

The genuine common denominator is closer to **phenomenological and practical seriousness about experience**. That is philosophically interesting, but it is much thinner than interactionist dualism.

## 2. Advaita Vedānta: accurate preliminaries, incorrect alignment

The account of Brahman, adhyāsa and sākṣin is broadly recognizable. The difficulty begins when sākṣin is treated as an irreducible subject that can bridge Advaita to the Map.

### The witness is not an interacting dualist subject

In Śaṅkara’s final ontology, Brahman alone is real; the empirical subject–object structure is provisional. Current specialist treatment explicitly says that causation does not cross between the empirical and ultimate orders and that, ultimately, there are no second things such as independent properties or individuals. The witness relation is therefore not an ultimate relation between a subject and objects. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/spr2025/entries/shankara/)

That creates two problems for the article:

- At the **conventional level**, mind and matter can be discussed as distinct, but this distinction belongs to the provisional appearance that Advaita ultimately dissolves.
- At the **ultimate level**, there are not two relata available for the Map’s Bidirectional Interaction.

Consequently, the two-level doctrine does not “resolve” the interaction problem. It removes the interaction problem by denying the ultimate independence of its relata.

### Advaita is not “more comfortable with substance dualism”

The article says Advaita’s eternal ātman makes it more congenial to substance dualism. This is misleading. Advaita does not posit an individual conscious substance alongside a material substance. Ātman is identical with nondual Brahman, and the differentiated individual and world are not ultimately second realities. It is better classified here as a **nondual anti-physicalist rival** than as a variant of Map dualism. [topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/)

Advaita supports the proposition that reductive physicalism is not the only serious ontology. It does not support a quantum interaction between an individual witness and an independently real material world.

## 3. Buddhism: heavily flattened and selectively reconstructed

### No-self is broader than rejection of an eternal soul

The article says anattā targets the eternal, unchanging ātman. That is true but incomplete in a load-bearing way. The canonical not-self analysis is applied to consciousness itself: consciousness is to be regarded as neither “mine,” nor self, nor what one is. The associated problem is not merely permanence; it concerns ownership, agency and the existence of a subject standing behind the aggregates. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mind-indian-buddhism/)

The article therefore underdescribes the conflict. A momentary phenomenal property can be impermanent while still being “irreducible,” but that logical possibility does not establish:

- an owner or bearer of the property;
- a persisting subject linking moments;
- a primitive fact about which stream is mine;
- a nonphysical agent capable of selecting physical outcomes.

Those are precisely the elements needed by the Map’s indexical and agency clusters.

### “Property dualism within Buddhist flux” is a Map proposal, not Buddhist doctrine

Some classical Indian Buddhist schools treated occurrences of consciousness as ultimately real, and recent scholarship emphasizes that classical Indian Buddhists generally rejected physicalism in favour of dualist or idealist alternatives. But that does not make Buddhism, without qualification, a form of contemporary property dualism. It makes school-level differentiation indispensable. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/60496/chapter/522498382)

The article should separately treat at least:

- early Buddhist aggregate analysis;
- Abhidharma realism about mental events;
- Yogācāra idealism or phenomenalism;
- Madhyamaka anti-foundationalism;
- modern naturalized or physicalist reconstructions.

At present, “Buddhism” functions as a container from which whichever element best fits the Map is selected.

### The witness argument begs the question

The claim that observing thoughts requires an observer distinct from the observed is not established by Buddhist meditation. It imports a familiar subject–object model into traditions that often use contemplative practice to deconstruct precisely that model.

At least three alternatives remain open:

1. A mental occurrence is reflexively or metacognitively represented without a separate observer.
2. One mental event takes an earlier or simultaneous event as its object.
3. “Witnessing” is the phenomenology of a self-model becoming less salient, not evidence for a metaphysically distinct witness.

Recent Buddhist philosophy explicitly explores consciousness that is real yet subjectless, and disputes whether reflexive self-illumination must be posited at all. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/60496/chapter-abstract/522498182)

The article’s inference from phenomenological decentring to an irreducible observing subject is therefore not a neutral report. It is a Map-compatible interpretation that should be labelled and defended as such.

### Yogācāra is not a solution to the Map’s interaction problem

If the apparent external world is consciousness-dependent, changing mind-to-body causation into mind-to-mind causation does not solve the Map’s problem. It abandons the Map’s ontology of two distinct relata. An idealist does not explain how nonphysical consciousness interacts with independent matter; the idealist denies that independent matter exists.

The current *Buddhism and Dualism* article now handles this correctly, calling Yogācāra the Map’s principal Buddhist rival and warning that it must not be conscripted as evidence for the Map. The August 9 synthesis has not absorbed that correction. [topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/)

### Karma does not establish nonphysical causation

That intentions have consequences establishes mental causation only in a broad sense. It does not establish that a nonphysical subject injects or selects physical outcomes. Buddhist causal theories can understand intention as a dependently arisen event within a causal stream, without a substance-agent and without a physics-transcending causal contribution.

The main article moves too quickly from:

> intentions condition actions and future experience

to:

> consciousness shapes physical outcomes without injecting force.

That is the conclusion the Map needs, not a conclusion supplied by Buddhist karma. The updated neighbouring article now acknowledges the difference between Buddhist event-causation and the Map’s persisting-subject agent-causation. [topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/)

## 4. Daoism: the weakest and most projective section

The Daoist material is largely a sequence of Map-generated analogies rather than a sourced account of Daoist philosophy.

The claims that:

- dào is “fundamental reality” and specifically a process;
- wu wei resolves the Map’s witness/selector problem;
- Daoism suggests consciousness–matter interaction is natural;

are not established from Daoist texts or specialist scholarship.

A prominent current specialist treatment instead presents Daoism as a naturalistic, holistic framework that rejects an internal ruling homunculus and often declines to formulate the Western subjective-mind/objective-world problem at all. On that reading, Zhuangzi’s distributed bodily agency is more naturally a challenge to a privileged witness-selector than support for one. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/daoism/)

The citation base is especially weak: “Lao Tzu, *Tao Te Ching*, various translations” is not an auditable reference. There are no chapter locators, no specified translation, no Zhuangzi passages, and no specialist work directly supporting the consciousness–matter interpretation. [topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/)

The section should either become a careful treatment of specific Laozi and Zhuangzi arguments or be explicitly relabelled as a speculative comparison. As written, it substantially flattens Daoism into Whiteheadian process metaphysics and then treats that reconstruction as evidence for the Map.

## 5. “Process haecceitism” does not resolve the no-self conflict

The article presents process haecceitism as its principal original resolution. It instead generates a dilemma.

### Horn one: causal history individuates the process

The proposal says experiential streams are individuated by their particular causal histories. But the Map defines haecceity as a primitive, nonqualitative, nontransferable thisness. Causal history is relational and descriptive; it is not primitive thisness. [concepts/haecceity](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/haecceity/)

More importantly, the Map’s own *Vertiginous Question* article explicitly says that different bodies, brains and causal histories already explain why organisms are numerically distinct—and equally explicitly says this does **not** answer why this perspective is mine. Process haecceitism therefore supplies an answer that a neighbouring article has already ruled out as answering the wrong question. [topics/vertiginous-question](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/vertiginous-question/)

Under this horn, “process haecceitism” is really **causal-relational process individuation**. It can distinguish streams, but it does not explain indexical mine-ness.

### Horn two: a primitive fact remains beyond causal history

Suppose causal history does not exhaust the explanation and there remains a primitive fact that this stream is mine. Then changing the bearer from “substance” to “process” does not reconcile the view with Buddhist no-self. It retains exactly the ungrounded further fact that reductionist and Madhyamaka accounts contest.

A further diachronic dilemma follows:

- If one primitive thisness persists across the changing moments, the theory has reintroduced a persisting identity fact functionally similar to the disputed bearer.
- If every moment has a new thisness, there is no single continuing subject available for agent-causation or for the Map’s indexical objection to Many Worlds.

The current Tenets page is more candid: it says the Many-Worlds objection requires a non-deflationary “I,” while Madhyamaka treats that further question as ill-formed. It calls this a bedrock disagreement. The present article incorrectly redescribes the disagreement as solved. [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

### The electron analogy provides no support

The article suggests that standard quantum mechanics supplies a precedent because qualitatively identical electrons remain numerically distinct. The philosophy of quantum mechanics does not deliver that result straightforwardly. Permuting identical particles generally does not generate a new physical arrangement; quantum theory is underdetermined between individual and non-individual metaphysical packages. Primitive thisness is one optional interpretation, while relational discernibility and non-individuality are others. Physics supplies no evidential preference for the haecceitistic package. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+3Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+3Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+3](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qt-idind/)

The proposal can therefore be retained as a speculative metaphysical option, but it should no longer be called a resolution. It either abandons primitive haecceity in favour of causal individuation or preserves primitive haecceity and leaves the Buddhist objection untouched.

## 6. Citation currency and fidelity

### General assessment

Most named references are real, and I found no obvious invented direct quotation. The larger problem is **inferential fidelity**: studies and traditions are repeatedly asked to support propositions substantially stronger than those their sources establish.

The philosophical bibliography is unevenly current. The empirical material reaches 2025, but central interpretive sections still rely on Siderits 2007, Garfield 1995 and Gupta 1998 without corresponding engagement with major 2025–2026 literature. Particularly conspicuous omissions include Siderits’s 2025 *Buddhist Physicalism?*, current specialist treatments of Śaṅkara and Daoism, and 2026 work on “new-wave” illusionism. These sources existed before the article’s 9 August revision. [OUP Academic+4OUP Academic+4OUP Academic+4](https://academic.oup.com/book/60496/chapter-abstract/522498149)

There are also concrete auditability defects:

- The text invokes “Tallis’s regress (2011),” but the bibliography contains only Tallis 2024.
- “Lao Tzu … Various translations” makes textual verification impossible.
- The Śaṅkara reference supplies no book section, passage or page supporting the article’s particular witness claims.
- “Conditioned arising” is incorrectly identified as *saṅkhāra*. The relevant term is *pratītyasamutpāda* or *paṭiccasamuppāda*; *saṅkhāra/saṃskāra* refers to conditioned formations.
- The Whitehead–Buddhism comparison is presented as a mapping rather than a contested analogy. [topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/)

### Fox et al. 2012

The article accurately gives the study title but overreads the finding. Fox et al. conducted a preliminary cross-sectional comparison of meditators with differing experience. Their outcome was accuracy in rating tactile sensitivity across body regions, compared against averaged cortical and psychophysical measures taken from previous research. It was not a randomized training study and did not test whether subjects had more accurate access to phenomenal properties as such. [PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article)

It therefore cannot support the article’s claim that, were phenomenal consciousness illusory, meditation training should eventually make practitioners discover that “there was nothing there.” Better tactile calibration is equally compatible with improved metacognition about representational or quasi-phenomenal states.

The current repository changelog has already corrected this causal drift elsewhere, explicitly replacing “training improves” with the narrower “experience predicts” formulation and noting the absence of a located direct replication. The correction has not propagated fully into this article’s argument.

### Yang et al. 2025

Yang et al. is real and recent. It is, however, an intensively sampled case study of one advanced practitioner. It reports deactivation of regions associated with self-related processing and activation of regions associated with perception and perceptual sensitivity. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053811924004658)

That is evidence about neural correlates of a reported meditation sequence. It does not establish:

- an irreducible process-witness;
- persistence of a metaphysical subject–object structure;
- nonphysical consciousness;
- consciousness operating independently of neural systems.

“Consistent with reduced self-model salience while awareness-related processing continues” would be a defensible description. “Neuroimaging supports” the Map’s process-witness interpretation is not.

### Demirel et al. 2025

Demirel et al. pooled EEG data across laboratories and identified source-level power and connectivity patterns associated with lucid dreaming. The study investigates perception, memory, self-awareness and cognitive control during REM sleep. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40258661/)

It does not:

- validate Tibetan dream-yoga doctrine;
- support filter theory;
- show consciousness operating free of sensory or neural constraints;
- demonstrate nonphysical mind-to-body causation.

Volitional control of dream contents is control within an internally generated cognitive state. It is fully compatible with neural causation. The study can support the narrower claim that lucid dreaming has empirically detectable neural signatures and permits some metacognitive control during REM sleep.

### Cessation research

The cited extended-cessation result is a preprint based on dense sampling of three practitioners. Crucially, its authors classify the target state as one in which consciousness is suspended. They report that this suspension can occur without global neural suppression, challenging predictions made by particular versions of Global Neuronal Workspace and Integrated Information Theory while favouring their Active Inference interpretation. [Sciety](https://sciety.org/articles/activity/10.1101/2025.09.06.674021)

The article reverses the relevant inference. Continued regional brain activity during a state classified as unconscious does not show consciousness continuing independently of neural activity. It may challenge theories that predict a particular global neural signature for unconsciousness, but it does not support filter theory or dualism.

## 7. The underweighted counterarguments

### Illusionism

The article does acknowledge the illusionist reply that meditators may be improving the accuracy of quasi-phenomenal self-representation. That is a useful concession. It then treats Fox et al. as residual evidential pressure because training allegedly ought to dissolve an illusion.

Contemporary “new-wave” illusionism need not make that prediction. It explicitly allows the appearance of phenomenal consciousness to remain compelling even under impeccable reasoning, more like a persistent perceptual illusion than a correctable conceptual mistake. Increasing discriminatory expertise can therefore coexist with illusionism. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/62981/chapter/565377959)

More damagingly, the omitted Siderits 2025 argument supplies a **Buddhist-internal** route to illusionism. It proposes that first-person theory of mind creates an inner mirroring structure, that the subject–object dichotomy is illusory, and that qualia can be understood as artifacts of a useful fiction. Siderits presents the overall physicalist reconstruction as exploratory rather than settled, but its existence is enough to refute the article’s suggestion that illusionism simply threatens or contradicts Buddhism’s practical project from outside. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/60496/chapter-abstract/522498835)

A Buddhist practice can target conventionally real suffering, attention and self-representation while denying an intrinsically existing owner or irreducible qualitative property. The article must therefore argue why contemplative discrimination selects phenomenal realism over this interpretation. Fox et al. does not do that work.

### Madhyamaka anti-foundationalism

The article treats Madhyamaka primarily as the view that empty things nevertheless exist conventionally. That is correct as far as it goes, but it misses the force of the objection.

Madhyamaka does not merely deny that consciousness has an immutable essence while leaving the Map’s ontology otherwise intact. It challenges attempts to ground propositions or phenomena in self-validating foundations, and it applies emptiness to consciousness as well as to matter. Candrakīrti also criticizes literal consciousness-only readings of Yogācāra. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/madhyamaka/)

The article attempts to preserve compatibility by saying that irreducibility is “a relation between descriptions” and that the Map operates conventionally. This buys compatibility by weakening the Map’s commitment:

- A descriptive or explanatory nonreduction claim is not yet the Map’s metaphysical claim that something beyond the material exists.
- Conventional mind–matter discourse does not establish two independently real relata.
- Conventional causation does not establish nonphysical selection of physical outcomes.
- Conventional use of “I” or “this awareness” does not establish primitive haecceity.

Primitive thisness is especially difficult to reconcile with Madhyamaka because it functions as an irreducible identity fact that depends on nothing else. Calling its bearer a process rather than a substance does not remove that foundational role.

The current Tenets page correctly calls Madhyamaka’s rejection of the vertiginous question a bedrock disagreement. The synthesis should follow that calibration rather than claiming conventional-level compatibility has solved the problem. [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

## 8. Integration with neighbouring articles

The article has **excellent navigational integration**: it links to many relevant concepts, empirical articles and comparative clusters. It also deserves credit for noting experiential divergences such as cessation versus plenitude and affective-tone differences rather than treating every contemplative report as identical. [topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/)

Its **proposition-level integration**, however, is poor. Several direct inconsistencies have emerged:

1. The synthesis treats Yogācāra as a solution to interaction; the current *Buddhism and Dualism* article treats it as a rival that must not be conscripted as evidence.
2. The synthesis treats Madhyamaka compatibility as secured at the conventional level; the current neighbour concedes that such a reading cannot support the Map’s metaphysical and causal tenets.
3. The synthesis treats karma as support for Bidirectional Interaction; the neighbour distinguishes Buddhist event-causation from the Map’s substance-leaning agent-causation.
4. The synthesis treats witnessing phenomenology as revealing an observer; the neighbour says it supports the Map only if that appearance survives Buddhist deconstruction.
5. *Haecceity* individuates a process by causal history; *The Vertiginous Question* explicitly says causal history does not answer the indexical question.
6. The synthesis says process haecceitism supports the No Many Worlds tenet; the Tenets page says that deflating the subject into a process weakens the indexical objection correspondingly.
7. The article’s use of Fox et al. remains stronger than the correction standard now recorded in the changelog. [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

The closing claim that “None has occurred” among the listed challenges should be removed. Buddhist illusionism has been explicitly developed; cessation may be unconsciousness according to the cited researchers themselves; and the process-haecceity account contains an unresolved internal contradiction. [topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/)

## Concrete potential improvements

### Specific articles

1. **`topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/`** — Replace the single convergence thesis with a school-by-school claim matrix distinguishing: experiential reality, anti-eliminativism, epistemic irreducibility, metaphysical fundamentality, dualism, mental causation, nonphysical interaction and primitive identity. Do not count agreement on one column as agreement on the others.
2. **`topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/`** — Mark every substantial paragraph as either **historical report**, **contested interpretation**, or **Map reconstruction**. “Property dualism within Buddhist flux,” wu wei as selector theory, and Whiteheadian readings of Buddhist momentariness belong in the third category.
3. **`topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/`** — Rewrite the Advaita section to describe Advaita as a nondual anti-physicalist rival. Delete the claim that two levels of truth resolve interaction and the suggestion that Advaita is naturally substance-dualist. Add precise Śaṅkara passage and translation locators.
4. **`topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/`** and **`concepts/buddhism-and-dualism/`** — Divide “Buddhism” into early Buddhist, Abhidharma, Yogācāra and Madhyamaka positions. Incorporate Siderits 2025 both for classical Buddhist nonphysicalism and for the naturalized, illusionist countermodel.
5. **`concepts/witness-consciousness/`** — Add competing analyses of witnessing: reflexive awareness, temporally ordered metacognition, subjectless awareness, attentional decentring and self-model attenuation. Remove any unargued inference from “thoughts are observed” to “a distinct observer exists.”
6. **`concepts/haecceity/`**, **`topics/vertiginous-question/`**, and **`tenets/`** — Decide whether process identity is grounded by causal history or by primitive thisness. If causal history is sufficient, stop claiming that the theory answers the vertiginous question or supports the No Many Worlds objection. If primitive thisness remains, present Buddhist no-self and Madhyamaka as unresolved bedrock opposition rather than reconciliation.
7. **`concepts/haecceity/`** — Remove the presentation of identical quantum particles as positive evidence for haecceitism. Replace it with the accurate conclusion that quantum physics underdetermines individualist, relational and non-individualist metaphysical packages.
8. **The Daoism section and its neighbouring Chinese-philosophy articles** — Separate Laozi, Zhuangzi and later religious Daoist materials. Add named translations, chapter references and current specialists. Remove consciousness–matter and witness-selector claims unless a Daoist source actually advances them.
9. **`topics/dream-consciousness/`** and **`topics/lucid-dreaming-and-dualist-rendering/`** — Reframe Demirel et al. as evidence for neural signatures and metacognitive control during lucid REM sleep. Delete claims that the study validates dream yoga, filter theory or nonphysical interaction.
10. **The cessation and neurophenomenology cluster** — Mark the extended-cessation study as an N=3 preprint whose authors classify the state as suspension of consciousness. Limit its implications to challenges for specific neural theories; do not present residual activity during unconsciousness as evidence for dualism.
11. **`concepts/process-philosophy/`** — Correct *saṅkhāra* to *pratītyasamutpāda/paṭiccasamuppāda* where dependent arising is intended. Treat Whitehead–Buddhist correspondences as selective analogies, not translations between equivalent systems.
12. **`topics/cross-traditional-convergence-on-consciousness-irreducibility/`** — State the exact proposition on which traditions allegedly converge. Add explicit tests for shared human cognitive architecture, cultural transmission, translation effects, doctrinal training and selection of reports. Convergence on phenomenological vocabulary should not be counted as independent convergence on ontology.

### Site methodology

1. Require comparative articles to use a three-layer format: **source tradition’s claim; live scholarly dispute; Map’s proposed appropriation**. Automated drafting should not merge those layers into one voice.
2. Require primary-source locators for every doctrinally load-bearing claim: text, chapter or verse, edition, translator and page. Prohibit bibliography entries such as “various translations.”
3. Extend the citation ledger beyond metadata verification. Every important citation should separately record: **source existence**, **quotation/paraphrase fidelity**, **study design**, and **whether the cited source entails the article’s conclusion**.
4. Introduce proposition-level synchronization checks across linked articles. Examples of invariant questions are: “Is Yogācāra an ally or rival?”, “Does karma entail nonphysical causation?”, “Does causal history answer the vertiginous question?”, and “Is Madhyamaka compatibility metaphysical or merely descriptive?”
5. Use a mandatory empirical-calibration block for scientific citations: sample size, observational versus experimental design, preregistration or preprint status, direct finding, supported inference, and speculative extrapolation.
6. Require at least one recent adversarial specialist source for every central comparative claim. A current source that challenges the Map should not be relegated to a sibling article while the synthesis retains the older favourable framing.
