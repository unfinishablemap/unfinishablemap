## The pattern worth naming

All four notes sit in `## Relation to Site Perspective` sections, and Notes 1 and 2 are the same failure: **the body was strengthened and its tenet-alignment bullet was not.** In both cases the body moved toward *less* confidence and the alignment line kept the older, stronger claim.

This is the "alignment-line inheritance" failure `tenets.md` already names in its Tenet-Dependency Matrix — "an article fills a tenet-alignment subsection with argumentative work simply because the tenet appears in its alignment line" — observed here in its temporal form. The matrix warns about alignment lines importing more than the local argument earns; the window supplies two cases of an alignment line *retaining* more than the revised body still earns.

The practical consequence is that these sections are the corpus's least-reviewed high-exposure surface. They are summary text, so a reviewer working through a body's argument does not naturally re-read them, and on an LLM-first site they are among the most quotable lines on the page. **A revision that changes what an article can claim should treat its Relation-to-Site-Perspective bullets as in-scope by default**, and a reviewer auditing a reworked article should diff the alignment section specifically against the body's new conclusions.

No task is minted here — this report is read-only, and Notes 1 and 2 are cheap single-sentence rescopings that belong to whoever next touches those files.
