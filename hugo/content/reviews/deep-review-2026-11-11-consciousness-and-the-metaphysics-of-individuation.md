## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **P3: the empty-individualism definition misplaced Kolak's categories (attribution).** FIXED.
  - L67 defined the category as "personal identity is less determinate than we think … Subject boundaries are conventional, not metaphysical. The self is a useful fiction".
  - Kolak's own text was fetched (archive.org full text of the Springer edition) and grepped, along with Parfit's.
  - **(a) Kolak's definition** (p. xxii): "either there exist no continuously existing, self-identical persons over time in the sense ordinarily understood — the sort of view developed by philosophers as diverse as Buddha, Hume and most recently Derek Parfit, what I call The Empty Individual View of Personal Identity, or simply Empty Individualism for short".
  - **(b) Empty individualism is reductionist, not conventionalist.** On p. 18 Kolak writes that "Empty Individualism implies reductionism by stipulating that the boundaries of persons consist in, or are reduced to, underlying physiological and/or psychological borders". On pp. 10–11 he says that on both closed and empty individualism "personal identity is closed under known individuating borders". So "boundaries conventional, not metaphysical" is the wrong slot. On pp. 10–11 the boundaries-in-our-maps claim ("The boundaries we draw along the borders between us exist only in our maps of ourselves") is Kolak's *own*, open-individualist, thesis.
  - **(c) "Fiction" belongs to Hume.** "useful fiction" occurs 0 times in *Reasons and Persons*. Its 8 "fiction" hits are Sidgwick-on-Hume ("the permanent identical 'I' is not a fact but a fiction") and science-fiction usages. The only "useful fiction" in Kolak concerns Rawls's Noumenal Subject. The fiction reading is Hume's: *Treatise* 1.4.6 says "The identity, which we ascribe to the mind of man, is only a fictitious one" (Gutenberg #4705, grep 1). It cannot sit on the Parfit slot.
  - **Parfit** (*Reasons and Persons*, archive.org text) says: "They are Reductionist because they claim (1) that the fact of a person's identity over time just consists in the holding of certain more particular facts". He also writes "Personal identity is not what matters. What fundamentally matters is Relation R" and "Reductionists claim that, in some cases, questions about personal identity are indeterminate".
  - New L67 (paraphrase, no quotation): the definition now matches the bridge article and adds Kolak's qualifier. Parfit's narrower claim is marked as Kolak's filing. "Useful fiction" and "conventional" are gone. The "associated with" qualifier is kept.
- **Felt certainty used as a strike against a rival (calibration).** FIXED in the same L67 sentence. The old clause, "it conflicts with the phenomenological certainty that *this* subject is experiencing *this* moment", let felt this-ness count against empty individualism. The empty individualist predicts the same datum. `topics/kants-paralogisms-and-the-maps-subject` §Three Exposures names this exact move ("Felt mine-ness used as evidence … gives it no support over rivals that predict the same datum"). [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) grades its external evidence D. New: "it must deny that the felt certainty of *this* subject experiencing *this* moment tracks any deep fact". The clause now states what the rival is committed to, not that the feeling refutes it. A tenet-accepting reviewer would flag the old wording, so this was a calibration error, not a bedrock disagreement.
- **Hare's solipsism concession had the wrong referent (attribution; came in with 2be358d087).** FIXED. L111 had "Hare himself concedes it 'commits me to a kind of solipsism' (2010)", with "it" meaning egocentric presentism. In Hare 2010 (MIT open-access manuscript, pdftotext, grep) "egocentric presentism" occurs 0 times. The sentence reads: "Part of the answer may have to do with what perspectival realism entails. My adopting the view commits me to a kind of solipsism – not the kind of solipsism according to which only I exist, but a kind of solipsism according to which I am surrounded by a sphere of intrinsic privilege." The sibling page `concepts/egocentric-presentism` L48 attaches the quotation correctly to perspectival realism; the 10-06 copy moved it. New: "Hare himself concedes that its sibling, perspectival realism, 'commits me to a kind of solipsism' (2010)". The quotation is verbatim.

The PubMed 28122878 abstract concludes that severing the hemispheric connections "splits visual perception, but does not create two independent conscious perceivers within one brain". So perception is divided and the perceiver is not, which is the paper's title. New: "split-brain patients may remain one conscious perceiver even though visual perception is split". The hedge is kept.
