## Executive Summary

The article's central reframing (enhancement modifies the interface, not the mind) is well-executed and honestly hedged in places, but three structural problems survive: (1) a verified **wrong-work citation** — the Bostrom & Sandberg paraphrase describes their *Science and Engineering Ethics* 2009 paper, not the cited "Wisdom of Nature" chapter, and misframes even the right paper's skeptical treatment of the therapy/enhancement distinction; (2) the **identity-security argument equivocates** between irreducibility and immutability, and its confident free-will claim in the pharmaceutical case contradicts the article's own later concession that enhancement could shrink the domain of conscious action; (3) falsifiability condition #2 is **logically inverted**, and the equity section rests on an unargued "equal fundamental capacity" premise that the corpus's own phenomenal-variation apex renders unstable. None of these requires reconceiving the article; all are fixable in one refine pass.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Patricia Churchland)

"The entire apparatus — 'consciousness still selects among quantum possibilities at the interface' — is folk psychology wearing a lab coat. When the article's pharmaceutical eliminates impulsive aggression, we have a complete story: serotonergic modulation of prefrontal-amygdala circuits. The article adds a spectral chooser who 'might have chosen to override the impulse' and then worries about what the drug did to *its* menu. Nothing in the neuroscience of impulse control requires that second subject, and the article never says what the chooser contributes that lateral prefrontal inhibition doesn't. The 'instrument built for an unconsulted musician' metaphor in the consent section presupposes exactly what needs demonstrating — a musician who exists prior to and independent of the instrument. Developmental neuroscience shows the 'musician' being assembled *by* the instrument."

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Daniel Dennett)

"'You are still you, but the you-ness feels alien' — this is the Cartesian Theater with a renovation permit. The article inflates an introspective *seeming* (continuity of selfhood) into a metaphysical guarantee (indexical identity persists), then treats the seeming's possible disruption as merely 'phenomenological strain' that can't touch the underlying fact. Heads I win, tails you lose: if enhanced subjects feel continuous, dualism is confirmed; if they feel discontinuous, it's just the interface. And the claim that materialism risks 'death-and-replacement' under radical enhancement ignores forty years of work — Parfit showed that if identity isn't what matters, gradual psychological continuity gives you everything you should care about. The article buys 'stronger foundations for continuity' by positing a featureless pellet whose persistence conditions it never states."

### The Quantum Skeptic (Max Tegmark)

"The 'Enhancement and the Quantum Interface' section worries that a neural implant 'might bypass those sites entirely' — which sites? The article concedes in the same paragraph that 'we do not yet know the precise mechanism.' You cannot derive a design constraint from an unspecified mechanism. Decoherence timescales in neural tissue are femtoseconds-to-picoseconds against millisecond dynamics; if there were quantum sites whose preservation enhancement engineering must respect, that would be *measurable*, and the article proposes no measurement. The 'practical ethical imperative' to evaluate enhancement 'for its effects on the mechanisms through which consciousness interacts with the brain' is an imperative no regulator could act on — there is no assay for it."

### The Many-Worlds Defender (David Deutsch)

"The Tenet 4 paragraph is the article's weakest physics: 'enhancement decisions are irreversible in a way they would not be under many-worlds: there is no other branch where you made the opposite choice.' This misstates Everettian decision theory twice. First, branching occurs over quantum events, not over deliberations; a decision that is determined by your brain state may not branch at all. Second, even where branching occurs, the Deutsch-Wallace theorem recovers precisely the standard decision weights — my successors in low-weight branches do not make my choice 'reversible' or lighter, any more than an insurance policy makes a house fire reversible. Nothing about MWI diminishes the weight of an enhancement decision for the person who wakes up enhanced. The article should either engage the decision-theoretic literature or drop the 'weight of decisions increases' claim."

### The Empiricist (Karl Popper's Ghost)

"Credit where due: the article has a 'What Would Challenge This View?' section. But examine the conditions. #1 (a 'successful materialist account of consciousness') is a promissory note about a demonstration no one can operationalize. #2 is *logically inverted* — see Critical Issue 3. #3 (enhanced individuals reporting being a 'different subject') relies on first-person reports the framework elsewhere treats as interface-mediated and therefore corrupt-able by the very modification being tested; a dualist can always attribute the discontinuity report to interface distortion. #4 concedes the quantum concern is severable. And the equity section's premise — all conscious subjects equal, all variation interface-side — is unfalsifiable by construction: any observed cognitive or phenomenal difference gets booked to the interface, preserving the equality claim against every possible observation. That is not a discovered symmetry; it is an accounting convention."

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

"The article's anxieties are conditioned by the very reification it defends. It fears the enhanced person may find 'the you-ness feels alien' — but the you-ness was always alien, always a construction retrospectively claimed. That the article must invent a subject who 'was never constituted by the interface' to keep enhancement from touching the self shows the self doing no work: strip it away and every practical question the article raises — consent, responsibility, equity — survives intact, answerable in terms of continuity of dispositions and relations. The instrument-and-musician metaphor is telling: search for the musician apart from all instruments ever played and you find nothing. Enhancement ethics does not need a permanent subject; it needs care for the stream of experience, which the article's own 'experiential continuity' worry quietly concedes is what actually matters."

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Wrong-work citation — Bostrom & Sandberg paraphrase describes a different 2009 paper, and misframes it (VERIFIED)
- **File**: topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism.md
- **Location**: §Enhancement as Interface Modification, first paragraph + References #1
- **Problem**: The article attributes to Bostrom & Sandberg (2009) "The Wisdom of Nature: An Evolutionary Heuristic for Human Enhancement" the claims that they "distinguish therapeutic interventions (restoring normal function) from enhancement (exceeding normal function), framing both in terms of information-processing gains." Verified against the full text (nickbostrom.com/evolution.pdf): "The Wisdom of Nature" contains **zero** occurrences of "information processing" and no definitional therapy/enhancement framing — its thesis is the evolutionary optimality challenge ("if this enhancement is so good, why didn't we evolve it?"). The paraphrase instead matches Bostrom & Sandberg, "Cognitive Enhancement: Methods, Ethics, Regulatory Challenges," *Science and Engineering Ethics* 15(3), 311–341 (2009), which defines cognitive enhancement as "the amplification or extension of core capacities of the mind through improvement or augmentation of internal or external information processing systems." Framing drift compounds the wrong pointer: even the correct paper treats the therapy/enhancement distinction *skeptically* — "the distinction between therapy and enhancement is often difficult to discern, and it could be argued that it lacks practical significance" — whereas the article presents them as endorsing it as a load-carrying framework.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Re-point Reference #1 to the *Science and Engineering Ethics* paper (DOI 10.1007/s11948-009-9142-5) and soften the paraphrase: they *define* enhancement in information-processing terms while noting the therapy/enhancement line is blurry. The blurriness actually *helps* the article's dualist reframing (if the materialist distinction is unstable on its own terms, an interface-relative recasting has something to offer) — one clause could turn a miscitation into an argument.

### Issue 2: Irreducibility ≠ immutability — the identity-security argument assumes what bidirectional interaction denies
- **File**: topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism.md
- **Location**: §Identity and the Enhanced Self, first two paragraphs
- **Problem**: "If consciousness is irreducible to its physical substrate, then radical cognitive enhancement *cannot* alter the fundamental subject of experience" — the inference from irreducibility to immunity is a non-sequitur. Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction) commits the Map to physical→mental causal influence; nothing in the tenets states the *persistence conditions* of the subject or guarantees them against physical intervention. Irreducibility rules out identity with the substrate; it does not rule out dependence-for-persistence on the substrate (compare: a broadcast is irreducible to any one receiver, yet destroying the transmitter ends it). The article's own third paragraph ("the you-ness feels alien") shows the pressure but resolves it by fiat — "metaphysically secure" — without stating what secures it.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Downgrade "cannot alter" to a conditional the tenets actually license: *if* the subject's individuation is interface-independent (a further commitment the Map may adopt but should name), then identity persists. Cross-link [personal-identity](/topics/personal-identity/)'s actual argument for indexical persistence rather than gesturing at it, and mark the additional premise explicitly.

### Issue 3: Falsifiability condition #2 is logically inverted
- **File**: topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism.md
- **Location**: §What Would Challenge This View?, item 2
- **Problem**: "If radical cognitive enhancement produced dramatic performance gains with no change in the phenomenal character of experience, this would suggest that what's being enhanced *is* the mind rather than an interface." Backwards on the article's own model. §Identity states "An interface that filters differently produces different experiential content" — so the interface model *predicts* phenomenal change under enhancement, and performance-without-phenomenal-change would be evidence **against** the interface framing (or, more precisely, evidence that the enhanced processing runs outside consciousness entirely — which threatens Tenet 3's causal-efficacy story, not the interface metaphor). Meanwhile "what's being enhanced is the mind" would predict *more* phenomenal change, not none. As written, the article's flagship falsifiability section offers a disconfirmation condition that actually disconfirms the opposing view's least plausible reading.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Rewrite item 2: performance gains with no phenomenal change would suggest the enhanced processing proceeds outside the conscious channel — consistent with interface *boundaries* but pressure on any claim that consciousness is doing the newly-enhanced cognitive work; large phenomenal shifts tracking interface changes are what the model predicts.

### Issue 4: Internal contradiction — confident free-will preservation (§Moral Responsibility) vs. conceded free-will erosion (§Quantum Interface)
- **File**: topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism.md
- **Location**: §Moral Responsibility Under Enhancement ¶2 vs. §Enhancement and the Quantum Interface ¶2
- **Problem**: The pharmaceutical case asserts flatly: "Under dualism, this does not remove the agent's capacity for free choice. Consciousness still selects among quantum possibilities at the interface." Two sections later the article argues that enhancement "could, in principle, *reduce* the domain of free action" because new pathways may "operate outside wherever consciousness acts." These cannot both stand unqualified: a pharmaceutical that "alters neurotransmitter dynamics" is exactly the kind of microstructural intervention the later section says might relocate or bypass the sites of conscious selection. The confident sentence forecloses a possibility the article itself later opens.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Hedge the pharmaceutical case to match: "Under dualism this need not remove the capacity for free choice — though see below for the caveat that interventions in neural microstructure could in principle narrow the domain where that capacity operates."

### Issue 5: Equity argument rests on an unargued and (corpus-internally) contested premise
- **File**: topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism.md
- **Location**: §Equity and Access, first paragraph
- **Problem**: "If all conscious subjects possess the same fundamental capacity for experience and agency — differing only in the interfaces through which they operate" is stipulated, not argued. No tenet asserts the equality of conscious subjects, and [the phenomenal-variation apex](/apex/phenomenal-variation-within-a-species/) establishes that "one experiential palette per species, varying only in vividness" is empirically inadequate — aphantasia, synaesthesia, and source-attribution spread are documented structural variation. The premise can be saved by booking *all* variation to the interface, but that move is unfalsifiable by construction (see Popper persona) and the article should not lean an equity argument on it silently. Note also the tension is with a sibling article the Map itself publishes.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Reframe the equity claim as conditional and mark the accounting move explicitly: *to whatever extent* observed cognitive inequality is interface-side, dualism makes enhancement-access inequality a matter of unequal instruments for subjects of undetermined — possibly equal — standing; the moral conclusion survives in weakened form without the indefensible universal premise.

### Issue 6: Tenet 4 paragraph misstates decision weight under many-worlds
- **File**: topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism.md
- **Location**: §Relation to Site Perspective, Tenet 4 paragraph
- **Problem**: "enhancement decisions are irreversible in a way they would not be under many-worlds: there is no other branch where you made the opposite choice. The weight of enhancement decisions increases when each person's experiential trajectory is singular." Everettians do not hold that decisions are reversible or weightless: (a) deliberate choices supervening on classical brain states need not branch at all; (b) where branching does occur, Deutsch–Wallace decision theory recovers standard expected-utility weights, so each successor bears their branch's outcome fully. The paragraph attacks a position no careful MWI proponent holds, which is exactly the strawman exposure a hostile outer reviewer would seize on.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Replace the reversibility claim with the defensible indexical point the Map actually holds: under Tenet 4 there is a unique fact about *which* experiential trajectory is yours, so enhancement is a wager with one resolution rather than a portfolio of outcomes — and note that Everettians dispute that this changes decision weights (Wallace 2012).

## Counterarguments to Address

### Materialism has better identity resources than the article grants
- **Current content says**: Under materialist views, "sufficiently radical enhancement might constitute a form of death-and-replacement"; dualism "provides a stronger foundation for personal continuity... than materialism can offer."
- **A critic would argue**: Psychological-continuity theorists handle radical enhancement via gradualism and overlapping chains of connectedness; Parfit argues identity is not what matters, so even a "replacement" verdict carries no ethical sting the theory can't absorb; animalists secure continuity through biological life, untouched by cognitive enhancement. The comparative claim is stated against the weakest materialist option.
- **Suggested response**: Keep the comparative claim but scope it: dualism secures *strict* identity where materialist accounts secure *what-matters* continuity — and argue (rather than assume) that strict identity is the ethically relevant kind for consent and desert.

### The consent asymmetry proves too much
- **Current content says**: Prenatal enhancement is "choosing the parameters of their experiential life" for an unconsulted subject; the parental counterargument is acknowledged but deflected with "the stakes feel different."
- **A critic would argue**: If configuring the interface of an unconsented subject is the wrong-making feature, then *all* parenting — nutrition, language environment, schooling — is equally infected, and the article gives no principled line between impermissible configuration and ordinary upbringing. "The stakes feel different" concedes there is no argument, only an intuition.
- **Suggested response**: Locate the line in *irreversibility during critical periods* (the article already gestures at synaptic pruning via [consciousness-interface-development](/topics/consciousness-interface-development/)) — deliberate, permanent foreclosure of perceptual territories differs in kind from influences the subject can later revise.

### The design-constraint imperative is empirically empty as stated
- **Current content says**: "any enhancement technology should be evaluated... for its effects on the mechanisms through which consciousness interacts with the brain."
- **A critic would argue**: No assay exists or is proposed; a regulator following this imperative could neither approve nor reject anything. An unactionable imperative is rhetoric, not ethics.
- **Suggested response**: Concede the current unactionability explicitly and point to the Map's behavioural proxies ([brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary](/topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary/)'s repurposing-vs-extension evidence; loss-of-agency phenomenology reports) as the interim evaluation surface.
