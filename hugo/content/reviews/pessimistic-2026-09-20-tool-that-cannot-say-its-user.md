### Issue 1: The private-language absorption lands on exactly the reply §293 cancels — and the article's own source says so

- **File**: `obsidian/apex/tool-that-cannot-say-its-user.md`
- **Location**: *The Gap in Meaning Itself*, the two-sentence Wittgenstein paragraph — "it shows that a language *consisting entirely* of private terms is incoherent, and does not show that a public language cannot *refer to* private states. Public criteria get 'pain' going; the private state is what the word is about."
- **Problem**: Three layers, and the third is the one that matters.
  1. The narrowing is wrong about the target. §293's beetle-in-a-box is not about a wholly-private language. It stipulates a word with "a use in these people's language" — a *public* word in a shared language-game — and argues its putative private referent drops out. Verified verbatim from the Anscombe text: "if we construe the grammar of the expression of sensation on the model of 'object and designation' the object drops out of consideration as irrelevant."
  2. The article's reply is precisely the object-and-designation model: public criteria fix the word, the private state is the object it designates. §293 is the section aimed at that move. The article does not engage it.
  3. **The article's own source set already contains the acknowledgement it drops.** `topics/philosophy-of-language-under-dualism` — cited by name two sentences earlier in the same section, and listed in `apex_sources` — states the beetle-in-a-box and then says: "This challenge cuts deep. If phenomenal vocabulary cannot refer to private conscious states, the semantic gap described earlier dissolves." Its dualist response is a *different* move from the apex's (language about consciousness is socially constituted, consciousness itself is not). The apex instead took the beetle-free version from `concepts/language-and-consciousness` (grep for *beetle* / *§293* in that file: 0), kept the phrase "the strongest linguistic challenge" from the source that concedes, and dropped the concession. The result is the apex asserting confident absorption where its own strongest source records a challenge that cuts deep.
- **Severity**: High — the whole semantic flank of the synthesis rests on this paragraph, and the fix is cheap because the material already exists in-corpus.
- **Recommendation**: Restate the challenge at its real strength (a public word whose private referent cancels out, per §293), then absorb using the source's actual move rather than the object-and-designation one, or mark honestly that the Map declines §293's grammatical reading rather than having answered it. ~80–120 words; there is 1259 of headroom.

### Issue 3: The capacity reply is a free parameter presented as a successful prediction — inside the paragraph that charges rivals with epicycles

- **File**: `obsidian/apex/tool-that-cannot-say-its-user.md`
- **Location**: *What the Tool Demands of Its User*, closing — "Conscious animals below the capacity threshold should lack deep recursion, and they do." Reinforced by Synthesis pillar two (*directional dependence*) and by the Synthesis close, "the tool-relation explains [the asymmetry] without epicycles."
- **Problem**: The dissociation objection is the sharpest available evidence against directional dependence: conscious animals lack recursion. The reply adds an auxiliary — consciousness is necessary but not sufficient; expanded working-memory capacity is the other ingredient — and then presents the auxiliary's fit to the data as a confirmed prediction. It cannot be one. Every non-human in the sample is below threshold and lacks recursion; the only above-threshold case is the one species that has recursion. There is no possible disconfirming observation in the available sample, and every rival account (including a purely non-conscious syntactic-machinery difference) predicts the identical distribution. The auxiliary does no independent work; it absorbs the counterexample. This would be an ordinary cost honestly borne, except that the Synthesis eight paragraphs later convicts rivals of needing "epicycles" while the article's own reply to its sharpest objection is one. The prior review flagged the *citation* for the underlying capacity figures and the article duly hedged them ("a contested cross-species comparison"); the structural cost of the reply was never raised.
- **Severity**: High (unfalsifiability of a Synthesis pillar, compounded by an internal inconsistency with the article's own economy claim)
- **Recommendation**: Mark the reply as an auxiliary rather than a prediction — the pattern is *consistent with* the capacity account and does not discriminate it from a non-conscious architectural account — and soften or qualify the "without epicycles" charge in the Synthesis so the article is not applying two standards. Alternatively, state the falsifier Popper keeps naming (a demonstration of unconscious WM *manipulation*), which would give the face a real exposure and earn the prediction language back.

### Issue 4: "Five structurally different materials" — the article names three

- **File**: `obsidian/apex/tool-that-cannot-say-its-user.md`
- **Location**: *Five Ways of Failing* — "each diagnoses a different relation between conscious content and linguistic form: content with dimensions language lacks resolution for, content bound to non-discursive states, content with no linguistic correlate at all" … "A tool that fails in five structurally different ways against five structurally different materials is telling you about the materials." Plus the lead ("at least five phenomenologically distinct ways") and Synthesis pillar three ("five qualitatively different modes").
- **Problem**: The count is load-bearing in three places and the article's own enumeration of the materials yields three, not five. The argument's form requires a one-to-one mapping from failure modes to content types; the gloss supplies a three-way one, which leaves *mismatch register* and *degradation through repetition* unassigned to any distinct material. Compounding this: the five-mode taxonomy carries no empirical or scholarly source anywhere in the corpus — `topics/phenomenology-of-linguistic-failure`'s references are Gendlin, Wittgenstein (*Tractatus*), Nagel, Levine, Chalmers, none of which supply a taxonomy. The modes are the Map's own carving, which is legitimate as phenomenology and not legitimate as a *count* doing evidential work, because a different carving yields three or seven. The article discounts the convergence of the five *faces* via the common-cause null with exemplary care and applies no comparable discount to the granularity of the five *modes*. The lead's "at least five" is the right hedge; it is dropped everywhere the count is actually used.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Either extend the gloss so each mode is mapped to its own material, or drop the numeric framing where it is evidential — the argument survives on *qualitative heterogeneity* without needing the integer. Carry the lead's "at least" into the Synthesis pillar, and mark the taxonomy as the Map's own phenomenological carving rather than a finding.
