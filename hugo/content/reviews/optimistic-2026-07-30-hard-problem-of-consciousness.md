## Verdict on the rivals question: CONFIRMED

The rivals are genuinely engaged, not name-checked. Four dedicated sections — **Russellian Monism** (L199–201), **Analytic Idealism** (L203–205), **The Combination Problem / panpsychism** (L207–218), **Mysterianism** (L220–224) — plus **Epiphenomenalism** (L189) and **IIT** (L191–193), which are irreducibility-respecting in the same sense.

What makes it work is a repeated three-move template. Every rival section runs it:

1. **State the rival in its own terms, and grant what it achieves.** "Analytic idealism *dissolves* the hard problem by making consciousness the sole ontological primitive" (L205); "Panpsychism *avoids* the hard problem by placing experience in fundamental particles" (L209); Russellian monism "proposes consciousness as the intrinsic nature behind physics' relational structure" (L201). The verbs concede success at the thing the rival is trying to do.
2. **Name the rival's characteristic cost** — combination for panpsychism and Russellian monism, law-likeness of appearance for idealism.
3. **State the Map's relation honestly, at its true strength.** This is the move the sibling lacked, and it is done differently in each case:
   - **Parity, not defeat** (L201): *"The required 'grounding laws' are no simpler than dualism's psychophysical laws."* This concedes that Russellian monism is not worse off — a concession running against the Map's rhetorical interest — and it is the only conclusion available given [Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits), which forbids winning on simplicity. An article that wanted to score points here had an obvious and unavailable-to-it move; it declined.
   - **Commitment labelled as commitment** (L205): *"The Map maintains physical reality exists distinctly from consciousness."* "The Map maintains" marks this as a framework commitment rather than an argument. The reader is told which of the three moves is doing evidential work and which is not.
   - **Explicit refusal to claim a refutation** (L189, epiphenomenalism): *"The [phenomenal-concepts-strategy](/concepts/phenomenal-concepts-strategy/) supplies one, which is why the Map presses the bare-correlation version hard without refuting epiphenomenalism from inside its own framework."* The article names the opponent's best reply, grants it works, and states exactly how far the Map's own objection reaches.
   - **Rival welcomed rather than resisted** (L222–224, mysterianism): cognitive closure is a rival that says nobody wins, and the article enrols it as *support* for Tenet 5 rather than treating it as a threat.

**Why this is worth copying.** The sibling's failure was not that it omitted rivals; it was that it had no template for saying what the Map's relation to a rival *is* when the relation is not victory. Sections 1–2 of the template are easy and common across the corpus. Section 3 is the scarce part, and this article demonstrates four distinct honest values for it — parity, labelled commitment, bounded objection, welcomed rival. That inventory is the transferable artifact.

### The symmetric-burden table (L211–218)

The four-row table — Physicalism / Panpsychism / Idealism / Dualism, each with a Core Challenge — is the structural device that makes the rival engagement honest rather than dismissive. **The Map puts its own framework in the table** with an unanswered question in the same column as everyone else's ("How do distinct substances causally connect?"), then L236 cross-references the table from a dedicated **"Open Problems for the Map's Framework"** section: *"These are acknowledged as open questions, not dismissed—the combination problem table in the previous section makes clear that every framework carries an analogous burden."*

The table device is near-unique in the corpus: only [concepts/emergence.md](/concepts/emergence/) L136 carries a comparable one, and its version is three rows without the Map's own burden as a peer entry.
