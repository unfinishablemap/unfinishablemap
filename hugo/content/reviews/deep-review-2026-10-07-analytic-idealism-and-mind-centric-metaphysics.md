## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **IIT misstated (L60)** — "any system with non-zero integrated information has some form of experience". IIT's exclusion postulate confines experience to a *local maximum* of Φ; the subsystems and supersets of a complex have non-zero Φ and no experience. Verified at the publisher of record (Tononi & Koch 2015, *Phil Trans B* 370:20140167, PMC4387509: the cause–effect structure is "specified over a single set of elements—neither less nor more—the one over which it is maximally irreducible"; the union of two complexes is "not maximally irreducible" and has no superordinate experience). Three sibling pages state the postulate correctly (`quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity` L74, `consciousness-and-collective-phenomena` L66, `concession-convergence` L57); this page and `consciousness-and-the-physics-of-information` L68 carried the false version. **Fixed**: → "any system forming a local maximum of integrated information, however simple, has some form of experience; its exclusion postulate withholds experience from the parts and supersets of such a maximum".
- **Cited-author stance (L60)** — IIT was filed as a "variety" of panpsychism with no note that its authors decline the label. Same source: "IIT was not developed with panpsychism in mind", IIT "clearly implies that not everything is conscious", while "in line with the central intuitions of panpsychism, IIT treats consciousness as an intrinsic, fundamental property of reality". **Fixed**: added "which is why Tononi and Koch decline the panpsychist label even while treating consciousness as a fundamental property". The "converge on refusing emergence" verdict is retained since the source endorses the fundamental-property reading.

### Medium Issues Found

- **Meillassoux sentence (L86, the 10-05 insertion)** — "On Meillassoux's definitions, as the Map applies them to itself, analytic idealism is a *subjectalism* that absolutises thought". The hedge was mis-aimed (the definition is being applied to Kastrup, not to the Map), and "absolutises thought" narrowed Meillassoux's own gloss as the Map quotes it on `correlationism-and-the-ancestrality-argument` L46 ("absolutizing thought, or certain of its characteristics", covering "all forms of idealism"); Kastrup absolutises phenomenal consciousness, not thought. **Fixed**: → "analytic idealism is a *subjectalism*: it absolutises the mental term of the subject–object correlation. The Map is neither that nor a correlationism … Meillassoux does not discuss Kastrup; the classification is the Map's own". Source/Map separation is now explicit.
- **Style (L64)** — "This is not merely difficult; it threatens to be …" is the flagged "This is not X. It is Y." construct. **Fixed**: → "The difficulty threatens to be as intractable as …".
