## Details

### (1) Experimental bounds: current? correctly characterised?

**What the article says.** The "Experimental Stakes" section states: "*The most-quoted verdict on GRW dates from 2022, not from the present. Reporting the Gran Sasso germanium-detector null result of 2020 and its confirmation that August by the Majorana Demonstrator, Ball found the original GRW model lying 'just within this tight window: It survived by a whisker' (Ball, 2022). The simplest Diósi-Penrose model was ruled out by the same experiments, though Barbatti's (2024) atomistic refinements have opened new channels for gravity-collapse testing.*" [topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse/)

**Assessment.**

- *The Diósi–Penrose ruling-out is correct in substance but mis-sourced.* The parameter-free DP model was ruled out by Donadi, Piscicchia, Curceanu, Diósi, Laubenstein & Bassi, "Underground test of gravity-related wave function collapse," *Nature Physics* 17(1), 74–78 (2021) (DOI 10.1038/s41567-020-1008-4; arXiv:2111.13490), whose abstract states the result "rules out the natural parameter-free version of the Diósi–Penrose model." [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13490) The article correctly restricts the claim to the *parameter-free* version (its linked companion page notes DP survives for larger R₀), which is the right nuance. But it never cites this paper — see (2).
- *The Majorana claim is imprecise and lacks the erratum.* The primary paper is Arnquist et al. (Majorana Collaboration), "Search for Spontaneous Radiation from Wave Function Collapse in the Majorana Demonstrator," *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 129, 080401 [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.05507) (published 16 August 2022). It carries an **Erratum, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 130, 239902 (2023)**, correcting a typographical error in the DP limit; the corrected 95% C.L. lower bound is R_DP > (2.54 ± 0.03)×10⁻¹⁰ m, with the CSL limits unaffected and the DP limit remaining "the most stringent" as of that publication. An article last modified in September 2026 that leans on this result should cite the corrected value, not a journalist's summary.
- *The currency hedge is the substantive error: the article understates current exclusions.* The article frames "whether the whisker-width margin survives the intervening results" as unsettled. It is now largely settled against the original parameters. Aprile et al. (XENON Collaboration), "Challenging Spontaneous Quantum Collapse with the XENONnT Dark Matter Detector," *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 136, 120201 (published 23 March 2026; arXiv:2506.05507), reports "**New world-leading limits on the free parameters of the Markovian continuous spontaneous localization and Diósi-Penrose models are set, improving previous best constraints by two orders of magnitude and a factor of five, respectively,**" [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05507) and states flatly that "**the original values proposed for the strength and the correlation length of the continuous spontaneous localization model are excluded experimentally.**" [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05507) The concrete numbers are λ/r_C² < 3.0×10⁻³ s⁻¹m⁻² for CSL and R₀ > 4.9×10⁻¹⁰ m at 90% C.L. for DP — the latter "an improvement by approximately a factor of five compared to the previous world-leading limit by the Majorana Demonstrator," and constituting "the first-time exclusion of the commonly assumed values (r_C^GRW, λ^GRW) for a white noise field." This is decisive for the article's thesis and is not obscure: the site's own changelog references XENONnT/arXiv:2506.05507 in adjacent work, and Kristian Piscicchia (lead of the companion VIP prediction paper) is quoted in Phys.org ("Testing quantum collapse theory with the XENONnT dark matter detector," May 2026) saying "*For CSL we are more than a hundred times more sensitive than previously leading experiments.*" [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-05-quantum-collapse-theory-xenonnt-dark.html) The article's "survived by a whisker" framing, carried from 2022, is now misleading without the 2026 update.
- *Other current constraints the article ignores.* Piscicchia et al., "X-ray emission from atomic systems can distinguish between prevailing dynamical wave-function collapse models," *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 132, 250203 (18 June 2024) [APS Journals](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.132.250203) — directly relevant to the article's claim that different collapse mechanisms make divergent predictions; Altamura, Vinante & Carlesso, "Improved bounds on collapse models from rotational noise of the LISA Pathfinder mission," *Phys. Rev. A* 111, L020203 (2025); [APS Journals](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/g7xn-bwfs) Figurato et al., "On the effectiveness of the collapse in the Diósi–Penrose model," *New J. Phys.* 26, 113004 (2024). [APS Journals](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/g7xn-bwfs) The cantilever/optomechanical program (Vinante et al.) and cold-atom bounds are likewise unmentioned in the body.
- *Barbatti is oversold.* The article says Tomaz, Mattos & Barbatti (2024) "*have opened new channels for gravity-collapse testing.*" The paper (verified: *Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys.* 26(31), 20785–20798, 2024, DOI 10.1039/D4CP02364A) is substantially *critical* of DP: it flags "challenges from an atomistic perspective, such as gravitational self-energy saturation and limited extensivity," [ChemRxiv](https://chemrxiv.org/engage/chemrxiv/article-details/6652e78a21291e5d1d4c53dc) and finds DP would let a 1 kg iron superposition survive ~1 s [ChemRxiv](https://chemrxiv.org/engage/api-gateway/chemrxiv/assets/orp/resource/item/6697610a5101a2ffa89b4b57/original/manuscript_rev.pdf) — i.e., surprisingly *weak* collapse for macroscopic systems, a difficulty as much as an opportunity. "Opened new channels" cherry-picks the optimistic reading.
- *Colored/dissipative and Adler-vs-GRW handling is adequate but thin.* Reference #11 (Carlesso & Donadi, arXiv:2508.18822, 2025) genuinely covers "the colored and dissipative generalization of such models and their experimental tests," [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18822) so the article has a fair basis for its passing claim there. It does not, however, distinguish Adler's preferred CSL parameter region (larger λ, more strongly constrained) from the original GRW–Pearle values — which matters precisely for the "survived by a whisker" claim.

**Net:** the article overstates DP's testing prospects (Barbatti), understates current CSL/DP exclusions (missing XENONnT 2026), and mis-sources its two anchor results.

### (3) No-collapse alternatives

**Credit where due.** This is the article's strongest suit relative to its genre. It states up front that "*no-collapse alternatives like Bohmian mechanics [are] empirically equivalent at current precision*"; [topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse/) it devotes a paragraph to conceding that "*the consequences below follow from the collapse reading, but that reading is not itself forced by the data... The stakes the Map draws are therefore conditional on an interpretive choice the evidence does not yet decide*"; [topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse/) and it explicitly notes that a collapse null result "*leaves [QBism] untouched, having never posited an objective state to collapse.*" [topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse/) It also states the failure mode: "*if falsified, the Map must find alternative ground.*" [topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse/) That is honest and unusual, and it passes the key test — the article does acknowledge that if a no-collapse interpretation is correct, its stakes framing collapses.

**But engagement is thin and asymmetric.**

- *Bohmian mechanics* is invoked only for empirical equivalence. The article never notes that Bohm is fully deterministic and no-collapse — which directly undercuts its own repeated argument that collapse supplies "*the openness that libertarian free will requires.*" [topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse/) If Bohm is live (as the article concedes), the "stochastic openness" selling point is not a stake at all; it is interpretation-relative. This is a merits-level objection the article owes an answer to and does not give.
- *Everett/many-worlds* is not engaged on the merits here; it is dispatched by the "No Many Worlds" tenet and a link to the probability-problem page. Asserting a tenet is not arguing against a rival.
- *Decoherence-only* readings are characterised as delivering mere "*practical inaccessibility*" rather than genuine definiteness — a fair Bell/GRW-style point, but contestable (a Wallace-style Everett-plus-decoherence view rejects the framing) and stated without the counter.
- *Relational QM (Rovelli)* — a leading no-collapse, observer-relative interpretation squarely relevant to a "does consciousness matter to physics" article — is absent entirely.

**Bottom line:** the article passes the honesty test (conceding the conditional) but fails the secondary test of giving merits-based reasons against the rivals rather than bracketing them behind tenets.

### (4) Chalmers–McQueen: rendered accurately?

**What the article says.** "*The most fully worked hybrid of this kind is the consciousness-modulated CSL model developed by David Chalmers and Kelvin McQueen... Their proposal combines CSL's continuous collapse mechanism with Integrated Information Theory (IIT): differences in integrated information (Φ) between superposed conscious states determine collapse rates, so systems with higher integrated information experience faster and stronger collapse.*" [topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse/)

**Assessment against the primary text** (Chalmers & McQueen, "Consciousness and the Collapse of the Wave Function," arXiv:2105.02314 / Gao ed., OUP 2022; verified against the consc.net full text):

- **The mechanism is misattributed to Φ-magnitude.** Chalmers & McQueen *reject* scalar Φ as the collapse basis: "*The underlying problem is that Φ is not a genuine physical correlate of consciousness... It is merely a physical correlate of a scalar degree of consciousness.*" [consc](https://www.consc.net/papers/collapse.pdf) Their worked model instead makes the collapse *rate* depend on the **Earth Mover's Distance between the Q-shapes** of the superposed states: "*we can make the rate of collapse of a superposition of two Q-shapes Q1 and Q2 depend on the extended Earth movers' distance... This ensures that larger superpositions will tend to collapse faster.*" [consc](https://www.consc.net/papers/collapse.pdf) The driver is *distance between conscious states*, not "higher integrated information → faster and stronger collapse." The article's rendering is precisely the scalar-Φ reading C&M went out of their way to abandon; "stronger collapse" has no clear counterpart in their dynamics.
- **The Born-rule/outcome-selection point is the deep problem.** C&M's default model preserves Born statistics for *which* outcome occurs; consciousness affects *timing/rate*, not the selection. They concede this under their own "Objection 4: Does consciousness just roll the dice?": "*if consciousness always collapses via the Born r[u]le, then any effect of consciousness on action will at best be a sort of dice-rolling role. It will probabilistically select between different available outcomes, but it will not yield a qualitatively special outcome*" [consc](https://www.consc.net/papers/collapse.pdf) — and respond, "*we are inclined to concede that most of what this objector says is correct.*" [consc](https://www.consc.net/papers/collapse.pdf) The article's flagship position, by contrast, is that "*phenomenal properties bias which outcome the process delivers.*" [topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse/) That outcome-biasing move corresponds to C&M's explicitly *non-preferred* add-on: "*collapses due to agentive experience might be biased in such a way that more 'intelligent' choices... tend to be favored than they would be according to the Born rule... We do not find this picture especially attractive, but it is at least worth putting it onto the table.*" [consc](https://www.consc.net/papers/collapse.pdf) The article never tells the reader that its central mechanism is the one C&M set aside.
- **Partial credit.** The article correctly observes that the hybrid's rate-modulation is "*visible in the statistics by design*" and therefore "*parts company with the Map's default reading, which holds aggregate Born statistics exactly ([P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2)).*" [topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse/) That is a genuine and correct distinction. But it leaves the impression that the *only* difference is rate-vs-outcome, when in fact C&M's outcome selection is *also* Born-preserving, so the Map's "bias outcomes while preserving Born statistics exactly" is in tension not just with the hybrid but with the whole family — which is exactly the bias-without-deviation dilemma the article books elsewhere ([P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3), mechanism-debt). C&M's Objection 4 concession is the strongest available statement of that dilemma and should be cited where the article raises it.
- **Taxonomic mislabelling.** The article files C&M under "consciousness-modulated collapse (the Map's position)." But C&M frame their project as a development of the von Neumann–Wigner idea ("*Does consciousness collapse the quantum wave function? This idea was taken seriously by John von Neumann and Eugene Wigner*"), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02314) and on their view Q-shape/consciousness *is* the collapse basis rather than a modulator of an independent process. They stress they are "*exploring... rather than endorsing*" [consc](https://www.consc.net/papers/collapse.pdf) the view and offer a fully *materialist* version ("*Φ causes collapse... it involves nothing nonphysical*"), which sits awkwardly with the article's use of them as a worked instance of dualist modulation. "The consciousness-modulated CSL model developed by" C&M overstates both their commitment and the fit with the Map's category.
- **The Zeno problem is invisible.** C&M's central technical hurdle is the quantum Zeno problem ("*if consciousness or its physical correlate was a superselection observable... we could never wake up from a nap*"), [consc](https://www.consc.net/papers/collapse.pdf) solved via "approximate super-resistance" using CSL-style continuous localization. The article cites C&M repeatedly but never mentions the Zeno problem — even though it separately praises Stapp's quantum-Zeno *approach* as a fallback, which risks leaving readers unaware that the Zeno effect is a *problem* for consciousness-collapse, not merely a resource.

### (5) Omitted counterarguments

The article books some debts honestly ([P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2) Born-preservation, [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3) bias-without-deviation, [P-Q10](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q10) no worked toy model). But the following major objections are absent from the body:

- **Energy non-conservation vs. the article's own causal-closure claim.** The article asserts, in "Bidirectional Interaction," that consciousness "*bias[es] which outcome becomes actual rather than overriding the probabilities, leaving causal closure intact at the level of conservation laws.*" [topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse/) This is exactly the disputed point. GRW/CSL are known to violate energy conservation (the localization-induced heating the article elsewhere treats as a *test signature*), and Cucu, "Does Consciousness-Collapse Quantum Mechanics Facilitate Dualistic Mental Causation?" *Journal of Cognitive Science* 21(3), 429–473 (2020), argues directly that "*energy and momentum are probably not conserved in collapse processes,*" [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/CUCDCQ) so the closure-preserving hope is "premature." For a page defending interactionist dualism, asserting closure-intact without confronting this literature (incl. Cucu & Pitts, *Mind and Matter* 17(1), 95–121, 2019) [PhilArchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/CUCTTT) is a significant gap.
- **Neural decoherence timescales (Tegmark).** Nowhere does the article address Tegmark, "Importance of quantum decoherence in brain processes," *Phys. Rev. E* 61, 4194 (1 April 2000), which finds "*the decoherence time scales (∼10⁻¹³–10⁻²⁰ s) are typically much shorter than the relevant dynamical time scales (∼10⁻³–10⁻¹ s), both for regular neuron firing and for kinklike polarization excitations in microtubules.*" This is the standard objection to any quantum-brain interaction. Even to rebut it (via Hagan, Hameroff & Tuszynski, *Phys. Rev. E* 65, 061901, 2002, who recompute ~10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵ s), the article must engage it; a page about consciousness modulating collapse *in neural systems* cannot simply omit the decoherence problem.
- **The tails problem.** GRW/CSL leave residual "tails"; McQueen (the article's own cited author), "Four tails problems for dynamical collapse theories," *Stud. Hist. Phil. Mod. Phys.* 49, 10–18 (2015), [Chapman University](https://digitalcommons.chapman.edu/philosophy_articles/22/) argues the *structured* tails problem is unsolved and renders GRW ontology "Everettian in disguise." [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.13706) The article's confident claim that objective collapse yields "*genuinely definite states*" [topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse/) (vs. decoherence/Everett) is precisely what the tails problem contests. Unmentioned.
- **Relativity / Lorentz invariance.** The article discusses the flash ontology (Bell, Tumulka) approvingly but omits the standing problem that CSL and DP lack satisfactory relativistic formulations, and that even Tumulka's rGRWf ("A Relativistic Version of the Ghirardi–Rimini–Weber Model," *J. Stat. Phys.* 125, 825–844, 2006; interacting version, 2021) is Lorentz-invariant only at the level of entire flash distributions, with no relativistic account of individual becoming [PhilSci-Archive](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/10058/1/GRWf-1013.pdf) — directly relevant to the article's "Time acquires directionality" claims.
- **Collapse theories don't need consciousness (the "quantum zombie" objection).** The article's own picture #1 concedes GRW/CSL run mindlessly. It never confronts the consequence C&M raise as their Objection 2: a physicalist can let the *physical* correlate (physical Q-shape) do all the collapse work — "*Collapse of the PCC states does all the causal work, and collapse of consciousness is causally irrelevant.*" This is the sharpest objection to the whole modulation program and it is omitted.
- **Interaction problem / Kim's causal exclusion / the pairing problem.** For an interactionist-dualist thesis, the absence of any engagement with Kim's exclusion argument or the pairing problem (how does a non-physical mind couple to *this* brain's quantum state rather than another's?) is a notable gap — especially acute for a "biasing" mechanism, which needs a pairing relation to specify *whose* phenomenology biases *which* collapse.
- **The "empirically idle" horn in the literature's own terms.** The article's bias-without-deviation dilemma is correct but under-cited; it should acknowledge that "biasing a stochastic process without changing its statistics" is, by construction, either a detectable Born-rule deviation or causally idle-in-effect — the exact fork C&M concede and Cucu presses.

## Recommendations
