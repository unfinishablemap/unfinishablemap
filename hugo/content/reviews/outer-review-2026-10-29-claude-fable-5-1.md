# Hostile Referee Report: “Where the Substance Commitment Enters” (unfinishablemap.org/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

**Verdict: DEMOTE-TO-COHERENCE-ONLY.** The article’s thesis is that the substance commitment enters “below the tenet” through agent causation and the indexical objection. It fails on both routes. Agent causation needs a persisting *substance*, not a *mental* substance. The indexical objection is not a route by which the commitment enters: it is Tenet 4’s own primary rationale, and it draws on a subject the site posits elsewhere. On the site’s own record the commitment enters nowhere by argument. It is posited in the background-commitments note, and this page misdescribes where it is used as if it were where it comes from.

### 2.1 The tenet-neutrality claim

The article opens by saying the Dualism tenet is neutral and that the lean arrives through “two commitments made below the tenet”. [where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) Taken alone, the first half is true: the tenet’s Definition does say it is neutral between substance and property dualism. [tenets](/tenets/) The second half is false, and the falsehood is what makes the page work.

1. **Route (b) is not below the tenets.** The indexical objection is the argument that, in the tenets page’s own words, “carries the tenet’s weight” for No Many Worlds. [tenets](/tenets/) A commitment that a *stated tenet’s* main rationale depends on enters at tenet level. Calling it “below the tenet” only works by reading “the tenet” as Tenet 1 alone. That is **tenet leakage** run backwards: weight drains out of one tenet into another, and the article audits only the one that was drained.
2. **Posit One is not neutral between the dualisms.** Background-commitments defines primitive subject individuation as a fact holding “prior to and independent of any physical or functional criterion”. [background-commitments](/tenets/background-commitments/) A property dualist individuates subjects by their bearer, the organism or brain. A subject individuated independently of every physical criterion is either a non-physical particular or a haecceity, and the site’s own `concepts/haecceity` page says the indexical rejection of many-worlds “presupposes haecceitism”. [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/) So the five-tenet package rules out ordinary property dualism wherever Tenet 4 runs. The neutrality survives only in the Definition paragraph.
3. **Zimmerman (2010) is in the reference list and goes unanswered on exactly this point.** Zimmerman’s paper argues that property dualism is unstable and pushes toward emergent substance dualism. [deep-review-2026-09-20-where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/reviews/deep-review-2026-09-20-where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) His abstract asks “what material objects could be the bearers of the qualia?” [wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-8349.2010.00189.x) If he is right, Tenet 1’s neutrality is unstable at the tenet level, before agency or Everett come into it at all. The article cites him only as an example of “naturalistic substance dualism”. [where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) That is a **performative inoculation citation**: the source most damaging to the page’s central claim is on the list, and the page never engages what it says.
4. **Coherence inflation in the aspect reading.** The article says the tenet also admits an aspect reading “on which the Map builds its ontology of structure and actuality”. [where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) So the Map builds on an aspect ontology, runs agency and Everett on a substance-grade subject, and calls the tenet neutral, all on one page and with no account of how the three fit together.

**Assessment:** the neutrality claim is honest about a sentence and dishonest about a system. The background-commitments page already admits that “five *independent* commitments” is imprecise. [background-commitments](/tenets/background-commitments/) The article banks that admission and keeps the neutral headline. That is **confession-without-correction**.

### 2.2 Does agent causation require a persisting substance?

Split the claim in two. **(i) Agent-causal views need a persisting substance-agent:** true by definition for Chisholm, O’Connor, Clarke and Lowe. **(ii) That substance must be, or lean toward being, a mental substance:** false. Only (ii) does work for the Map, and the article’s evidence supports only (i).

- **Property-dualism strawman.** The article defines the property-only rival as mental properties supervening “without any substance bearing them”. [where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) No standard property dualist holds this: the bearer is the organism or brain, which is a persisting substance. The `topics/free-will` page admits as much when it lists “or substance-bearing property dualism” as satisfying the requirement. [free-will](/topics/free-will/) Once that disjunct is allowed, “substance-leaning” no longer separates anything from anything.
- **O’Connor (2000) refutes the inference, and the article cites him.** In *Persons and Causes* and his survey chapter, O’Connor treats agent-causal power as ontologically emergent “while still being powers and properties of the biological organism”. [newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/T.OConnor/OConnor-OHOFW2001-Dualist_AC_Theories.pdf)[toconnor](https://toconnor.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/OConnorAgentCausalTheories11.pdf) That is property dualism with substance monism. [newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/T.OConnor/OConnor-OHOFW2001-Dualist_AC_Theories.pdf) The Further Reading gloss enlists O’Connor in the tradition that “grounds the persisting-subject requirement”. [where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) He does ground it, but in a physical organism. This is a **co-optation firewall failure**.
- **Steward (2012)** gives agent-causal settling to whole animals, and the OUP description of *A Metaphysics for Freedom* (2012) calls her libertarianism “resolutely non-dualistic”. The 2026-10-22 clause concedes that this makes the step to the non-physical depend on irreducibility. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)[where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) But irreducibility is Tenet 1, which the article says is neutral. So with the Steward clause in place, the article supplies **no** route from agent causation to a non-physical substance. The lead, the meta description and the diagnostic table were not changed to match. That is confession-without-correction at the level of a single edit.
- **Lowe trivialises the requirement.** On Lowe’s view all causation is substance causation, so a falling rock needs a persisting substance too. [agent-causation](/concepts/agent-causation/) A requirement every inanimate cause meets cannot select substance dualism. Lowe *is* a non-Cartesian substance dualist, but on independent grounds, not because agency needs a bearer. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._J._Lowe_(philosopher))[oup](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/personal-agency-9780199217144) Two more problems: the site’s own 10-20 review files Lowe as a “border case” for agent-causal classification, and the Map denies Lowe’s uncaused-volition premise without answering the regress Lowe gives for it. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)[deep-review-2026-10-20-event-causal-libertarianism](/reviews/deep-review-2026-10-20-event-causal-libertarianism/) The article still says Lowe “reaches the same requirement”. [where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)
- **Clarke (2003)** is missing from the article and is the most dangerous omission. He builds the strongest integrated agent-causal account in the literature and then, in *Libertarian Accounts of Free Will* (OUP; ch. 10, pp. 196–209), concludes “we should find the substance causation required by any agent-causal account to be impossible”. An article claiming agent causation as the Map’s entry point for substance has to deal with the leading agent-causal theorist’s verdict that the required substance causation cannot exist.
- **Event-causal libertarianism (Kane, Ekstrom, Mele’s modest libertarianism).** These authors deliver authorship through indeterministic reasons-causation by a psychologically continuous agent. [agent-causation-libertarian-free-will-2026-01-14](/research/agent-causation-libertarian-free-will-2026-01-14/) The article dismisses this rival with a single clause (“event causation under another name”), which assumes [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2). The site’s register scores [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2) *“no independent evidence”*. [agency-and-will](/positions/agency-and-will/)[where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) Using [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2) to settle the matter **begs the question**.
- **No-self and bundle accounts (Parfit, Siderits, Garfield, Metzinger, Strawson, Ganeri).** The article runs together two different things:

- *synchronic* persistence across a single causing, which takes milliseconds to seconds;
- *diachronic* further-fact persistence across a life.

A Strawsonian SESMET, a thin subject that Strawson says is wholly physical and short-lived, persists across one act of causing. [philpapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/STRTSA-22) Ganeri’s owner/agent self and Siderits’s Buddhist reductionism give a functional agent without a further fact. Metzinger’s self-model theory explains why a substance *seems* to be present. Diachronic further-fact persistence is needed only by the desert and self-creation row of the diagnostic, not by agent causation as such. Yet the article’s heading says agent causation itself needs the persisting subject. The `pudgalavada` link points to a Buddhist precedent, but the body never engages the Vasubandhu-style refutations of the pudgala.

**Assessment:** the “requires” claim holds for a persisting substance-agent and fails for a substance-*dualist* subject. The article quietly moves from “substance-causal” to “substance-dualist”.

### 2.3 The diagnostic

The diagnostic is billed as “a single question”, but it is a lookup table whose verdicts are fixed in advance.

- **It fails its own test case.** The article admits the single question returns *no* for the indexical row, but the verdict is “substance-leaning” anyway. [where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) A test that has to be overruled on one of its six rows does not discriminate between cases. The rows decide the outcome, and the rows were written to agree with the posit.
- **Row 3 is circular.** “A real further fact fixes which branch... is *me*” → “needs persisting subject”. A further fact of personal identity just *is* a persisting-subject fact, so the row tells us nothing.
- **Rows 1 and 5 differ only in wording.** “The agent *originates*” (substance) versus “mental states cause physical effects at quantum indeterminacies” (no substance) mark different metaphysics only if the article says what the agent adds beyond its mental states. It never does. The work is again done by [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2), which has no independent evidence.
- **The third failure mode forbids argument.** “Inferred subject” says the fix for arguing from felt unity or mine-ness is “cite the posit instead”. [where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) That turns a known gap in evidence (the Kantian paralogism point) into a rule against offering evidence. This is the **constitutional-attractor effect** in pure form: the framework protects its foundation by banning attempts to support it, so the foundation can never be tested or lose credence.
- **The constrain-versus-establish gate is not applied.** The table assigns which readings an argument *needs*. Nowhere does the page say that the posit is not *supported* by any of the arguments that need it. A reader sees six rows and two “homes” and gets **coherence inflation**: the more places the posit is used, the more secure it looks, though the evidence for it has not changed.

### 2.4 The indexical argument against many-worlds

The article (as amended 2026-09-19) presents the indexical objection as a second “home” that needs a determinate persisting “I” but no causal power. [where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)[deep-review-2026-09-20-where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/reviews/deep-review-2026-09-20-where-the-substance-commitment-enters/) On its own terms the objection is unsound or begs the question in four ways.

1. **It equivocates between cheap and expensive indexicality.** Self-locating questions such as “which branch am I on?” are well-formed in centred-world semantics (Lewis 1979) with no further-fact subject. Saunders and Wallace (2008) argue, using Lewis’s treatment of personal fission, that before branching “speakers cannot say in advance which branch or world is theirs”. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/36445018_Saunders_and_Wallace_on_Everett_and_Lewis)[philpapers](https://philpapers.org/s/Simon%20Saunders) So before measurement the question is ordinary uncertainty, and after it the question is answered by observation. Sebens and Carroll (2018) formalise post-measurement self-locating uncertainty. [background-commitments](/tenets/background-commitments/) Vaidman’s sleeping-pill device makes the same point. The tenets page replies that these accounts “presuppose the very centred subject”. [tenets](/tenets/) They presuppose a centred *perspective*, which every theory, collapse theories included, also needs, and not a primitive, physically unconstrained individual. The objection gets its force by trading one notion for the other: an **epistemic-to-metaphysical slide**.
2. **It proves too much (the fission test).** The same question, “which successor is *me*?”, comes up in a single classical world with teletransporter branch-lines, Parfit’s duplication cases and split brains. The Map’s own register, [P-I4](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i4), answers it: fission “start[s] new subjects rather than sharing an old one”. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/) Apply [P-I4](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i4) to Everett branching: the pre-branch subject ends and two new subjects begin, one per branch, and each can truly say “I am on *this* branch”. That is a coherent many-subjects Everett, built from the Map’s own fission verdict. The Map can block it only by appeal to Posit Three, global exclusion of unchosen alternatives. Background-commitments admits that posit is asserted, not argued, and that Wilson (2020) and the diverging-worlds reading reject it without internal error. [background-commitments](/tenets/background-commitments/)
3. **A persisting non-physical subject is compatible with many-worlds.** Albert and Loewer’s single-mind and many-minds interpretations (“Interpreting the Many Worlds Interpretation”, *Synthese* 77: 195–213, 1988) give each observer a non-physically individuated mind that follows exactly one branch. On that view there *is* a fact about which branch I am on, and the wavefunction still never collapses. So a persisting, determinate, non-physical subject neither requires nor secures single-world actuality. The second “home” cannot carry the anti-Everett conclusion even if the posit is granted. A dualist site should have seen this first.
4. **It presupposes what it is used to diagnose.** The article does not claim the indexical objection *establishes* the persisting subject. It says the objection *draws on* one, which background-commitments posits. [where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)[background-commitments](/tenets/background-commitments/) So the “second home” is a place where the commitment is *used*, not a place where it *enters*. Calling it an entry point is the article’s central category mistake. It also undercuts the double-counting guard the article praises itself for: if both homes only consume a posit, there is no evidence to double-count, only one posit used twice.

**Other relevant literature.** None of the following is engaged in the article. Of these, only Lewis 2004 and Albert–Loewer are even partly sympathetic.

- **Deutsch, Wallace and Greaves’s decision-theoretic programme.** On Greaves’s fission view there is no uncertainty at all, and rational care-measures replace the indexical question.
- **Lewis (2004), “How Many Lives Has Schrödinger’s Cat?”** This is a *probability* objection, which Papineau answered in the same AJP issue, not an indexical one. [sas](https://sas-space.sas.ac.uk/888/)
- **Tegmark.** Treats branch-observers as physically individuated substructures.
- **Wilson (2020).** Indexical actuality: each Everett world is actual only to its inhabitants. [oup](https://academic.oup.com/pq/article-abstract/71/2/444/5850506) This meets “nonactual alternatives” without collapse. [background-commitments](/tenets/background-commitments/)

**Assessment:** sound only as a statement of a framework-boundary posit. As an argument it equivocates, proves too much, and does not engage the dualist many-minds option.
