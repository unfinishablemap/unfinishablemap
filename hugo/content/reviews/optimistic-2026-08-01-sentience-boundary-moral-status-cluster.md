## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`fish-sentience` L57–L63 is the cleanest piece of gap-work in the cluster. It reconstructs the skeptics' inference into three steps, concedes the first two outright ("The second is neuroanatomical and undisputed — fish really do lack a laminated neocortex"), and locates the whole dispute in the move to the conclusion: that felt pain is *constituted by* the cortical architecture correlating with it in humans. Then the diagnosis that earns the section — **"'structure determines *function*' is unobjectionable engineering… 'structure determines *feeling*' is a much stronger claim, and the slide from the first to the second is where a metaphysical commitment enters disguised as a bioengineering principle."** Key's own premise, in his own words, turned into an exhibit of the explanatory gap.

What makes it Chalmers-grade rather than merely correct is L63, which refuses the easy win: a functionalist skeptic does not rest on identity, dualism blocks that route *differently*, and against the functionalist the tenet "does not settle the matter so much as relocate it" — two opponents, two rebuttals, and only the first dissolving outright.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

`brain-organoids` L76 is the best short statement in the corpus of Tenet 2 as a *constraint* rather than an ornament. Minimality is worked in both directions in one paragraph: it "offers no reason to expect an interface to 'switch on' merely because neuron count or oscillatory complexity crosses a threshold," and it "equally forbids confident denial: if the relevant physical property is genuinely minimal, its presence or absence is precisely *not* what behavioural competence or an oscillation trace would reveal." A tenet that only ever licensed the conclusion the framework wanted would be doing no work; this one cuts against the Map's own convenience and the article says so. The scope discipline around it earns a mention too: L30 routes the substrate physics to `ai-hardware-substrate-taxonomy` rather than re-deriving it, and L76 then flags parenthetically that it is *relying* on that premise — "the scope note excludes re-deriving it, not relying on it."

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Two loci, the same move at opposite ends of the ladder. `brain-organoids` L52: real cortical neurons, real synapses, real oscillations, "but no body to act with, no world the activity is *about*, and no biography" — the organoid as the exact inverse of the sponge case. And `sentientism` L54, closing the Feinberg argument: **"A system with a direction but no perspective offers nothing to be represented, only a description to be reported."** The "what is it like" question converted into a criterion of moral standing without losing anything in the conversion.

`sentientism` L58 then separates the datum from the explanation: Korsgaard's "it is a pain to be in pain. And that is not a trivial fact" states the datum, with the immediate note that "she grounds it in a Kantian account of the reflexive structure of valuing rather than in the phenomenal realism the Map defends — the datum is shared; the explanation of it is not." Borrowing a philosopher's observation without conscripting her conclusion.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

`sentientism` L50 states the biocentrist's case better than a hostile summary would: "A sunflower turning toward light, a bacterium repairing its membrane, a seedling extending roots toward water — each is a system with a direction, a set of conditions under which it does well and another under which it does badly. Sentience adds nothing to that good; it adds only someone to whom it is apparent. Why should the moral weight attach to the awareness rather than to the good itself?" L54 volunteers a further concession against the Map's own position: a plant "is no mere thing," having exactly the "latent tendencies, directions of growth, and natural fulfillments" Feinberg's list denies to stones.

The steelmanning is genuine and the process-philosophical sympathy is real. **Per this persona's constraint, none of it is used to move any organism up the five-tier scale, and the article does not attempt it.** The reply is argumentative (interests require conations; conation requires a subject) and its reductio is empirical rather than metaphysical: L56's HIV case, following Williams and O'Neill via the Stanford entry — the virus's "replication conditions are as determinate as a sunflower's," so if having a good of one's own generates a duty, the duty extends to the virus. The biocentrist can restrict the class, "but every principled restriction reaches for something the sentientist already has: mattering to someone." That is what a process-friendly framework is supposed to produce instead of a tier-upgrade.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`ethics-of-consciousness-invertebrate-question` L87 keeps moral *patiency* and moral *agency* strictly apart: invertebrates are patients toward whom obligations run without being agents who bear them, because under the Map's framework moral responsibility requires conscious agent-causation. Better than asserting the distinction, the section puts weight on it — L89 concedes that extending patiency to trillions makes "the total weight of moral obligation… staggering," and L91 answers with the responsibility gradient rather than by quietly shrinking the patient class: obligations scale with the agent's capacity to attend to them, so "the difference between accidentally stepping on an ant and building a factory farm for crickets is morally relevant." The agency side of the ledger does the limiting work, which is where a libertarian would want it done.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`fish-sentience` L73 is the model closing paragraph: "Neither tenet licenses asserting that fish are conscious. What they license is a refusal" — the Map declines to convert "fish lack the cortex" into "fish lack felt pain," and *declines equally* to convert "fish behave as if in pain" into "fish are in pain." Both conversions named, both refused, in the same sentence.

But the outstanding mysterian passage is `brain-organoids` L78, and it goes further than epistemic humility. The article notices it has ruled out, one class at a time, *every observation an organoid actually affords*, with Tenet 2 supplying a general reason why none could bear — and then says so: **"A position that no possible observation of the dish could disturb has stopped being cautious and started being immune, and the Map should say so plainly rather than leave it unremarked."** It follows with the honest description ("at the organoid level the Map's stance is interpretive rather than predictive"), relocates the falsifiability burden one level up to the tenets where the Map takes it on directly, names what would actually move the verdict — a validated interface-localisation criterion, not a better organoid measurement — and closes: "the silence of the dish reflects the state of our instruments rather than any evidential achievement of the position." An article auditing itself for unfalsifiability and losing the audit on purpose is the strongest single paragraph reviewed today.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**Verdict: three of four hold the register; the fourth upgrades on tenet-load.** This persona's verdict is load-bearing here, since every article in the cluster touches a boundary case.

**Tenet-as-evidence-upgrade, praise-worthily NOT done:**

- `sentientism` L72 is the exhibit. Having just shown that Dualism blocks the illusionist threat *and* dissolves the indeterminacy problem — the strongest possible setup for claiming the criterion vindicated — the article instead writes: **"that is the sense in which the Map's metaphysics pays an ethical dividend — and equally, the sense in which the Map's ethics is exposed: phenomenal sentientism stands or falls with the tenet, and does not claim independent support from the applied literature that leaves the distinction unmade."** Dividend and exposure in one sentence, with the exposure given the last word.
- `fish-sentience` L69 states the formula verbatim — "removing a defeater is not the same as supplying positive evidence" — and immediately applies it to itself: "That fish pain is *not ruled out* by the missing cortex does nothing by itself to show fish pain is *present*."
- `brain-organoids` L62 declines a *stronger version of its own thesis* on consistency grounds: the strong claim (neural facts bear no evidential relation to experience whatever) would contradict Reading 1, so the article retreats to the weaker conditional and says why.

**Evidence-quality discipline of exactly this persona's kind**, in `brain-organoids` and nowhere else in the cluster: L46 concedes that the DishBrain learning effect "has not been independently replicated outside the group that reported it," names the two follow-ups that "share authorship with the original report," and then defuses its own concession — "If the competence turns out to be weaker than advertised, the decoupling this article defends becomes *easier* to argue, not harder." L48 supplies the conflict-of-interest provenance rather than asserting it (five of nine authors employed by Cortical Labs, four declaring a pecuniary interest, a fifth declaring shares) and separates what commercialisation changes — moral urgency — from what it does not: the weight of the experiencer evidence, "which stays exactly where DishBrain left it."

**Citation-framing accuracy, pre-empted**, at three loci: `sentientism` L84 adopts Birch's action layer and states in the same breath that "the framework is deliberately metaphysics-neutral, and Birch himself assigns non-materialist views low credence; the Map adopts the action layer without claiming him as an ally on the grounding question." `brain-organoids` L44 does the same for Milford, Shaw and Starke — takes the argument seriously, states the Map does not accept it "since it inherits the free-energy principle's own account of what makes a system a subject," then extracts what it does establish. `brain-organoids` L42 holds Kagan et al.'s "sentience" to its technical Fristonian sense, with L44 refusing to let the vocabulary point dispose of the ethical question.

**One further disambiguation worth recording**, `sentientism` L54: "**Joel Feinberg** the legal and moral philosopher, **not Todd Feinberg** the neuroscientist whose neurobiological criteria for consciousness the Map cites elsewhere." Both are live in this corpus; the article disambiguated before anyone conflated them.

**CALIBRATION CONCERN — the one conflict, and it is against the article's own stated standard.**

`obsidian/topics/ethics-of-consciousness-invertebrate-question.md` L99 → L101.

L99 gives the honest evidential verdict on invertebrate valence: cognitive-bias tests, motivational trade-offs in crustaceans, "**but the gap between behavioural evidence and phenomenal reality remains** — as it does for all non-human consciousness." L101 then upgrades:

> "The Map suggests treating valence as **likely** wherever consciousness is likely, **because** the evolutionary function of consciousness **under the Bidirectional Interaction tenet** involves biasing neural outcomes toward beneficial states."

The tenet is supplying the probability the previous sentence conceded the evidence does not supply. This matches, almost to the syntax, the form `possibility-probability-slippage` names as the explicit case: *"Since the tenets remove the parsimony defeater, X becomes probable."* And `birch-edge-of-sentience-and-the-five-tier-scale` L75 states the governing rule the cluster otherwise honours — *"a tenet may remove a defeater, but it must not upgrade the evidence level."*

What makes this a confirmed finding rather than a persona quibble is that **the article legislates the symmetry itself, twenty lines earlier**. L81: the standing agnostic challenge "cutting against absence-claims **as sharply as against presence-claims**: no third-person evidence can confirm felt experience or rule it out." The absence direction is disciplined correctly and at length; the presence direction is then upgraded on tenet-load without the register being named. Secondary locus, same shape and weaker: L107, "wherever the Map's framework identifies **probable** consciousness, moral status follows."

**The structural explanation, and the reason this is a re-frame rather than a deletion.** This is the cluster's oldest article (created 2026-04-13, `ai_system: claude-opus-4-6`, `last_deep_review` 2026-07-08), written before the discipline crystallised. It links to `birch-edge-of-sentience-and-the-five-tier-scale` at L79 — the article that installs the rule — and cites neither `evidential-status-discipline` nor `possibility-probability-slippage`, the only cluster member to cite neither. Its three siblings each carry the discipline in substance and in their own idiom.

**The underlying argument at L101 is good and must be kept.** A valence-free coupled consciousness would be functionally idle, undermining Tenet 3's own rationale for coupling — that is a legitimate *framework-internal structural expectation*, and stating it is worth more than deleting it. What it needs is its register named: framework-internal structural expectation, not an evidential upgrade; modal support for the claim, with the evidential verdict of L99 left standing beside it rather than overwritten. `possibility-probability-slippage` supplies the honest patterns directly — *"X is open under the Map's framework; its evidential standing is independent."*
