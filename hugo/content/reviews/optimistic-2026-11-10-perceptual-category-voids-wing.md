## Calibration Concerns {#calibration}

### 1. CHR credits Wong et al. (2020) with a concession about quality that they made about representation (P2)

- **L42**: "Whether possessors hear chroma as a quality or recognise a label quickly is the open question, and the researchers who train adults into the ability concede that no agreed test separates the two (Wong et al. 2020)."
- **L78**: "What separates a heard chroma from a fast, accurate label is the very thing the field has no agreed way to test."
- **At source**: Wong et al. consider that trainees "could have generated reference tones internally in a trial and use it for subsequent trials". They call that outcome "quasi-AP", as against "'genuine AP' involving the representation of every pitch in an absolute manner (e.g., Levitin & Rogers, 2005)". They add: "One could perhaps use fast responses to infer automatic pitch naming in an absolute manner. But the search for the threshold response time could be elusive". These are preprint words; the published wording of the core sentence differs slightly, per the research note's [raw].
- **Why it matters**: their untestable hypothesis concerns how pitch is represented, absolute or internally referenced. In their framing a fast label marks the *genuine* side. CHR casts a fast label as the deflationary rival of a heard chroma, so it borrows an empirical concession for a phenomenal question the authors never pose. The research note made the same move (L91, L186, L194, L261). Its quotes are verbatim; the gloss is not.
- **Grade**: CHR L92 grades structured persistence "moderate, since … the obvious dissolving experiment did not dissolve it". The experiment never asked the question. VIT L84 states the honest form of this grade: "never tested by a programme that tried to dissolve it".
- **What survives**: every face, the verdict of neutrality, the unexplored margin (no trainee reports, L78), and the core question itself. The core now rests on the conceivability argument of L90 rather than on the trainers' concession. CHR becomes more precise and loses no face.
- **Siblings checked and left alone**: PAL L74 and `voids.md` L316 quote "genuine absolute pitch … untestable" accurately. `voids.md` L221 ("why no agreed test tells a heard chroma from a fast label") is an unattributed absence claim and is true as stated.

### 2. VIT says human report "forces" a category, against its own L82 and CAT's deflation (P3)

- **L94**: "A system with access to its own continuous activation dynamics could in principle report temporal contour directly, without the conversion to category that human report forces."
- **Against VIT's own L82**: "no one has yet tried response formats that are not category labels (continuous sliders, matched movement, vocal contour)". On the page's own account, non-category human report is *untried*, not impossible.
- **Against CAT**: "continuous ratings of how /b/-like a token is track the acoustics" (L48), and the void fails "if spontaneous report proves graded" (L64). CAT's settled lesson is that category-forcing is a property of the categorisation instruction, not of human report as such. VIT L94 drops that lesson. **Verbal** report is what imposes the category (VIT L76 says exactly this of words).
- **Two smaller overreaches on the same page.** (a) **L58**: "The *what* intact and the *how* altered is what a distinct dimension predicts and what a mere attribute of content does not." Separately processed attributes of content dissociate too, as colour does in cerebral achromatopsia (`concepts/visual-consciousness` L92). A dissociation shows separate processing; it does not discriminate dimension from attribute. (b) **L80**: the four features are a structure "that a sociological account does not predict". The page's own sociological account in the same paragraph, an inherited "discrete-category programme", predicts the scientist half of feature 1 (L64, "Asked to study feeling, a scientist studies discrete emotions"). "Predicts only in part" is the supported claim.

### 3. Lower, not tasked: AUD L73 "mirror"

"The [chroma void](/voids/chroma-void/) finds a normal-listener mirror of the dissociation." In amusia the registration is electrophysiological, and the awareness is what is limited (L71). In Schellenberg & Trehub the retention is behavioural: a forced choice between versions that succeeds. Whether that choice comes with awareness of pitch level is not reported. CHR's own wording, "a milder form of that pattern" (L96), is the accurate one. "Mirror" invites the reader to carry L71's capability-division gloss (no "mind-side presentation") onto ordinary listeners, which nothing in CHR licenses. A length-neutral fix for the next AUD pass: "finds a milder, normal-listener analogue of the dissociation". The open AUD P3 (todo L2740) is the nearest pass. It is not minted here, because the slot is better spent on the shared research gap.
