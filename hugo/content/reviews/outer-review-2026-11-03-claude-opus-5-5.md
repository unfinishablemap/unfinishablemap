# Referee Report: “Consciousness and Motor Selection” (unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/, live version modified 2026-10-27)

**Verdict: major revision.** The article is unusually candid for a dualist mechanism piece. It concedes that classical accumulators are causally closed, quantifies the Zeno observation burden, and flags the anti-Zeno sign problem. But its Libet section is thin and asymmetric, and its quantum section counts observation events without stating the condition the physics actually imposes. At least four citation defects need fixing. The firewall that “the core argument does not depend on” Zeno sits awkwardly with the article’s own admission that the agency argument needs a non-classical mechanism, [research/anti-zeno-effect-and-sign-of-conscious-observation-2026-08-05](https://unfinishablemap.org/research/anti-zeno-effect-and-sign-of-conscious-observation-2026-08-05/) and it never names the alternative the Map’s own register prefers. Several linked pages also still overstate what this article claims.

## TL;DR

- **Neuroscience (Q1):** The article represents Libet 1983, Schurger 2012 and Sjöberg 2024 broadly accurately. It is cherry-picked and dated, though: it omits Maoz et al. 2019, Schurger et al. 2021 (TiCS), Bredikhin et al. 2023, Gavenas et al. 2024 and Fried et al. 2011, and it treats Libet’s W-time as valid while rejecting the readiness potential’s meaning. The one direct “Schurger” quotation appears verbatim in Schurger’s 2018 eNeuro paper, not in the 2012 PNAS paper it is attributed to.
- **Quantum Zeno (Q2):** The article honestly confronts Tegmark and Hagan, and it quotes Stapp’s 2000 reply accurately. But it quantifies only a count (3,000–30,000 events per 300 ms), never the operative Zeno condition (interval short relative to the system’s Zeno/correlation time). It understates Georgiev’s 2015 result and omits Bourget’s 2004 quantitative “weakness of will” objection and Koch & Hepp 2006. Tegmark 2000 is cited in the body but missing from the references.
- **Integration and method (Q4–Q5):** The article never mentions that the Map’s own register demotes Stapp-Zeno ([P-Q4](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q4)) below post-decoherence selection. Its “resolving indeterminacy in a direction the agent intends” is outcome selection, which Stapp’s Process-1 mechanism does not supply. Pages including agent-causation, retrocausality, von-neumann-wigner-interpretation and conscious-vs-unconscious-processing still overstate or contradict it. The public changelog was last modified 2026-10-15 and does not show the 2026-10-27 edit.

## Q1. Libet, Schurger and the 2020s readiness-potential literature

**What is accurate (verified):**

- **Libet 1983:** RP onset about 550 ms and W about 200 ms before movement. [concepts/libet-experiments](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/libet-experiments/) This matches Schurger et al.’s (2021) summary, which notes that type II (spontaneous) RPs begin about 500 ms prior and type I about 1,000 ms prior. [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8192467) The reference (*Brain* 106, 623–642) is correct, though the title is truncated: it omits “(readiness-potential): The unconscious initiation of a freely voluntary act”. [oup](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/147/7/2267/7685995)
- **Schurger, Sitt & Dehaene 2012:** PNAS 109(42): E2904–E2913 is correct. The paraphrase is fair: according to the model, when the imperative to move is weak, the moment of threshold crossing “is largely determined by spontaneous subthreshold fluctuations in neuronal activity.” [chapman](https://digitalcommons.chapman.edu/psychology_articles/175/)
- **Sjöberg 2024:** *Brain* 147(7): 2267–2269 is correct, and so is the description. Patients pass through akinetic mutism, then deficits in voluntarily initiating movement, then remission within weeks. “All of them reported a subjective experience of making 100% effort.” The quotation “completely irrelevant to the neuroscientific discussion about free will” is verbatim. [oup](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/147/7/2267/7685995) The article also correctly notes that this is not an endorsement of conscious causation. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/) Sjöberg’s essay closes on brain research “dealing with the substrate of the soul”, a monist framing. [oup](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/147/7/2267/7685995)[nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11224596)

**Findings:**

1. **Probable quote misattribution (Medium).** The article says: “As Schurger put it, the RP may reflect ‘ongoing stochastic fluctuations in neural activity that favor the spontaneous emission of a movement at certain times more so than at others.’” [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/) That sentence appears verbatim in Schurger’s single-author 2018 *eNeuro* paper (“Specific Relationship between the Shape of the Readiness Potential, Subjective Decision Time, and Waiting Time…”). There it introduces “Recent evidence”, citing Schurger et al. 2012, Murakami et al. 2014 and Schmidt et al. 2016. [eneuro](https://www.eneuro.org/content/5/1/ENEURO.0302-17.2018)[nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5815661/) I could not find it in the 2012 PNAS abstract, which is the only Schurger work in the reference list. Either cite Schurger (2018) or replace it with the 2012 wording.
2. **Cherry-picked and dated evidence base (High).** The “Libet Challenge Weakened” section rests on two items: a 2012 model and a 2024 three-page essay. It omits:

- **Schurger, Hu, Pak & Roskies (2021)**, “What Is the Readiness Potential?”, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 25(7): 558–570. This is the field’s standard reassessment. [simplypsychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/freewill-determinism.html) It argues that recent advances “call for a reassessment of its relevance for understanding volition and the philosophical problem of free will.” [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8192467) It would strengthen the article’s case, but it also reports facts the article needs: veto decisions “are also preceded by an RP”, and the point of no return for vetoing sits about 200 ms before movement. [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8192467) The underlying study is Schultze-Kraft et al. (2016, *PNAS* 113(4): 1080–1085): “Cancellation of movements was possible if stop signals occurred earlier than 200 ms before movement onset, thus constituting a point of no return.”
- **Maoz, Yaffe, Koch & Mudrik (2019)**, *eLife* 8: e39787. RPs were found for arbitrary decisions “but were strikingly absent for deliberate ones.” [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/312609165_Neural_precursors_of_decisions_that_matter-an_ERP_study_of_deliberate_and_arbitrary_choice)[topics/free-will](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/free-will/) This is the most relevant finding for an article about willed motor selection. The Map’s own libet-experiments and free-will pages cite it; this article does not. [concepts/libet-experiments](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/libet-experiments/) A fair citation should also note that eLife’s reviewers initially found the Bayes-factor evidence “inconclusive”. The authors then added further analyses, none of which supported an RP in deliberate decisions. [elifesciences](https://elifesciences.org/articles/39787v1/peer-reviews)
- **Bredikhin, Germanova, Nikulin & Klucharev (2023)**, two papers: a complex replication in *Consciousness and Cognition* and “(Non)-experiencing the intention to move” in *Neuropsychologia* 185: 108570. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370464961_Non-experiencing_the_intention_to_move_On_the_comparisons_between_the_Readiness_Potential_onset_and_Libet's_W-time)[scitechdaily](https://scitechdaily.com/does-free-will-exist-new-study-challenges-classic-libet-experiments/) The replication reproduced Libet’s key findings but found large interindividual variability in introspective reports. W-reports shifted with instructions and condition order while RP onset did not. As Bredikhin put it in HSE University’s press release, “It appears that the classical Libet paradigm is not suitable for answering the question of whether we have free will while making decisions.”
- **Gavenas, Rutishauser, Schurger & Maoz (2024)**, *Nature Communications* 15: 7285. A spiking-network model in which slow ramping beginning about 2 s before threshold crossing emerges from bounded spontaneous fluctuations. [nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37398452) In the authors’ words, “slow ramping signals reflect bounded spontaneous fluctuations that emerge from quasi-winner-take-all dynamics in clustered networks that are temporally stabilized by slow-acting synapses”. This is a fully classical, mechanistic account of the ramp, and it sharpens the article’s own concession that the accumulator story is causally closed.
- **Fried, Mukamel & Kreiman (2011)**, *Neuron* 69(3): 548–562, single-unit SMA recordings. The Map’s motor-selection page cites it, but this article does not. Its abstract reports that “a population of 256 SMA neurons is sufficient to predict in single trials the impending decision to move with accuracy greater than 80% already 700 ms prior to subjects’ awareness.” The sample was 1,019 neurons recorded in 12 subjects. This is the strongest pre-awareness evidence on the *other* side.
3. **Asymmetric treatment of W-time (High).** The article discounts the RP as averaged noise, yet relies on W as a valid timestamp: “The 200ms window of conscious awareness before movement is … the agent operating within a noisy system.” [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/) The literature that undermines the RP’s interpretation (Bredikhin 2023; Schurger et al. 2021’s discussion of W) equally undermines W as a measure of when conscious intention occurs. The 200 ms window also coincides with the point of no return for vetoing (Schultze-Kraft et al. 2016, which the Map’s own libet-experiments page cites). [nih +2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4743773) That is a poor place to locate an agent’s operative contribution. The article should either defend W’s validity or drop the 200 ms window as the locus of agency.
4. **“Artifact of averaging neural noise” slightly overstates Schurger (Low).** In the 2012 model the RP shape arises from averaging threshold-crossing epochs of autocorrelated fluctuations *plus* an imperative input; the authors call the threshold crossing the “neural decision to move now”. [pnas](https://pnas.org/content/109/42/E2904/tab-article-info) The 2021 TiCS review is more careful: the RP is “highly replicable” in group averages, and the dispute concerns its interpretation and single-trial reality. [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8192467) Prefer “reinterprets the pre-movement ramp as largely reflecting spontaneous fluctuations, not an early decision”.
5. **Missing relevance asymmetry (Medium).** Maoz 2019 cuts both ways. It removes the RP from deliberate choices, which is good for the Map. But Maoz and colleagues read deliberate decisions as driven by different *neural* processes, not by a non-physical selector. [google](https://translate.google.com/translate) The article’s proposal concerns exactly the deliberate case, so it should engage this rather than fall silent.

## Q2. The quantum Zeno proposal versus decoherence

**What is accurate (verified):**

- **Tegmark (2000):** *Phys. Rev. E* 61, 4194. Decoherence timescales are “~10^{-13}–10^{-20} seconds” against dynamical timescales of “~0.001–0.1 seconds”. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9907009) The article’s “twelve-order-of-magnitude gap” is right at the slow end (10⁻¹³ against 10⁻¹ s).
- **Hagan, Hameroff & Tuszyński (2002):** *PRE* 65, 061901. The revised 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ s figure is correct, as is the article’s restriction of it to microtubules. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/)
- **The rebuttal-of-the-rebuttal:** Reimers et al. 2009 (*PNAS* 106(11): 4219–4224) and McKemmish et al. 2009 (*PRE* 80, 021912) are correctly cited. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/)
- **Arithmetic:** 0.3 s / 10⁻⁴ s = 3,000, and 0.3 s / 10⁻⁵ s = 30,000. Correct. This replaces an earlier “hundreds of thousands” figure that the Map’s 2026-08-08 pessimistic review quoted. [reviews/pessimistic-2026-08-08-strawman-audit](https://unfinishablemap.org/reviews/pessimistic-2026-08-08-strawman-audit/)
- **Stapp (2000)**, arXiv:quant-ph/0010029: the quotation is accurate. Stapp writes that “the Quantum Zeno Effect described above is not destroyed by the fact that S(t)_b is a mixture: that makes no difference at all.” Incidentally, Stapp’s paper misquotes Tegmark as “10⁻²³ seconds”. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0010029v1)

**Findings:**

1. **Counts events but never states the operative condition (High).** Stapp’s own derivation runs as follows. Between successive projections separated by d, “Hd is small… the terms linear in d drop out.” [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0010029v1) The Zeno suppression therefore requires d to be short relative to the system’s intrinsic transition timescale (the Zeno time, roughly ħ/ΔH), not merely relative to the decoherence time. The article’s “either way” paragraph computes how many events a 300 ms window would need *if* the interval matched Hagan’s coherence time. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/) It never estimates ΔH for a motor-program transition, the correlation time of the neural environment, or where those place the system relative to the Zeno/anti-Zeno crossover. The anti-Zeno point is raised and then deferred to sign-problem-for-conscious-observation. Until one order-of-magnitude estimate is supplied, the quantitative section remains a bounding exercise on an unknown, not a test. The Map’s own quantum-zeno-effect and timing-gap-problem pages concede that “no neural correlation time has been measured or computed.” [concepts/timing-gap-problem](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/timing-gap-problem/)[concepts/quantum-zeno-effect](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/quantum-zeno-effect/)
2. **Georgiev understated (Medium).** The article’s paraphrase, that Georgiev “replies that a mind projecting in the basis decoherence has already fixed is redundant with it”, is softer than Georgiev’s own result. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/) His abstract (IJMPB 29(07): 1550039, DOI 10.1142/S0217979215500393) states three things:

- “The simulations unambiguously showed that the quantum Zeno effect breaks down for timescales greater than the brain decoherence time.” [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.4741)
- He proved that “local projections cannot decrease the von Neumann entropy of the unconditional brain density matrix.” [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.4741)
- This “establishes that Stapp’s model is physically implausible but leaves a door open … provided the brain has a decoherence-free subspace.” [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.4741)

In the body he concludes that mind efforts “can only lead to extra decay of probability”, so “the best strategy of the mind is to withhold performing any projective measurements.” [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.4741) That is a no-go claim, not a redundancy claim. Stapp’s 2015 *NeuroQuantology* reply (“No-Go for Georgiev’s No-Go Theorem”; “The Monte Carlo simulation is not a simulation of my theory”) should be cited so readers can see the actual exchange. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281229307_No-go_theorem_for_Stapp's_quantum_Zeno_model_of_mind-brain_interaction) Note also that NeuroQuantology is a low-standing venue for both sides. [research/anti-zeno-effect-and-sign-of-conscious-observation-2026-08-05](https://unfinishablemap.org/research/anti-zeno-effect-and-sign-of-conscious-observation-2026-08-05/)
3. **Bourget (2004) omitted (High).** David Bourget, “Quantum leaps in philosophy of mind”, *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 11(12): 17–42, is the most directly relevant philosophical critique of Stapp for *motor* selection. Stapp replied in the same issue (pp. 43–49). [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/BOUQLI)[philpapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/BOUQLI) Bourget explicitly sets decoherence aside (“improper mixtures can be subject to the Zeno effect”). He then argues that, given conscious events of at least about 20–50 ms and a Libet-sized window of about 100 ms, only 2–5 collapses are available. A 50/50 quantum switch would then fail “between 3% and 25%” of the time, so “weakness of will should be commonplace even in the context of simple choices.” He notes that this “is independent of the quantum nature of Stapp’s proposal.” [newdualism](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Bourget/QLPM.htm) This is a quantitative, falsifiable objection aimed at exactly this article’s thesis, and it also bears on the 200 ms window in Finding 3. Its absence is the largest single gap in counterargument coverage.
4. **Koch & Hepp (2006) omitted (Low–Medium).** Koch & Hepp, “Quantum mechanics in the brain”, *Nature* 440: 611, [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/440611a) is the canonical mainstream dismissal. In his reply (“Quantum Mechanics in the Brain”), Stapp argued that their essay “focuses exclusively on conceivable quantum effects that almost all physicists believe will not survive the rigors of a hot wet brain, while ignoring… the quantum Zeno effect.” One sentence acknowledging the exchange would close an obvious gap.
5. **“Choice of question” treated only in passing (Medium).** The article usefully concedes that “‘observation’ in physics ordinarily denotes a physical measurement coupling rather than an agent’s attention”. It also concedes that energy-conservation compatibility “is part of what the interpretation must argue”. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/) It then defers the basis-choice problem to process-1-specification-problem. For a motor article, the specific question is what projector P(E) corresponds to “reach for the glass”, and over which degrees of freedom. Stapp (2000) places it on “the low frequency (< 40Hz) part of the coulomb part of the electromagnetic field”, [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0010029v1) while Schwartz, Stapp & Beauregard (2005) place it at calcium-ion channels in nerve terminals. The article adopts the ion-channel site without noting the discrepancy. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/) It should also note that Tegmark’s 10⁻²⁰ s figure was computed for a superposition of a neuron’s firing state, not specifically for a calcium ion in a presynaptic channel.
6. **Kiefer & Joos (1998) is cited for a stronger claim than it explicitly makes (Low–Medium).** The paper (arXiv:quant-ph/9803052) does treat the quantum Zeno effect as a decoherence phenomenon: “The most dramatic consequence of a strong measurement-like interaction of a system with its environment is the quantum Zeno effect.” It also notes that for states “not sufficiently resolved by the environment, Fermi’s Golden Rule can be recovered.” [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9803052v1) But it states no explicit condition that repeated observation “adds something only while it arrives before the environment has resolved the same alternatives.” That is a defensible inference, not a quotation-level finding. Label it as the Map’s reading.
