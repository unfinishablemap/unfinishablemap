## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. Wegner & Wheatley (1999): I-Spy result overstated in scope, and the design misdescribed.** FIXED.
The article said that "under arranged conditions of priority, consistency, and exclusivity" subjects "feel authorship regardless of whether they caused the act". Full text (*American Psychologist* 54(7), pp. 487–489, read from the author-hosted PDF): the study "focused on the role of priority of thought and action when there is consistency between the thought and action and when the exclusivity of thought as a cause of action is ambiguous"; "By varying the timing, we thus manipulated priority." Result: forced stops were rated M = 52% intended overall, higher when the prime word came 1 or 5 seconds before the stop than 30 seconds before or 1 second after (quadratic effect F(1, 47) = 5.00, p < .05; 51 undergraduates, 27–40 valid responses per time point). That is a timing-dependent rise in rated intention for forced acts, not authorship "regardless of" causation. The three principles are the theory; the experiment manipulated one. Rewritten to state the theory, the manipulation and the direction of the result. The earlier stability note on the "I-Spy vs three-conditions" labelling asymmetry concerned vocabulary across pages and is untouched; this is a result-scope finding.
