## Executive Summary

The page's neutral core (§Entanglement Is Relative to a Factorisation) and its three-level decomposition are sound, well-sourced, and honestly hedged. The defects are in how the page applies its own decomposition to the Map. **First**, §The Map's Exposure argues the preferred mechanism's dependence at Level 1 — the system/environment split that Carroll–Singh derive and Stoica's no-go covers — which the page has just declared "shared" by "everyone who uses quantum mechanics"; the Map-specific exposure (Level 2, which split consciousness selects *within*) is asserted in one clause, and the page's closing claim that it "records a dependency the Map's preferred mechanism had not stated" is contradicted by [concepts/many-worlds.md](/concepts/many-worlds/) L116–118, which already states that the selection programme "relies on the same einselection machinery" that selects bases "only approximately and relative to observers". **Second**, the page's central circularity charge against entanglement-based individuation reads Zanardi–Lidar–Lloyd's "operationally accessible interactions and measurements" as access *by an observer* (L44, L46, L84), when the accessible algebra can be fixed by dynamics and locality without any subject — a point the 2026-09-25 outer review that prompted the page conceded in its own text. **Third**, the Tenet 4 parity is stated one-sidedly: the Everettian's branch structure is exactly as factorisation-relative as the Map's subjects (Stoica 2022's title says so), the Map already concedes this parity at [concepts/many-worlds.md](/concepts/many-worlds/) L118, and `tenets.md` L121–123 already books the indexical argument's dependence on subjecthood; the page cites none of these and so overstates the novelty and the one-sidedness of the risk. Four further findings (Stapp unverified at source, a one-stem absence claim about Chalmers–McQueen stated in article prose, the outer review's five demands rendered as three, and a cross-cut/internal-entanglement slip in the only in-framework argument against the binding hypothesis) are medium. The page is 67 words under its hard gate, so every fix must be length-neutral and the review names the sentence each fix can be paid from.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would read L46 — "If the TPS is fixed by what is accessible *to an observer*, then using entanglement to constitute the observer is circular" — and say the page has smuggled folk psychology into a physics result. Zanardi, Lidar and Lloyd's "operationally accessible" means the algebra of interactions the apparatus can implement; it is fixed by couplings, not by anyone's experience. Cotler et al. and Carroll–Singh, whom the page cites, show how to read a decomposition off the Hamiltonian with no observer in the loop. So the "observer" that the page says the question "returns to" (L44) is a placeholder for *physical access conditions*, and the circularity the page charges the binding cluster with dissolves as soon as the placeholder is cashed physically — which is the physicalist's whole programme. The page half-knows this: L64 grants that a Level 1 uniqueness theorem would "rescue Level 1 and the object-level bipartition". But it puts the circularity charge in the lead section without that condition attached.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett would attack L78 and L96 from the other side. The page needs "a determinate 'I'" for the indexical argument and worries that TPS-relativity dissolves it. Dennett's reply: good — there was never a determinate "I" in the sense required, only a centre of narrative gravity, and the factorisation literature is now saying the same thing in Hilbert-space vocabulary. The page's response at L96 ("the Map holds the tenet as a starting point that survives the loss of this argument") is honest but it is also the ESD's Mode Three move: the disagreement is at the framework boundary, and the page should say so with the tenet's own "subjecthood dependency" paragraph (`tenets.md` L121–123) in hand rather than presenting the parity as a fresh risk. Dennett would also note L90's "both-edged support" for Tenet 1 followed by L100's "adds no support to any tenet" and ask which sentence the author believes.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark, as a source the page uses extensively, would object to what is done with his and Carroll–Singh's criteria at L64: "Carroll and Singh select subsystems by *minimising* entanglement growth, and Tegmark's cruelest cut by minimising interaction; a bound subject on the entanglement view is a system that stays maximally entangled internally." Carroll–Singh minimise entanglement growth *across* the system/environment cut; a subject that is strongly entangled internally and weakly entangled with its environment is exactly the kind of quasi-classical subsystem their algorithm isolates. Only the *recursive* cruelest cut (L60, "then recurse") points away from binding, because it would go on cutting the brain. The sentence treats two different quantities — cross-cut entanglement growth and internal entanglement — as if minimising one opposed maximising the other. The same slip recurs at L76 ("selects maximally *non*-interacting parts. The hypothesis needs the opposite"): the hypothesis needs parts that interact strongly *with each other* and weakly with the rest, and a minimum-interaction cut is precisely what isolates such a whole as one part. Tegmark's actual problem for the hypothesis is the Zeno limit and the quarter-bit bound, both of which the page states correctly at L60; the Hamiltonian-criterion argument at L64/L76 is the page's weakest in-framework move and is presented as its most natural.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch would accept L78 gladly — "whatever fixes one fixes the other" — and then point out that the page has conceded more than it needed to. Branches are decoherence-defined and decoherence is factorisation-relative; the Everettian has *no* pairing-law resource, only the same Hamiltonian-mereology programme the page surveys. So the parity is symmetric, and if anything the Map, which at L84 at least names a candidate (pairing fixes the factorisation), has one more move than Everett does. The page does not say this, and the Map's own [concepts/many-worlds.md](/concepts/many-worlds/) L116 already does ("Decoherence selects bases only approximately and relative to observers … The branch structure — which worlds exist — depends on how you slice the wave function"). Per the direct-refutation lens this is a *missed unsupported-move identification*: the Everettian helps himself to a branch structure his own framework has not earned, and the page lets him.

### The Empiricist

Popper's ghost would ask what the page's central claim risks. "The Map's preferred mechanism … owes an account of which factorisation consciousness selects within" (L74) is a demand, not a claim, and the page is careful to say it "adds no support to any tenet" (L100). That is fine for a booked-constraint page. The empiricist's actual complaint is L68: "a search of the Chalmers–McQueen text for *factori-* returns nothing" is offered as evidence that the most developed consciousness-collapse model "never raises the factorisation question as such". A one-stem search does not establish absence; Chalmers and McQueen could raise the question under "tensor product", "subsystem", "decomposition" or "which systems" — and the page's own next clause quotes them asking "which systems can collapse quantum wave functions", which *is* the Level 2 question. So the page's own quotation undercuts its absence claim in the same sentence.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would find the page congenial in its diagnosis and confused in its remedy. L82's Stoica — "there exists something beyond relations, the intrinsic essence of the 'stuff in relation'" — is the svabhāva move: relations underdetermine, therefore posit an essence. The page rightly marks Stoica as a "convergent ally on the premise, not … a fellow dualist" (L82), but it then reaches for the same shape of posit at L84 (a non-physical subject whose pairing privileges a factorisation), while conceding it is circular, collides with [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2), and is "a new, unwritten posit". The dependent-origination reading is that the factorisation problem is not a debt to be paid but a demonstration that "the subject" and "the subsystem" arise together and neither is prior; the page's three-level ledger presupposes that a prior subject exists to be paired. This is a framework-boundary disagreement and the page need not answer it, but L86's "The Map cannot help itself to the second without a pairing law" should be read as conceding that the page's positive direction currently has no content.

### Issue 1: The mechanism's exposure is argued at the level the page calls shared, and the "not previously stated" claim is false to the corpus

Levels 2 and 3 are the Map's own debt." The Map-specific part — that the *dualist* must say which split the paired subject selects within, whereas the physicalist neuroscientist inherits the split from whatever physics everyone uses — is Level 2, and it appears only as one clause at L74 ("owes an account of which factorisation consciousness selects within") and one at L92 ("that is the pairing debt again"). This is the inverse of the conflation L50 warns against: a problem at one level passed as a problem at another. It also makes L40's "runs deeper" misleading — the mechanism's exposure is *wider* (it reaches the non-speculative wing) but *shallower* (Level 1, shared) than the binding cluster's (Levels 2–3, Map-specific). Separately, L100's "had not stated" is contradicted by [concepts/many-worlds.md](/concepts/many-worlds/) L116–118: "Decoherence selects bases only approximately and relative to observers … The Map does not press this as an independent argument: its own [selection programme](/apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme/) relies on the same einselection machinery." The apex had not booked it; the corpus had. Rule: ESD §Constrain vs. Establish (a constraint on everyone is not a liability of one side) and the page's own L50 anti-conflation rule.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Rewrite L74's final three sentences to split explicitly: (a) the Level 1 dependence is shared with every decoherence-based account and is booked here for completeness, not as a liability; (b) the Map-specific debt is Level 2 — the pairing must say *which* system/environment split the einselected menu belongs to, because a paired subject, unlike a physicalist observer, cannot inherit the split from the physics it is not identical with. Replace L100's "had not stated" with "the apex statement had not stated (the concept page on many-worlds already notes the shared einselection dependence)". Pay for the words by cutting the L68 search-method sentence (Issue 6).

### Issue 2: "Operationally accessible" is read as "accessible to an observer", and the circularity charge rides on the slide

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/quantum-factorisation-problem.md`
- **Location**: L44 "Kabernik, Pollack and Singh (2020) extended it to *generalised bipartitions* fixed by the irreducible representations of a restricted observable algebra, which returns the question to whose access, and so to the observer"; L46 "If the TPS is fixed by what is accessible *to an observer*, then using entanglement to constitute the observer is circular unless something independent of the observer fixes the TPS first"; L84 "the subject's accessible observables would fix its own system".
- **Problem**: Zanardi, Lidar and Lloyd's relativity is to an *algebra* of "operationally accessible interactions and measurements" (quoted at L36). An algebra can be fixed by the Hamiltonian's locality structure, by the couplings available, or by the spectrum (Cotler et al., Carroll–Singh, Loizeau–Sels, all cited at L62) with no subject anywhere. The page's "whose access, and so to the observer" (L44) is attached to an abstract-only citation (ref 7 marked "cited from the abstract") as though it were part of the result, and L46 then conditions the circularity charge on the observer reading. This is the operational-versus-subject form of the *Epistemic Reading ≠ Metaphysical Reading* rule in `evidential-status-discipline` §Five Cross-Cutting Rules: the evidence (operational relativity of the TPS) supports the algebra-relative reading; the page's conclusion (entanglement cannot constitute the observer without circularity) requires the subject-relative one. The 2026-09-25 ChatGPT outer review that prompted the page conceded the point in its own paragraph (its L118: "This does not mean that all decompositions are physically equivalent: locality, interaction structure and experimental control can privilege some divisions"). The page's disclaimer *is* present — L46's "unless something independent of the observer fixes the TPS first", and L64's grant that a uniqueness theorem would rescue Level 1 — but the lead-section charge is stated unconditionally and the condition arrives twenty lines later.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: At L44, attribute the inference: "…restricted observable algebra. On the Map's reading this returns the question to what fixes the algebra — dynamics and locality on the mereology programme, an observer's access on the operational one." At L46, state the condition in the same sentence: "If nothing observer-independent fixes the TPS — the question the mereology programme below is trying to settle — then using entanglement to constitute the observer is circular." L84 already hedges adequately once L46 is fixed. Net length: roughly +15 words; pay from Issue 6.

### Issue 4: The only in-framework argument against the binding hypothesis conflates cross-cut with internal entanglement

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/quantum-factorisation-problem.md`
- **Location**: L64 "Carroll and Singh select subsystems by *minimising* entanglement growth, and Tegmark's cruelest cut by minimising interaction; a bound subject on the entanglement view is a system that stays maximally entangled internally. ## Counterarguments to Address

### The circularity charge (L46)
- **Current content says**: if the TPS is fixed by what is accessible to an observer, using entanglement to constitute the observer is circular.
- **A critic would argue**: the accessible algebra is fixed by couplings and locality; the mereology programme is the observer-free way of doing exactly that; the charge only bites if that programme fails, which the page says is unresolved.
- **Suggested response**: state the charge conditionally in the sentence that makes it (Issue 2), and say plainly that the Map's Level 2 debt survives even if Level 1 is solved — that is the page's real point and it does not need the observer reading to make it.

### The parity risk (L78)
- **Current content says**: the Everettian can reply that the Map's subjects are no more privileged than branches.
- **A critic would argue**: (from the Map's side) branches are equally unprivileged and Everett has no pairing resource; (from Everett's side) the Map has conceded that its indexical "I" is TPS-relative, which is what the deflationist always said.
- **Suggested response**: Issue 3's rewrite — symmetric debt, cite `many-worlds` L116–118 and `tenets` L121–123, route the disagreement to the subjecthood posit.

### The mechanism's exposure (L74)
- **Current content says**: the factorisation debt belongs to the whole interface programme.
- **A critic would argue**: at Level 1 it belongs to *everyone*; the page has just said so; a shared debt is not an exposure.
- **Suggested response**: Issue 1's split. The Map-specific claim is worth making and is currently one clause.
