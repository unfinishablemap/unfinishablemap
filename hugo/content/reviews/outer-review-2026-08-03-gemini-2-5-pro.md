# Pre-Publication Referee Report: Epistemic and Methodological Deficiencies in "The Unfinishable Map"

## Foundational Metaphysical and Methodological Critiques

The manuscript submitted under the title *The Unfinishable Map* constructs an elaborate defense of interactionist dualism, anchored by a rigid quintet of central tenets: Dualism, Minimal Quantum Interaction, Bidirectional Causation, Rejection of Many-Worlds, and the Limits of Occam's Razor. The central ambition of the work is to demonstrate the ontological irreducibility of consciousness and to establish a framework wherein a non-physical mental substance or property exerts downward causal influence on the physical brain. To achieve this, the manuscript aggregates arguments from contemplative neuroscience, quantum biology, and classical philosophy of mind (specifically, the Knowledge Argument, the Zombie Argument, and the Explanatory Gap).   

However, a rigorous pre-publication audit of the manuscript’s logical inferences, empirical accuracy, and counterargument coverage reveals pervasive structural vulnerabilities. The text systematically relies upon question-begging framings, fundamentally misinterprets the neuroscience and quantum physics literature it cites to support its conclusions, and structurally insulates itself from the most formidable contemporary physicalist frameworks by attacking outdated straw-man versions of reductionism. The following sections detail the profound methodological, empirical, and philosophical failures that render the arguments invalid.

## The Illusionist Meta-Problem and the Evasion of Contemporary Eliminativism

The manuscript’s most egregious philosophical failure lies in its handling of illusionism. The text attempts to neutralize illusionism (as advanced by Frankish, Dennett, and contemporary theorists) by invoking the "regress problem." The authors argue that if phenomenal consciousness is an illusion generated by a cognitive representational system, that representation is either phenomenally conscious (thus relocating the hard problem rather than solving it) or it is not (meaning the "seeming" of the illusion is left unexplained, as an unconscious system cannot experience a "seeming").   

This critique is anachronistic and operates on an unexamined presupposition that fundamentally begs the question. By defining the introspective "seeming" as an intrinsically phenomenal property, the manuscript relies on the very dualist intuition it is supposed to prove. It completely ignores the advanced mechanistic literature of the 2020s that explicitly models how non-phenomenal, purely functional cognitive systems generate the false, yet absolutely subjectively convincing, intuition of phenomenality.

The manuscript’s failure to engage with François Kammerer’s (2021) definitive work on the illusion of conscious experience demonstrates a severe lack of scholarly rigor. Kammerer provides a comprehensive theoretical framework explaining why the illusion of phenomenal consciousness is uniquely counterintuitive and almost inextricable, without relying on a meta-level phenomenal gap. Kammerer’s framework illustrates how a cognitive architecture can form judgments with 100% subjective confidence regarding non-existent phenomenal properties through exclusively functional tracking mechanisms.   

Furthermore, the manuscript entirely omits Daniel Shabasson’s (2021/2022) inference theory of the illusion problem, which rigorously dissolves the alleged "regress". Shabasson outlines how three cognitive hypotheses—introspective opacity (the subject's lack of access to the sub-personal processes forming introspective judgments), the infallibility intuition (the disposition to judge introspective classifications as error-free), and the justification constraint—interact to generate a systemic disposition for the cognitive architecture to draw erroneous *unconscious inferences* about sensory states.   

Because the manuscript simply asserts that "the seeming itself is what it represents", it bypasses the burden of refuting these precise architectural models. By ignoring the explicit cognitive-functional models designed to explain the "problem intuitions" (ineffability, intrinsicness, and privacy), the text’s primary refutation of physicalism’s most robust contemporary alternative is logically circular and entirely disconnected from the current scholarly dialectic.   

## The Equivocation Between Biological Computationalism and Anti-Physicalism

In its effort to dismantle physical reductionism, the manuscript exhibits a persistent and fatal inability to distinguish between the failure of substrate-independent functionalism and the failure of physicalism *tout court*. The text explicitly cites Milinkovic & Aru (2026) on "biological computationalism," correctly noting their conclusion that brain computation is inseparable from its biological substrate. However, the manuscript then executes a catastrophic logical leap, arguing that because functional reduction strips away the precise biological properties that matter for consciousness, consciousness must be ontologically irreducible, thereby validating dualism.   

This constitutes a textbook category error. Milinkovic and Aru’s (2026) argument is deployed in the literature specifically to target multiple realizability and computational functionalism (the premise that consciousness could be instantiated in non-biological substrates, such as silicon). It is a defense of biological naturalism—a form of Type-B physicalism. By asserting that specific biological hardware is strictly required for conscious processing, biological computationalism grounds consciousness *more* firmly in physical reality, not less.   

| Empirical/Theoretical Concept | True Target in Academic Literature | The Manuscript's Erroneous Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Biological Computationalism (Milinkovic & Aru 2026)** | Substrate-independent functionalism (AI/Silicon consciousness). | Proof of anti-reductionist interactionist dualism. |
| **Pain Asymbolia** | Strict causal-role functionalism (demonstrating a separation between motivation and phenomenology). | Proof that phenomenal properties are entirely non-physical. |
| **Failure of Disjunctive Reduction (Kim 1992)** | Universal bridge laws across disparate biological species. | Proof of the ontological irreducibility of the mind. |

The manuscript systematically conflates arguments against *functionalism* with arguments against *physicalism*. The fact that phenomenal consciousness cannot be cleanly functionalized—as Jaegwon Kim conceded regarding qualia—simply means that phenomenal properties may be locally identical to specific, highly complex biological properties via a posteriori necessity. The manuscript ignores the extensive philosophical literature defending local reduction and type-identity theory, prematurely declaring physicalism defeated simply because abstract, substrate-neutral computational functionalism proves inadequate.   

## Contemplative Neuroscience, Neuroplasticity, and the Causation Fallacy

A significant portion of the manuscript is dedicated to the integration of contemplative practice and neurophenomenology, arguing that these fields provide empirical validation for bidirectional interactionist dualism. This section is riddled with empirical non-sequiturs, confirmation bias, and the misappropriation of clinical data.

### The Misrepresentation of Structural and Functional Brain Changes

The text acknowledges the null findings of Kral et al. (2022), which utilized a heavily powered randomized controlled trial (N=218) to conclusively refute earlier, highly cited claims (such as Lazar et al. 2005) that mindfulness meditation causes structural brain changes (e.g., cortical thickening or increased grey matter density). Having conceded this significant blow to the macroscopic "neuroplasticity" argument, the manuscript falls back on *functional* changes—specifically, the modulation of the Default Mode Network (DMN), increased gamma synchrony (Lutz et al. 2004), and disrupted hierarchical organization observed in 7-Tesla fMRI scans during jhāna states (Demir et al. 2025).   

The logical fracture occurs when the text asserts that because a "conscious intention" leads to these functional neural changes, this phenomena serves as definitive evidence for "top-down causation," thereby refuting epiphenomenalism and reductive physicalism. This assumption begs the question by treating the "intention" as ontologically independent from the brain. In any standard physicalist framework, a "conscious intention" is mathematically and physically identical to, or realized by, a pre-existing physical brain state (e.g., specific predictive processing priors established in the prefrontal cortex). When a meditator intends to focus their attention on their breath, this physical neural state causally interacts with the DMN and salience networks, naturally producing functional network reorganization.   

Remarkably, the manuscript cites Laukkonen, Friston, and Chandaria (2025), who explicitly outline an *active inference theory of consciousness* that models these exact meditative states through purely physical, computational precision-weighting mechanisms. Yet, the manuscript ignores the fundamental physicalist implications of the very paper it cites, opting instead to interpret the data strictly through an unearned dualist lens. To claim that functional brain changes induced by meditation refute physicalism requires proving that the initial "intention" was entirely non-physical and injected novel, non-determined causal force into the neuroanatomy. The manuscript provides no theoretical framework or empirical evidence for this extraordinary claim, rendering its neurophenomenological argument circular.   

### The Phenomenological Fallacy of Non-Temporal Consciousness

In one of its most egregious metaphysical overextensions, the manuscript attempts to utilize the phenomenology of deep meditative states to construct a "category mismatch argument" against physicalism. Drawing on first-person reports of "timelessness" in jhāna and rigpa states, and citing Frischhut's (2026) "Extended Now Interpretation," the text argues that the deepest level of consciousness is inherently non-temporal. Because physical processes are inherently temporal (involving chronological succession and thermodynamics), the manuscript concludes that consciousness cannot be fully described in physical terms.   

This commits a fundamental use-mention error and a profound phenomenological fallacy. The phenomenology of experiencing no passage of time does not entail that the biological subject having the experience exists outside of time. Laukkonen et al. (2025) perfectly capture this distinction within a physicalist framework: when temporal-depth priors in a predictive processing model are mathematically flattened via active inference, the cognitive system ceases to represent succession. The *representation* of time ceases, but the physical, electrochemical processing required to maintain that non-representational state continues to occur in linear, thermodynamic time.   

The manuscript illegitimately inflates a psychological, phenomenological condition (a brain state wherein time-tracking functional networks are inhibited) into an ontological claim about the fundamental nature of the mind. To appropriate Frischhut (2026)—a paper explicitly analyzing the *psychological awareness* of time—to support a cosmological framework involving the Wheeler-DeWitt equation and the post-decoherence quantum selection of a non-temporal entity, constitutes an extreme and invalid distortion of the cited literature.   

## The Physical Impossibility of "Minimal Quantum Interaction"

The manuscript attempts to resolve Jaegwon Kim's Exclusion Argument—which states that if physical events have sufficient physical causes, mental events must be either physically reducible or epiphenomenal—by denying the causal closure of the physical domain. It posits a "Minimal Quantum Interaction" tenet, arguing that physics is fundamentally incomplete at the level of quantum indeterminacies, ostensibly allowing a non-physical consciousness to bias wave-function collapse without injecting energy or violating conservation laws.   

### The Amplification Chasm and the Violation of the Born Rule

The text cites Luo et al. (2025) regarding radical pair stability in avian cryptochrome 4a to argue that quantum coherence can survive in warm, wet biological environments for microseconds, aiming to bridge the gap between quantum mechanics and neurobiology. However, avian magnetoreception is a strictly closed, physical, evolutionary mechanism. Co-opting this biological reality to argue for non-physical, top-down mental causation is highly problematic and misrepresents the nature of quantum biology.   

More fatally, the manuscript fails to comprehend the statistical constraints of quantum mechanics. If a non-physical consciousness "biases" otherwise undetermined quantum outcomes (e.g., selecting specific pointer states post-decoherence), it must navigate a mathematical dilemma:

1. It respects the statistical distribution dictated by the Born Rule, in which case the conscious "choice" over time is mathematically indistinguishable from random noise, yielding no coherent top-down agency, intentionality, or free will.
2. It violates the Born Rule by forcing non-random, biologically advantageous macroscopic outcomes (e.g., ensuring a specific neural cascade fires consistently to move an arm), which would constitute a massive, highly detectable physical anomaly.

Let the probability of a physical quantum state ∣ψ⟩ collapsing into an eigenstate ∣ai​⟩ be defined by the standard Born rule:
P(ai​)=∣⟨ai​∣ψ⟩∣2

If the interactionist-dualist mind biases this collapse to ensure a specific, willed behavioral outcome, it forces P(ai​) to deviate systematically from the theoretically predicted squared modulus of the amplitude. The manuscript explicitly claims that its framework respects the "no-Born-statistics-violation" constraint. This results in a direct logical contradiction: one cannot systematically bias an indeterminate system to achieve unified, macroscopic, non-random behavioral goals without fundamentally altering the long-term probability distribution of the underlying quantum events.   

### The Misunderstanding of Decoherence and Einselection

The manuscript’s reliance on a "post-decoherence selection programme" demonstrates a flawed understanding of modern quantum physics. It argues that decoherence leaves the system in an "improper mixture," leaving a causal gap that requires a non-temporal conscious selector to settle which pointer state obtains. This ignores the standard framework of environment-induced superselection (einselection). The physical environment itself continually measures the system, forcing it into a preferred basis through rapid entanglement and information dissipation. There is no lingering causal "gap" waiting for a non-physical mind to make a selection. The manuscript weaponizes quantum vocabulary to mask a classical "God of the gaps" argument, inserting dualism into the microscopic spaces where human measurement is currently technically limited.   

## Epistemic Overreach and Phenomenal Conservatism

To insulate its core metaphysical claims from skeptical critique, the manuscript relies on a tiered taxonomy of Husserlian evidence and Michael Huemer's Phenomenal Conservatism (PC). It posits that "Layer 1" (the sheer existence of experience) and "Layer 2" (what attended experience is qualitatively like) possess apodictic or near-apodictic authority, while only "Layer 3" (peripheral or structural features) is fallible.   

This epistemic maneuver is structurally flawed when applied to metaphysical ontology. Phenomenal conservatism states that if it seems to a subject S that P, then S has prima facie justification for believing P, absent defeaters. However, the manuscript illicitly conflates the *epistemic seeming* of a qualitative state with the *metaphysical seeming* of ontological irreducibility. While a subject may have prima facie justification for the belief "I am experiencing pain," they do not possess introspective, apodictic authority over the proposition "My experience of pain is ontologically distinct from my physical neurobiology."   

The manuscript concedes that the "meta-seeming that qualitative character is irreducible may be more vulnerable to cognitive penetration", yet it subsequently treats the explanatory gap as a verified ontological chasm rather than a highly theory-laden conceptual artifact. The failure of the text to rigorously differentiate between the *content* of an experience (which is subjectively accessed) and the *theoretical categorization* of that experience (which requires third-person metaphysical validation) renders its reliance on first-person authority philosophically inert. If introspective opacity—as argued by modern illusionists—acts as a systemic psychological defeater for our natural intuitions about phenomenality, then Phenomenal Conservatism cannot be invoked to bypass the necessity of providing a positive, mechanistic account of how non-physical substances interact with physical ones.   

## The Contradiction of Rejecting Many-Worlds

The manuscript’s fourth tenet requires the strict rejection of the Many-Worlds Interpretation (MWI) of quantum mechanics. The justification relies solely on the "indexical objection"—the claim that MWI cannot answer "why *this* branch is mine." The text states that branching universes generate self-locating uncertainty, destroying the determinate "thisness" of an individual's experiential history.   

This critique is severely underdeveloped and scientifically subordinate. The self-locating uncertainty in Everettian mechanics (as discussed extensively by Wallace, Saunders, and others in the physics literature) is a feature of the physical mathematics, heavily researched and resolvable through decision-theoretic frameworks. By rejecting MWI solely because it conflicts with a pre-theoretic dualist intuition about personal identity, the manuscript explicitly violates its own epistemological mandate to follow the evidence. The manuscript allows its unargued commitment to a unified, persisting conscious substance (a "substance-leaning sub-reading" it admits to adopting to protect agent causation) to dictate its physics, rather than allowing physics to constrain its metaphysics.   

## Untested Counterarguments: The Zombie and Knowledge Arguments

The manuscript leans heavily on Chalmers' Zombie argument and Jackson's Knowledge argument (Mary's Room) as definitive proofs of the explanatory gap. However, the manuscript systematically fails to test these arguments against standard contemporary physicalist rebuttals.   

Regarding the Zombie argument (the premise that a physical duplicate without consciousness is conceivable, therefore metaphysically possible, meaning physicalism is false), the text addresses Dennett’s refutation—that zombie conceivability tracks conceptual confusion rather than metaphysical possibility. The manuscript claims Dennett is wrong because "meditative training would dissolve [phenomenal concepts]; instead, training sharpens phenomenal discrimination". This is a severe non-sequitur. Meditative training sharpening cognitive discrimination of internal sensory states has absolutely no bearing on the metaphysical modal status of philosophical zombies. A purely physical machine equipped with recursive, predictive-processing attention mechanisms would also demonstrate "sharpened discrimination" when its attention is turned inward. Sharpened functional discrimination does not validate ontological dualism.   

Regarding the Knowledge Argument, the manuscript notes that Frank Jackson later retracted his own argument due to worries about epiphenomenalism. However, the manuscript attempts an intellectual sleight of hand, claiming that Jackson's retraction actually *supports* the site's "Bidirectional Interaction" tenet—arguing that if consciousness were causally inert, Mary could not form beliefs or talk about her new experiences. This ignores the entire physicalist counter-argument: Mary *can* talk about her experiences precisely because the experience of red *is* a physical brain state that causally links to her motor cortex. The manuscript simply refuses to conceptualize the physicalist perspective, preferring to force a false trilemma where dualist interactionism is the only survivor.

The following five critical weaknesses justify the rejection of this manuscript, each representing a failure to engage accurately with the 2020-2025 academic literature.

1. **Mishandling of the Illusionist Meta-Problem:** The manuscript dismisses illusionism through a question-begging "regress problem" regarding phenomenal seemings, systematically ignoring the contemporary functional and inference-based solutions to introspective opacity that do not require phenomenal meta-representations.

- *Citation:* Kammerer, F. (2021). "The illusion of conscious experience." *Synthese*, 198: 845–866.
- *Verdict:* Unready for submission due to a logically circular, question-begging dismissal of the modern illusionist meta-problem literature.
2. **Equivocation on Biological Computationalism:** The text erroneously cites biological computationalism as evidence against physicalist reduction, conflating the failure of substrate-independent computational functionalism with the ontological failure of physicalism itself.

- *Citation:* Milinkovic, B., & Aru, J. (2026). "On biological and artificial consciousness: A case for biological computationalism." *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*.
- *Verdict:* Unready for submission due to a categorical and fatal conflation of biological naturalism (Type-B physicalism) with anti-reductionist interactionist dualism.
3. **Neurophenomenological Non-Sequitur:** The project interprets functional neural changes resulting from meditative intention (e.g., DMN modulation) as evidence for top-down non-physical causation, entirely ignoring that these exact neurodynamic changes are exhaustively and currently modeled by physicalist active inference frameworks.

- *Citation:* Laukkonen, R., Friston, K., & Chandaria, S. (2025). "A beautiful loop: An active inference theory of consciousness." *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*, 176, 106296.
- *Verdict:* Unready for submission due to persistent circularity and a failure to test alternative, closed-system physicalist explanations for active inference data.
4. **Ontological Leap from Meditative Timelessness:** The argument linking meditative timelessness to a category mismatch against physicalism commits a severe use-mention error, illegitimately inflating the phenomenology of flattened temporal depth priors into a metaphysical claim about the atemporality of the conscious subject.

- *Citation:* Kral, T. R. A., et al. (2022). "Absence of structural brain changes from mindfulness-based stress reduction: Two combined randomized controlled trials." *Science Advances*.
- *Verdict:* Unready for submission due to the framework's failure to recognize how the collapse of the structural neuroplasticity thesis fundamentally weakens its broader empirical claims regarding top-down mental causation.
