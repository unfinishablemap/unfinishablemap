## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. Hume credited with the mechanism he judged insufficient: FIXED.** The tragedy section said of Hume's resolution: "The emotional distance between audience and represented event lets the painful passions be appropriated and redirected." Distance through awareness of fiction is Fontenelle's account, which Hume reports and then sets aside as incomplete ("perhaps it wants still some new addition"). His counter-case is Cicero's narration of the Verres butchery: "Neither is the sorrow here softened by fiction: For the audience were convinced of the reality of every circumstance." Hume's own mechanism is the predominance of the sentiment of beauty, which gives the passion "a new direction", and he says outright that tragedy softens "by an infusion of a new feeling, not merely by weakening or diminishing the sorrow." The sentence now says the redirection is the work of the stronger sentiment of beauty and names the Fontenelle point and the Cicero case. Text read at davidhume.org (`/texts/empl1/tr`, Tr 6 to Tr 10); the new quotation "convinced of the reality of every circumstance" was matched by literal string.

**2. Anti-hedonist result read as evidence for dualism: FIXED.** The Dualism paragraph closed with "The explanatory gap widens here: the structure most demanding explanation—high value coinciding with displeasure—is the structure physical and hedonic accounts are least equipped to deliver." The dissociation of aesthetic value from hedonic valence is a datum a physicalist who is a pluralist about value predicts just as well, and none of the four authors the article reconstructs argues from it to dualism. A reviewer who accepts the tenets would still flag the sentence, so this is a calibration error, not a framework-boundary disagreement. The sentence now says the dissociation tells against hedonism and not against physicalism, and that the step to dualism runs through the further premise that aesthetic value consists in evaluative phenomenal character; what the negative categories add is that the hedonic shortcut around that premise is closed. The "note on evidential weight" paragraph already carried the constrain-versus-establish distinction and is unchanged.

### Medium Issues Found

**3. "The avalanche" is not Kant's example: FIXED.** The Kant paragraph illustrated the dynamically sublime with "the storm, the avalanche". Kant's list in section 28 is overhanging rocks, thunderclouds, volcanoes, hurricanes, the ocean in tumult and a high waterfall; *avalanche* occurs 0 times in the Bernard translation (Project Gutenberg 48433). Changed to "the storm, the volcano". The mathematically sublime examples (the starry sky, the ocean) are Kant's, from the General Remark, and stand.

**4. Korsmeyer's self-description sat awkwardly against the article's vocabulary: FIXED.** The article treats "conversion accounts" as the hedonist's rival reading. Gilmore (2025, section 2.3.2) reports that Korsmeyer "endorses what she identifies as a 'conversionary account'", on which disgust and fear "are not transformed into different, pleasurable emotions, but rather serve (are 'put to use')" in a valuable response (citing Korsmeyer 2011: 132). One sentence added saying so, attributed to Gilmore's report, so a reader who knows her label does not take the article to have misfiled her.
