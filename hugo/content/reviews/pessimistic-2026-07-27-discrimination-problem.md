# Pessimistic Review — The Discrimination Problem

**Date**: 2026-07-27
**Content reviewed**: `obsidian/concepts/discrimination-problem.md` (2919 words; `last_deep_review: 2026-06-19`; no prior pessimistic review targeting this file)

## Executive Summary

The article is structurally strong — its central thesis (any proposed discriminator is itself a functional state, so it cannot settle a question about whether functional duplication suffices for phenomenality) is clean, and the *Generalisation Across the Catalogue* section performs exemplary discipline in refusing to let the four instances transfer evidence to one another. Two confirmed defects are both **calibration regressions against sibling articles**: the article states the NCC-depends-on-report claim in the flat form the Map's own `neural-correlates-of-consciousness.md` explicitly disowns, and it half-drops the regime qualifier on the Rebouillat 2021 finding that two sibling articles flag as load-bearing. A third issue is a framing problem: the *Voids Connection* asserts "a region of fact" without marking that this presupposes the realist answer to the very question the article says the channel cannot settle. The boundary-case section makes five specific empirical claims with zero citations and no links to the Map pages that carry them.

All defects below were checked against the live files. Items I checked and found **clean** are listed in the final section so later passes do not re-chase them.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

"You have built an entire concept page on a distinction — *P* versus *Q* — whose only credential is that people find it intuitive. The article's structural-asymmetry argument is valid *given* that there are two properties. But that premise is the whole dispute, and you have smuggled it in by naming the page after a *problem* rather than a *hypothesis*. When 'the discrimination problem' is a permanent fixture of your catalogue with four downstream instances, you have made an unfalsifiable posit load-bearing across five articles and called that discipline."

The article's honest reply is available and partially present (the *Why It Is Stubborn* section acknowledges the prior-commitment requirement), but the *Voids Connection* undoes it — see Issue 3.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

"Your treatment of heterophenomenology is the fairest I have had from a dualist site, and then you spoil it: 'the method's announced neutrality is just the discrimination problem under a different name.' No — neutrality is the *method working*. You are complaining that a tool designed to bracket a question brackets the question. The article grants that 'for Dennett this is a feature' but does not engage why I think it is a feature: because the seemings *are* the data, full stop, and the demand for phenomenal backing is a demand for something that does no explanatory work."

Assessment: the engagement here is honest and does not commit boundary-substitution (it marks the disagreement rather than dressing it as an in-framework refutation). But it stops one move early — it never states why Dennett thinks bracketing is sufficient, only that he does.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

Little purchase here; the article's only quantum content is the *Bidirectional Interaction* subsection, which explicitly concedes the signature "would have to be unusually small" and that distinguishing it from noise "is itself a discrimination problem in another register." That is a properly discounted claim.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

No engagement surface. The article does not touch MWI.

### The Empiricist (Popper's ghost)

"'It is not merely difficult; it appears principled.' On what evidence? The article's argument for *principled* is the zombie stipulation — and a stipulation is exactly the thing that cannot establish a principled limit. Worse, the same sentence says the instruments are 'our best **current** epistemic instruments', which is a contingency claim. You cannot have both in one sentence. Your own `brain-internal-born-rule-testing` article gets this right — 'the corridor's unfalsifiability is instrument-relative rather than principled' — and this page does not inherit that discipline." (See Issue 3; the sibling wording is verified at `obsidian/topics/brain-internal-born-rule-testing.md:157`.)

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

"The article treats *P* and *Q* as two candidate properties one of which must obtain. The third possibility — that the question is malformed because neither the self doing the introspecting nor the property being sought is the kind of thing that has determinate presence-or-absence — is nowhere considered. The *Boundary Cases* section closes with 'which of two phenomenality profiles — present or absent — is compatible with them', which stipulates bivalence at exactly the point where it is least warranted."

Assessment: a genuine unexplored option. The Map has `cessation-versus-plenitude` and `samkhya-three-way-distinction` in the corpus; neither is linked from here. Noted as an expansion opportunity rather than a defect.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: NCC report-dependence stated in the flat form the Map's own NCC article disowns

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/discrimination-problem.md`
- **Location**: line 68, *The Third-Person Channel*
- **Current text**: "Neural correlates of consciousness (NCC) research identifies brain activity that *covaries* with reportable experience in human subjects, but covariation is not identity, and **the methodology presupposes that report tracks experience in the human case**—begging the question one level down."
- **Problem**: `obsidian/concepts/neural-correlates-of-consciousness.md` lines 180–191 sets out a six-way graded taxonomy of how NCC paradigms ground consciousness-attributions (direct report, post-trial report, behavioural proxy, no-report paradigm, clinical marker, theory-laden classification) and then states explicitly: *"The honest generalisation is therefore **not** that every NCC study presupposes concurrent first-person report, but that the validation chain for these proxies ultimately traces back to report in the paradigms where report is available."* The discrimination-problem article asserts precisely the generalisation that sibling page was refined to disown. The no-report paradigm (optokinetic nystagmus, pupillometry) exists specifically to subtract the reporting footprint, and the article does not mention it — the string "no-report" does not appear in the file.
- **Severity**: High. This is a confirmed cross-article calibration regression, and it weakens rather than strengthens the article's own case: the honest version (the validation chain traces back to report *indirectly*) is a better argument than the flat version, because it survives the obvious objection.
- **Recommendation**: Replace with the graded claim and cite the sibling. Something in the register of: NCC paradigms ground consciousness-attribution in several different ways, only the first of which leans on concurrent report; the discrimination problem bites at the validation chain, where the report-free paradigms are calibrated against report-based ones and inherit their first-person grounding indirectly. Add `[[neural-correlates-of-consciousness]]` to the body and Further Reading.

### Issue 2: Rebouillat 2021 regime qualifier half-dropped — the deceptive-cue condition is omitted

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/discrimination-problem.md`
- **Location**: line 111, *Asymmetry-Breaking Signatures*
- **Current text**: "...including regimes where human metacognition shows anti-correlation **under weak internal evidence** (Rebouillat et al. 2021)..."
- **Problem**: The regime is *weak internal evidence **plus deceptive external cues***, and both sibling articles flag the full specification as load-bearing:
  - `obsidian/concepts/anti-correlated-metacognitive-signal.md:55` — a section titled *Regime-Conditional, Not Universal*: "The anti-correlation claim is **conditional on the experimental regime** and is **not a universal inversion**... Outside the **weak-internal-evidence-plus-deceptive-cue** regime, classical positive correlation between confidence and accuracy is reliably observed." Line 57: "This qualifier is load-bearing. Treating the Rebouillat finding as a universal inversion would overstate its evidential reach."
  - `obsidian/topics/anti-correlation-probes-for-ai-consciousness.md:54` — "This is not a universal inversion. Outside the weak-internal-evidence-plus-deceptive-cue regime, confidence tracks accuracy positively."
  - `obsidian/concepts/training-contamination-confound.md:37` — "confidence rising as internal evidence weakens **under deceptive cues**."
- Stating the regime as weak internal evidence alone materially widens the claim, and it does so in the one paragraph where the article is licensing an *upgrade* of an absence-claim. The overstated regime makes the asymmetry-breaker look more broadly applicable than it is.
- **Severity**: High. Confirmed against three sibling files; the corpus is uniform and this file is the outlier.
- **Recommendation**: Amend to "anti-correlation under weak internal evidence combined with deceptive external cues" (or "in the weak-internal-evidence-plus-deceptive-cue regime"), matching the corpus wording. One-clause fix. The citation metadata itself is correct and needs no change.

### Issue 3: The voids framing presupposes the realist answer to the question the article says is undecidable

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/discrimination-problem.md`
- **Location**: line 131, *The Voids Connection*
- **Current text**: "The discrimination problem identifies a structural [void](/voids/): **a region of fact** about which our best **current** epistemic instruments are systematically unable to deliver verdicts. **It is not merely difficult; it appears principled.**"
- **Problem**: Two distinct issues in one sentence pair.
  1. *Realism presupposition.* A "region of fact" that instruments cannot reach is available only if there is a fact — i.e. only if phenomenal realism is true. The article at line 62 sets out the illusionist reading on which there is nothing for introspection to fail to detect, and its entire thesis (line 76) is that settling realism-vs-illusionism "requires a prior metaphysical commitment... imported from outside the data." The *Dualism* subsection marks its dependency correctly ("**If** consciousness is non-physical... the discrimination problem is exactly what we should expect"); the *Voids Connection* drops the conditional and asserts the void flat. Under illusionism the problem dissolves rather than becoming a void, and the section should say so.
  2. *Epistemic/metaphysical register collision.* "Our best **current** epistemic instruments" is an instrument-relative, contingent claim; "it appears **principled**" is an in-principle claim. The sibling `obsidian/topics/brain-internal-born-rule-testing.md:157` disciplines exactly this distinction — "the corridor's unfalsifiability is **instrument-relative rather than principled**" — and this page has not inherited it. Whatever argument grounds "principled" is not given; the zombie stipulation cannot supply it, since a stipulation is not a demonstration of a limit.
- **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: Mark the framework dependency (the void is a void *given* phenomenal realism; under illusionism the problem is dissolved rather than occluded — which is itself the bedrock clash the article already names), and separate the two registers: the *in-channel* limitation is principled given the structural-asymmetry argument, while the reach of *current* instruments is contingent.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The no-report paradigm as a partial answer to the third-person channel

- **Current content says**: NCC methodology presupposes that report tracks experience, begging the question.
- **A critic would argue**: The field saw that objection twenty years ago and built no-report paradigms in response. Optokinetic nystagmus and pupillometry track conscious content with no report at all; the Cogitate-style adversarial collaborations are explicitly designed to make rival theories cash out incompatible predictions. Whether they *succeed* is contested (Dehaene and Changeux argue the no-report paradigm fails to separate consciousness from conscious access), but an article claiming the channel is structurally blocked owes an account of why the field's best attempt to unblock it fails.
- **Suggested response**: Adopt the Map's own graded position from `neural-correlates-of-consciousness.md:191` — the report-free paradigms are calibrated against report-based cases and inherit first-person grounding indirectly, so the dependence moves down the validation chain rather than disappearing. This is a stronger argument than the one currently in the article, and it is already written in the corpus.

### Bivalence at the boundary cases

- **Current content says**: "the live disagreement is not about the observable facts but about which of two phenomenality profiles—present or absent—is compatible with them" (line 105).
- **A critic would argue**: Both the Nagarjuna line and, more prosaically, the graded-consciousness literature reject that the profiles are binary. The Map has `degrees-of-consciousness` and `minimal-consciousness` pages; a partial or graded profile is a third option the sentence excludes by construction.
- **Suggested response**: Note that the binary framing is a simplification adopted for the structural argument, and that graded profiles inherit the same discrimination problem at every degree rather than escaping it.
