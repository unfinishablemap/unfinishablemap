## Executive Summary

The wing's two newest pages (sham neurofeedback, Parkinson's) are among the most carefully sourced on the Map: each reports what its trials measured, names what was read only in abstract, and declines support its data do not give. The older metabolic page has not kept pace with its siblings. Its first line of evidence describes Stender et al. (2014) as showing a pattern "threshold-like rather than gradual" where the authors write "gradual transition" and "tight correlation"; its gamma-surge section omits the caveats that three sibling pages already carry; and its own falsifier about lucidity far from death is met in part by a 2024 survey no live page cites. ## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
The neurodegeneration page (L129) says the order of faculty loss "does not discriminate filter from production" and that "the dualist case rests on the hard problem". That is the right division of labour: the clinical record constrains, and the argument for irreducibility is made elsewhere.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
The neurofeedback page (L93) declares Minimal Quantum Interaction and Dualism "not engaged" by the trials. A page that says which tenets its data cannot reach is more useful to a quantum-interface programme than one that finds a role for every tenet.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
The neurofeedback page (L43) separates what blinding equates (belief about one's arm) from what it cannot (the session-by-session experience of contingent control). The first-person difference between the arms is identified without being inflated into evidence.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
The conversion page treats the symptom as active generation (a prior the system fulfils, L87) and still declines to read that as more than an architectural description. No tier is raised on process-friendly grounds anywhere in the wing.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
The Parkinson's page lets the evidence run against the reading the Map's own kinesia page had stated (L65, "what it does not fit is the specific prediction the kinesia page stated") and credits the freezing row to the rival reading (L67). Agency is taken seriously enough to be tested.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)
The neurodegeneration page (L89) on musical responsiveness: whether the response is experienced "cannot be settled from the outside". The limit is stated at the point where the page would most like to cross it.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)
The strongest verdict in the wing is the neurofeedback page's "The Map does not count it as support" (L85) followed by the debit entered at L87. The conversion page's partition (L121: architecture *strongly supported*, sedation recovery a *realistic possibility*, interface reading a *live hypothesis*) is the scale used as intended. The Birch persona's concern is the metabolic page: its lede says consciousness "fails to track metabolic supply" across five settings, and for the first of them the cited authors conclude the opposite. That is flagged as Priority item 2, a calibration repair, and no expansion is proposed on that page until it is made.

#### 1. The "lucidity far from death" falsifier is partly met by a published survey the Map does not cite (two files)

- **Files and anchors** (count 1 each):
  - `topics/consciousness-under-extreme-metabolic-constraint` L115: `**Terminal lucidity without proximity to death.**`
  - same page L101: `with the majority of patients dying within hours to days of the lucid episode`
  - `topics/consciousness-and-neurodegenerative-disease` L67: `often occurring in the final days of life`
  - same page L75: `with lucidity appearing only in rare, transient episodes near death`
- **Defect**: the metabolic page says the filter account "would lose its specificity" if lucid episodes "occurred routinely in neurodegenerative patients at random points in disease progression—not concentrated near death", and treats this as untested. Griffin et al. (2024, *Alzheimer's & Dementia* 20(4), 2434-2443) asked. Of 279 episodes reported by 151 bereaved caregivers, 48.0% occurred in people who lived more than six months afterwards, 34.1% between one week and six months before death, and 17.9% within seven days (5.6% + 4.0% + 8.3%). The authors: "not all signal impending death", and the most common type "occurs among PLWD who continued to live > 6 months after the episode". `grep -rl` across `obsidian/` finds the paper only in one research note (`research/autonoetic-first-vulnerability-ordering-2026-10-09`); no live article cites it. The neurodegeneration page cites a different Griffin paper (2025) and still writes "often occurring in the final days of life"; its L75 also calls the episodes "rare" two paragraphs after reporting that 61.6%, 67% and 81% of caregivers had seen one.
- **Proposed text, metabolic L115** (append to the paragraph): "This condition is partly met. In a survey of 151 bereaved caregivers describing 279 lucid episodes, 48% occurred in people who lived more than six months afterwards and about 18% within the final week; the authors conclude that "not all signal impending death" (Griffin et al., 2024). The survey is retrospective, took up to two of each caregiver's most memorable episodes, and was not designed to estimate prevalence, and it does not show that episodes far from death match the near-death cases in depth. It does remove the assumption that lucidity clusters at the end, so the dying-disrupts-the-constraint explanation covers at most a minority of reported episodes."
- **Metabolic L101**: after the Batthyány and Greyson sentence add a clause pointing down the page, e.g. "a timing that a later caregiver survey with a wider sampling frame does not reproduce (see the falsifier list below)". Leave "Terminal lucidity is the chronic case suddenly behaving like an acute one" as the Map's reading of the near-death subset only, and say so.
- **Neurodegeneration L67**: replace "These episodes typically last minutes to hours, often occurring in the final days of life." with "These episodes typically last minutes to hours. The classic case reports cluster in the final days of life, but in the one caregiver survey that recorded timing, about half of the reported episodes came more than six months before death [12]."
- **Neurodegeneration L75**: "only in rare, transient episodes near death" becomes "only in transient episodes, most of them not near death on the one survey that recorded timing [12]", and the next sentence's "the rare collapse near death" needs the matching scope: the interface-degradation / filter-dissolution story as written explains only the near-death subset. Check the pronoun in the sentence after each replacement.
- **New reference entry (both pages)**: Griffin, J. M., Kim, K., Finnie, D. M., Lapid, M. I., Gaugler, J. E., Batthyány, A., Bangerter, L. R., Biggar, V. S., & Frangiosa, T. (2024). Developing and describing a typology of lucid episodes among people with Alzheimer's disease and related dementias. *Alzheimer's & Dementia*, 20(4), 2434–2443. https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.13667
- **Length**: metabolic 3140, headroom 859; neurodegeneration 3454, headroom 545. Not net-zero. Roughly +130 and +70 including the reference entries.

#### 2. The metabolic page reports Stender (2014) and Xu (2023) against their authors' stated readings

- **File**: `topics/consciousness-under-extreme-metabolic-constraint` (3140, headroom 859; not net-zero).
- **Anchors** (count 1 each): L57 `But the pattern it reveals is threshold-like rather than gradual.`; L37 `consciousness fails to track metabolic supply`; L87 `producing a threshold rather than a gradient`; L79 `precisely the neural signatures associated with conscious processing`; L81 `A production model must explain why a system generates more organised activity as its energy supply fails.`
- **Defect (a), Stender**: L57 says the pattern is "threshold-like rather than gradual" and that "Consciousness does not fade smoothly". The paper says: "a tight correlation exists between level of conscious awareness and energy turnover in the brain", and, because most patients fell between the two group means, "this finding is consistent with a gradual transition between the states". The abstract's summary is "a significant correlation between whole-brain energy metabolism and level of consciousness". The page's lede lists "minimally conscious states" first among settings where consciousness "fails to track metabolic supply"; in this study it tracks. The follow-up in 131 patients (Stender et al., 2016) reports that "42% of normal cortical activity represents the minimal energetic requirement for the presence of conscious awareness" and calls this "a global energetic threshold effect". `topics/covert-consciousness-and-cognitive-motor-dissociation` L85 already quotes that sentence and says "Production predicts this triangulation". The two pages answer the same question in opposite directions and do not link.
- **Proposed text, L57** (replace from "But the pattern it reveals" to the end of the paragraph): "The authors read their data as a graded correlation: "a tight correlation exists between level of conscious awareness and energy turnover in the brain", and because most patients' values fell between the two group means they call the finding "consistent with a gradual transition between the states". A follow-up in 131 patients put the minimal requirement at 42% of normal cortical activity and described "a global energetic threshold effect" (Stender et al., 2016). This line therefore shows consciousness tracking cortical metabolism down to a floor, which is what a production model predicts, as the Map's page on [covert consciousness](/topics/covert-consciousness-and-cognitive-motor-dissociation/) says of the 2016 result. The filter reading can describe the floor as a channel's minimum operating requirement; nothing in these data favours that description."
- **Dependent edits**: L37 lede, drop "minimally conscious states," from the list of settings (or say the first line tracks metabolism and is included as the baseline); L87 bullet, "producing a threshold rather than a gradient" becomes "a floor under a graded correlation, which production accounts predict equally"; L93 and L133 already say the pressure concentrates on the intensification cases, so they stand. Copy the Stender 2016 reference entry from the covert page (its L138) and keep the "(abstract)" tag.
- **Defect (b), Xu**: L79 calls the surge "precisely the neural signatures associated with conscious processing" and L81 says "A production model must explain why a system generates more organised activity as its energy supply fails." The authors supply the explanation and the limits themselves: "the resultant global hypoxia markedly stimulated gamma activities in two of the patients"; "both of these patients had a history of seizures"; the activation "is suggestive of elevated conscious processing in these patients, it does not demonstrate it", since "none survived the cardiac arrest"; and "it remains possible that this neurophysiological surge is epiphenomenal or pathological". Three siblings already carry these caveats (`apex/altered-states-as-interface-evidence` L159, `topics/anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface` L99, `voids/edge-states-and-void-probes` L129). This page carries only the n=2 caveat.
- **Proposed text, L79** (replace "precisely the neural signatures associated with conscious processing." with): "signatures associated with conscious processing in waking subjects. The authors attribute the surge to the global hypoxia that followed withdrawal, note that both patients who showed it had a history of seizures, and write that the activation "is suggestive of elevated conscious processing in these patients, it does not demonstrate it": none survived to report, and "it remains possible that this neurophysiological surge is epiphenomenal or pathological.""
- **Proposed text, L81** (replace the anchor sentence): "The authors' own account is physical: hypoxia stimulated the activity."
- **Also on L73**: "These occur during acute physiological crisis" states as fact the timing that L75 calls contested; "are reported after" is the neutral form.
- **Rules**: zombie possibility and epiphenomenalism are untouched (the "epiphenomenal" in the Xu quotation is the authors' word for the surge, not a thesis about consciousness; keep it inside the quotation marks). The filter reading stays "compatible with".
- **Stability Notes honoured**: the 2026-09-23 review fixed 42/44 internally and softened one Xu phrase; it did not compare L57 with the paper's Discussion. Its ledger entry "Stender et al. 2014 — real-correct" covered metadata and figures, which do hold.

#### 3. Conversion page: a self-contradiction on Hoover's sign, a DOI that resolves to another paper, a range whose source the gloss misdescribes, and a mouse study with no species

- **File**: `topics/conversion-disorder-as-consciousness-side-fault` (3814, headroom 185). Budget the four edits at about +25 words in total; no cut is needed.
- **(a) L75**, anchor `Hoover's sign is a force measurement in the *unaffected* limb` (count 1). The page's own L59 describes the sign correctly: hip extension "in the 'paralysed' leg" returns "when the patient flexes the *opposite* hip". McWhirter et al. (2011) define it as "weakness of voluntary hip extension with normal involuntary hip extension during contralateral hip flexion against resistance". The force assessed is in the affected leg. Replace with: "Hoover's sign is a force measurement in the affected limb, taken while the patient's effort is directed at the other one". #### 4. Sham-neurofeedback page omits the one double-blind trial in which the real arm beat an active sham

- **File**: `topics/sham-controlled-neurofeedback-and-the-consciousness-comparator` (3424, headroom 575; not net-zero, about +75 with the reference).
- **Anchors** (count 1 each): L53 `because few studies used the controls that would show it.`; L89 `Where that literature is well controlled, it shows that learning to move a signal and benefiting clinically come apart.`; L35 `The best double-blind trials find that the real arm does no better.`

- **Proposed text, L53** (append): "One trial did use such a control and found a difference. Young et al. (2017) gave 36 unmedicated adults with depression two sessions of feedback from the amygdala or from a parietal control region, double-blind; twelve of nineteen in the amygdala arm and two of seventeen in the control arm had depression scores fall by more than half. It is one small trial with one-week follow-up, and its control arm trained a different region, so it does not match the EEG trials' design. It is the case the sham-equivalence finding has to be scoped around."
- **Proposed text, L89**: "Where that literature is well controlled, it shows" becomes "In the EEG trials above, which are the best controlled, it shows"; L35: "The best double-blind trials find" becomes "The best double-blind EEG trials find". L81 already scopes by condition and protocol and stands; add "and one small fMRI trial in depression found a specific effect" to its list of unsettled points.

The page should say that it complicates both and supports neither. The debit at L87 is unaffected.
- **Reference entry**: Young, K. D., Siegle, G. J., Zotev, V., Phillips, R., Misaki, M., Yuan, H., Drevets, W. C., & Bodurka, J. (2017). Randomized clinical trial of real-time fMRI amygdala neurofeedback for major depressive disorder: Effects on symptoms and autobiographical memory recall. *American Journal of Psychiatry*, 174(8), 748–755. https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2017.16060637 (abstract)
