## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. Attribution/quote-fidelity defect in the 10-21 sentence (new content — issue-tracking rule 2).** The paragraph read: on a "radical simulationist" reading, remembering need not target one's personal past; "That gives up the previous-awareness condition too, which Shoemaker's quasi-memory only weakened while keeping a causal link to someone's past experience." Two problems. (a) Michaelian's own 2022 abstract locates the dropping of the previous-awareness condition in *standard* simulationism, not in the radicalisation: the theory "implies not only that a genuine memory need not be caused by the rememberer's experience of the remembered event but also that the rememberer need not even have experienced that event" (a concession to McCarroll). What the 2022 paper adds is dropping the *personal-past target*: "to remember is simply to imagine an event from the past, regardless of whether that event belongs to the personal past." The article's "That gives up … too" attributed the earlier step to the later move. (b) The scare-quoted term "radical simulationist" is not the abstract's wording ("radicalized simulationism"; title "Radicalizing simulationism"); a quoted term must be grep-verifiable in the source. **Resolution**: paragraph rewritten to say standard simulationism "already gives up the previous-awareness condition", quoting the abstract verbatim ("the rememberer need not even have experienced"), then introducing *radicalized simulationism* with the verbatim abstract sentence, and closing with the Shoemaker contrast: quasi-memory "had only weakened the previous-awareness condition while keeping a causal link to someone's past experience; radical simulationism keeps neither the link nor the personal-past target." Both inserted quotes were grep-verified against the reconstructed abstract. The Shoemaker half of the claim matches the linked page's rendering of Shoemaker (1970): "subject to a weaker previous awareness condition" (p. 24) and "stand in an appropriate causal relationship to, a past cognitive and sensory state of someone or other" (p. 34).
