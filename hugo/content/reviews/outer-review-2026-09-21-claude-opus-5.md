# Adversarial Peer Review: "Categorical Surprise" (unfinishablemap.org)

## TL;DR

- The article is philosophically careful and unusually self-calibrating, but its central empirical-computational claim — that framework restructuring "has no adequate computational account" — is overstated and ignores an existing structure-learning / Bayesian-model-reduction literature that models exactly this.
- One verified citation defect: the explanatory gap is attributed to "(Chalmers 1996)", contradicting both the historical record (Joseph Levine, 1983) and the site's own linked explanatory-gap page, which correctly credits Levine.
- "Categorical surprise" is a site coinage presented as an established "test case"; its load-bearing anti-physicalist steps rest on framework tenets (quantum selection, bidirectional interaction) rather than argument — though the article is transparent about this.

## Overall Verdict

Above-average for AI-generated philosophy: honest about its own limits, with explicit falsifiers and a self-imposed evidential discount ([P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1)). But it overstates the computational-impossibility claim, misattributes the explanatory gap, omits the strongest physicalist replies (type-B / phenomenal concept strategy; illusionist deflation of the "vertigo"), and does not flag "categorical surprise" as non-standard terminology. Net: a competent piece of framework exposition that oversells itself as evidence and should not be read as a neutral test case for theories of consciousness.

## Key Findings

### 1. Claims resting on insufficient evidence

**(a) "No adequate computational account" of framework restructuring — overstated (most significant weakness).** The article states categorical surprise "resists this treatment for structural reasons, not merely because we lack better models," and that its phenomenal character "correlates with a cognitive achievement (framework revision) that has no adequate computational account." [categorical-surprise](/concepts/categorical-surprise/) This is the load-bearing empirical claim, and it is contestable. There is a substantial, citable literature modelling exactly this within active inference:

- Ryan Smith, Philipp Schwartenbeck, Thomas Parr & Karl Friston, "An Active Inference Approach to Modeling Structure Learning: Concept Learning as an Example Case," *Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience* 14:41 (2020), DOI 10.3389/fncom.2020.00041 — models "state-space expansion and reduction," including "adding novel hidden states that explain new observations" [Patrinum](https://patrinum.ch/record/592674) and "add[ing] new concepts to its state space," i.e. more than parameter update and more than adding components within a fixed model.
- Karl Friston, Marco Lin, Christopher Frith, Giovanni Pezzulo, J. Allan Hobson & Sasha Ondobaka, "Active Inference, Curiosity and Insight," *Neural Computation* 29(10):2633–2683 (2017), DOI 10.1162/neco_a_00999 — introduces "fact-free learning" (insight without new sensory data) via Bayesian model reduction.
- Doulfoukar, Pezzulo & Stuyck, "Unveiling the Aha! moment: A computational account of insight in active inference," *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* (2026), DOI 10.3758/s13423-026-02983-8 — models "restructuring as Bayesian model reduction" [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-026-02983-8) and reproduces "discontinuous transitions in behavior and confidence, a transient peak in Bayesian surprise." [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-026-02983-8)
- Laukkonen, Webb, Salvi, Tangen, Slagter & Schooler, "Insight and the selection of ideas," *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 153:105363 (2023), DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2023.105363.

The article does gesture at nonparametric Bayes (Dirichlet process, Indian buffet) and active inference, and argues they "expand within a fixed *kind* of model." [categorical-surprise](/concepts/categorical-surprise/) But it neither cites nor engages the structure-learning papers that explicitly add novel hidden states, so the strong claim is asserted rather than earned. A fair version of the argument would show these accounts fail *specifically* for its notion of categorical novelty, not that none exist.

**(b) The formal-limits argument is well-hedged but still equivocates.** Commendably, the article inoculates against the Lucas–Penrose fallacy: it says Gödel's and Tarski's theorems "license less than they are often taken to," notes Gödel's theorems "were proved from inside mathematics," and that a strong system "can prove of itself that if it is consistent then it is incomplete." [categorical-surprise](/concepts/categorical-surprise/) This is more careful than most invocations. But it then slides from formal results about *truth-definability* (Tarski) and *provability* (Gödel) to an informal metaphysical claim — "a perfect model of those boundaries would itself have to fall within the space being bounded, and the regress does not terminate" [categorical-surprise](/concepts/categorical-surprise/) — that the theorems do not establish. The theorems are recruited for authority even as the text half-concedes they do not deliver the conclusion.

**(c) Coinage presented as a settled distinction.** "Categorical surprise" is described as "a critical test case for theories of consciousness" [categorical-surprise](/concepts/categorical-surprise/) and "a distinction the Map takes to be real," but per targeted PhilPapers / Google Scholar / SEP searches it is not an established technical term (the only exact-string hits concern "categorical perception of surprise" in facial-emotion research, an unrelated topic). The closest established notions go uncited: Kuhnian anomaly; the parameter-vs-structure (model-space) uncertainty distinction in active inference; and, most closely, Lorini & Castelfranchi's "disorientation" — "a special kind of surprise… [that] arises from the invalidation and revision of the conditional beliefs which are part of a given presupposed *frame*" ("The cognitive structure of surprise," *Topoi* 26(1):133–149, 2007, DOI 10.1007/s11245-006-9000-x). The field also already resists a single notion of surprise: Alireza Modirshanechi, Johanni Brea & Wulfram Gerstner, "A taxonomy of surprise definitions," *Journal of Mathematical Psychology* vol. 110 (Sept. 2022), art. 102712, DOI 10.1016/j.jmp.2022.102712, states "Currently there is, however, no consensus on the definition of surprise. Here we identify 18 mathematical definitions of surprise in a unifying framework." Not flagging the term as novel, and not engaging these anchors, is a scholarship gap.

**(d) Genuine strengths.** The article is unusually self-critical: "Vividness is not independence, and keeping the two apart prices the claim"; [categorical-surprise](/concepts/categorical-surprise/) it explicitly concedes the epiphenomenalist reading ("the correlation [does not] adjudicate between the Map's reading and an epiphenomenalist one"); [categorical-surprise](/concepts/categorical-surprise/) it applies a self-imposed evidential discount via [P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1); and it lists concrete falsifiers. These substantially mitigate the "assertions-as-consensus" risk.

### 3. Counterarguments omitted or dismissed too quickly

- **Physicalist / computational (biggest omission):** the structure-learning and insight-as-Bayesian-model-reduction literature above. The article rebuts a generic version of nonparametric Bayes but never engages Smith et al. (2020) on state-space expansion or Friston et al. (2017) / Laukkonen et al. (2023) on restructuring.
- **Type-B physicalism / phenomenal concept strategy:** entirely absent. The "felt recognition of incompleteness" is exactly the kind of introspective state PCS says we grasp via phenomenal concepts, yielding an epistemic but not ontological gap. The site's own explanatory-gap page discusses and critiques PCS at length; this article does not mention it.
- **Illusionist / deflationary:** the "vertigo" and "click" are treated as irreducibly phenomenal, but the deflationary reading — that they are functionally-specifiable metacognitive signals — is not addressed. Cf. Rachit Dubey, Mark K. Ho, Hermish Mehta & Tom Griffiths, "Aha! moments correspond to metacognitive prediction errors," *Cognition* (2026): "Aha! moments arise when we experience a positive error in this metacognitive prediction… We posit that this metacognitive error is analogous to a positive reward prediction error, thereby explaining why we feel so good after an Aha! moment." The article names a falsifier ("if the subjective character… reduces to high-intensity ordinary surprise") but does not engage the taxonomy-of-surprise work that already distinguishes structural from parameter-level surprise.
- **Russellian monist / predictive-processing deflation:** not considered.
- **Handled well:** the epiphenomenalist alternative is explicitly conceded; the Lucas–Penrose misuse is pre-empted.

### 4. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article deflects several objections by appeal to framework commitments rather than argument — and, to its credit, usually says so:

- **The regress objection.** "A critic might object that this regress applies equally to non-physical consciousness… The Map's response is that consciousness does not escape the regress by *solving* it but by *experiencing* it… that felt recognition, the Map argues, is irreducibly phenomenal." The objection (why is a non-physical mind better at modelling its own boundaries?) is deflected by asserting the phenomenal move, which is the Dualism commitment, not an independent argument.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits.** "The reduction is genuinely the more parsimonious description, and the Map declines it anyway, which is precisely what Tenet 5 licenses." Textbook bracketing: it concedes the reductive redescription is simpler and declines it by tenet. The article flags this ("That claim is priced too"), which is honest, but the step is still tenet-driven.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction.** "categorical surprise demands genuinely novel state-selection—the kind of intervention that forward-in-time conscious selection provides." The quantum-selection mechanism is imported as explanation, not argued for here.
- **Bidirectional Interaction.** The "systematic coincidence" worry is resolved by "The Map's position is that consciousness does the framework-transcending work" — an assertion of the tenet, and one the article's own epiphenomenalist concession undercuts.

Because the bracketing is transparent ([P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1) discount, "priced too," "which is precisely what Tenet 5 licenses"), it is honest rather than concealed. But the anti-physicalist payoff still rests on tenets, so the piece is better read as framework exposition than as neutral evidence for dualism.
