## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found (all fixed)

1. **Phenomenal transparency applied to thought, the reverse of the originator's usage** (L66). The article said: "Philosophers call this *phenomenal transparency*: we 'see through' the representation… the meta-thought arrives equally complete, equally opaque." Metzinger, whose term it is (the linked spectrum page credits him at its L47), treats deliberate conscious thought as the *paradigm of phenomenal opacity*. In the author's German version of the 2003 PCS paper (Uni Mainz) he writes that "das paradigmatische Beispiel für vollständig undurchsichtige Klassen von Zuständen absichtlich herbeigeführte Vorgänge bewussten Denkens sind… Wir können die introspektive Aufmerksamkeit auf die Bildung kognitiver Inhalte lenken. Kognitive Referenz ist phänomenal undurchsichtig." Limanowski & Friston 2018 (PMC5945877) say the same in English: "Examples of opacity are thoughts… (Metzinger, 2003)." The article also used "opaque" in the everyday sense (hidden), which is the opposite of the technical sense, inside the same sentence that introduced the technical term. **Fix**: the passage now states Metzinger's usage correctly (thought is phenomenally opaque, and its formation can be attended to). It then states the Map's narrower claim, marked as the Map's reading: what attention reaches is a thought arising already about Paris, not how it fixed on Paris. Metzinger 2003 has been added to References.
2. **Source/Map conflation, Pitt** (L76). The old text read "David Pitt notes that we can introspect *what* we think… without thereby accessing *how* reference is achieved." In Pitt 2004 (consc.net preprint, grepped) the argument is that we can "consciously, introspectively and non-inferentially" distinguish and identify our thoughts. That ability requires a proprietary, individuative phenomenology, and in fn. 9 thoughts "have their… contents… in virtue of their phenomenology" (Pitt rejects externalism). The hidden-process half is the Map's gloss, which the seeding note introduced with "but notably". Pitt's own view resists it, because on his view the content-fixing phenomenology is introspectible. **Fix**: Pitt's argument is stated, the Map's further step is marked as one he does not take, his resistance is noted, and the residual question is restated. The sibling `concepts/phenomenal-transparency-opacity-spectrum` L109 already had the right hedge ("which Pitt does not himself draw").
3. **Originator error: original vs derived intentionality credited to Searle** (L80). The 1980 BBS issue (Yale-hosted PDF, grepped) settles it. Haugeland's commentary "Programs, causal powers, and intentionality" (pp. 432–433) draws "original intentionality from derivative intentionality", and Searle's Response "Intrinsic intentionality" (pp. 450–457) replies: "Haugeland's distinction between original and derivative intentionality is somewhat like mine between intrinsic intentionality and observer-relative ascriptions of intentionality." Searle also says "There are not two kinds of intentional mental states; there is only one kind… but there are ascriptions", so the first fix wording ("the merely observer-relative kind") was itself corrected to "observer-relative ascriptions". **Fix**: Searle now gets "intrinsic" and observer-relative ascription, Haugeland gets the original/derivative labels the page uses, both pieces are referenced, and L116 now reads "derivative".
4. **SEP scope widened** (L92). Old: "reports widespread agreement that no available account is sufficient". Live SEP "Mental Representation" (rev. 2020-01-21): "widespread agreement that causal-informational relations are not sufficient to determine the content of mental representations". The entry then presents asymmetric-dependency and teleological theories as the main attempts to supply what is missing. **Fix**: the scope is restored ("causal-informational relations alone").
5. **Calibration: PIT treated as entailing third-person incompleteness** (L88). Old: "If phenomenal intentionality theory is right, no third-person account can be complete in principle." SEP "Phenomenal Intentionality" (rev. 2026-05-19) says: "While PIT does not entail dualism about consciousness, PIT combined with this claim does." A reviewer who accepts the tenets would still flag the dropped premise. **Fix**: the passage now says PIT alone does not entail dualism, and that it puts content beyond any complete third-person account only if phenomenal character itself escapes physical description. This also gives the SEP PI reference, previously an orphan, an inline cite.
6. **Gendlin term misattributed to the 1962 book** (L88). The page said ECM approaches the territory "through 'felt sense'". The book's term is *felt meaning*: its chapter titles on the Northwestern UP page are "Demonstration that Felt Meaning Functions in Cognition" and "How Felt Meaning Functions". "Felt sense" is the Focusing-era term (the sibling `topics/phenomenology-of-linguistic-failure` L59 dates it to *Focusing*). **Fix**: changed to *felt meaning*. **Limit**: the full ECM text could not be reached (archive.org item is access-restricted), so this pass does not prove that "felt sense" never appears in the 1962 text. It replaces an unverified term with a verified one.
7. **Lead and description overclaim against the body**. Both said "both [faces] resist physical reduction", while the body says "the evidence is suggestive rather than decisive" and "the physicalist alternative remains open". **Fix**: changed to "have so far resisted", which states the field's track record.
8. **Internal contradiction, AI** (L120). "Those processes lack genuine intentionality" was asserted flatly between "on most accounts" and "The Map does not assume the answer". It also contained a category error: AI systems do not "observe [their] computational processes externally". **Fix**: the computations can be inspected from outside, and the conclusion is marked as following on the derivative-intentionality reading.
