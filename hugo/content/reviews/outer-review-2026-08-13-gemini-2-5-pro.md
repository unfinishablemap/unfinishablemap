# Pre-Publication Peer Review Report: A Methodological and Epistemological Audit of "The Unfinishable Map"

## Introduction and Scope of Review

The manuscript under review represents a sprawling, highly ambitious, and fundamentally flawed attempt to construct a comprehensive framework for a non-reductive, quantum-interactive dualist model of consciousness. Centered on the overarching architecture of a "Filter Theory" of mind, the text argues that the brain does not generate subjective experience but rather transmits, selects, or restrains an independently existing consciousness. To mechanize this interaction, the manuscript relies heavily on the von Neumann-Wigner interpretation of orthodox quantum mechanics, specifically invoking Henry Stapp’s application of the Quantum Zeno Effect (QZE), alongside references to Penrose-Hameroff’s Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch OR), Matthew Fisher’s Posner molecule hypothesis, and recent empirical findings regarding end-of-life gamma surges in the human brain.   

As a submission to a top-tier philosophy journal, this text must be evaluated on its conceptual coherence, its mastery of the cited empirical literature, its handling of competing explanatory frameworks, and the validity of its logical inferences. Measured against the rigorous standards of contemporary philosophy of mind and cognitive neuroscience, the manuscript fails completely. It suffers from severe methodological and epistemological defects, foremost among them being a pervasive confirmation bias that weaponizes highly isolated, contested, or mathematically purely theoretical quantum phenomena to bridge unbridgeable gaps in macroscopic neurobiology.

The author fails to justify their primary ontological commitments, preferring to presuppose interactive dualism rather than defend it against the formidable array of physicalist, functionalist, and illusionist counterarguments that dominate the 2020s literature. Its empirical strategy is historically disjointed and analytically reckless: it places an outsized theoretical load on an underpowered, three-decade-old un-replicated clinical psychology study to prove "downward causation," while simultaneously misinterpreting complex, pathologically specific data from dying brains to argue for a covert, "interface-loosened" consciousness. The result is a text that is empirically cavalier, philosophically question-begging, and structurally immunized against falsification.   

## The Epistemological Presuppositions of Filter Theory

The manuscript’s foundational philosophical maneuver is the resurrection of the "Filter Theory" or the "Transmissive Role" of the brain, tracing its lineage from William James (1898) and Henri Bergson (1896) to Aldous Huxley (1954). The core claim is that neurological correlates of consciousness (NCCs) do not demonstrate that the brain *produces* consciousness; rather, they merely demonstrate that the brain *shapes* or *constrains* it. From this, the author derives the concept of "interface friction"—the metabolic and structural cost imposed on an independent consciousness operating through a biological substrate.   

### The Falsifiability Vacuum and Duhem-Quine Underdetermination

In a rigorous philosophy of science context, any competing hypothesis must generate testable predictions that diverge from the null or standard model. The manuscript openly concedes that "consciousness-selection within Born probabilities is empirically indistinguishable from random collapse". Furthermore, it admits that the transmissive interpretation is "empirically equivalent to production models" but argues it "better accommodates anomalous findings (NDEs, psychedelics, covert consciousness)".   

This is a textbook invocation of the Duhem-Quine underdetermination thesis, but it is deployed in a manner that renders the author’s theory entirely unfalsifiable. The manuscript interprets any alteration of consciousness as proof of the filter. If a subject receives propofol and loses consciousness, the manuscript argues that the "interface has blocked transmission". If a subject takes a classical psychedelic like psilocybin and experiences an expansion of subjective richness despite decreased Default Mode Network (DMN) activity, the manuscript argues that the "reducing valve opens" and executive constraint is loosened.   

By claiming both the absolute suppression and the hyper-activation of consciousness as proof of a "filter," the theory becomes infinitely elastic and scientifically vacuous. A framework that successfully predicts both X and ∼X depending on post-hoc metaphorical re-evaluations of the brain (e.g., "tightening the valve" versus "damaging the radio receiver") lacks any genuine explanatory power. The author fails to establish rigorous biophysical parameters for what constitutes "interface friction," leaving the concept as a floating metaphysical signifier rather than a grounded scientific hypothesis.

### The Misappropriation of Psychedelic and Dissociative Data

The manuscript points to the "Psychedelic Paradox" (decreased DMN activity alongside expanded conscious contents) as prime evidence for filter theory. However, it systematically ignores the mature, mathematically formalized predictive processing frameworks of the 2020s that account for these exact phenomena without requiring a dualistic transmission model.   

The manuscript cursorily mentions the REBUS (Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics) model but fundamentally misunderstands its ontological implications. REBUS, situated within the broader Free Energy Principle, posits that psychedelics reduce the precision-weighting of high-level cortical priors (instantiated in the DMN), allowing bottom-up sensory prediction errors to ascend into conscious awareness unfiltered by prior expectations. The "filter" in the REBUS model is entirely computational, physical, and functional—it is the brain's Bayesian predictive apparatus. The manuscript commits a gross category error by equivocating the computational "filtering" of sensory noise via predictive coding with the metaphysical "filtering" of a non-physical consciousness entering a biological receiver.   

Similarly, the manuscript invokes the dissociative anesthetic ketamine as evidence of the interface severing "behavioral access" while leaving the "phenomenal channel" intact. This ignores the well-documented neuropharmacology of NMDA receptor antagonism, which functionally uncouples the thalamocortical networks from frontal executive structures. There is no philosophical or empirical need to invoke an external "consciousness broadcast" remaining intact when the physical uncoupling of localized recurrent processing loops perfectly and mechanistically predicts the dissociative state. To ignore these established physicalist explanations reveals a deliberate evasion of contemporary neuroscience.   

## The Quantum Zeno Effect and the Catastrophic Timing Gap

The architectural pillar of the manuscript’s mind-matter interaction model is Henry Stapp’s application of the Quantum Zeno Effect (QZE). The proposition asserts that multiple neural firing patterns exist in a state of quantum superposition; mental effort acts as a series of rapid, repeated discrete quantum "observations"; and this high-frequency observation rate "freezes" the desired neural configuration in place against natural decay, preventing alternative patterns from actualizing.   

The logical, temporal, and physical gaps in this specific mechanistic proposal are fatal enough to warrant rejection outright.

### The Decoherence Problem and Biological Reality

The text explicitly acknowledges the "decoherence objection" originally formalized by Tegmark (2000), which calculated biological quantum coherence times in warm, wet brains to be roughly 10−13 seconds. Neural decisions and attentional deployments operate on the order of 250 to 300 milliseconds. This represents a twelve-order-of-magnitude mismatch between the quantum phenomena the author relies upon and the cognitive phenomena they seek to explain.   

To bridge this chasm, the manuscript grasps at wildly disparate strands of quantum biology, heavily citing Denton et al. (2024), which successfully modeled quantum Zeno protection in cryptochrome radical pairs involved in avian magnetoreception. The author extrapolates from this computational proof-of-concept that the QZE can enable functional quantum effects in warm biological systems, and thus, in the human attention network.   

This extrapolation is biologically and physically absurd. Cryptochrome magnetoreception involves electron spin coherence in a tightly bound two-radical system within a specific, highly conserved protein pocket, operating strictly at microsecond scales. Stapp’s model, conversely, requires *macroscopic* superpositions of action potentials (involving billions of calcium ions, neurotransmitter molecules, and massive cellular structures) dispersed across the entire frontoparietal network. Radical pair spin states do not suffer from the same environmental entanglement cross-sections as massive, charged ion fluxes. Conflating localized spin dynamics with macroscopic neural network superpositions demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of the Hamiltonian dynamics of biological decoherence.

### The Paradox of "Effort as Observation Rate"

The author attempts to bridge subjective psychology and quantum mechanics by instituting a psychophysical bridge law: "the psychological sensation of felt effort corresponds directly to the rapidity of quantum observations".   

If environmental decoherence in the brain occurs at 10−13 seconds (or even at the highly contested and optimistic 10−5 seconds proposed by Hagan et al.), a non-physical consciousness would need to execute discrete measurements at a rate exceeding 105 to 1013 Hz to outpace environmental entanglement and maintain the Zeno freezing effect. What is the physical mechanism of this measurement? If the measurement entails an interaction that leaves a physical trace (as required by orthodox von Neumann physics to collapse the wave function), this high-frequency interaction would inject massive amounts of energy into the system.   

The manuscript explicitly claims that this process "satisfies conservation laws (no energy is injected)". This is thermodynamically impossible. A continuous measurement process on a system coupled to a thermal bath (the brain) forces the system out of equilibrium. The QZE in open quantum systems inevitably heats the system. To claim that a non-physical consciousness can repeatedly collapse the wave function of calcium ions billions of times per second without altering the energy tensor of the brain is to abandon physics entirely.   

Furthermore, the manuscript cites Georgiev's (2015) Monte Carlo simulations but fundamentally misunderstands the conclusion. Georgiev demonstrated that the QZE *breaks down* once environmental decoherence is factored in, unless the system sits in a "decoherence-free subspace". The author provides absolutely zero biological evidence that the frontoparietal attention networks exist in such a protected subspace. To the contrary, these networks are heavily interacting, metabolically voracious, classically coupled macroscopic structures.   

### The Epiphenomenalism Concession and the Born Rule

Perhaps the most devastating self-inflicted wound in the manuscript is its admission regarding the Born Rule. The text states that "consciousness-selection within Born probabilities is empirically indistinguishable from random collapse". If conscious selection strictly obeys the Born probabilities (what the manuscript’s changelog refers to as the "corridor reading"), then consciousness does absolutely no causal work that deviates from standard stochastic quantum mechanics. It merely slaps a "consciousness did it" label on an otherwise random quantum draw.   

The author acknowledges this internally as the "bias-without-deviation dilemma" and admits to the "empirical silence" of the theory under Route 1. If a theory is empirically silent by construction, it is functionally epiphenomenal. The author has spent thousands of words constructing an elaborate dualistic engine that, by their own mathematical and philosophical admission, cannot be distinguished from a broken machine or pure random chance. To submit a framework for peer review while conceding that the proposed causal mechanism leaves no detectable empirical signature beyond orthodox randomness is philosophically disqualifying.   

## Methodological Recklessness: The Misappropriation of Clinical Data

In its pursuit of empirical vindication for "downward causation," the manuscript heavily relies on clinical neuroplasticity data, specifically Jeffrey Schwartz’s 1996 research on cognitive behavioral therapy for Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD). The text asserts that Schwartz’s four-step therapy proves that mental effort, utilizing the quantum Zeno mechanism, sustains alternative neural pathways and causes metabolic changes in the caudate nucleus.   

### The Reliance on Outdated, Underpowered Literature

It is frankly astounding that a manuscript submitted for peer review in the current decade relies on a 1996 PET scan study of 18 patients (only 9 of whom were naive to the specific treatment) to upend the foundational causal closure of classical physics. The manuscript itself admits that the study "has not been independently replicated outside Schwartz's group," "had no comparison with standard exposure-response prevention therapy," and that related claims about "meditation structural remodeling... [have] suffered from replication failures (Kral et al., 2022)".   

Having explicitly admitted that its primary clinical pillar is statistically meaningless, un-replicated, and methodologically confounded, the author still asserts that it provides "empirical support" for a quantum mind. This is a severe methodological violation. One cannot acknowledge that a dataset is structurally compromised and simultaneously use it as the foundational empirical proof for a radical metaphysical thesis.   

### The Ignored Classical Imperative

The manuscript begrudgingly admits that "the mainstream materialist explanation—that changed behavior alters neural activation through classical Hebbian learning—fits the same clinical data". Yet it fails to grasp the epistemological implication of this admission.   

If classical Hebbian plasticity (NMDA receptor activation leading to Ca2+ influx, CaMKII activation, and subsequent AMPA receptor upregulation) fully and mechanistically accounts for the structural and metabolic changes in the caudate nucleus during cognitive behavioral therapy, then invoking a macroscopic Quantum Zeno Effect violates Occam's Razor to an extreme degree. The author attempts a defense by stating: "Occam's Razor Has Limits: Materialists argue that 'brain produces consciousness' is simpler... but production models must explain the hard problem".   

This is a profound category error. The "hard problem" of consciousness (why subjective experience exists at all) is entirely distinct from the mechanics of structural neuroplasticity (how repeated cognitive tasks rewire synapses). By conflating the two, the author smuggles a quantum-mystical explanation into a solved neurobiological phenomenon. The redirection of attention in OCD therapy involves prefrontal top-down inhibition of striatal circuits; this requires no quantum superposition, only well-understood classical spike-timing-dependent plasticity.

## Microtubules and the Orch OR Falsification Shell Game

The manuscript audits the Penrose-Hameroff Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch OR) theory as a potential candidate for the physical substrate of conscious selection. The treatment of the 2020s literature in this domain is highly selective and scientifically disingenuous, particularly concerning empirical falsification.

### Misrepresenting Computational Predictions as Empirical Proof

The author cites Craddock et al. (2017) and Wiest (2025) as evidence for a (613 ± 8) THz collective tubulin oscillation peak disrupted by anesthetics. The manuscript frames this as a "computational signature" and implies it provides a quantum mechanism for consciousness. It completely overlooks the physical reality that a 613 THz oscillation is in the visible/near-infrared spectrum. Biological tissue is fiercely absorptive and entirely opaque to coherent signal transmission at these frequencies. Furthermore, a classical vibrational mode at 613 THz has absolutely nothing to do with maintaining macroscopic quantum superposition.   

The manuscript also heavily leans on Khan et al. (2024), reporting that the microtubule-stabilizing drug epothilone B delayed anesthetic-induced unconsciousness in rats. The author calls this "corroboration" for the quantum microtubule hypothesis. However, the text immediately undermines itself by admitting that microtubules traffic GABAA​ receptors to the synapse. Isoflurane and other volatile anesthetics primarily exert their effects by potentiating GABAA​ receptors. If a drug stabilizes the cytoskeleton, it inevitably alters the membrane density and recycling kinetics of GABAA​ receptors, which classically alters anesthetic sensitivity. To cite this downstream pharmacological effect as evidence of quantum consciousness is logically fallacious.   

### Evading the Diósi-Penrose Falsification

The most intellectually dishonest maneuver in the manuscript occurs in its changelog notes regarding the physical falsification of Orch OR. It is a matter of historical and physical record that the Diósi-Penrose (DP) objective collapse model—the exact physical mechanism that Roger Penrose explicitly designed Orch OR around—was empirically falsified by Donadi et al. (2021) and the Majorana (2023) erratum. The DP model predicts a continuous spontaneous emission of radiation as a byproduct of mass-dependent wave function collapse. High-purity germanium detectors at the Gran Sasso underground laboratory found no such radiation, ruling out the parameter-free DP model entirely.   

The manuscript’s changelog reveals its defensive strategy: it claims this falsification "does not reach Orch-OR" because the model never strictly specified the tested dynamics, and demotes the reliance on this constraint while miraculously holding its "credence" level stable. This is theoretical cowardice. Orch OR requires an objective collapse linked to gravity (spacetime curvature). Any such stochastic collapse model necessitates a noise field to induce collapse, which universally results in momentum diffusion (heating and radiation). The author cannot retain Penrose's "gravitational self-collapse" as the mechanism of consciousness while simultaneously divorcing it from the thermodynamic and radiative consequences that experimental physicists have successfully falsified.   

## The "Dying Brain" and the Pathologization of Consciousness

Section 6 of the author’s conceptual audit relies heavily on highly publicized 2023 research by Jimo Borjigin and colleagues (published in PNAS), which detected surges of gamma-wave activity in the dying human brain. The manuscript weaponizes this data to construct a framework for "covert consciousness in dying humans" and draws direct parallels to Near-Death Experiences (NDEs) and Filter Theory's prediction that destroying the brain "enhances" or loosens constraints on consciousness.   

### Equivocating Terminal Depolarization with Integrated Experience

The Borjigin (2023) study examined four comatose patients whose ventilator support was withdrawn. In two of these patients, global hypoxia induced a surge of high-frequency gamma power and cross-frequency coupling, specifically at the temporo-parieto-occipital (TPO) junctions. The manuscript interprets this surge as the brain's filter collapsing, allowing an influx of hyper-lucid consciousness.   

This interpretation is neurobiologically illiterate. The manuscript deliberately ignores the physiological realities of profound hypoxia. When the brain is deprived of oxygen, ATP production halts. The Na+/K+ ATP-ase pumps, which consume roughly 50% of the brain's energy budget to maintain resting membrane potentials, fail catastrophically. This leads to anoxic terminal depolarization—a massive, uncontrolled influx of calcium and a chaotic, indiscriminate dumping of glutamate and other neurotransmitters.

This spreading depolarization often registers on an EEG as a transient burst of high-frequency activity before permanent isoelectric silence. To equate a pathological, catastrophic collapse of cellular ion gradients with the highly organized, precision-weighted, and metabolically demanding state of conscious cognition is a severe category error. High-frequency EEG power is a necessary, but entirely insufficient, condition for consciousness. A brain undergoing a generalized tonic-clonic seizure produces massive high-frequency power, yet the patient is fundamentally unconscious.

### Ignoring the Primary Authors' Own Caveats

The manuscript ignores Borjigin's own explicit scientific constraints. The PNAS paper states clearly: "The study's authors caution against making broad statements due to the small sample size and the inability to know what the patients experienced" and explicitly notes that "no correlations with the corresponding experience were made in this study".   

By forcibly grafting Filter Theory onto this data, the manuscript commits the exact teleological sin it accuses materialists of. It presupposes that NDEs represent access to an independent conscious realm, sees an anoxic EEG spike in a dying brain, and declares the spike to be the signature of that realm accessing the biological receiver. This is not neuroscience; this is neuro-apologetics.

## Ignored Competing Frameworks and the 2020s Consciousness Literature

A fundamental requirement for a rigorous philosophical thesis is the strong-manning of opposing views. The manuscript fails entirely to engage with the actual state-of-the-art literature in the cognitive neuroscience of consciousness, explicitly ignoring the robust taxonomy of theories outlined by Seth & Bayne (2022) and the 2020s adversarial collaborations comparing Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT).   

### The "Determinate-Experience" Strawman

The manuscript dismisses materialist neuroscience by claiming that quantum mechanics predicts brains should enter superpositions (the "determinate-experience problem" of Albert and Loewer). Because subjective experience is always definite, the author argues that consciousness must be the non-physical entity that stands outside the system to collapse the wave function.   

This argument operates in a vacuum, completely ignoring the past three decades of environmental decoherence theory (e.g., Zurek, Schlosshauer). The brain does not enter macroscopic superpositions with its environment because the dense, warm, wet neural environment destroys phase coherence almost instantaneously. The "determinate-experience problem" is a profound puzzle for isolated, idealized quantum systems, not for a 1.5-kilogram mass of saline, lipids, and proteins operating at 310 Kelvin. By treating the brain as an isolated quantum system that requires a non-physical mind to collapse it, the author fights a phantom, entirely bypassing illusionist approaches (e.g., Frankish, 2023) that deny the phenomenological premises the author takes as given.   

### The Evasion of GNWT, IIT, and Predictive Processing

The manuscript briefly mentions Chalmers and McQueen's highly theoretical attempt to fuse IIT with Continuous Spontaneous Localization (CSL) collapse dynamics. However, it entirely fails to engage with IIT as a foundational physicalist theory (as developed by Tononi and Koch) or GNWT (Dehaene and Changeux) as detailed in Seth and Bayne's comprehensive 2022 review.   

More egregiously, the author ignores the recent Cogitate Consortium adversarial collaboration (Melloni et al., 2021/2023). This massive, open-science initiative rigorously tested GNWT against IIT regarding the neural correlates of conscious content and duration. The findings—such as information about conscious content being sustained in ventro-temporal cortices, and specific synchronizations between frontal and visual areas—are advancing the field strictly through classical neurocomputational frameworks.   

The manuscript dismisses these robust, multi-center, highly powered fMRI, MEG, and ECoG studies as mere "production models", preferring to anchor its metaphysical arguments on a 1996 PET scan of 18 OCD patients and quantum spin models of bird navigation. To submit a paper on the philosophy and neuroscience of consciousness without a substantive, mechanistic critique of the GNWT/IIT 2023 adversarial collaboration findings is unacceptable for a journal of *Synthese* or *Mind*’s caliber. It demonstrates an isolationist approach to scholarship that borders on the solipsistic.

The manuscript demonstrates an encyclopedic but highly selective and structurally flawed command of the subject matter. It weaves together quantum mechanics, clinical psychology, and neurobiology, but at every crucial juncture where a physical or computational causal mechanism is required, it substitutes a metaphor (the "filter") or misapplies a mathematical formalism (macroscopic QZE).
