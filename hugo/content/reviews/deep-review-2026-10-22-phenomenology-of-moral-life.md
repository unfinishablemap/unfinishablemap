## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **L137 (Occam's Razor Has Limits) ran Tenet 5 forward.** It said the reductive account "cannot accommodate the gestalt character, the deliberative oscillation of attention, or the deepening through sustained regard", and that its parsimony is "false economy". This conflicts with the page's own L73 concession that the neural and phenomenological descriptions "may pick out the same process". It also conflicts with `tenets.md` L172: the Tenet 5 self-binding "forbids running anti-parsimony forward to establish that functional organisation does not suffice". A tenet-accepting reviewer would still flag it, so it is a calibration error.
  - **Resolution**: the reductive account can model all three as functional dynamics, and what it leaves open is why they are felt. The tenet now works only defensively, and the insufficiency conclusion is left to the hard-problem and conceivability arguments at L121.
- **L115 misdescribed Haidt's model.** The page said extended deliberation "fits poorly into the confabulation model". But Haidt 2001 (p. 819) gives reasoning a causal role "when reasoning runs through other people", and allows private reasoning to override an intuition "by sheer force of logic", though this "is hypothesized to be rare". I verified this from raw text quoted in Fine 2006. Positions that shift over days therefore sit inside his model.
  - **Resolution**: the first qualification now says the dispute is about how often reasoning leads, not whether it can. "show" was also softened to "present evidence that".
- **L63 (Prinz) fails the cited-author-stance leg, and one detail is unattested.** Prinz is a constructive sentimentalist and relativist. His sentiments represent response-dependent, secondary-quality-like properties (NDPR review, pp. 84–85, 101). The page cited him for the perceptual reading without saying so.
  - "Disgust, anger, and sympathy" did not match the moral emotions Prinz is reported to centre: anger, contempt and disgust for other-blame, and guilt and shame for self-blame.
  - **Resolution**: the list now reads "anger, disgust, and guilt", and a clause adds that "for him the properties so detected are response-dependent and culturally variable, a sentimentalist relativism the Map does not share".
- **L55 and three sibling lines used the banned "not X. It is Y." construction.** These were L55 ("not decorative—it is"), L65 (McDowell), L89 (guilt) and L93 (self-interest). All four were rephrased to state the positive claim, with the contrast integrated.

### Medium Issues Found

- **L119 called nomological epiphenomenalism "systematically coincidental", a straw man.** A Chalmers-style view posits psychophysical laws, so the correlation is lawful.
  - **Resolution**: the page now concedes that laws "can make the correlation between the two regular rather than accidental". It then presses the idle-fit point: "a fit this close, with the felt side doing no work, is less plausible". The anti-epiphenomenalist conclusion stands, and no concession was made. L121 "no coincidence" became "no idle accompaniment" so that the pronoun and the concept still bind.
- **L125 made two unsourced claims about *mettā*.** It said the practice is "uncovering something already present", and that practitioners report "correction from the object of attention".
  - **Resolution**: the paragraph now rests on Buddhaghosa's *Visuddhimagga* ch. IX: graded stages, the barriers broken down, and anger expected at the enemy stage. I verified this in raw text in Ñāṇamoli's compilation (Wheel 7, accesstoinsight).
  - The perception-like reading is now labelled "On the Map's reading". The paragraph concedes that "skilled cultivation predicts the same course, so these reports are consonant with moral realism without testing it against projection".
  - The overclaim "does not reveal a projection mechanism; it reveals more moral structure" was removed. The Visuddhimagga reference was added.
- **L85 made Kant a straw man.** "Not a rational inference" ignored *Achtung*, the feeling that the moral law itself produces through reason (Groundwork 4:401n, from secondary quotation of the Gregor translation).
  - **Resolution**: Kant now locates the demand "in reason, whose recognition of the moral law itself produces the feeling of respect (*Achtung*)". The Map's contrast is that the felt quality is perceived in the situation, not produced by rational recognition.
- **L89 contradicted the linked page's definition.** It called a triage case that has a right answer a "tragic dilemma", but `voids/normative-void` L68–L72 defines tragic dilemmas as cases where "no choice is right".
  - **Resolution**: L89 now says "genuine moral conflict", and normative-void is presented as extending the case to tragic dilemmas, "where on its analysis no choice is right".
- **L131 used a straw target ("amygdala activation") and an overclaim ("compounds the explanatory gap").**
  - **Resolution**: it now says "no functional description of the circuitry involved". The moral case "multiplies the instances, sharpening the hard problem rather than supplying a separate argument for dualism", which matches L121.
- **L103 stated the Map's reading as fact.** "Not a series of discrete neural events" was asserted without a label.
  - **Resolution**: it now opens "on the Map's reading", the same form as the L133 fix of 10-21.
