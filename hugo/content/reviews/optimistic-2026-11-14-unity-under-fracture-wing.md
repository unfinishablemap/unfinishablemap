## Executive Summary

All four pages carry the same discipline: they name what fractures, name what survives, and then concede that the survival does not discriminate between the Map and its rival. HEM: "Hemispherectomy sharpens the discriminating question; it does not by itself answer it." INT: the interpreter data are "a signpost, not a refutation in either direction." MDM: "the standoff is between two metaphysical defaults, not between science and speculation." HEM and INT are the strongest. HEM's restriction of its own headline argument to anatomical hemispherectomy is a model of self-correction, and INT's anarchic-hand mirror is the page's own observation. Read together, the four make one map: every *function* the assaults reach breaks (channels, narrated reasons, timing as a read-off, autobiography), and what is left each time is the unity of the present field. No page currently points to the others, so no reader ever sees that map. CIS has the most to gain. It is the oldest page, it is the only one that names none of the others, and two of its sentences have fallen behind specialised siblings that now say the same thing more carefully.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

MDM puts the dispute where Chalmers puts it. The verificationism objection is stated as an objection to a premise, not as a refutation: "the architecture entails elimination only if "undetectable" licenses "unreal."" (MDM L57). CIS L133 makes the cleanest conceptual cut in the wing. It splits the *composition* question (which experiences fall within one stream) from the *ownership* question (whose stream it is), and observes that "drawing a boundary does not supply the indexical fact the boundary encloses." Chalmers would see in that the unity analogue of the hard problem: a full theory of binding would still leave the second question open. INT L61 gives the illusionist the whole of narrated agency and keeps only the field. That is the concession pattern Chalmers recommends: grant everything functional and isolate the residue.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The wing's main virtue here is restraint. HEM never puts a quantum gloss on hemispherectomy. CIS's quantum sections are conditional throughout, and L127 ends by placing the self-forming picture at "a framework-internal consequence, not an established feature of agency." Stapp would value MDM L67 more. It refuses to concede that the phenomenal residue is undetectable *in principle*: Tenets 2 and 3 make consciousness efficacious at the interface, and `positions/quantum-interface` keeps one horn of the discriminability dilemma specifiable. Process 1 stays an open empirical question rather than being handed to Dennett as a free concession.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Nagel's 1971 brain-bisection paper (cited in `concepts/unity-of-consciousness` L91) concluded that split-brain cases leave no whole number of minds to count. HEM finds the one surgery where the count has an answer: after *anatomical* hemispherectomy, "one mind or two?" has nothing left to be about (L44). HEM then finds where Nagel's puzzle comes back in a sharper form. In functional hemispherotomy the disconnected tissue is cut off, arguably, more completely than a callosotomy leaves either hemisphere (L86). Nagel would also welcome CIS's opening, "The self is not a static object but an ongoing achievement." (L36), and its insistence that remembering is re-encountering experience "as one's own" (L121).

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

CIS's "ongoing achievement" framing and its diachronic integration (working memory, episodic memory, protention) are process thinking: the self is made, moment by moment. The Map already has the page that states this as doctrine, `concepts/integration-as-activity` ("Integration is something consciousness does, not what it is"), and CIS does not link it (0 links either way). This praise stays on the compatibility axis. No organism or system is moved up the evidential scale on process-philosophical grounds anywhere in the wing.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

CIS L127's "self-forming across time" is Kane's vocabulary of self-forming actions. It is stated conditionally and carries the mechanism-debt anchor, which is the right register; Kane is not cited (see Ideas for Later). INT does Kane a different service. L63 separates unity of the *field* from unity of *control* and sends the control question to `the-divided-will`. Kane needs a single chooser at the moment of a self-forming action, and INT honestly declines to supply one from interpreter data. Kane would rather know the gap is there than have it papered over.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

HEM L92 contains the wing's most McGinn-like sentence: for functional hemispherectomy "the Map does not know whether the disconnected remnant hosts anything at all." The page also explains why this is hard, not merely unknown. An island of awareness is defined as unable to report itself, so the behavioural criteria are "constitutively unavailable" (L86). MDM L67 admits that the Map's warrant for the residue is "avowedly non-empirical". CIS L133 leaves the vertiginous question closed to every theory of binding. Each page marks a limit and does not fake a solution.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

On calibration, this is the strongest wing reviewed this month.

- **HEM L78**: Fisher 2022 is one patient: "an existence proof against the strong form of the deflation … The Map treats it at that strength and no higher." HEM L88 then shows the reverse care. It checks that Fisher's "functional hemispherectomy" was an infarction, not a surgical disconnection, so the islands caveat does not reach the article's best case. HEM L90 moves the caveat to where it does reach: four of Kliemann's six participants had functional operations.
- **HEM L94** declines the tempting tier upgrade. If a severed remnant could host an island of awareness, the filter reading gets no free point: "A filter or interface account owes some story about what makes a piece of severed cortex an interface for anyone". This is a boundary case of the organoid kind, and the page assigns it no tier at all. That is the right way to read Bayne, Seth and Massimini's question-titled paper. The pattern is *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating*, enacted rather than merely named.
- **INT L77**: Pinto 2017 carries three qualifications (n = 2, the cross-field failure replicated, and Schechter and Bayne's agency-versus-experience objection), and the page notes that the objection "cuts in both directions". INT therefore does not read Pinto as evidence *for* phenomenal unity.
- **CIS L151 and L191**: DID reports are kept as reports. On felt continuity, "every branch-relative successor feels the same continuity, so treating the feeling as confirmation reads felt weight as evidential weight."
- **The wing-level restraint deserves naming.** Four cases that are each "consistent with" the interface reading could easily be summed into "supported by". None of the four pages does that, and nothing in this review should be read as doing it. Any future synthesis of the wing must keep that restraint (see Expansion Opportunities).

## The Wing's Unclaimed Insight: A Survival Map

Laid side by side, the four pages describe a single pattern:

| Assault | Page | What breaks | What survives | Does it discriminate? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Half the substrate removed | HEM | specific channels ("capable but costly" word and face recognition) | unity per se (anatomical cases only) | No. Reorganization predicts the same (L106) |
| Reasons confabulated | INT | narrated authorship | the unity of the present field | No. Construct versus narrate is under-determined (L65) |
| No Cartesian Theater | MDM | timing as a read-off; a central stage | the phenomenal residue, as a framework commitment | No. Two metaphysical defaults (L67) |
| Narrative dissociated | CIS | autobiographical continuity (DID; sleep seams) | reported minimal self; the ownership question | No. Report-based (L151) |

Two things follow, and the Birch persona's restraint sets their limit. First, what breaks is always a function and what survives is always the same item: the unity of the present field and whose field it is. That is a coherent profile, and it is the profile an interface reading predicts. Second, all four "no" entries stand, and four non-discriminating cases add up to one non-discriminating pattern. The pattern is still worth showing to readers, because each page alone looks like an isolated defence, while the set shows that the Map's unity claim has a single, consistent shape across very different evidence. `apex/self-construction-constructor` already uses MDM and INT as sources. HEM is the substrate-loss case that apex lacks.

There is a second, sharper point. INT answers the **confabulation route** to illusionism about unity: unreliable narration does not reach the field (L61–L63). It never mentions the **other route**, which is Dennett's own: MDM's denial that there is a single canonical stream to narrate over (MDM L39, "There is no single stream of consciousness"). MDM handles that route, as a framework boundary (L69). Each page covers half of the illusionist's case and does not know the other half exists. One sentence in INT and one pipe in MDM would join them.
