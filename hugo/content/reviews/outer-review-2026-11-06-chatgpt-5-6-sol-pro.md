# Independent critical audit: “Substance Dualism vs Property Dualism”

## Overall judgment

The article is a useful and unusually candid orientation piece, and its October 2026 revisions have materially improved it. It now distinguishes the neutrality of the Dualism tenet from the Map’s downstream use of a persisting subject, narrows the combination problem toward panpsychist and Russellian views, and gives a much better-calibrated account of avian magnetoreception. [concepts/substance-property-dualism](/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)

It nevertheless needs **major revision** before it can function as a reliable source-critical comparison. I found no fabricated bibliography entries: all fourteen listed references exist. The more serious problems are mismatches between sources and claims:

- Lowe is assimilated too closely to a conventional immaterial-mind picture.
- Zimmerman is attributed an argument from property dualism to “panpsychism or epiphenomenalism” that his paper does not make.
- Bailey, Rasmussen and Van Horn are incorrectly credited with a haecceitistic answer to the pairing problem.
- Frankish is cited for illusionism, but his answer to the article’s regress objection is omitted; the linked Map article expressly concedes that the bare regress fails.
- The decoherence section moves from the fact that decoherence does not by itself solve the measurement problem to the unsupported possibility of post-decoherence conscious outcome bias.

The central position—“the Map accepts either view”—is defensible at the level of the Dualism tenet and the irreducibility arguments. It is not defensible without qualification as a description of the Map’s complete operational architecture, because the agency cluster and a principal anti-Everett argument depend on Posit One’s persisting, individuated subject. The article itself now acknowledges that dependency, making its continued description of substance dualism as merely a “philosophical preference” too weak. ## 1. Recent-version and changelog check

The live article is dated 29 October 2026. Two recent changes are especially relevant.

On 23 October, the site updated the SEP citation to Robinson and Weir’s 2025 revision, corrected the account of Chalmers so phenomenal properties are lawfully tied to physical organisation rather than said to arise from complexity, and narrowed the combination problem from property dualism generally to constitutive panpsychist and Russellian views. Those were sound corrections. [workflow/changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

On 29 October, the article replaced the old unconditional “tens of microseconds” claim about avian magnetoreception with the much more careful present formulation: approximately 2–10 μs inferred from behavioural radiofrequency experiments, with the older longer estimate tied to a result that has not held up straightforwardly. The changelog also expressly recognises that cryptochrome concerns protected electron-spin chemistry rather than tubulin conformations and therefore does not corroborate the microtubule estimate. That is a significant methodological improvement. [workflow/changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

The remaining defects therefore belong to the current version, not merely to a stale search-engine copy.

## 2. Cartesian dualism and the historical interaction problem

### What is accurate

The basic Cartesian contrast is accurately stated: Descartes treats body as extended and divisible and mind as thinking, unextended and not divisible in the corresponding way. Elisabeth’s challenge is also correctly identified as a problem about how an unextended mind could act on an extended body. [concepts/substance-property-dualism](/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)

### The pineal-gland sentence is misleading

“Descartes answered … by pointing to the pineal gland” conflates two different parts of Descartes’s position.

The pineal gland was his proposed physiological locus for mind–body interaction. But his philosophical response to Elisabeth was not simply “the pineal gland.” He appealed to mind–body union as a third primitive notion, distinct from the notions of mind and body, and argued that this union was known through ordinary embodied experience rather than derived from the separate concepts of thought and extension. One may agree that this response is unsatisfactory, but it should be represented before it is rejected. The cited *Meditations* does not by itself support the claims about Elisabeth, the pineal gland or the later correspondence. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/emotions-17th18th/LD2Descartes.html)

A more accurate formulation would be:

> Descartes located the physiological interaction at the pineal gland, while answering Elisabeth’s metaphysical challenge by treating mind–body union as a primitive notion. Neither move supplies the requested mechanism.

### Leibniz is described anachronistically

The sentence shifts from “conservation of momentum” to “inject energy” as though those were one argument. Leibniz’s historical criticism concerned conservation of motion and, more specifically, total direction: Descartes had supposed the soul might redirect animal spirits without changing their quantity of motion, whereas Leibniz held that direction was conserved too. The modern objection in terms of energy or momentum conservation is related, but it should not be put into Leibniz’s mouth without qualification. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/leibniz-causation/)

The article also treats quantum indeterminacy too quickly as an “opening.” Standard quantum mechanics does not merely leave a blank after specifying possible outcomes; it constrains their probability distribution. A consciousness-dependent selector would therefore be an additional psychophysical law. It must specify its preferred basis, whether it preserves the Born probabilities, how it avoids detectable signalling or conservation anomalies, and what observation could distinguish it from ordinary stochastic evolution. Quantum indeterminacy makes such a proposal formulable; it does not by itself establish unused causal room.

## 3. Contemporary substance dualism

### Lowe: the largest taxonomic oversimplification

The article says Lowe’s naturalistic substance dualism treats mental substances as part of nature but outside the physical world described by physics. This obscures what is specifically non-Cartesian about Lowe.

Lowe’s dualism is principally a distinction between a person or subject of experience and that person’s organised biological body. He does not require the person to be capable of disembodied existence. Nor does he hold that persons can bear only mental properties: on his account, a person may possess physical properties in virtue of possessing a body. It is therefore misleading to place him under a generic picture of a “mental substance” standing over against the physical world. [ResearchGate+2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225702750_Non-Cartesian_Substance_Dualism_and_the_Problem_of_Mental_Causation)

The linked *Four-Category Ontology* article is more accurate. It presents Lowe’s framework as one in which substances are the bearers and exercisers of causal powers, and it explicitly notes that the Map departs from Lowe over quantum intervention and the causation of volition. The main article should import that precision. [concepts/four-category-ontology](/concepts/four-category-ontology/)

The claim that property dualism fits drugs, brain damage and neural tracking better than views in which mental substances can exist independently is also poorly discriminating. It may count against a strongly separable Cartesian soul, but not against Lowe’s embodied subject or Hasker’s brain-generated emergent subject. Both are designed to accommodate deep brain dependence.

### Hasker: substantially right, but insufficiently sourced

The distinction between property emergence and Hasker’s substance emergence is well made. Hasker argues that the developed brain produces a new individual subject with novel causal powers, not merely a new set of properties. [huntington.hykucommons.org+1](https://huntington.hykucommons.org/concern/generic_works/4c6ce94d-4020-415d-b47c-1d6c85ebe76e)

There are two deficiencies:

1. Hasker is named repeatedly but has no entry in the bibliography.
2. “Positing unified mental substances from the start” does not accurately characterise an emergent-substance theory. Hasker treats unity as primitive once the subject emerges, but the theory still owes an account of when, why and how one subject rather than several emerges from a developing or divided neural system.

Thus Hasker avoids the constitutive combination of micro-subjects, but he does not eliminate every unity problem. He relocates it to subject generation, individuation, persistence and possible fission.

### Zimmerman: a material attribution error

The article states that Zimmerman argues property dualism faces pressure toward either panpsychism or epiphenomenalism. That is not the argument of *From Property Dualism to Substance Dualism*.

Zimmerman’s central route is:

1. Some qualia may need an adverbial construal as modifications of the conscious bearer.
2. The property dualist must then identify the bearer.
3. Ordinary bodies and brains are vague in their boundaries.
4. Exact qualia bearers may therefore have to be unusual physical objects—a form of “speculative materialism.”
5. Such speculative materialism may not be preferable to emergent substance dualism.

He mentions in passing that some property dualists flirt with panpsychism, but panpsychism is not the conclusion of his argument. Epiphenomenalism is not one of its two principal destinations at all. [OUP Academic+2](https://academic.oup.com/aristoteliansupp/article-abstract/84/1/119/1780052)

The omission of Penelope Mackie’s direct published reply is also significant. Mackie argues both that ordinary material objects can remain plausible bearers and that Zimmerman undervalues speculative materialism. She does not refute substance dualism, but she directly challenges the inference for which Zimmerman is being cited. [Wiley Online Library+1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9264.2011.00305.x)

The Zimmerman sentence should therefore be replaced, not merely qualified.

### The Bailey–Rasmussen–Van Horn attribution is wrong

The article lists “invoking haecceities” as the Bailey, Rasmussen and Van Horn response to the pairing problem. The Map’s own dedicated pairing article now says the opposite: their argument proceeds through attacks on Kim’s spatial premise, the possibility of located souls, non-haecceitous laws and singular causation. Haecceitous laws appear only as a possibility in a footnote; they do no positive work in the paper. The Map’s actual haecceity extension is its own reconstruction, with Swinburne identified as the nearer published relative. [concepts/pairing-problem](/concepts/pairing-problem/)

This is both a source error and a direct contradiction between linked articles.

The main article also says the quantum interface “supports” the spatial response too easily. The pairing page is more careful: interface locality may address where interaction occurs, but it does not non-circularly ground why this subject is coupled to this brain. The pairing law and the onset of pairing remain owed. [concepts/pairing-problem](/concepts/pairing-problem/)

## 4. Property dualism and its varieties

### The core definition is standard but not exhaustive

The article defines property dualism as physical objects bearing irreducible phenomenal properties, with no separate mental substance. That captures the familiar combination of property dualism with substance materialism. It should not be presented as exhausting property dualism.

The current SEP notes that Chalmers takes property dualism to entail nonphysical properties while remaining neutral on whether nonphysical substances also exist. The Map’s own canonical scoping article similarly recognises “substance-bearing forms of property dualism.” [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dualism/)

The article therefore needs to separate at least four questions that its binary currently runs together:

| Dimension | Main alternatives |
| --- | --- |
| What bears experience? | Physical organism, nonphysical subject, process, bundle |
| What kinds of properties exist? | Physical only, irreducible phenomenal, neutral/intrinsic |
| What causes action? | Events, properties, substances/agents |
| What persists? | Organism, immaterial subject, psychological continuity, no further subject |

A property dualist may accept a persisting physical substance as the bearer of irreducible phenomenal properties. Conversely, accepting a persisting substance does not by itself establish a nonphysical substance.

### Chalmers is accurately introduced but causally miscontextualised

The account of Chalmers’s early naturalistic dualism is broadly accurate: phenomenal or protophenomenal properties are fundamental, and psychophysical laws connect them to physical organisation. [personal.lse.ac.uk+1](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/ROBERT49/teaching/ph103/pdf/Chalmers_The_Conscious_Mind.pdf)

But Chalmers’s 1996 framework is not an example of interactionist property dualism. He says the psychophysical laws do not interfere with physical laws and that the physical laws already form a closed system. He consequently accepts at least a significant degree of epiphenomenalism, while discussing strong interactionism as a possible way of denying closure rather than as his own default account. The Map’s linked *Interactionist Dualism* page states this correctly; the comparison article should do the same. [personal.lse.ac.uk+2](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/ROBERT49/teaching/ph103/pdf/Chalmers_The_Conscious_Mind.pdf)

It is correct that property dualism is logically compatible with interactionism. It is misleading to move from Chalmers’s naturalistic dualism to that general compatibility without marking the causal departure.

### Russellian monism is not simply a property-dualist variety

Russellian monism is placed under “Varieties of Property Dualism,” but it is a broader family. There are physicalist, neutral-monist, idealist and explicitly dualist Russellian versions. A physicalist Russellian monist does not accept the article’s opening claim that materialism fails, while a neutral monist may reject the physical/nonphysical property dichotomy altogether. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+2](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2024/entries/russellian-monism/)

This produces a local contradiction:

- The article says both substance and property dualism reject materialism.
- It then includes a family containing explicitly physicalist variants as a species of property dualism.

Russellian monism belongs in a neighbouring or intersecting category, not unqualifiedly inside property dualism.

### The Pautz quotation exists, but its scope is narrowed incorrectly

The Pautz quotation is real. He argues that **primitivist** Russellian monism requires brute grounding laws structurally comparable to psychophysical laws. He immediately contrasts it with reductive Russellian monism, whose identities are supposed to avoid that particular cost, before arguing separately against the reductive horn. [PhilPapers+1](https://philpapers.org/archive/PAUCRM.pdf)

The article currently makes the quotation sound like a verdict on Russellian monism generally. It should identify the target as primitivist Russellian monism and then state Pautz’s complete dilemma.

### The combination problem is still overgeneralised

The October revision correctly narrowed the initial combination claim. But later sections continue to treat solving the combination problem as removing “the consideration favouring substance dualism” and say substance dualism avoids it “entirely.” [concepts/substance-property-dualism](/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)

Three qualifications are required:

1. The combination problem directly affects constitutive panpsychist and some Russellian views, not every property dualism.
2. Chalmers-style macro-level psychophysical laws and emergent property dualism need not begin with micro-subjects.
3. A primitive unified substance avoids constitutive subject-combination by stipulation, but inherits questions about subject emergence, individuation, neural distribution, pairing and fission.

“Sidesteps by positing primitive subject unity” would be more accurate than “avoids entirely.”

### The arguments against materialism are presented as conclusions

Mary, zombies and the intrinsic character of qualia are said to “all point beyond physics.” Given the Map’s declared tenet this is understandable as site perspective, but in a comparative concept article it needs attribution:

> Dualists take these arguments to show that physical facts do not exhaust the facts.

Each has live physicalist replies: ability and acquaintance responses to Mary, phenomenal-concept strategies, challenges to the conceivability-to-possibility inference, and illusionist or representational accounts of qualitative character. The current wording converts the Map’s commitment into a supposedly neutral result.

## 5. Is the illusionist challenge stated at full strength?

No.

### What the article gets right

It correctly identifies the basic challenge: illusionists deny phenomenal properties in the traditional intrinsic sense while seeking a physical explanation of why subjects confidently judge and report that they possess them. It also correctly identifies the meta-problem as the task of explaining our judgments that consciousness presents a distinctive explanatory problem. [concepts/substance-property-dualism](/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)

### The regress response begs the central question

The article replies that an introspective seeming must itself be phenomenal and therefore starts a regress. Frankish’s position is precisely that “seeming” here means a nonphenomenal self-representation: a system represents itself as having a greenish phenomenal state without possessing such a state. He also explicitly invokes an account on which treating modes of presentation as phenomenal does not create an infinite regress. [Achraf Kassioui+1](https://www.achrafkassioui.com/library/Keith%20Frankish%20-%20Illusionism%20as%20a%20Theory%20of%20Consciousness.pdf)

The linked Map article now concedes this point in unusually clear terms: the bare regress assumes what the illusionist denies and “proves nothing”; the substantive issue is whether a sufficiently detailed meta-representational bridge can explain the apparent qualitative and unified character of consciousness. [concepts/illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/)

Thus the main article and its linked explainer currently give opposite assessments of the same objection.

### The strongest positive illusionist account is missing

A full-strength presentation would include:

- quasi-phenomenal properties: real physical properties that cause or constitute our dispositions to judge that phenomenal qualities exist;
- the distortion thesis: introspective mechanisms systematically mischaracterise internal states;
- functional seeming: discrimination, confidence, report, memory, attention and behavioural control without intrinsic phenomenal properties;
- subtle illusionism, on which introspection caricatures something real rather than inventing an entirely empty referent;
- specific self-model and attention-schema proposals.

The current literature no longer consists merely of the generic claim that “the brain creates an illusion.” Graziano distinguishes several illusionist models, and Gorbachev and Frankish classify multiple forms of introspective distortion. [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11521794/)

### The visual-perception analogy does little work

Saying that fallible visual perception does not prove external objects unreal is true, but it does not meet the illusionist’s argument. Illusionists do not infer nonexistence from fallibility alone; they propose that the brain’s introspective model represents neural states as possessing properties they lack. The relevant question is whether this positive model can explain the reports and apparent qualities, not whether all imperfect perception has an unreal object.

### Witness consciousness is not presently an independent defeater

The article treats reports of contentless or witness consciousness as evidence that consciousness persists after representation has been eliminated. That inference is too quick.

A report after meditation still involves memory, metacognition, attention and retrospective conceptualisation. An illusionist may accept altered self-modelling or unusually sparse representational content without accepting intrinsic, representation-free awareness. The linked illusionism article more accurately characterises contemplative reports as first-person pressure rather than independent evidence. [concepts/substance-property-dualism](/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)

The article would need:

- a defensible operational account of “contentless”;
- controls for memory and report reconstruction;
- engagement with minimalist self-model interpretations;
- an argument from phenomenological report to ontology.

### What a balanced conclusion should say

The full-strength verdict is not that illusionism has explained consciousness, but that the article has not yet shown that it cannot. The best dualist objections concern the apparent self-evidence of experience, the absence of an independent vantage point from which introspective “distortion” can be established, and whether functional accounts explain only reports rather than what reports appear to concern. Those are stronger than the bare regress and are already developed in the linked article.

## 6. Is the decoherence objection stated at full strength?

Only partly.

### The numerical presentation is broadly accurate

Tegmark’s paper reports decoherence times around $10^{-13}$–$10^{-20}$ seconds compared with neural dynamical times around $10^{-3}$–$10^{-1}$ seconds. Hagan and colleagues obtained $10^{-5}$–$10^{-4}$ seconds after revising assumptions in a specific Orch OR tubulin model. [DOI+1](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194)

The article is also now properly cautious about magnetoreception. Kobylkov and colleagues inferred approximately 2–10 μs from behavioural radiofrequency results, while Luo and colleagues provide computational evidence about protein and solvent stabilisation of candidate cryptochrome radical pairs—not a measurement of neural coherence. [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6936046/)

### Hagan is treated too generically

Hagan’s calculation is not evidence that arbitrary neural quantum states can survive for $10^{-5}$–$10^{-4}$ seconds. It concerns a particular model, particular degrees of freedom and contested parameter choices. The article should not present it simply as a general revision of “the” neural decoherence time.

It also omits McKemmish and colleagues’ direct biological critique. They argue that tubulin lacks essential properties required by the original Orch OR proposal and that known physical paradigms do not make microtubule quantum computation biologically feasible. That paper is not the last word, but it is too directly relevant to omit from a purportedly balanced mitigation section. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.80.021912)

### “Faster microtubule dynamics may relax the requirement” is unsupported

No reference is attached to this sentence, and it is too indeterminate to evaluate. Which dynamics, which coherent degree of freedom, what operation time, and what neural amplification pathway are intended? A shorter required operation time helps only after those quantities have been specified.

### The post-decoherence move is the central scientific weakness

The article says decoherence does not determine a single measured outcome and that consciousness could therefore bias outcomes after decoherence. The first clause states a genuine measurement-theory issue. The second does not follow.

Decoherence can suppress locally accessible interference whether or not one endorses an interpretation in which it supplies literal collapse. If the proposed neural operation requires coherent interference, the loss of coherence is damaging independently of the measurement problem.

If consciousness acts only after decoherence, the article has moved to a different proposal. It must then specify:

1. What counts as the relevant measurement event.
2. Which basis consciousness selects in.
3. How a mental state maps to an outcome probability.
4. Whether the Born rule is preserved exactly, conditionally or violated.
5. How causal efficacy is possible if the unconditional statistics remain unchanged.
6. How signalling and conservation constraints are satisfied.
7. What observation would differ from standard stochastic quantum mechanics.

An unresolved interpretation problem is not positive evidence for a consciousness-sensitive selection law. At present, “could bias” is a tenet-compatible modal speculation, not a mitigating scientific result.

### The objection is more demanding than “warm and wet”

A full-strength feasibility test should require the Map to identify:

- the physical degrees of freedom carrying the alternatives;
- how the state is prepared;
- coherence or distinguishability times under physiological conditions;
- the timescale of the proposed conscious intervention;
- amplification from the microscopic event to synaptic or spiking consequences;
- robustness against thermal and biochemical noise;
- the psychophysical dynamics;
- a discriminating experimental prediction.

Until those are supplied, cryptochrome establishes only the limited proposition that specialised protein architecture can sustain biologically useful spin chemistry for microseconds. The article now acknowledges that limitation, but still uses it rhetorically as though it materially reduces the burden on an unspecified neural interface. [concepts/substance-property-dualism](/concepts/substance-property-dualism/)

## 7. Does “the Map accepts either view” cohere with the rest of the site?

### At tenet level: yes

The Dualism tenet requires irreducibility. A physical organism with irreducible phenomenal properties and a distinct nonphysical subject can both satisfy that requirement. The binding problem, knowledge argument, explanatory gap and many of the voids do not require a separate mental substance. The canonical *Where the Substance Commitment Enters* article states this clearly. [concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

Minimal quantum interaction and bidirectional interaction are also, in principle, neutral between the two ontologies. An irreducible property or a distinct subject could be assigned a psychophysical causal role—provided a coherent law is supplied.

### At full architecture level: no

The canonical scoping article says Posit One supplies a numerically determinate, diachronically persisting subject. The agency cluster uses that subject as the bearer and exerciser of causal powers; the indexical anti-Everett argument uses it as the subject whose branch identity is a further fact. The page explicitly says that the “operational framework runs substance-leaning while the tenet stays neutral.” [concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

That is more than a preference. It is a conditional dependency:

- Rejecting a persisting subject leaves Tenet 1 intact.
- It requires revision or abandonment of the current agent-causal argument.
- It also removes the present haecceitistic form of the indexical objection to many-worlds.

The main article now says those dependencies are “load-bearing,” but still describes the choice globally as merely preferential. Those formulations should be reconciled.

### A persisting agent does not establish nonphysical substance dualism

The canonical article correctly notes that physical-animal and organism accounts can supply a persisting agent. Property dualism can likewise be combined with a physical substance bearer possessing irreducible phenomenal properties. [concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

The agency argument therefore has at least two steps:

1. Agent causation needs a persisting bearer of powers.
2. That bearer must be nonphysical.

The first does not entail the second. The second must come from an independent irreducibility argument plus an account of why irreducible phenomenal properties cannot belong to a physical organism. The comparison article currently slides too quickly from persisting subject to substance-dualist leaning.

### The anti-Everett “advantage” is also overstated

Substance dualism does not by itself deliver haecceitistic branch identity; haecceity is an additional commitment. Conversely, a property dualist could treat a physical subject as haecceitistically individuated. The canonical page candidly says the indexical argument declares its primitive-subject presupposition rather than deriving it. [concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

A mental substance therefore provides a natural home for the Map’s identity posit, but does not independently explain or vindicate it. The article should call this **fit with Posit One**, not an unqualified metaphysical advantage.

A precise replacement for “the Map accepts either view” would be:

> The Dualism tenet and irreducibility arguments accept either ontology. The Map’s current agency and indexical-identity modules additionally assume a persisting, primitively individuated subject through Posit One; property-dualist versions of the Map must either supply such a bearer or revise those modules.

## 10. Unsupported claims, missed counterarguments and stale coverage

The reference list is not uniformly stale. Robinson and Weir 2025 and Luo et al. 2025 are current, while Descartes, Whitehead, Tegmark, Lowe and Zimmerman remain historically or dialectically central. The problem is selective staleness where the article purports to characterise an active debate.

The illusionism discussion stops bibliographically at Frankish 2016 and Chalmers 2018 even though the linked article cites substantial 2022–2025 work. The substance-dualism discussion omits Mackie’s direct response to Zimmerman. The decoherence discussion foregrounds Hagan 2002 without the highly relevant McKemmish et al. 2009 challenge, and the fresh cryptochrome source belongs to a biologically specialised spin-chemistry domain rather than the proposed neural mechanism. [PubMed Central (PMC)+3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11521794/)

The main unsupported or under-supported claims are:

- Property dualism’s neural dependence fits the evidence better than contemporary substance dualism.
- Quantum indeterminacy provides an unoccupied causal opening.
- Faster microtubule dynamics relax the coherence requirement.
- Consciousness could bias outcomes after decoherence.
- Witness consciousness removes representational content rather than altering it.
- Contemplative consensus would provide substantial evidence for or against substancehood.
- Zimmerman establishes pressure toward panpsychism or epiphenomenalism.
- Bailey, Rasmussen and Van Horn invoke haecceities.
- A primitive mental substance explains unity rather than relocating it.
- Haecceity explains branch identity rather than stipulating a further identity fact.

Several important counterarguments are absent or underweighted:

- **Causal exclusion for interactionist properties:** rejecting closure is only the start; the article must say which physical transitions are not physically sufficient and how mental properties alter them.
- **Physical-substance agent causation:** an organism or animal may be the persisting agent while bearing irreducible phenomenal properties.
- **Subject-generation and fission:** emergent substance dualism must say when one subject appears and what happens in split, duplicated or gradually replaced systems.
- **Primitive-unity cost:** treating subject unity as basic should be counted as a theoretical posit, just as brute psychophysical or grounding laws are counted against property dualism.
- **The direct reply to Zimmerman:** property dualists need not concede that bearer vagueness makes their view less plausible than emergent substance dualism.
- **Framework-neutral illusionism:** the illusionist can accept every report, confidence judgment and behavioural effect while denying the dualist’s ontological interpretation.
- **Born-rule and no-signalling constraints:** any efficacious quantum selection model owes more than the observation that decoherence is not a complete interpretation of measurement.
- **Process and neutral alternatives:** Whitehead and Russellian monism demonstrate that the substance/property binary is not exhaustive, but they may also fall outside the Map’s stated dualism rather than count as alternative ways of satisfying it.

The article’s “What Would Challenge This View?” section also needs stronger discriminators. Solving the panpsychist combination problem would affect only certain property-dualist options; contemplative agreement would remain interpretation-laden; and an apparent conscious state without an expected neural correlate would not by itself distinguish a nonphysical substance from an irreducible property or an incomplete neural measurement. More direct challenges would concern subject individuation, fission, causal intervention, interface localisation and whether a persisting subject adds predictive or explanatory power.
