## Executive Summary

Five pages written between 10-01 and 10-09, by different forks for different queue tasks, reach the same verdict in the grammar `episodic-memory` adopted on 08-04. A first-person state can **present** a fact without **certifying** it. Felt ownership presents a memory as one's own (quasi-memory L102). Felt pastness presents an episode as caused by one's past (episodic-memory L100: "Immediacy is not authentication"). A sincere report of seeing presents a visual seeming (Anton L111). Felt initiation presents a thought as self-generated (thought-insertion L108). A report of absence presents absence (metacognitive-access L130, Rule 2). Each page grades the Map's subject or the Dualism tenet "compatible, no support". Each declines a tempting upgrade by name. The one first-person judgement the Map lets come near certification, that experience is occurring at all, is fenced exactly: Anton L101 shows that self-stultification protects only the global-negative cell. It is among the best-calibrated clusters the corpus has produced on first-person evidence.

What is missing is the rule's name. No page states it once, so a future article can repeat the error without contradicting any single sentence. The `evidential-status-discipline` page has the obvious slot (priority item 3). Three smaller gains are ready:

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Anton engages Chalmers on his own ground and fairly. L82 quotes his concession ("in cases of blindness denial, subjects believe that they are having visual experiences when they likely have none") and his restriction to systems "whose cognitive mechanisms are unimpaired". L84 then tests whether the restriction is principled: it holds only if impairment has markers independent of the error. The answer is "mostly it can", with the two pressure cases named. Chalmers would recognise his position and see its cost priced honestly. The page also keeps the hard problem where it belongs (L117): what stays unexplained "is the hard problem and nothing special to this syndrome".

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The wing barely touches physics, and says so (IEM L108 "No bearing"; Anton L123). Its one quantum-adjacent move is a restriction, and Stapp would respect it. Quasi-memory L116 grants that the personal-identity page's replica reply ("*this* consciousness" making "*these* quantum selections") individuates a subject "only if one persisting selector made the selections, and a later selector's memory of them cannot show that one did". The selection story is not allowed to do identity work it cannot do.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Anton's L44 formula is the strongest pro-first-person sentence in the wing because it gives something up: authority attaches "to *seemings*, not to *reports*". The seeming keeps its authority. Only the report loses it. The global/modal × negative/positive table at L96–99 is a genuinely new instrument, and the corpus has no other typology of failed self-report. IEM L62 adds the phenomenologist's distinction that matters most here: "Ownership and self-identification are separate questions, and IEM answers only the second."
