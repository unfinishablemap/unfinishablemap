## Lens 1 — Did the recalibration overshoot? (primary)

**Verdict: mostly correctly pitched, with one genuine overshoot, now fixed.**

### CRITICAL — the Gandhara concession was over-generous on two counts (fixed)

The recalibration's load-bearing historical sentence read:

> Indo-Greek exchange through Gandhara created possible influence channels between Western and Indian thought, **and the contact period overlaps the centuries in which both traditions did their formative work on the self.**

Both halves of the bolded claim fail against the record:

1. **Chronology — the channel post-dates the Western sources the article actually cites.** Alexander reached Bactria and Gandhara in 331–325 BCE. Plato died c. 348 BCE; the self-moving-soul argument (*Phaedrus*, *Phaedo*) — which this article names as the Western tradition's opening move on mental causation — is entirely pre-contact. Aristotle died 322 BCE. So "the contact period overlaps the centuries in which **both** traditions did their formative work" is false for the Western side as the article uses it. The channel overlaps the later *Indian* systematisation (the Nyāya Sūtras are dated between the 6th c. BCE and the 2nd c. CE), not the Greek material the article relies on.
2. **Scholarly default — the documented traffic is artistic, not doctrinal.** The mainstream position on Greco-Buddhism is that Hellenistic influence on Gandharan *art* is clear while a noticeable Greek influence on Gandharan Buddhist *doctrine* is not assumed. The article's "nothing establishes that the channel carried the specific doctrines at issue" is true but reads as a bare absence of evidence, when the stronger and more accurate statement is that doctrinal transmission is not the scholarly default.

Together these made the concession larger than the history warrants — the classic over-concession shape, which runs against the Map and so does not self-correct.

**Resolution**: the Indian/Western bullet was rewritten to date the channel (Alexander in the 320s BCE, after Plato's death), to separate the art channel from the doctrine channel, and to locate where the channel *does* bite (the later Indian systematisation). **The near-independence verdict itself is unchanged** — this corrects the *reason* offered for the verdict, not the verdict, so it preserves fidelity to [P-D3](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/) rather than reversing toward the retired flat-independence reading. A duplicated statement of the verdict ("strong enough to count against… not strong enough…") was dropped from the bullet, since the lead already carries it verbatim; this offsets most of the added length.

Wiredu's actual claim is narrower — that the Akan scheme does not draw the material/immaterial contrast, not that Akan thought is materialist. Summary tightened to "whether it even draws the material/immaterial contrast a dualist reading needs."
