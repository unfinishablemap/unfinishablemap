- **Hu et al. 2023** (*Nat Neurosci* 26, 751–764) — **claim-defect ×3, all CORRECTED.**
  1. **WRONG AGENT LIST.** The article named the tested agents as *"propofol,
     isoflurane, sevoflurane, and ketamine"* at three loci (L53, L75, ref-1
     annotation). **Sevoflurane was never tested.** The agents are **propofol,
     pentobarbital, ketamine, and isoflurane** (methods: "Pro 120, Pen 50, Ket
     250, Iso 2%"). Corrected at all three loci, and the pharmacology triad
     rewritten so the GABA-A / volatile / NMDA partition matches the real agent
     set (propofol + pentobarbital = GABA-A at distinct binding sites).
  2. **MISATTRIBUTED SOURCE PHRASE.** The article asserted *"The authors describe
     this explicitly as an active reboot."* **They do not.** The abstract's own
     words are "once assumed to be a passive process, is now considered as an
     active and controllable process" and "This pathway to recovery is an active
     process and occurs independent of anesthetic choice." **"Reboot" appears
     nowhere in the paper.** The "reboot" vocabulary traces to the press-release
     framing of a *different* paper the article also cites — Mashour et al. 2021
     eLife, publicised as *"Escape from Oblivion: How the Brain Reboots After
     Deep Anesthesia."* Replaced with the authors' two verified verbatim
     characterisations plus an explicit statement that *active reboot* is the
     Map's name for the mechanism class, not their coinage.
     **This claim was ratified as "web-verified TRUE" by the 07-14 review** —
     see Systemic Note below.
  3. **SPECIES ELIDED.** The mouse study was presented as an unqualified fact
     about "emergence from general anaesthesia" (lead L39, §Worked Exhibit L75).
     "In mice" added at both anchors.
- **Mashour 2024** (*Neuron* 112(10), 1553–1567) — **claim-defect, CORRECTED.**
  The verified verbatim asymmetry statement is *"the neurobiology of exiting the
  anesthetized state is not a simple mirror image of entering the anesthetized
  state."* Correct and now quoted. But the article's gloss — *"induction rapid
  and stereotyped, emergence protracted and agent-specific"* — is **not his
  characterisation**. Full-text search of PMC11098701: **"stereotyped" NOT
  PRESENT, "stereotypic" NOT PRESENT, "protracted" NOT PRESENT, "agent-specific"
  NOT PRESENT, "drug-specific" NOT PRESENT, "anesthetic-specific" NOT PRESENT.**

  hysteresis — the concentration required to *become* unconscious exceeds that

## Systemic Note

The 07-14 review "web-verified TRUE" a claim that is false, and recorded it as a
stability note instructing future passes not to touch it. The failure mode is
quote-aggregator-ratification-corrupts-verbatim with a twist: the "active
reboot"/"reboots" vocabulary *does* exist in the anaesthesia-emergence press
corpus, attached to a **different paper this same article cites**. A search that
confirms the vocabulary exists in the neighbourhood, without confirming it exists
in *this* paper, produces a false ratification — and a stability note then
hardens it against correction. Two operational lessons: (1) verify a claimed
source phrase against the source's own text, never against the topic's press
coverage; (2) a stability note is not evidence, and a later pass finding
publisher-level contradiction should void it explicitly rather than defer.

More generally: this pass confirms **empirical-claim fidelity is orthogonal to
citation metadata**. Every cite here was metadata-perfect after 07-14. Four
critical defects survived that pass because they live in the *paraphrase*, not
the bibliographic tuple.
