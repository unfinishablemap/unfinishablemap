## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
INH L60–L62 is the passage Chalmers would most want to have written about his own view. The page's gloss on Saad, "Selection sorts physical types under a law; it does not write the law", explains why naturalistic dualism needs a fixed law and an evolving receiver. The page then accepts the cost instead of hiding it: psychophysical harmony on the inbound side is booked as an unpaid debt (L126). CEB L73 reads the zombie argument as a "reductio against the view that biological function exhausts fitness", which puts the hard problem where it belongs, inside evolutionary explanation.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
INH L115 gives Process 1 its fairest hearing on the Map. Question-selection is the one route on which "each measurement keeps its Born statistics while situation-level frequencies shift, and evolution can see the difference". The page also records honestly that adopting it would be a material change, "a decision for the Map's editors, not a repair". MET L94 ties the onset of a phenomenal transition to the point where "biological architecture first supported that interface".

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
MET L74 is clear-sighted about what a behavioural marker is: UAL is "a proxy read from the outside", and "Neither addresses why there is something it is like to be the organism that learns or selects." SC L79 carries the Nagelian point down to the floor. Grant the bacterium minimal experience, and the behaviour "would look exactly as it does". Neither page makes the outside view stand in for the inside one.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
CEB L121 states the process, panpsychist and Russellian reframings at full strength: the question becomes "how did experiential integration increase?", "a scalar question that evolution handles well". MET L84 keeps the panpsychist "always, in degree" as a live alternative on which there was no transition to detect. Neither page lets this praise do evidential work. **Process-compatibility here is praised as a tenet-coherent option, not as an engine of tier-upgrade.**

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
CEB L85–L87 argues that if consciousness directs behaviour, "genuine purpose re-enters the biological world" as local directedness, which divides biology into teleonomic and actually directed systems. INH's selectability constraint is the hard test this view needs. Agency that leaves every fitness statistic unchanged is invisible to evolution. Kane would welcome a page that names that cost instead of assuming the evolutionary support for agency is free.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)
SC's tractability thesis (L65–L69) is the wing's mysterian centre: "does this cell choose?" may be "a question the world declines to answer". SC holds it as a wager at moderate credence ([P-CS5](/positions/consciousness-scope/#p-cs5)), which is the right register. NLA L81 says the same thing at the next rung: "The lower bound of the interface is not settled here. What is settled is that behavioural markers alone will never settle it."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)
This persona's verdict matters most here, because two pages sit on minimal-organism boundary cases.
- **Tenet-as-evidence-upgrade, praise-worthily declined, twice.**
  - NLA L77: "A sponge experience that made no difference to any physical outcome would add no non-physical footprint at all, and minimality would have nothing to weigh against it." - **Registered calibration.** SC records the parsimony default as "undefeated but not positively established" ([P-CS5](/positions/consciousness-scope/#p-cs5), moderate credence). That is the tier discipline the five-tier scale asks for, applied to the prokaryotic floor.

- **One place the discipline has not arrived**: CEB L101 says consciousness "is genuinely adaptive" if it is efficacious, and L143 says "evolution can select for it". ## Source-fidelity check {#fidelity}

**Attributions the driver named: VERIFIED.** MET credits the major-transitions framework to Maynard Smith & Szathmáry (1995). The deep review of 08-07 checked the *Nature* table item by item, and I found nothing to reopen. MET L68–L70 and SC L57 attribute UAL to Ginsburg and Jablonka, which is correct. **MET L70, the UAL dating. CONTRADICTED (compression); the taxa and the driver claim survive.**
- **What the page says**: UAL "dates the transition to around the Cambrian explosion, roughly 540 million years ago, which they argue consciousness helped drive."
- **What the source says** (Birch, Ginsburg & Jablonka 2020, Europe PMC raw JATS, each string count 1 unless noted):
  - "Unless UAL is much more widespread in the animal kingdom than suggested above, it hints at three separate origin events for conscious experience: one in the vertebrate lineage, one in the arthropods, and one in the coleoid cephalopod molluscs."
  - "If UAL is present in all or most extant vertebrates, this points to an origin event in the vertebrate case no later than the origin of the vertebrates, in the Cambrian period." The dating is explicitly "no later than", and it is for the vertebrate event only.
  - The driver claim: "associative learning, both limited and unlimited did not just originate in the Cambrian explosion, but was one of the major driving forces behind that explosion", described as "logically independent of the core components of the UAL framework". The authors add: "we think the assumption that associative learning drove the Cambrian explosion is not itself experimentally testable."
  - Survives: "most vertebrates, some cephalopod molluscs … and some arthropods (including honey bees and fruit flies)" (count 1, matching the 08-07 fix), and UAL as "only a positive marker" (count 1, the SC L57 quote).
- **The defect**: three origin events, the vertebrate one dated "no later than", become one transition at one date. The driver claim's subject, associative learning both limited and unlimited, becomes "consciousness". The authors' own untestability caveat is dropped. The 08-07 ledger certified "consciousness helped drive" as faithful because it checked the verb and not the subject (`citation-ledger-ratifies-the-reading-not-just-the-metadata`). It never checked the dating sentence.
- **Why it matters to the Map**: MET's thesis speaks of "a phenomenal transition" and "that onset" in the singular. On the source's own reading there would be three, and that bears on what any eligibility condition can require (item 2(d)). The three-origins claim has **zero** live hits and zero research hits (`three separate origin|independent origins? of consciousness|origin events`).
