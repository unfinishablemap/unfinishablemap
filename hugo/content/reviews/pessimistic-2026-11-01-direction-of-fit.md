## Executive Summary

The article's exposition is now accurate. Its argument is not yet consistent with that exposition. The Searle section correctly separates *direction of fit* (satisfaction conditions: which side is at fault in a mismatch) from *direction of causation*. The Map-voice sections then argue as if world-to-mind fit were itself causal efficacy:

- "They 'aim' at nothing because they do nothing" (L73).
- "World-to-mind direction of fit is, at its core, a description of consciousness acting on the world" (L107).
- Two label swaps at L77 and L107.

On that basis L33 claims direction of fit "provides evidence for" Bidirectional Interaction. Two tenet checks have already flagged that sentence (09-17 #6; 10-21 Family 1), and it was never tasked. The body says neural error-correction "is not obviously captured" (L65), but Relation to Site Perspective concludes "Neural states do not bear 'should' relations to the world; intentional states do" (L109). ## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The Map-voice argument treats world-to-mind fit as causal efficacy, which the article's own Searle section separates (Task A, P2)
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/direction-of-fit.md`
- **Location**: L33, L73, L77, L107 (two sentences), L119 (Further Reading gloss)
- **Problem**: L51 defines world-to-mind states as those "satisfied when the world comes to match their content". L53 pairs fit with "a second notion that runs the opposite way", direction of causation. On that definition an inert desire for rain is still satisfied if it rains. So L73's "They 'aim' at nothing because they do nothing" does not follow. Applying the article's own definition first, this is a gap in the inference, not a contradiction manufactured from a definition. The argument the article *can* make is narrower and stronger, and it uses the article's own L53. Searle builds causation into an *intention's* satisfaction conditions ("satisfied only if it causes the very movement it represents"), so an epiphenomenal intention would never be satisfied. Desires carry no such implication. Three more loci repeat the conflation:
  - L107: "World-to-mind direction of fit is, at its core, a description of consciousness acting on the world".
  - L107: "Direction of fit makes the bidirectional picture concrete: perception flows world-to-mind…". This gives causation labels under a fit heading. Perception has *mind-to-world fit*, so a reader of L49 takes the sentence as a label swap.
  - L77: "world-to-mind causal chains". By L53's terminology, causation from belief to body runs mind-to-world.

  On top of this, L33 says direction of fit "provides evidence for" Tenet 3. A physicalist who takes intentions to be efficacious brain states (Searle, by the article's own L85) accepts every premise. The argument presses epiphenomenalism. It does not favour interactionism. [P-MC4](/positions/arguments-for-mental-causation/#p-mc4) already scopes the creation loop that cites this page as "an argument against epiphenomenalism rather than a fourth route to irreducibility". Tenet check 09-17 #6 asked for "the L85 concession should be echoed at L33". Tenet check 10-21 #12 listed L33 under "fits the tenet without evidencing it". Neither was minted. `grep -F direction-of-fit` in todo.md finds only two notes that mention the page in passing (L549, agentic-social; L793, positions) and no task that names it.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Rebuild L73 on causal self-reference. Rescope L33 to "bears on" with the physicalist parity stated. Fix the three labels. Bring the L119 placebo gloss ("Empirical evidence that belief content drives…") into line with the body's settled tier. This does not weaken the placebo calibration. It stops the navigation label from outrunning it.

### Issue 2: L109 converts the body's epistemic claim into a metaphysical one and begs the question (Task B, P2)
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L109 "The normative dimension of direction of fit resists physical reduction. Neural states do not bear \"should\" relations to the world; intentional states do." ### Issue 3: L83's "metaphorical" clause is the live source of a sibling clause cut on 2026-10-12 (Task C, P3)
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L81 "they exercise world-to-mind fit" and "This is precisely what substances exercising causal powers looks like from the intentional perspective." L83 "Event-causal theories struggle here." / "the \"direction\" of fit becomes metaphorical" / "Agent causation preserves the literal directedness".
- **Problem**: There are four problems:
  - L83 equates event-causal theory with physicalism ("If desires are just neural events"). An event-causal account can take the causing events to be mental, as the Map's interactionism could.
  - It is a non sequitur. The normative relation (which side is at fault in a mismatch) is fixed by the intention's content, whatever the causal relatum is. Agent causation adds a bearer of causal power, not the "should".
  - L81's "exercise world-to-mind fit" is a category slip, because fit is a property of the state. Its "precisely" claims the scene for agent causation when an event-causal description fits it equally well.
  - `concepts/agent-causation` L71 carried the same claim ("a literal directedness event-causal accounts render metaphorical"). Commit `bb3d8edc54` (2026-10-12) cut it as unargued boundary-substitution, after the ChatGPT and Gemini outer reviews of 2026-10-12 objected that "a physically implemented goal representation can literally guide prediction, error correction, attention, and motor control". The agent-causation sibling now reads "an intention genuinely aims to make reality conform to its content" (`grep -c -F` = 1). The source paragraph here is untouched. This is the fix-by-file-leaves-siblings-live pattern.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Recast L81/L83 so that direction of fit frames the agent/event choice without deciding it. Keep the Map's distinctive step (the normativity argument) as the Map's reading.

### Issue 4: The biological-objection reply answers a strawman; teleosemantics is a theory of what content is (Task D, P3)
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L89 "Some argue direction of fit reduces to biological function." and "evolutionary function may explain *why* these structures exist without explaining *what they are*"
- **Problem**: The objection is unattributed ("Some argue") and is stated as an adaptive-history story. The actual position is teleosemantics, and it is constitutive. SEP "Teleological Theories of Mental Content" (verbatim): "According to teleological theories, the contents of mental representations depend, at least in part, on functions, such as the functions of the systems that use or produce them". It frames the task as the "content-determination question" ("in virtue of what do representational states have the content they have"). Millikan's account covers directive as well as descriptive representations, and "'pushmi-pullyu representations', i.e. of representations that are at once descriptive and directive" (SEP §3.2, verbatim). So "why, not what" misses. SEP supplies an in-framework reply the article does not use (verbatim): "Teleological theories share the strategy of explaining the norms pertaining to mental content as deriving from so-called functional 'norms', which are regarded as descriptive rather than prescriptive." The unmet step is whether a descriptive norm is the "should" at issue. That is an unsupported-move identification, which is better than the current reply.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Name teleosemantics and Millikan (1995). Concede that it is constitutive. Relocate the reply to the descriptive-versus-prescriptive step. Millikan (1995) was checked at Crossref: *Philosophical Perspectives* 9, first page 185, DOI 10.2307/2214217. The end page 200 comes from two secondary listings and should be confirmed at install.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The parity objection to the normativity argument
- **Current content says**: Physical description does not entail that a state ought to match reality (L65, L109), so normativity may be grounded in phenomenal character (L67).
- **A critic would argue**: Phenomenal description does not entail an "ought" either. A felt wrongness is fallible, and it is a descriptive fact about experience. The gap argument removes the physicalist's ground without supplying the dualist's.
- **Suggested response**: Concede that the parallel *locates* the gap without closing it (the optional clause in Task B), and leave PIT's conditional at L67 as the Map's bet. A full answer, for example that phenomenal intentionality gives content and the norm of content in one stroke, belongs in [intentionality](/concepts/intentionality/) or [cognitive-phenomenology](/concepts/cognitive-phenomenology/), not here.

### Direction of fit does not discriminate interactionism from physicalism
- **Current content says**: World-to-mind states "provide evidence for" Bidirectional Interaction (L33, L107).
- **A critic would argue**: Every physicalist who rejects epiphenomenalism holds that intentions cause bodily movement. Searle, whom L85 cites, is one.
- **Suggested response**: Task A. The argument's real target is epiphenomenalism, as [P-MC4](/positions/arguments-for-mental-causation/#p-mc4) already says.
