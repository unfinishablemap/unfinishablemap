- Schaffer 2010 (Monism: The Priority of the Whole) — **real-correct**. Quote
  verified against the author's own PDF (jonathanschaffer.org/monism.pdf, p. 31)
  *and* the publisher-deposited abstract via Crossref/OpenAlex. Source sentence:
  "The monist holds that the whole is prior to its parts, and thus views the
  cosmos as fundamental, with metaphysical explanation dangling downward from the
  One." Ellipsis elides ", and thus views the cosmos as fundamental," — a
  single-sentence, near-contiguous, non-distorting elision. Framing corrected
  (below).
- Miller 2018 (Can Subjects Be Proper Parts of Subjects?) — **real-correct**.
  "is equivalent to the combination problem" is **verbatim** in the abstract:
  "…there is a 'de-combination problem' facing the cosmopsychist, which is
  equivalent to the combination problem as they are both concerned with subjects
  being proper parts of other subjects." The article's gloss ("both concern
  whether subjects can stand in proper-parthood relations") and its inference
  ("whatever escape route helps one camp helps the other equally") both track
  Miller's own conclusion, which proposes two escape routes "for both theorists".
  *Equivalent* is Miller's word, not the article's amplification. Crossref
  confirms Ratio 31(2):137–154, published-print 2018-06.
- Miller 2021 (The Decombination Problem … is not the Heterogeneity Problem …) —
  **real-correct**. Abstract: Nagasawa and Wager "suggest that the de-combination
  problem can be solved in the same way that the problem of heterogeneity for
  Schaffer's priority monism can be solved. I suggest that this is not the case.
  They are not the same problem and the solutions to the heterogeneity problem do
  not work for the de-combination problem." The article's gloss is accurate,
  including its correct identification of the *target* (the priority reply).
  Citation confirmed independently in the SEP bibliography: JCS 28(3–4): 112–115.
- Shani 2022 (Cosmopsychism, Coherence, and World-Affirming Monism) —
  **real-correct**. Abstract: "cosmopsychism suffers from no insurmountable
  individuation problem (IND)… the widespread tendency to view IND as a
  mirror-image of micropsychism's combination problem (CP) is mistaken. In
  particular, what renders CP insolvable, namely, the commitment to the coupling
  of phenomenal constitution with phenomenal inclusion, is, from the standpoint
  of cosmopsychism, an entirely nonmandatory assumption." Both the term
  (*individuation problem*) and the position (contesting the mirror) are Shani's
  own, not the Map's inference. Crossref: Monist 105(1), 6–24.
- Shani & Keppler 2018 (Beyond Combination) — **real-correct**; body claim

- Albahari 2019 (Perennial Idealism) — **real-correct**. Abstract confirms both

### Medium Issues Found

- **Inline-without-reference orphan (§2.4 cross-reference rule).** The Stanford
  Encyclopedia was cited in three separate places — for the constitutive
  cosmopsychism definition, for the no-single-originator hedge, and for the
  conditional framing of the conceivability point — with no References entry.
  Two of those are near-verbatim paraphrase of SEP wording, so the omission was
  load-bearing rather than cosmetic. **Resolved**: References entry added, naming
  the supplement as well as the main entry, since one of the three claims lives
  only in the supplement.
- **Under-registered opposing claim (Shani & Keppler).** The article's objection
  to filtering ("Localisation explains why one modulation is dynamically distinct
  from another, not why a distinct modulation is a subject") was stated as though
  Shani and Keppler had left subject-generation untouched. Their abstract
  explicitly claims to address "the generation of subjects", and the SEP
  supplement records Shani sketching "an intelligible explanation of each organic
  subject's capacity for experience, and its having of a perspective". The
  objection may still be right, but it was dismissing a claim the authors make
  without acknowledging that they make it — an under-argued dismissal of the kind
  the Map's own discipline warns against. **Resolved**: their claim is now stated
  before the Map's objection, and the objection is phrased as a shortfall against
  what they attempt rather than as a gap they never noticed.

### Low Issues Found

- **Quote framing, Schaffer (L29).** The quote was introduced as "Schaffer writes
  that 'the whole is prior to its parts…'", which silently replaced the source
  sentence's grammatical subject ("The monist holds that…") with Schaffer
  himself. No misrepresentation resulted — Schaffer defends monism in that very
  paper — but the quotation now begins where the source sentence begins.
  **Resolved.**
- The article's paraphrase of the SEP conditional had drifted: it gave the
  antecedent as "*if* the conceivable separation licenses the modal inference",
  whereas SEP's antecedent is a construal of the subject-summing problem.
  **Resolved**: the antecedent now matches the source, and names the supplement
  so a future verifier looks in the right document.

### Counterarguments Considered

- *The Quantum Skeptic on Shani & Keppler.* SED is a classical stochastic theory
  with a zero-point field, and identifying cosmic consciousness with "the vacuum
  state of quantum field theory" mixes registers. The article does not endorse
  the identification, and Shani and Keppler's own abstract pairs SED with the QFT
  vacuum in exactly the way the article reports, so the article is reporting the
  proposal faithfully and the strain belongs to the proposal.
- *The Empiricist on the Map's Tenet 5 argument.* The Tenet 5 reason leans on
  Miller's mirror thesis, which Shani 2022 contests — but the article states it
  conditionally ("**If** de-combination is the combination problem
  mirror-imaged…") and flags the contest explicitly one section earlier. The
  calibration is already correct; no change made.
