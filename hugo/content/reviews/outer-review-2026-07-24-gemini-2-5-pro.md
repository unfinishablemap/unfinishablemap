# Referee Report: "Volitional Control" and the Agent-Causal Metaphysics of Consciousness

## Introduction and Scope of the Audit

This report provides a comprehensive pre-publication review of the manuscript titled "Volitional Control," alongside its immediately surrounding conceptual corpus, submitted for evaluation in the domain of philosophy of mind and cognitive neuroscience. The manuscript undertakes an ambitious, yet fundamentally flawed, defense of a non-reductive, agent-causal theory of conscious volition. The author’s central thesis posits that the "phenomenology of volition"—specifically the qualitative, first-person experiences of effort, initiation, sustained control, and veto—accurately tracks genuine, non-physical top-down causal efficacy. To defend this libertarian metaphysical architecture, the text marshals a specific array of neuroscientific data—heavily relying on selected reinterpretations of the Libet paradigm by Schurger (2012), surgical resection data from Sjöberg (2024), cortical stimulation studies by Desmurget (2009), and clinical neuroplasticity research by Schwartz (1996, 2005)—to systematically dismantle epiphenomenal, compatibilist, and eliminative materialist accounts of mental causation.

The purpose of this audit is to rigorously evaluate the manuscript's empirical accuracy against the prevailing 2020–2025 neuroscientific and philosophical literature. The analysis critically assesses the methodological integrity of the article’s scientific citations, identifying where legacy results have been superseded, challenged, or reinterpreted by contemporary computational frameworks. Furthermore, the review isolates inferences within the manuscript that suffer from *petitio principii*—specifically, conclusions that are framed as empirical discoveries but which logically hold only if the author's underlying dualist and agent-causal metaphysical commitments are already implicitly granted. Finally, the audit highlights robust competing explanatory frameworks, most notably predictive processing and active inference, which the article conspicuously leaves untested.

The ensuing analysis demonstrates that the manuscript cannot be accepted for publication. It relies on a systematic mischaracterization of current neuroscientific consensus, conflates distinct neuroanatomical functions to preserve its philosophical priors, and forces classical biological data into an unsupported quantum-mechanical framework that the cited empirical data explicitly contradicts.

## The Deflation of the Libet Paradigm and the Misappropriation of the Stochastic Accumulator Model

The manuscript's primary strategy for neutralizing the threat posed by the Libet paradigm is to invoke the stochastic accumulator model, originally popularized by Schurger, Sitt, and Dehaene (2012). The traditional Libet experiment posed a formidable challenge to libertarian free will by demonstrating that a slow-ramping cortical readiness potential (RP) typically precedes the subject's conscious awareness of the urge to move, colloquially known as W-time. By citing Schurger's 2012 findings, the manuscript argues that the RP does not represent an unconscious neural decision to act. Instead, it reflects autocorrelated neural noise drifting toward a motor execution threshold. From this, the manuscript leaps to the conclusion that because the RP is merely stochastic background preparation, a non-physical conscious agent is left free to "select" or "veto" the threshold crossing at the precise moment of decision.

This argument is structurally incoherent and relies on an empirical paradigm that has been substantially revised in the 2020–2025 literature. First, from a purely conceptual standpoint, the manuscript attempts to harmonize a stochastic accumulator model with an agent-causal "selector." This constitutes a profound mathematical and logical contradiction. The stochastic accumulator model explains the RP specifically by positing that movement is triggered *precisely because* the random walk of neural noise happens to spontaneously cross a motor threshold. If a non-physical consciousness is required to "select" which option actualizes at the threshold, or to push the accumulation over the line via the Quantum Zeno effect, the system is no longer a stochastic accumulator. It is a deterministically interrupted system governed by a hidden variable (the conscious agent). The manuscript utilizes the mathematical elegance of Schurger's noise model to defeat Libet's deterministic threat, only to immediately violate the mathematical premises of that very model by injecting a non-physical causal agent at the threshold boundary.

More critically, the manuscript ignores the contemporary empirical literature surrounding the stochastic accumulator model, which severely undermines the article's reliance on the phenomenology of intention. Recent work by Schurger and colleagues—specifically the 2024–2025 probe-based paradigms developed by Gavenas, Schurger, and Maoz—has directly tested whether the buildup of the RP correlates with a subject's conscious awareness of motor preparation. In these paradigms, subjects are interrupted at random intervals and asked to report whether they were consciously preparing to move.

Contrary to the assumptions embedded in the manuscript's thesis, these recent studies found that pre-probe RP buildups were *not* related to the reported awareness of motor preparation. The findings strongly suggest that the RP is metacognitively accessible only in response to external task demands, and it does not reflect the onset of a conscious intention to move. Instead, reported preparation was related to beta desynchronization over the contralateral motor cortex shortly before probe onset. By entirely omitting this 2024–2025 literature, the manuscript preserves a false dichotomy: it assumes that if the RP is not an unconscious decision, it must automatically leave a functional void for a conscious, agent-driven intention to fill. The current literature demonstrates a deep disconnect between the phenomenological awareness of intention and the stochastic dynamics of motor preparation, rendering the manuscript's claim that "phenomenology tracks genuine causal structure" empirically baseless at the level of motor initiation.

## The SMA Resection Argument: Anatomical Conflation and the Reality of Cognitive Deficits

To further disarm the readiness potential and argue for the causal efficacy of conscious effort, the manuscript relies heavily on recent clinical findings by Sjöberg (2024) regarding patients who have undergone surgical resection of the supplementary motor area (SMA). The manuscript notes that the SMA is widely considered the generator of the readiness potential. Therefore, it argues, if the RP were truly the source of volition, removing its anatomical source should severely impair a patient's capacity for voluntary action. The manuscript cites Sjöberg to highlight that post-resection patients suffering from "SMA syndrome" (which often presents as akinetic mutism) nevertheless retain their subjective sense of voluntary action, reporting a feeling of making "100% effort" to move even when they cannot. The manuscript triumphantly concludes that the RP tracks mere motor preparation, not decision-making, and that the agent's volitional capacity—evidenced by the persistent feeling of effort—remains insulated from the physical brain.

This argument constitutes a profound neuroanatomical conflation and a highly selective, philosophically dangerous reading of the clinical data. The manuscript treats the "SMA" as a monolithic structure, entirely ignoring the well-established functional, cytoarchitectonic, and connectivity-based dissociation between the SMA proper and the pre-SMA. The SMA proper, located posteriorly, is primarily involved in motor execution and projects directly to the spinal cord. In contrast, the pre-SMA, located anteriorly, has extensive connectivity with the prefrontal cortex and is deeply implicated in higher-order cognitive control, working memory, conflict resolution, and the initiation of internally generated, volitional action.

When surgical resection induces SMA syndrome, the resulting akinetic mutism and loss of voluntary motor initiation are not merely physical execution failures; they are accompanied by profound executive dysfunctions. The 2020–2025 clinical and neuropsychological literature extensively documents that tumors and resections in the SMA complex lead to severe deficits in cognitive control, attention, planning, reasoning, and cognitive flexibility. For example, studies assessing patients with SMA lesions (such as those by Bala et al., 2025, and Sjöberg's broader research group) consistently show pronounced impairments in the Stroop test, the Wisconsin Card Sorting Test (WCST), verbal fluency, and the Tower of London test.

The manuscript ignores these cognitive deficits entirely, focusing exclusively on the patient's retained *feeling* of making an effort. By doing so, the text misses the devastating philosophical implications of the full clinical picture. If a patient with a resected pre-SMA loses the actual capacity for executive cognitive control, planning, and internally generated action, yet *still phenomenologically feels* as though they are exerting "100% effort", this does not prove that volition is independent of the brain. Rather, it proves the exact opposite of the manuscript's thesis: it demonstrates that the phenomenology of effort is functionally dissociable from the actual causal machinery of volition. The feeling of effort is preserved as an impotent, orphaned phenomenological artifact, while the actual causal capacity (executive control and conflict resolution) has been physically excised by the surgeon's scalpel. This clinical reality fatally undermines the article’s core tenet that the phenomenology of volition is a reliable indicator of non-physical causal power.

## Parietal Stimulation and the Multiplexing of Sensorimotor Integration

To support the hypothesis that consciousness selects neural outcomes via an abstract "willed intention," the manuscript cites the highly influential 2009 cortical stimulation study by Desmurget et al. The text claims that direct electrical stimulation of the posterior parietal cortex (PPC) produces a conscious intention or urge to move without actual movement, whereas stimulation of the premotor cortex produces actual movement without the subject's conscious awareness. From this double dissociation, the manuscript infers that the parietal cortex is the specific locus where non-physical "intention" enters the physical system, constituting a causal interface before the signal is routed to motor execution areas.

This interpretation relies on an antiquated, highly modular, and Cartesian view of the posterior parietal cortex that has been thoroughly superseded by recent systems neuroscience. The 2020–2025 literature demonstrates that the PPC is not a passive receptacle for "pure intention" handed down by a conscious agent. Instead, it is a highly complex association cortex that multiplexes spatial attention, sensory expectation, and motor planning within a continuous sensorimotor loop. The PPC operates as an integrative hub within a predictive processing framework, where it continuously updates internal models of the body and the environment based on anticipated sensory feedback.

Recent studies utilizing high-density electrode arrays in human subjects (e.g., Gavenas et al., 2024/2025) demonstrate that signals in the motor and parietal cortices correspond strongly to sensory expectation rather than pure motor intent. Furthermore, graph-theory analyses of multisensory integration show that the occipital-parietal module integrates visual cues into reach plans, while temporal-frontal modules integrate auditory instructions, forming a tightly coupled, highly distributed sensorimotor interface.

When Desmurget stimulated the PPC in 2009, he did not isolate a pure, agent-causal "intention." Rather, the electrical stimulation artificially activated the neural networks responsible for the *sensory expectation* of a movement. In the absence of an actual efference copy or a descending motor command to cancel this expectation, the brain's predictive processing architecture retrospectively interpreted this intense, anomalous sensory prediction as a "conscious intention" or an "urge." By treating the PPC as a Cartesian theater where a non-physical soul hands a script to the physical body, the manuscript ignores robust contemporary computational frameworks that explain the stimulation phenomenon purely through the biological mechanics of sensory prediction errors and forward models. The inference that the PPC represents an interface for non-physical causation holds only if one ignores a decade of computational neuroscience regarding how the brain constructs subjective states from sensorimotor mismatches.

## Neuroplasticity, Cognitive Behavioral Therapy, and the Question-Begging Quantum Zeno Hypothesis

Perhaps the most egregious example of empirical overreach and metaphysical smuggling in the manuscript is its heavy reliance on Jeffrey Schwartz’s research on Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) to argue for "self-directed neuroplasticity" governed by the Quantum Zeno effect. The manuscript posits that because OCD patients can voluntarily alter the metabolic activity of their caudate nucleus through conscious cognitive reframing (a four-step method emphasizing relabeling and revaluing intrusive thoughts), this constitutes evidence of "top-down causation without prior bottom-up causation". Following Henry Stapp's model of the quantum mind, the text argues that conscious attention holds desired neural states in place via rapid quantum observations (the Quantum Zeno effect). This mechanism allegedly allows consciousness to select among superposed neural states, thereby directing neuroplasticity without violating the conservation of energy.

This argument is scientifically vacuous, clinically misleading, and philosophically question-begging. Schwartz’s 1996 and 2005 studies, while historically interesting in demonstrating the biological impact of psychotherapy, merely demonstrate the neurobiological efficacy of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), specifically variants of Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP) and cognitive reappraisal. The 2020–2025 clinical and psychiatric literature on OCD universally recognizes that psychotherapeutic interventions alter brain networks through thoroughly classical mechanisms of experience-dependent neuroplasticity, such as Hebbian learning, long-term potentiation (LTP), and synaptic pruning.

When a patient engages in cognitive reframing (e.g., actively telling themselves "it's only my OCD, not reality"), they are utilizing highly evolved prefrontal cortical networks to exert top-down inhibitory control over hyperactive limbic and striatal circuits, specifically the cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical (CSTC) loop. This is a classical, physical, neural process. The behavioral changes enacted by the patient lead to the repeated firing of alternative, non-compulsive neural pathways, which, over time, physically re-weights synaptic connections through standard biological mechanisms. There is absolutely no requirement—nor any empirical justification—to invoke the collapse of quantum wave functions or the Quantum Zeno effect to explain why CBT changes the physical structure of the brain. The manuscript nominally admits that materialist accounts involving Hebbian learning exist, yet it capriciously dismisses them to force a quantum interpretation that the data simply does not demand.

Furthermore, the manuscript's invocation of quantum coherence in warm, wet biological tissue remains deeply problematic and is handled with insufficient rigor. While the text cites Hagan et al. (2002) to wave away Max Tegmark's devastating decoherence calculations, the 2020–2025 physics and quantum biology literature remains highly skeptical of sustained, macroscopic quantum coherence in the brain at timescales relevant to cognition. Recent critical reviews emphasize the sheer biophysical impossibility of maintaining the sub-picosecond coherence times required for Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch OR) or Zeno-effect selection in the thermal noise of the cerebral cortex.

By building its metaphysical architecture on the fringes of quantum biology, the manuscript abandons philosophical rigor in favor of ontological promiscuity. The logic of this section is a textbook example of *petitio principii*: the author assumes that the mind is a non-physical quantum observer in order to interpret the OCD neuroimaging data, and then uses that very interpretation as empirical "proof" of the non-physical quantum observer. To an uncommitted reader, the empirical data presented by Schwartz is vastly better explained by classical synaptic plasticity driven by prefrontal executive function.

## Intentional Binding, Predictive Processing, and the Phenomenology of Agency as Retrospective Inference

The manuscript heavily leans on the broader "phenomenology of volition"—the qualitative feeling of authorship, effort, and choice—as primary, irreducible evidence for an agent-causal power. To ground this empirically, the text references the phenomenon of "intentional binding" (traditionally associated with Brass and Haggard) and clinical dissociations like anarchic hand syndrome and delusions of control in schizophrenia. The argument advanced is that because the sense of agency (the feeling of initiating an action) can dissociate from the sense of ownership (the feeling that my body is moving), the feeling of agency must be tracking a real, distinct causal intervention by a non-physical agent in the physical world.

This conclusion demonstrates a terminal failure to engage with the dominant computational and theoretical framework of the last decade of cognitive science: predictive processing and active inference. In the 2020–2025 literature, intentional binding and the sense of agency are not viewed as transparent windows into non-physical causation. Instead, they are universally understood as the products of a reconstructive, post-dictive neuro-computational process.

According to comparator models and predictive coding architectures, the brain generates a forward model (an efference copy) of the expected sensory consequences of a planned motor action. When the actual sensory feedback matches the top-down prediction, the brain tags the action with a "sense of agency". Intentional binding—the subjective temporal compression between a voluntary action and its sensory outcome—is a post-hoc perceptual illusion generated by the brain to assert causal continuity and bind cause and effect together in conscious experience.

Recent systematic reviews of intentional binding in neuropsychiatric disorders, such as schizophrenia and autism spectrum disorder, emphasize that disturbances in agency arise from noisy predictive signaling, aberrant precision weighting, and faulty sensorimotor integration—not from a malfunctioning "quantum selector" or a detached Cartesian soul. When a patient with schizophrenia experiences a delusion of control, it is because their internal forward models fail to accurately predict the sensory consequences of their actions, leading the brain to attribute the movement to an external source.

The manuscript entirely fails to test its agent-causal hypothesis against this robust, computationally explicit framework. By completely ignoring predictive processing, the text commits the fallacy of taking introspection at face value. It assumes that because a decision *feels* like a unified, top-down, non-physical intervention, it must metaphysically *be* one. This is akin to arguing that because a mirage looks like water, the physics of optics must be rewritten to accommodate spontaneous hydrology. The failure to engage with the predictive coding account of intentional binding leaves the manuscript's central pillar—that phenomenology tracks metaphysical causation—entirely undefended against its strongest contemporary rival.

## Specific Weaknesses Justifying Rejection

The manuscript suffers from fundamental structural, empirical, and philosophical flaws. The following five specific weaknesses highlight where the text omits, contradicts, or mishandles current peer-reviewed evidence from the 2020–2025 literature:

1. **Mischaracterization of the Stochastic Accumulator and Metacognitive Access:** The manuscript relies heavily on early interpretations of Schurger (2012) to frame the readiness potential as neural noise awaiting a conscious "selection," but completely omits the 2024–2025 probe-based research which empirically demonstrates that pre-probe RP buildups are *not* related to reported awareness of motor preparation, thereby severing the link between the RP and conscious intention (Gavenas et al., *Imaging Neuroscience*, 2025).
2. **Conflation of SMA Anatomy and the Omission of Cognitive Deficits:** The manuscript utilizes Sjöberg's SMA resection data to argue that the SMA is irrelevant to volition because the subjective "feeling" of effort persists post-surgery; however, it fatally omits the profound executive function, working memory, and cognitive control deficits that characterize SMA syndrome, effectively ignoring the cytoarchitectonic dissociation between the SMA proper and the pre-SMA (Bala et al., *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 2025; Sjöberg et al., 2024).
3. **Failure to Address Predictive Processing Accounts of Intentional Binding:** The manuscript cites intentional binding and the phenomenology of agency as proof of non-physical agent causation, failing to test this claim against the dominant 2020–2025 predictive processing frameworks that successfully explain these phenomena as retrospective, computational inferences based on sensorimotor matching (e.g., *Psychiatry and Clinical Neurosciences*, 2024).
4. **Misinterpretation of Parietal Stimulation as "Pure Intention":** The text relies on Desmurget (2009) to posit the posterior parietal cortex as the specific locus of conscious intention, blatantly ignoring recent systems neuroscience frameworks that demonstrate the PPC multiplexes sensory expectation, spatial attention, and motor planning, rather than acting as a Cartesian theater for a non-physical will (Freedman & Ibos, *Neuron*, or Gavenas et al. preprints, 2020–2025).
5. **Question-Begging Quantum Interpretations of Standard Neuroplasticity:** The manuscript invokes Stapp's Quantum Zeno effect to explain Schwartz's OCD neuroimaging data, omitting the fact that standard experience-dependent synaptic plasticity (Hebbian learning) induced by Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT/ERP) fully and parsimoniously accounts for caudate nucleus normalization without requiring biologically implausible macroscopic quantum coherence (Thorsen et al., *Current Behavioral Neuroscience Reports*, 2021; *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 2025).

## Verdict

The manuscript must be rejected because it systematically misrepresents the empirical state of contemporary neuroscience and cognitive psychology to force classical biological data into a preconceived, scientifically unwarranted, and question-begging quantum-dualist framework.
