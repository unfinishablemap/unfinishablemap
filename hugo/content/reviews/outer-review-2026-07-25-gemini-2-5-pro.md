# Review Report: Metaphysical and Empirical Deficits in "Panpsychism" and the Case for Interactionist Dualism

The manuscript under review executes an ambitious, though ultimately flawed, structural maneuver to establish interactionist dualism as the necessary ontology of consciousness. Operating by elimination, the text constructs a theoretical theater in which physicalism and panpsychism are forced into mutual destruction. Physicalism, the manuscript asserts, is defeated by the "hard problem" of consciousness, specifically the alleged impossibility of deriving subjective experience from structural, non-experiential physical properties. Panpsychism—predominantly assessed through the lens of Russellian monism—is subsequently dismantled via the "combination problem," wherein the manuscript decrees that subjectivity is inherently non-compositional, making the aggregation of micro-experiences into a unified macro-subject logically unintelligible. Having engineered the failure of these two dominant paradigms, the manuscript proposes a non-reductive, bi-aspectual interactionist dualism, ostensibly mediated by quantum indeterminacy, as the sole logical survivor.

While the manuscript demonstrates an adept familiarity with the historical taxonomy of the philosophy of mind, its argumentative architecture is deeply compromised. A rigorous audit reveals that the text operates upon severe epistemological double standards, shielding its own foundational premises through bracketed framing while demanding exhaustive mechanistic accounts from its theoretical rivals. Furthermore, the manuscript is profoundly detached from the contemporary (2020–2025) empirical literature in quantum physics, cognitive neuroscience, and animal biology. It relies on quantum collapse mechanisms that have been recently falsified, dismisses physicalist and illusionist paradigms without engaging their most sophisticated modern articulations, and utilizes biological paradigms that the scientific community has heavily criticized or superseded. The ensuing audit deconstructs the manuscript’s logical inferences, empirical accuracy, and counterargument coverage to justify a recommendation of outright rejection.

## The Epistemological Double Standard and the Framing of the Combination Problem

The central argumentative load of the manuscript is borne by its treatment of the combination problem. The author correctly identifies that panpsychism, particularly in its panprotopsychist and Russellian variants, struggles to articulate a coherent mechanism by which discrete micro-experiential entities (e.g., fundamental particles) synthesize to form a unified, macroscopic conscious subject. Drawing upon the classic formulations of William James and the modern systematization by David Chalmers, the text parses this difficulty into subject combination, quality combination, and structural mismatch.

However, the manuscript weaponizes this problem through an unearned metaphysical decree: it declares that subjectivity is "inherently non-compositional" and that phenomenal unity cannot be achieved through physical or experiential aggregation. Because panpsychists cannot provide a transparent logical deduction for phenomenal bonding, the manuscript dismisses the entire paradigm. Yet, in the very next logical step, the author pivots to defend interactionist dualism by positing "phenomenal unity as primitive". The text asserts that consciousness is an ontologically basic, uncomposed entity that simply brings its intrinsic unity to the neural processes it couples with.

This represents a fatal epistemological asymmetry and a textbook example of bracketed framing. The manuscript demands that panpsychism supply a transparent, mechanistic, logical deduction for how unity arises, rejecting primitive "phenomenal bonding" as an ad hoc evasion. Simultaneously, the manuscript shields its own dualist framework by declaring unity a primitive axiom that requires no mechanistic explanation. If a dualist is permitted to posit phenomenal unity as an irreducible primitive to solve the binding problem, the panpsychist is equally entitled to posit phenomenal bonding as an irreducible primitive of micro-experiential relations.

Furthermore, the manuscript brackets the interaction problem—the notorious physical and conceptual difficulty of explaining how an immaterial, non-spatial substance can causally influence the physical universe without violating the causal closure of the physical or the conservation of energy. The author frames the combination problem as a "purely conceptual" mystery that demonstrates logical incoherence, while downplaying the interaction problem as a mere "empirical" problem that is safely "addressed via quantum mechanics".

| Theoretical Paradigm | Epistemic Standard Applied by Manuscript | Metaphysical Concession Granted by Manuscript |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Physicalism** | Must logically deduce phenomenal experience from structural, non-experiential properties without explanatory gaps. | None. "Brute emergence" is rejected as unintelligible and logically incoherent. |
| **Panpsychism** | Must mechanistically explain how bounded micro-subjects combine into a unified macro-subject. | None. "Phenomenal bonding" or fusionism is rejected as ad hoc or disguised emergence. |
| **Interactionist Dualism** | Permitted to leave the mechanism of immaterial-to-material causation as an empirical promissory note. | High. "Phenomenal unity" is granted as an irreducible primitive; the interaction problem is downgraded. |

This framing is philosophically gerrymandered. If consciousness is fundamentally non-physical, non-spatial, and uncomposed, proposing that it can bias spatial, physical, quantum states requires a mapping between incommensurable ontological categories that is every bit as conceptually opaque as the combination of micro-subjects. The manuscript attempts to win a theoretical debate not by providing a superior explanation, but by defining its own explanatory deficits as acceptable primitives while defining its rivals' deficits as fatal logical flaws.

## The Empirical Falsification of the Quantum Collapse Mechanism

To operationalize its interactionist dualism and domesticate the interaction problem, the manuscript relies heavily on the hypothesis that consciousness causally influences physical outcomes by biasing indeterminate quantum states. The text specifically references Henry Stapp’s quantum Zeno approach and the orchestrated objective reduction (Orch-OR) theory pioneered by Roger Penrose and Stuart Hameroff to provide a physical substrate for this mind-matter interface.

This reliance renders the manuscript empirically obsolete, as it entirely ignores the watershed 2020–2025 literature that has systematically dismantled the viability of macroscopic, gravity-related quantum wave function collapse. The manuscript requires a mechanism by which quantum superpositions in the brain are sustained long enough to be biased by consciousness, and subsequently collapsed into definite states. Penrose's model posits that quantum wave function collapse is an objective physical process driven by gravitational instability. Specifically, when a mass exists in a quantum superposition across two locations, it generates a superposition of different, incompatible spacetime curvatures. This superposition is fundamentally unstable and decays on a timescale defined by τ≈ℏ/ΔE, where ΔE is the gravitational self-energy of the difference between the two mass distributions. This gravity-related objective reduction, formalized mathematically as the Diósi-Penrose (DP) model, predicts a spontaneous collapse accompanied by a Brownian-like diffusion of the particles' motion, which, for charged particles, results in the emission of bremsstrahlung radiation (X-rays).

In a definitive experimental study published in *Nature Physics*, Donadi et al. (2021) conducted an ultra-sensitive underground test of this exact mechanism at the Gran Sasso National Laboratory. Operating 3,100 meters beneath a mountain to shield against cosmic rays, the researchers utilized a high-purity coaxial p-type germanium detector to measure the spontaneous radiation emission rate predicted by the DP model over a 62-day period. The experiment found no such excess radiation, setting a lower bound on the spatial cutoff parameter R0​≳0.54×10−10 m, which is an order of magnitude larger than Penrose’s original prediction. This result decisively falsified the natural, parameter-free version of the Diósi-Penrose model, severely constraining the parameter space for any gravity-induced collapse theory.

The manuscript fails to acknowledge this empirical falsification, treating quantum objective reduction as a viable, off-the-shelf mechanism to solve the interaction problem. While defenders of the DP model argue that introducing arbitrary free parameters or "colored noise" might rescue the mathematics, the specific physical substrate the manuscript relies upon has been empirically cornered. Furthermore, the manuscript's invocation of Stapp's (2006) Quantum Zeno model to explain volitional control is anachronistic. Contemporary physics and neuroscience literature provides no evidentiary basis for the premise that the warm, wet, noisy environment of the brain can maintain the necessary quantum coherence for the Zeno effect to be modulated by an external, non-physical consciousness at the timescale required for neural action potentials. The author's bracketed claim that quantum interaction is somehow empirically insulated is a defensive maneuver contradicted by the actual practice of physics, which routinely tests, constrains, and falsifies spontaneous collapse models.

## Evasion of Predictive Processing and the Physicalist Counterargument

In its haste to dismiss standard physicalism via the "Anti-Emergence Argument," the manuscript systematically mischaracterizes contemporary functionalist frameworks. The text concedes only that physicalism—specifically Predictive Processing (PP)—can explain "attention, perception, and action mechanisms," but insists that "functional organization does not explain why prediction is accompanied by experience". The author asserts that physical properties capture only relational structure, leaving an "intrinsic-nature-void" that physicalism cannot penetrate, concluding that the "feltness" of consciousness is completely untouched by predictive architectures.

This cursory dismissal ignores a vast, dominant body of literature from 2020–2025 wherein physicalists actively bridge the gap between predictive dynamics and phenomenal character. In a foundational paper in *Neuroscience of Consciousness*, Seth and Hohwy (2021) advance the predictive processing framework not merely as a model of unconscious behavior, but as a systematic, principled foundation for identifying the neural correlates of consciousness (NCCs). In this paradigm, the brain is not a passive processor of structural relations; it is a self-evidencing system that continuously generates top-down predictions about the hidden causes of sensory signals, updating its internal generative model to minimize prediction errors (or Bayesian surprise).

Contemporary theorists do not view PP as explaining mechanisms "in the dark." Instead, they map specific phenomenological structures directly onto the computational imperatives of precision-weighting and error minimization. Conscious sensory experience is literally constituted by the posterior prediction that best suppresses prediction errors across a deep hierarchical generative model. The phenomenal properties of an experience—its unity, its transparency, its spatial extension—are mathematically homologous to the structural properties of this high-dimensional generative model.

By framing physical properties as strictly describing external relational structure, the manuscript relies on an outdated, static view of physicalism. Active inference and predictive processing describe a dynamic topology where the reduction of free energy mathematically necessitates the generation of a unified virtual model of reality. The manuscript’s failure to engage with the argument that phenomenal properties *are* the precision-weighted structural properties of a generative model is a glaring omission. A submission to a top-tier philosophy journal cannot simply assert that physicalism fails to touch phenomenology without directly rebutting the specific phenomenological-to-computational mappings proposed by the Free Energy Principle and active inference literature of the current decade.

## Illusionism and the Meta-Problem of Consciousness

The manuscript’s foundational premise—that the "hard problem" is an insurmountable ontological barrier—relies entirely on the infallibility of first-person introspection. The author references illusionists such as Daniel Dennett and Keith Frankish, but dismisses them summarily by stating that "explaining the 'illusion' of phenomenal consciousness without generating a new meta-level gap" is impossible because "seeming is itself experiential".

This brief, almost flippant caricature completely ignores the explosive development of Illusionism and the "Meta-Problem of Consciousness" in the 2020–2025 literature. The meta-problem, formalized by David Chalmers (2020) in the *Journal of Consciousness Studies*, shifts the theoretical focus away from the hard problem itself, asking instead: why do we have the *intuition* that consciousness poses a hard problem in the first place?. The meta-problem challenges theorists to explain our problem intuitions (the belief that consciousness is non-physical, ineffable, and directly known) in topic-neutral, computational, or evolutionary terms without making explicit appeal to phenomenal consciousness in the explanation.

Contemporary illusionists have spent the last several years providing rigorous, non-circular solutions to this meta-problem. François Kammerer (2021) in *Synthese* explicitly tackles the exact objection the manuscript raises—the charge that illusionism is self-stultifying because illusions require conscious subjects. Through his framework of "Meta-Cartesianism," Kammerer explains why the illusion of phenomenal consciousness is uniquely powerful and almost inextricable, without needing to posit that the illusion itself requires phenomenal "seeming".

The illusionist solution to the meta-problem demonstrates that our cognitive architecture forces us to model our own sensory states as possessing irreducible properties. Shabasson (2021), also in *Synthese*, outlines three specific cognitive mechanisms that generate this illusion: *introspective opacity* (the subject lacks access to the complex neural processes forming introspective judgments), the *infallibility intuition* (the subject is disposed to judge their own categorizations as fundamentally true), and the *justification constraint*. Because the brain lacks the bandwidth to represent the sprawling neural etiology of its own visual processing, it outputs a compressed, low-dimensional representation. Due to introspective opacity, the system cannot see the representational nature of this output, and instead models it as a direct acquaintance with a primitive, non-relational, ineffable quality—a "quale".

We are computationally compelled to judge that we possess irreducible phenomenal properties, even though we do not. If our brains are evolutionarily hardwired to erroneously generate the *belief* in an explanatory gap, then the explanatory gap is an artifact of metacognition, not a feature of the physical universe. By ignoring the detailed metacognitive mechanisms proposed by contemporary illusionists to explain *why* we falsely believe in phenomenal properties, the manuscript treats the hard problem as an unassailable datum rather than a highly contested psychological phenomenon. Declaring illusionism self-refuting without engaging the models of introspective opacity currently debated in the literature renders the manuscript’s foundational anti-physicalist argument deeply vulnerable.

## Misrepresentation of Russellian Monism and Mereological Composition

The manuscript expends considerable effort attempting to dismantle Russellian Monism (RM), utilizing the threat of epiphenomenalism and the combination problem to argue that RM is an unstable midpoint that must inevitably collapse into dualism. Citing Brian Cutter's (2019) instability argument, the author claims that RM faces the "Contingency Thesis," which supposedly proves that consciousness-constituting quiddities make no distinctive causal contribution to the physical world, thereby forcing RM into the trap of epiphenomenalism.

This treatment is highly selective and systematically ignores sophisticated defenses of RM published between 2020 and 2025. The manuscript’s reliance on the contingency thesis to establish epiphenomenalism in RM has been forcefully rebutted by theorists such as Adam Pautz, Torin Alter, and Sam Coleman. The contingency thesis, often derived from Jaegwon Kim's exclusion argument, posits that the connection between a dispositional property (like negative charge) and its categorical ground (the quiddity) is contingent; therefore, if the quiddity were swapped for a different one, the physical disposition would remain unchanged, rendering the original quiddity causally idle.

However, recent literature points out a severe equivocation in this argument regarding the nature of modality. As Alter and Coleman (2023) argue, even if swapped-quiddity scenarios are *metaphysically* possible, it does not follow that the actual laws of nature are insensitive to quiddity identity. The nomological laws of our universe may dictate that only specific quiddities can ground specific physical dispositions. If distinct quiddities cannot make identical contributions to dispositions without contravening actual laws of nature, then quiddities are not causally idle. By conflating metaphysical possibility with nomological possibility, the manuscript prematurely declares RM epiphenomenal, ignoring a major sector of the current literature that successfully integrates RM with mental causation.

Furthermore, the manuscript’s handling of the combination problem relies on the dogmatic assertion that subjectivity is "inherently non-compositional" and that perspectives are defined by what they exclude, making the merging of subjects conceptually impossible. This completely overlooks recent mereological models of composite subjectivity. Luke Roelofs (2021), in the book-length defense *Combining Minds*, articulates a comprehensive framework for constitutive panpsychism and "manyism," detailing precisely how macro-subjects can be composed of micro-subjects without violating logical coherence. Roelofs demonstrates that sharing parts and functional integration can give rise to composite consciousness, directly challenging the manuscript's central decree that perspectives cannot merge. By omitting mereological defenses of subject combination, the manuscript knocks down a straw man of panpsychism in order to clear the theoretical field for its preferred dualism.

## Biological Baselines, UAL, and the Methodological Crisis in IIT

To ground its philosophical claims empirically, the manuscript attempts to use biological boundary cases—specifically *C. elegans*, the freshwater *Hydra*, and slime molds (*Physarum polycephalum*)—to test panpsychist predictions regarding the distribution of consciousness. The author argues that panpsychism fails because highly sophisticated cognitive behaviors in slime molds occur without neurons, which the author interprets as evidence for "cognition-without-consciousness," allegedly contradicting panpsychist expectations of phenomenal continuity.

This biological analysis is theoretically impoverished and relies on outdated heuristics. The manuscript cites the concept of "Unlimited Associative Learning" (UAL) as a behavioral marker but fails to engage with the robust 2020–2025 literature surrounding it, primarily the evolutionary transition models of Ginsburg and Jablonka (2020), and the multidimensional framework established by Jonathan Birch et al. (2020). Birch et al. (2020) in *Biological Reviews* argue forcefully that animal consciousness should not be treated as a monolithic, binary threshold that a species either crosses or fails to cross. Instead, they propose a multidimensional framework for evaluating consciousness across diverse taxa.

| Dimension of Animal Consciousness (Birch et al., 2020) | Description and Evolutionary Significance |
| --- | --- |
| **Perceptual Richness** | The level of detail, bandwidth, and multidimensionality of sensory input processing. |
| **Evaluative Richness** | The capacity for valence; experiencing stimuli as positively or negatively valenced (pain/pleasure). |
| **Diachronic Unity** | The integration of experience across time, enabling complex memory and anticipation. |
| **Synchronic Unity** | The integration of different sensory modalities into a single, cohesive phenomenal scene at a given moment. |
| **Self-Consciousness** | The organism's capacity to distinguish its own body and agency from the external environment. |

By forcing simple organisms into a rigid, binary "conscious vs. non-conscious" categorization to critique panpsychist continuity, the manuscript misrepresents the current scientific consensus. Modern comparative cognition views the phylogeny of consciousness as a gradual, multi-dimensional accretion of capacities, rendering the manuscript's binary gotcha-arguments against panpsychism biologically naive.

More egregiously, the manuscript utilizes Giulio Tononi’s Integrated Information Theory (IIT) as a serious theoretical foil, noting its alignment with panpsychism and its use of the Φ metric. While the manuscript mentions in passing that IIT faces a "pseudoscience accusation," it fails to address the sheer scale of the paradigm crisis surrounding the theory in the 2023–2025 literature. In 2023 and 2024, an open letter signed by over 100 prominent neuroscientists and published via *Nature Neuroscience* explicitly characterized IIT as "unfalsifiable pseudoscience".

The scientific backlash against IIT is not merely a philosophical disagreement; it is rooted in profound methodological failures. Critics argue that calculating Φ for any real biological system is computationally intractable, requiring knowledge of how every component of the system at every spatial and temporal scale constrains past and future states. Because Φ cannot actually be measured in humans, the theory is empirically vacuous in practice. Furthermore, the results of the Templeton-sponsored Cogitate adversarial collaboration (2023/2024) significantly complicated IIT's claims regarding the spatial correlates of consciousness, leading to accusations of theoretical overreach and a failure of falsifiability. Relying on IIT's mathematical postulates to draw metaphysical conclusions about panpsychism, while sidestepping the severe empirical indictments of IIT's mathematical formalism in the current literature, represents a critical failure of peer-level scientific engagement.

### Required Revisions: Weaknesses Justifying Rejection

The manuscript exhibits severe structural, empirical, and philosophical deficits that preclude publication in its current form. The author must address the following critical weaknesses:

1. **Falsified Quantum Physics Substrate**: The manuscript’s reliance on macroscopic, gravity-related quantum wave function collapse to ground its "Minimal Quantum Interaction" mechanism ignores the definitive empirical falsification of the parameter-free Diósi-Penrose model by Donadi et al. (2021) in *Nature Physics*.
2. **Evasion of the Meta-Problem of Consciousness**: The manuscript dismisses illusionism as self-refuting without engaging the 2020–2025 meta-problem literature; it must address Kammerer’s (2021) *Synthese* models of introspective opacity which provide purely computational explanations for why human cognition falsely generates the intuition of an explanatory gap.
3. **Misrepresentation of Predictive Processing (PP)**: The manuscript wrongly brackets physicalism as merely explaining structural functions "in the dark," failing to address Seth and Hohwy’s (2021) *Neuroscience of Consciousness* framework which explicitly maps hierarchical prediction error minimization directly to phenomenal structure.
4. **Omission of Contemporary Defenses of Russellian Monism (RM)**: The manuscript prematurely dismisses RM as epiphenomenal based on the contingency thesis, ignoring robust recent rebuttals by Alter, Coleman, and Pautz (2021/2023) that separate metaphysical possibility from the actual nomological laws governing quiddities.
5. **Outdated Application of Animal Consciousness Thresholds**: The manuscript's use of *C. elegans* and slime molds to challenge panpsychist continuity relies on obsolete binary consciousness thresholds, entirely ignoring Birch et al.'s (2020) widely adopted multi-dimensional framework for the phylogeny of animal consciousness.

**Verdict:**
This manuscript requires rejection and major, fundamental revision, as its central philosophical arguments rely on falsified quantum mechanics, epistemological double-standards regarding explanatory primitives, and a systematic failure to engage with the strongest 2020–2025 physicalist, illusionist, and mereological counter-literature.
