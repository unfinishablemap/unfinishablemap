# External referee report: **“Quantum Holism and Phenomenal Unity”**

**Recommendation:** **Major revision**

## Overall assessment

This is a stronger and more candid article than its title might suggest. It explicitly concedes that:

- quantum non-separability does not, without an interpretation, entail metaphysical “oneness”;
- entanglement does not solve the hard problem;
- the proposed relation between quantum and phenomenal unity is analogical and locational rather than constitutive;
- the empirical case remains tenet-driven rather than data-driven.

Those concessions are intellectually valuable. They prevent the article from making the crude inference that “quantum mechanics is mysterious, consciousness is mysterious, therefore consciousness is quantum.” [topics/quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity/)

Nevertheless, its central argument remains substantially overstated. The valid physical premise is that, **relative to a specified division into subsystems**, an entangled pure state cannot be represented as a product of the subsystem states. It does not follow that the system is one subject, one experience, one metaphysically simple entity, or even one privileged physical individual. The article oscillates between the modest claim that entanglement is a suggestive formal analogue and the much stronger claim that it supplies the one kind of physical “genuine whole” that classical mechanisms cannot supply.

The empirical sections are weaker still. The treatment of the quantum Zeno effect is technically misleading; the MRI-entanglement discussion materially misrepresents Warren’s classical objection; the microtubule-decoherence literature is compressed into a more balanced-looking dispute than the evidential record warrants; and the epothilone result is not a discriminating test of a quantum-consciousness mechanism. The article also fails to incorporate corrections made elsewhere on the site on 24 September: the site now describes the MRI result as one unreplicated study and concedes that Everettian branches can contain unified experiences, with rejection of Many Worlds securing only indexical identity rather than unity itself. # 1. Physics and empirical claims

## 1.1 What entanglement establishes—and what it does not

The article is correct that an entangled pure state is non-separable: given a specified tensor-product decomposition into systems A and B, it cannot be written as

∣ψ⟩AB​=∣ϕ⟩A​⊗∣χ⟩B​.

Its statement that the whole can possess properties not assignable separately to either subsystem is therefore unobjectionable in a formal sense.

The problem is the slide from this fact to “genuine ontological holism” and then to “genuinely one thing.” The article does acknowledge that this is an interpretation-dependent move, but it subsequently uses the stronger language as though the interpretive issue had been settled. The statement that entanglement is “the physical structure that most closely mirrors phenomenal unity” remains an assertion rather than a demonstrated comparison. [topics/quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity/)

### The missing factorisation problem

Entanglement is not a property of a state considered without qualification. It is a property of a state **relative to a division of the Hilbert space into subsystems**. Zanardi, Lidar and Lloyd explicitly show that subsystem structure and the associated notion of entanglement depend on the operationally accessible interactions and observables. This does not mean that all decompositions are physically equivalent: locality, interaction structure and experimental control can privilege some divisions. But the article must specify the relevant division rather than treating “the entangled state” as a self-identifying individual. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0308043)

For a consciousness theory, the missing questions are therefore:

- Which physical degrees of freedom are the relata?
- Why is that tensor-product decomposition privileged?
- What determines the spatial and temporal boundary of the entangled complex?
- Why does it correspond to one subject rather than several nested or overlapping subjects?
- Which entanglement measure, threshold or topology tracks changes in phenomenal unity?

Without answers, entanglement does not solve the subject-boundary problem. It supplies many candidate decompositions and many overlapping correlations, not an automatic census of experiencers.

### Decoherence exposes an important mismatch

Decoherence itself occurs because the system becomes entangled with environmental degrees of freedom. As Schlosshauer explains, system–environment entanglement suppresses locally observable interference while the larger global state remains entangled. Thus increasing entanglement with the environment can be precisely what destroys the locally usable coherence that quantum-consciousness proposals need. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.06282v1)

This creates a dilemma for the article’s structural analogy:

- if any entanglement is ontological unity, decoherence continually produces enormous wholes comprising brain and environment;
- if only selected internal entanglement counts, the theory needs a principled criterion distinguishing consciousness-relevant entanglement from ubiquitous environmental entanglement.

At present it supplies neither.

## 1.2 The quantum–phenomenal structural mismatch

The article focuses on one shared abstract feature: resistance to decomposition. But phenomenal unity has much richer structure:

- a first-person or perspectival centre;
- determinate contents and relations among contents;
- modality, spatial organisation and valence;
- temporal extension;
- graded and partially dissociable forms;
- ownership by a particular subject.

Entanglement as such supplies none of those. A maximally entangled pair does not intrinsically represent redness, pain, agency, temporal flow or for-me-ness. Nor does the Hilbert-space formalism say which correlations should count as contents of one experience.

Consequently, the article does not yet establish a structural match. It identifies a **single coarse analogy**—non-factorisability—and leaves the rest of the proposed mapping unspecified. What is needed is at least a candidate psychophysical map:

physical degrees of freedom⟶phenomenal contents and relations

together with an explanation of how:

- entanglement strength corresponds to degrees of unity;
- entanglement structure corresponds to conjoint phenomenology;
- the relevant basis corresponds to experienced features;
- physical temporal evolution corresponds to the specious present;
- one entangled complex is paired with one subject.

The site’s own positions register now acknowledges that the subject-pairing law is absent. That admission bears directly on this article and should be stated here, not left elsewhere in the corpus. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

## 1.3 The article’s argument against classical mechanisms is conditional, not established physics

The opening claim that “every classical approach” can describe only relations among separate things is not a result of classical physics. It is a metaphysical premise about what kind of physical realisation could constitute phenomenal unity.

A physicalist or functionalist need not claim that neurons literally fuse into a mereologically simple object. Such a theorist can claim instead that:

- one organism or recurrent network realises a globally integrated state;
- the state is unified by causal, representational or functional organisation;
- being one experience does not require its physical realiser to contain no distinguishable parts.

Bayne and Chalmers analyse phenomenal unity in terms of experiences being subsumed within an encompassing experience and possessing conjoint phenomenology. That analysis does not entail that the physical realiser of the encompassing experience must itself be a simple substance or an indivisible object. [Consciousness Online+1](https://consc.net/papers/unity.pdf)

The article can reasonably argue that recurrent processing, global workspace broadcasting or information integration fail to explain *why* there is phenomenal consciousness. But that is the general hard problem. It does not yet establish the more specific conclusion that classical organisation has a structural defect from which quantum organisation is exempt.

The argument should therefore be reformulated conditionally:

> Given realism about phenomenal unity, and given the additional premise that a physical correlate must mirror unity through non-separability rather than merely realise it through organised causal structure, entanglement is a distinctive candidate.

That would be a defensible framework-relative claim. The present universal conclusion—“all classical approaches fail”—begs the central question against rival theories.

### IIT is treated too dismissively

The criticism that IIT defines integration as unity and therefore merely stipulates its conclusion is incomplete. IIT does make an identity claim, but it attempts to constrain that identity through intrinsic cause–effect power, irreducibility, exclusion and a structured cause–effect repertoire. One may reject those claims, but the article should address them rather than describe IIT as a one-line relabelling exercise.

There is also a logical error in the sleep paragraph. From the fact that not every kind of neural integration accompanies consciousness, it does not follow that “integration alone” fails on IIT’s terms: IIT has never claimed that indiscriminate signal exchange or memory consolidation is sufficient. It claims that a particular form and maximum of intrinsic causal integration matters. The real objections concern whether Φ is well-defined, computationally tractable, empirically discriminating and phenomenologically adequate—not simply that sleeping brains still process information. Tononi’s cited paper supports a considerably richer account than the article acknowledges. [RCNi Company Limited](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/pdf/10.2307/25470707)

### Global workspace is criticised for something it does not necessarily claim to solve

The distinction between access unity and phenomenal unity is legitimate. But the article should distinguish:

- workspace models offered as accounts of conscious access;
- stronger physicalist interpretations identifying phenomenal consciousness with globally available or recurrently stabilised representation.

Showing that broadcasting leaves a dualist’s hard problem untouched does not independently refute the latter identification. It identifies the point of foundational disagreement.

The current **Unity of Consciousness** page already handles Dennett better: it admits that saying a narrative “requires a narrator” assumes the phenomenal subject that Multiple Drafts denies, so the exchange exposes a bedrock disagreement rather than an internal refutation. The target article should import that calibration. [concepts/unity-of-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/unity-of-consciousness/)

## 1.4 Decoherence: some correct numbers, but an unbalanced presentation

### Tegmark

The account of Tegmark is broadly correct. He concluded that estimated decoherence times for the neural degrees of freedom he modelled were vastly shorter than neuronal processing times and therefore that cognition should be treated effectively classically. His published article does support the target’s general characterisation, although any numerical range should be tied to the particular physical models and degrees of freedom being estimated. [APS Journals](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194)

### Hagan, Hameroff and Tuszynski

Hagan and colleagues did recalculate relevant times under Orch OR assumptions, changing the modelled superposition distance, charge/dipole treatment and dielectric parameters. The 10−5–10−4s range is traceable to their paper. [APS Journals](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevE.65.061901)

Two corrections are needed:

1. **“Thermal equilibrium conditions equivalent to death” is not an adequate attribution to Hagan.** Hagan discusses the relevance of non-equilibrium living systems, but the “equivalent to death” formulation is rhetoric appearing in Wiest’s later advocacy article. It should not be presented as though it were a conclusion established by the 2002 calculation. [APS Journals+1](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevE.65.061901)
2. Hagan’s result is a theoretical recalculation conditional on an Orch OR model. It is not an empirical measurement of coherence in neuronal microtubules.

### Reimers and McKemmish

The sentence saying Reimers and McKemmish “re-examined that recalibration” compresses distinct critiques into a direct replication dispute that did not occur.

- Reimers and colleagues analysed weak, strong and coherent regimes of Fröhlich condensation and argued that the biologically relevant coherent regime would require inaccessible conditions.
- McKemmish and colleagues examined the wider biological feasibility of Orch OR, including whether tubulin possesses the required switching and coherence properties, and concluded that known physical paradigms did not support quantum computation in microtubules. [APS Journals](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevE.80.021912)

Those papers are relevant criticisms, but neither is simply a point-by-point repeat of Hagan’s dielectric-parameter calculation. The literature should be presented as a set of different model-dependent disputes, not two teams measuring the same quantity and obtaining rival values.

### “The dispute is live, not settled either way” implies too much evidential symmetry

There remains legitimate uncertainty about which microscopic quantum effects might occur in living tissue. But the present evidence is not balanced between:

- an experimentally established, cognition-relevant entangled state in neural microtubules; and
- effective classical neural dynamics.

No accepted experiment has demonstrated the particular long-range, functionally controlled microtubule entanglement required by the article. A theoretical possibility calculation and a biological-feasibility critique are not symmetrical empirical confirmations. The appropriate conclusion is:

> Relevant neural entanglement has not been ruled out as a matter of principle, but it has not been demonstrated, and the specific Orch OR realisation remains highly conjectural.

## 1.5 Quantum biology is a precedent for specialised mechanisms, not for neural binding in general

The article is right that warm biological conditions do not make every functionally relevant quantum effect impossible. Radical-pair spin chemistry is a genuine counterexample to any blanket claim that biology must be classical at all relevant scales.

But the inference to neural consciousness is too quick. Avian magnetoreception—assuming the radical-pair account—is an exceptionally specialised photochemical system with evolved reaction partners, constrained geometry, spin-selective chemistry and limited timescales. It shows that biology can exploit particular quantum dynamics; it does not show that cortical or microtubular systems sustain brain-wide entanglement.

Luo and colleagues’ 2025 paper is a QM/MM computational study showing how protein and solvent reorganisation can stabilise proposed long-range charge-transfer radical-pair states. It supports the chemical plausibility of candidate magnetoreceptor states. It does not demonstrate in-vivo compass function, neural entanglement or phenomenal relevance. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41236751/)

The article should keep four propositions rigorously separate:

1. quantum mechanics underlies molecular chemistry;
2. some biomolecules sustain short-lived coherent or spin-correlated dynamics;
3. evolution exploits those dynamics for biological function;
4. the human brain sustains consciousness-relevant entanglement.

Evidence for the earlier propositions does not automatically transfer to the later ones.

## 1.6 The quantum Zeno paragraph is technically misleading

The claim that the quantum Zeno approach “requires only discrete attentional observations—each instantaneous—rather than long-lived coherence” should be removed.

The quantum Zeno effect concerns retardation or alteration of quantum evolution through sufficiently frequent measurements, interactions or more general quantum operations. In Denton and colleagues’ cryptochrome model, the relevant operation is **spin-selective radical-pair recombination**, not attention or conscious observation. The paper explicitly describes the Zeno dynamics in those chemical terms. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-55124-x)

Moreover, Denton’s model does not eliminate lifetime requirements. Its calculations find that magnetosensitivity is strongly attenuated when coherence falls below an approximately 700-nanosecond requirement in the model, and some of its largest effects occur under idealised assumptions or toy-system conditions. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-55124-x)

A QZE consciousness model would still have to specify:

- the quantum state being repeatedly acted upon;
- the physical operator or measurement interaction;
- the intervention rate;
- the coherence and relaxation times;
- how attention changes the Hamiltonian, measurement context or coupling;
- how the resulting microscopic change affects neural activity.

Invoking “instantaneous observations” does not bridge a 300-millisecond behavioural timescale. A sequence of instantaneous interventions is still a dynamical process whose rate and physical implementation matter.

This paragraph also conflicts with the Map’s current positions register, which demotes Stapp’s QZE model relative to post-decoherence selection. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

## 1.7 Decoherence does not select an outcome—but that does not favour consciousness

The article is broadly right that decoherence by itself does not solve the measurement problem. But “the system remains in a mixture of possible outcomes” is imprecise.

After tracing over the environment, the reduced density matrix behaves like an **improper mixture** in the decoherence-selected basis. The larger system–environment state remains entangled under unitary evolution. Whether an additional actualisation postulate is required depends on the interpretation. Schlosshauer emphasises that decoherence is interpretation-neutral and does not itself supply a full interpretation or new empirical predictions. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.06282v1)

The article then commits a gap-filling inference:

> Decoherence does not select one actual outcome; therefore consciousness could act at that point.

The first proposition is a genuine foundational issue. The second is compatible with the Map’s tenets but receives no support from the first. An explanatory gap is not positive evidence for a particular occupant of the gap.

The Map’s post-decoherence programme is substantially more honest about this. It calls the proposal a speculative architecture, admits that its components are not independent evidence, and lists unresolved debts concerning the selection locus, mathematical definition of alternatives, temporal menu refresh, mechanism and amplification. [apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme/)

## 1.8 Epothilone and anaesthesia

The article accurately reports the headline finding: epothilone-B-treated rats showed an average 69-second delay in loss of righting reflex under 4% isoflurane, with a reported Cohen’s d=1.9. [Jove Visualize](https://visualize.jove.com/39147581-microtubule-stabilizer-epothilone-b-delays-anesthetic-induced-unconsciousness-in-rats)

Its own qualification—that classical microtubule functions must first be excluded—is correct and should be made more prominent. The study shows that manipulating microtubules can alter an anaesthetic behavioural endpoint. It does not show that:

- an entangled microtubule state existed;
- isoflurane disrupted such a state;
- epothilone preserved entanglement;
- the behavioural delay reflected phenomenal consciousness rather than motor, arousal or pharmacokinetic effects.

Loss of righting reflex is an operational proxy, not a direct measurement of phenomenal consciousness.

A later related mouse study further complicates the proposed directional inference. Epothilone D and vinblastine shifted isoflurane response curves toward greater sensitivity, paclitaxel produced a small shift toward resistance, and epothilone D did not significantly change fixed-concentration induction latency. The authors described microtubule-modulating drugs as affecting anaesthetic sensitivity in **disparate directions**. This is not a direct replication of the epothilone B experiment, but it undermines any simple prediction that stabilisation should reliably produce anaesthetic resistance. [Springer Nature Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12871-025-02956-9)

Wiest’s 2025 article exists and makes the argument attributed to it, but it is an argumentative review with no new data, not independent experimental confirmation. It also uses the same disputed MRI and microtubule literature, so it should not be counted as an additional evidential line. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niaf011/8127081)

## 1.9 The MRI “entanglement signature” is overstated and Warren is misrepresented

Kerskens and López Pérez did report unusual heartbeat-locked zero-quantum-coherence MRI signals and interpreted them as possible evidence of consciousness-related entanglement. Their own abstract uses cautious language—essentially, that they *may* have witnessed entanglement—and acknowledges the difficulty of distinguishing quantum correlations from classical NMR effects. [SETI](https://www.seti.net/Neuron%20Lab/NeuronReferences/Quantum%20Brain%20Functions%20Kerskens_2022_J._Phys._Commun._6_105001.pdf)

The target article overcompresses three claims into one apparently coherent experimental result:

> present in waking, disappeared in sleep, and correlated with short-term memory.

In fact:

- the published experiment involved 40 participants overall;
- the sleep-related pattern came from seven participants, only two of whom reported falling asleep, plus a twenty-minute case observation;
- the short-term-memory association came from a separate later study, which is not included in the target article’s bibliography. [SETI+1](https://www.seti.net/Neuron%20Lab/NeuronReferences/Quantum%20Brain%20Functions%20Kerskens_2022_J._Phys._Commun._6_105001.pdf)

“Disappeared during sleep” is therefore too categorical, and the memory claim currently has an orphan citation.

More seriously, the description of Warren is inaccurate. Warren did not merely suggest that the signal was an artefact such that there might be “no real signal to explain.” He argued that intermolecular multiple-quantum-coherence signals are well-known, essentially classical NMR phenomena; that structured biological tissue can produce complex contrast; that important sequence parameters were inadequately reported; and that the experiment did not establish entanglement. He explicitly presented a classical dipolar/multicompartment account of how such signals can arise. [ResearchGate+2ResearchGate+2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369521721_Comment_on_%27Experimental_indications_of_non-classical_brain_function%27_2022_Journal_of_Physics_Communications_6_105001/fulltext/641f118366f8522c38d2ad3e/Comment-on-Experimental-indications-of-non-classical-brain-function-2022-Journal-of-Physics-Communications-6-105001.pdf)

The target’s statement that Warren supplied “no alternative classical explanation” is therefore false. This is the most serious citation-fidelity defect in the article.

The proper conclusion is:

> Kerskens and López Pérez reported an unusual MRI signal and advanced a controversial entanglement-witness interpretation. Warren argued that established classical iMQC physics and undercharacterised tissue structure suffice to block that inference. No independent replication establishing entanglement has yet been identified.

That conclusion also matches the site’s corrected 24 September calibration. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

## 1.10 Zero-lag synchrony

The article does something commendable here: it reports Baum’s quantum argument and immediately presents Vicente and colleagues’ classical dynamical-relaying counterexample.

Vicente et al. showed that two remote neural populations can self-organise into zero-lag oscillations despite long conduction delays when coupled through an appropriate relay topology. That is an existence proof that zero-lag synchrony does not require quantum nonlocality. [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.0809353105)

But the implications are not carried through:

- The article’s later falsifier says that *if* zero-lag synchrony can arise classically, a motivation for quantum binding would weaken.
- The article has already cited a peer-reviewed model demonstrating exactly that possibility.

Falsifier 2 has therefore already been met. Zero-lag synchrony should no longer be counted as evidence or motivation for quantum binding unless new empirical constraints exclude the relay class of mechanisms in the relevant neural systems.

The article’s statement about synchrony across regions ten centimetres apart also needs a direct experimental citation. Baum is a philosophical preprint, not the appropriate source for the underlying neurophysiology.

# 3. Serious counterarguments missed or underplayed

## 3.1 Realisation does not require literal physical fusion

The article assumes that one experience can only be physically realised by something that is itself “literally one thing.” That premise is not defended.

Analogously, a single computation, organism, control system or electromagnetic mode can have physically distinguishable components while constituting one higher-level process. A physicalist can therefore accept every fact about phenomenal part–whole structure while denying that the underlying neural realiser must be simple or non-composite.

To turn this into an argument rather than an intuition, the article needs to explain why:

one phenomenal state⇒one non-separable physical individual

rather than:

one phenomenal state⇒one appropriately organised physical process.

At present the first conditional is assumed.

## 3.2 The combination problem is not solved; it becomes a pairing problem

The article links to the combination problem but does not apply it to its own proposal.

If the microphysical constituents are not conscious, entangling them does not explain why a subject appears. If they are proto-conscious or conscious, the standard subject-combination problem returns. Under the Map’s interactionist dualism, a related problem remains even without micro-subjects:

> Why is this nonphysical subject paired with this selected entangled complex, rather than a subcomplex, a larger brain–environment state, or another overlapping entangled decomposition?

Entanglement does not supply a subject-pairing law. The Map now explicitly acknowledges that it lacks one. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

## 3.3 The unity described by Bayne and Chalmers is internally articulated

Phenomenal unity is not featureless simplicity. A total experience has distinguishable visual, auditory, bodily and cognitive aspects. Bayne and Chalmers’ subsumptive unity is therefore a structured part–whole relation: experiences can be parts of one encompassing experience without first existing as independent subjects that later fuse. The current site article makes this distinction explicitly. [concepts/unity-of-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/unity-of-consciousness/)

That weakens the target’s repeated contrast between:

- classical multiplicity; and
- phenomenal “intrinsic singularity.”

Phenomenal experience is simultaneously one and many: one encompassing field with internal articulation. A successful structural comparison therefore needs more than physical non-factorisability.

## 3.4 Unity is graded and dissociable

The article treats phenomenal unity as nearly binary. The site’s own wider discussion recognises that synchronic, diachronic, access and subject unity can dissociate, drawing on split-brain cases, sleep, dissociation and distributed nervous systems. It concludes that “unity” may comprise several related problems requiring different explanations. [concepts/unity-of-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/unity-of-consciousness/)

This matters empirically. A quantum binding theory should predict what happens to its proposed entangled substrate when:

- perceptual contents remain unified but report access fails;
- interhemispheric integration is disrupted;
- temporal continuity changes;
- subject unity appears to divide or become indeterminate;
- anaesthesia gradually deepens.

A binary “entangled versus not entangled” picture is unlikely to capture these patterns without a richer model.

## 3.5 Entanglement does not privilege phenomenal content

Suppose a large neural system is genuinely entangled. Nothing in that fact alone determines:

- which basis corresponds to colours, sounds or intentions;
- why some correlations are experienced and others are not;
- why irrelevant environmental correlations are excluded;
- why the state is experienced from one point of view;
- why the same degree of entanglement should not correspond to radically different phenomenology.

This is a quantum version of the multiple-realisation and inverted-qualia problem. A structural theory needs psychophysical laws, not merely a physical resemblance.

## 3.6 Collapse may remove the very entanglement invoked to explain unity

The proposal alternates among three possible loci:

1. the pre-collapse entangled state;
2. the collapse or reduction event;
3. the post-collapse actual state.

These are not interchangeable. If phenomenal unity corresponds to the entangled state, why is the conscious moment associated with collapse? If it corresponds to the reduction event, what mathematical feature of that event supplies unity? If it corresponds to the selected outcome, that outcome may no longer possess the same entanglement.

The article needs one explicit model rather than moving among entanglement, collapse and conscious selection according to which objection is being answered.

## 3.7 Everettian phenomenal unity

The article’s claim that quantum binding only explains unity if collapse is real is untenable. An Everettian can say that each decohered branch contains a subject with one unified experience. The metaphysical dispute is then about branching identity, probability and actuality—not about whether an experience inside a branch is unified.

The Map has now corrected this elsewhere, but the target still uses unity to argue for collapse. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

## 3.8 Illusionism is not answered by requiring someone “to be deceived”

Frankish’s illusionist need not postulate a Cartesian subject to whom a false phenomenal appearance is presented. The proposal is that introspective systems form representations and dispositions that lead to phenomenal judgments without themselves possessing the properties represented. [Keith Frankish](https://keithfrankish.github.io/articles/Frankish_Illusionism%20as%20a%20theory%20of%20consciousness_eprint.pdf)

The article’s regress argument therefore assumes the realist conception of appearance that the illusionist rejects. This can support a statement of disagreement, but not a refutation.

Two additional statements should be removed or qualified:

- “Every conscious being reports” unity is literally false: many putatively conscious organisms, infants and impaired humans cannot report anything.
- Claims about nirodha-samāpatti, rigpa and contentless witness consciousness require specialist citations and engagement with rival interpretations. They cannot be introduced as uncontested introspective data.

# 4. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article is commendably open that its conclusion is tenet-driven. Nevertheless, several devices insulate the position from meaningful revision.

## 4.1 Classical successes are classified away in advance

Any classical mechanism is assigned to BP1, access or “mere coordination,” while BP2 is defined as the residue that such mechanisms cannot touch. This risks making failure true by definition.

A classical model could explain progressively more of:

- perceptual binding;
- cross-modal integration;
- report;
- metacognition;
- self-model formation;
- changes in unity under lesion, sleep and anaesthesia.

The article can always reply that “phenomenal oneness” remains. That may be a legitimate hard-problem stance, but it means empirical classical success cannot test the structural claim. The claimed failure is philosophical rather than empirical and should be labelled accordingly.

## 4.2 The proposed falsifiers are poorly operationalised

### “Complete classical determinism”

Fundamental neural matter is quantum mechanical whatever consciousness theory is true. The relevant rival is not literal fundamental classical determinism, but whether an effective classical stochastic model fully explains the relevant neural and behavioural evidence. The proposed criterion sets the burden unrealistically high.

### Zero-lag synchrony

This condition has already been met in principle by Vicente’s relay model. It should be recorded as a lost motivation, not retained as a future falsifier. [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.0809353105)

### Failure to replicate Kerskens

“Sustained failure” has no threshold: how many laboratories, protocols and years count? The article should state that, absent independent replication with a validated entanglement witness, the result presently carries little positive weight.

### Anaesthetic mechanisms “fully classical”

One need not explain every action of every anaesthetic before the epothilone result loses discriminating force. Demonstrating a plausible classical pathway from microtubule modulation to LORR is sufficient to show that the result does not uniquely favour the quantum model.

### Unified AI consciousness

There is no agreed independent test for “genuine phenomenal unity” in an AI. The proposed falsifier relies on resolving the very other-minds problem at issue and is therefore not presently operational.

## 4.3 A missing outcome explanation is treated as an available intervention point

The argument repeatedly proceeds:

1. standard physics leaves an interpretive gap;
2. therefore consciousness may enter there.

But almost any additional ontology can be placed in an underdetermined gap. The Map needs positive constraints showing why consciousness:

- acts at this locus;
- acts through this operator;
- produces these probabilities;
- preserves no-signalling;
- avoids observable Born-rule deviations;
- pairs outcomes with particular subjects.

The site’s own post-decoherence article concedes that these components have not been assembled into a worked model. [apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme/)

## 4.4 “Structural resemblance” can retreat when stronger claims fail

The article initially says entanglement supplies genuine ontological unity and addresses the structure-matching problem. It later concedes that the relation is only analogical and locational.

The weaker formulation is defensible, but it also forfeits much of the advertised explanatory gain. If entanglement neither constitutes nor explains phenomenal unity, then “structural match” needs an independent measure by which it can outperform:

- causal integration;
- recurrent closure;
- global availability;
- dynamical attractors;
- self-modelling;
- other nondecomposable mathematical structures.

Otherwise the claim is too elastic to be tested.
