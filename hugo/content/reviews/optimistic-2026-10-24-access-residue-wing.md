**Calibration flags** (all on the hub; repaired in the Priority List):
1. **L57: "Acknowledging A-consciousness *strengthens* the dualist position."** Physicalists accept the distinction, Block among them (`first-order-representationalism` L80: "Block is himself a *physicalist*"). The distinction helps the dualist by isolating the target. It does not evidence the dualist claim. Item 1(a).
2. **L61: "For the Map the result confirms rather than threatens the dualist reading".** The clause before says "phenomenal consciousness is not shown to be absent, it is simply left untouched", and a result that leaves P untouched cannot confirm a thesis about P. "Even a maximally rich functional workspace" misdescribes J-space. The GWT hub lists its disanalogies: a single feedforward pass, no competing input processors, working memory carried by attention (L110). The hub reports the result "without settling the phenomenal question" (L112). Item 1(b).
3. **L71: GWT's three functions read as evidence that "consciousness is functionally efficacious rather than epiphenomenal".** These are functions of conscious *access*. Read as access, the sentence is trivially true, since access is defined by function. Read as phenomenal consciousness, it is the transfer the page reports Block warning against: functions of access-machinery "illicitly transferred" to phenomenal consciousness (L53). This is where CoT's L103 claim about the hub fails. Item 1(c).
4. **L111: "then P-consciousness requires non-physical explanation."** The conclusion does not follow from the two premises given. Type-B physicalists accept both and deny it: Block and Stalnaker answer no to "If there is no a priori entailment from microphysical truths to phenomenal truths, is physicalism about the phenomenal false?" (Chalmers & Jackson 2001, checked this run). The missing premise comes from conceivability, whose modal rationalism is contested. The same paragraph's "Block's dissociations strengthen dualism" repeats flag 1. Item 1(d).
5. **L119: selection "among possibilities physics leaves undetermined".** This escapes closure stated as sufficiency without the chance-fixing concession. The corpus wording is at `hempels-dilemma` L115 and `causal-closure` L138. Item 2(a).
6. **L133: "Dualism is more complex but handles both access (functional) and phenomenology (irreducible)."** This uses Tenet 5 as a scorecard against physicalism, and dualism posits phenomenology rather than handling it. The next sentence says the distinction shows "*what* it cannot (phenomenology)", which asserts flag 4's conclusion as shown. The corpus pattern is "governs only the Map's own reasoning" (VI L111, CoT L109). Item 2(b).
7. **L143: the Further Reading gloss "great ape intelligence shows what's possible without full A-consciousness".** The spoke says the opposite of what the gloss reports. `baseline-cognition` L55 says its "consciousness" means "primarily *phenomenal consciousness* … not merely information-processing capacity ([access-consciousness](/concepts/access-consciousness/))", and allows "even if functionalist accounts grant apes sophisticated access consciousness". The gloss is an animal-capacity claim the spoke does not make. Item 3(c).

### The shared shape: what the functional story leaves over

Each page explains a functional achievement without a fact about experience, then names what is left:
