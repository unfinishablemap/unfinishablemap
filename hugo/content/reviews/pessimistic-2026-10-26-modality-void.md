## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would say the article has quietly conceded her case and dressed the concession as a void. Its second paragraph grants that "psychophysics, anatomy and behavioural testing recover modality facts the subject cannot", and its own indirect-methods section grants that "a count fall[s] out of a sense's coordinates rather than out of intuition". That is eliminativism about the folk taxonomy of the senses, stated in the article's voice. What remains after the concession is a claim that subjects are "handed an unauditable verdict" — but the verdict is a *neural* classification whose unreliability is already a neuroscientific finding (oral referral, McGurk), and the article's own gloss on Hurley and Noë agrees that the explanatory work is done "by charting relations between brain, body and world", not by any first-person residue. She would add that the third deflation's closing phrase, "a stake no third-person taxonomy can retire", is the only sentence in the article that resists her, and it is asserted rather than argued: a theory of metacognition that explains why first-order sensory classification has no independent monitor (because none was ever selected for) retires the "why" the article says cannot be retired. The honest article would scope the phrase to what a *taxonomy* cannot do.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett would relish the McGurk paragraph and then turn it. The article grants Fréchette that the classification is "evidently given" and calls the tag "present, evident and unrevisable". But Fréchette himself says the classification "may ultimately be proven right or wrong within a given perspective" — the *experience* persists against knowledge, the *judgement* is revised the moment the mechanism is explained. That is heterophenomenology's lesson exactly: the subject's report is data, not authority, and the article's "unrevisable" smuggles authority back in. Dennett would also press the Dualism paragraph: an article that says experience carries no modality tags at all, two screens after saying the tag is present and evident, is an article that has not decided what the mind side is. He would read the inconsistency as evidence that "the mind side" is doing no work — the tags are neural, the lack of an auditor is neural, and the dualist vocabulary is an overlay.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark has little purchase here and would say so: the article's Bidirectional Interaction remark is explicitly "an angle, not a finding", and it correctly describes the tenet's mechanism as selection among physically underdetermined *outcomes* (the 09-07 fix). His one point is structural. If the interface is "modality-blind on the mind side", as the remark says, then every quantum-level selection the tenet posits is made without access to which channel the downstream content belongs to — which makes the interface less like a perceiver choosing and more like a bias with no sensory handle. The remark should either say what the selecting consciousness *does* have access to, or stop implying that an unread tag is a feature rather than a cost.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch would note that the article does not touch No Many Worlds and does not read it off experience, which is correct discipline. He would instead attack the Occam paragraph on his own ground. "Five is the simplest count" is false even on the article's terms: a parsimonious carving favours *fewer* kinds, not the number a tradition happened to settle on, and the paragraph supplies no measure on which five is simplest. If the Map wants a Tenet 5 instance it must show parsimony *selecting* the wrong answer — the historical cases in `tenets.md` are of that form — and the senses case shows familiarity selecting it instead. Calling that a parsimony failure is the kind of loose use of "simplicity" that lets the Map's opponents say Tenet 5 is a licence rather than a principle.

### The Empiricist

The Popperian finds the void's core claim well formed and falsifiable in its first-personal scoping, and would say the 09-07 fix improved it: "a false tag arrives with exactly the evidence a true one carries" is a claim a dissociating patient population would refute, and the common-cause paragraph names that test honestly and says none is cited. His complaints are the two absolutes that survived. "A stake no third-person taxonomy can retire" quantifies over all possible third-person work on the strength of no argument; and "nothing here separates the readings" in the Dualism paragraph is true but sits beside a claim ("what one would expect if the mind side carries no modality tags") that *does* purport to separate them, in the Map's favour, by a premise the article elsewhere denies. He would also ask that the Keeley gloss, taken from O'Callaghan at second hand (the reference list says so), not be used to make O'Callaghan "go further" than the article has shown either author to go.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would accept the void's three faces as a demonstration that the senses are not self-established — the carving is dependent on taxonomic convention, the tag on a mechanism, the boundary on routing — and would object only to the article's recoil from that result. The third deflation calls the dissolution of the counting face a threat to be survived; the Madhyamika calls it the finding. He would also notice that the article's defence of the attribution face turns on a *subject* who is "handed an unauditable verdict about their own experience", and that the verdict, the handing and the owner are all posited rather than found: strip the owner and what remains is a classification without a classifier, which is what the McGurk evidence actually shows. Nothing in the article answers this, and nothing needs to — but the Relation section should not then count the void as consonant with a mind side of any particular shape.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The Dualism paragraph runs on the "no tags" premise the lead abandoned

- **File**: `obsidian/voids/modality-void.md`
- **Location**: §Relation to Site Perspective, Dualism paragraph — "This void is consonant with that *result*: a mind-side boundary with no modality-specific signature is what one would expect if the mind side carries no modality tags at all."
- **Severity**: **High** — an internal contradiction in the tenet section, and an epistemic/metaphysical equivocation of the kind the skill lists as a critical issue
- **Problem**: The lead (09-07 formulation) says "the modality of an experience is *delivered* along with the content, evidently and without option"; the McGurk paragraph grants Fréchette "that modality classification is part of experience itself" and says "The tag is present, evident and unrevisable". On the article's own thesis, then, the mind side *carries* modality tags — seeing is given as seeing — and what it lacks is a second channel to audit them. The Dualism paragraph says the opposite: consonance with the apex's supramodal signature is "what one would expect if the mind side carries no modality tags at all". That is the pre-refine "format, not datum" premise, which the 09-07 review's Issue 1 found to be the direct negation of Fréchette's thesis and had replaced in the lead. It was not replaced here because the same review told the refine to "preserve verbatim" the consonance sentence as "the honest and sufficient version" — correct under the old lead, stranded under the new one.

  The equivocation is the one the skill flags: the article's evidence supports an *epistemic* claim (a tag the subject cannot audit) and the Dualism paragraph recruits it for a *metaphysical* one (a mind side without tags). Note also that the article's actual thesis cuts the other way from the consonance it claims: if experience is modality-tagged on the mind side, a supramodal mind-side boundary is not "what one would expect" from the void; the void is silent on it. The honest relation is compatibility without prediction.
- **Recommendation**: Replace the sentence with one that states the compatibility and denies the prediction, naming the tags experience carries so the paragraph cannot be read as a tagless mind side. The following sentences ("The apex's carving is drawn from the brain side … Consonant, not probative — a physicalist reads the same signature as late convergence …") survive unchanged: consonance there means compatibility, and the rival reading is about the neural boundary. See Priority List item 1 (+26).

### Issue 2: The Tenet 5 instance rests on two premises the article elsewhere denies or never argues

- **File**: `obsidian/voids/modality-void.md`
- **Location**: §Relation to Site Perspective, Occam paragraph — "Five is the simplest count and the one consciousness reports. … The parsimonious carving is the one the evidence supports least; parsimony here selects an answer rather than tracking one." Also §The Counting Face, opening sentence — "Introspection answers *five*".
- **Severity**: **Medium–High** — a tenet connection manufactured from a mislabelled premise (calibration family: data presented as instancing a tenet it merely sits beside)
- **Problem**: (a) *Consciousness does not report five.* The counting face's own close says "the *drawing* is not what introspection does", and Macpherson, in the very passage the paragraph quotes, attributes the five to the "Aristotelian" tradition and says the folk concept is "simply silent" on the number. Five is inherited, not introspected. The opening "Introspection answers *five*" repeats the same error inside the counting face and contradicts its last sentence. (b) *Five is not the simplest count.* Parsimony, as `tenets.md` binds it (L69: "parsimony as a *truth-tracking* heuristic — the claim that the simpler theory is more likely true"), would favour fewer kinds, not a conventional number; the paragraph gives no measure on which five is simplest. (c) *"the one the evidence supports least"* is unsupported: Macpherson's verdict is that the count is *open*, not that five is the worst-supported candidate. With (a) and (b) gone, "parsimony here selects an answer rather than tracking one" has no parsimony in it. What the case does show is familiarity standing in for warrant — a real lesson, and a narrower one than a Tenet 5 instance.
- **Recommendation**: Two edits, same file. Zero-cost swap of "Introspection" for "Tradition" at the counting face's opening, and a rewrite of the Occam paragraph that keeps the verified Macpherson quotations, drops "simplest" and "consciousness reports", and states the narrower carry-over (felt obviousness is no measure of warrant; parsimony proper did not select the five). See Priority List item 2 (0 and +21).

- **Severity**: **Medium**
- **Problem**: Two things. First, the deflation is answered for the counting and attribution faces and the boundary face is dropped, although the article's own classification (L74) gives the boundary face a first-personally *occluded* half with exactly the attribution face's structure ("the subject's verdict on which sense a rerouted channel joins is generated by the rerouted system itself"). The answer should carry it. Second, "no third-person taxonomy can retire" is a universal quantified over all third-person work, and the "why" it attaches to is a question third-person theory can plausibly answer (a metacognitive architecture that monitors first-order classifications through the same first-order signals, because nothing selected for a second monitor).
