## Executive Summary

This wing is where the Map's tenets stop being commitments and start being *specifications*, and its dominant virtue is that it says out loud which of its own results are coherence results rather than evidence. Three articles independently draw the line the Hardline Empiricist persona exists to check for — `laws-and-dispositions` concedes that its central cross-product elimination "turns on internal coherence and the avoidance of epiphenomenalism, not on empirical adequacy"; `the-psychophysical-control-law` concedes "in-principle falsifiability, not current discriminating power"; `consciousness-and-causal-powers` answers the degenerating-research-programme charge by conceding that per-trial discrimination is forbidden *by construction*. The Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist converge across this wing rather than conflicting, which by this skill's own criterion means the tension has been resolved honestly.

Two verified defects, both invisible from any single-article view and both of the "a position moved and stranded its dependents" shape. **First**, `concepts/psychophysical-laws` L116 states that the Map "specifies attention as the mental variable and quantum Zeno maintenance as **the** mechanism" — contradicting P-Q4 (Zeno is *demoted*), P-Q1 (post-decoherence selection is the strongest candidate), and its own L149 thirty-three lines later ("the post-decoherence outcome selection the Map endorses"). **Second**, `concepts/causal-powers` is the corpus's sole holdout asserting the Map "favours powers realism" over powerful qualities, while five other articles — including the flagship it reciprocally links to — treat powerful qualities as the Map's adopted property-side commitment.

One structural observation worth more than either: **seven of the eight articles carry no reference to the positions register**, and the eighth, `concepts/type-token-causation`, shows exactly what the other seven are missing.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers is the wing's principal interlocutor and it treats him with the rare combination of deference and disagreement. `psychophysical-laws-bridging-mind-and-matter` states his three bridging principles accurately and at his own level of care — including the point most summaries drop, that Chalmers *explicitly leaves the scope of double-aspect information open* ("does *all* information have a phenomenal aspect, or only some?"). It then locates the divergence precisely: not on irreducibility, which both accept, but on direction. Chalmers' laws are supervenience laws running upward; the Map needs causal laws running downward.

The sharpest Chalmers moment is the supervenience-vs-selection table in `concepts/psychophysical-laws`, which sets Direction / Input / Output / Compatible-with-epiphenomenalism / Developed-by side by side. The final row is the one that earns the praise: it lists Chalmers under supervenience laws with "(extensively)" and Stapp and Eccles under selection laws with "(partially)". The Map does not pretend its own side of the table is as developed as his.

Chalmers would also recognise, and approve of, the article's handling of his own quantum turn. `bridging` §"The Super-Resistance Principle" reports Chalmers and McQueen's proposal as "a research program worth exploring" rather than an endorsed position, and records that they retain sympathy for Many-Worlds — a fact that cuts against the Map's Tenet 4 and is reported anyway.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp gets the most detailed treatment of any candidate mechanism in the wing, and `the-psychophysical-control-law` does something none of the others attempt: it converts his proposal into an actual **control-law form**. "Observation frequency = f(attention intensity), where f is monotonically increasing" is identified as a *proportional controller*, with "the gain parameter — how much observation rate changes per unit of attention — would be the fundamental psychophysical constant in Stapp's framework."

That is the right kind of respect. It takes a proposal usually discussed as a metaphysical stance and asks what its equation would look like, then immediately flags that "the law's proportionality assumption is also just that — an assumption. The true relationship might be nonlinear, threshold-dependent, or state-dependent."

Stapp would be less pleased by, but ought to concede, the same article's arithmetic: Tegmark's decoherence times of 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻²⁰ seconds imply "observation rates of roughly 10¹³ to 10²⁰ Hz across that range... a physically implausible demand for a biological system." The article shows the working rather than gesturing at "the decoherence objection."

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The wing's Nagel contribution is a structural one, easy to miss: **first-person experience is treated as a constraint on the law, not as an output of it.** `the-psychophysical-control-law` lists "Phenomenological consistency" among the five constraints any control law must satisfy — "If the law says doubling effort doubles physical effect, then harder tasks should feel twice as effortful as slightly hard ones. If the law implies a threshold, there should be a phenomenological correlate of crossing it. First-person experience provides an independent check on the law's form."

Nagel's complaint about objective science is that it discards the point of view it cannot represent. Here the point of view is retained as a *test*: a proposed law can fail by contradicting how effort feels. The companion §"Conceptual Asymmetry" then states the cost of that with no softening — "Phenomenal variables — how much attention, how intense the effort — have none of these" (units, standardised instruments, intersubjective verification).

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

`consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions` is the most Whitehead-friendly article in the corpus, and it earns the description without the usual costs. Its adopted position — the powerful-qualities identity theory, where a property's qualitative nature and its dispositional power are the same thing — is precisely the move that avoids crude substance dualism while keeping mind real. The closing line is unabashedly process-philosophical: "we know what it is like to be a powerful quality because we *are* powerful qualities."

The persona constraint requires checking whether that resonance is being used as an engine of evidential upgrade. **It is not.** The sentence is explicitly conditioned ("If the Map's commitment to the reality of phenomenal character holds"), and the article's central elimination argument disclaims empirical force in its own text (quoted under Birch below). The article also refuses the nearest available Whiteheadian shortcut: it declines Russellian power-monism — its closest competitor, correctly identified as Mørch's pairing of powerful qualities with monism — on the ground that power-monism "still inherits the combination problem." Experience-all-the-way-down is *rejected* here, on argument, by the article most sympathetic to it.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`consciousness-and-causal-powers` §"For Free Will" gives Kane exactly the structure he wants and then hands him his own hardest problem unresolved. The trilemma of selection is stated cleanly — at an indeterminate decision point the options are determinism, randomness, or mental causation, and "only genuine causal powers deliver the third."

What earns the praise is the next sentence: "The [luck objection](/concepts/quantum-indeterminacy-free-will/) cuts against constrained and unconstrained powers alike: whatever consciousness does at the gap, what grounds the specific bias remains live." Kane's own event-causal libertarianism has spent thirty years on the luck objection. The Map anchors selection in phenomenal contents (values, desires, deliberations) rather than in uncaused arbitrary choice — Kane's own strategy — and then states that "whether those contents need further grounding is left open" rather than declaring the problem solved.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The wing's best McGinn passage is one where the Map *declines* his position, for a reason he should respect. `the-psychophysical-control-law` acknowledges cognitive closure as a logical possibility and then refuses to lean on it: "Cognitive closure functions as an unfalsifiable exit: if the law cannot be specified, that becomes evidence of closure rather than evidence against the framework. A research programme that reserves the right to invoke cognitive closure whenever progress stalls has immunised itself against failure."

This is intellectual humility of the load-bearing kind — humility about the framework's own standing rather than humility deployed to protect it. The article commits instead to the harder diagnosis (scientific immaturity plus conceptual asymmetry) which can be shown wrong by continued failure, and which the Galileo comparison in §"Scientific Immaturity" makes concrete: "Before Galileo's systematic experiments, 'heavier objects fall faster' was the best available law of motion: intuitively plausible, qualitatively correct in everyday experience, and quantitatively wrong."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This wing is the strongest evidential-discipline performance I have read in the corpus, and the verdict is load-bearing because every article here is tenet-load-bearing by construction. Four independent instances of registers being kept separate:

1. **`laws-and-dispositions` §"Dispositions, Consciousness, and the Interface"** builds a cross-product argument that only governing-laws-paired-with-powerful-qualities survives all four eliminations — and then disclaims its force in the same paragraph: *"This elimination turns on internal coherence and the avoidance of epiphenomenalism, not on empirical adequacy: by the article's own lights the powerful-qualities and the inert-categorical readings are empirically indistinguishable, since both predict the same physical events. The argument here is that only one of them is metaphysically cogent, not that observation could decide between them."* An article that has just won a nine-cell elimination tournament tells you the win is not an empirical one.

2. **`the-psychophysical-control-law` §"Falsifiability"** refuses to bank the word: *"This is in-principle falsifiability, not current discriminating power... The framework's empirical content is therefore deferred and conditional — a promissory note that becomes a live test only once the measurement framework the Conceptual Asymmetry section calls for exists."* §"Why Specification Matters" repeats the discipline rather than relying on the earlier hedge: *"the law specifies what would count as falsifying evidence without yet making that evidence obtainable."* Two statements of the same limit, ten sections apart, is what prevents a downstream reader from harvesting only the optimistic half.

3. **`bridging` §"The Specification Problem"** distinguishes the two things most frameworks conflate: *"Developed proposals like Stapp's generate predictions that are testable in principle — the framework is not inherently unfalsifiable. But honesty requires acknowledging that no discriminating test currently exists... The gap between 'testable in principle' and 'currently testable' is real."*

4. **`consciousness-and-causal-powers`** does the discipline three separate ways. §"Architecture and Implementation Candidates" separates an architecture-level commitment (Phenomenal Biasing) from implementation candidates that may individually fail, then states plainly: *"These mechanisms are hypothesised, not empirically established... The case for the Map's reading rests on framework coherence and phenomenological fit."* §"Introspective Evidence" **discounts the wing's own most congenial evidence**, citing Nisbett and Wilson (1977) on confabulation and Wegner (2002) on the dissociation of felt will from causal involvement, and demoting the phenomenology to "suggestive but unweighty." And §"Relation to Site Perspective" answers the Popperian degenerating-programme charge by conceding its premise: the framework "forbids per-trial discrimination by design — a structural feature of strict Tenet 2 plus no-signalling, not a refinement introduced to evade falsification," then names the condition under which it would inherit the problem anyway.

**Tenet-as-evidence-upgrade, praise-worthily not done.** The clearest instance is `bridging` §"Relation to Site Perspective" under Bidirectional Interaction. The article has the structural opportunity to convert Tenet 3 into support for the mechanisms and declines: after the Newton analogy ("the absence of a vindicated mechanism does not refute the phenomenological evidence any more than the absence of a mechanism for gravity refuted falling objects before Newton") it immediately adds *"But honesty requires acknowledging that the tenet's empirical credibility depends on whether future work can achieve mechanistic specificity."* The analogy is allowed to remove a defeater; it is not allowed to supply evidence. This is the *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating* pattern executed correctly, and the wing runs it consistently enough that it reads as a house style rather than an accident.

**The one article that also prices the debt in the register.** `concepts/type-token-causation` L144 is the wing's template: *"Until that debt is paid, the selection thesis is citable as a framework-internal coherence result, not as established mental causation ([mechanism debt](/positions/quantum-interface/#mechanism-debt))."* It states the coherence-vs-evidence distinction *and* points at the register entry that carries the band. The other seven articles do the first half only.

## Content Strengths

### `obsidian/concepts/anomalous-monism-and-the-denial-of-strict-psychophysical-laws.md`

- **Strongest point**: It identifies the Map's real physicalist opponent correctly. Anomalous monism agrees with the Map that strict *reductive* bridge laws fail, and splits on the verdict — Davidson takes the no-law exit, the Map takes the minimal-law exit. Framing the rivalry as a *shared premise with divergent conclusions* is far more useful than the usual dualism-vs-physicalism setup.
- **Notable quote**: "Anomalous monism is thus a rival ontology, not a friend wearing different clothes."
- **Why it works**: The epiphenomenalism objection is used as a lever rather than a scoreboard. Honderich's green-and-French pears and Sosa's loud-and-lethal gunshot are deployed to show that causal relevance tracks *nomic* properties — which is exactly why the Map wants a genuine law rather than bare token-identity. The article then reports Davidson's reply ("Thinking Causes," 1993) in its strongest form, that events cause simpliciter and property-relative causation is a category error, and concedes it is "powerful" before recording why critics find it changes the subject. Best-of-both-sides handling of a view the article is arguing against.

### `obsidian/topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions.md`

- **Strongest point**: The insistence that the laws axis and the properties axis are not separable, and that the Map's interface model needs a *specific cell* of the cross-product. The elimination is genuinely four-cornered: pure dispositional essentialism fails on the zombie-world disanalogy (no fundamentality, so no internal anchor); governing-laws-with-categoricalism fails because "the 'local' in 'local prescription' requires a property at the site that is itself causally contributing, not merely a categorical placeholder"; pandispositionalism fails on the Mary point; Humeanism supplies summary rather than prescription.
- **Why it works**: It converts a metaphysics-seminar topic into a structural requirement on the Map's own interface, and then says which half of the pairing does which job — the governing view contributes *nomic necessity*, powerful qualities contributes *phenomenal causation*. §"The Dispositional Modality Problem" then discharges the one role the argument had left dangling, using pain asymbolia to show that "pain" is a *compound* of two distinct powerful qualities, so the felt quality present at a site selects its own manifestation. That is a real piece of philosophical work, not a restatement.

### `obsidian/topics/the-psychophysical-control-law.md`

- **Strongest point**: It is the wing's honest accounting article, and the honesty is structural rather than decorative. It opens by conceding that "commitment alone does not constitute specification," diagnoses the gap on three axes (immaturity, conceptual asymmetry, the t-shirt problem), refuses the mysterian exit, and closes with three concrete developments that would narrow the space.
- **Why it works**: Every candidate control law is given a *form* — proportional controller, probability-reweighting controller, switching controller — so that the differences between Stapp, Eccles and policy-selection become differences in control architecture rather than in rhetoric. The switching-controller analysis is the sharpest: policy selection has minimal bandwidth requirements and fits Libet, but "pushes the hard question backward," because "consciousness selects policy A" still needs a micro-level story.

### `obsidian/topics/consciousness-and-causal-powers.md` (`ai_modified` today)

- **Strongest point**: The architecture/implementation split, which is a genuine methodological advance for the wing. Committing to Phenomenal Biasing at the architecture level while holding Selection of Action, Sustained Attention and Volitional Modulation as *candidate implementations* means the twelve-to-sixteen-orders-of-magnitude decoherence gap becomes "a constraint on the Zeno *candidate*, not on the architecture."
- **Why it works**: It lets the article state the quantitative objection at full strength — "attention runs at 10–30 Hz. The mechanism would need observations at ≥10¹³ Hz" — without the framework collapsing, and without the move looking like an escape hatch, because the architecture-level claim is stated *first* and independently. §"What Consciousness Does Not Do" is the other half of the same discipline: five explicit exclusions (no action at a distance, no energy creation, no macroscopic violations, no guaranteed success, no retroactive influence) that mark where the framework would be refuted by its own commitments.

### `obsidian/concepts/psychophysical-laws.md`

- **Strongest point**: The structural taxonomy at L92, which is the wing's most compressed piece of synthesis: causal laws divide into *trajectory-altering* and *authority-selecting* modes, the latter modelled on Schaffer's trumping preemption via `delegatory-causation`; the trajectory-altering mode then subdivides by Born-compliance into corridor dualism, minimum-outside-the-corridor dualism, and trumping dualism. Naming the adopted default (corridor dualism) while keeping the alternatives visible as a taxonomy is exactly right — and it matters structurally, since P-Q1's moderate band is held *partly because* the Map keeps the minimum-outside-corridor route open.
- **Why it works**: The comparative coupling table (attention / intention / valence / qualia / unity, each with development level and main objection) ranks the Map's own preferred family as most-developed *and* most-challenged in the same row, and rates three of the five families "Low" or "Very low" with "No quantum mechanism" and "Purely speculative" written into the table. Self-scoring that visibly is uncommon.

### C1 — `concepts/psychophysical-laws` L116 names quantum Zeno as *the* Map mechanism, contradicting the register and its own L149

> **The Selection Mechanism**: Where Chalmers leaves the mechanism abstract, the Map (following [Stapp](/concepts/stapp-quantum-mind/)) specifies attention as the mental variable and quantum Zeno maintenance as the mechanism—affecting which quantum possibilities become actual without injecting energy.

Three independent contradictions, each checked at source:

- **P-Q1** is titled "Post-decoherence selection is the strongest current mechanism candidate."
- **The same article, L149**, thirty-three lines later: "The **post-decoherence outcome selection the Map endorses** preserves Born statistics." The same file names two different Map mechanisms.

Propagation sibling, verified: `obsidian/research/chalmers-psychophysical-laws-2026-01-17.md` **L146** carries the identical sentence in un-wikilinked form ("The Map (following Stapp) specifies attention as the mental variable and quantum Zeno maintenance as the mechanism"), and is the apparent origin. Both have Hugo mirrors (`hugo/content/concepts/psychophysical-laws.md` L120, `hugo/content/research/chalmers-psychophysical-laws-2026-01-17.md` L148).

**⚠️ Relationship to the existing open P3 on [topics/psychophysical-laws-bridging-mind-and-matter.md](/topics/psychophysical-laws-bridging-mind-and-matter/)** (the "Family V" / `prefers [A-Za-z ]{0,12}Stapp` task): this is the **same defect family on different files with a different string**. ### C2 — `concepts/causal-powers` is a stranded property-side commitment

`obsidian/concepts/causal-powers.md` **L41** states, of the powerful-qualities identity theory: *"While this view has appeal, The Unfinishable Map favours powers realism"* — and reinforces it at L75 ("This aligns with powers realism rather than Humean or categoricalist accounts: phenomenal states... are dispositional properties with intrinsic capacities"), L81 and L85.

A corpus-wide grep for `powerful qualit` and `powers realism` finds `causal-powers` is the **sole** article asserting that preference. Five others treat powerful qualities as the Map's adopted position:

- `topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions` L126: "**The Map adopts powerful qualities as the property-side commitment**"; L170 names it "the property-side ally."
- `topics/consciousness-and-probability-interpretation` L87: "**on the Map's property metaphysics**... phenomenal properties are *powerful qualities*."
- `concepts/russellian-monism` L97 and L150, and `topics/russellian-monism-versus-bi-aspectual-dualism` L157, both build on the powerful-qualities framing.

The two articles link to each other reciprocally, so a reader arriving from either lands on the opposite verdict. The chronology fits the stranding shape: `causal-powers` `ai_modified` 2026-07-06, `laws-and-dispositions` deep-reviewed 2026-07-14.

**The fix is a re-labelling, not an argument change**, and this should be stated in the task so nobody rewrites the philosophy. `causal-powers` L85's argument — that powers realism "rules out epiphenomenalism as a coherent option" because "to *be* a phenomenal property is to *have* certain causal capacities" — is delivered equally by powerful qualities; `laws-and-dispositions` L144 runs the same anti-epiphenomenalism argument for the powerful-qualities cell explicitly. One nuance to preserve honestly: the two are not contradictory *in extension* — powerful qualities satisfies L41's own definition of powers realism ("at least some fundamental properties are dispositional") — so what must go is the **contrastive preference sentence**, not the powers vocabulary. There is a residual substantive point too: L75's "phenomenal states... are dispositional properties" sits closer to the pandispositionalist horn that `laws-and-dispositions` rejects via the Mary point, so the identity formulation should replace it rather than sit beside it.

`causal-powers` is 1800 words against a 2500 soft threshold — **700 words of headroom**, the most in the wing — so this correction is affordable in place.
