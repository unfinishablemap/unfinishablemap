## Executive Summary

The sourcing is sound. Every quoted string I could reach is verbatim at source: Hume ×5, Rolfs et al. ×3, Scholl & Nakayama, Choi & Scholl, and Kominsky & Scholl. The article also discloses its secondary-source dependencies honestly (Michotte, Anscombe's verb list, Biran via one abstract).

The weaknesses are in the argument, in four places.

1. **The perceptual floor proves too much.** Adaptation, capture and postdiction are general signatures of perception. Choi and Scholl's own abstract says postdiction was "previously demonstrated for low-level motion phenomena", and nobody concludes from that that motion is never perceived. Every psychophysics source the article quotes frames its result as showing that causality *is* perceived, and so does Siegel (2009), whom L57 says the void "leaves intact". The lede's "We never perceive causation" therefore overstates what the body defends. The body's defensible thesis, at L57, is that experience never shows *what causation consists in*.
2. **The introspective floor is spared the test the perceptual floor gets.** The Michotte stamp is put through four modern dissociations. The felt power of the will gets only Hume's 1748 paralytic, and Biranian effort then survives as the "strongest candidate" and the Dualism tenet's lead. The modern paralytic exists, and it comes with a mechanism. With the hand paralysed and anaesthetised, attempting to move it shifts its felt position by more than 20°, and the shift grows with effort (Gandevia et al. 2006). The research note listed "Wegner-style I-spy paradigms" for this floor and flagged intentional binding as unsurveyed. The article dropped both.
3. **The parity argument defeats an objection nobody is cited as holding.** No one is named for the view that mental causation is mysterious "because we never observe it". The historically live intuitive objection is Elisabeth's heterogeneity worry, and the Map's own history page covers it. That page is not linked. Hume's parity is also double-edged, and the article never says so: Hume used it to conclude that motion "may be, and actually is, the cause of thought and perception" (*Treatise* 1.4.5.30).
4. **The Occam and Minimal Quantum Interaction paragraphs slide from a limit of access to a claim about the world.** L103 presupposes structure beyond regularity. L107 calls the Map's selection "less exotic" because physics has no push anyway. But the Map's own `selection-only-channel` L76 says that on the strong reading the channel "stays invisible to any third-person test". So the void's own approach of last resort, the interventionist proxy at L91, cannot reach the Map's proposal. That is the cost, and L107 states it backwards.

There is also an unflagged cross-page divergence. `topics/phenomenology-of-resistance-across-domains` L116 takes the Biranian datum as "phenomenological evidence for bidirectional causation". This void treats the same datum as a speculative lead that the paralytic blocks (L65, L105). Neither page links the other. The disagreement belongs under the open NEEDS-HUMAN Tenet 3 entry (todo L659, which already holds the felt-effort loci under K17), not in a new task.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

"Read your own L51: '*Seeing causation*, on the best current evidence, is one or more direction-tuned detectors firing on kinematic templates.' That is the neuroscience *succeeding*. You have a retinotopic, direction-selective, adaptable routine, which is about as complete a story as vision science has for anything. You call what is left over a void. But the leftover is the folk-metaphysical 'secret connexion', which no neuroscience was ever going to deliver, because it is not a thing. Your phenomenology at L95 says the push 'thins under scrutiny, as a repeated word thins into sound'. That is semantic satiation and adaptation, a neural effect you have just described in first person. The introspective floor goes the same way. Felt effort is a centrally generated command signal: it rises when curare weakens the muscle (Gandevia & McCloskey 1977) and it drives felt movement in a paralysed hand (Gandevia et al. 2006). What the article catalogues is the folk concept of causal power coming apart under measurement. That is what elimination looks like before the vocabulary changes."

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

"Hume told you where the impression comes from. The mind spreads itself on objects: the felt necessity is a projection from expectation, not a perception that falls short of a hidden relation. You have taken his projectivism and turned it back into a realism with a hole in it. You posit a 'relation it purports to show' (L37), and the stamp then fails to show it. But the stamp's content *is* the projection. There is no further relation being withheld.

"On the will, the comparator model predicts your paralytic outright. The efference copy goes out whether or not the limb answers, and the predicted consequences are what is felt. Moore and Haggard (2008) show the experience of action mixing prediction and retrospective inference. When the outcome is highly predictable, binding occurs 'even on trials where the action produced no effect'. That is Hume's paralytic as a timing measurement. Biran's 'relation of will to resistance' is the system's model of its own command. You may call that privileged access to causation if you like, but it is privileged access to a signal.

"Your parity point is one I accept, and I would ask you to accept its other half. If we never see anything push anything, then 'nobody can see how neurons could produce experience' is no objection to production either. Hume drew that conclusion himself, nine years before the *Enquiry*."

### The Quantum Skeptic

"L107 says that if physics contains no push, your biased selection 'asks for a kind of influence that no causal picture displays anyway, which makes it less exotic than it looks.' Nobody ever found your proposal exotic because it lacks a push. It is exotic for three reasons. It adds a variable outside the physical state space to a dynamics whose functional laws, the Schrödinger evolution plus the Born rule, are complete over that space. It must act inside decoherence times that I estimated at 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻²⁰ seconds in warm neural tissue. And on the strong reading your own channel page adopts, it is built to leave no third-person trace. Russell makes this worse, not better. If physics *is* functional dependency among physical magnitudes, then a dependency on a non-physical magnitude that appears in no equation is the most exotic thing on offer. You wrote 'That is a point about strangeness, not support.' It is not even a good point about strangeness."

### The Many-Worlds Defender

"The theoretical floor rests on Russell in 1913. That was before relativity's light-cone structure was absorbed into field theory, before microcausality, and before retarded propagators did the causal bookkeeping of every scattering calculation. Physics *does* contain causal structure. Frisch (2014) argues at book length that causal reasoning is ineliminable from it, and Cartwright (1979) argued that causal laws cannot be reduced to laws of association. Deutsch and Marletto's constructor theory goes further and puts counterfactuals, what transformations are possible and impossible, at the foundation. Your heading 'No Replacement From Physics' asserts what your body only conditionally entertains ('If that is right…').

"Note too what L107 conceals. Under unitary quantum mechanics there is no collapse for consciousness to bias. 'No push in physics' is common ground, but your proposal needs a *collapse* in physics, and that is the extra dynamical posit. Talk of pushes hides it."

### The Empiricist

"The core of this void is defined so that nothing could count against it. 'Every channel we have delivers content *about* a relation and never the relation' (L39) is true of every property under any representational theory of mind, so it cannot single out causation. L71 is admirably modest ('consistent with a genuine limit, though not proof of one'), but nowhere does the article say what would show the limit is *not* genuine. The catalogue knows how to do this: `voids/handedness-void` is the void whose boundary moved.

"There is a Biranian test, and it has been run. If felt effort were acquaintance with the will's causal relation to the limb, it should track what the effort *achieves*. It tracks what is *sent*. Felt heaviness rises when curare weakens the muscle (Gandevia & McCloskey 1977), and felt displacement occurs in a hand that cannot move (Gandevia et al. 2006). That does not refute Biran. A Biranian can say the felt relation is to the motor command and not to the limb, which is L65's own relocation. But it is the shape of evidence your Dualism paragraph needs to face, and it is absent."

### The Buddhist Philosopher

"The first chapter of my *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* examines conditions. Nothing arises from itself, from another, from both, or from neither. I did not conclude that the causal relation is hidden on a 'far side' (L37). I concluded that it has no own-being to be hidden. Your void reifies what it fails to find. It assumes there is a 'secret connexion' and then laments our exclusion from it. Dependent arising is conventionally real and ultimately empty, and there is no remainder for experience to miss.

"Your Biranian thread reifies twice over. 'The power of the will' presupposes a self that holds power. Volition (*cetanā*) is one conditioned factor among others, not an owner exerting force. Hume's paralytic is not a puzzle about where the power went. It shows that there was never a power-holder there to begin with. The Map's own `concepts/buddhism-and-dualism` page would have given you a fourth option beside Hume, Biran and Russell: not inaccessible, but empty."

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The perceptual floor's evidence proves too much, and the lede asserts what the body concedes away (interpretive equivocation)
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/causal-impression-void.md`
- **Location**: L37 ("We never perceive causation"), L39 ("every channel we have delivers content *about* a relation and never the relation"), L45 ("Four findings show the stamp coming apart from anything it could be a sighting of"), L57 (the reply to Anscombe), and the description ("why causation itself never appears in experience").
- **Problem**: the misfire profile (adaptation, contextual capture, postdiction) is a general signature of perception. Motion has aftereffects. Postdiction was "previously demonstrated for low-level motion phenomena", in Choi & Scholl's own abstract, the one the article quotes at L49. The L57 inference, that a seeing which can be "produced by capture, reversed by adaptation and settled by postdiction … cannot simply be awareness of the relation", is the argument from illusion. Applied evenly, it would show that motion and colour are never perceived either. The article also inverts its sources' framing without flagging it. Scholl & Nakayama ("observers can perceive higher-level properties … One such property is causation"), Choi & Scholl ("we can easily perceive not only motion, but also higher-level properties such as causality"), Kominsky & Scholl ("We can perceive not only low-level features … but also seemingly higher-level properties such as causality") and Rolfs et al. (perceptual, against cognitive) all read their results as showing that causality *is* perceived. At L57 the article itself concedes that Siegel may be right that causation is in the content of experience, and says its reply "leaves Siegel intact". That holds for only half of Siegel's paper. Her 2009 abstract (OpenAlex, checked today) separates the representation thesis from the perception thesis and defends both: "I defend the claim that we can perceive causation". The article reports only the first, and the second is the direct contrary of its lede. On a representationalist view, fallible but veridical causal content just *is* perceiving causation, as fallible motion content is perceiving motion. The thesis the body can defend is L57's second clause: experience "says nothing about what the relation consists in". The lede, the L39 classification and the description assert the stronger claim, that causation never appears in experience. The anchoring audit will not catch this, because the hedges are present and the claim is still the wrong one. The knife-and-bread example at L57 adds a second, smaller overreach: no study tested knife and bread, and the findings are all on 2D disc animations.
- **Severity**: High. This is the opening sentence, the truncation-surface claim, and the voids-register entry (`voids.md` L305 repeats "Causation itself never appears in experience").
- **Recommendation**: state the thesis as the nature claim throughout the front-loaded surfaces. Say once, in L57, that the misfires show fallibility and template construction, and that the void rests on the nature clause instead. Replace "the seeing of the knife cutting the bread" with "a seen launch". Priced in Priority 1. The `voids.md` L305 register line is a navigation surface that will keep asserting the old claim. Fix it in the same pass, length-neutrally: "Causation itself never appears" → "What causation consists in never appears".

### Issue 2: The introspective floor is spared the dissociation test, and the Dualism lead rests on the spared floor
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/causal-impression-void.md`
- **Location**: L61–L65 (the introspective floor), L87 ("No direct approach is available, with one contested exception: Biranian effort"), L95 (the Map's own agency phenomenology), L105 (the Dualism tenet paragraph).
- **Problem**: the perceptual stamp is met with four modern dissociations. The felt power of the will is met with one 1748 thought experiment. Biranian effort is then promoted as "the strongest candidate for a route into the void" and as the Dualism tenet's only lead. The modern record is directly on point and goes unmentioned:
  - **Gandevia et al. 2006** (*J Physiol* 571(3):703–710, doi 10.1113/jphysiol.2005.103093). This is Hume's paralytic in the laboratory. With the arm paralysed and anaesthetised by ischaemia, attempted movements shifted the hand's perceived position by "more than 20 deg", and "the size of the position illusion increased when the level of effort during paralysis increased". The felt result of willing is produced by the outgoing command, with no movement and no resistance overcome.
  - **Gandevia & McCloskey 1977** (*J Physiol* 272(3):673–689). Felt effort, measured as perceived heaviness, rises when curare weakens the muscle. It tracks the "centrally generated 'effort' or direct voluntary command", not the outcome.
  - **Moore & Haggard 2008** (*Conscious Cogn* 17(1):136–144). The experience of action combines prediction with retrospective inference, and when predictability is high, binding occurs "even on trials where the action produced no effect". This is the agentive twin of Choi & Scholl's postdiction.
  - The Map already carries **Desmurget et al. 2009** at `phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity` L119: parietal stimulation produces felt intention and even a false belief of having moved.

  The research note's dissociation-mapping list (note L162) included "Wegner-style I-spy paradigms", and its Gaps section flagged intentional binding as unsurveyed. Both disappeared in the article. The asymmetry favours the dualist-friendly lead. None of this refutes Biran: a Biranian can say the lived relation is to the motor command, which is L65's own relocation. But it changes what the relocation *costs*. The relocation lands on an outflow signal with a physiological description, so "consciousness epistemically privileged with respect to causation" (L105) needs an argument that acquaintance with one's own command is acquaintance with *causation* and not with a signal. There is also an internal tension. L95's own first-person report ("effort and readiness but no passage of influence from will to limb … nothing that shows up as an item") is the Humean verdict, given in the Map's voice, against the lead that L65 and L105 keep open.
- **Severity**: High (asymmetric evidence handling on the article's only Dualism connection).
- **Recommendation**: add the modern paralytic (Gandevia et al. 2006) after the Hume palsy quote. Rewrite L65's relocation clause to say where the relocation lands. Make L95 mark its report as the Humean one. Note in L105 that the paralytic is now measured. Priced in Priority 2.

### Issue 3: The parity targets an unsourced objection, misses the live one, and is not marked as double-edged
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/causal-impression-void.md`
- **Location**: L39 ("The epiphenomenalist intuition that mental causation is mysterious because we never see the mind push anything proves too much") and L99 ("Pressure against mental causation includes an intuition … that consciousness causing anything is peculiarly mysterious because we never observe it doing so").
- **Problem**: no holder is cited, and "the epiphenomenalist intuition" is an odd ascription. Epiphenomenalists such as Jackson deny mental-to-physical efficacy on closure and explanatory grounds, not on observational ones. The intuitive objection with a pedigree is Elisabeth's 1643 heterogeneity challenge (how can an unextended soul move the bodily spirits?), and Kim formalised it as the pairing problem. Both are on the Map at `topics/history-of-the-interaction-problem` L55–L65 and L108, which this void does not link. Hume's parity has real purchase on Elisabeth's challenge: if we grasp no body-to-body power, the unlikeness of mind and body is no special bar. Hume makes that very move in *Treatise* 1.4.5.30 ("any thing may produce any thing"). But in the same paragraph Hume concludes that "motion may be, and actually is, the cause of thought and perception". So the parity also disarms any complaint that brains cannot *intelligibly* cause experience. The Map can live with that, since its anti-physicalist arguments are entailment-gap arguments, not causal-opacity arguments. But the article must say so, or it reads as a one-way weapon.
- **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: name the target as the Elisabeth intuition, with a piped link to the history page at zero word cost. Add one clause to L101's "what this buys" paragraph marking the symmetry, citing *Treatise* 1.4.5.30. Priced in Priority 3.

## Counterarguments to Address

### Fallibility is not non-acquaintance
- **Current content says**: because the causal impression can be captured, adapted and postdicted, it cannot be awareness of the relation (L57).
- **A critic would argue**: the same three signatures hold for motion, which is perceived. Naive realists and disjunctivists deny that illusions show veridical cases to be non-perceptual. Butterfill (2009, *Phil Q* 59(236):405–428, the same issue as Siegel 2009) defends a perceptual account of seeing causings that the misfires do not touch.
- **Suggested response**: concede that the misfires show fallibility and construction. Rest the void on the nature claim, which no misfire argument is needed for: even veridical causal content says nothing about what the relation consists in.

### The modern paralytic has a mechanism
- **Current content says**: the paralytic is the "standing objection" to Biran (L65), and the Biranian route is the one contested exception (L87).
- **A critic would argue**: the paralytic is now an outflow-signal result (Gandevia et al. 2006) with a comparator-model explanation. Felt effort tracks the command, not the achievement, so the "relation of will to resistance" is a relation to one's own motor signal.
- **Suggested response**: accept the relocation explicitly. Then argue, or leave open with a stated cost, whether acquaintance with one's own command is acquaintance with causation. That is the honest form of the lead.

### Parity cuts both ways
- **Current content says**: the intuition that mental causation is peculiarly mysterious proves too much (L39, L99).
- **A critic would argue**: then so does every complaint that physical processes cannot intelligibly *cause* experience, and Hume concluded exactly that matter causes thought (*Treatise* 1.4.5.30).
- **Suggested response**: one clause saying the parity is symmetric. The Map's case against physicalism runs through what physical facts fail to *entail*, not through causal opacity, so the symmetry costs it nothing it relies on.

### Physics does contain causal structure
- **Current content says**: Russell (1913), physics has no causes; heading "No Replacement From Physics".
- **A critic would argue**: Frisch (2014, *Causal Reasoning in Physics*, CUP) and Cartwright (1979, *Noûs* 13(4)) argue that causal notions are ineliminable. Microcausality and retarded propagators encode causal order.
- **Suggested response**: one clause noting that Russell's eliminativism is contested. Better still, observe that even physics' causal structure (order, propagation, no-signalling) is structure, not the "secret connexion", which keeps the void intact on its strongest footing.
