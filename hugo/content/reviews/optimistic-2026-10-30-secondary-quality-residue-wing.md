## Executive Summary

The wing is the Map at its most disciplined about evidence. All three pages state their own evidential ceiling in so many words. The colour page: "Removing a proposed defeater … does not upgrade the residue into evidence for dualism". The boundary page: the identification "is Map-internal synthesis and carries no external evidential weight". The Sellars page: "enlists the *diagnosis* … while declining the *cure*". The opportunity is structural. Read in order, the three pages form an argument none of them states: the line cannot be cleanly drawn; wherever it is drawn, the quality ends up in experience; and once there, its homogeneity meets the neural grain. Three reciprocal edits and one completed criterion test would make that argument visible. The one calibration concern sits on the seam page, where the grain argument is credited with more dualist-specific force than its own author drew from it.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
Chalmers would recognise his own Eden paper used with care. Colour ontology L72 states what the paper does *not* claim: it "declares itself neutral" on phenomenal-versus-representational priority and "even allows a type-B analogue". It then marks the Map's step as the Map's: "the bridge from Chalmers's content thesis to a phenomenal residue is one the Map builds, not one the paper asserts". The boundary page's thesis, "structure described" against "quality undergone" (L35), is the explanatory gap pushed back to the seventeenth century. Chalmers would also value the Sellars page's framing of the grain problem as a "pre-Chalmers line of pressure on physicalism" (L61). It is pressure from an independent source rather than a restatement of the hard problem.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
Stapp would note, with approval, that the wing makes no quantum claims. It sits upstream of the interface. Its subject is how classical physics came to treat the inventory of primary qualities as complete. The Map's quantum pages need exactly that diagnosis before they can reopen the question, and the wing does not borrow their authority to strengthen its own case. Stapp could also point out that Sellars' late "pure-process ontology" (L51) belongs to the same family as the process readings of quantum events he favoured. The page does not draw that link, which is right: it would be resonance, not evidence.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
Nagel's *View from Nowhere* runs the same diagnosis: the objective conception was built by setting aside how things appear, so it cannot be complete by construction. Colour ontology's single question to each family — "once the theory has said what colour is, where has the quality gone?" (L40) — is the Nagelian question posed to a whole literature. Its structure section ends on the line Nagel would most want kept: "Structure being tractable does not make the quality that carries the structure tractable" (L78).

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
Whitehead named this problem the "bifurcation of nature". He would praise the boundary page's finding that the cut "admits no clean criterion" and the diagnosis that the clean two-category world is "simplicity through stipulation" (L87). He would value Sellars' pure-process endpoint even more. **His praise stops at the colour sorting.** `process-philosophy` L124 records that his critique of bifurcation refuses "any theory of psychic additions". The colour page's result, that four of five ontologies deposit the quality *in experience*, is an address he would reject, because it re-bifurcates nature at a new place. The wing does not hide this: colour ontology L92 grants that "Nothing in the colour literature discriminates" among the dualist, physicalist and primitivist readings. Whitehead-friendliness here is praise for the critique, not for the Map's choice of address.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
Kane's interest is narrow here. The Sellars page keeps "the person and the space of reasons" as things that "cannot be dropped" (L65) and points to `argument-from-reason` for rational causation. A manifest person who acts for reasons is the agent Kane needs. The wing rightly leaves agency to the pages that argue it.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)
McGinn appears in the wing in person, as a convert to colour primitivism (colour ontology L60: "McGinn (1996, a convert from his own earlier dispositionalism)"). He would admire how often the wing says what it has not done. The boundary page: "it leaves the Map owing an account of its own individuator that it does not have" (L77). The colour page: "These are costs rather than refutations; the dispute is open" (L84). The Sellars page: "a scientific realist whose best problems outrun his own solution" (L63). All three are calibrated ignorance, and none is false modesty.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)
For this wing Birch's verdict decides the matter, and on the three target pages it is strongly favourable. Each page had a structural opening to promote its finding into evidence for dualism, and each declined it in its own words:
- **Colour ontology** L36: "The sorting is compatible with the Map's first tenet and offers no discriminator in its favour". L92 states the persona's named pattern almost verbatim: removing a defeater "restores the standing of a question" and does not upgrade it. The 09-30 Stability Notes fix the tier at **compatible**.
- **Boundary** L71: the instability "weakens one common argument *against* taking experience as fundamental; it does not establish that experience *is* fundamental". It also separates the attested instability (Berkeley; Bolton 2022; Maund 2024) from the Map's own identification of that instability with the gap.
- **Sellars** L63: "enlists the *diagnosis* … while declining the *cure*". Presenting Sellars as a dualist ally "would misattribute a commitment he explicitly refused."

**Calibration concern (not praise): the seam page.** Here the two personas conflict. Whitehead praises `grain-mismatch` for finding that quality is fundamental. Birch asks what the grain argument actually selects. Its conclusion is that homogeneous quality is not identical with discrete neural structure. Sellars' *sensa* (primitive sensory items inside an expanded physics), Russellian monism and panpsychism all satisfy that conclusion. So does dualism. The argument removes the identity theorist's defeater. It does not choose dualism over these non-reductive rivals. Yet L81 calls it "one of the Map's most direct routes to dualism", and L83 reads it as "evidence that consciousness possesses its own non-physical properties". Neither sentence notes that the argument's author drew no such conclusion: Sellars' sensa are "physical-1" by his own classification (Sellars page L51). The page's only body nod to a non-physicalist rival is a Further Reading line for `panpsychism` (L96). This is the possibility/probability slippage the persona exists to catch. It has stood since the page was created on 03-11 (`git log -S "most direct routes"`). None of the six grain-mismatch deep-review files on disk (03-11 to 07-18) raises it: a grep of `reviews/*grain-mismatch*.md` for `sensa|own resolution|expanded physics|physical-1` returns nothing. The fact that exposes it sits on the Sellars page, which none of them read beside it. Minted as task 4, as a refine-draft rather than an expansion.

- **Strongest point**: the "does and does not license" section (L67–L79). It confines the instability to a defensive conclusion against one parsimony argument. It then steelmans the rival that escapes even that, ontic structural realism, in its moderate form (Esfeld and Lam 2008). Task 3 makes that answer the result of a test rather than an assertion.

### topics/colour-ontology-and-the-secondary-quality-residue
- **Strongest point**: the objection to its own result from the physicalist theory of experience (L86). The page grants the sorting is right and that it takes no side on the hard problem. What it rules out is the *cheaper* move, "dissolving the residue with a theory of colour alone".
- **Notable quote**: "The colour debate delivers the residue to the doorstep of the theory of experience and stops there; whether it survives physicalist treatment beyond that door is the hard-problem debate, on which the sorting takes no side." (L86)
- **Why it works**: the structure section (L74–L78) turns the Map's own concession, that colour structure is scientifically tractable, into a point against reflectance physicalism, and it does this with witnesses from both sides (Pautz, an eliminativist; Gert, a primitivist). ### concepts/sellars-manifest-and-scientific-images
