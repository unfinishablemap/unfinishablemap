## Source-Reporting Ledger (non-quoted empirical claims)

- **Killgore 2010** (PubMed 21075236): real-correct metadata, **result misreported**. The page said "a consistent order of cognitive loss: tonic alertness… first, then working memory, then executive functions — planning, inhibition…". The abstract reports broad consensus only for alertness, attention and vigilance. It reports "much less agreement" on memory and executive function, says executive tasks give "inconsistent findings", and says "many convergent and rule-based reasoning, decision making and **planning** tasks are relatively unaffected". The page therefore had planning degrading, which reverses the source, and it presented a temporal sequence the source does not report. Seed: fatigue note L41 and L74, a misreading of Alhola & Polo-Kantola 2007 (PMC2656292) "**First and foremost**, total SD impairs attention and working memory, but it also affects other functions", where "first and foremost" means primarily, not first in time. "Tonic alertness" (note L75) is absent from PMC2656292. **Fixed** (E5) to the differential-vulnerability picture. The fix carried through to every dependent sentence: lead, fatigue intro, headings, Levinas alignment, "What the Three Voids Reveal", Classification, Bidirectional tenet line.
- **Internal contradiction (CRITICAL), fixed**: the old Levinas paragraph said "what degrades first represents the highest elaborations of subjectivity". By the page's own account, what degrades first is alertness. It now says the fit is partial.
- **Montupil et al. 2023** (PMC10539891 full text): real-correct for the three-states taxonomy. The preceding sentence, "Low doses produce analgesia and amnesia; deeper agents lose environmental awareness before self-awareness, and self-awareness before internal thought", is **absent** from the paper (0 hits for analgesi/amnesi/drunken; no ordering). Seed: fatigue note L88-89, which attributes it to the same PMC ID. **Sentence cut**: the three-states sentence carries the point.
- **Menon 2023** (PMC10524518 full text): real-correct metadata, **does not support the claim it was attached to**. Zero hits for meditation or psychedelic, and "flow" appears only as blood or information flow. Menon supports "associated with self-referential processing" only. Seed: absorption note L111 and L113 (DMN block whose URL is Menon). **Fixed**: Menon moved to the self-reference clause. Meditation now cites Brewer et al. 2011 (PubMed 22114193: main DMN nodes "relatively deactivated in experienced meditators across all meditation types") and flow cites Ulrich et al. 2014 (PubMed 23959200: "Relative decreases in neural activity were observed in the medial prefrontal cortex", read by the authors as "decreased self-referential processing"). The unsupported "continuum… psychedelic states" sentence was cut. "Genuinely deactivates" / "This is not merely subjective report" (an overclaim and the banned cliché) became a correlate claim with the caveat "a quieter network is not self-monitoring switched off". The summary in "What the Three Voids Reveal" was aligned ("dampened", "corroboration").
- **Brickman, Coates & Janoff-Bulman 1978** (PubMed 690806): metadata real-correct, **result over-extended**. A cross-sectional study (22 winners, 22 controls, 29 paralysed accident victims) was reported as longitudinal "return to baseline", and "paraplegics recover much of theirs" is not in it. The abstract says paraplegics' idealised past "did not help their present happiness". Lucas 2007 (PubMed 17469954) contradicts the general claim "life events produce only temporary changes": disability brought "moderate to large drops in happiness… followed by little adaptation over time". Seed: habituation note L60 (Wikipedia). **Fixed** (E1).
- **Habituation in disorders**: the page's claim that reduced habituation "produce[s]" sensory flooding was unsourced, and neither Rankin 2009 (PMC2754195: no hits for the disorders) nor the note's PMC4288050 supports it. McDiarmid, Bernardos & Rankin 2017 (PubMed 28579490) report abnormal habituation in ASD, schizophrenia and Tourette's, "predictive of symptom severity", and discuss it "as a cause, symptom, or therapy". Seed: habituation note L75, L182. **Fixed** (E3) with the causal direction now conditional.
- **Singer & Klimecki 2014** primer: metadata real-correct (PubMed 25247366), but the body could not be retrieved, and the abstract covers only definitions. The claim it carried, "distinct neural circuits that only selective fatigue exposes as separable", has the method wrong.
