## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

"You say at L84 that phenomenal properties are *defined* by how they appear from the first-person perspective, and then conclude that they have no third-person translation. Of course they don't — you defined them that way. Folk psychology has always defined its posits so as to resist reduction; that is why it will be eliminated rather than reduced. The 'paradox' is an artefact of a vocabulary I decline to use." *Bedrock (per 07-28 stability note); recorded under Counterarguments, not as an issue.*

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

"L146 says consciousness's causal efficacy is 'evidenced by our ability to report on phenomenal states'. Reports are evidence that *something* causes reports. Heterophenomenology grants that and asks what; you have assumed the answer inside a parenthesis. Your own Tenet 3 page runs this as the self-stultification *argument* — an argument, with premises. Here it is a parenthetical fact." *Real: the parenthesis presents the Map's argument as a datum without linking to it. Folded into Issue 3.*

"Also, L70: 'We don't infer other minds from behavior; we perceive mental states directly.' Your own `problem-of-other-minds` page (L86) asks 'Yet what exactly do we perceive? ... I seem to perceive the expression, not the feeling.' Your second-person page says engagement removes the *theory-mediated inference* route but not the *first-person experience of contingency* route. Here the contested direct-perception thesis is stated as fact." *Real but low; Language table.*

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

"L46 illustrates observer-independence with *electrons* — 'electrons behave the same whether observed by humans, instruments, or hypothetical aliens' — and then L122 tells me that at quantum measurement 'the observer's perspective would be part of what determines outcomes.' Pick one. If the electron example is true, the quantum section is false; if the quantum section is your position, do not open by asserting its negation about the very system in question. And L122's 'the measurement problem's persistence suggests objectivity may reach genuine limits' — persistence of an open problem in physics is evidence of a hard problem, not of a limit on objectivity. GRW, Bohm and Everett are all observer-free solutions still on the table; a problem being unsolved does not favour the observer-dependent solution." *Real; Issue 4.*

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

"L148 says Everett 'cannot explain why *this* outcome is experienced rather than all outcomes.' All outcomes *are* experienced, by different continuers; the residual question is indexical and I say it is confused." *Bedrock per stability note (MWI indexical brevity); not re-raised.*

### The Empiricist (Popper's Ghost)

"L50 upgrades an epistemic limit to 'structurally impossible, not merely difficult' by citing constitutive exclusion. But your constitutive-exclusion page says (L67) that its bridge from 'cannot access reality independent of our contribution' to 'our contribution helps constitute reality' is 'the Map's own inference … a coherence claim, not a derivation', and (L57) that Nagel 'limits what objectivity can *capture*, not what it can *reach*'. A coherence claim cannot 'sharpen' anything into an impossibility result. And 'impossible' is exactly the word that makes the thesis unfalsifiable: 'difficult' predicts that better methods yield diminishing returns; 'impossible' predicts nothing." *Real; Issue 1.*

"L84: 'Consciousness provides none of these' — none of units, publicly accessible causal effects, or observer-independent reference points. But your Tenet 2 and Tenet 3 say consciousness biases quantum outcomes and produces reports. Those *are* causal effects, and reports are public. Your measurement-standards page is careful — 'no publicly accessible causal effects *of the right kind*' (L70), and 'We can measure the *effects* of consciousness on the physical world' (L114). You dropped the qualifier and thereby contradicted your own quantum section." *Real; Issue 2.*

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

"'Consciousness *is* perspective' (L42) and 'consciousness is the observer' (L80) reify a standpoint into a thing that then 'resists' description. Perspective is a relation, not a substance; a relation is empty of own-being and there is nothing there to be irreducible. Your Husserl section (L58) — objectivity constituted through convergent perspectives — is closer to my view than your Dualism paragraph is; you might notice the tension." *Bedrock (Dualism vs anti-substantialism, treated at length in `neurophenomenology-and-contemplative-neuroscience` L165–167); not an issue here.*

(b) The sibling's Nagel entry (L57) reads him as limiting "what objectivity can *capture*, not what it can *reach*", with "objective ascent" preserved; the target's "the aspiration becomes blindness" is stronger than the sibling's reading of the same source. (c) *Lead, primary check owed*: Nagel's 1974 paper closes by proposing an "objective phenomenology" — a method for describing the subjective character of experience in terms accessible to beings incapable of having it. If that passage stands (four PDF mirrors were unreachable this run; verify at the Philosophical Review text before editing), then "Nagel's insight" as stated omits Nagel's own proposed remedy, and the sibling's weaker reading is the accurate one. (d) Corpus drift is already running the other way: today's deep review of `voids/intrinsic-nature-void` (changelog 2026-09-29T12:38) checked `the-subject-object-distinction-as-philosophical-discovery` L55 and recorded that it "does NOT claim the view from nowhere is impossible in the strong sense". - **Severity**: High (sibling-fidelity; the article is the wing's designated statement of "why third-person method cannot substitute for the first-person channel" per the 2026-09-16 wing review, so its overshoot propagates).

### Issue 2: L84 "Consciousness provides none of these" contradicts L122/L150 and flattens the sibling's qualifier

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/objectivity-and-consciousness.md`
- **Location**: L84 — "units require decomposable, additive quantities; instruments require publicly accessible causal effects; calibration requires observer-independent reference points. Consciousness provides none of these." Against L122 — "There would be no observer-independent description of quantum measurement—the observer's perspective would be part of what determines outcomes." — and L150 — "If consciousness biases quantum outcomes, then observer-independent description fails at the most fundamental physical level."
- **Problem**: If consciousness biases quantum outcomes (Tenet 2) and produces reports (Tenet 3, `tenets.md` L91), it has causal effects, and reports are publicly accessible. The article therefore asserts in one section that consciousness has no publicly accessible causal effects and in two others that it has them. The sibling it is summarising does not make this error: `consciousness-and-the-problem-of-measurement-standards` L70 says "no publicly accessible causal effects *of the right kind*", and L114 says "We can measure the *effects* of consciousness on the physical world, but measuring consciousness itself requires access to the side of the interaction that remains private. Bidirectional causation without bidirectional measurability is exactly what a dualist framework predicts." That last sentence is the reconciliation the target needs and does not have.
- **Severity**: High (internal contradiction across sections; the flattened claim is also the one an outer reviewer would quote).
- **Recommendation**: `refine-draft`. Restore "of the right kind" and add one clause carrying the sibling's asymmetry (effects measurable, phenomenal side not). Zero new references needed. Task minted.

### Issue 3: L146 attributes the bidirectional-causation rationale to Varela's mutual-constraints method

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/objectivity-and-consciousness.md`
- **Location**: L146 — "Neurophenomenology's mutual constraints approach recognizes this: first-person phenomenology and third-person neuroscience must constrain each other because consciousness participates in physical causation." And the parenthesis earlier in the same paragraph: "If consciousness is causally efficacious (evidenced by our ability to report on phenomenal states)".
- **Problem**: Neither the sibling nor the primary source gives that rationale. `concepts/neurophenomenology-and-contemplative-neuroscience` L59 states the method's mutual-constraint principle as "First-person reports and neural data inform each other; neither reduces to the other" — an epistemic-parity rationale. Sandved-Smith et al. (2025), verified at publisher this run: "Neither phenomenology nor neuroscience is more basic or epistemologically fundamental … distinct methods whose findings are not reducible to one another." Varela's enactivism is not interactionist dualism; the "because consciousness participates in physical causation" clause is the Map's gloss presented as the method's own reason. This is the *in-framework argument that actually appeals to Map commitments* pattern in the reasoning-mode checklist, applied to an ally rather than an opponent. The parenthesis compounds it by presenting the self-stultification argument as a datum.
- **Severity**: Medium (attribution framing; the Map's own position is unaffected).
- **Recommendation**: `refine-draft`. Change "recognizes this … because" to a compatibility claim ("is compatible with this: neither vocabulary reduces to the other, which is what a two-way interaction predicts"), and pipe-link the parenthesis to `[[bidirectional-interaction#The Self-Stultification Argument|…]]` so the argument is cited rather than assumed. Task minted.
