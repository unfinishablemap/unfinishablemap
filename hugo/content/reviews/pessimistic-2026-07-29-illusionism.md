## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The Map's stated ground for rejecting AST is the argument the same article calls worthless

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/illusionism.md`
- **Location**: L81, against L93 and L113
- **Problem**: L81 reads: *"The Map rejects AST because it faces the same regress as all illusionist theories: if the attention schema *seems* to be consciousness, something must be doing the seeming."* Twelve lines later, L93 reads: *"But the bare regress assumes "seeming" is itself phenomenal—precisely what illusionists deny. ... he is right that the bare regress proves nothing: a representational system need not instantiate what it represents."* L113 repeats it: *"The bare regress—"to be under an illusion something must experience the illusion"—assumes the conclusion illusionists deny."* The article therefore grounds a rejection ("because") on a premise it twice concedes is question-begging. Under the direct-refutation discipline this is a **boundary-substitution**: framework-level disagreement dressed as an in-framework refutation. Two siblings already carry the correct version — [topics/attention-and-the-consciousness-interface.md](/topics/attention-and-the-consciousness-interface/) L98 (*"Engaging AST inside its own framework is not refutation but identifying a step the framework has not earned"*) and L100 (*"Neither side closes the case against the other inside the rival's framework"*), and [concepts/attention-schema-theory.md](/concepts/attention-schema-theory/) L68 (*"Graziano prefers 'caricature' to 'illusion': a caricature implies something real being represented"*).
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Rewrite L81 on the sibling's model — AST's unearned step is the identification of *being modelled as phenomenal* with *being phenomenal*, not the bare regress. Preserve the second sentence of L81 (*"AST explains why we report being conscious, not why there's anything it's like to be us"*), which is sound and does not depend on the regress.
- **Same defect, other files** (do not lose these): [concepts/mental-effort.md](/concepts/mental-effort/) L124 — *"This faces a regress — if the feeling is an illusion, the *seeming* to feel must itself be explained. As Tallis observes, "misrepresentation presupposes presentation.""* — flat, no concession. [concepts/explanatory-gap.md](/concepts/explanatory-gap/) L117 — *"The illusion move faces a regress. ... The gap persists."* — flat, no concession.

### Issue 3: Graziano's six-hypothesis spectrum is presented as a two-way taxonomy, with a coined term

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/illusionism.md`
- **Location**: L69 heading "Big and Small Illusionism"; L71 *"Michael Graziano distinguishes two versions:"*; L73 *"**Hard (big) illusionism**"*; L75 *"**Soft (small) illusionism**"*
- **Problem**: Verified at the primary (eNeuro 11(10) ENEURO.0210-24.2024, and the PMC full text):
  - The paper proposes **six** hypotheses (magical mind; hard illusionism; illusionist IIT; illusionist GWT; Attention Schema Theory; deep illusionism), presented as a spectrum under an explicit umbrella framing: *"Illusionism is a general philosophical framework ... Here I describe the illusionist framework and propose six specific theories."*
  - Graziano's own contrast term is **"subtle illusionism"**, not "soft" and not "small". The phrases "soft illusionism" and "small illusionism" do not occur in the text; "Hard Illusionism" occurs as the heading of Hypothesis 2 only.
  - The abstract states the paper's purpose as correcting exactly the compression the Map performs: *"scholars tend to think only of the most extreme forms and miss the range of possibilities."*
  - AST is Hypothesis 5 in that same paper — i.e. Graziano places his own theory at the *less extreme* end. This bears directly on Issue 1: the regress objection at L81 lands on a hard-illusionist reading AST does not hold, and [concepts/attention-schema-theory.md](/concepts/attention-schema-theory/) L68 already records why.
- **Severity**: Medium-High
- **Recommendation**: Replace "distinguishes two versions" with the umbrella/spectrum framing and use Graziano's own pair (hard / subtle), noting AST sits toward the subtle end. Retire the invented "(small)" and "(big)" glosses; the article title's "Big and Small" is a title, not a defined technical pair.
- **Same defect, other file**: [concepts/explanatory-gap.md](/concepts/explanatory-gap/) L115 — *"Illusionism ... comes in two forms. Hard illusionism holds that phenomenal properties simply don't exist ... Soft illusionism holds that we misrepresent real neural processes as having phenomenal character."* **That file is at 3495/3500 words (`soft_warning`, 5 words of headroom) — any fix there must be strictly length-neutral or reducing.**

## Counterarguments to Address

### Strawson's "silliest claim" is predicated of the Denial, not of illusionism

- **Current content says** (L87): *"Galen Strawson called illusionism "the silliest claim ever made" in philosophy."*
- **A critic would argue**: Verified verbatim at nybooks.com (primary, "The Consciousness Deniers", 13 March 2018): the essay opens *"What is the silliest claim ever made? The competition is fierce, but I think the answer is easy. Some people have denied the existence of consciousness..."* Strawson names **"the Denial"** — denial of the existence of conscious experience — as the silliest claim. The essay **never mentions Keith Frankish and never uses the word "illusionism"**; the Deniers he names are Dennett, Rorty and Feyerabend. The article's own L61 makes the gap load-bearing: *"Frankish is careful that illusionism denies phenomenal consciousness *as traditionally conceived* ... not consciousness in every functional or everyday sense."* So the article records the distinction and then, twenty-six lines later, spends the quote as if the distinction did not exist. This was flagged by the 2026-06-09 outer review ([reviews/outer-review-2026-06-09-claude-opus-4-8.md](/reviews/outer-review-2026-06-09-claude-opus-4-8/) L105) and has not been fixed; the 06-27 and 07-16 quote-fidelity passes confirmed the *words* and did not check the *predication* — the citation-framing-accuracy lens failure exactly.
- **Suggested response**: Re-predicate, do not delete. *"Galen Strawson calls the denial of consciousness's existence 'the silliest claim ever made' in philosophy, and treats views of this family as instances of it."* **Sibling locus**: [concepts/explanatory-gap.md](/concepts/explanatory-gap/) L117 carries a worse form — *"As Galen Strawson put it, calling illusionism "the silliest claim ever made" in philosophy, the appearance of experience is itself experiential"* — which additionally routes the regress conclusion through the misattribution. (The regress substance *is* Strawson's: the essay argues *"any such illusion is already and necessarily an actual instance of the thing said to be an illusion."* Only the predication is wrong.)

### Frankish introduces the "eliminativism" label in order to reject it

- **Current content says** (L57): *"The two strands are independent, but Frankish (2016) grants that we might call illusionism "eliminativism about phenomenal consciousness." Placing it in that tradition matters: illusionism inherits eliminativism's dialectical history..."*
- **A critic would argue**: Verified verbatim in Frankish's own eprint of *Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness* (JCS 23(11-12): 11–39). The quoted words are exact, but the continuation is: *"The term is not ideal, however, suggesting as it does that belief in phenomenal consciousness is simply a theoretical error, that rejection of phenomenal realism is part of a wider rejection of folk psychology, and that there is no role at all for talk of phenomenal properties — claims that are not essential to the approach. ... I propose 'illusionism' as a more accurate and inclusive name."* Frankish coins "illusionism" *precisely to block* the inheritance the Map's next sentence asserts. The article's L157 "Honest limitation" does disclose that *"Frankish holds phenomenal-realism is separable from content-realism"*, which defuses much of this — but L57 is second-paragraph, truncation-visible prose and reads as a concession from Frankish rather than as a label he declines.
- **Suggested response**: One clause at L57, e.g. *"...grants that we might call illusionism 'eliminativism about phenomenal consciousness', though he rejects the label as suggesting a wider rejection of folk psychology he does not endorse."* This strengthens the article: the "Structural Parallel with Content Irrealism" section then visibly earns its own honest limitation rather than appearing to have Frankish's assent.

### The seeming/processing objection is classified as in-framework but closes phenomenal-realist

- **Current content says** (L119): the vantage-point, zombie-convergence, self-representation and seeming/processing objections *"all press Frankish using commitments his own framework endorses (mechanism, evidence, representational structure)"*, while only the intrinsic-quality objection *"runs through dualism and is honestly noted as framework-boundary disagreement"*.
- **A critic would argue**: the first half of L127 is genuinely in-framework — *"the 'rich illusion' and 'real experience' hypotheses predict identical phenomenology, and neither can appeal to the evidence to rule out the other"* is an underdetermination point Frankish must accept. But the objection closes: *"reports are not what makes the hard problem hard. Experience itself remains untouched rather than dissolved."* That asserts the residue whose existence is the point at issue. The objection is mixed; the classification sentence at L119 covers it wholesale.
- **Suggested response**: Either soften L127's last two sentences to the underdetermination result (which is the strong part), or add a half-clause to L119 acknowledging that the seeming/processing objection turns in-framework only up to the underdetermination point.
