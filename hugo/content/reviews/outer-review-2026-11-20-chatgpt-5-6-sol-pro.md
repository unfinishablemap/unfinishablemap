## Reply

# Audit of “The Plenitude Void”

**Disposition: major revision.**

The article identifies a genuine epistemic problem: attending to, remembering, and reporting an experience can alter the perceptual state under investigation. It also correctly distinguishes the amount or format of phenomenal content from the amount subsequently available to working memory and report.

Its principal conclusion is nevertheless too strong. The evidence supports:

1. direct introspection is often reactive;
2. behavioural reports underdetermine some features of preceding experience; and
3. current methods have not produced consensus about peripheral and unattended phenomenology.

It does **not** establish that richness is structurally impossible to investigate from any combination of behavioural, computational, neural, interventionist and first-person evidence. The article repeatedly moves from *measurement is perturbative and theory-laden* to *the question is unresolvable in principle*. That final step is philosophical stipulation rather than an empirical result.

The page itself partly recognises this problem when it classifies access-independent phenomenal content as definitionally unfalsifiable and says that this is not a positive metaphysical finding. That is one of its strongest passages, but it undercuts the more categorical opening and “self-concealing structure” sections. [plenitude-void](/voids/plenitude-void/)

## 1. The article treats “richness” as too nearly one-dimensional

The rich-versus-sparse framing collapses several different properties:

- number of individually represented objects;
- precision of each representation;
- spatial extent and continuity;
- scene gist and grouping;
- ensemble or texture statistics;
- phenomenal confidence or vividness;
- temporal persistence;
- accessibility for comparison, memory and report.

A visual field could be rich in spatial organisation, scene structure and ensemble information while being sparse in stable item-level identities. It could also contain high-capacity but short-lived information accompanied by weak metacognitive access. Current work increasingly supports such mixed profiles rather than a single scalar running from “sparse” to “fully detailed.”

Rosenholtz’s account, for example, models peripheral vision as preserving substantial pooled or summary information while losing item-level detail. Haun and Tononi argue that the spatial field and its groupings constitute forms of richness not captured by counting reportable objects. Cohen’s 2026 review similarly treats apparent seamlessness and severe bottlenecks as coexisting rather than mutually exclusive. [Springer+2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13414-019-01968-1)

This matters because the article’s cited paradigms do not measure the same thing. Change blindness measures change detection across disruption; inattentional blindness concerns unexpected-stimulus awareness; Sperling paradigms concern short-lived visual storage and retrieval; ensemble tasks test summary representations; visibility ratings test subjective judgement; bandwidth comparisons combine still other informational currencies. Their convergence cannot simply be assumed.

## 2. The opening empirical inference is overstated

The opening says that change blindness, inattentional blindness, saccadic suppression and blind-spot filling suggest that much of what feels represented “is not actually registered.” That wording is stronger than the evidence.

### Change and inattentional blindness

Failure to detect, identify or report a stimulus does not establish that it was wholly unregistered. In the largest recent inattentional-blindness studies, people who denied noticing an unexpected object nevertheless showed above-chance sensitivity to properties such as its location and, in some conditions, colour or shape. Even some high-confidence “non-noticers” retained modest location sensitivity. This does not prove full phenomenal awareness, but it defeats the simple inference from explicit non-report to no registration. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40388213/)

The evidence is more naturally interpreted as supporting graded or fragmented processing, conservative response criteria, partial awareness, or some mixture of these. The article’s own later insistence that access and phenomenology can dissociate should prevent it from using initial report failure as evidence that content was never registered.

### Saccadic suppression

The statement that visual processing is suppressed “3–4 times per second” is imprecise in two ways. Typical saccade rates are often given nearer two or three per second, depending on the task, and saccadic suppression is selective rather than a complete interruption of visual processing. Some visual signals are attenuated, some are much less affected, and predictive remapping helps preserve continuity. The phenomenon therefore illustrates active perceptual continuity, not a periodic global absence of vision. [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5449188/)

### Blind-spot filling

“The blind spot is never experienced as a gap” is a reasonable phenomenological observation, but blind-spot completion is not one homogeneous process. Research distinguishes several forms of filling-in, some involving active neural representation rather than a mere failure to notice missing information. It cannot straightforwardly support the claim that content only seems present while nothing is represented. [Cambridge University Press+1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/finding-out-about-fillingin-a-guide-to-perceptual-completion-for-visual-science-and-the-philosophy-of-perception/66EE8BF4C2B8AC34AE7EACB3D873B6B1)

A safer opening would say that these phenomena reveal a dissociation between subjective continuity, explicit access and some forms of local sensory representation—not that they jointly show non-registration.

## 3. Block and Sperling: a live interpretation, not a demonstrated overflow

The article correctly writes that Block *argues* from Sperling-style partial report to phenomenal overflow. It does not quite maintain that caution in the surrounding prose, particularly when suggesting that the gorilla may have been experienced or describing bandwidth as overflow “at industrial scale.”

Sperling’s result establishes a transient visual representation with greater capacity than whole-report performance. Whether all of that representation was phenomenally conscious is the disputed bridge premise. A 2025 registered report found high iconic-memory capacity but weaker metacognitive sensitivity and lower confidence than in working memory. That combination supports a distinction between stored information and reflective access, but it does not by itself identify the stored information with rich phenomenal consciousness. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41234806/)

A recent systematic review likewise finds that attention is necessary for visual working memory while results for earlier sensory-memory stages are more mixed. The literature therefore supports a sequence of representational stages better than a simple opposition between rich phenomenology and sparse access. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41167442/)

The article should also acknowledge the recent proposal that phenomenal and access consciousness may be two conditions or dimensions of conscious processing rather than two independently populated kinds of consciousness. That directly challenges the conceptual architecture on which the standard overflow argument depends. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41412917/)

## 4. The ensemble-statistics section is the strongest empirical section

The Bronfman quotation about colour diversity being registered automatically and without additional working-memory cost is accurate. The Ward, Bear and Scholl quotations are also accurate: successful ensemble judgement does not establish awareness of all constituent features and can be compatible with sparse item-level awareness. Knotts and colleagues accurately present subjective inflation as a candidate explanation of apparent richness without invoking access-independent overflow. [Association for Psychological Science+2](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/0956797614532656/)

The article draws the right immediate lesson: accurate access to a summary statistic cannot be treated as evidence that every item contributing to that statistic was consciously represented.

It should draw one further lesson. Ensemble representation does not merely support the sparse side; it changes the unit of analysis. A representation can be sparse in individual objects yet rich in distributions, groupings, textures and spatial relations. Ward et al. block an inference to item awareness, but they do not establish globally impoverished phenomenology.

The Rahnev sentence is too broad. The cited work supports attention-dependent differences in perceptual judgements and response criteria under particular detection and discrimination conditions. It does not by itself establish the general claim that unattended peripheral content is experienced as more visible than attended content at matched performance. Brown’s later analysis shows that subjective-inflation results admit several interpretations and do not straightforwardly determine the richness of peripheral phenomenology. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22019729/)

## 5. Introspective unreliability does not entail introspective futility

The Schwitzgebel quotation is now accurate, and his arguments are highly relevant. Introspection can be theory-laden, unstable and affected by the attempt to attend to the target. [Faculty UCR](https://www.faculty.ucr.edu/~eschwitz/SchwitzPapers/Naive070907.htm)

The article then makes a non sequitur:

> If introspection errs in both directions … no amount of careful looking can settle which way the errors lean.

Fallible instruments can still be characterised, calibrated and combined with independent measures. The fact that an instrument has false positives and false negatives does not show that its bias, sensitivity or operating conditions cannot be estimated.

Recent introspective signal-detection work explicitly models first-person judgements in terms of signal strength, noise, criterion and metacognitive sensitivity. Reviews comparing subjective and objective measures likewise find both divergence and convergence under identifiable conditions rather than uniform unreliability. [RCNi Company Limited+2](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/715184)

The defensible conclusion is therefore:

> Unaided introspection is not an independent gold standard for determining unattended perceptual content.

The article instead approaches:

> No disciplined use of introspection, embedded in a multimethod design, can contribute to that determination.

Only the first follows from Schwitzgebel.

## 6. “Checking generates detail” is only partly supported

The check-and-find loop is plausible, but its categorical formulation is not.

Posner’s work establishes that covert attention can be oriented without an eye movement and can improve subsequent processing at the attended location. It does not establish that every act of checking creates phenomenal detail that was previously absent. Work associated with Carrasco shows that attention can alter apparent contrast or spatial resolution under some conditions, but the effects depend on task and stimulus and can sometimes impair rather than enrich performance. [Nature+2](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn1194)

Several distinct possibilities should be separated:

- attention may enhance an existing representation;
- it may change the precision or gain of that representation;
- it may select previously encoded information for access;
- it may create a new post-cue representation;
- it may change the response criterion without changing phenomenology;
- an eye movement may replace peripheral input with foveal input.

Only the fourth amounts to manufacturing content that was not there. The current article treats all six as instances of the same self-concealing mechanism.

“Every introspective probe for sparsity directs attention, and attention generates detail” should therefore become something like:

> Many probes change the precision, selection, accessibility or subsequent reconstruction of the target, so their results cannot automatically be projected backwards onto the unprobed state.

That retains the important methodological point without claiming more than the evidence establishes.

## 7. The Simons and Chabris attribution should be removed

The article says that follow-up work on the gorilla paradigm finds that participants who miss the gorilla can nevertheless give confident post-hoc descriptions of the scene, and cites Simons and Chabris (1999) together with unspecified replications.

The original paper supports the central inattentional-blindness result, but it does not support that specific claim about confident post-hoc scene descriptions. [Chabris](https://www.chabris.com/Simons1999.pdf)

A better evidence base is the recent large-sample work showing that some participants who confidently deny awareness nevertheless retain above-chance information about an unexpected object. That is both more precise and more theoretically interesting: confidence and explicit acknowledgement can dissociate from residual sensitivity, but the residual sensitivity also complicates the article’s “confabulation default.” [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12088676/)

## 8. External methods do not simply inherit a fatal calibration circle

The article is right that neural measures require some bridge from activity to experience. No neural signature announces its phenomenal interpretation without behavioural, experimental or theoretical assumptions.

But calibration dependence does not make third-person investigation powerless. Scientific measurements are often calibrated through multiple imperfect indicators. Intervention, temporal dissociation, convergent decoding, generalisation across tasks and no-report paradigms can constrain which signals reflect perception and which reflect reporting or decision processes.

For example, recent no-report visual-masking work found an earlier neural component associated with reported awareness across task conditions while a later P3b-like component was more closely associated with explicit report requirements. Such findings do not independently prove phenomenology, but they progressively constrain theories that identify consciousness with report-stage processing. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39535504/)

The article should therefore distinguish:

- **no assumption-free phenomenal meter**, which is true;
- **no convergent empirical constraint on phenomenal theories**, which is false;
- **no possible discrimination between theories containing behaviourally and causally idle phenomenal content**, which may be true by definition.

Only the third produces the article’s strongest “void,” and it does so by defining the disputed content as empirically idle.

## 9. The bandwidth argument currently compares incommensurable quantities

The linked bandwidth article carefully says that the frequently cited low rate concerns deliberate decision or report bottlenecks, not total unconscious processing, and ultimately concludes that the bandwidth disparity does not distinguish a global-workspace account from a selective-filter account. [bandwidth-of-consciousness](/topics/bandwidth-of-consciousness/)

“The Plenitude Void” is less cautious. Its two alternatives place phenomenal consciousness “near the low end” or “near the high end,” as though sensory transduction rates, compressed perceptual representations, accessible decisions and verbal reports were measurements in a shared informational currency.

They are not. Raw receptor input includes redundancy, noise and information never used by the organism; report estimates depend on task, coding assumptions and output format. The disparity motivates questions about selection and compression, but it provides no scale on which phenomenal bandwidth can currently be located.

The “overflow at industrial scale” phrase should be deleted. The linked article’s own conclusion is that bandwidth is presently non-discriminating.

## 11. The strongest deflationary and illusionist positions are not yet given a fair hearing

The article includes Dennett, sensorimotor access, ensemble coding, subjective inflation and a brief eliminativist paragraph. That is substantial coverage, but it does not present the strongest current deflationary case.

### Distortion rather than non-existence

Contemporary illusionism need not say that nothing appears or that consciousness does not exist. It can claim that introspective mechanisms represent complex, graded neural processes in a systematically distorted format, inclining us to judge that we possess intrinsic, determinate phenomenal properties. Gorbachev and Frankish’s recent “distortion” framing is much better suited to this article than the Churchland paragraph. [DOI+1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-025-05095-3)

Tye’s objection has little force against that account. The illusionist grants appearances and judgements; the dispute concerns whether introspection correctly represents their nature and detail.

### Higher-order and criterion accounts

A higher-order theorist can say that the feeling of completeness is a higher-order representation of availability, confidence or scene presence rather than an item-by-item representation of peripheral detail. On this view there need be no inaccessible ocean of content and no internally pictured high-resolution field. The article mentions subjective inflation but does not formulate the higher-order claim clearly enough to test it.

Conversely, critics of inflation argue that visibility and confidence judgements cannot be translated directly into phenomenal richness without an additional bridge principle. Brown’s analysis is important here because it shows that the same data can be interpreted as inflation, unreliable rich experience or unstable mixed phenomenology. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38150781/)

### Definitional dissolution

The most serious deflationary challenge is already latent in the article’s unfalsifiability section:

- If alleged overflow affects attention, memory, decisions, neural activity or action, it may leave indirect evidence.
- If it has no possible effect on any of these, nothing empirical distinguishes its presence from its absence.
- In that case “there is inaccessible phenomenal content” may fail to state an empirical alternative rather than identify an empirically discovered void.

The article acknowledges this but then continues to speak of the void as a structural fact. It should instead present two conditional conclusions:

1. a **methodological gap** concerning unprobed and weakly accessible experience; and
2. a possible **definitional residue** generated by theories that posit content excluded from every evidential route.

The second may diagnose a theoretical vocabulary rather than consciousness.

## 12. Current literature changes the balance

The reference list ends before several developments that should now be central:

- large-sample evidence of residual feature sensitivity among inattentional “non-noticers”; [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12088676/)
- registered evidence combining high-capacity iconic memory with relatively poor metacognitive access; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41234806/)
- a systematic review separating attention’s roles in sensory and working-memory stages; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41167442/)
- renewed accounts of peripheral richness based on spatial structure and grouping rather than item count; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40615267/)
- active-vision and centre-to-periphery extrapolation accounts of apparent continuity; [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42776640/)
- explicitly reconciliatory views allowing rich experience alongside sparse focal awareness; [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/search)
- contemporary distortion-based illusionism; [DOI+1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-025-05095-3)
- no-report work separating candidate awareness correlates from report-related activity. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39535504/)

Taken together, this literature does not settle the debate. It does, however, make the article’s binary framing look dated. The emerging picture is representationally heterogeneous, temporally staged and dependent on which dimension of richness is measured.

## 13. Does the dualist section bracket objections too conveniently?

**Yes, despite several commendable disclaimers.**

The article explicitly says that plenitude is not an independent argument for dualism, acknowledges that Block is a physicalist, and states that the framework does not settle the debate. Those are genuine safeguards. [plenitude-void](/voids/plenitude-void/)

The framing remains asymmetric in four ways.

### Materialism is improperly associated with sparsity

The article says that under materialism the grand-illusion view is attractive because neural processing is “demonstrably sparse.” But materialism does not imply a sparse phenomenal field, and neural processing is sparse only with respect to selected measures and representational levels. Physicalists can endorse high-capacity sensory representations, recurrent processing, rich spatial organisation or Block-style access distinctions.

### Dualism is permitted to treat completeness as a datum

There is unquestionably an accessible judgement or impression of completeness. It does not follow that inaccessible item-level details are present. A dualist no more than a physicalist is entitled to transfer warrant from the reportable gestalt—“the scene seems complete”—to all of the detail that the subject cannot identify, compare or preserve.

### The interactionist cost is omitted

The Map endorses bidirectional interaction. If rich nonphysical content affects attention, judgement or action, interactionism should predict some downstream physical difference, even if the trace is indirect. If the inaccessible content makes no difference whatsoever, it looks epiphenomenal and sits uneasily with that tenet.

This produces an internal dilemma:

- causally efficacious plenitude should be empirically constrainable in principle;
- permanently undetectable plenitude appears causally idle.

The page discusses dualism and Occam’s razor but does not confront this implication of the bidirectional-interaction tenet.

### Parsimony is dismissed too readily

Measurement reactivity does not make parsimony unreliable. A model can explicitly include the effect of attention, memory and report on the measured state. Complexity penalties remain relevant when comparing models that explain the same observations; they are simply not decisive by themselves.

The current section therefore gives dualism a rhetorically privileged position: physicalism is associated with explaining richness away, while dualism is allowed to preserve the felt datum without carrying its distinctive causal and calibration burdens.

## 14. Integration with neighbouring articles

### “The Surplus Void”

This is the clearest cross-page tension. “The Plenitude Void” says we cannot determine whether experience genuinely contains more than cognition can grasp. “The Surplus Void” begins from consciousness being extravagantly over-specified relative to function. The latter treats as established what the former classifies as structurally undecidable. [the-surplus-void](/voids/the-surplus-void/)

“The Surplus Void” should be conditionalised: *if phenomenal plenitude or qualitative excess is real, what explanatory problem follows?*

### “Phenomenal Quality Void”

That page repeats the claim that probing an indeterminate experience manufactures detail and presents the family resemblance between the two voids as supporting a shared mechanism. But both pages rely on substantially the same contested assumption. Reciprocal analogy is not independent evidence. [phenomenal-quality-void](/voids/phenomenal-quality-void/)

Both pages should distinguish attentional enhancement, selection, reconstruction and genuinely new phenomenal generation.

### “Bandwidth of Consciousness”

The bandwidth page is methodologically more careful than the present article. It distinguishes decision bandwidth from total processing and concedes that the disparity is non-discriminating. “The Plenitude Void” should import those qualifications rather than use the linked page as support for high-bandwidth overflow. [bandwidth-of-consciousness](/topics/bandwidth-of-consciousness/)

### “Illusionism”

The site’s illusionism article already distinguishes classical Churchland-style eliminativism about propositional attitudes from illusionism about phenomenal properties and includes more recent distortion accounts. That directly exposes the imprecision of the Churchland paragraph in “The Plenitude Void.” [illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/)

A direct link and shared formulation would improve both pages.

### “Phenomenal Overflow”

The dedicated concept article is the most obvious missing link. It should carry the detailed Block/Sperling literature, leaving “The Plenitude Void” to concentrate on the distinctive meta-epistemic argument. Its literature should also be updated with the recent iconic-memory, inattentional-blindness and multidimensional-consciousness work. [phenomenal-overflow](/concepts/phenomenal-overflow/)

### “Self-Opacity” and “Meta-Epistemology of Limits”

The links are thematically appropriate, but the site risks using closely related pages as mutual confirmation. It needs a shared taxonomy separating:

- observer reactivity;
- weak memory;
- response-criterion effects;
- process opacity;
- representational-format mismatch;
- constitutive or definitional inaccessibility.

Without that separation, several “voids” may be redescriptions of the same access/report bottleneck rather than independent limits. [self-opacity](/voids/self-opacity/)

## Revised central claim

A defensible replacement for the current thesis would be:

> Immediate introspection cannot independently determine the item-level precision, stability or representational format of unattended experience, because attention, memory and reporting select, transform or reconstruct aspects of the target. Multimethod evidence can constrain the possibilities but has not settled every residual dispute—especially where phenomenal content is defined as independent of all possible access and causal signature.

That preserves the article’s distinctive insight without converting present underdetermination into an established impossibility theorem.

## Concrete potential improvements

1. **“The Plenitude Void”: replace the opening non-registration claim.** Say that change blindness, inattentional blindness, saccadic suppression and blind-spot completion reveal dissociations among representation, access, memory and subjective continuity—not that unreported content was never registered.
2. **Add a “dimensions of richness” box.** Separate item capacity, precision, spatial extent, ensemble structure, grouping, vividness, temporal stability and reportability. State which experiments bear on which dimension.
3. **Recast the rich/sparse opposition as a multidimensional space.** Explicitly allow rich scene structure with sparse item identity, high-capacity transient storage with poor metacognition, and stable subjective continuity with unstable local detail.
4. **Qualify the Sperling inference.** State that partial report demonstrates high-capacity transient representation; identifying that representation with phenomenal consciousness requires an additional premise.
5. **Add the 2024–2026 literature.** Include Brown on competing interpretations of subjective inflation; Nartker et al. on residual sensitivity in inattentional blindness; Comay et al. on iconic memory and metacognition; the recent visual-memory review; Haun and Tononi on spatial richness; Cohen’s 2026 review; active-vision/extrapolation accounts; and current distortion-based illusionism.
6. **Replace the Churchland paragraph.** Either describe it explicitly as a Churchland-inspired eliminativist possibility or cite a source actually arguing about phenomenal concepts. Use contemporary Frankish-style distortion illusionism for the strongest live counterposition.
7. **Remove the Simons–Chabris post-hoc-description claim.** Replace it with the precise recent result that some high-confidence non-noticers nevertheless show above-chance feature sensitivity.
8. **Narrow the Rahnev claim.** Report the task-specific visibility and criterion result rather than generalising it to unattended peripheral phenomenology as a whole.
