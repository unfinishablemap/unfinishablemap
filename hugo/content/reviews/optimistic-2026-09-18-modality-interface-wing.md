## Executive Summary

This wing is in better structural health than its staleness dates suggest. It has a genuinely shared calibration spine — an "accommodation, not proof" discipline that recurs verbatim across the modality articles and is doing real epistemic work rather than sitting as boilerplate — and one of the five articles in scope already contains the corpus's best worked example of falsifiability discipline. The wing's weaknesses are not doctrinal; they are distributional. One spoke (auditory) is roughly half the length of its siblings and carries **zero** dissociation or pathology cases while every other modality article carries several, and the 3D-world article — whose own subject is cross-modal spatial binding — is linked by exactly **1 of the 8** other members of the wing and links back to exactly **1**.

Two premises in the commissioning brief did not survive measurement, and both corrections are good news; see §"Two Brief Premises Corrected" below.

## Two Brief Premises Corrected

**(a) The wing has six modality spokes, not four.** `ls obsidian/topics/ | grep -F "consciousness-and-the-interface"` returns **six**: auditory, chemosensory, interoceptive, tactile, **thermal**, **vestibular**. The brief named four.

**(b) There is no missing `visual-consciousness-and-the-interface`, and the absence is principled rather than accidental.** Vision is covered twice — `concepts/capability-division-in-vision` (the interface-relevant treatment) and `concepts/visual-consciousness` (the phenomenal treatment) — and the wing's own apex synthesis, `apex/cross-modal-capability-division`, explicitly credits capability-division-in-vision as the article where "the division was stated first for vision, the most data-rich sense and the sharpest test case," with each subsequent modality restating it "while removing one of vision's conveniences." That is an articulated rationale for the differing shape, not an accident of order-of-writing. Measured: 2 of the 6 spokes link `capability-division-in-vision` in body text, and **0 of 6** link `visual-consciousness` — which is the correct routing, since the interface-relevant vision content lives in the former. The hypothesised gap is not there.

The real structural gap is elsewhere, and the measurement that found it is in Priority 2.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The wing is an extended argument that the explanatory gap is modality-general rather than an artefact of starting from colour, and it makes that argument by *subtraction* — each spoke removes one of vision's conveniences and asks whether the gap survives. Chemosensory removes the most: "There is no projection from molecular structure to perceived quality that the brain merely transduces… no place where the smell is, no axis along which it sits." The carvone enantiomer case is the cleanest statement of the gap in the corpus, because it strips away the one move that makes identity theory feel natural in the visual case — locating the quality in a stimulus property. Chalmers would recognise the strategy as the right one: not one knock-down case, but a demonstration that every attempt to naturalise the quality by anchoring it in the stimulus fails differently in each modality.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Restrained, and correctly so. The wing almost never reaches for quantum mechanism; the 3D article is the exception and it routes carefully, naming the quantum-holism programme and the entanglement-binding hypothesis as the Map's *mechanism-story* for phenomenal binding while stating plainly that "the mechanism is an open problem and the Map does not claim to have settled it." Stapp would want more; what he gets instead is a wing that does not spend its quantum credit on cases that do not need it.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The auditory article earns Nagel's approval by noticing something genuinely sharp: "It is no accident that the canonical statement of the gap — Nagel's question of what it is like to be a bat — reaches for an *auditory* creature." That is a real observation about the dialectic and not a decoration. The tactile article's treatment of Merleau-Ponty's reversibility is the wing's phenomenological high point — the touching hand as both pole of the relation, with the roles oscillating but never coinciding, and the honest note that this is "a striking *phenomenological datum*; it is not, by itself, an argument that the subject is non-physical."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

The wing avoids crude substance dualism throughout, and the vision article's closing speculation is the clearest instance: the mind-side contribution "may not be an additional processing step but a different mode of existence for information that the brain has already computed." That is a dual-aspect framing, not a two-substances-pushing-each-other one. **Per this skill's evidential-status constraint, none of that praise is offered as a warrant for upgrading any claim on the five-tier scale**, and the wing does not invite it to be — see the Hardline Empiricist below, who has the load-bearing verdict here.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Present but thin, and appropriately so — this is a perception wing, and Tenet 3 shows up as *perceptual* activity (sniffing to sample, listening selectively, the hand moving across a surface) rather than as libertarian choice. The tactile article's handling is the strongest: active haptics is "the modality where the active, directed character… is least deniable — there is barely a passive haptic percept to fall back on," immediately followed by the concession that "the enactivist and the predictive-processing theorist re-describe the same movement-contingent perception in wholly physical terms."

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The 3D article's §"What Would Challenge This View?" is the best epistemic-humility writing in the wing and among the best in the corpus. It sorts six challenges into three tiers — genuinely in-practice, in-practice *only conditional on instrumentation the framework does not have*, and in-principle-inaccessible-by-the-framework's-own-commitments — and then closes by conceding the score: "only (2) is genuinely in-practice as stated… The framework's net falsifiability is genuinely modest — narrower than a six-item challenge list invites the reader to assume." An article that builds its own falsifiability list and then tells the reader the list is weaker than it looks is doing something most philosophy does not do.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This persona's verdict is the most favourable in the review, and it is load-bearing.**

The wing's calibration spine is real and I measured it rather than inferring it. Counting both variant forms separately (`accommodation, not a proof` and `accommodation, not proof` — the first pattern alone gives a false zero on the auditory article, which uses the "a proof" form), **7 of the 8** wing members carry at least one explicit calibration marker from the family {`accommodation, not (a) proof`, `not as evidence that settles the dispute`, `settles dualism`}. It is not a stock phrase pasted in; each article states what specifically it is *not* claiming. Auditory: "The Map does not claim audition settles dualism." Tactile: the naturalist literature it leans on is "engaged here as *rivals* the interface reading must out-accommodate, not as allies to be conscripted." Chemosensory: it borrows Barwich's data "and engages her metaphysics as a rival rather than enlisting it."

Three specific pieces of restraint are worth naming as strengths:

1. **Tenet-as-evidence-upgrade is praise-worthily *not* done.** The chemosensory article had the clearest available opportunity to slide up the scale: state-indexed valence (alliesthesia) is *stronger* evidence for constitutive valence than fixed tone, and the article says so — then immediately refuses the upgrade, because state-indexing "is fully explained by homeostatic integration — the valence tracks a regulated variable, which is exactly what a physicalist account of feeding predicts — so it is accommodation, not proof." Having just argued its own case is stronger than previously thought, it declines to bank the gain. That is exactly the discipline.

2. **The wing repeatedly concedes that its own best data are physicalist mechanisms.** Tactile on C-tactile afferents: "A dedicated affective fibre system is a *physicalist mechanism*; the Map does not claim the felt pleasantness of a caress needs a non-physical carrier." Chemosensory on the limbic bypass: "the limbic-bypass anatomy is itself a *physicalist mechanism*." Each then locates the residue precisely at the explanatory gap rather than at the mechanism.

3. **The 3D article separates what the puzzle delivers from what the tenets deliver, explicitly.** Its §"Relation to Site Perspective" states that the explanatory-gap claim holds "whether the reader's wider metaphysics is dualist, panpsychist, neutral-monist, Russellian, or sophisticated-functionalist," and then: "*Because* of Tenets 1 and 3 — not because of the 3D-world puzzle — the Map reads the dual-domain division this way… The division is the Map's reading, not the puzzle's verdict." That is the "tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating" pattern stated as cleanly as the discipline asks for.

**The Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist converge here rather than conflicting**, which on this skill's own criterion means the wing has resolved the tension honestly. I found no possibility/probability slippage in the five articles in scope and am therefore raising no calibration concern.

## Content Strengths

### `topics/three-dimensional-world-representation-problem`
- **Strongest point**: The §"Three Dimensions of the Problem" preamble, which pre-emptively disarms its own structure. It concedes that two of its three sub-problems are existing corpus arguments "in spatial dress" — construction is the binding problem, perspective is the indexical-identity problem — and that counting them as independent "would double-count what the corpus already counts elsewhere." Only the presence problem is claimed as distinctive.
- **Notable quote**: "the reader should understand that the three sections do not deliver three independent confirmations of the dualist conclusion."
- **Why it works**: Self-administered double-counting audits are rare and this one costs the article two-thirds of its apparent argumentative force. It is the single most creditable paragraph in the five articles.

### `topics/chemosensory-consciousness-and-the-interface`
- **Strongest point**: The two-stage argument in §"A Quality Space With No Ordering to Read Off" — first that the percept is not in the molecule, then that it is not in the *structure of the stimulus space* either. The second stage is what makes the article more than a restatement.
- **Notable quote**: "Where a quality space has a tidy geometry, the identity theorist has a foothold: the quality *is* its place in the space, and the space is physical. Smell offers no such foothold."
- **Why it works**: It identifies the precise reductive manoeuvre available in the visual case and shows the modality that denies it. This is the freshest article in the wing (`ai_modified` 2026-09-14) and it shows.

### `topics/tactile-consciousness-and-the-interface`
- **Strongest point**: §"A Body Whose Boundary Can Be Moved" refuses to over-claim the rubber-hand illusion, distinguishing the *boundary* of the felt body (which the illusion moves) from the *ownership* within which the boundary is felt (which it does not touch).
- **Notable quote**: "The rubber-hand illusion relocates the *boundary* of the felt body; it does not explain, and does not dissolve, the ownership the relocated boundary is felt within."
- **Why it works**: The rubber-hand illusion is routinely over-read in both directions in the wider literature. Naming what it does not show, and handing that residue explicitly to `consciousness-and-the-ownership-problem` rather than claiming it, is good corpus hygiene as well as good argument.

### `concepts/capability-division-in-vision`
- **Strongest point**: §"The Epiphenomenalist Pressure" argues the selection case, then demotes it. It raises the point that epiphenomenal phenomenal structure would be hard to explain evolutionarily, concedes the spandrel reply, and explicitly re-ranks its own reasons.
- **Notable quote**: "The Map's reply does not lean on the selection argument alone; it rests on the first ground above… and treats the evolutionary consideration as corroborating rather than load-bearing."
- **Why it works**: Naming which of your own two arguments is doing the work, and demoting the more rhetorically appealing one, is the behaviour the evidential-status discipline is trying to produce.

### `topics/auditory-consciousness-and-the-interface`
- **Strongest point**: The framing move in the opening — audition is developed not as another example but as the modality supplying "three kinds of evidence the visual case lacks or only weakly displays." The article knows why it exists.
- **Notable quote**: "Audition strips that crutch away. Sounds from different sources arrive superimposed in a single pressure wave at each ear, and the auditory system must decompose that one signal into distinct perceived sources without a spatial map to sort them by."
- **Why it works**: Bregman's auditory scene analysis is exactly the right case for a binding argument that wants to escape vision's same-location heuristic, and the apex synthesis leans on this article for precisely that. The article's thinness (Priority 1) is a matter of what it omits, not of what it gets wrong.

- **Corpus-wide coverage of the obvious candidates** (files containing the term, across all of `obsidian/`): `amusia` **0 files**; `auditory agnosia` **0 files**; `congenital deafness` **0 files**; `tinnitus` **17 files** — but all 17 are passing mentions of 1–2 occurrences each, mostly in research notes and workflow archives, and **the auditory article itself contains zero occurrences of `tinnitus`**.

Tinnitus is the strongest candidate and the Map has already done the research. The research note [research/charles-bonnet-syndrome-generative-model-release-2026-09-16.md](/research/charles-bonnet-syndrome-generative-model-release-2026-09-16/) (4917 words, written two days ago, currently unconsumed) works out the deafferentation/release reading of percept-without-stimulus in detail and **explicitly names tinnitus as the auditory analogue** — twice, in its summaries of Painter et al. 2018 ("Frames CBS alongside phantom-limb pain and tinnitus as maladaptive plasticity after peripheral damage") and of the release hypothesis ("Analogy: phantom limb, tinnitus"). The auditory article can borrow a framing that has already been sourced and citation-checked.

What belongs there, concretely: tinnitus as audition's percept-without-stimulus (the release/deafferentation reading, with the same "this is a physicalist mechanism and the Map adopts it" concession the wing's other spokes make); auditory agnosia as audition's counterpart to visual-form agnosia, which would let the article connect to the two-streams material `capability-division-in-vision` already owns; amusia as a selective loss of exactly the pitch/timbre qualia the article's third section is about. That last is the sharpest, because the article currently argues that pitch and timbre are vividly determinate yet have no external home — and amusia is the case where they go missing while hearing is otherwise intact.

**Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1 (Dualism) — a percept with no stimulus is the limiting case of the article's existing "the quality is not in the signal" argument. Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction) — attentional modulation of tinnitus is a live literature.
