## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Taylor 1986 chapter page range wrong on both ends** (pp. 264-304 → pp. 256-314). Fixed. Introduced by the 2026-08-04 refine; survived a pessimistic review whose executive summary called the citation apparatus "unusually clean" — because that review checked *quotations*, and this is a locator on an unquoted chapter reference. See `quote-fidelity-defects-survive-metadata-reviews` read in the other direction: a quote-fidelity pass does not certify locators either.

2. **Internal contradiction between the illusionism coda and the Occam's-limits tenet section.** The coda claimed "What the metaphysics buys is **not a different verdict in any actual case** but a different account of what the uncertainty in hard cases is." Four paragraphs later the Relation to Site Perspective section says that dismissing sentience in invertebrates and artificial systems on parsimony grounds "is exactly the false parsimony the tenet rejects, and the asymmetry of costs under uncertainty runs the other way" — which *is* a different verdict in actual cases. The coda is also wrong on its own: a functionalist who precisifies "sentient" so insects fall outside returns a settled *no*; the Map returns *unknown*, and unknown-plus-precaution acts differently from settled-no.

   This is the `over-concession-gets-ratified-not-merely-missed` pattern in its exact diagnostic form — an over-claim running *against* the Map, carrying the tell "not … in any actual case", collecting endorsement rather than challenge across two prior reviews. Fixed by scoping the concession to what it can support and connecting it to the precautionary layer it was contradicting: "not a **settled** verdict in any hard case but a different account of what the uncertainty there is … which is why precaution rather than stipulation is the response it calls for."

### Medium Issues Found

3. **Dropped qualifier on Taylor's duty claim.** SEP: "Taylor maintains that the intrinsic value of **wild** living things generates a prima facie moral duty on our part to preserve or promote their goods as ends in themselves." The HIV case survives the correction — a virus is wild in Taylor's sense — and the article already concedes Taylor has priority-principle machinery for it.
