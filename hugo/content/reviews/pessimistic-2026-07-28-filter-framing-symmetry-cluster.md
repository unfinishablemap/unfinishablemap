## Executive Summary

The two articles fail the symmetry discipline from opposite directions, and neither failure is the one the audit's blunt reading suggests. `edge-states-and-void-probes.md` is an unusually well-calibrated article that has already done the *harder* half of the discipline — it explicitly refuses to count its supportive cases as independent confirmations, twice — but has done none of the *easier* half: it engages zero members of the disruptive cluster, while naming that exact debt in a subordinate clause it never pays. `mind-brain-separation.md` has the inverse and worse problem: it cites all four disruptive clusters and converts every one of them into filter-supporting evidence, which is the double-counting failure in its inverted form. The more serious finding for that article is separate and verifiable: it is a **stale hub that now contradicts two dedicated articles** — `terminal-lucidity-and-filter-transmission-theory.md` and `illusionism.md` — both substantially recalibrated after its last touch on 2026-06-20. A third, independent defect is an **inverted conditional** in `edge-states-and-void-probes.md`'s report of Kondziella et al. (2019), verified against the published abstract.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

`mind-brain-separation.md`'s "division of faculties" is folk psychology with a table of contents. Its criterion for assigning a faculty to "mind" is that neuroscience has not yet derived it — "have not been derived from descriptions of neural firing patterns despite sustained effort" (§ Faculties Apparently Intrinsic to Mind). That is a report on the state of the literature dressed as an ontological partition. The article's own § What Would Challenge This View? confirms the structure: every disconfirming condition is "neuroscience finally explains X." A division whose membership is defined by current explanatory debt will migrate as the debt is paid, which is exactly the eliminativist's prediction, not a rival to it.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

The illusionism section (lines 87–91) runs the bare regress — "If we *seem* to have qualia, something is doing the seeming" — and treats it as decisive. The Map's own [concepts/illusionism.md](/concepts/illusionism/) line 93 states flatly that Frankish "is right that the bare regress proves nothing: a representational system need not instantiate what it represents." So the hub article deploys as a knockdown an argument the dedicated article concedes is worthless, and never reaches the meta-representational bridge where the dedicated article locates the real disagreement. See Critical Issue 3.

### The Quantum Skeptic

No new purchase. `edge-states-and-void-probes.md` § Minimal Quantum Interaction correctly marks its REBUS-to-collapse suggestion as "speculative," which is the right register, and no decoherence number is claimed that could be wrong. Not pressed here.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

`mind-brain-separation.md` line 111 — "choosing feels like determining which possibility becomes real because it *is* determining which possibility becomes real" — reads as bald phenomenology-to-metaphysics. On the charitable reading it is conditional on the No-Many-Worlds tenet already being granted, which is tenet-coherence rather than evidence-elevation, and the 2026-06-20 deep review ruled it so. That ruling holds; I do not re-litigate it. The residue is stylistic (the flat "because it *is*"), not a defect worth a task.

### The Empiricist

`edge-states-and-void-probes.md` earns real credit here: § Transcendence or Edge-Mapping? states four discriminating predictions, reports that the evidence fits edge-mapping, and explicitly refuses to let the noncommunicability escape hatch count as an evidential upgrade. That is falsificationist hygiene most of the corpus does not reach. The failure is elsewhere — a framing whose central claim is *convergence* must show the convergence survives the cases that cut the other way, and this article never looks at them.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Both articles reify what the traditions they cite deconstruct. `edge-states-and-void-probes.md` is aware of this — its § Zero-Person Perspective concedes the phenomenology is "equally describable" under panpsychism, neutral monism, or Metzinger's naturalism, and that "on parsimony grounds that physicalist reading carries the lighter commitment." `mind-brain-separation.md` makes no such concession while asserting consciousness "provides ... the subject for whom processing matters" (§ The Brain as Rendering Engine). The article notices the regress risk in the next sentence and does not answer it.

### Issue 2: `edge-states-and-void-probes.md` inverts the conditional in Kondziella et al. (2019)

- **File**: `obsidian/voids/edge-states-and-void-probes.md`
- **Location**: § Near-Death Experiences as the Weakest Leg, line 128
- **Problem**: The article states Kondziella et al. "found NDE phenomenology **more common in people prone to REM-sleep intrusion** (47% versus 14%)." The study found the converse. Verified against the published abstract (PeerJ 7:e7585): evidence of REM intrusion was more common **in people with near-death experiences (47%)** than in those scoring ≤6 on the Greyson scale (26%) or in those without such experiences (14%). The reported percentages are P(REM intrusion | NDE) versus P(REM intrusion | no NDE) — not, as the article has it, the NDE rate conditioned on REM-intrusion proneness. Two consequences: (a) the article manufactures a *directionality* (proneness → NDE) that a symmetric association does not carry, which is what its inferential use requires — it presents Kondziella as "supplying a wholly physiological candidate mechanism" that "strengthens the ... dying-brain production reading"; (b) the intermediate 26% group is dropped, and that group is the one that makes the association look graded rather than binary. Separately, "wholly physiological" over-describes the measure: REM intrusion here is **self-reported** in a crowdsourced sample of 1,034 laypeople, not physiologically recorded.
- **Not propagated**: grep-verified that this framing appears nowhere else in the corpus. [concepts/near-death-experiences.md](/concepts/near-death-experiences/) line 105 handles REM intrusion correctly and with an appropriate limitation ("producing out-of-body sensations but not coherent narratives"). Single locus, cheap fix.
- **Severity**: High (empirical-claim fidelity — the paraphrase does not match what the study found)
- **Recommendation**: Restate as: REM-sleep intrusion was more commonly reported by people who had had NDEs (47%) than by those who had not (14%), with an intermediate rate among sub-threshold experiences. Downgrade "supplying a wholly physiological candidate mechanism" to a physiological candidate mechanism resting on self-report, and note that the association does not by itself fix the causal direction. This *weakens* the sentence's support for the production reading slightly — which is the honest outcome, and consistent with the section's own stated purpose of demoting NDEs as evidence in either direction. Length-neutral.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The convergence-across-edge-states argument

- **Current content says**: `edge-states-and-void-probes.md` — recurrence of the edge-profile "across independent traditions" and across meditation, psychedelics, aesthetic absorption, and philosophical inquiry is the article's central datum.
- **A critic would argue**: all four routes converge because all four perturb the same cortical hierarchy, and the article has already conceded the machinery for this — its own line 154 grants that "a common cause is the more parsimonious explanation of a shared signature." A critic will add the cases the article omits: anaesthesia and slow-wave sleep perturb the same system and produce the *opposite* profile, which is what a production account predicts and what the filter account must explain away.
- **Suggested response**: the article is one clause from having the answer. It needs to say that the filter reading accommodates the disruptive cases by the interface narrowing rather than the filter loosening, that this is the *same kind* of move the production reading makes for the supportive cases, and that the symmetry is why neither cluster discriminates. This concedes nothing the article has not already conceded and closes the opening.

### Terminal lucidity as filter-theory evidence

- **Current content says**: `mind-brain-separation.md` — "the most dramatic evidence ... precisely as filter theory predicts."
- **A critic would argue**: the first prospective study found the dramatic terminal subtype in 4.1% of events, with the modal episode brief and trigger-linked — a profile consistent with transient cholinergic or noradrenergic surges from preserved brainstem nuclei.
- **Suggested response**: already written, in the Map's own dedicated article. The hub needs to inherit it, not invent a reply.

## Strengths (Brief)

`edge-states-and-void-probes.md` is one of the better-calibrated articles in the corpus and the remediation must not damage it. It volunteers counter-evidence against its own thesis (the Vollenweider metabolic-hyperfrontality data, which it correctly says "the production reading predicts"); it refuses to enlist Metzinger, stating outright that "the Map should not enlist him as an ally" and that the physicalist reading "carries the lighter commitment"; it demotes its own most rhetorically useful evidence in a section headed "Near-Death Experiences as the Weakest Leg"; it concedes the data "fit dualism; they do not select it" against panpsychism and neutral monism; and it states the anti-double-counting discipline twice, unprompted. Only two narrow defects were found in it, both fixable in roughly zero net words.

`mind-brain-separation.md`'s core structure — a three-way partition into mind-side, brain-side, and interface faculties — is genuinely useful and is not what is broken. Its § What Would Challenge This View? states five reasonably specific disconfirmers, and its Whitehead section correctly flags that process philosophy "is closer to neutral monism than the Map's substance dualism" rather than annexing it. The article's problem is staleness against a corpus that moved, not a bad frame.
