## Primary Lens — Doctrinal Attribution and Reversal Check

The Zhu Xi / Wang Yangming pairing was checked specifically for the reversal failure mode (an attribution that reads plausibly but runs backwards). **No reversal present.** Verified at the SEP:

- ***xin ji li* direction — CORRECT.** Van Norden's entry has Wang asking "Is there any Pattern outside the mind?" and shows Wang rejecting the search for pattern in external things, against Zhu Xi's emphasis on grasping pattern by investigating external objects and studying the classics. The article's direction (Wang: principle already complete in the heart-mind; Zhu Xi: found by investigating things outside) is the right way round.
- ***li* as immanent, never apart from *qi* — CORRECT but under-qualified.** Thompson's entry: "*li* is not present apart from *qi* formations"; *li* "exist immanently in processes and things." However the entry also records that Zhu "still felt the need to prioritize *li* ontologically and ethically," *li* being "permanent possibilities of *qi* formation." The article's flat "never floating free" was accurate on inseparability but silently dropped the priority qualification — which matters, because that priority is the closest thing in Zhu Xi to a two-category structure, i.e. precisely what the article claims the vocabulary lacks. Fixed by stating the priority and then showing why it does not yield a mental/physical pair.
- ***liangzhi* attributed to Wang — CORRECT**; rendering re-attributed (below).
- **Chinese character / romanisation match — ALL CORRECT.** Checked individually: 氣/*qi*, 理/*li*, 道/*Dao*, 心/*xin*, 格物/*gewu*, 良知/*liangzhi*, 心即理/*xin ji li*, 知行合一/*zhi xing he yi*. No mismatches. New strings added this pass (形/*xing*, 神/*shen*, 神滅論/*Shenmie lun*) verified likewise.

## Critical Issues Found

**1. Unrestricted negative — "never split mind from matter" (FIXED).** The article's headline claim, carried in the lead, the description and the basin-edge section, was that Chinese thought *never* divided mind from matter. That flat *never* is falsified inside the tradition by the *xing*–*shen* (形/神, form/spirit) controversy of roughly the fourth to sixth centuries: Buddhist writers argued the spirit is separable from the body (rebirth requires something that persists), their opponents denied it, and the dispute peaked with Fan Zhen's *Shenmie lun* (神滅論, 507) and its sharpness-to-a-blade argument, provoking some sixty commissioned replies under Emperor Wu of Liang. This is exactly the kind of unrestricted negative that does not survive scrutiny, and it was load-bearing for the basin-edge argument.

Fixed by **calibration, not retraction**. A new section, "The Body and Spirit Debate," states the exception and then shows why the argument survives it: both sides argued in the shared vocabulary of *qi*; what divided them was *persistence* (does the spirit outlast the body?) rather than constitution; nobody asked why a bodily process should be accompanied by anything it is like; and Fan Zhen's own analogy is an inseparability thesis of the organicist kind, nearer a supervenience claim than to either Cartesian dualism or a refutation of it. The claim the article now defends is the scoped one — the tradition's central categories offered no place to *lodge* an explanatory gap, even in the period when it argued hardest about body and spirit. Lead, description and basin-edge section all updated to match; the lead forward-references the new section with the named-anchor pattern.

Supporting note: the article was the *outlier* in its own cluster. Both sibling articles already hedge with "largely do not generate"; only this article carried the unhedged form. The calibration brings it into line rather than departing from the corpus.

**2. Coverage gap — Chinese Buddhism absent (FIXED).** An article titled "Chinese Philosophy of Mind" treated only Confucianism, Daoism and Neo-Confucianism, with no acknowledgement that Chinese Buddhism imported an Indian analysis of mind wholesale (the Faxiang school transmitting Yogācāra "consciousness-only" doctrine after Xuanzang's seventh-century return) and is not organicist in the article's sense. Left unstated, the article's basin-edge claim over-reached its actual subject. Fixed with an explicit scoping paragraph: the argument concerns the indigenous Confucian, Daoist and Neo-Confucian strands rather than everything ever thought in China, with the Buddhist material routed to [eastern-philosophy-consciousness](/topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness/).
