## 1. Accuracy of the exposition of Griffin

### 1.1 The two theses: accurately represented

The article correctly reproduces Griffin’s division of ontological dualism into:

1. the **numerical thesis** that mind is an actuality distinct from the brain; and
2. the **qualitative thesis** that mind differs in ontological kind from the entities composing the brain.

It also correctly presents nondualistic interactionism as accepting the first and rejecting the second. Griffin explicitly makes precisely this distinction and objects to treating “interactionism” as synonymous with dualistic interactionism. [New Dualism Archive](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

The article also handles an important qualification well: Griffin’s mind is not a Cartesian substance numerically identical through time. It is a temporally ordered society of successive occasions, numerically distinct from the neuronal enduring individuals composing the brain. [New Dualism Archive](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

There is nevertheless a small ontological imprecision in the article’s description of the mind as “a further actual entity presiding over” the neurons. In Griffin’s strict process vocabulary, the momentary dominant occasion is an actual entity; the persisting mind is the serial society of such occasions. The current phrase risks making the enduring mind itself sound like one continuously persisting actual entity. A more exact formulation would be:

> At each moment a new dominant occasion arises as a higher-level actuality; the mind over time is the personally ordered society of those occasions.

That correction matters because the Map’s contrast with its own persisting subject depends partly on this diachronic distinction.

### 1.2 Compound individuals: accurate architecture, overstated application

The article accurately explains Griffin’s distinction between aggregational societies and compound individuals. Griffin says that aggregational societies lack an overall experiential unity and dominant member, whereas compound individuals contain higher-level occasions that coordinate unified response and action. Animals with central nervous systems are his clearest examples. [New Dualism Archive+1](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

The article is also right that Griffin regards the human mind as a society of dominant occasions arising from neuronal activity, rather than as the identity or sum of the neurons’ experiences. Griffin adopts James’s “101st feeling” idea: the higher-level experience is new and numerically distinct from the lower-level experiences rather than identical with their collection. [New Dualism Archive](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

However, the sentence **“Each neuron is itself a compound individual”** is too categorical. Griffin says that living cellular occasions emerge from organelles **“by hypothesis”**; organelles “may be supposed” to be compound individuals; and molecules and atoms “might” be. He expressly says that which enduring things qualify as compound individuals is an empirical question, provisionally answered by whether their behaviour seems to require a central agent. Since neurons are living cells, the article’s interpretation is reasonable, but it should remain marked as Griffin’s hypothesis rather than a settled element of the ontology. [New Dualism Archive+1](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

A further issue is the word **“mechanism”** at the beginning of this section. The compound-individual/dominant-occasion distinction is a metaphysical architecture or causal schema, not yet a mechanism in the ordinary scientific sense. It supplies no quantitative rule for:

- when a dominant occasion arises;
- which neuronal events constitute its immediate environment;
- why there is one rather than several;
- how its appetition changes a particular later neuronal transition; or
- what empirical signature distinguishes that influence.

The article later faults Griffin for supplying no quantum mechanism. It should therefore avoid first presenting the dominant-occasion structure itself as a mechanism.

### 1.3 The composition objection needs sharper classification

The article is right to treat Kim’s request for a composition principle as serious, but **“combination problem”** is too broad unless its variants are separated.

Griffin does not say that the neuronal subjects literally fuse or sum to become the macro-subject. He says that their activity partly creates a new, 101st subject that is also partly self-creative. The primary burden is therefore not straightforward **constitutive subject-summing**, but:

- **subject generation:** why does this configuration produce an additional subject?
- **individuation:** why exactly one dominant occasion, with these boundaries?
- **specificity:** why does it possess this particular experience?
- **diachronic unity:** why do successive dominant occasions constitute one person?
- **causal coordination:** how does that subject exert coherent control over many later neuronal occasions?

Kim’s composition demand reaches these questions, but the article should say that Griffin relocates the subject-summing problem into a **new-subject or 101st-subject problem** rather than simply inheriting the standard constitutive version. Griffin’s text itself emphasizes that the higher-level experience is new rather than identical with the lower-level experiences. [New Dualism Archive+1](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

This would also improve parity with the Map. The Map does not face constitutive summation either, but it faces analogous individuation and coupling questions. The current article recognizes this, but its terminology makes Griffin’s burden sound more like the standard panpsychist problem than it really is.

### 1.4 Prehension: quotation correct, context incomplete

The quotation about brain cells deriving guidance by prehending the mind’s appetitions is exact. But in Griffin’s book it occurs while he is drawing an analogy between brain–mind interaction and human prehension of the cosmic mind’s ideals. It is not part of a detailed neural account of downward causation. [New Dualism Archive](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

The quotation still supports the proposition attributed to it, but its context should be disclosed. Otherwise it looks like Griffin is there deriving a specific mind–brain model, when he is mainly presupposing that model as one side of an analogy.

More importantly, the article omits the **temporal structure** of Whiteheadian causation. Occasions prehend completed prior occasions, not contemporaneous occasions. Griffin consequently describes the new mind occasion as causally dependent on brain activity occurring a fraction of a second earlier; after its completion, its appetition can become data for later neuronal occasions. [New Dualism Archive+1](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

The implied sequence is therefore:

> earlier neurons → present dominant occasion → later neurons → next dominant occasion

That is a more informative account than two undifferentiated arrows labelled “brain influences mind” and “mind influences brain.” It also exposes testable or at least sharpenable questions about temporal grain, latency, neuronal synchronization and the continuity of personal agency.

## 2. “Closure kept, completeness denied”: the principal conceptual problem

The article’s heading is rhetorically effective but philosophically unstable. Griffin accepts one closure thesis while rejecting at least two others.

### The closure distinctions the article should make

| Thesis | Griffin’s position |
| --- | --- |
| **Entity-domain closure:** physical events have no causes belonging to a wholly nonphysical domain | Accepted, because Griffin defines actual causes as physical–mental occasions |
| **Causal-property closure:** physical effects are caused only in virtue of physical properties | Rejected; Griffin expressly allows causation in virtue of an occasion’s mental aspect |
| **Microphysical sufficient-cause closure:** every microphysical event has a sufficient microphysical cause | Rejected |
| **Physics-only causal/explanatory completeness:** physics can give a complete causal account of bodily motion | Rejected |
| **Determinism or complete predictability** | Rejected, but logically distinct from causal closure |

Griffin says that the physical domain is not invaded by wholly nonphysical entities because minds themselves have physical poles. Yet he immediately adds that downward causation can occur from the **specifically mental aspect** of a dominant occasion and rejects Kim’s principle that the causation of a physical–mental entity must always occur solely in virtue of its physical aspect. [New Dualism Archive+1](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

Thus Griffin retains closure only under a **revisionary entity-level definition of “physical.”** He does not retain the doctrine ordinarily doing the work in physicalist exclusion arguments: that physical events have microphysically sufficient causes or are caused exclusively in virtue of physical properties.

The article eventually reaches approximately this result when it says Griffin denies microphysical closure in Chalmers’s sense. But its opening formulation—“He accepts the closure of the physical domain to non-physical causes”—can mislead readers into thinking that he accepts orthodox physical causal closure and merely disputes explanatory reach. He does not.

A better heading would be:

> **Entity-Domain Closure Retained; Microphysical Sufficiency Denied**

The “pipe dream” sentence about physics predicting or explaining all bodily motion is genuine, but inability to predict should not carry the argument. Indeterminism, epistemic unpredictability and explanatory incompleteness do not by themselves establish causally efficacious mentality. Griffin’s stronger basis is his explicit rejection of complete brain determination and his assertion of mental-as-mental downward causation. [New Dualism Archive+1](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

### Consequences for the Chalmers classification

Chalmers defines type-D by denial of microphysical causal closure and irreducible phenomenal-to-physical causation. Crucially, he says type-D need not be Cartesian substance dualism: it can involve one type of entity bearing both physical and phenomenal fundamental properties. [Consciousness.net](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

That means the article’s conclusion that Griffin and Chalmers merely disagree about whether “dualism” names a difference in kind is incomplete. Chalmers’s type-D is principally a **causal-structural category**, not a claim that there must be two substances or even two entity kinds. Griffin can therefore be:

- type-F-like concerning the intrinsic experiential nature of basic actuality; and
- type-D-like concerning fundamental higher-level causation and denial of microphysical closure.

Chalmers himself says that high-level phenomenal dispositions not constituted by a causally closed microphysical base would collapse into type-D, and describes the principal difference between D and F as the presence of fundamental causation above the microphysical level. [Consciousness.net](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

The fairest classification is therefore **a D/F hybrid**, not merely “F according to ontology, D according to structure.”

### The comparison table needs rebuilding

The existing row for type-F monism is particularly problematic:

- **“Mind numerically distinct: no”** is not entailed by type-F monism. Type-F specifies the relation between phenomenal/intrinsic and physical/dispositional properties; it does not by itself settle whether a macro-subject is numerically distinct from its constituents.
- **“Physics complete: yes”** conflates causal-dynamical completeness with metaphysical or explanatory completeness. Type-F accepts microphysical causal closure but is motivated precisely by the claim that physics leaves intrinsic nature unspecified.
- **“Interaction: none distinct from the physical”** obscures the intended claim. A better entry would be: “phenomenal causation realized through or constitutive of the physical causal network; no additional higher-level causal influence.”

Chalmers’s own account explicitly contrasts type-F’s acceptance of microphysical causal closure with type-D’s rejection of it, while also saying that physics is silent about intrinsic categorical nature. [Consciousness.net+1](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

The table should replace **“Physics complete?”** with separate columns for:

1. microphysical sufficient-cause closure;
2. fundamental causation above the microphysical level;
3. metaphysical completeness of physics;
4. numerical distinctness of the macro-subject; and
5. ontological difference in kind.

## 3. Citation and quotation audit

### Griffin 1998

The high-load quotations checked—the two theses, temporal-society description, dominant occasions, degree rather than kind, closure, mental-as-mental causation and physics “pipe dream”—are accurately transcribed and correctly attributed to *Unsnarling the World-Knot*. The page references are consistent with the primary text. [New Dualism Archive+4](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

The problems are interpretive rather than textual:

- “Each neuron is itself a compound individual” removes Griffin’s hypothetical status.
- “Mechanism” overstates the explanatory development.
- The prehension quotation lacks its cosmic-mind context.
- The closure heading does not preserve Griffin’s distinctions.

The Hartshorne attribution is handled properly: the article says Griffin takes the term from Hartshorne rather than pretending to quote Hartshorne directly.

### Kim 1999

The Kim quotations are genuine:

- his judgment that calling panexperientialism physicalism is misleading;
- the “wish list” language; and
- his request for an independently motivated composition principle. [Religion Online+2](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/)

Two corrections are needed.

**First, Kim was not responding to *Unsnarling the World-Knot*.** His article responds to Griffin’s essay “Materialist and Panexperientialist Physicalism,” and Kim says at the end that he hopes to read Griffin’s book in the future. The current phrase “Two published responses” appears immediately after the book exposition and can readily be read as identifying two responses to the book. It should say:

> Kim’s 1999 response to Griffin’s Process Studies essay, and Chalmers’s later taxonomy, identify two pressure points. [Religion Online+1](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/)

**Second, the “wish list” presentation needs Kim’s immediate qualification.** Kim explicitly says Griffin is **not exactly** practising wish-list metaphysics, because Griffin begins with only an “iota” of mentality rather than inserting full human consciousness and agency at the base. He then says the foundation nevertheless reminds him of a wish list. The article’s word “reminiscent” partially preserves this nuance, so this is not a misquotation, but the qualification should appear when such a rhetorically charged phrase is used. [Religion Online](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/)

More seriously, the article omits Kim’s strongest substantive reply. Griffin argues that microphysicalism makes organisms and rocks organisationally equivalent and denies genuine higher-level powers. Kim replies that:

- determination is not identity;
- microstructural determination permits novel macro-properties and causal powers;
- structure and relations among constituents are crucial; and
- the same constituents differently configured can support radically different causal powers.

That is a direct physicalist challenge to Griffin’s argument for dominant actualities and deserves at least as much space as the more dismissive “wish list” phrase. [Religion Online+1](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/)

### Chalmers 2003

The article correctly reports that Chalmers lists Griffin among type-F views. It also correctly states that Chalmers does **not** explicitly apply the high-level-type-F-to-type-D argument to Griffin. The article responsibly labels that application as the Map’s reading. [Consciousness.net+1](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

The problem is therefore not attribution but interpretation. Because Chalmers expressly allows type-D property dualism with only one entity type, his classification cannot be reduced to a disagreement with Griffin about ontological kinds. The article should describe Griffin as occupying the overlap between two orthogonal dimensions:

- intrinsic-nature monism or panexperientialism; and
- causally open higher-level interactionism.

### Griffin 1997

The statement that Griffin’s parapsychology book treats the nondualistic mind–brain distinction as making survival conceivable matches the publisher’s synopsis almost word for word, and the book’s contents include a chapter titled “The Mind–Body Relation and the Possibility of Life after Death.” [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Parapsychology_Philosophy_and_Spirituali.html?id=ImRKC5iov34C)

That makes the statement credible, but it is currently a **synopsis-level citation**, not a page-verified primary-text citation. The article should either:

- give the relevant chapter and page from the book;
- label the statement as the publisher’s description; or
- omit it until the underlying passage has been checked.

### Methodological observation

The changelog’s str.count procedure is useful for detecting fabricated or altered quotations, but exact string matching does not establish:

- that the quotation is responding to the work claimed;
- that an immediate qualification has not been omitted;
- that the page’s paraphrase respects the surrounding argument;
- that a publisher synopsis is being distinguished from authorial text; or
- that a quotation is doing the evidential work assigned to it.

The Kim and prehension cases illustrate why quotation verification requires a context window and attribution audit, not only string identity. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

## 4. Serious missing arguments and recent literature

### 4.1 The strongest omitted physicalist answer

Kim’s structure-and-determination response should be the first addition. At present, readers hear that physicalism needs a composition principle but not that physicalists deny Griffin’s inference from microstructural determination to the absence of novel macro-properties or causal powers. That omission makes Kim appear to offer only a burden-shifting challenge rather than a positive competing account. [Religion Online+1](https://www.religion-online.org/article/physicalism-and-panexperientialism-response-to-david-ray-griffin/)

The article should then ask the decisive question:

> What explanatory work does a dominant occasion perform that cannot instead be performed by a structurally organised system possessing novel but microstructurally determined causal powers?

Griffin may answer that such powers still cannot yield a unified subject or libertarian self-determination. But that answer must be made rather than assumed.

### 4.2 Individuation threatens circularity

Griffin says the classification of compound individuals is empirical and turns on whether behaviour seems to require a central agent. This risks circularity:

1. infer a dominant occasion because unified, spontaneous behaviour appears to require one;
2. explain the behaviour by invoking the dominant occasion.

The article should ask for independent criteria identifying:

- the spatial or functional boundary of the dominant occasion;
- its temporal duration;
- the conditions under which it divides or disappears;
- whether several dominant occasions can coexist;
- whether the agent criterion distinguishes genuine experience from merely integrated control.

Split-brain cases, anaesthesia transitions, distributed nervous systems and graded loss of consciousness are obvious pressure cases. They do not straightforwardly falsify Griffin, but they force the individuation rule to become more than “where a central agent seems required.”

### 4.3 Diachronic personal identity is underexamined

The article notes that Griffin’s mind is not numerically one through time but does not fully assess the cost. A series of occasions related by unusually intimate prehension may explain causal continuity without yet explaining why the successor is literally the same person rather than an intimately informed replacement.

This matters directly to the Map because its downstream substance commitment relies on a persisting subject. The disagreement should be framed not simply as “generated versus independent” but as:

- numerical persistence versus process continuity;
- ownership of earlier experiences;
- responsibility across successive occasions; and
- whether indexical identity survives replacement by momentary subjects.

### 4.4 Mental causation is not automatically explained by panexperientialism

Recent work challenges the familiar claim that Russellian panpsychism has a special advantage in explaining mental causation. Baysan argues that, on one reading, its explanation addresses the wrong phenomenon and, on another, analogous explanations are available to less revisionary competitors. That argument is not directed specifically at Griffin, but it bears directly on the article’s assumption that placing experience inside the causal order constitutes an explanatory advance. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10670-024-00816-5)

The relevant question for Griffin is:

> Does saying that the dominant occasion acts in virtue of its mental aspect explain mental causation, or merely redescribe the desired causal role in Whiteheadian vocabulary?

The same question should be asked of the Map’s “selection” language.

### 4.5 The recent combination literature complicates the article’s dilemma

The contemporary debate distinguishes subject combination, quality combination, structural combination and new-subject emergence. Siddharth’s 2024 treatment argues that proposed solutions often misunderstand the precise subject-composition problem, while Kadić’s monadic panpsychism attempts to avoid both subject combination and emergent macro-subjects by locating rich experience in one relationally constituted microsubject. Kadić’s model itself exposes individuation and dominance questions closely analogous to Griffin’s. [Hrčak+1](https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/320260)

These works matter for two reasons:

1. the Map should not treat “the combination problem” as one undifferentiated objection; and
2. Griffin is not the only nondual view that avoids literal subject summation.

The article’s “nearest rival” claim remains plausible under the Map’s selected axes—numerical mind–brain distinctness, irreducibility and reciprocal causation—but it should be labelled **metric-relative**, not presented as an unqualified ranking.

### 4.6 Dual-aspect monism is a more serious neighbour than the table suggests

Recent work by Hashemi develops Russellian panpsychism as dual-aspect monism and argues that such a view can offer a distinctive answer to causal exclusion. Whether successful or not, this is precisely the family against which the article’s assertion of a further dominant entity should be tested. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12136-024-00605-3)

The article briefly says Griffin rejects the brain “known from within” account because unity and agency require a further entity. It should develop the reply:

- Why can one organised entity not possess irreducible inner and outer aspects?
- Why does unity require numerical addition rather than aspectual unity?
- What evidence distinguishes “brain plus dominant occasion” from “one brain-process with two irreducible aspects”?

Without this, the article establishes Griffin’s preference but not the superiority of his numerically additive ontology.

### 4.7 Closure remains a live and more sophisticated debate

Recent closure literature does not reduce to choosing between strong microphysical closure and complete openness. Saad argues that a substantially weaker closure premise may still generate a causal problem for dualism. Wilson’s 2025 response to a newer causal argument, by contrast, disputes whether revised “physically acceptable” closure arguments secure their physicalist conclusions. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12136-024-00620-4)

The lesson for this article is methodological: Griffin and the Map do not escape exclusion merely by denying strong sufficient-cause closure. They must also explain why their higher-level causes are not redundant, screened off, epiphenomenal or instances of objectionable overdetermination.

### 4.8 The quantum graft is not cost-free

The article says a Whiteheadian could graft a Stapp-style quantum locus onto Griffin’s view. That is logically possible, but it is not a neutral implementation detail. It would raise questions about:

- whether quantum events replace or merely instantiate actual occasions;
- whether the causal efficacy resides in intrinsic appetition, measurement-context selection or outcome selection;
- whether Griffin’s ubiquitous process causation becomes localized to a special neural interface; and
- whether the graft converts the model into precisely the type-D microphysical opening that Griffin’s broader process metaphysics was intended to supersede.

The article should present the graft as a substantive hybrid theory rather than evidence that Griffin’s account is already compatible with the Map’s mechanism.

## 5. Is the Map protecting its tenets unfairly?

### What the article gets right

There is **no large-scale tenet-protective bracketing** here. The article makes several concessions that materially weaken the Map’s previous formulations:

- It says Griffin is not refuted.
- It concedes that Tenet 1 does not establish a difference in kind.
- It identifies Griffin as a counterexample to [P-D2](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d2)’s claim that bidirectional efficacy “selects dualism.”
- It says the composition burden has a structurally related counterpart for the Map.
- It treats Griffin’s relation to No Many Worlds as compatibility rather than deduction.
- It applies the Occam tenet symmetrically and admits that Kim’s “wish list” concern can also be directed at the Map. [nondualistic-interactionism](/concepts/nondualistic-interactionism/)

This is strong parity discipline, and the changelog’s later internal review correctly identifies it as such. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

### Residual asymmetry 1: “mechanism” versus “no mechanism”

The article sometimes describes the Map as possessing a quantum mechanism while treating Griffin’s prehension as merely leaving the mechanism unspecified. The Map’s own tenet page is more cautious: it says the quantum interface makes downward causation available without showing it actual, admits that no worked coupling model exists, and acknowledges that a Born-preserving selection process may lie close to epiphenomenalism. [tenets](/tenets/)

A symmetrical comparison would say:

- Griffin supplies a metaphysical causal relation and temporal architecture but no quantitative neural implementation.
- The Map supplies a proposed physical locus and outcome-selection constraint but no worked psychophysical coupling law.

Each is more specific along one dimension and underdeveloped along another.

### Residual asymmetry 2: Tenet 2 is treated as a standard rather than a commitment

The tenets page explicitly says the five tenets are chosen starting points rather than proven conclusions. Tenet 2 stipulates quantum-level, minimal, conservation-respecting influence. Griffin’s failure to satisfy it establishes distance from the Map, not an evidential defect in Griffin unless the article independently defends why mental causation must be quantum-located and minimal. [tenets](/tenets/)

The NDI article usually respects this distinction, but phrases such as “where Tenet 2’s rules-out clause forbids conservation-law violation outright, Griffin discounts the law” can make the contrast sound adjudicative. It is primarily a difference in commitments and empirical risk, not a demonstrated victory for the Map.

### Residual asymmetry 3: conservation is compressed on Griffin’s side

“Griffin discounts the law” is not false, but it compresses three positions:

1. universal conservation is not established beyond doubt;
2. a broader concept of energy might preserve conservation; and
3. if genuine freedom and universal conservation conflict, Griffin would surrender the latter.

Griffin presents both conservation-preserving and conservation-rejecting possibilities. [New Dualism Archive+1](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html)

The article should then apply the same burden to the Map: explaining how consciousness changes which event occurs without changing any conserved quantity is not equivalent to showing that a coherent, dynamically complete model exists.

### Residual asymmetry 4: “physical in every sense physics uses”

The claim that Griffin’s neurons are “physical in every sense physics uses” is too strong. Griffin’s point is that physics correctly characterizes their external, relational or physical poles while failing to exhaust their intrinsic actuality. A safer formulation is:

> Griffin’s neuronal occasions satisfy the structural and dispositional descriptions supplied by physics, while possessing an additional experiential pole not captured by those descriptions.

This matters because the existing phrase quietly makes Griffin look straightforwardly physical at the point where his redefinition of physicality is most controversial.

### Residual asymmetry 5: the Map’s persistence claim is not yet an earned advantage

The article contrasts Griffin’s brain-generated serial mind with the Map’s persisting subject. That identifies a genuine difference, but a persisting subject cannot simply be counted as an explanatory gain. It brings debts of its own:

- how it becomes coupled to one brain;
- why that coupling is stable;
- how memory and personality changes track neural changes;
- what individuates one subject from another; and
- how a non-generated subject enters or leaves the biological system.

The article acknowledges the coupling problem but could state more plainly that Griffin’s process-continuity debt and the Map’s enduring-subject coupling debt are rival explanatory costs, not one explained structure versus one unexplained structure.

### Residual asymmetry 6: the one-world comparison is framework-relative

The NDI article is appropriately cautious in calling Griffin a “natural single-world ally.” But definite concrescence is not itself an argument against Everettian quantum mechanics. It is a metaphysical model of actualisation that would need a separate bridge to the quantum formalism. The neighbouring process-philosophy article occasionally moves from compatibility to language implying that each concrescence produces the one outcome rather than branches, even while admitting Whitehead did not address quantum interpretations. [process-philosophy](/concepts/process-philosophy/)

Accordingly, Tenet 4 should be recorded as **compatible but unsupported by Griffin’s doctrine**, exactly as the NDI article mostly does.

## 6. Integration with neighbouring Map articles

### 6.1 Process Philosophy

Integration is conceptually close but textually inconsistent.

The process-philosophy page correctly describes actual occasions, personal order and dominant occasions, and it acknowledges that process metaphysics lacks a distinctive empirical prediction or detailed quantum mechanism. [process-philosophy](/concepts/process-philosophy/)

Three corrections are needed:

1. **“Consciousness isn’t generated by physical processes”** conflicts with Griffin’s explicit claim that each higher-level mind occasion arises from prior brain activity. The intended point is presumably that experience does not emerge from wholly nonexperiential matter. The page should say that rather than denying all generation by physical processes. [process-philosophy](/concepts/process-philosophy/)
2. **“Prehension as retrocausal inheritance”** is misleading or erroneous without a highly specialized definition. The same page correctly says that occasions prehend their predecessors. Ordinary Whiteheadian prehension is inheritance from the settled past, not backward causation from the future. [process-philosophy](/concepts/process-philosophy/)
3. The page presents process philosophy as dissolving the mind–matter distinction generally, whereas Griffin’s nondualistic interactionism retains a numerically distinct mind–brain relation. It should distinguish generic process monism from Griffin’s dominant-occasion variant.

The process page currently contains no explicit textual mention of nondualistic interactionism, despite using its central machinery. [process-philosophy](/concepts/process-philosophy/)

### 6.2 Panpsychism

The panpsychism page gives Whitehead useful coverage, but its dominant framing remains the familiar picture of electrons possessing micro-experiences that must somehow combine into a human subject. It does distinguish subject, quality and structural combination problems, yet it does not give Griffin’s nonconstitutive 101st-subject model a named place in the taxonomy. [panpsychism](/concepts/panpsychism/)

It should distinguish at least:

- constitutive micropsychism;
- emergent or nonconstitutive panpsychism;
- Whiteheadian dominant-occasion models;
- cosmopsychist derivation;
- monadic or relational non-combination models; and
- panprotopsychism.

That would prevent the NDI article from appearing as an isolated exception rather than one member of a broader family of attempts to avoid literal subject summation.

No reciprocal mention of nondualistic interactionism currently appears on the panpsychism page. [panpsychism](/concepts/panpsychism/)

### 6.3 Interactionist Dualism

The interactionist-dualism article opens by defining interactionism in terms of consciousness being nonphysical and ontologically distinct from the body. It later recognizes that interactionism is compatible with both property and substance dualism, but Griffin shows that even this remains too narrow: reciprocal mind–body causation does not by itself entail a difference in ontological kind. [interactionist-dualism](/concepts/interactionist-dualism/)

The taxonomy should separate:

- **interactionism:** reciprocal causal influence between mental and bodily processes;
- **dualistic interactionism:** interaction plus ontological difference in kind;
- **nondualistic interactionism:** interaction between numerically distinct same-kind experiential actualities.

That change should propagate to [P-D2](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d2). “Bidirectional Interaction selects dualism” should become **“Bidirectional Interaction selects interactionism.”** The additional move to dualism-in-kind should be attributed to the downstream persisting-subject and agency commitments.

No reciprocal mention of nondualistic interactionism currently appears in the interactionist-dualism article. [interactionist-dualism](/concepts/interactionist-dualism/)

### 6.4 Combination Problem

The combination-problem page has already improved its parity by saying the Map avoids the constitutive form while retaining an interface-unity burden. [combination-problem](/concepts/combination-problem/)

It still says that process philosophers propose that experiential drops “compound naturally.” That is too undifferentiated. Griffin’s own position is precisely that the macro-subject is **not** the identity or sum of the lower subjects; it is a new dominant actuality. The page should add a subsection distinguishing:

- subject summation;
- phenomenal bonding;
- new-subject emergence;
- dominant-subject selection; and
- interface unity.

There is currently no explicit reciprocal mention of nondualistic interactionism on the combination page. [combination-problem](/concepts/combination-problem/)

### 6.5 Apex integration

The 20 October change to *Process and Consciousness* was a genuine integration improvement: it replaced the claim that interactionist dualism avoids the combination problem entirely with the more accurate concession that it avoids the panpsychist form while owing a structurally related coupling account. It also added the apex article’s first link to NDI. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

The integration problem is therefore now mainly **hub-to-spoke rather than spoke-to-hub**: NDI links outward extensively and the apex now links inward, but the core process, panpsychism, combination and interactionism concept pages have not yet been reciprocally updated.

## Final judgment

The article’s central achievement is genuine: it exposes a taxonomic failure in the Map’s previous inference from irreducibility plus bidirectional causation to dualism-in-kind. It represents Griffin’s basic doctrine accurately and does not pretend to refute it. That is exactly the role a serious nearest-rival article should play.

Its principal revision should be to stop treating “physical closure,” “physics completeness,” “microphysical sufficiency,” “predictability” and “ontological monism” as variations of one axis. Once these are separated, Griffin appears not as someone who simply “keeps closure” while rejecting completeness, but as someone who preserves a broadened physical domain while rejecting the closure principles normally used by physicalists. That also reveals why Chalmers can classify the causal architecture as type-D without thereby accusing Griffin of Cartesian substance dualism.

There is little outright tenet-protective bracketing. The remaining bias is subtler: the Map’s quantum proposal is occasionally credited as a mechanism despite its admitted lack of a worked coupling law, while Griffin’s prehensive architecture is treated as merely naming a relation. Both should be evaluated under the same questions of individuation, causal law, physical implementation and empirical discrimination.
