## Executive Summary

The quotations on this page are accurate wherever the source could be fetched: 24 quoted spans were checked and all match. The defects come from the one source the page did not read. Oliver Sacks's "Stereo Sue" (*The New Yorker*, 2006) is free to read, is the primary record of the case, and changes three things the page says. Before she gained stereopsis Barry told Sacks she *could* imagine it; she herself offered the grey-to-colour analogy that the page credits to a 2024 blogger; and Sacks rejected that analogy in the same essay. ## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would accept the page's own verdict that dualism gains nothing, and then ask why the case is filed as a void. Barry lacked binocular cortical processing; training supplied it; the report changed. The interesting datum, which the page omits, is that a professor of neurobiology believed her textbook knowledge gave her "special insight" into the missing state and was wrong. Churchland reads that as evidence about how poorly folk self-assessment tracks one's own representational repertoire. On her reading nothing here is closed to inquiry.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would press the forward face on its evidence. The only verbatim "couldn't imagine" on the page dates from a lecture at age twenty, before Barry had read anything. Her only recorded pre-acquisition report as an expert is that she could imagine it. The claim that she could not is a verdict delivered after the change, about a state the page itself says she "can no longer inspect". So the forward face rests on the kind of retrospective judgement that the backward face treats as unreliable. Dennett's standing challenge, raised by Pete Mandik in the 2006 Brains Blog thread on this very case, is that nobody has given an argument that the experience is required for the knowledge. Mandik also offers a candidate route across: wiggle stereograms viewed without noticing the wiggle.

### The Quantum Skeptic
Nothing to attack. The page says three of the five tenets "have no bearing", and that is correct.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Likewise nothing to attack; the page makes no indexical or interpretive claim.

### The Empiricist
A Popperian would credit the page for naming a test (Schwitzgebel's catch-trial design) and then note two places where it declares the ground unexplored without looking far. "The Map's research found no study of it" is false for the aperture question: Zlatkute's 2019 thesis ran two experiments on it. And the forward face counts Bridgeman as a second case of a quality unavailable in advance, though his paper's abstract reports a Wirt threshold of 200 arcsec *before* the film. He had coarse stereopsis and it improved. One case remains, not two.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would observe that "stereopsis" is treated in the forward face as a single thing one either has or lacks, while the definition face and the Chopin abstract ("not a unitary construct") say it is dependently designated. Barry recalled a blade of grass standing out in childhood; Bridgeman had 200 arcsec; Sacks's own stereopsis went flat beyond a few feet after three weeks in a small room. On the page's own sources the binary that the forward face needs is a convention laid over a graded capacity.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The page omits Barry's pre-acquisition report, which was that she could imagine stereopsis
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/stereopsis-void.md`
- **Location**: lead (L37) "reports that she could not picture what she lacked until she gained stereopsis at 48"; forward face (L45, L47) "I couldn’t imagine what he was talking about" and "Sacks's reply, as Krulwich gives it, was a question".
- **Problem**: The verbatim "couldn't imagine" is from the lecture at twenty and precedes her study, as the page's own next sentence shows. Krulwich gives Sacks's question and not her answer. Sacks (2006) gives the answer: "Sue said she thought she could—after all, she was a professor of neurobiology, and she had read plenty of papers on visual processing, binocular vision, and stereopsis. She felt this knowledge had given her some special insight into what she was missing—she knew what stereopsis must be like, even if she had never experienced it." Nine years later she wrote: "You asked me if I could imagine what the world would look like when viewed with two eyes. I told you that I thought I could. . . . But I was wrong." The page's own reference 8 (Popova) says the same in paraphrase ("Barry was at first certain she could"), and the research note logged it at L94 and set it aside. The record is therefore: at twenty, could not imagine; as a professor, believed she could; after acquisition, judged that belief wrong. The page reports the first and third and drops the second. The second is the strongest evidence for the page's own [inventory-blindness](/concepts/inventory-blindness/) reading, and it is also what exposes the forward face to the Dennett objection above.
- **Second omission, same source**: the lead says Barry "was stereoblind from infancy". Sacks records that, asked whether she had ever seen in three dimensions, she "answered yes, perhaps—very occasionally, as a child, lying in the grass, she might suddenly see a blade of grass stand out from its background", and he concludes she "may have had a few brief and rare stereo experiences in early life, but there was no way to be certain of this."
- **Internal tension**: L57 says "Once across, the person can no longer inspect the earlier state". The forward face's evidence that prior imagining fell short is a judgement made once across.
- **Severity**: High (the central case is reported from popular paraphrase while the free primary source says something more specific and less convenient).
- **Recommendation**: add the pre-acquisition answer and the "But I was wrong" letter from Sacks (2006); say plainly that the shortfall is a retrospective verdict; qualify "from infancy". Two host sentences repeat the simplified claim and need the same correction (Priority List item 1).

### Issue 2: The Mary comparison is credited to the wrong originator, and Sacks's rejection of it is missing
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/stereopsis-void.md`
- **Location**: L55 "The comparison with Mary belongs to Maria Popova (2024), who writes that Barry had "been living in Mary’s Room". The Map's research did not find Barry or Sacks making it." Also L41 "is a close real-life relative of Mary".
- **Problem**: Three things in the record contradict this paragraph. (a) Barry made the comparison in substance in her first letter to Sacks. Sacks (2006): "Sue had suggested, in her original letter to me, that her experience might be akin to that of someone born totally color-blind, able to see only in shades of gray, who is suddenly given the ability to see in full color." Popova quotes the same letter in the page's own reference 8 ("Imagine a person who saw only in shades of gray suddenly able to see in full color"). (b) Sacks rejected it: "While I liked the poetry of Sue's analogy, I disagreed with the thought ... With color, there is no precursor—we either see a world of color or we do not—but all of us live and move in a three-dimensional world." (c) The explicit link to Jackson's Mary was made in October 2006, eighteen years before Popova, by Tony Dardis on the Brains Blog ("One guesses that Sacks has philosophically famous Mary in mind"), with Ken Aizawa and Pete Mandik discussing the knowledge argument in the comments. "Belongs to" is a priority claim and it is wrong. The stronger consequence is for L41: the neurologist who documented the case argued that depth, unlike colour, had a precursor in Barry's experience, and that argument cuts against "close" in "close real-life relative".
- **Severity**: High (wrong originator; a sourced disanalogy from the case's primary author is absent from a page whose lead leans on the analogy).
- **Recommendation**: rewrite L55 to give Barry's own analogy, Sacks's reply and the 2006 Dardis post, keeping Popova for the phrase "Mary's Room". Soften L41 to "a real-life relative" and state the precursor disanalogy in one clause.

### Issue 3: Bridgeman was stereo-deficient with measurable stereopsis, so the forward face has one case
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/stereopsis-void.md`
- **Location**: L51 "A second vision scientist reports a similar surprise."; L57 "From the stereoblind position this face behaves as unexplorable by imagination, testimony and study, on the evidence of these two cases."; L41 "The evidence from surprised experts is two people, both reporting on themselves."
- **Problem**: The page says it has not read Bridgeman (2014), and it reads Chopin et al. (2019) at abstract level. Bridgeman's abstract is equally available and says: "the author acquired improved stereoscopic vision"; "had been deficient in stereo vision all of his life"; "the Wirt stereo threshold decreased from 200 to 80 arcsec, and stereoscopic vision became a vivid experience." A 200 arcsec threshold is measurable stereopsis. Levi et al., which the page quotes, also say "stereo deficient", never stereoblind. Bridgeman's surprise is real and belongs on the page, but he is evidence about a change of degree. He cannot stand as evidence "from the stereoblind position".
- **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: one added sentence giving the abstract's figures, and rescope L57 and L41 so the stereoblind claim rests on Barry alone.

### Issue 4: "The Map's research found no study of it" — a study exists, from the department the theory came from
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/stereopsis-void.md`
- **Location**: L75 "whether a stereoblind person could have a version of it through an aperture is an empirical question, and the Map's research found no study of it."
- **Problem**: Giedre Zlatkute, *Exploring depth and distance perception in strabismus* (PhD thesis, University of St Andrews, 2019, doi:10.17630/10023-18959). Its overall conclusion bears on the whole page: "there are more similarities between these two groups in the perception of 3D space than suggested by anecdotal reports and conjecture." The hedge "the Map's research found" keeps the sentence literally true, but the residue the page files as "unexplored" has been explored, with a result that favours the definition face over the forward face.
- **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: replace the "no study" clause with the thesis finding at abstract level, flagged as abstract-only, and note that it does not say whether fully stereoblind participants were among those who reported the impression.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The precursor objection (Sacks 2006; Mandik 2006)
- **Current content says**: Barry is "a close real-life relative of Mary", with the only stated disanalogy being that her knowledge was incomplete.
- **A critic would argue**: Mary has no colour experience of any kind. Barry had depth experience from occlusion, perspective, motion parallax and touch, and possibly fleeting stereo in childhood. Sacks: "a three-dimensional world was already a perceptual reality for her". Mandik: visual and tactile experiences of space are subtypes of spatial experience, so the stereoblind "can know what it's like (partially) to see depth". The case is nearer to Hume's missing shade than to Mary.
- **Suggested response**: state the disanalogy and keep the weaker claim. Aizawa's reply in the same thread is available as the counter: there is plausibly something it is like to see *in stereo* that differs from seeing depth by other cues, which is Vishwanath's distinction and already the page's definition face.

### The retrospective-verdict objection
- **Current content says**: knowing about it did not supply it.
- **A critic would argue**: the evidence is her later judgement that her earlier imagining was inadequate, and the page elsewhere says the earlier state cannot be inspected after crossing.
- **Suggested response**: concede it. The astonishment at acquisition ("to my astonishment", "I had no idea what I had been missing") is contemporaneous with the change and does not depend on inspecting the earlier state; rest the face on that and present "But I was wrong" as her verdict.

### Sacks is presented as one pole of a dispute he had already described
- **Current content says**: "Published reports conflict", with Sacks holding that depth is seen only binocularly.
- **A critic would argue**: Sacks (2006) wrote: "While most people may not notice any great change if they close one eye, we stereophiles are sharply conscious of the change ... Perhaps we rely more on stereoscopy, or perhaps we are simply more aware of it." That is the PuffOuter/StaySamer split with two candidate explanations, five years before Schwitzgebel named it. Schwitzgebel himself notes that "Sacks acknowledges the possibility of some individual variation". The same essay also supplies a long-term case for the backward face that the page lacks: Paul Romano, an ophthalmologist who lost stereopsis for nine months and wrote of being "reduced to a two-dimensional world".
- **Suggested response**: optional enrichment; one clause crediting Sacks with the observation would be enough.
