## TL;DR (blunt verdict)

**REVISE-HARD.** The article's central move — disambiguating open individualism (an identity thesis) from cosmopsychism's de-combination problem (a grounding problem) — is correct, genuinely useful, and better executed than most of this corpus; its load-bearing citations (Kolak, Zuboff, Miller 2018, Chalmers 2017, Shani, Nagasawa & Wager, Goff, Albahari) are real and, where quoted, verbatim-accurate. But four defects are disqualifying for a page presented as a settled bridge article: (1) it misattributes the "derivation problem" label to the SEP, which in fact calls it the "decombination problem" and credits Albahari — "derivation problem" is Nagasawa & Wager's own coinage; (2) it softens Gregory Miller into a skeptical "contested-verdict" witness for the Map when Miller is a constructive optimist who thinks subject-subject parthood is coherent and the problem soluble, and it omits his 2021 paper — the single most on-point work in the literature for the article's priority-monism claims; (3) it omits the entire physicalist rival wing (IIT's exclusion postulate, GWT, predictive processing, illusionism), so the "the Map structurally avoids both problems" boast is tested only against opponents who already grant phenomenal realism; and (4) it rests both its anti-MWI and anti-open-individualism rejections on the same undefended haecceity posit without noticing the doubled dependency, and glosses its interaction problem as "empirically approachable" at exactly the locus where the Map's own unresolved Born-rule problem sits.

- The claimed structural asymmetry (Map avoids composition on both ends) is largely **earned by construction**, not smuggled — and the article honestly concedes the interaction-problem cost. This is the article's strongest dimension.
- The fatal weaknesses are **counterargument coverage** (no physicalist rival engaged at all; the strongest anti-symmetry cosmopsychist argument ducked) and **bibliographic currency** (nothing from the 2021–2024 de-combination literature that bears directly on the thesis).
- Open individualism, if true, would collapse the haecceity posit that the No-Many-Worlds tenet *also* depends on. The article half-notices this and does not follow the thread — the most important thing it fails to say.

## Dimensional Analysis

### Dimension 1 — Argumentative Support: is the asymmetry EARNED?

The first thing to establish is that the article does **not** claim an asymmetry between de-combination and combination — and is right not to. Following Miller and Chalmers, it explicitly asserts **symmetry**: de-combination and combination are "the same puzzle approached from opposite ends," "cousins, not twins," "two faces of the same underlying question — *how does one become many?*" This is faithful to the primary literature. Miller (2018) states the de-combination problem "is equivalent to the combination problem" (verbatim, *Ratio* 31(2):137–154); Chalmers (2017) writes that "the resulting decomposition problem seems just as hard as the combination problem" (verbatim, consc.net full text). The article reproduces both accurately. [PhilArchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/MILCSB-2)[Consc](https://consc.net/papers/combination.pdf)

The genuinely load-bearing asymmetry claim lies elsewhere, and this is where scrutiny bites. The article argues that the Map "structurally avoids" **both** combination and de-combination because interactionist dualism "posits neither micro-experience nor a single cosmic field, so there is nothing to combine or de-combine." This is **earned by construction, not stipulated**: it is analytically true that a view positing no phenomenal parts and no phenomenal whole faces no phenomenal-composition problem. It is not coherence inflation; it is a definitional consequence of substance dualism. The article could in fact have strengthened it with a citation it omits — Chalmers (2017) himself concedes the point directly: "By contrast, dualism does not suffer nearly as badly from a combination problem. This is especially clear for substance dualism, which postulates fundamental entities (subjects of experience) that bear macrophenomenal properties. There is no analog of the subject combination problem for such a view" (consc.net/papers/panpsychism.pdf). A rare case in this corpus where the Map *undersold* a claim its own rejected authority would have backed.

Where the article slides is the **epistemic-to-metaphysical / constrain-vs-establish** boundary in one specific sentence: "the interaction problem is at least *empirically approachable* — it concerns a locus (quantum indeterminacy) where physics leaves outcomes undetermined — whereas combination and de-combination are *conceptual* problems." This trades a conceptual mystery for an empirical one and quietly scores it as progress. To its credit, the article immediately hedges: "Whether trading a conceptual mystery for an empirical one counts as genuine progress is a question the Map acknowledges rather than presumes to have settled." That is confession-with-partial-correction, and it is better than the corpus norm. But the confession is incomplete: the "locus (quantum indeterminacy)" where the interaction is claimed to be empirically approachable is precisely where the Map's own **Born-rule / statistical-indistinguishability problem** lives — minimal quantum influence must not perturb the Born statistics, and the changelog shows an unresolved multi-dossier research effort on exactly this (`born-rule-derivation-attempts`, `probability-decision-theory-against-many-worlds`). "Empirically approachable" is doing quiet rhetorical work that the Map's own unsolved problem undercuts, and the article neither cross-links those dossiers nor flags the tension.

**Verdict on Dimension 1: mostly earned.** The structural-avoidance claim is legitimate; the "empirically approachable" gloss is an unadvertised liability.

**Two integrity defects:**

1. **SEP terminology misattribution (High).** The article states: "The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* frames it as the *derivation problem*." This is false. The current (Spring 2026) SEP *Panpsychism* supplement, "Possible Solutions to the Subject-Summing Problem," reads verbatim: "they arguably face an analogous problem, which Miri Albahari (2020) calls 'the decombination problem': in virtue of what do 'big' conscious subjects (such as the universe) ground 'little' conscious subjects (such as us)?" "Derivation problem" is instead **Nagasawa & Wager's** own coinage — verbatim from their chapter: "Instead of the combination problem, however, priority cosmopsychism faces the derivation problem." The article has crossed two distinct sources' terminology and hung the wrong label on the wrong authority. (Note that the SEP's "(2020)" also corrects the article's Albahari year.)
2. **Miller stance-softening / selective omission (High).** The article enrolls Miller for the verdict that "none of these has clearly succeeded: cosmopsychism has *relocated* the combination problem rather than eliminating it," and notes his two symmetric responses. It then stops, and thereby inverts his dialectical posture. Miller (2018) does not conclude the problem is unsolvable; he **proposes a solution he endorses** — the absolute/relative phenomenal-unity distinction (denying his "Unity/Boundedness Inconsistency Thesis"), which he says "should be adopted" and which lets "both … the cosmopsychist [and] the panpsychist … avoid" the reductio. His PhD project is titled a *Constitutive Russellian Solution* and argues "we can indeed be composite subjects." Miller is a constructive optimist about subject-subject parthood, not a skeptic delivering an "unsettled, contested" verdict for the Map's benefit. Presenting him as the latter is a soft co-optation: a naturalistically-minded composite-subjectivity theorist recruited to support a "the debate is unsettled, so the Map's exit looks better" conclusion he does not hold. [University of Liverpool Repository](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3030931/1/200647716_Sep2018.pdf)

**Stale / missing seminal works (High, cumulative).** For an article "last substantively revised 22 July 2026" whose entire subject is the de-combination problem, the bibliography effectively stops in 2019:

- **Miller (2021), "The Decombination Problem for Cosmopsychism is not the Heterogeneity Problem for Priority Monism," *JCS* 28(3-4):112–115** — omitted. This is the single most on-point paper for the article's own claim about Nagasawa & Wager's priority route; its verified thesis (abstract, verbatim): "They are not the same problem and the solutions to the heterogeneity problem do not work for the de-combination problem." Its absence is the bibliography's worst hole.

In particular, what renders CP insolvable, namely, the commitment to the coupling of phenomenal constitution with phenomenal inclusion, is, from the standpoint of cosmopsychism, an entirely nonmandatory assumption."

No phantom (fabricated) citations were found. The defects are misattribution, stance-softening, and staleness — not invention.

### Dimension 3 — Counterargument Coverage (the article's worst dimension)

**(a) Closed individualism.** The Map declares itself closed-individualist but does not defend the position: it engages neither Olson's animalism, Schechtman's narrative view, nor the psychological-continuity tradition, and concedes the boundary's ground is "a void the Map does not claim to have filled." Asserting one's own position while deferring its defense to a void is tenet-protective bracketing, not argument.

**(b) Empty individualism.** Parfit is disposed of in a single clause (filed under Kolak's "empty" bin); Zuboff is handled well. But the sharpest empty-individualist objection goes unstated: the Map's primitive haecceity is precisely the "deep further fact" Parfit denies, and Sider-style stage theory / momentary-subject views are never mentioned. The article never lets the reductionist press on the very posit the Map most needs.

**(c) Reductive physicalism — wholly absent, and this is the decisive gap.** The article's dialectic runs entirely inside the panpsychist/idealist family. There is no mention of:

- **IIT's exclusion postulate**, which is directly germane: IIT forbids overlapping conscious subjects (only the local maximum of Φ is a subject — verified at Scholarpedia/IEP and IIT 4.0), which is a *physicalist* answer to the individuation/de-combination boundary problem the article treats as intractable. The article should have to say why the Map's boundary-void is preferable to IIT's exclusion-by-fiat.
- **Global Workspace Theory, higher-order theories, and predictive processing / active inference** (Friston–Clark–Seth–Hohwy) — the dominant physicalist research program on the unity and boundaries of consciousness, entirely unengaged.
- **Illusionism (Frankish, Dennett).** This is the strongest rival to the whole framing: if there are no phenomenal subjects, then combination, de-combination, *and* the Map's interaction problem all dissolve together. The article assumes phenomenal realism throughout and never earns it. Engaging the *convenient* opponents (fellow subject-realists) while ignoring the one who denies the shared premise is a missing-strongest-rival failure.

**(d) Priority-monist cosmopsychism.** The article lists Nagasawa & Wager, Shani, and Shani & Keppler as strategies that "have not clearly succeeded," but engages only the weak reading (the strategies fail to dissolve a symmetric problem). It never confronts the strong reading: that priority monism's ontologically-prior whole may not need a de-combination *solution at all* because local subjects are grounded *derivatives*, not *proper parts* — so the mirror-image assumption is itself the mistake. This is precisely Shani's (2022) claim that the mirror-image view "is mistaken" and that the constitution/inclusion coupling is "nonmandatory," and it is precisely the transfer Miller (2021) adjudicates (arguing it fails, which would have *helped* the Map's symmetry thesis while demonstrating the article had done the reading). Ducking both the strongest pro-cosmopsychist argument and the paper that answers it is the coverage failure that most damages the article's credibility with an expert reader.

### Dimension 4 — Tenet-Protective Bracketing

This dimension is, surprisingly, the article's second-strongest — calibration is above corpus norm — but it contains one deep unforced error.

**Well-handled:** The rejection of open individualism is marked honestly as framework-boundary disagreement rather than refutation: "the rejection is honest engagement at a framework boundary, not a refutation from inside open individualism's own resources," and the striking concession that "the Map's closed-individualist commitment is perhaps best understood as tenet-driven rather than empirically compelled; an open individualist could read the same first-person data the other way without contradiction." That is confession *with* correction — the claim is demoted to tenet-driven in place. Good, and consistent with the "concede-then-locate" discipline the changelog shows the site now applying to its No-MWI claims elsewhere.

**The deep error the article does not notice:** The Map rejects **both** many-worlds and open individualism using the **same** move — primitive haecceity / indexical thisness. The No-Many-Worlds tenet's load-bearing argument is the indexical objection ("why am I *this* branch?"), which "is meaningful only on a non-deflationary conception of the subject, an 'I' whose thisness picks out a determinate this-one." Open individualism denies exactly that thisness. So the same undefended posit does double duty across two tenets, and **if open individualism is right that felt this-ness is perspectival illusion, then both the anti-OI argument and the entire No-MWI tenet collapse together.** The article gets within one sentence of this ("The felt this-ness is compatible with both readings … neither reading is forced by the phenomenology alone") but does not draw the conclusion: its rejection of open individualism is not independent evidence against it — it is the same bet as No-MWI, staked twice. An open individualist who is *also* a dualist (a live and coherent combination) would accept the Map's ontology of a non-physical mind while rejecting its individuation and its MWI argument wholesale. The article never asks whether open individualism threatens the *dualism* rather than merely competing on *identity* — the question most worth answering.

**Occam handling:** Clean. The article correctly refuses to reject the rivals on parsimony grounds, noting Tenet 5 "binds the Map's own use of parsimony as tightly as its critics'." No bounded-Occam asymmetry abuse.

**Claude-on-Claude circularity:** References 11 and 12 are the Map's own AI-authored articles (Southgate & "Oquatre-sept"/"Oquatre-huit," C.) cited as sources. Given that the entire corpus is Claude-generated and Claude-reviewed (per the changelog), using self-citations as load-bearing references without flagging their status is an unaddressed circular-evaluation problem. Low severity here (they are cross-references, not evidential authorities), but it should be marked.

## Bottom-Line Verdict

**REVISE-HARD.**

Not RETAIN: the SEP misattribution, the Miller stance-softening, the ~2019 bibliographic cutoff, and the total absence of the physicalist rival wing are too many load-bearing defects for a page presented as a settled bridge article. Not DEMOTE-TO-COHERENCE-ONLY or DELETE: the core disambiguation is genuinely correct and a real contribution — conflating open individualism with the de-combination problem is a common error, and the article dispels it cleanly with real, accurately-quoted primary sources; the structural-avoidance argument is valid; and the Dimension-4 calibration is above corpus average. The article is fixable without demolition. FLAG-AS-PERPETUALLY-CONTESTED is inappropriate: the disputes here are tractable literature-coverage and attribution problems, not bedrock stalemates.

## Article-Specific Fixes (keyed to slug and passage)

1. **`/topics/open-individualism-and-the-de-combination-problem/`, §"The De-Combination Problem," sentence "The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* frames it as the *derivation problem*."** Correct to: SEP calls it "the decombination problem" (crediting "Miri Albahari (2020)"); "derivation problem" is Nagasawa & Wager's term ("Instead of the combination problem, however, priority cosmopsychism faces the derivation problem"). Split the two attributions cleanly.
2. **Same section, §"The Cosmopsychist Replies" and the "critical verdict" paragraph.** Stop presenting Miller as delivering an "unsettled/relocated" verdict. State his actual position: the problems are equivalent *and* he proposes a solution he endorses (the absolute/relative phenomenal-unity distinction) available to both camps. Add his 2021 escalation — that the de-combination problem is not reducible to, and worse than, the heterogeneity problem: "They are not the same problem and the solutions to the heterogeneity problem do not work for the de-combination problem."
3. **Add Miller (2021), *JCS* 28(3-4):112–115 to References and cite it in the priority-cosmopsychism bullet (Nagasawa & Wager).** It is the definitive treatment of whether Schaffer's heterogeneity solutions transfer to cosmopsychism; its omission is the bibliography's worst hole.
4. **§"The Cosmopsychist Replies," the Nagasawa & Wager bullet.** Engage the strong anti-symmetry reading with Shani (2022, *The Monist* 105(1):6–24, DOI 10.1093/monist/onab020): "the widespread tendency to view IND as a mirror-image of micropsychism's combination problem … is mistaken … [the coupling that renders CP insolvable] is, from the standpoint of cosmopsychism, an entirely nonmandatory assumption." Do not leave symmetry asserted as settled; the mirror-image thesis is itself the contested claim.
5. **§"Relation to Site Perspective," Dualism paragraph, the "empirically approachable" sentence.** Cross-link the Map's own unresolved Born-rule / statistical-indistinguishability dossiers and concede that the "empirically approachable" locus is exactly where the Map's own unsolved quantum problem sits. Otherwise the concession is cosmetic.
6. **§"Relation to Site Perspective," No-Many-Worlds paragraph.** Add the doubled-dependency observation: the anti-OI and anti-MWI rejections both rest on primitive haecceity, so the OI rejection is not independent evidence and both tenets fall together if thisness is illusory. State whether open individualism threatens the *dualism* or only the *individuation* — the coherent open-individualist-dualist position shows it may threaten more than the article admits.
7. **Add a physicalist-rival paragraph.** At minimum: IIT's exclusion postulate as a physicalist answer to the boundary problem; and illusionism (Frankish/Dennett) as the rival that dissolves combination, de-combination, and the interaction problem together by denying the shared phenomenal-realist premise. Say why the Map's void is preferable.
8. **Strengthen the structural-avoidance claim with Chalmers (2017)'s own concession:** "dualism does not suffer nearly as badly from a combination problem. This is especially clear for substance dualism … There is no analog of the subject combination problem for such a view." Genuine primary-source support the article currently leaves on the table.
9. **Harmonize the Albahari year (2019 → 2020) to the SEP-preferred date**, and flag References 11–12 as internal AI-authored cross-references rather than independent sources.

## Site-Wide Methodology Improvements

1. **Terminology-provenance linter.** Add an automated check that any sentence of the form "[Named authority] calls/frames it as [term]" is verified against that authority's own text. The SEP/"derivation problem" error is a class defect: term genealogies (derivation = decombination = decomposition) get cross-wired to the wrong source. The author-stance ledger should carry a companion **term-attribution ledger**.
2. **Constructive-vs-critical stance field in the author-stance ledger.** The Miller failure is not fabrication or crude inversion but *stance-softening*: a constructive theorist cited only for his destructive half. Extend the co-optation firewall to flag any author cited solely for a problem-statement when that same author proposes an *endorsed solution* in the same work — force the review to record "does the cited author think this problem is soluble?" as a field distinct from "does the work exist / is it quoted correctly?"
3. **Same-named-problem currency SLA.** An article whose subject *is* a named problem (the de-combination problem) should be gated against the last five years of literature on that exact problem before it can read as "settled." Miller 2021, Shani 2022, Petersen 2021, and Mørch 2024 are all directly on-topic and post-date the bibliography. Add a "same-named-problem recency" trigger distinct from the general citation-age trigger the changelog already contemplates.
4. **Missing-strongest-rival linter: mandatory cross-paradigm check.** This article engaged only intra-subject-realist opponents. For any article claiming a framework "avoids" a problem, require at least one rival from *each* major paradigm the Map opposes (here: a physicalist theory — IIT/GWT/predictive processing — and an eliminativist/illusionist option). "Avoids problem X" claims are especially prone to being tested only against opponents who share the disputed premise.
5. **Doubled-posit dependency tracker.** The haecceity posit silently underwrites both the No-MWI tenet and the anti-open-individualism rejection. A per-tenet dependency graph should surface when a single undefended posit is load-bearing for two or more tenets/rejections, so "independent" arguments are not double-counted — the same discipline the changelog's Posit-Three / global-exclusion work applied to the agency route, generalized.
6. **Born-rule exposure flag.** Any article invoking "quantum indeterminacy" or "the interface" as *empirically approachable* should be required to cross-link the Map's open Born-rule / statistical-indistinguishability research and register it as an unresolved cost, to stop "empirically approachable" doing unearned rhetorical work across the corpus.
7. **Self-citation provenance marking.** Internal AI-authored articles used as references (the Oquatre-N pseudonym convention) should be automatically labeled as such in the reference list, so the Claude-on-Claude circularity is visible to readers and cannot be mistaken for independent corroboration.
