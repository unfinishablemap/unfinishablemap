## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would start at L62. "This felt weight doesn't reduce to probability estimates or preference rankings; it's an experiential quality guiding attention during choice." That is asserted, not argued, and the guiding role it names is the one neuroeconomics already models. In Krajbich, Armel and Rangel's (2010) attentional drift-diffusion model, "fixations guide the comparison process" between item values (PubMed 20835253, abstract). The Map can hold that the *felt* character does not reduce (Tenet 1). It cannot also say the *guiding* does not reduce without arguing it. She would add that L115 concedes her point: the choosing/observing contrast "maps onto the neural distinction between willed and automatic processing". A contrast that maps onto neural categories is something a neural account can absorb.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett would agree with every row of the L108–L113 table. Choosing does differ from observing, because in choosing the agent's own deliberative processes are among the causes. A compatibilist says that too, so the table discriminates nothing. His real target is L143–L147.
- **The dilemma at L145.** "Either it has phenomenal character (relocating the problem) or it doesn't (mysterious why it's compelling)." The second horn is the illusion problem, which illusionists take on as a research programme. Calling it "mysterious" assumes it cannot be solved.
- **"Four independent tricks" at L147.** No illusionist posits four tricks. One self-model reading the brain's own control signals yields initiation, sustained control, effort and veto together. Its calibration to demand comes from the signals it reads.
- **Wegner at L141.** Wegner is not an illusionist about phenomenology. "The experience of willing an act arises from interpreting one's thought as the cause of the act" (Wegner & Wheatley 1999, abstract). He grants the experience and denies its authority about causes. The "what is the seeming?" dilemma therefore does not touch him.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark would read L163. "The iterative character of sustained voluntary attention — James's 'successive efforts' — matches consciousness repeatedly selecting among competing neural patterns at points of quantum indeterminacy." James's renewals come every few seconds; decoherence is 10⁻¹³ s or faster. Any iterative control process "matches" a renewal rhythm, so the fit costs nothing and shows nothing. "Choosing feels like biasing outcomes, not injecting energy" (L163) offers a feeling as support for a tenet about empirical constraint. No phenomenology can tell a tiny energy injection from a bias. The spoke says so: `self-control-as-skill-and-scaffolding` L106 calls the economy "not the empirical-constraint minimality the tenet names". The Map's endorsed post-decoherence path (tenets L91) does sidestep the coherence-time objection. But the hub does not say which path it means.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch would read L165. "Choice phenomenology presupposes singular actualisation. You experience A as *the* outcome, not one branch among equals." On Everett, each post-branching agent experiences exactly one outcome as *the* outcome, and feels openness before and closure after. The phenomenology is identical, so it cannot presuppose collapse. The tenets matrix says as much: rejecting MWI needs "the further global-exclusion posit—branch-relative agency is otherwise available" (tenets L170). The Map may reject MWI, but not on this datum. Under the Map's own discipline, "presupposes" should be "fits".

### The Empiricist

The Popperian goes to L155.
- **(3), "effort phenomenology decoupled from cognitive load", is already instantiated.** Naccache et al.'s (2005) patient "experienced no conscious feeling of mental effort and showed no SCR, in spite of exhibiting normal executive control" (PubMed 15949516). The Map's effort spoke files that case as "interface asymmetry — phenomenal access is not identical to the underlying operation, as the interface model predicts" (`mental-effort` L92). A falsifier the standing reply absorbs is decorative and is struck (`project/evidential-status-discipline`, The Decorative-Falsifier Rule).
- **(4), meditative dissolution, has a symmetry problem.** L151 concedes that sharpening under practice is "consonant ... rather than probative", because a self-model trained on a tradition's vocabulary would sharpen too. By the same token, a self-model trained on a no-doer vocabulary would dissolve the distinction, and the Map could absorb that. *Lead only*: I have not checked whether any page already gives that reply.
- **(5), "100% accuracy at timescales ruling out conscious contribution", is unreachable in practice.** It is not absorbed, so it stays, but it carries no weight.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would take L135–L137 at its word. "Advanced meditators describe watching decisions arise before identifying with them — intention appears, followed by a distinct moment of 'signing off.'" On the Abhidharma reading the article cites, the "signing off" is one more conditioned mental event (cetanā), not an agent outside the stream. The observation supports dependent arising at least as well as a selector. L137's "the witness must still steer a little, or it could not cause its own reports" brings in Tenet 3 as a premise inside an argument meant to bear on Tenet 3. L135 also lists "Buddhist, Tibetan, and Hindu traditions" as convergent witnesses. Tibetan contemplative practice is Buddhist, and all three report through introspection, one instrument (`apex/phenomenology-of-consciousness-doing-work` L149).

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Libet's veto misreported, and its deflation omitted
- **File**: [concepts/phenomenology-of-choice-and-volition.md](/concepts/phenomenology-of-choice-and-volition/), L102 (and L161).
- **Problem**: "Libet himself recognised 'free won't' as the strongest evidence for conscious causal efficacy." In "Do We Have Free Will?" (1999; read in full in its Kane-edited Handbook reprint), the veto is a possibility: "Potentially available to the conscious function is the possibility of stopping or vetoing", and "Conscious will might block or veto the process". His conclusion is "it would not initiate a voluntary act but it could control performance of the act". His one evidential comparison is with a conscious "trigger": "there is no evidence for this, such as there is for a veto function". His "prima facie evidence that conscious mental processes can causatively control some brain processes" is "the almost universal experience that we can act with a free, independent choice" (p. 563). That is the phenomenology this article is about, not the veto. The 1985 BBS abstract is also a proposal: "It is proposed that conscious will can function in a permissive fashion". The phrase "free won't" has zero hits in the 1999 text (control: "veto" 29 hits). Haggard, writing in the joint 2001 JCS paper, introduces it as "(so called 'free won't')". **Coiner unresolved.** Gregory and Ramachandran are leads only and unverified. Obhi & Haggard's "Free Will and Free Won't" (2004, *American Scientist*, DOI 10.1511/2004.4.358) postdates Haggard's "so called", so it is not the source. Libet's *Mind Time* (2004) was not checked: the Google Books index returned 0 even for "veto", so that zero is uninformative.
- **Also**: "exerting top-down suppression of premotor areas" is uncited and attributed to the wrong study. The claim comes from Kühn, Haggard and Brass (2009), whose "effective connectivity *suggests*" it (PubMed 19072994). The Brass & Haggard (2007) abstract says only "a candidate brain area that reflects the crucial decision to do or not to do". The hub's spoke `volitional-control` L106 has said "not a demonstrated source exerting inhibition" since 2026-07-25 (`0189b802c0`). That followed outer review 2026-07-24 ChatGPT §6, which flagged exactly this "correlation-to-causation slide", with three-way convergence. The hub was not updated.
- **Missing deflation**: `libet-experiments` L91 and L185 carry Schultze-Kraft et al. (2016) and Filevich, Kühn and Haggard (2013). The hub carries neither, and ends "strengthens the case that conscious agency operates at a genuine decision point".
- **Severity**: High (a misreported source, and a hub verdict stronger than its spoke).
- **Recommendation**: Priority List item 1.

### Issue 2: "Genuine alternatives" is not James's phrase
- **Location**: L60, "William James called this the feeling of 'genuine alternatives.'"

- **Severity**: High (a fabricated quotation, small in scope).

## Counterarguments to Address

### The veto as the Map's interaction site
- **Current content says**: dFMC "exerts" top-down control (L102, L161), "the site at which the Map locates conscious veto".
- **A critic would argue**: Wessel and Aron (2017) treat stopping as a global suppressive network recruited by surprise in general (abstract quoted in `research/voids-veto-void-2026-09-18` L108). On that account, felt self-stopping is Wegner's apparent mental causation applied to inhibition.
- **Suggested response**: keep the locus as a *candidate correlate*, and say the signature fits a conscious veto without showing it (item 1).

### Illusionism at full strength
- **Current content says**: the seeming of effort is a dilemma; four tricks would be needed.
- **A critic would argue**: one self-model; the seeming is a representation, not an experience; compellingness is the illusion problem, open but not mysterious.
- **Suggested response**: concede the bare regress, press the illusion problem as a debt, and mark the disagreement as a framework boundary at Tenet 1 (item 2).

### The effort distribution
- **Current content says**: the brain-only account must derive why felt effort attaches to contested selections.
- **A critic would argue**: it has. Felt effort is a cost signal, so it attaches where control is hard. On Bermúdez's account the most skilled control is the least effortful.
- **Suggested response**: keep the distribution as the Map's reading, and grade it as fit without evidence (item 3).
