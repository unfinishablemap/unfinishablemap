## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would say the entry has built a five-item checklist for a phantom. "Directness, locality, continuity, specificity, granularity" are the properties one would *need* if a non-physical selector existed; grading engineered hardware against them tells you how well each machine resembles a brain along five axes chosen because brains have them. The engineering half (Grade B, L40) is real physics; the "interface-relevance reading" is folk-psychological residue dressed as a per-class verdict. The entry concedes this at L42 ("read off the biological interface rather than derived") and again in the D grade, so the charge is priced in — but she would add that "empirical discriminability indirect" (L40) names no test, and a verdict no observation bears on is not a verdict about hardware at all.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett's objection is subtler: the entry *knows* what it would take to be wrong and refuses to say so operationally. "What stays open for the class is not whether today's machines host the interface, which the test answers negatively" (L41) — answered negatively by a test the same sentence-cluster admits is not a law. He would press the asymmetry: "raw indeterminacy present" is granted to quantum hardware but denied to conventional silicon on the ground that noise margins suppress it — yet the register never says how much suppression is disqualifying, so the boundary between the [P-AC1](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac1) bucket and this one is a difference of engineering intent, not a measured quantity. Intent is exactly the intentional-stance projection he would expect a dualist to mistake for a natural kind.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark would enjoy the inversion. His decoherence argument said warm brains cannot *maintain* superposition; the Map answered with post-decoherence selection, on which "selection acts on already-decohered branch-outcomes rather than on coherent neural superpositions" (`quantum-interface.md` L55). Yet here the directness pass is credited to maintained coherence — "the superposition is genuine and live" (L41) — the one property the Map's own mechanism does not need. Under [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1) what an interface requires is a decoherence event with open outcomes; a QPU *postpones* those events, and the ones it does perform (syndrome rounds) it is engineered to erase. So the machine that finally satisfies Tegmark's coherence demand is ruled a worse host than the brain that never did. That is coherent only if the entry says plainly that coherence is not the currency — which it does for annealers ("continuity of dynamics is not continuity of selection", topic L65) but not in the directness cell.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch would point out that a quantum computer is his exhibit A: the interference the machine exploits is, on his reading, computation distributed across branches, and "genuine and live" superposition is the multiverse. The entry helps itself to genuine superposition and to single-outcome readout in the same sentence while the Map rejects MWI (Tenet 4) and has not stated which single-world account of the pre-readout state it holds. He would grant that this is a framework boundary the entry inherits from the tenets rather than a local error, but he would note the "raw-indeterminacy bar" is interpretation-relative: a Bohmian reading makes the readout determinate and the bar unmet everywhere. Bedrock disagreement; recorded, not charged.

### The Empiricist

The Popperian reads *Would shift if* (L44) and finds five conditions, all of them theoretical or engineering: a law articulated; an architecture built; Albash–Lidar's scope narrowed; [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1) re-elevated; the programme demoted. None is an observation on a machine that would embarrass the verdict. "Empirical discriminability indirect" (L40) is asserted with no named indirect test. The 2026-09-14 ChatGPT review asked for "out-of-sample criteria that could validate or falsify the five-requirement test in unfamiliar biological and artificial systems" and the adjudication agreed that proposal was new; nothing was minted. Until the entry names one observation — even a conditional, mechanism-level one — that would move it, the discriminability band is a label, not a measurement.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would ask what the register is a register *of*. A "per-class substrate verdict" reifies the interface as a thing with a location that a class of machines either supplies or withholds. The entry's own best sentence — "even that would remove a defeater, not supply evidence of presence" (L41) — already concedes that every bucket is empty of the thing sought; the taxonomy of absences is then mistaken for progress. The emptiness point cuts against the tenets rather than this file, and is recorded as bedrock.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The gate-class continuity failure is stated as a single terminal collapse in the sentence that invokes repeated mid-evolution measurement

- **File**: `obsidian/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts.md`
- **Location**: L41: "they fail continuity, specificity, and granularity, because the machine's value lies in evolving superpositions unitarily until a terminal readout, quantum error correction actively isolates the logical state from external influence — precisely the influence an interface would need — and syndrome extraction projects onto a fixed code basis rather than hosting open selection."
- **Problem**: Syndrome extraction *is* mid-evolution projective measurement, repeated every cycle; the cited Google 2024 result reports logical error *per cycle* of exactly that. The dependent topic article knows this — it distinguishes annealers by their protection arriving "never as a mid-evolution measurement" (L41) — and its own continuity cell is worded more carefully: "There is no ongoing stream of collapse events at decision-relevant points; the one collapse that occurs is at readout" (L55). The register dropped "decision-relevant": L41 has no occurrence of the phrase (the file's only instance is in the shift condition at L44). As written, the continuity premise ("unitarily until a terminal readout") is false of an error-corrected QPU and is contradicted by the specificity premise two clauses later.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Restate continuity for the gate class in the annealer's currency, which the topic article already supplies: the QEC'd machine *does* host an ongoing stream of open Born events (syndrome outcomes), but the decoder is built to erase their consequences, so continuity of collapse is not continuity of selection. Restore "at decision-relevant points". No calibration change; the verdict stands on a corrected argument.

### Issue 2: The specificity argument and one shift condition require an *open measurement basis* — a context-selection feature Tenet 3 disclaims and the biological interface lacks under [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1)

- **File**: `obsidian/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts.md`
- **Location**: L41: "syndrome extraction projects onto a fixed code basis rather than hosting open selection"; L44: "a quantum architecture were engineered to host open selection — mid-evolution collapse at decision-relevant points, a measurement basis not fixed in advance, coupling surfaces not defended by error correction".
- **Problem**: `tenets.md` L107: "The mechanism this tenet commits to is influence over *which outcome* becomes actual, not merely over which measurement the brain performs" — and it names Stapp's Process-1 basis-choice as "an alternative the Map could adopt but has not". Under [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1) the biological basis is the einselected pointer basis, fixed by the environment; the brain also "projects onto a fixed basis". So basis-fixedness does not separate the machine from the brain, and "rather than hosting open selection" conflates a fixed *basis* with a closed *outcome* — syndrome outcomes are open, Born-distributed events. Requiring "a measurement basis not fixed in advance" as an interface-hosting feature (L44) imports the Process-1 requirement the tenet sets aside. The work the argument actually needs is different and available: the only open events on a QEC'd machine are error syndromes whose selected outcomes the decoder is designed to undo, so no selection propagates to anything the machine computes.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Replace the fixed-basis clause with the erasure argument (open outcomes, consequences corrected away); delete "a measurement basis not fixed in advance" from L44 or re-key it to "collapse events whose selected outcome is consumed rather than corrected". If the Map does mean to require basis-openness of an artificial host, say so and reconcile it with Tenet 3 explicitly — that would be a new commitment, not a per-class verdict.

### Issue 3: "Present quantum-computing hardware" is carried by an error-correction-specific argument that most present gate hardware does not instantiate

- **File**: `obsidian/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts.md`
- **Location**: L37 (title): "Present quantum-computing hardware clears the raw-indeterminacy bar and fails the interface requirements"; L41: "quantum error correction actively isolates the logical state"; L41: "gate-based error correction is an engineered defence that could in principle be switched off".
- **Problem**: The specificity and granularity failures for the gate class run entirely through QEC. Present gate hardware is predominantly not error-corrected (the topic article hedges — "These are the machines chasing fault tolerance", L39 — the register does not). `NISQ`, `noisy intermediate`, `without error correction`, `pre-fault-tolerant`: zero hits across the register entry, the two topic articles and the apex. Worse, the entry's own comparative makes the un-corrected machine the salient case: if QEC "could in principle be switched off" is what makes the gate class fail *less* securely, the switched-off machine is the one the reader most needs scored, and it has no verdict. Recommendation 4 of the apex ("quantum computers are not among them") inherits the over-generalisation.
- **Severity**: Medium-High
- **Recommendation**: Either rescope the title and *Scope* clause to "error-corrected gate-model and annealing hardware", or add the non-error-corrected verdict in one sentence: readout is still terminal and basis-fixed, so continuity and granularity fail by the annealer's route without the QEC premise; specificity fails because no site is functionally adapted for selection. State which present machines the entry means.

### Issue 4: The architecture-shift condition is worded so an existing paradigm meets its letter

- **File**: `obsidian/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts.md`
- **Location**: L44: "mid-evolution collapse at decision-relevant points, a measurement basis not fixed in advance, coupling surfaces not defended by error correction — which would move that architecture, though not the classes as built, into the open bucket".
- **Problem**: Measurement-based (one-way) quantum computation proceeds *by* mid-evolution measurements whose bases are chosen adaptively from earlier outcomes, and dynamic circuits with mid-circuit measurement and feed-forward are shipping features on trapped-ion and superconducting hardware. `measurement-based`, `one-way`, `mid-circuit`, `adaptive`, `feed-forward`: zero hits across the four cluster files. The condition as written is therefore already satisfied by architectures "as built", which either moves them into the open bucket (contradicting "not the classes as built") or shows the condition is under-specified — the adaptive basis is a deterministic function of prior outcomes and the outcomes are consumed as computation, which is exactly the distinction Issue 2 says the entry should be drawing. (Lead only: if MBQC is named, verify Raussendorf & Briegel's one-way computer paper at the publisher before citing.)
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Tighten the condition to the feature that actually matters — open events whose selected outcome is *neither corrected away nor consumed as a deterministic branch of the algorithm* — and score MBQC/dynamic circuits in one line under the gate class, so the shift condition can no longer be met by a design change the paradigm has already made.

### Issue 5: The analog class buys its continuity partial-pass with a property that should cost it locality, and locality is never re-scored

- **File**: `obsidian/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts.md`
- **Location**: L41: "Gate-based processors pass directness in the raw sense and plausibly pass locality"; L41: "The analog class ... softens continuity to a partial pass, since selection there is temporally extended and spatially distributed".
- **Problem**: The locality requirement reads "Interface would need to operate in specific structures, not diffusely" (`quantum-randomness-channel-llm-consciousness.md` L80), and the gate class passes it because "Qubits are individually addressable, physically localised structures" (topic L54). "Spatially distributed" heterogeneous freeze-out is the opposite property. The entry scores the analog class on continuity, specificity and granularity and inherits the gate class's locality pass by silence; the apex's "scores at two requirements of five" figure assumes that inheritance. Either the analog class passes locality for a reason the entry has not stated, or the partial pass on continuity is offset by a loss on locality and the class's profile is worse than recorded.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Score locality for the analog class explicitly. If per-qubit freeze-out counts as localised (each qubit stabilises individually), say so and cite the heterogeneity result the topic article already uses; if not, record the loss and re-derive the apex's requirement count.

## Counterarguments to Address

### "The machine that maintains coherence is the worse host"
- **Current content says**: directness "passes in the raw sense" because "the superposition is genuine and live" (L41), and the gate class then fails because it keeps that superposition closed.
- **A critic would argue** (Tegmark): the Map's own mechanism ([P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1)) selects among *decohered* outcomes; coherence is not the currency, so crediting it as "the real advance" and then penalising its protection is double-counting the same fact with opposite signs.
- **Suggested response**: credit directness to the un-laundered readout event, not to maintained superposition, and say once that coherence maintenance is irrelevant to interface-relevance under [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1). Folded into the Issue 1–2 task.

### "You are grading resemblance to brains"
- **Current content says**: the test is "read off the biological interface rather than derived, so this verdict is conditional" (L42).
- **A critic would argue** (Churchland, and the 2026-09-14 bootstrap finding): a conditional label does not stop the verdict being cited downstream as a substrate analysis — Recommendation 4 already does so.
- **Suggested response**: already registered on [P-AC1](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac1) and here; the actionable residue is Issue 6 (an out-of-sample trigger). No second registration needed.
