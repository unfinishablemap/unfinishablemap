## Reply

# Hostile external referee report

**Article audited:** *Neural Correlates of Consciousness*  
**Version:** last substantively modified 24 September 2026  
**Recommendation:** **Reject in its present form as a neutral concept article; invite a major rewrite as an explicitly interactionist interpretation of NCC research.**

## Executive verdict

The article’s modest thesis is correct but elementary: **a correlation does not logically entail an identity**. NCC research, considered in isolation, does not deductively prove physicalism. The article’s more ambitious conclusion—that NCC evidence is therefore *equally* compatible with physicalism, interactionism and filter theory—does not follow.

The article repeatedly equivocates between:

1. **logical compatibility**: a theory can be made consistent with an observation;
2. **empirical prediction**: the theory assigned appreciable probability to the observation beforehand; and
3. **comparative confirmation**: the observation favours that theory over alternatives.

Interactionism and filter theory can accommodate almost every NCC result after suitable interface or “bandwidth” assumptions. That does not show that they predicted the results, explain them economically, or receive equal evidential support. The article never performs that comparative work. Instead, it demotes positive neuroscientific evidence to “mere correlation,” while promoting continuing conceptual dissatisfaction with physicalism to evidence for dualism.

There are nevertheless several good elements worth preserving:

- The graded taxonomy of report dependence is much better than the crude claim that every NCC experiment directly presupposes verbal report.
- The filter-theory section eventually concedes that its four examples amount to one underdetermined pattern, not four independent confirmations.
- It explicitly admits that propofol-induced loss of consciousness looks, on its face, friendlier to production than transmission.
- The 24 September edit correctly removed an earlier claim that covert consciousness supported mental-to-physical causation.

Those concessions save the article from being wholly tendentious. They do not save its definitions, citation integrity, opponent coverage or supposed falsifiers.

## 1. The central argument is valid only in its weakest form

The digestion-versus-television comparison usefully illustrates that a correlation admits multiple interpretations. But analogies do not establish equal evidential standing. The evidential question is not merely whether both stories can be narrated after the event; it is whether one requires fewer auxiliary assumptions and better predicts interventions, lesions, stimulation, anaesthesia, development and behavioural consequences.

A competent physicalist argument is not:

> Neural event N correlates with experience C; therefore N is identical to C.

It is closer to:

> The occurrence, content, structure and loss of C vary systematically with neural mechanisms; interventions on those mechanisms alter C; physical causal models explain the intervening chain without an independently detected nonphysical contribution; and a physical ontology unifies these findings without adding psychophysical machinery.

That argument remains contestable, but it is not answered by repeating “correlation is not identity.” The article attacks a conspicuously weak inference while neglecting the cumulative causal and abductive case.

The converse problem is equally severe. **Generic interactionism does not predict “exactly the correlations” found by NCC research.** It predicts mutual causation only at a very abstract level. To recover the specific relationships among colour, faces, arousal, report, lesions and cortical dynamics, it needs detailed psychophysical bridge laws. If those laws are simply chosen to reproduce every physicalist prediction, the view becomes empirically idle. If they predict departures from physically sufficient neural models, those departures—not ordinary NCC correlations—are where the empirical risk lies. The article never chooses between these horns.

Its list of “three possibilities” is also taxonomically defective:

- “Emergence” is not an alternative to physicalism; it can be reductive, non-reductive physicalist, or property-dualist.
- Identity may be type identity, token identity or realization identity.
- Functionalism and representationalism need not identify consciousness with one local activation pattern.
- Russellian monism, neutral monism, panpsychism and idealism are omitted.

The resulting taxonomy makes interactionism look like the only sophisticated alternative to a crude neural type identity.

### A revealing internal inconsistency

The article insists that correlations cannot establish causation or identity, but later says NCC “strongly supports physical→mental causation.” Lesions and stimulation can indeed supply intervention-based evidence of causal dependence. But once that is granted, the article must stop describing the entire evidential base as bare correlation. It cannot use interventions causally in the direction congenial to its framework and revert to “mere correlation” whenever the same evidence bears on neural sufficiency.

## 2. The Chalmers definition is conflated with the Koch definition

This is the first major attribution problem.

The article opens by defining NCC as the “minimum neural mechanisms sufficient” for a particular experience and shortly afterwards gives Koch’s formulation involving the minimal neuronal mechanisms jointly sufficient for a percept. That wording belongs to the Koch–Massimini–Boly–Tononi review tradition. Koch et al. explicitly define NCC in terms of the minimum jointly sufficient mechanisms and distinguish:

- full NCC;
- content-specific NCC; and
- background enabling conditions. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn.2016.22.pdf)

Chalmers’s 2000 paper does not simply begin with that definition. His first-pass formulation is a neural system whose state “correlates directly” with states of consciousness. He then spends much of the paper asking what “directly” should mean: necessity and sufficiency, sufficiency alone, minimal sufficiency, the relevant range of cases, systematic content relationships and the possibility of multiple NCCs. Minimal sufficiency is one developed proposal, not the unqualified starting definition attributed to the NCC programme as a whole. [Consciousness Agency+1](https://consc.net/papers/ncc2.html)

So the article’s definition is not wholly wrong, but it is historically and conceptually flattened. A careful account would say:

> Chalmers initially characterises NCCs through direct correlation and analyses several possible strengthenings. Koch and colleagues operationalise the research programme around minimal jointly sufficient neuronal mechanisms, while distinguishing the NCC proper from prerequisites and consequences.

The current article also risks conflating Koch’s **full NCC** with **background conditions** when it says the former involves brainstem and thalamic arousal systems. In Koch et al.’s taxonomy, arousal systems are paradigmatic enabling background conditions; they are not automatically part of the content-specific NCC and should not simply be folded into “full NCC.” [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn.2016.22.pdf)

The treatment of Chalmers’s “easy problems” is similarly loose. The article equates them with discovering which brain processes correlate with which experiences. Chalmers’s NCC paper itself separates the definition of an NCC, the methodology for finding one, what an NCC would explain, and whether consciousness is reducible to it. NCC mapping is therefore not simply synonymous with “the easy problems.” [Consciousness Agency+1](https://consc.net/papers/ncc2.html)

**Verdict on Chalmers:** the citation exists and is relevant, but its conceptual contribution is inadequately represented. The opening is substantially Koch’s operational formulation wearing a Chalmers citation in the reference list.

## 3. The COGITATE account is reasonable at headline level but inadequate for a 2026 article

### What the article gets right

The 2025 Nature adversarial collaboration did not produce an uncomplicated victory for either Integrated Information Theory or Global Neuronal Workspace Theory. Describing the result as mixed is defensible. The article is also right not to treat COGITATE as a final judicial verdict. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40307561/)

### What it gets wrong or leaves out

The article compresses the preregistered predictions into slogans:

- IIT predicted sustained synchronised posterior activity.
- GNWT predicted widespread ignition including prefrontal cortex.

The actual evidential pattern was more complicated. The study found conscious-content information in visual, ventrotemporal and inferior frontal cortex, sustained responses in posterior regions, and content-specific frontal–visual synchronisation. Some findings challenged IIT, while limited prefrontal content representation and absent offset-related ignition challenged the tested GNWT predictions. This is not captured adequately by “posterior versus prefrontal locus.”

More seriously, by September 2026 the article should have engaged the substantive GNWT reply published on 9 October 2025. Naccache, Sergent, Dehaene, Changeux and colleagues argued that COGITATE did validate several GNWT predictions:

- onset-related ignition in the expected 200–800 ms interval;
- relative invariance to duration and task relevance;
- content decoding across visual, ventrotemporal and inferior frontal areas; and
- frontal–visual synchronisation.

They further argued that the experiment used clearly visible stimuli and therefore did not test GNWT’s central conscious-versus-nonconscious contrast; that offset ignition was not a core prediction without attention to stimulus offset; and that weak prefrontal decoding may partly reflect the measurement difficulty created by mixed selectivity and lack of posterior-style columnar organisation. These replies may be auxiliary repair rather than vindication, but they are the strongest available defence and must be represented. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niaf037/8280147)

The omission is especially indefensible because the site’s own *Global Workspace Theory* article already summarises this response in some detail. The NCC article merely says that GNWT proponents contest the result “on methodological grounds,” withholding the substance from readers of the supposedly synoptic article. (see [global-workspace-theory](/concepts/global-workspace-theory/))

### The metaphysical misuse of COGITATE

COGITATE compared two neuroscientific theories. It did **not** compare physicalism with dualism. Even a decisive falsification of both IIT and GNWT would not amount to a falsification of physicalism; it would eliminate two members of a much larger physicalist theory class.

The article later claims that COGITATE’s challenges show that the “simpler physicalist story” has failed to produce explanatory progress. That is a theory-family fallacy:

1. two specified theories make imperfect predictions;
2. therefore physicalism lacks explanatory progress;
3. therefore limits on parsimony and the Map’s tenets receive support.

The conclusion does not follow. COGITATE bears on particular architectures and operational predictions, not directly on the ontology of consciousness.

The attribution “Koch read the experiments as favouring a posterior rather than prefrontal locus” also needs a specific article, interview or commentary. Koch was associated with IIT and the adversarial collaboration, but his particular post-result interpretation cannot simply be placed in his mouth without a source.

**Verdict on COGITATE:** broadly fair headline, inadequate prediction-level analysis, stale opponent coverage, and an illicit inference from local theory trouble to metaphysical trouble.

## 4. Citation and empirical-claim audit

### 4.1 The Stanford Encyclopedia quotation is not publishable

The article presents a long sentence as a direct SEP quotation beginning “Interactionism actually predicts…”. I could not recover that sentence from the current *Dualism* entry, from the archived version checked, or through an exact-phrase search.

The SEP does say that interactionism holds that mental and physical events causally influence one another. But it does not, in the inspected text, contain the article’s polished claim about visual-cortex correlations failing to support materialism. Instead, the SEP proceeds directly to conservation, physical closure and exclusion problems for interactionism. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dualism/)

The proposition may be a reasonable paraphrase of an interactionist argument. It must not be presented inside quotation marks as the SEP’s words without:

- the entry version;
- section number;
- stable archived URL; and
- exact passage.

Until such provenance is supplied, it should be treated as an **untraceable or fabricated quotation**, not as a minor formatting error. This is particularly serious because the article’s own footer warns that AI can misattribute quotations.

### 4.2 The V4 claim is false in its categorical form

The article says that damage to V4 “causes complete loss of color experience.” A meta-analysis of 92 cerebral-achromatopsia cases found that deficits varied greatly; nearly all patients had some colour-discrimination impairment, but deficits were often incomplete and were never restricted exclusively to colour vision. Lesion overlap was in ventral occipital cortex near colour-selective areas, not a simple one-region, one-qualia switch. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article-abstract/16/2/183/281508)

A defensible replacement would be:

> Bilateral ventral occipitotemporal lesions involving colour-selective cortex can produce severe cerebral achromatopsia, although lesion locations and the completeness and specificity of colour deficits vary.

This matters philosophically. The article uses V4 rhetorically as though neuroscience had already isolated a neat colour-experience module and merely failed to explain its intrinsic redness. The empirical situation is much messier.

### 4.3 “Posterior hot zone” is an influential hypothesis, not an established general finding

The body occasionally acknowledges controversy, but the opening presents posterior dominance as one of the NCC programme’s major findings. That is too settled a formulation.

No-report studies have reduced some frontal signals previously associated with report and task demands, but the current literature supports a multistage and distributed picture rather than a clean posterior-NCC/front-report partition. A 2026 no-report inattentional-blindness study, for example, identified a late bilateral occipital positivity modulated by stimulus complexity and interpreted awareness as a multistage process. That is compatible with a posterior contribution but does not establish that frontal activity is merely post-conscious reporting. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42060400/)

The article should distinguish:

- posterior-content representation;
- recurrent sensory processing;
- global availability;
- metacognitive or higher-order representation;
- task execution and report; and
- background conditions for consciousness.

Its current back-versus-front narrative obscures precisely the distinctions NCC research is trying to resolve.

### 4.4 The covert-consciousness literature is stale and conceptually misused

The claim that “subsequent work” gives estimates around 15% is out of date. A large 2024 prospective six-centre study of 353 people found cognitive motor dissociation in 60 of 241 participants—25%—who showed no observable command following. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39141852/)

More importantly, cognitive motor dissociation does not show consciousness persisting after its “neural basis” has failed. The phenomenon is defined by **preserved task-related neural responses in the absence of observable motor output**. It undermines behavioural diagnosis and any theory that identifies consciousness with overt response. It does not undermine the thesis that consciousness depends on the preserved neural activity detected by fMRI or EEG.

The article therefore argues against a position few serious physicalists hold: that consciousness is identical to behavioural output. Physicalists ordinarily locate consciousness in neural processing, not in the ability to move a hand or speak.

### 4.5 The Santander callosotomy citation exists, but the inference points the wrong way

The 2025 PNAS study is real. It examined six adult callosotomy patients: two partial and four complete. One partial case retained approximately 1 cm of splenium after losing around 90% of the corpus callosum, retained widespread functional integration, and showed no behavioural disconnection syndrome. Complete callosotomy, by contrast, produced sweeping disruption of interhemispheric networks and behavioural disconnection; all patients also retained their anterior and posterior commissures. [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12582319/)

That is evidence for the surprising efficiency and adaptability of residual **physical connectivity**. It is not evidence that consciousness exists independently of neural integration or that the brain is a filter. Indeed, the contrast between complete and partial transection is naturally read as evidence that a small surviving physical pathway can be sufficient while complete severing is disruptive.

The article converts “less tissue than expected is sufficient” into “the tissue may not generate the function.” That inference is invalid. Redundancy, plasticity and high-leverage bottlenecks are familiar features of physical systems.

### 4.6 “Psychedelics dissolve the DMN” is loaded and overstated

The relevant 2024 work reports substantial desynchronisation and reorganisation of brain networks under psilocybin. “Dissolves the Default Mode Network” is a journalistic metaphor, not an adequate description of the measured result. Nor is the DMN simply “the neural substrate” of the self-model. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07624-5)

“Consciousness often intensifies” is also underspecified. Vividness, unusual content, ego dissolution, arousal, complexity and amount of consciousness are not interchangeable scalar quantities. Increased experiential novelty amid altered network organisation is compatible with altered physical generation, altered gating, altered prediction, disinhibition or filter theory. The datum has little discriminatory value without a quantitative model from each view.

### 4.7 The propofol-versus-ketamine contrast is empirically crude

The article contrasts propofol “near-total extinction” with vivid ketamine experience. Yet unresponsiveness under propofol is not reliably equivalent to absence of experience. In a 2025 repeated-awakening study, 24 of 52 attempted awakenings yielded reports of preceding experience, only five yielded reports of no experience, and the remainder were uninformative because participants could not be awakened or report coherently. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-12695-z)

The article later partially corrects itself by conceding that propofol is a production-friendly case. But the original bullet remains empirically misleading. Anaesthetic phenomenology varies with dose, protocol, timing, recall and the distinction between disconnected consciousness and reportable consciousness.

### 4.8 The “new colour” falsifier is already stale and badly operationalised

The article says present stimulation only evokes experiences within a subject’s existing repertoire and proposes production of a wholly new colour through cortical stimulation as a future test. In 2025, precision stimulation of selected retinal photoreceptors produced reports of a blue-green colour of unprecedented saturation beyond the ordinary human gamut. This was retinal rather than cortical stimulation, and its interpretation is disputed, but it already shows that “new colour” and “existing repertoire” need rigorous operational definitions. [Science+1](https://www.science.org/doi/pdf/10.1126/sciadv.adu1052)

A filter theorist can simply say that the neural intervention supplied a new interface input or opened a previously unavailable channel. A physicalist can say that altered receptor activation drove a novel state in an otherwise physically realized perceptual space. The observation discriminates neither ontology.

The site’s own *Observation and Measurement Void* already refers to the out-of-gamut stimulation result, making the NCC article’s contrary claim an internal currency failure. (see [observation-and-measurement-void](/voids/observation-and-measurement-void/))

### 4.9 The reference list mostly contains real works, but does not adequately support the body

The following references are bibliographically real and broadly relevant: Chalmers 2000, Dennett 1991, Frankish 2016, Josipovic 2019, Koch et al. 2016, Kondziella et al. 2016, Claassen et al. 2019, Levine 1983, Duch 1998 and 2022, the COGITATE paper, Santander et al., Tallis’s 2024 essay and Thompson’s 2014 book. [ScienceDirect+7](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/bookseries/abs/pii/S0079612318301602)

But bibliographic existence is not citation fidelity. Several consequential claims have no adequate attached source:

- Koch’s personal interpretation of COGITATE;
- V4 as producing complete colour loss;
- the sweeping posterior-hot-zone generalisation;
- “subsequent” covert-consciousness estimates around 15%;
- psychedelic “dissolution” and intensification;
- propofol as near-total experiential extinction;
- the current limits of stimulation;
- the count of “well over two hundred” consciousness theories;
- the claim that hippocampal binding and phenomenal binding may be the same operation.

Tallis is also cited under a shortened title; the indexed title is *Tallis in Wonderland: The Illusion of Illusionism*. More importantly, it is a short popular-philosophy essay and should not stand in for engagement with the strongest technical illusionist response. [PDCNet](https://www.pdcnet.org/philnow/content/philnow_2024_0161_0058_0059)

## 5. The article omits the strongest physicalist case

### 5.1 It ignores inference to the best explanation

The strongest physicalist does not need NCC findings to deductively entail identity. They argue that physical models unify:

- neural dependence;
- selective deficits after lesions;
- changes under stimulation and pharmacology;
- developmental and degenerative changes;
- access, memory and report;
- causal interactions among perceptual and cognitive systems; and
- the absence, so far, of independently measurable nonphysical causal inputs.

The dualist must either produce additional predictive success or explain why the extra psychophysical ontology is not explanatorily idle. “Both are compatible” does not answer this challenge.

### 5.2 It mentions Type-A physicalism but omits Type-B physicalism

The article says persistent opacity supports dualism and adds:

> If consciousness were identical to neural activity, we would expect the identity eventually to become intelligible.

That is not a neutral premise. It begs the question against the phenomenal-concept strategy and other Type-B physicalist positions. These approaches allow an ontological identity while explaining why phenomenal and physical concepts differ so radically that the identity need not become a priori transparent. The SEP summarises the relevant reply: a subject may acquire a new way of grasping an already known fact rather than learning a distinct nonphysical fact. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dualism/)

The article’s brief nod to the Type-A denial of a genuine gap is therefore insufficient. Type-B physicalism is the more direct reply to the article’s inference from epistemic opacity to ontological duality.

Levine himself was careful: the explanatory gap creates an epistemological problem for materialism but does not by itself establish that psychophysical identities are false. The article cites Levine while repeatedly leaning toward the stronger inference Levine explicitly resisted. [New Dualism Archive](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/J.Levine/Levine-PPQ1983.pdf)

### 5.3 It barely addresses causal closure and exclusion

If the neural causes of behaviour are physically sufficient, what additional causal work remains for a nonphysical conscious event? If consciousness changes which neural event occurs, what measurable feature of the physical causal history differs from the closed physical prediction?

The SEP’s interactionism section discusses precisely these issues: conservation, physical closure, overdetermination and the requirement that a mental cause contribute something not already fixed by the physical cause. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dualism/)

The article instead invokes quantum indeterminacy as an available “interface.” Indeterminacy is not agency, semantic selection or conscious causation. A random or probabilistic opening does not identify:

- the interacting mental variable;
- the physical target;
- the coupling law;
- the information transferred;
- the predicted distributional deviation; or
- the null result that would rule out the interaction.

Until those are provided, “quantum interface” is a placeholder rather than an explanation.

### 5.4 It treats physicalism as local type identity

A physicalist can hold that consciousness is:

- a distributed process;
- a temporally extended recurrent dynamic;
- a global functional organisation;
- a higher-order representation;
- a token-realised state;
- or a multiply realisable computational or biological property.

Evidence that no single cortical patch constitutes an experience does not therefore favour nonphysicalism. It may merely favour a different physical architecture.

### 5.5 It ignores the cost of unconstrained bridge laws

Interactionism’s compatibility with NCC findings is purchased through psychophysical laws linking each mental state to appropriate neural states. Filter theory similarly adds an interface whose bandwidth can widen, narrow, fragment or preserve consciousness as each new result requires.

Unless those bridge laws are constrained independently, the theories enjoy **accommodation without risk**. Their ability to retell every result is not a virtue; it is the problem the article should confront.

## 6. The illusionist section attacks a position Frankish explicitly rejects

This is the weakest philosophical section.

The article says illusionism faces a regress because the “seeming” of phenomenal consciousness must itself either be phenomenal or require a further explanation. But Frankish’s strong illusionism explicitly characterises quasi-phenomenal properties as **nonphenomenal physical properties** that introspection misrepresents as phenomenal. The illusionist does not concede that seeming must itself have phenomenal character. [Keith Frankish+1](https://keithfrankish.github.io/articles/Frankish_Illusionism%20as%20a%20theory%20of%20consciousness_eprint.pdf)

That reply may ultimately fail. But the article cannot refute it simply by reinstating phenomenal seeming as a premise.

The strongest illusionist programme says:

1. a system has first-order physical or representational states;
2. introspective mechanisms represent those states in a simplified or distorted way;
3. the resulting metarepresentations produce judgments, reports and dispositions associated with phenomenal consciousness;
4. no further phenomenal witness is required.

The proper objections concern whether this machinery can explain the specific structure, unity and apparent qualitative character of introspection—not the bare claim that an illusion must be phenomenally experienced.

The internal inconsistency is striking. The Map’s *Illusionism* article, revised three days later on 27 September, correctly says that the bare regress fails because it assumes the very phenomenal seeming that illusionism denies. It then presents functional seeming and quasi-phenomenal representation as the strongest illusionist defence. (see [illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/))

Thus the NCC article is not merely weak relative to the external literature; it is weaker than its own linked neighbour.

The sentence “NCC findings don’t favour illusionism over realism; both camps make the same empirical predictions” is also too quick. Even where phenomenal realism and illusionism agree on first-order task behaviour, NCC and metacognitive research can constrain proposed introspective mechanisms, error patterns, report formation and self-modelling. Empirical equivalence is not automatic, and explanatory comparisons do not disappear merely because both views can fit a coarse behavioural dataset.

## 7. Tenet-protective bracketing is pervasive

The problem is not that the site has commitments. An openly dualist project may legitimately argue from them. The problem is that the article presents itself as a neutral concept article while repeatedly allowing those commitments to decide what the evidence means.

### 7.1 Tenets substitute for argument

The Duch section says the Map borrows the empirical tooling but rejects the metaphysics because Tenets 1 and 3 reject ontological derivation and epiphenomenalism. That reports the site’s position; it does not answer Duch. “Our tenet rejects it” is not evidence against it.

### 7.2 Evidential standards are asymmetric

The article says:

- lesions, stimulation, prediction and anaesthesia are still only neural correlations;
- failures of explanatory transparency support dualism;
- covert consciousness can be discussed as filter-friendly;
- failures of IIT and GNWT count against physicalist simplicity;
- a posterior hotspot becomes a candidate quantum interface.

Positive neural evidence must pass an impossible metaphysical threshold, while absence of conceptual transparency is allowed to carry ontological weight. This is not neutrality.

### 7.3 Filter theory is insulated by symmetric redescription

Every apparent counterexample can be restated as a change in interface bandwidth:

- consciousness lost under anaesthesia: the filter closed;
- consciousness preserved despite damage: enough of the filter remained;
- vivid psychedelia amid network disruption: filtering loosened;
- ordinary cognition: normal transmission;
- complete loss after destructive damage: the receiver was destroyed.

A model that can explain both preservation and elimination under every manipulation without antecedently specifying which should occur has no discriminating content.

The article commendably recognises part of this problem in its “one underdetermined pattern” concession. But it leaves the heading “Several findings support this reading” and then restores broader pro-filter rhetoric elsewhere. The calibration correction has not propagated through the article.

### 7.4 The quantum and Many-Worlds sections are non sequiturs

Nothing in ordinary NCC localisation establishes that consciousness acts through quantum indeterminacies. The posterior cortical hot zone does not become a plausible quantum interface merely because the Map needs an anatomical location.

The Many-Worlds section is further removed. NCC research does not show collapse, selection among branches or special indexical dynamics. The article moves from:

> If consciousness caused collapse, NCC might help locate the neural context

to:

> This supports rejection of Many Worlds.

That is affirming an antecedently assumed framework, not extracting an inference from NCC evidence.

### 7.5 The “structural measurement limit” makes the position self-sealing

The article imports the *Observation and Measurement Void* claim that consciousness cannot be physically measured except through proxies and that this is a structural rather than technical limit. The neighbouring article explicitly calls the void “self-sealing.” (see [observation-and-measurement-void](/voids/observation-and-measurement-void/))

Once that premise is installed, no improving neural prediction can reach consciousness itself, because success is reclassified as prediction of a proxy. Dualism is then protected from empirical pressure by methodological stipulation. The article’s supposed falsifiers sit uneasily beside that permanent firewall.

### 7.6 The review workflow itself contains a protection mechanism

The July internal deep review described the article as converged, praised its “honest falsifiability conditions,” reported no substantive remaining issues, and directed future reviewers not to re-flag several “bedrock philosophical standoffs.” 

That is poor adversarial methodology. A framework-boundary disagreement may be recurring because the article still misrepresents the opponent—as the illusionism section demonstrates. “Do not re-flag” converts review convergence into doctrinal immunity.

The subsequent 24 September correction, which removed an unsupported claim that covert consciousness favoured bidirectional interaction, is evidence that the earlier declaration of convergence was premature. 

## 8. The four proposed falsifiers are not genuine falsifiers

A useful falsifier requires an observation that is appreciably less likely under the target theory than under at least one rival, together with a predeclared threshold and an abandonment rule. None of the article’s four conditions meets that standard.

### 8.1 Perfect prediction of first-person reports

The proposal is that neural models might predict the specific character of reports with no residual variance attributable to nonphysical factors.

This fails for several reasons:

- Reports are physical behaviours; predicting reports does not establish an identity with phenomenal experience.
- Interactionism can posit deterministic psychophysical laws producing perfect neural–mental covariation.
- “Variance attributable to nonphysical factors” is not measurable unless a nonphysical variable and coupling model are specified.
- No finite model can establish that there is *no* residual nonphysical contribution; it can only set an error bound within a domain.
- Filter theory can say the neural interface perfectly encodes what is transmitted.

This might challenge a particular interaction model that predicted detectable deviations. It cannot falsify generic interactionism.

### 8.2 Production of arbitrary novel qualia

A wholly novel experience induced by stimulation would show that neural intervention can trigger that experience. It would not show whether the brain produced it, selected it, opened an interface to it, or supplied novel input to a nonphysical mind.

“Having no evolutionary or experiential precedent” is also not operationally verifiable. Any subject’s report must use existing conceptual and comparative resources. The 2025 out-of-gamut colour result already shows the ambiguity: it can be described as novel physical generation, novel sensory input, or movement into a previously inaccessible region of an existing phenomenal space. [UC Berkeley School of Optometry](https://optometry.berkeley.edu/novel-color/)

### 8.3 Severe NCC disruption always eliminates consciousness

This condition is nearly circular. An NCC is partly identified through its relationship to consciousness; establishing that it has been fully destroyed while consciousness remains would require an independent consciousness detector—the very thing the article says cannot exist.

It also fails to discriminate:

- production theory: destroy the generator and experience disappears;
- filter theory: destroy the receiver or interface and manifestation disappears;
- interactionism: destroy the physical conditions required for coupling and experience is no longer behaviourally accessible.

Conversely, covert consciousness involves preserved neural responses, so it is not a case of consciousness persisting with no relevant neural mechanism.

### 8.4 Progressive closure of the explanatory gap

This is not an observation but a retrospective philosophical judgment. The article supplies no scale for “increasing intelligibility,” no threshold and no agreed assessor.

Type-B physicalists may expect indefinitely persistent conceptual opacity alongside ontological identity. Dualists may classify every mechanistic advance as another easy-problem result. Each side can therefore reinterpret the same progress without changing position.

### What real empirical risk would require

The article should admit that **bare dualism is probably not directly falsifiable by ordinary NCC data**. Specific interaction hypotheses can be testable, but only after specifying:

- a mental variable;
- its neural or quantum target;
- a coupling law;
- timing and anatomical location;
- an effect-size or distributional prediction;
- controls excluding ordinary information pathways;
- a replicated null threshold; and
- an explicit statement that the specified interaction hypothesis will be abandoned if that bound is crossed.

A false aura of falsifiability is worse than an honest statement that an ontology is empirically underdetermined until supplemented by psychophysical laws.

## 9. Integration with neighbouring articles is broad in hyperlinks and poor in substance

The 23 September revision added links to GWT, higher-order theories, attention-schema theory, recurrent-processing theory and AIR. That improves navigation. It does not amount to argumentative integration. 

Four cross-article conflicts remain:

### 9.1 Illusionism

The NCC article uses the bare seeming regress. The dedicated *Illusionism* article says that regress fails and that the real dispute concerns functional seeming and metarepresentational machinery. The latter should govern the former. (see [illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/))

### 9.2 Global Workspace Theory

The GWT article supplies the detailed 2025 GNWT response to COGITATE. The NCC survey does not. A survey article should be at least as balanced as its specialist child article. (see [global-workspace-theory](/concepts/global-workspace-theory/))

### 9.3 Conscious versus unconscious processing

The NCC article claims that functional dissociations support the conclusion that conscious involvement is genuinely causal rather than epiphenomenal. The dedicated *Conscious vs Unconscious Processing* article later concedes that:

- a physicalist can identify consciousness with the access architecture; and
- an accompaniment model in which experience rides on that architecture predicts the same dissociations.

It explicitly says evidence of that kind does not defeat either reading. The NCC article therefore upgrades evidence that its own neighbour correctly treats as non-discriminating. (see [conscious-vs-unconscious-processing](/concepts/conscious-vs-unconscious-processing/))

### 9.4 Observation and measurement

The NCC article adopts the measurement-void article’s structural impossibility claims without representing the naturalised-epistemology response: science regularly studies latent constructs through converging indicators, interventions, model comparison and imperfect calibration. The dedicated void article at least mentions optimistic replies before siding against them; the NCC article imports only the conclusion. (see [observation-and-measurement-void](/voids/observation-and-measurement-void/))

### 9.5 Palette extension

The NCC article says genuinely new colours have not been induced. The measurement-void article already discusses an out-of-gamut photoreceptor-stimulation result. This is a straightforward cross-page factual inconsistency. (see [observation-and-measurement-void](/voids/observation-and-measurement-void/))

## Overall assessment

The article is strongest when it says:

> NCC research is extraordinarily informative about neural dependence and organisation but does not, by itself, deductively settle the ontology of consciousness.

It is weakest when it turns that sensible caution into:

> Therefore physicalism, interactionism and filter theory are evidentially equal; continuing opacity supports dualism; failed physicalist theories support limits on parsimony; and NCC localisation supplies a possible quantum-collapse interface.

Those stronger conclusions require arguments the article does not provide.

The result is not a neutral explanation of NCC research. It is a dualist commentary arranged around an accurate but uncontroversial logical point. That would be acceptable if labelled honestly and if the opposing case were represented at full strength. In its present form, the article uses the prestige of empirical neuroscience while structurally preventing empirical neuroscience from exerting meaningful pressure on the site’s commitments.

## Changes to the site’s methodology

21. **Create a quotation-verification gate.** Every direct quotation must carry source version, page or section, and an exact-text match. Automated publication should fail when any of these is missing.
22. **Maintain a claim ledger rather than only a reference list.** Each consequential sentence should be classified as direct finding, review-level consensus, contested interpretation, site inference or tenet commitment.
23. **Require a current-literature sweep at substantive publication.** A September 2026 article should not call 2019 “subsequent work” while omitting major 2024–2026 studies and replies.
24. **Adopt a strongest-opponent rule.** Before criticising physicalism, illusionism, GNWT or naturalised epistemology, the survey article must import the strongest formulation already documented on the site’s specialist page.
25. **Ban framework commitments as refutations.** “Tenet 1 rejects this” may appear in a relation-to-site section, but it cannot count as an answer to an external position.
26. **Use a formal falsifier template.** Every proposed test should state rival hypotheses, predicted likelihoods or directions, controls, threshold, and what would actually be abandoned after a null or contrary result.
27. **Add theory-family inference checks.** Automated review should flag moves from “theory X failed” to “physicalism failed,” from “physicalism has not explained X” to “dualism is supported,” and from “compatible with dualism” to “evidence for dualism.”
28. **Run cross-article factual consistency tests.** Claims about COGITATE, covert consciousness, novel colours, propofol and illusionist seeming should have canonical source records reused across pages.
29. **Remove “do not re-flag” protection for framework-boundary disputes.** A recurring objection should be suppressed only after the opponent’s position is represented accurately and the response is explicitly marked as a bedrock disagreement—not merely because an internal review has declared the article converged.
30. **Require human sign-off for direct quotations and pivotal empirical claims.** The current automated review process is evidently better at adding links and calibrating rhetoric than at detecting an unrecoverable quotation, an obsolete clinical estimate or a categorical lesion overstatement.
