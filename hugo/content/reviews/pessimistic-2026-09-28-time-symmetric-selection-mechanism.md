## Executive Summary

The body of the article is well calibrated. The mechanism itself has one serious internal conflict. The Libet resolution needs the readiness potential, which sits hundreds of milliseconds in the past, to stay open to selection. The modified growing block says the past is made of already-collapsed and irreversible outcomes. Squaring the two needs exactly the long-lived macroscopic neural superposition that the opening paragraph says this pathway avoids. Three smaller problems add to this. The article calls the selection both "retrocausal" (L71, L147, L201) and "not backward causation" (L106). The Bidirectional tenet paragraph brings back the inference from time-symmetry to retrocausality that the 07-30 pass removed from the body. And the "speculative but not unfalsifiable" verdict rests on three signatures: one the article itself calls non-discriminating, one with no stated prediction, and one that confuses a measurement limit with indeterminacy.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would pass over the quantum material and go after step 3 of the Mechanism (L91): "Consciousness participates in determining which transaction completes." The article never says what "participation" is, beyond being "not at a time". In the Dualism paragraph (L197), consciousness is "what makes there be a definite transaction". But on the transactional interpretation, transactions complete everywhere: in photodetectors, in stellar plasma, in every ion channel. Either something non-conscious completes those, and then the brain needs no extra selector, or consciousness completes all of them, which is panpsychism and goes well beyond the Map's dualism. The article never says which. Its "Dualism" argument therefore reduces to the claim that the selector is not a physical event because it is defined as what the physics leaves open. This is framework-boundary disagreement at bottom, but the question of what completes the transactions that don't involve a brain is a real gap. The Map should answer it, not just mark it.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett would go after the Phenomenological Fit section, especially "We don't experience lateness" (L116). The absence of felt lateness is exactly what the postdictive and constructive accounts predict: intentional binding (Haggard), Wegner's apparent mental causation, and Dennett and Kinsbourne's multiple drafts. A brain that backdates its own intention timestamps would produce "decisions feel simultaneous with their enactment". Libet's own data put reported W-time about 200 ms *before* movement, not at enactment. So the sentence misdescribes the phenomenology it cites. The section's opening hedge ("illustrative, not discriminating") admits that a forward-causal account predicts the same reports. It does not admit that a *purely physical* constructive account predicts them too, and that account is the rival Dennett actually holds.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark's objection is the strongest, and the article's own concessions make it sharper (see Issue 1). The opening paragraph (L39) sells time-symmetric selection as addressing Libet "rather than requiring macroscopic neural superpositions to persist long enough for forward-in-time selection to operate". Yet the Mechanism's step 1 is "The brain enters quantum superposition", and the Libet resolution has selection fixing "the entire correlated history—including the readiness potential" (L102). In the transactional interpretation, every absorption completes a transaction. The ion-channel and synaptic events that make up a 350–500 ms readiness-potential ramp are millions of separate absorptions, each of them its own completed transaction. No single transaction spans them, so "the selected package" cannot include them unless they stayed uncollapsed, which is the very superposition the decoherence section concedes fails by three to five orders of magnitude. Moving the burden onto the Zeno mechanism doesn't help, because the article itself says Zeno works "at each observation event" (L122). That is a time-local interface, and it cannot reach back 500 ms.

Tegmark would also note that "Atemporal selection is maximally minimal" (L199) sits badly next to L106, where the selection "fixes what the temporal ordering is". Fixing half a second of correlated neural history is not the smallest possible intervention.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch would say the article is honest that Vaidman, the TSVF's main developer, is an Everettian (L81). That concedes the formalism the Map uses works without collapse. The No Many Worlds paragraph (L203) says only that "MWI eliminates what the model requires". That is correct as boundary-marking, and it is appropriately modest. Deutsch's sharper point is that the Occam paragraph (L205) calls the transactional interpretation "well-developed physics" while leaving out that it is a minority interpretation facing Maudlin's contingent-absorber objection. The article's siblings (`concepts/retrocausality` L62–L64, `concepts/transactional-interpretation-of-quantum-mechanics` L56–L64, `topics/forward-in-time-vs-time-symmetric-selection` L61) all foreground Maudlin. This article, the one that actually *uses* the transactional interpretation as a mechanism, never mentions him. On the Everettian's view, pairing "draws on well-developed physics" with a silence about the main objection is using Occam's Razor selectively.

### The Empiricist

Popper's ghost would take apart L191, "The framework is speculative but not unfalsifiable". Look at the three signatures offered:

- **Contemplative phenomenology** (L187). The article's own L110 says phenomenology "motivates and illustrates the model without adjudicating between it and its forward-in-time rival". It is also compatible with physicalist constructivism. So a signature the article has already called non-discriminating cannot be what makes the framework falsifiable.
- **Timing correlations** (L188). "Does the relationship … match what atemporal selection predicts?" The article never says what that prediction is. Since the selection "determines the entire correlated history" (L102), *every* observed timing relationship is by construction part of the selected package, so no timing result could go against it.
- **Neural indeterminacy gaps** (L189). See Issue 4. The test is also asymmetric: closing the gap would count against the model, but "persists" has no end date, so the model can never lose on this test in practice.

The one real empirical commitment the mechanism has goes unstated. If selection "biases which outcome within the distribution becomes actual" (L161), then outcomes of the selected neural events would, in principle, depart from Born statistics. The article instead says the interpretations "make identical predictions for current experiments" (L183). That leaves the reader unsure whether the model predicts a Born-rule deviation (which would make it testable) or not (which would make "biases" empty).

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would point at "you select among *your* neural patterns, not someone else's" (L159) and at an atemporal selector standing outside the sequence it fixes. That is close to the permanent, unconditioned self (ātman) that Madhyamaka analysis rejects. The article then lists "Cessation states and the 'timeless' quality of deep concentration" (L187) as evidence. But the traditions that describe nirodha and jhāna read these states as the *absence* of a self-agent, not as the self glimpsing its own atemporality. Citing them in support of an atemporal selector reads them against the traditions that produced them, and the article should at least say so.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The Libet resolution conflicts with the modified growing block and with the "no macroscopic superposition" advantage
- **File**: [topics/time-symmetric-selection-mechanism.md](/topics/time-symmetric-selection-mechanism/)
- **Location**: L39 ("rather than requiring macroscopic neural superpositions to persist"), L89–L104 (Mechanism and Resolving Libet), L137–L149 (Modified Growing Block: "Past exists (accumulated collapsed outcomes)"; "Once actual, they're irreversibly past")
- **Problem**: For selection to "determine the entire correlated history—including the readiness potential", the neural states making up the readiness potential must still be open when selection occurs. The growing block says anything already actual is irreversibly past. So either (a) readiness-potential neural events were actualised when they happened, and selection can't include them, which breaks the Libet resolution; or (b) they stayed in superposition until the selection, which needs the long-lived macroscopic superposition L39 says this pathway avoids and which the decoherence paragraph concedes is 3–5 orders of magnitude short. The transactional interpretation adds a third difficulty: each absorption is its own transaction, so no single "transaction" spans a 500 ms neural ramp. The modified growing block puts atemporality "at the level of possibility" (L149), but the Libet resolution needs it to reach into what is already *actual*.
- **Severity**: High. This is the mechanism's central claim, and the article's own sections contradict it.
- **Recommendation**: Add a short subsection (about 150–250 words) under Resolving Libet or Open Questions that states the problem outright and gives the Map's best answer. Candidates: (i) the readiness potential is part of a classical correlated history whose *initial conditions* come from an earlier micro-selection, so selection fixes only the quantum seed and not the whole ramp; (ii) PTI's pre-spacetime possibility realm is where the correlation is fixed, with the time-ordering cost made explicit. Tone down L39's comparison with forward-in-time selection to match whichever answer is chosen: if (i), the "structural difference" from forward-in-time selection shrinks a lot. Do not strengthen the decoherence concession (per the 07-30 stability note).

### Issue 2: Retrocausal or not? Inconsistent statements of the article's core claim
- **File**: [topics/time-symmetric-selection-mechanism.md](/topics/time-symmetric-selection-mechanism/)
- **Location**: L71 ("The retrocausal reading this article develops"); L106 ("The selection is not backward causation but atemporal constraint satisfaction: it runs in no direction"); L147 ("**Retrocausal structure**"); L201 ("backward-pointing causation has the same status as forward-pointing"); related concept `[[retrocausality]]`
- **Problem**: The article says in some places that its mechanism is retrocausal and in others that it is not backward causation at all. These are different metaphysical claims, and they get different treatment from critics (Maudlin targets retrocausal transactional interpretations; "atemporal constraint satisfaction" invites the charge of being empty). A reader or LLM pulling out "what the Map claims" gets conflicting answers.
- **Severity**: Medium-High.
- **Recommendation**: Pick one framing and use it throughout. The safest is the L106 atemporal framing, with "retrocausal" kept only as the *description of the formal structure* that other interpreters give it (e.g. "what the retrocausal literature calls backward influence the Map reads as atemporal constraint"). Fix L71, L147 and L201 to match.

### Issue 3: The Bidirectional tenet paragraph brings back the time-symmetry → retrocausality inference that the body removed
- **File**: [topics/time-symmetric-selection-mechanism.md](/topics/time-symmetric-selection-mechanism/)
- **Location**: L201: "Time-symmetric physics makes bidirectional causation natural. If fundamental laws are time-symmetric, backward-pointing causation has the same status as forward-pointing."
- **Problem**: L71, fixed on 2026-07-30, says time-symmetry does not imply retrocausality (Price 2012: "maybe", only given further ontological assumptions; Friederich and Evans 2023). The tenet section states the inference the body just disowned. Classical mechanics and electromagnetism have time-symmetric laws and are not retrocausal, because the causal arrow comes from boundary conditions. This is the "sweep fixes the disclaimer and strands its dependents" pattern: the 07-30 repair touched L69–L71 but not the tenet section. The 07-30 stability note says not to "tighten" the formalism into a retrocausality result, and L201 does exactly that in a different section.
- **Severity**: High. It contradicts a stability-noted correction.
- **Recommendation**: Rewrite L201 conditionally: "*If* time-symmetric laws are read with the ontology the transactional interpretation supplies, backward-pointing influence stops being anomalous …". Cross-reference the L71 caveat.

### Issue 4: "Neural indeterminacy gaps" confuses a measurement limit with indeterminacy (epistemic/metaphysical equivocation)
- **File**: [topics/time-symmetric-selection-mechanism.md](/topics/time-symmetric-selection-mechanism/)
- **Location**: L189: "**Neural indeterminacy gaps**: The 60% prediction accuracy in Soon et al. leaves substantial room for undetermined factors… if it persists despite improved techniques, something beyond measured neural activity contributes to outcomes."
- **Problem**: Soon et al.'s about 60% is fMRI decoding accuracy against a 50% chance level, from a limited set of voxels at BOLD resolution. The shortfall is *epistemic* (limits of measurement). The heading and "undetermined factors" draw a *metaphysical* conclusion (indeterminacy). The final clause is true but trivial: "something beyond *measured* neural activity" includes unmeasured neural activity. This falls under the skill's interpretive-equivocation check. (It is separate from the 07-30 note that Soon is not *overstated*; the problem here is the reading-shift, not the figure.)
- **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: Rename the item (e.g. "Decoding ceilings"), and say that a lasting ceiling is evidence against the model only if it survives full-resolution measurement, which no foreseeable technique provides. Or cut the item.

## Counterarguments to Address

### What completes non-brain transactions?
- **Current content says**: Consciousness is "what makes there be a definite transaction" (L197).
- **A critic would argue**: The transactional interpretation (Cramer, Kastner) completes transactions everywhere without minds. If physics completes them elsewhere, the brain case needs no extra selector. If consciousness completes them all, the view is panpsychist.
- **Suggested response**: Say that the Map takes on only the *biasing* of transactions at the brain interface, with non-brain transactions completed by the physics' own stochastic rule, and that this is exactly what `concepts/transactional-interpretation-of-quantum-mechanics` L80 records the Map declining from Cramer. Reword L197 to fit.

### "No felt lateness" is predicted by constructive/postdictive accounts
- **Current content says**: Decisions "feel simultaneous with their enactment" (L116).
- **A critic would argue**: Intentional binding and postdictive timestamping predict this, and Libet's W precedes movement by about 200 ms.
- **Suggested response**: Qualify the phenomenology ("do not feel *late relative to the choice*") and name the physicalist constructive account alongside the forward-causal one in the L110 hedge.
