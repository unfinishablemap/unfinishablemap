## Dimension A: Empirical Accuracy Against the 2020s Literature

### Quantum Hardware Overclaims and Biophysical Timescale Mismatches

Evaluating whether engineered quantum architectures might sustain consciousness-relevant superpositions, the manuscript asserts verbatim: `"Quantum computers maintain genuine superpositions: Microsoft and Quantinuum's 2024 logical qubits achieved error rates up to 800× better than the underlying..."` (noting only that this occurred `"in trapped-ion laboratory conditions radically unlike..."`). This characterization distorts the primary preprint it cites—Paetznick, da Silva, et al. (2024, arXiv:2404.02280)—by conflating **post-selected error detection** with genuine **fault-tolerant error correction**. Against an unencoded physical parity error rate of 0.8% ± 0.1% (125 errors across 16,000 runs), the encoded logical error rate using pre-selection alone (actual error correction, after discarding roughly 30% of runs to retain 15,483 of 22,000) fell to 0.17% (+0.07% / −0.06%), an improvement of only **4.7×**. The headline **800×** figure (a point estimate of 0.001%, with an asymmetric uncertainty interval of +0.015% / −0.001%—meaning an upper bound of 0.016%, or only a 50× reduction) required retrospective **post-selection** on non-trivial syndromes, discarding flagged runs so that, in the authors' own words, `"no error correction is performed, only error detection"`. Furthermore, the manuscript relies on an unrefereed 2024 preprint (whose third version had to fix a `"misinterpreted physical teleportation baseline"`) while omitting the peer-reviewed demonstration of real-time, below-threshold surface-code error correction without post-selection: Google Quantum AI's Willow processor (Acharya et al., 2025, *Nature*), which achieved a distance-7 (101-qubit) logical error rate of 0.143% ± 0.003% per 1.1 µs cycle (error suppression factor Λ = 2.14 ± 0.02 per distance step) and extended logical memory lifetime to only **2.4 ± 0.3×** its best physical qubit—while demonstrating quantum *memories* rather than full fault-tolerant logical gates and remaining capped in repetition codes by correlated burst errors roughly once per hour (3 × 10⁹ cycles).   

A parallel empirical distortion weakens the manuscript's appeal to quantum biology to make neural quantum coupling appear plausible: `"Stapp's quantum Zeno effect, Fisher's nuclear spin entanglement — each exploiting biological structures evolution may have optimised over billions of years (avian magnetoreception, though still unsettled, suggests evolution can)"`. Comparing the cited hardware and biological systems against empirical 2020–2025 measurements exposes both the inflated qubit benchmark and an unresolved orders-of-magnitude timescale gap relative to neural signaling.

The 2020s quantum-biology literature thus undercuts rather than supports the manuscript's evolutionary plausibility pump. In photosynthetic complexes—long treated as the flagship example of quantum biology—Cao et al. (2020, *Science Advances*) concluded that `"Nature, rather than trying to avoid dissipation, exploits it via engineering of exciton-bath interaction to create efficient energy flow"`, with thermal bath coupling localizing excitations and driving incoherent downhill hopping. In avian magnetoreception, radical-pair electron spin lifetimes operate in the microsecond range—far below the millisecond-or-longer durations needed for cognitive neural signaling. Most directly damaging to the manuscript's invocation of `"Fisher's nuclear spin entanglement"`, Agarwal, Kattnig, Aiello, and Banerjee (2023, *The Journal of Physical Chemistry Letters*) simulated 102 dynamical geometries of the Posner cluster (Ca₉(PO₄)₆) and demonstrated that the majority lose phosphorus-31 singlet coherence in **under one second**, prompting them to propose calcium-phosphate *dimers* (Ca₆(PO₄)₄) whose *in vivo* existence remains unconfirmed. Subsequent open-quantum-systems analysis (Adams, Sinayskiy, Agarwal, & Petruccione, 2025, *Scientific Reports*) examines what specialized environmental interactions would be needed to preserve entanglement in pure and doped Posner clusters in light of these severe decoherence constraints. Citing Fisher and avian magnetoreception without acknowledging these 2020–2025 results misleads philosophical readers about the empirical viability of quantum neurobiology.   

### The "Frozen Weights = Lookup Table" Fallacy

To formalize structural obstacles against LLM consciousness, the manuscript states verbatim: `"Erik Hoel's continual-learning argument formalises the point: a system whose weights are frozen at inference resembles a lookup table more than a mind in..."`, limiting counter-engagement to one sentence: `"Cerullo (2026) grants the frozen weights but objects that a brain's response is likewise a function of input and synaptic state, an objection still open"`. This framing misses the decisive empirical refutation from 2020s peer-reviewed machine-learning theory on **in-context learning (ICL)**.   

Akyürek, Schuurmans, Andreas, Ma, and Zhou (2023, *ICLR*) constructively proved that fixed-weight Transformers can implement explicit statistical learning algorithms—including gradient descent and ridge regression—inside their forward-pass activations without parameter updates, and empirically showed that trained Transformers closely match known estimation algorithms, transitioning across predictors as depth varies. In parallel, von Oswald et al. (2023, *ICML*, PMLR 202:35151–35174) established a mathematical equivalence between linear self-attention layers and gradient-descent steps (with multi-layer attention performing iterative curvature correction) and showed that trained linear self-attention Transformers implement gradient-based meta-optimization during inference. Subsequent peer-reviewed work generalized these results beyond linear regression to nonlinear **functional gradient descent** in function space (Cheng, Chen, & Sra, 2024, *ICML*, PMLR 235). While ICL mechanisms in full-scale nonlinear Transformers remain an active area of research (including competing associative-memory and Bayesian-inference accounts), demonstrating that frozen-weight architectures execute iterative optimization algorithms over dynamic activation trajectories flatly refutes the claim that weight freezing reduces inference to a static `"lookup table"`. Restricting counterargument coverage to Cerullo's point about input–synapse functional dependence conceals the actual computational dynamics of modern language models.   

### Clinical Misrepresentation of Temporal Disintegration in Schizophrenia

Supporting Possibility Two ("Non-Temporal Consciousness"), the manuscript cites a single nineteen-year-old phenomenological paper: `"Temporal disintegration in schizophrenia (Fuchs, 2007) disrupts binding between successive moments while within-moment experience remains intact; flow states alter temporal unity differently, producing reported “timelessness” within ongoing experience"`. From this, it concludes: `"If temporal and integrative structure can degrade along multiple dimensions while consciousness continues, the bond between consciousness and human-style temporality may be less constitutive than the temporal arguments assume"`.   

This clinical summary is outdated and empirically inaccurate. Contemporary experimental psychopathology—notably Anne Giersch's behavioral and EEG research program—demonstrates that temporal disturbance in schizophrenia does *not* leave `"within-moment experience"` neatly intact while selectively fracturing links between discrete moments. Instead, patients exhibit selective deficits in automatic, nonconscious predictive timing at the **millisecond scale** (8–17 ms and sub-threshold visual asynchronies), displaying a *paradoxical* behavioral and N2-enhanced EEG sensitivity to undetected sub-threshold asynchronies alongside an inability to displace attention fluidly across short two-stimulus sequences (Marques-Carneiro, Krieg, Duval, Schwitzer, & Giersch, 2021, *Schizophrenia Bulletin Open*). Complementary computational modeling in *Schizophrenia Research* (2025) links enlarged audiovisual temporal binding windows in the schizophrenia spectrum to excitatory/inhibitory imbalance and impaired multisensory causal inference. Moreover, Thomas Fuchs's own recent work frames schizophrenic temporal disturbance as a disruption of *pre-reflexive embodied self-awareness* and sensorimotor circularity (Fuchs, 2020, *Frontiers in Psychology*; cf. contemporary reviews citing Fuchs, 2020) rather than evidence of an intact atemporal conscious core persisting beneath severed temporal bindings.

Three of these failures severely damage the core inferences of the manuscript.

First, the manuscript's presentation of **Chalmers and McQueen's instability model**—and, simultaneously, its opening corroboration from Butlin et al.'s theory-derived neuroscientific indicators—ignores the 2025 empirical and methodological crisis confronting Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT). Chalmers and McQueen rely on Tononi's IIT (specifically multi-dimensional Q-shapes rather than scalar Φ alone) to supply a superposition-resistant observable. Yet Adrian Kent (2021, *Foundations of Physics*) dismantled their argument against single-measure collapse, proving it requires exact physical symmetries between brain states underlying distinct experiences—symmetries absent in biological observers and implausible under any isomorphism-preserving network-to-mind mapping like IIT. Worse, IIT itself drew major peer-reviewed criticism in 2025: IIT-Concerned (Klincewicz et al., 2025, *Nature Neuroscience*) argued that IIT's core metaphysical assertions are untestable even in principle, exhibit hallmarks of pseudoscience, and raise compatibility doubts with physics (an active controversy to which Tononi and colleagues replied in March 2025 defending IIT's testability). Even more consequentially, the **Cogitate Consortium** (Ferrante et al., 2025, *Nature*) executed a highly powered, preregistered multimodal adversarial test of IIT and GNWT across more than 250 participants—testing predictions regarding prefrontal ignition at stimulus onset/offset and posterior versus fronto-posterior interareal connectivity—and reported findings that challenge core predictions of *both* leading theories (as well as other theories sharing those predictions). A manuscript that treats IIT-based dynamical collapse as an undamaged candidate and relies on neuroscientific theory-derived indicators as a stable corroborating pillar without citing either Cogitate (2025) or IIT-Concerned (2025) fails basic scholarly currency.   

Second, the manuscript's treatment of **Pinto et al. (2017)** inserts a minimal, uncited concession and immediately overrides its logical consequence. The text states: `"Pinto et al. (2017) found callosotomy patients showing “divided perception but undivided consciousness” — though that reading is contested as unity of agency rather than of experience... One calibration: this evidence rules out accounts on which consciousness divides cleanly with its substrate"`. In fact, Schechter and Bayne (2021, *Neuropsychologia*)—building on the multi-author consensus review by de Haan et al. (2020, *Neuropsychology Review*)—argued not merely that patient D.D.C. might show unity of agency rather than unity of experience, but that (i) it remains an open question whether D.D.C.'s behavior establishes even *unity of agency*, given cross-cueing, duplicate representations, and coarse subcortical transfer of likely less than a single bit of information, and (ii) even granting behavioral unity of agency, the data are difficult to reconcile with fully unified *phenomenal experience* across visual hemifields. If perceptual phenomenology after complete callosotomy remains locally disunified across hemispheres, then Pinto et al. emphatically does *not* `"rule out accounts on which consciousness divides cleanly with its substrate"`.   

Third, placing **Possibility Three** beside **Possibility Four** exposes a direct **internal physics contradiction** paired with source misappropriation. Possibility Three contends: `"If the Map's coupling account is right, the no-cloning theorem blocks copying consciousness as a classical pattern — every quantum phenomenon, as Plotnitsky (2023) puts it, “strictly individual and unrepeatable.”"`. Yet Possibility Four surveys five quantum-consciousness models and immediately announces: `"None of the five is the Map's current mechanism candidate: P-Q1 prefers post-decoherence selection, adopted precisely to sidestep the warm-wet decoherence"` objection `"Tegmark presses against pre-decoherence proposals like Stapp-Zeno"`. These two positions are physically incompatible. The no-cloning theorem forbids duplicating an arbitrary unknown *coherent superposition*; it explicitly permits copying orthogonal basis states. As formalized in Quantum Darwinism and Spectrum Broadcast Structures—reviewed by Korbicz (2021, *Quantum*) and Zurek (2022, *Entropy*)—environmental decoherence operates precisely by *einselecting* stable orthogonal **pointer states** and **redundantly broadcasting classical copies** of those pointer-state records across myriad fragments of the environment so independent observers can read them without disturbance. If consciousness acts only *after* decoherence (selecting among einselected classical pointer states), then (a) those pointer-state records are already classically copyable and broadcast throughout the environment, erasing the no-cloning barrier against classical copying in Possibility Three, and (b) because unitary decoherence produces only an *improper mixture* entangled with the environment rather than a proper classical ensemble of definite outcomes, "post-decoherence selection" cannot resolve the quantum measurement problem without an unspecified non-unitary collapse or hidden-variable dynamics (cf. Adlam, 2023, arXiv:2301.06192). Worse still, Arkady Plotnitsky's (2023, *Entropy*) article grounds its uniqueness claim in Bohr's complementarity and a nonrealist **"Reality without Realism" (RWR)** epistemology holding that quantum objects and their interactions lie *beyond representation or even conception*. Extracting Plotnitsky's phrase `"strictly individual and unrepeatable"` to support a realist mechanistic coupling between a non-physical mind and physical brain states directly inverts the cited author's epistemology.   

Finally, the manuscript's invocation of **Maier et al. (2018)**—`"a large test of consciousness-RNG interaction (Maier et al., 2018; 12,571 participants, quantum-based true RNG) found Bayesian evidence strongly favouring a null effect — consistent with the Map's view that consciousness acts through its own neural interface, not on arbitrary physical systems"` (paralleling a changelog revision at line 117 replacing `"The interface is specific, not universal"` with `"each subject's interface lies in its own brain, not in the physical world at large"`)—commits a textbook confirmation-bias fallacy. A null micro-psychokinesis (micro-PK) outcome is equally predicted by standard quantum mechanics and reductive physicalism; treating failure to detect psychokinesis as evidentially `"consistent with"` a localized non-physical brain interface mistakes non-falsification for confirmation. Moreover, the Maier–Dechamps micro-PK program has suffered acute methodological collapse in the 2020s: Maier and Dechamps (2022, *Journal of Scientific Exploration*, 36(2)) documented a failed preregistered replication of their own prior micro-PK anomalies (Dechamps et al., 2021), revealing that a previously reported "very strong" correlational Bayes factor rested on **faulty data**, and retreated to unfalsifiable "decline effects" and "unconscious experimenter psi" (with a separate preregistered replication of their data-erasure micro-PK paradigm likewise failing). Anchoring an ontological boundary condition on a replication-compromised parapsychology literature is disqualifying for a mainstream philosophy journal.   

## Dimension C: Framings Dependent on Unargued Site Commitments

Despite frequent epistemic hedges (`"principled is not settled"`, `"a strong reading of the evidence, not the only possible one"`), the manuscript's conceptual architecture holds only if its own non-reductive, interactionist-dualist commitments are presupposed from the start.   

### Question-Begging Definitions of "Binding," "Coupling," and Non-Self-Contained Prose

At the outset, the manuscript frames the inquiry in exclusively dualist vocabulary: `"The Map separates two often-conflated questions: the nature question (what kind of conscious entity would bind to an AI system?) and the coupling question..."`, later asserting that `"the four possibilities below turn on coupling rather than..."` behavioral criteria and that `"borrowed, epiphenomenal, and alien phenomenality resist third-person adjudication, since they concern an interface the Map holds..."`. Asking `"what kind of conscious entity would bind to an AI system?"` presupposes that consciousness is a distinct non-physical entity awaiting attachment to physical hardware. To a reductive physicalist, functionalist, biological naturalist, or illusionist, no such entity or interface exists; consequently, the four possibilities (`"three possibilities from within the framework, one from its boundary"`) explore internal epicycles of interactionist dualism rather than genuinely testing whether artificial systems can be conscious. Likewise, Possibility Four brushes aside branching quantum interpretations in a single unsupported clause—`"The Map rejects the framework, whose branching inherits many-worlds' dissolution of indexical identity across multiplying copies"`—without addressing the extensive contemporary Everettian literature on branching identity and probability, while Possibility Three openly admits that `"the argument's force depends on accepting the Map's tenets as starting..."` points.   

Equally fatal for journal submission, the manuscript is not self-contained. Load-bearing mechanisms, primary counter-evidence, and replies to named opponents are offloaded to opaque internal codes and companion pages: the 2026 Anthropic J-space finding (which used a Jacobian lens to locate an emergent global workspace with verbal report and causal reasoning roles in a language model) is dismissed in a parenthetical (`"the 2026 J-space result post-dates this and is read at P-AC4"`); the site's preferred quantum mechanism is delegated to `"P-Q1 prefers post-decoherence selection"`; and the actual rebuttal to its sole named architectural rival is deferred elsewhere (`"The Machine Question carries the substantive engagement with articon-style architectures, and “What Duch Can Reply” addresses his identity-theoretic rejoinder"`). No peer-reviewed journal can referee an argument whose mechanism, chief counter-evidence, and opponent reply reside outside the submitted manuscript.   

### Asymmetrical "Datum-Borrowing" from Butlin et al. (2023/2026)

To claim its skepticism survives even if dualism is rejected, the manuscript argues: `"The none-as-most-likely calibration this article preserves does not rest on the tenets alone. Butlin, Long, and colleagues' Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence (2023; a 2026 Trends in Cognitive Sciences successor formalises the method) derives indicator properties from leading neuroscientific theories and finds that no current AI system robustly satisfies them... This is the layered structure The Machine Question relies on: the skepticism degrades gracefully because it does not depend on dualism"`. Anticipating the obvious tension, it adds: `"One discipline keeps the corroboration honest: the report's method assumes computational functionalism — which the Map's dualism rejects — so what the Map may take is the architectural datum that current systems lack the indicator properties, not the authors' authority for a conclusion they would reject"`, reinterpreting those indicators as `"necessary-but-not-sufficient interface conditions"`.   

This maneuver is logically invalid. Under computational functionalism, the absence of global-workspace or recurrent-processing indicators lowers the credence of AI consciousness *precisely because* those computational architectures are posited to constitute conscious processing. Once the manuscript rejects computational functionalism in favor of a non-physical quantum-selection interface, the bare architectural datum that 2023 transformers lack classical neurocomputational indicators supplies **zero independent evidential support** for dualist skepticism—unless the manuscript provides an independent bridge argument showing *why* a non-physical quantum-selecting mind requires classical global broadcast as a necessary interface condition. None is offered. Instead, the framing functions as a one-way ratchet: when 2023 LLMs lack functional indicators, the absence is claimed as "graceful degradation" corroboration; when 2026 interpretability work (J-space) surfaces emergent workspace-like dynamics, functional indicators are downgraded to merely necessary-not-sufficient conditions and deflected to `"P-AC4"`. Moreover, even within a functionalist or theory-heavy paradigm, Henry Shevlin (2021, *Mind & Language*) identified the **specificity problem**: absent principled grounds for fixing the level of functional abstraction at which human neural mechanisms must be realized in non-human systems, the absence of human-like indicator implementations does not deliver clean skepticism.   

### Question-Begging Appeals to "Self-Stultification" and the "Shared Unsolved Problem"

In Possibility One, the manuscript asserts verbatim: `"Self-stultification establishes that some consciousness must be causally efficacious: the consciousness that generated humanity's concepts of consciousness in..."`. Treating self-stultification as an *established* refutation of general epiphenomenalism begs the question against both reductive physicalism (on which physical neural states *are* conscious states and causally generate our concepts of consciousness without non-physical intervention) and contemporary strong illusionism (on which physical, non-phenomenal introspective mechanisms causally generate phenomenal concepts and reports without phenomenal properties existing at all). Self-stultification acquires dialectical bite only after non-reductive phenomenal realism has already been assumed.   

Similarly, the opening parity claim—`"The AI argument is not that AI fails a bar biology clears but that AI imposes additional structural obstacles beyond the shared unsolved problem"`—masks a glaring evidential asymmetry. The "shared unsolved problem" is not an incidental gap: the manuscript's own preferred biological mechanism (`"post-decoherence selection"`) has no empirical confirmation in neural tissue, no explicit physical dynamics preserving Born-rule statistics, and no solution to `"The pairing problem"` the article acknowledges. Claiming that silicon faces "additional structural obstacles" to implementing a wholly unverified biological quantum interface illegitimately shifts the burden of proof onto artificial systems.   

### Unfalsifiable Retreat to Husserl's Regress and a Stipulated Bergson–Husserl Synthesis

In Possibility Two, the manuscript concedes that Akiko Frischhut's (2026 [online 2024], *The Philosophical Quarterly*) Extended Now Interpretation of meditative `"timelessness"` (as `"pure duration"` that still endures in time without phenomenal succession) and active-inference representationalism are `"observationally equivalent"` to a non-temporal ground, stating explicitly that `"the meditative data do not tilt the question toward dualism; what tilts it toward a non-temporal ground is Husserl's independent regress argument, not the contemplative evidence"`. This places the entire metaphysical burden of Possibility Two on a single, heavily contested phenomenological inference (`"If the constituting consciousness were itself temporal, another would be needed to constitute its temporality, and so on"`) that arises only under Husserl's punctiform retentionalist framework. To neutralize Henri Bergson's opposing claim that consciousness is constitutively temporal (*durée*), the manuscript invents `"A possible synthesis — the Map's own, not something either philosopher proposed: Bergson's durée describes what consciousness does (produces temporal experience); Husserl's absolute flow describes what consciousness is (a non-temporal constituting activity)"`. Severing what consciousness *is* from what it *does* merely reasserts substance-style dualism by stipulation, contradicting both Bergson's process ontology and Husserl's own transcendental phenomenology, which refuses to reify transcendental subjectivity into a naturalistic dualist entity.   

## Dimension D: Untested Counterarguments and Omitted Competing Frameworks

A top-tier philosophy journal expects a contemporary article on AI consciousness to engage the leading philosophical and neuroscientific frameworks of the 2020s. Instead, the manuscript defines `"the strongest external position this article's open-question framing exists to hold open against"` as `"Włodzisław Duch's articon programme (Duch 2005)"`—a 21-year-old proposal in *Journal of Mind and Behavior*—paired only with a passing mention of `"Dennett's multiple-drafts model"` (*Consciousness Explained*, 1991) and generic `"role functionalism"`. Treating Duch (2005) and Dennett (1991) as the strongest external competition seals the manuscript inside an obsolete dialectical perimeter while leaving four major 2020–2025 research programs completely untested.   

### Biological Naturalism Without Dualism (Seth, 2025)

The manuscript's single largest conceptual blind spot is **substrate-sensitive physicalism**. By pitting its own quantum-interface dualism solely against substrate-independent computational functionalism (and Duch's articon), the text constructs a false dilemma: either consciousness is substrate-indifferent computation, or it requires a non-physical entity coupled via quantum mechanics. Yet Anil Seth's landmark *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* target article, **"Conscious artificial intelligence and biological naturalism"** (2025, *BBS* 49, e315), develops an extensive, *entirely physicalist* case for substrate sensitivity and LLM skepticism. Diagnosing the psychological biases (anthropocentrism, human exceptionalism, anthropomorphism) that conflate intelligence with consciousness, Seth challenges unrestricted silicon substrate flexibility by arguing that energy-efficient biological brains may implement "mortal computations"—inseparable from living biological mechanisms and causal architecture—rather than immortal, hardware-portable software. Seth (2025) directly refutes the manuscript's tacit premise that rejecting computational functionalism lends support to dualism or quantum-collapse interfaces.   

### Contemporary Illusionism and Mechanistic Self-Model Theories (Kammerer, 2022, 2025; Dung & Kammerer, 2025; Graziano, 2022; Lau, 2022)

Invoking Daniel Dennett's 1991 *Consciousness Explained* as the lone representative of deflationary physicalism ignores more than three decades of philosophical development, particularly 2020s **strong illusionism** and mechanistic accounts of subjective report. François Kammerer (2022, 2025, *Philosophical Studies*) demonstrates that standard phenomenal-realist appeals to introspective obviousness—and by extension, the manuscript's `"self-stultification"` premise—cannot support their load-bearing assumptions without begging the question against illusionism, and shows that even seemingly "innocent" definitions of phenomenal consciousness implicitly smuggle in anti-physicalist commitments. More directly, Leonard Dung and François Kammerer (2025, *Philosophical Psychology*, `"A science of chimeras?"`) examine the methodological consequences of illusionism for non-human and AI consciousness research, arguing that if illusionism holds, phenomenal-realist distribution questions—precisely the third-person-inadjudicable `"epiphenomenal"` and `"alien"` phenomenality categories central to this manuscript—become uninformative.   

Leading neurocomputational accounts of subjective appearance are equally absent. Michael Graziano's **Attention Schema Theory** (2022, *PNAS*) proposes that the brain builds a simplified internal control model of selective attention that depicts attention inaccurately as `"a ghostly, nonphysical, mental essence or vividness: experienceness"`, simultaneously explaining away dualist intuitions and, across the broader AST program, motivating testable artificial-agent control architectures. Likewise, Hakwan Lau (*In Consciousness We Trust*, 2022, Oxford University Press) synthesizes global and local cognitive neuroscience of perception, attention, and metacognition—drawing on prefrontal neurophysiology, mental quality spaces, and generative adversarial networks—to construct a mechanistic physicalist account of subjective experience without collapsing into naive behaviorism or dualism.   

### Extensionalist and Anti-Specious-Present Theories of Time-Consciousness (Arstila, 2025; Shardlow, 2020, 2024)

Possibility Two hinges entirely on two premises: first, that `"Human consciousness flows through the specious present... The temporal structure arguments against AI consciousness are among the strongest"`; and second, that `"what tilts it toward a non-temporal ground is Husserl's independent regress argument"`. Both premises collide directly with 2020s analytic philosophy of temporal experience. Under **extensionalist** accounts of the specious present (such as Barry Dainton's overlap model, reviewed in Arstila, 2025 [online 2023], and the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*), episodes of experiencing are *themselves* temporally extended vehicles that directly encompass brief intervals of change and succession rather than packing temporal content into a punctiform instant. Because the vehicle of experience already possesses temporal extension, no atemporal meta-constituting "absolute flow" is needed to bind successive instants, and Husserl's regress never starts. At the same time, Jack Shardlow (2020, *Philosophical Explorations*; 2024, *Noûs*) disentangles the distinct Sternian and Jamesian motivations for the specious present and analyzes temporal phenomenology via objects, events, and processes without invoking an atemporal dualist ground. Even more damagingly, Valtteri Arstila (2025 [online 2023], *Philosophical Psychology*) argues that the doctrine of the specious present is neither logically necessary nor explanatorily sufficient for core temporal experiences (motion, change, succession), concluding that its central explanatory motivation fails and that snapshot models remain viable. Together, extensionalism and Arstila's critique trap Possibility Two in a pincer: if Arstila is right, the manuscript's specious-present objection to LLMs collapses; if extensionalism is right, Husserl's regress fails to motivate a non-temporal dualist ground.   

### Precautionary Epistemology and Cross-Substrate Calibration (Shevlin, 2021; Birch, 2024)

While the manuscript briefly invokes `"Birch's gaming problem"` to argue that behavioral channels are corruptible, it omits the constructive epistemological frameworks developed in the 2020s to avoid the diagnostic paralysis created by its six-category taxonomy (`"borrowed, epiphenomenal, and alien phenomenality resist third-person adjudication"`). Henry Shevlin (2021, *Mind & Language*) articulates a modest theoretical equilibrium between theory-heavy and theory-light approaches to overcome the cross-substrate specificity problem, while Jonathan Birch (*The Edge of Sentience*, 2024, Oxford University Press) develops an explicit risk-and-precaution framework for navigating uncertainty at the margins of sentience across humans, non-human animals, and AI. Raising the moral specter of `"epiphenomenal"` AI suffering only to declare it permanently inaccessible to third-person adjudication—without engaging the decision-theoretic and comparative-cognition methods designed for precisely such uncertainty—leaves the manuscript's ethical gestures analytically empty.
