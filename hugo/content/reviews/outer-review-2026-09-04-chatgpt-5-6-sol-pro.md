# External referee report: “Value in Selection Positions”

**Recommendation: major revision.**

I inspected the live register entry, last modified 27 August 2026, its linked empirical articles, the quantum-interface dependency register, and the changelog through 3 September 2026. The entry has improved its terminology and now explicitly acknowledges both underdetermination and inherited mechanism debt. Those concessions are substantive. They do not, however, support its current classification of P‑VS2 as a grade-C, developed, directly discriminable, framework-independent position. [value-in-selection](/positions/value-in-selection/)

## 1. Overall verdict

The register’s strongest position is P‑VS1’s admission that value-sensitive selection is an aspiration held on prior commitments rather than an empirically established result. Its weakest position is P‑VS2’s claim that the proposed battery directly discriminates phenomenal causal efficacy from mechanism-sufficiency.

The problem is not that the empirical phenomena are fictional. Reward-learning signals, wanting/liking dissociations, affective-forecasting errors, pain asymbolia and interoceptive dissociations are all real. The problem is **causal identification**: none of the cited paradigms varies phenomenal valence independently of the neural valuation, motivational, attentional and learning processes that could produce the same behavioural results. The entry has assembled a useful research agenda, not a direct falsification battery.

My central recommendations are therefore:

- Retain P‑VS1’s low credence and grade D.
- Downgrade P‑VS2 from moderate/C/developed/direct/non-internal to approximately low/D/programme/indirect/framework-internal.
- Treat P‑VS3 and P‑VS4 as philosophical or metaethical positions for which “empirical discriminability” is inapplicable, not indirect.
- Replace talk of Stapp providing a “worked physical implementation” with “a conceptual candidate lacking a worked neural or quantum model.”
- Recast every proposed P‑VS2 result in terms of competing quantitative causal models rather than “supports this horn/disconfirms that horn.”

## 2. Empirical and citation audit

### 2.1 Reward prediction error

There is a genuine correction in the newest forecasting article: it now distinguishes learned value V/Q, which may inform policy, from reward-prediction error δ, which updates that value. But the parent selection article still says that dopaminergic prediction-error signals “track expected valence,” and later contrasts felt liking with a “reward model denominated in dopaminergic prediction error.” Those formulations conflate three variables:

1. received reward;
2. predicted or learned value;
3. the discrepancy between the two.

A prediction error is not the stored expectation and normally is not itself the policy variable consulted at choice time. The newer terminology map gets this right; the older prose does not. [valence-and-conscious-selection](/topics/valence-and-conscious-selection/)

The framing is also scientifically dated. Dabney and colleagues found evidence for a population-level distributional code in mouse VTA neurons rather than one canonical scalar estimate. Other 2020s work shows dopamine responses varying with reward rarity and supports heterogeneous or efficiently coded prediction errors. This does not refute reward-prediction-error theories, but it does make “dopamine carries expected valence” an especially poor summary. [Nature+2Nature+2](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1924-6.pdf)

**Citation verdict:** Schultz 2016 is real and fairly attributed as a review of dopamine RPE coding. It is not an adequate primary-source foundation for the stronger claims the article draws from it. The problem is not fabrication but construct substitution.

**Required correction:** write that phasic activity in many dopamine neurons is consistent with one or more varieties of prediction-error signal; that such signals update learned value estimates; and that neither the errors nor those estimates should be identified with felt valence without a separate argument.

### 2.2 Wanting/liking dissociation

The two principal animal studies are real and substantially correctly described.

- Peciña and Berridge’s opioid microinjections altered positive taste-reactivity in a localized accumbens-shell region, while effects on intake were more spatially extensive.
- Tindell and colleagues showed that sodium depletion could transform the incentive salience of a cue for concentrated salt before rats had experienced that salt as pleasant in the new physiological state.

These findings provide strong evidence that incentive salience, learned prediction and operational taste-reactivity are dissociable. [wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork](/topics/wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork/)

The opening nevertheless overstates what the studies establish. It initially glosses “liking” as “felt hedonic impact,” then later correctly explains that Berridge’s quotation marks bracket consciousness and that objective “liking” is an orofacial or neural-behavioural measure distinct from subjective pleasure. The first formulation should be removed. Rat taste-reactivity is evidence about an operational hedonic process; it does not establish what, or whether, the animal consciously feels.

The slogan “dopamine drives wanting; opioids drive liking” is also too clean when exported to humans. Recent antagonist studies give a less modular picture: opioid antagonism can affect motivational measures as well as consummatory processing; dopamine antagonist effects are sometimes weak or absent; and one 2024 randomized study found no behavioural drug effect despite an opioid-related orbitofrontal imaging effect. The animal dissociation remains important, but the human pharmacology does not license a simple dopamine/wanting versus opioid/liking partition. [eLife+2eLife+2](https://elifesciences.org/articles/55797)

The claim that the dissociation establishes that there is “no single neural value currency” also does not follow. Distinct systems for incentive salience and consummatory response are compatible with later-stage common-currency representations in vmPFC or OFC. Indeed, the 2024 human paper discusses both common and reward-specific valuation architectures. Component dissociation is not evidence against every form of common coding. [wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork](/topics/wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork/)

The addiction claim should be labelled as the incentive-sensitization account, not presented as a settled description of addiction generally. It is a serious theory, but craving, pleasure, habit, withdrawal relief, model-based expectation and compulsive control failures vary across substances, stages and patients.

**Citation verdict:** no fabricated core wanting/liking citation was found. The two primary animal studies are accurately identified. The main faults are:

- objective “liking” intermittently becoming conscious pleasure;
- an over-simple neurotransmitter mapping;
- a theory-specific addiction interpretation presented too generally;
- an invalid inference from fractionated reward processing to the absence of all common currencies.

### 2.3 Affective forecasting

The classic references to immune neglect, focalism and forecasting error are real. The article also deserves credit for recognizing that selection requires correct ordering more than correct absolute magnitude: an intensity error that preserves preference rank need not alter choice. It additionally acknowledges that the forecasting literature is mixed. [affective-forecasting-gap](/concepts/affective-forecasting-gap/)

But the text then says that “both sides concede” a duration result. That is too categorical. Recent studies show considerable contextual accuracy, particularly relative or within-person accuracy. Carlson and colleagues found that forecast accuracy varies by component and context, while Moeck and colleagues found meaningful relative accuracy and comparatively small absolute errors in intensive longitudinal data. The correct conclusion is not that impact bias has disappeared, but that its size, sign and decision relevance are highly design- and outcome-dependent. [NCBI+1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10062255/)

The article also needs a sharper distinction between:

- **anticipated emotion**: a belief or judgment about how one will later feel;
- **anticipatory emotion**: the dread, excitement or relief felt now.

The assertion that anticipation “is itself felt” moves too quickly from the first to the second. People can make semantic, inferential or socially learned predictions without vividly simulating the predicted feeling. Present affect can carry a forecast, but not every forecast is a currently felt valence signal.

That matters to P‑VS2. Manipulating forecasts, mood or imagery ordinarily also changes attention, episodic retrieval, perceived risk, learned value and response strategy. Even a forecast-induced choice change would not establish that phenomenal valence entered a psychophysical selection law.

**Citation verdict:** the foundational citations are real and mostly fair. The evidential overreach lies in generalizing from some absolute or duration errors to the choice-relevant rank reversals P‑VS1 actually needs.

### 2.4 Pain asymbolia

Berthier, Starkstein and Leiguarda’s 1988 study is real and clinically important. Its six patients recognized painful stimulation but showed absent or inadequate withdrawal and emotional reactions. The same report, however, also records hemiparesis, cortical sensory loss, neglect, body-schema disorders, broader failures to react to threats and anatomically extensive lesions. This is not an intervention that removed phenomenal unpleasantness while leaving “all the physical machinery of pain” unchanged. [Stafforini PDF+2Stafforini PDF+2](https://pdf.stafforini.com/berthier-1988-asymbolia-pain-sensorylimbic.pdf)

The Map now concedes that the dissociation is not a pristine one-variable experiment and cites the contemporary taxonomic dispute. Yet the same article continues to call asymbolic patients “empirical partial zombies,” says they possess all the physical machinery except suffering, and later describes the syndrome as evidence for bidirectional interaction. Those statements undo the earlier qualification. The cautious and expansive readings cannot both stand. [pain-asymbolia](/concepts/pain-asymbolia/)

Several distinctions are being compressed:

- nociceptive detection;
- sensory quality;
- unpleasantness;
- bodily ownership or mineness;
- threat appraisal;
- action urgency;
- withdrawal and avoidance learning.

Berthier demonstrates that some of these can dissociate. It does not establish that the causal difference is the absence of an irreducible phenomenal property rather than damage to affective appraisal, policy selection, bodily salience or threat learning.

The omitted evidence is important. A heavily lesioned patient studied by Feinstein and colleagues retained robust—and in some conditions heightened—pain responses despite extensive insula, cingulate and amygdala damage, undermining any simple necessity claim for those structures. A 2024 focused-ultrasound study found that modulating insular targets could alter pain-related ratings and physiological or electrophysiological measures, supporting a causal neural contribution without isolating phenomenality. Gerrans has developed an active-inference/self-model account of asymbolia that directly contests the modular “intact sensory pain minus felt badness” interpretation. [NCBI+2NCBI+2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4734900/)

There is also a minor bibliographic issue. Duval and Klein first appeared online in July 2025, but the volume and pagination cited by the Map belong to the 2026 issue of *Philosophy of Science*. “2025 online first; 2026 issue publication” would remove the ambiguity. [resolve-he.cambridge.org+1](https://resolve-he.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/093BD46EB13CA8B9DE533B5EC82C816D/S0031824825100986a.pdf/pain_asymbolia_is_probably_still_pain.pdf)

For P‑VS2, the proposed prediction that asymbolia should impair “selection efficacy, not merely motivation” is not yet operational. Motivation is implemented partly by changes in evidence accumulation, vigour, response thresholds, effort allocation and policy value—all mechanisms of selection. Merely stipulating that the outcome must be selection rather than motivation does not separate them.

**Citation verdict:** no fabricated reference found. Berthier is real but over-read; the recent philosophical dispute is real; the issue year needs normalization.

### 2.5 Interoception

The article’s Craig-derived picture—heterogeneous bodily channels converging on “a single visceral-integration cortex” or “shared cortical integrator”—is now too centralized. Current evidence supports extensive convergence, but within a distributed and hierarchical system spanning multiple insular and cingulate regions, somatomotor and default-mode networks, thalamic and hypothalamic structures, brainstem nuclei, striatum, amygdala and other areas. The 2025 7-Tesla mapping study explicitly describes a whole-brain allostatic-interoceptive system, not one cortical hub. [interoceptive-consciousness-and-the-interface](/topics/interoceptive-consciousness-and-the-interface/)

Peripheral and brainstem work further shows organ-, layer- and modality-sensitive pathways rather than indiscriminate early convergence. Zhao and colleagues found multidimensional coding in vagal sensory neurons, while Ran and colleagues found organ-tuned and partly topographic organization in the nucleus of the solitary tract. These findings do not eliminate integration; they replace “one integrator” with parallel, partially convergent processing. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35296859/)

The phrase “objective heartbeat-detection performance” also requires qualification. Heartbeat-counting tasks can be solved partly through beliefs about normal heart rate, elapsed-time estimation and other non-interoceptive strategies. Later psychophysical work found substantial belief bias, imprecision and poor metacognitive calibration. Moreover, cardiac and respiratory task performance can be weakly correlated, challenging any unitary capacity called “interoceptive accuracy.” [Europe PMC+2Pure+2](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32464170)

“There is no neutral interoceptive percept” is an unsupported universal. Some interoceptive experiences are strongly valenced; it does not follow that noticing a heartbeat, breath phase or mild visceral sensation must already be pleasant or unpleasant in a selection-relevant sense.

Recent macaque work is especially relevant to the value-selection fork: pharmacologically induced peripheral bodily changes altered tolerance for aversive stimuli. That result shows how bodily state can bias decision behaviour through an ordinary peripheral–central neural pathway, without identifying phenomenal valence as an additional causal variable. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12002244/)

**Citation verdict:** the foundational references are genuine. The problem is that a Craig-centric 2002 architecture and contested heartbeat measures are being used as though they represented the current state of the field.

### 2.6 Reference-integrity summary

Across the five empirical clusters checked:

- **Fabricated core references found:** none.
- **Concrete metadata defect:** Duval and Klein should be standardized as 2026 issue publication, noting 2025 online-first status.
- **Mischaracterized constructs:** reward-prediction error as expected valence; objective “liking” as felt pleasure; asymbolia as all pain machinery minus phenomenology; distributed interoception as one cortical integrator.
- **Outdated simplifications:** unitary scalar dopamine coding; a clean dopamine/wanting versus opioid/liking split in humans; heartbeat counting as straightforward objective accuracy.
- **Source-class problem:** several load-bearing empirical claims are anchored primarily in reviews—Schultz 2016, multiple Berridge reviews, Craig 2002, Seth 2013, Barrett and Simmons 2015—where contemporary primary studies should carry the evidential burden.

### P‑VS1

P‑VS1 is the best-calibrated entry. It expressly says that the position is grounded in the Map’s priors, has no worked valence-to-physics coupling and has not defeated mechanism-sufficiency. The only significant calibration problem is that low “structural centrality” hides two different questions. P‑VS1 may be peripheral to the five tenets, yet it is load-bearing for the site’s claims about local teleology and the causal significance of phenomenal value.

### P‑VS2

P‑VS2’s grade C is based on the fact that “real experimental paradigms populate the battery.” That is the wrong evidential object. There is grade-C-or-better evidence that wanting and liking dissociate, that forecasting can err, and that asymbolia exists. There is not grade-C evidence that these paradigms discriminate a nonphysical phenomenal cause from neural mechanisms.

Likewise, “developed” and “direct” are untenable where:

- selection efficacy is undefined;
- “equal attentional salience” is not independently secured;
- phenomenal valence has no validated intervention;
- the quantum selector has no model;
- no effect-size or likelihood prediction is specified;
- several tests are admitted to face in-principle behavioural equivalence.

The “framework-internal only: no” rating is especially hard to defend. The experiments are external; their interpretation as tests of a valence-to-quantum coupling is wholly dependent on the Map’s dualist and quantum-selection architecture.

### P‑VS3

P‑VS3’s main rival cannot be just illusionism. One can be a realist about phenomenal consciousness while denying that phenomenal goodness is identical with intrinsic value. Alternatives include desire-satisfaction theories, objective-list theories, fitting-attitude or buck-passing views, response-dependent accounts, naturalist realism and various forms of expressivism.

Its stated defeater—something like a felt goodness that is not good—is also perilously close to being excluded by definition. If “evaluative phenomenal character” is introduced as the phenomenal property that constitutes value, asking for that property without value is not an independent test. The article needs cases formulated without presupposing its identity claim.

### P‑VS4

The aesthetic case does not yet establish six irreducible phenomenal value dimensions. Experiences of tragedy or the sublime may combine negative first-order affect with interest, absorption, relief, achievement, insight or higher-order enjoyment. More importantly, the value of an artwork, activity or achievement must be distinguished from the intrinsic value of the subject’s experience of it.

P‑VS4 also creates a problem for P‑VS1. If hedonic, aesthetic, epistemic, autonomy and relational values are genuinely irreducible, how does the supposedly unitary anticipatory-valence currency compare them? Either a later scalar decision variable commensurates them—weakening the practical force of pluralism—or conscious selection requires a plural/vector-valued rule rather than the simple maximization sketch currently offered. The register notices that P‑VS2 tests only the hedonic dimension, but does not repair the underlying aggregation problem.

## 4. Mechanism debt: honestly stated, inconsistently enforced

The target’s mechanism-debt paragraph is appropriately strong: no toy Hamiltonian, master equation or simulation exists; bias without aggregate Born-rule deviation remains unresolved; and value-sensitive selection requires an additional coupling. The upstream quantum-interface register makes the same point and says that downstream claims of causal work must not outrun it. [value-in-selection](/positions/value-in-selection/)

The debt is then relaxed in at least five ways.

### 4.1 “No implementation” becomes “worked out”

Within the target itself, the attentional coupling is first said to have “no worked physical implementation” and then described as “worked out in Stapp’s observation-rate model.” The parent article goes further, calling Stapp and Eccles “worked physical implementations.” But the quantum-interface register says no worked formal model currently exists and classifies Stapp-Zeno as a programme-level candidate demoted on decoherence-timing grounds. [value-in-selection](/positions/value-in-selection/)

A published verbal or mathematical proposal is not yet a worked neural implementation. The consistent wording would be:

> Stapp provides a conceptual mapping from attentional observation rate to Zeno stabilization, but the Map has no validated neural target, coherence calculation, master equation or simulation realizing that mapping.

### 4.2 P‑VS2 is allowed to be more direct than its upstream mechanism

The upstream register explicitly says that adding a second indirect psychophysical channel is not a move from indirect to direct. P‑VS2 nevertheless rates its tests “direct,” although the putative physical selector, its variables and its observable consequences have not been specified. [quantum-interface](/positions/quantum-interface/)

### 4.3 The pain article re-upgrades compatibility into evidence

The pain article first says that predictive-processing and dualist explanations share the dissociation, then later calls asymbolia evidence for bidirectional interaction. That is exactly the inference the mechanism-debt convention is supposed to prevent: a phenomenon compatible with mental causation is treated as positive evidence for it without a discriminating model. [pain-asymbolia](/concepts/pain-asymbolia/)

### 4.4 The middle path relocates rather than discharges the debt

“Valence modulates attention, and attention changes selection” can reduce the number of proposed mind-to-physics actuators. It does not explain how an irreducible phenomenal valence changes attention. Either:

- phenomenal valence causally changes a mental attentional variable, in which case an intramental causal law is owed;
- a neural valence representation changes neural attention, in which case mechanism-sufficiency is doing the work;
- or phenomenal and neural valence are identified, which abandons the dualist formulation of this particular fork.

The route through attention is a useful architecture, but it is not yet a mechanism.

### 4.5 The tests are disconnected from the Born-preservation problem

Even a behavioural valence effect would not show that a quantum selector produced it, much less that the selector was counterfactually efficacious while preserving exact aggregate Born statistics. P‑VS2 tests downstream psychology while the upstream debt concerns the possibility of a physically coherent selection process. The register needs an explicit bridge from psychophysical result to quantum-interface inference.

## 5. Why P‑VS2 is not presently a discriminating battery

The core identification problem can be represented schematically.

```
Mechanism-sufficient model:
stimulus/state X → neural valuation N → felt valence Φ
                              N → attention/motivation A → choice C

Value-sensitive interaction model:
the same pathways, plus Φ → hypothesized selector Q → A and/or C
```

Every proposed experiment either changes X or N, or infers Φ from report or behaviour. None intervenes on phenomenal valence while holding neural valuation, learned value, salience, attention and motivation fixed. Therefore, a correlation between reported valence and choice is expected under both models.

The individual tests have the following defects:

1. **Valence selection advantage.** Ordinary reinforcement learning, Pavlovian biases, attentional capture, threat processing and evidence-accumulation models all predict faster or more reliable responses to valenced stimuli. Matching “attentional salience” is also problematic because valence may partly constitute the measured salience.
2. **Uniform ten-bit bandwidth.** No derivation shows that either horn predicts a different bandwidth. A scalar valence currency being compatible with a bottleneck does not mean a shared bottleneck supports value-blindness.
3. **Neural selection signatures.** Correlation with valence, frontal theta or coherence remains correlational unless the hypotheses assign different quantitative distributions to the signatures.
4. **Pain asymbolia.** Lesions alter appraisal, bodily representation, motivation, learning and attention together. “Selection efficacy rather than motivation” currently names the desired interpretation rather than an independently measured variable.
5. **Attention-salience clamp.** A clamp could abolish valence effects under several ordinary mediation models. It may also alter the experience whose influence is supposedly being tested. A mediation analysis is not by itself a metaphysical intervention.
6. **Wanting/liking conflict.** Objective “liking” does not identify conscious pleasure; self-reported liking can be post-decisional; and incentive salience, learned value, habit and explicit preference can all diverge. A choice following reported liking would not isolate phenomenal causation.
7. **Disrupting the conscious hedonic signal.** No intervention is specified that disrupts conscious pleasure while leaving its complete neural basis and all downstream mechanisms intact. Anaesthesia, lesions, pharmacology, distraction and stimulation all alter physical variables.
8. **A complete mechanistic model.** Successful out-of-sample neural prediction would strongly favour mechanism-sufficiency, but it could not logically exclude a redundant or realization-level phenomenal cause. Conversely, residual error would primarily indicate model misspecification, not dualism. This is a credence shifter, not a direct falsifier.
9. **Minimal organisms.** “Felt rather than learned value” cannot be inferred from the very choice behaviour the proposal is intended to explain without an independent consciousness criterion.

P‑VS2 needs tests for which the competing accounts imply materially different likelihoods, not outcomes to which each account can append a retrospective interpretation.

## 6. Philosophical problems not resolved by the empirical literature

### 6.1 The fork is framework-forced, not empirically exhaustive

The entry correctly says that the fork is forced by the Map’s own commitments. That qualification is crucial. Outside those commitments, there is an obvious third family:

> felt valence is identical with, constituted by or realized in a neural valuation state, and is causally efficacious in virtue of that realization.

That position is neither illusionism nor “felt valence merely accompanies selection.” It is physicalist phenomenal realism. Unless it is represented, P‑VS2 cannot be described as framework-independent.

Even within dualism, the horns are not exhaustive. Selection might be directly intention-sensitive or reason-sensitive, with phenomenal valence merely one item of content informing intention. The Map’s own graduated middle path already demonstrates that the nominal binary admits intermediate causal architectures.

### 6.2 Value constitution does not imply selection causation

P‑VS3 may be true while P‑VS1 is false. “Suffering is intrinsically bad” is a claim about what constitutes value. It does not entail that suffering’s badness causes avoidance, enters a selection law or is what evolution optimized. Normative importance and causal efficacy are different relations.

The rhetoric sometimes slides between:

- value **matters normatively**;
- a representation of value **informs action**;
- phenomenal value **causes neural selection**.

Evidence for the first does not establish the third.

### 6.3 Nonrandom selection need not maximize felt valence

The parent article argues that without valence, selection would be arbitrary or purposeless. That is a false dichotomy. A selector could be structured by intentions, beliefs, commitments, policies, learned goals, moral norms, task rules or reasons that are not reducible to current hedonic tone.

People frequently select actions that worsen present anticipatory affect: entering an anxiety-provoking examination, disclosing wrongdoing, undergoing surgery, keeping a painful promise or resisting an addictive urge. The Map can redescribe all these as higher-order valence, but doing so risks making valence synonymous with “whatever ultimately wins,” rendering the hypothesis circular.

### 6.4 Anticipatory valence may be information rather than an actuator

A currently felt dread can represent future danger and influence deliberation. That does not show that its phenomenal character is the causal variable. The information carried by the state, its precision, learned associations or attentional consequences might do the work. The register needs an argument separating causal efficacy of representational content from causal efficacy specifically in virtue of phenomenal feel.

### 6.5 Plural intrinsic value destabilizes a scalar selection law

P‑VS4 says several value dimensions are irreducible. P‑VS1 says anticipated valence supplies a common scalar currency. Those claims can coexist only with an explicit account of:

- how distinct dimensions are compared;
- whether anticipatory valence aggregates them;
- whether selection uses a vector or scalar;
- how moral override differs from an ordinary change in learned policy;
- what happens in genuinely incommensurable cases.

At present, pluralism is added at the metaethical level while the selection mechanism remains hedonically scalar.

## 7. Competing frameworks and counterarguments insufficiently engaged

The site does already discuss reward-prediction error, incentive salience, affordance competition, active inference and illusionism. It should not be criticized for omitting those entirely. [value-in-selection](/positions/value-in-selection/)

The material does, however, omit or underdevelop several stronger alternatives.

### Computational and neuroscientific alternatives

- **Common-currency neuroeconomics:** shared valuation in vmPFC/OFC can coexist with wanting/liking fractionation.
- **Sequential-sampling models:** drift-diffusion, race, urgency-gating and attentional-DDM models can turn valence into changed drift rate, starting point, boundary or sampling allocation without phenomenal causation.
- **Pavlovian–instrumental transfer and action vigour:** these directly explain why valenced cues alter selection speed and effort.
- **Model-based/model-free arbitration, habit and revaluation:** these provide several ways wanting, explicit liking and choice can conflict.
- **Homeostatic reinforcement learning:** bodily state can recompute incentive values without positing a felt selector.
- **Somatic-marker, affect-as-information and risk-as-feelings accounts:** current feelings may guide action through ordinary embodied neural representation—a more serious middle position than “mere accompaniment.”
- **Constructed-emotion and appraisal theories:** valence may be distributed, context-dependent or conceptually constructed rather than a unitary primitive currency.
- **Global-workspace, recurrent-processing and higher-order accounts:** conscious access can affect report, planning and flexible control through neural availability without phenomenal properties entering a separate physical law.

### Metaphysical and causal alternatives

- Physicalist phenomenal realism and realization-based mental causation.
- Epiphenomenalist phenomenal realism: valence can be real and constitute welfare while remaining causally idle.

- Causal exclusion and overdetermination arguments.
- Interventionist accounts of mental causation, especially the requirement that a purported cause be independently manipulable or add predictive information after its physical realizers are controlled.

### Metaethical alternatives

P‑VS3 and P‑VS4 need engagement with desire-satisfaction, objective-list, perfectionist, fitting-attitude, response-dependent and expressivist accounts. In aesthetics specifically, the entry should distinguish:

- value of the experience;
- value of the represented object or artwork;
- epistemic or moral value obtained through the experience;
- mixed affect;
- higher-order or attitudinal enjoyment;
- relief, curiosity, absorption and achievement.

Without those distinctions, negative affect accompanying tragedy or the sublime does not yet prove an irreducible aesthetic phenomenal value dimension.

## Improvements to the site’s evidential methodology

1. **Adopt a claim–citation ledger.** For every empirical citation record species, sample, design, manipulation, operational construct, reported result, source type and the additional inference made by the Map.
2. **Require causal graphs for causal claims.** A proposed test should identify physical mediators, latent phenomenal variables, confounders and the intervention needed to distinguish causation from correlation.
3. **Impose a discriminating-test rule.** Do not call a test direct unless the rival hypotheses imply materially different P(data∣H), not merely different verbal interpretations after the result.
4. **Separate four evidential levels.**

- behavioural or neural dissociation;
- evidence that a phenomenal state is present;
- evidence that the phenomenal state is causally efficacious;
- evidence for a particular metaphysical or quantum mechanism.
5. **Propagate dependency calibration.** No downstream position should be “direct,” externally supported or developed as a causal-mechanism claim while P‑Q10 remains open, unless that downstream position supplies an independent bridge that genuinely discharges the debt.
6. **Use primary-source anchors.** Reviews may organize a literature, but each load-bearing empirical statement should be attached to at least one primary experiment and the strongest recent counterevidence.
7. **Require two serious mechanism-sufficient models for every proposed positive result.** Compatibility with dualism should never be counted as confirmation until the best neural/computational alternatives have been assigned explicit predictions.
8. **Create a controlled terminology glossary.** At minimum: reward, learned value, prediction error, incentive salience, objective liking, conscious pleasure, anticipated emotion, anticipatory emotion, valence, salience, motivation, selection efficacy and intrinsic value.
9. **Apply negative-evidence symmetry.** A successful neural model should count against an extra phenomenal cause without being described as deductively eliminating it; an unexplained residual should count first as model failure, not automatically as evidence for dualism.
10. **Replace support-direction labels with update rules.** State how large, replicable and specific an effect would have to be to move each confidence band, and how null results, manipulation checks and failed phenomenal isolation would be interpreted.
