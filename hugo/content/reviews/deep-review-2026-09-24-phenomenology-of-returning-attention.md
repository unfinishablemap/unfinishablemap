## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found
- **Brefczynski-Lewis et al. 2007, result-direction inverted.** The article said "experienced meditators showed less activation in attention-regulation regions ... The phenomenology of ease corresponds to measurable neural efficiency." The PubMed abstract (PMID 17596341) reports an **inverted U**: experts averaging 19,000 h showed *more* activation than novices, and only experts averaging 44,000 h showed less. The 2026-07-14 review's own note said "inverted-U," but the text never matched it. **Fixed**: the article now states the inverted U and scopes the ease claim to the highest-practice group. The sibling `concepts/meditation-and-consciousness-modes` already had this right, so nothing needed propagating.
- **Four-phase model misattributed to Lutz et al. 2008.** The model was credited to Lutz 2008, but Lutz et al. 2008 (PMC2693206 full text) describe *three regulative skills*: monitoring, disengaging and redirecting. The four-interval cycle (MW / awareness / shifting / sustained attention) is Hasenkamp et al. 2012's model (PMID 21782031 abstract: "This model proposes four intervals in a cognitive cycle"). **Fixed**: Lutz now gets the three skills and Hasenkamp the four-interval model.
- **Hasenkamp 2012 overclaimed.** The article said the paper found "experienced practitioners tending toward shorter wandering episodes and faster transitions." The abstract reports only that phase activations "were modulated by lifetime meditation experience." Hasenkamp's own summary (DailyGood) says experienced meditators *deactivated* a default-mode region more quickly after noticing, which is not shorter wandering episodes. **Fixed**: the claim is restated as experience-modulated activation with faster default-mode disengagement. The claim that the gap shortens is now supported by practitioner report plus Lutz 2008's verified statement that advanced practitioners "have an especially acute ability to notice when the mind has wandered."
- **Network labels drifted from the source.** The article said "dorsal attention network." Hasenkamp reports *executive-network* elements during shifting and sustained attention. **Fixed** in Phase Two and Phase Three. The article also said salience activity came "before any re-engagement"; that ordering holds only because the intervals were built around the button press. It is now restated as the interval ordering, and the button-press design is named.
- **Smallwood & Schooler 2015 framing.** The article called it a review of "cross-cultural findings on mind-wandering." The abstract has no cross-cultural component. **Fixed**: the article now cites it for the experimental literature linking mind-wandering outcomes to meta-cognition, which is in the abstract.
- **Orphan inline cite, Frankish.** The new section named "Frankish's distortion thesis" but had no References entry. **Fixed**: added Frankish 2016 (*JCS* 23(11-12):11-39, the source of quasi-phenomenal properties) and Gorbachev & Frankish 2025 (*Synthese* 205(6):247, "Illusionism and the distortion thesis", verified via Crossref). The distortion thesis appears in a co-authored paper, so the inline attribution now cites both works rather than giving it to Frankish alone.
- **Internal tension (new section vs body).** The article said "No one has yet shown that these particular reported structures carry distinct neural signatures," while the body cites salience, executive and default-mode signatures for the phases. **Fixed**: the sentence now says those correlates track the functional transitions, which the illusionist already grants, and do not show that refined reports track phenomenal rather than quasi-phenomenal properties.
