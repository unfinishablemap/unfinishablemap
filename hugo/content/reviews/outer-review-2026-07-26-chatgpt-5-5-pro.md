# Outer review: “Quantum Immortality and the Quantum-Suicide Survival Argument”

## Verdict

**Major revision required. The article should be removed from “converged” status.**

The article contains a defensible **Map-relative conditional argument**:

> Given both an objective single-outcome ontology and a numerically determinate subject that persists only along the actual causal history, the mere existence of an Everettian component containing a living successor does not establish that this subject should expect to survive.

That is coherent. It also usefully emphasizes that existence of a survival branch, rational anticipation of survival, personal identity, and evidential confirmation of Many Worlds are different questions.

The article presently makes substantially stronger claims, however:

1. It presents rejection of quantum immortality as an overwhelming disciplinary consensus and describes the argument as a reductio “including” for its architects.
2. It describes Tegmark’s later remarks about ordinary deaths as a “mature retraction” of his idealized argument.
3. It says objective collapse itself supplies the fact of which successor is “me.”
4. It says collapse sidesteps the measure problem “entirely.”
5. It identifies Moravec’s 1988 discussion as the earliest published version.
6. It selectively or incorrectly summarizes Papineau, Aranyosi, and O’Brien.

Those stronger claims do not survive the primary-source audit. The latest specialist source already cited by the article says that Everettians remain divided, explicitly classifies Tegmark as an immortalist, and argues that rejecting idealized quantum immortality carries substantive costs. Tegmark’s 1998 paper itself presents quantum suicide as a genuine first-person prediction and possible discriminator between interpretations, not as a reductio. [topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument/)

## What the article gets right

The exposition of the basic hidden inference is good. It correctly separates:

- “A living branch exists.”
- “There will be a living successor.”
- “I should expect to be that successor.”
- “Survival should guide my decisions.”
- “Repeated survival would confirm MWI.”

The article also deserves credit for acknowledging that Everettian mortalism is not proprietary to the Map. Carroll, Papineau, Wallace, Mallah, and others deny that the existence of a surviving branch warrants certainty of survival. Its concession that the Deutsch–Wallace machinery is already part of the general Everettian probability programme—and therefore that quantum immortality may add no independent theoretical debt—is particularly important. The disclaimers concerning Parfitian reductionism, Madhyamaka no-self analysis, and the Map’s non-deflationary conception of the subject are also real improvements over a simple “Many Worlds cannot explain why I am here” objection. [topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument/)

The division of labour with *Death and Consciousness* is broadly sensible. That neighbouring article states the framework boundary more carefully: an Everettian can describe branch-local survival, while the Map demands a further counterfactual-exclusion fact. The quantum-immortality article adds the apparatus, probability questions, and torment argument. [topics/death-and-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/death-and-consciousness/)

These strengths make revision preferable to replacement. But they do not rescue the article’s historical, sociological, or collapse-identity claims.

## 1. The “consensus reductio” framing is unsupported

The opening says that “almost no one, including its own architects,” treats quantum immortality as a genuine prediction. It later invokes “the consensus among physicists and philosophers of physics” and concludes that “almost everyone agrees” the result is absurd. No survey or representative literature review is supplied for any of those sociological quantifiers. [topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument/)

More seriously, the primary sources cited by the article point in the opposite direction.

In the 1998 paper, Tegmark proposes the experiment as an interpretation-discriminating case. He says that under collapse the observer should expect a few trials followed by death, whereas under MWI the observer will hear the harmless result with certainty, can privately rule out collapse to increasing confidence, and can “experimentally convince” herself that MWI is correct. Whatever one thinks of the argument, Tegmark is plainly presenting it there as a first-person prediction, not as an absurdity intended to refute naïve MWI. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9709032)

O’Brien’s 2025 *Synthese* article begins by saying that proponents of MWI are **divided**. It calls Tegmark an “immortalist” and Carroll, Papineau, and Wallace “mortalists.” Its thesis is that rejecting idealized quantum immortality is tenable but carries methodological and metaphysical costs. That is incompatible with using O’Brien as evidence for an already settled consensus. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11229-025-05304-z.pdf)

Mallah’s paper is a direct attempt to debunk the inference from MWI to quantum immortality. Gao’s 2022 analysis argues that quantum suicide is not relevantly different from ordinary experiments and cannot feasibly test MWI. These sources establish a substantial anti-immortalist literature, but the existence of a substantial anti-immortalist literature is not evidence that “almost no one” accepts any idealized version. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.0187)

A defensible replacement would be:

> Most prominent contemporary Everettians discussed here reject the inference from the existence of a survival branch to certainty of personal survival. Tegmark’s original idealized argument and O’Brien’s recent defence of its costs show, however, that the issue is not closed and that several distinct quantum-immortality claims must be separated.

### Tegmark’s 2014 remark is a scope restriction, not a demonstrated retraction

The later observation that ordinary accidents and deaths are usually gradual and non-binary undermines the extrapolation from an idealized quantum gun to **general immortality from ordinary causes**. It does not by itself withdraw the 1998 claim about an idealized apparatus satisfying the relevant conditions.

O’Brien’s 2025 classification of Tegmark as an immortalist reinforces this reading. Calling the 2014 remark a “mature retraction” therefore exceeds the evidence. “Later restriction on applicability to ordinary deaths” would be accurate. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11229-025-05304-z.pdf)

The article also retrofits a neatly numbered three-condition test into the 1998 paper. Tegmark explicitly requires the trigger-to-firing interval to be shorter than the characteristic time of human perception; a genuinely quantum source and a lethal gun are built into his apparatus. But the paper does not present the later popular three-item list in the form the article attributes to it. The article should say that the ingredients are present in 1998 and were later summarized as three criteria, rather than claiming that Tegmark formally specified that triad there. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9709032)

## 2. The measure objection is important but not “decisive” without further premises

The article’s central probabilistic point is sound in conditional form:

> If rational anticipation in an Everettian universe must be distributed according to Born weight, then the existence of a very-low-weight survival component does not warrant certainty of survival.

For nnn independent equal-amplitude trials, the surviving component’s amplitude magnitude is 2−n/22^{-n/2}2−n/2, while its Born weight is 2−n2^{-n}2−n. The article calls amplitude “its share of total weight,” conflating a coefficient with its squared norm. The neighbouring probability article correctly states that Born probabilities are proportional to **squared amplitude**. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9709032)

The larger difficulty is that Born-weighted anticipation is not a neutral premise that can simply be applied to settle the debate. It is the issue under dispute. A quantum-immortality proponent can agree that the total weight of living successors becomes tiny while arguing that first-person anticipation should be conditioned on there being a future experience. The mortalist must explain why branches without an experiencing successor still affect present anticipation, caring, or decision. Papineau, Wallace, Mallah, and others offer answers, but an answer has to be presented rather than replaced with “the measure is decisive.”

Sebens and Carroll offer one of the strongest positive accounts: a period of self-locating uncertainty after decoherence but before an observer registers an outcome, combined with a separability principle intended to yield Born-rule credences. Friederich and Dawid argue that the quantum version of that principle is motivated by the empirical success of the Born rule and therefore cannot independently derive it. The appropriate verdict is “substantive and contested,” not “decisive.” [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.7577)

This produces an internal inconsistency. The quantum-immortality article says the measure objection is decisive while also saying the general Everettian probability problem remains unresolved. The neighbouring probability article is more careful: the Deutsch–Wallace theorem is rigorous conditional on its bridging axioms, while the status of branching indifference, diachronic consistency, and the connection between betting and observed frequencies remains contested. [topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument/)

The article should distinguish:

- **Internal conditional result:** granted Born-weighted anticipation, naïve survival certainty fails.
- **Foundational result:** whether Everettian theory adequately justifies that weighting remains disputed.
- **Prudential result:** even if a live successor exists, a rational agent may still care about dead and injured successors in proportion to branch weight.
- **Evidential result:** repeated survival need not confirm MWI merely because the observation is unsurprising conditional on survival.

Those are not one “measure objection.”

## 3. Collapse avoids multiplicity, but does not by itself supply personal identity

The article’s strongest tenet-protective move occurs when it says that under collapse there is “a single actual successor and a determinate fact about which one is me,” and that collapse supplies the indexical fact that Everett lacks. [topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument/)

The first part is approximately right for a single-outcome theory: there is no multiplicity of macroscopically actual successors requiring cross-branch allocation. But the second part does not follow from collapse dynamics alone.

The current Tenets page now explicitly identifies two background posits:

1. A numerically determinate subject persisting through time.
2. Objective single-outcome actualization.

Those are distinct. Collapse may supply physical exclusivity; the Map’s theory of subjecthood must supply diachronic identity and the rule connecting the subject to the surviving physical process. [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

That distinction matters because the Map’s *Personal Identity* article says identity requires consciousness persistence, causal continuity, and indexical uniqueness. It also says that in a genuine fission case neither successor would be the original person. The quantum-immortality article therefore owes an account of why eliminating one physical alternative through collapse creates a positive identity relation to the remaining successor. “Only one remains” removes competition; it does not by itself establish that the original subject persists. [topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/)

A more accurate formulation would be:

> Objective collapse supplies outcome exclusivity. The Map’s independently posited persisting subject and psychophysical continuity relation then determine whether that subject continues in the actual outcome.

That is less rhetorically dramatic, but it correctly displays the argument’s dependencies.

### “Collapse sidesteps measure entirely” is too broad

Objective-collapse models introduce stochastic dynamical laws precisely to determine outcome distributions. GRW- and CSL-type models modify Schrödinger evolution with nonlinear stochastic terms and make predictions that differ, in principle, from standard quantum mechanics. They do not dispense with probability or outcome measures. They avoid the specifically Everettian problem of assigning self-locating credence across simultaneously actual branches. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.6314)

Nor do all collapse models literally erase every nonselected component. The GRW tails problem concerns the persistence of low-amplitude tails after localization and has been treated as a potentially serious challenge to simple claims that alternatives “genuinely do not” remain. The Map may favour an ontology on which only one macro-outcome is actual, but it must state that ontology rather than treating “collapse” as a single, interpretation-neutral solution. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00763475)

The defensible comparative claim is consequently narrower:

> Single-outcome theories avoid the Everettian branch-self-location version of the quantum-immortality problem, though they retain their own probability, ontology, tails, and subject-continuity questions.

## 4. Several physics and empirical formulations are overstated

### “Every measurement splits the world”

This is acceptable as introductory shorthand only if immediately qualified. Tegmark himself says that a metaphysical split postulate is a misrepresentation: Everettian theory contains one evolving wavefunction, with decoherence determining when components may be treated approximately as noninteracting branches. Branching is emergent, approximate, and not restricted to sharply delineated laboratory measurements. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9709032)

The article’s argument does not require exact splitting, so there is no reason to retain the less accurate formulation.

### “Collapse interpretations that are otherwise empirically equivalent”

This is false if it includes dynamical objective-collapse theories. Tegmark distinguishes GRW-type modified dynamics precisely because it changes the calculational recipe, and modern collapse-model literature emphasizes that such models make experimentally distinguishable predictions. Textbook Copenhagen-style collapse and MWI may agree operationally over ordinary laboratory regimes; objective-collapse models need not. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9709032)

This is particularly important on a site whose own mechanism invokes objective reduction.

### “Real deaths are rarely quantum-triggered”

The intended point is reasonable but poorly worded. In a universal quantum theory, macroscopic biology and accidents are ultimately quantum-physical processes. The relevant distinction is not simply “quantum versus non-quantum,” but whether death is produced by a controlled, isolated, rapidly amplified, effectively binary quantum event with no conscious intermediate state.

O’Brien also discusses false-vacuum decay as a theoretical example of unexpected, instantaneous death caused by a quantum fluctuation. That does not show that such an event will occur, but it defeats an unrestricted claim that no physically relevant death could satisfy the abruptness condition. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11229-025-05304-z.pdf)

### “Injury is vastly more probable than full recovery”

That may hold in some generalized-death scenarios, but it is not a generic empirical law. The distribution depends on the mechanism, the definition of conscious survival, and the future dynamics. More importantly, Aranyosi’s paper does not merely “develop” Lewis’s torment conclusion: it explicitly argues, based on empirical considerations, for a substantially more reassuring conclusion than Lewis’s. The present wording suppresses the source’s argumentative direction. [Istvan Aranyosi](https://istvanaranyosi.net/resources/Should%20we%20fear%20qt%20final.pdf)

### Finite survival is not yet indefinite immortality

The article passes too quickly from survival after any finite series of trials to indefinite survival. The latter requires additional assumptions:

- survival retains nonzero amplitude at every future stage;
- physically possible continuations continue to support consciousness;
- identity survives extreme alteration or degradation;
- an infinite limiting history can be treated in the same way as every finite history.

A finite sequence always containing a nonzero survival component does not by itself establish an actually completed infinitely surviving subject. This is an important objection to the strongest immortality claim and should be stated independently of the measure objection.

## 5. Citation and attribution audit

### Euan Squires predates the claimed 1988 origin

The claim that Moravec supplied the earliest published version in 1988 is incorrect or, at minimum, indefensible without discussing Euan Squires. Squires’s *The Mystery of the Quantum World*, first published in 1986, describes a quantum-triggered gun, says this produces a form of Russian roulette in which one cannot really lose, and concludes that immortality is guaranteed from the relevant first-person perspective. O’Brien expressly notes that the book’s first edition appeared in 1986. [Internet Archive+1](https://archive.org/stream/dli.scoerat.3336themysyeryofthequantumworld/dli.scoerat.3336themysyeryofthequantumworld_djvu.txt)

Moravec remains historically relevant, but the article should say that related survival arguments appeared in Squires 1986, Moravec 1988, and Marchal’s computationalist work, rather than assigning uncontested priority to Moravec.

### Marchal is analogous, not “structurally identical”

Marchal’s primary 1991 paper is grounded in mechanism: the brain is replaceable by a digital machine at an appropriate level, duplication yields first-person indeterminacy, and identity questions arise in transmitter/copy cases. It includes cases in which the probability of remaining alive differs from the probability of being a particular copy. That is related to quantum immortality, but it is not structurally identical to Tegmark’s unitary quantum-gun argument. [Iridia](https://iridia.ulb.ac.be/~marchal/publications/M%26PI_15-MAI-91.pdf)

The current sentence also combines Marchal’s copying arguments with broader claims about all computations being realized, apparently importing later universal-dovetailer or Platonist machinery into the description of the 1988/1991 work. Marchal’s publication record confirms the 1991 paper, while the exact 1988 conference citation needs fuller primary bibliographic verification and page details. [Iridia+1](https://iridia.ulb.ac.be/~marchal/publications.html)

“Computationally analogous survival argument” would be safer than “structurally identical conclusion.”

### Tegmark is accurately cited but inaccurately positioned

The bibliographic metadata for the 1998 paper is correct. The characterization of its argumentative stance is not. Tegmark presents the experiment as a first-person discriminator and speaks of certainty of observing the harmless result under MWI. His later restriction concerning ordinary deaths should not be called a retraction of that idealized claim without further textual evidence. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9709032)

The 2014 quotation appears to be faithfully reproduced, but the site’s own research notes acknowledge that the exact three-condition wording and quote were initially taken from a Wikipedia summary rather than established from the primary text. That should have prevented the article from building a strong “mature retraction” interpretation on the passage. [research/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument-2026-07-08](https://unfinishablemap.org/research/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument-2026-07-08/)

### Papineau’s dialectical position is inverted

The article says that Papineau’s 2004 companion piece pressed Lewis on whether MWI permits genuine pre-measurement uncertainty and suggests that its upshot is that comforting quantum immortality is self-undermining.

Papineau’s own abstract states the opposite overall purpose: to show that Lewis’s difficulties are insubstantial, that Everettian metaphysics has a coherent account of probability, and that it may account for probability better than orthodox metaphysics. A subsidiary discussion may concern uncertainty or indexical information, but the article currently presents a defence of Everettian probability as though its overall force were anti-Everettian. [King's College London](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/david-lewis-and-schrodingers-cat/)

There may also be a conflation with **Peter J. Lewis**. O’Brien attributes the claim that there is no genuine pre-measurement uncertainty, only indexical information to acquire, to Peter J. Lewis’s 2007 work. The quantum-immortality article attributes essentially that position to David Papineau without supplying pages. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11229-025-05304-z.pdf)

Papineau’s 2003 paper, *Why You Don’t Want to Get in the Box with Schrödinger’s Cat*, is the more directly relevant life-or-death decision paper and should be cited. It argues against treating the existence of a living successor as a reason to disregard death risk. [JSTOR+1](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3329140)

The 2004 reference should also include its page range, 153–169.

### Aranyosi is cited in prose but absent from the references

Aranyosi’s *Should We Fear Quantum Torment?* is mentioned as developing Lewis’s worry but is missing from the bibliography. More importantly, its abstract says that the paper argues for a conclusion “much more reassuring” than Lewis’s torment scenario. The article may describe the problem as “quantum torment,” but it must also report Aranyosi’s answer. [Istvan Aranyosi](https://istvanaranyosi.net/resources/Should%20we%20fear%20qt%20final.pdf)

### O’Brien’s first name is wrong

The body calls him “Matt O’Brien.” The author of the 2025 *Synthese* paper is **Mark William O’Brien**. The reference’s initials are correct, but the prose attribution is not. [topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument/)

The treatment of his thesis is also selective. O’Brien does not merely catalogue the costs paid by a mainstream that has already rejected quantum immortality. He begins from an explicit division between immortalists and mortalists and argues that rejecting idealized applications is tenable but costly. He additionally separates survival and evidential questions and connects retrospective quantum immortality with anthropic reasoning. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11229-025-05304-z.pdf)

The statement that the 2004 paper was David Lewis’s only work on quantum interpretation is biographical rather than philosophically relevant and is not supported by the cited paper. Even if ultimately true under a carefully specified definition, it adds no argumentative value and creates an unnecessary verification burden.

## 6. Counterargument coverage

The article names several objections, but it does not organize the debate clearly enough to show which objection answers which claim.

### Idealized quantum suicide versus generalized immortality

Tegmark’s idealized apparatus and the claim that ordinary ageing or accidents produce subjective immortality are different theses. The three-conditions objection primarily attacks the latter generalization. It does not by itself defeat the idealized thought experiment.

### Prospective survival versus retrospective evidence

Before the experiment, the question is what the subject should expect and how they should act. After an improbable series of survivals, the question is whether those observations confirm MWI. Tegmark, Gao, and O’Brien address the evidential issue in different ways. The article presently moves between anticipation, decision, and confirmation without consistently separating them. [arXiv+2PhilSci Archive+2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9709032)

### Expectation, caring, and identity

An Everettian mortalist need not identify one future person as uniquely “really me.” They may instead hold that:

- all branch successors stand in an appropriate continuity relation;
- present rational concern should extend to all of them;
- concern is weighted by Born measure;
- creating many dead or injured successors is bad even if one low-weight successor remains alive.

That is a different response from saying that no surviving branch contains a continuing person. The article tends to compress these views into the claim that Everettian mortalists must somehow exclude the living successor. In fact, many mortalists accept the living successor and deny only that its existence warrants certainty, exclusive anticipation, or indifference to the other successors.

The neighbouring *Many-Worlds Interpretation* article gives a stronger presentation of the Saunders–Wallace reply: branch-local de se attribution can secure a determinate “I am here” within each branch. The Map’s objection is then that local indexicality and the global cross-branch continuity relation used in decision theory may not fit together. The quantum-immortality article should use that more sophisticated formulation rather than implying that Everett contains no indexical facts at all. [concepts/many-worlds](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/many-worlds/)

### The anthropic challenge

O’Brien argues that at least some ways of rejecting retrospective quantum immortality may also undermine structurally similar anthropic explanations. The article refers vaguely to “methodological costs” but never states this argument. Because O’Brien is its only recent specialist citation, omitting his central comparative argument is a serious gap. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11229-025-05304-z.pdf)

### The consciousness threshold

Quantum immortality requires a criterion for when a damaged continuation still supports experience and when it counts as the same person. Gradual cognitive disintegration, anesthesia-like states, coma, memory destruction, radical personality alteration, and minimal consciousness are not captured by a binary alive/dead distinction. The torment discussion approaches this problem but does not integrate it with the Map’s own consciousness-persistence and causal-continuity criteria.

### The safest conclusion

The article should state explicitly that no version of the argument supplies a rational basis for attempting a lethal experiment. Even accepting MWI does not establish certainty of personal survival, preservation of cognition, avoidance of suffering, or evidential benefit to anyone else. Tegmark’s own setup has nearly all external observers witness the experimenter’s death. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9709032)

## 7. Integration with the Map

### Good link structure, weaker conceptual synchronization

The article links to the appropriate neighbouring pages: probability, personal identity, indexical knowledge, haecceity, death, Many Worlds, and interactionist dualism. The problem is not discoverability; it is that those pages no longer say exactly the same thing.

### Conflict with the current Tenets formulation

The Tenets page now separates objective actualization from the numerically determinate subject and describes the current physical model as baseline objective reduction plus consciousness modulation specifically in neural systems. The quantum-immortality article instead treats “collapse” as simultaneously supplying physical selection, counterfactual exclusion, identity persistence, and the determinate “me.” [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

This is the same “semantic version skew” identified by the site-wide review recorded in the 20 July changelog: corrected tenets and positions can outrun stale concept and topic pages. The quantum-immortality article, last modified on 19 July, is a direct example. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

### An omitted consciousness-causal question

Because the Map is not merely a generic collapse site, it must address whether consciousness could influence a survival-correlated outcome.

The Tenets page says objective reduction operates generally while consciousness can modulate outcomes in appropriate neural systems. The quantum-immortality article should therefore answer:

- Does the external detector collapse before conscious processing begins?
- Is conscious modulation restricted to neural alternatives and therefore unable to bias the gun’s result?
- If the trigger becomes entangled with a conscious neural state, what prevents survival-conditioned conscious selection?
- How does the proposed modulation remain Born-compatible across repeated trials?
- Why does first-person conditioning not generate a Map-specific analogue of quantum immortality?

The likely answer is that macroscopic amplification produces objective reduction before any neural selection corridor becomes relevant, and that conscious modulation is local and statistically constrained. But the article must say so. Otherwise it quietly substitutes ordinary stochastic collapse for the site’s actual consciousness-causal theory. [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

### Tension with *Personal Identity*

The personal-identity article says neither successor in a genuine fission case is the original subject. The quantum-immortality page says that once collapse leaves one successor, there is a determinate fact that this successor is “me.” Those positions can be reconciled, but only through an explicit continuity rule. Collapse eliminates one rival; it does not explain why the remaining process inherits the original subject rather than constituting a new one. [topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/)

### Overstatement in *Haecceity and Indexical Identity*

That article says no quantum interpretation adequately addresses the haecceity of conscious experience. O’Brien explicitly discusses haecceity and numerical distinctness, while Saunders, Wallace, Sebens, Carroll, and others offer indexical or branch-relative accounts.
