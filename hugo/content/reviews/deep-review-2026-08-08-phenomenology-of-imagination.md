## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. Zeman (2024) is single-authored — "et al." is wrong, and the previous
ledger ratified the error.** Verified at Crossref and OpenAlex: the *Trends in
Cognitive Sciences* 2024 review "Aphantasia and hyperphantasia: exploring
imagery vividness extremes" (28(5):467–480, DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2024.02.007) has
**exactly one author, Adam Zeman**. The 2026-06-27 ledger stated *"'et al.'
faithful (multi-author review)"* — a false ratification. RESOLVED: inline cite
and References entry corrected to `Zeman, A. (2024)`; volume/issue/pages added.

**2. Zeman (2024) recruited for the converse of what it reports** (empirical-claim
fidelity). The article read: aphantasics rely on abstract knowledge *"—though
some retain imagery in other sensory modalities (Zeman et al., 2024)."* Zeman's
abstract says the opposite-facing thing: aphantasia *"**often affects imagery
across several sense modalities**."* The genuine preservation finding in that
paper is about **dreaming**, not other modalities: *"Visual dreaming often is
preserved."* The article appears to have collapsed the dreaming result into a
cross-modal-preservation claim. RESOLVED: passage rewritten to state what Zeman
reports — cross-modal spread of the deficit, with visual dreaming frequently
preserved.

**3. Kind (2013) credited with an aphantasia/hyperphantasia range that postdates
her paper** (citation framing + anachronism). The article attributed to
*"The heterogeneity of imagination (Kind, 2013)"* a range running *"from
hyperphantasics … to aphantasics."* **"Aphantasia" was coined in 2015** —
Zeman, Dewar & Della Sala, *Cortex* 73:378–380 (verified at Crossref); Zeman's
own 2024 abstract calls it a *"recent coinage."* A 2013 *Erkenntnis* paper
cannot be the source for that range. RESOLVED: Kind now cited only for the
contested question her paper's title names — whether "imagination" denotes a
single kind — and the empirical range is anchored on Zeman (2024) with his
published figures (~3% extreme hyperphantasia, ~1% extreme aphantasia; imagery
"rivals perceptual experience in vividness").

**4. Walton's *quasi-emotions* mischaracterised, and the rebuttal aimed at the
strawman.** The article glossed quasi-emotions as *"make-believe states that
resemble genuine emotions without fully being them,"* then rebutted: *"the
resulting anxiety is not make-believe."* Verified at IEP's *Paradox of Fiction*
entry: for Walton the quasi-emotion is a **real** felt/physiological state; what
is make-believe is that it **is fear** — the state issues from second-order
beliefs about what is fictionally the case rather than from belief in an actual
threat. So the article's objection asserts something **Walton already grants**,
and the dropped qualifier inverts his position (§2.5 Qualifier Preservation /
Position Strength). RESOLVED: gloss corrected, and the objection re-aimed at the
contrast that actually bites — Walton's machinery requires an **external prop**
and a **game of make-believe** to fix the fictional truths, and self-generated
imagination supplies content, fictional truths, and response from one source.
The article's substantive point survives intact and is now stronger, because it
no longer depends on misreading Walton.
