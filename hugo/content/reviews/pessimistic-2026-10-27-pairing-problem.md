2. **The co-construction answer is circular in exactly the way its own source avoids, and it cannot reach the onset.** L75 "the developmental history itself constitutes the pairing relation" and L117 "the pairing relation is the causal interface itself" explain the possibility of mind–brain causation by appeal to mind–brain causation. Bailey, Rasmussen & Van Horn (2011) raise this very regress against themselves (the "Diachronic Pairing Question") and answer it by *separating* the two questions: the possibility of interaction "obtains in virtue of facts about the locations of A and M at times", while only the counterfactual gluing of soul to body over time "obtains in virtue of causal interaction between A and M" — "Making this distinction allows us to see that there is no objectionable circularity at play here." The article runs the two together. Separately, co-construction presupposes a consciousness already interacting with *this* embryonic nervous system at the first iteration; "billions of iterative interactions" explains why the coupling *stays* specific, not why *this* subject was the one at the first interaction, and [P-SC3](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc3) says the onset is undated. The 2026-04-15 review's "promissory note" finding was answered by adding mechanism (the five-criteria paragraph), not by addressing the regress. A third consequence: if pairing *is* the live causal interface, it lapses under anaesthesia and dreamless sleep, yet [P-SC3](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc3) holds that the same subject returns — pairing must be a standing relation, not an occurrent one. `refine-draft`: split the synchronic question (what makes interaction possible now — location at the interface, residue owed) from the diachronic one (why the coupling tracks this brain over a lifetime — co-construction, or a BRVH-style counterfactual gluing), say which one co-construction answers, and make the relation standing. Headroom permits this additively.

3. **Response 2 attributes to Bailey, Rasmussen & Van Horn an argument their paper does not contain.** L81–L93 present, under "Their argument:", a bolded dilemma — "Either haecceities exist or they don't" — with haecceities "ground[ing] pairing relations" on one horn and individuation collapsing on the other. The paper's text (6,055 words, extracted from the authors' PDF) contains zero occurrences of *thisness*, *brute*, *primitive* or *individuat-*; its abstract reconstructs Kim on two premises, "Kim's Dictum ... causation requires a spatial relation" and "Nowhere Man ... souls can't be in space", and attacks each. Haecceities enter only inside their generality condition GC ("if A and B share all of their non-haecceitous properties, then A is no more qualified to count as the cause of C than B"), which they *reject* via a symmetric-cracking case and singular causation. Their positive proposal is nomic, not haecceitist: "a law dictating that a physical structure exhibiting certain properties produces a nonspatial soul ... and ... a further law dictating that any soul produced by a structure will, under certain conditions, remain causally related to certain material parts of that same structure" — which is the closest published cousin of the Map's developmental answer, and goes unused. The same attribution is live in `haecceity` L103 ("appeals directly to thisness"), `objections-to-interactionism` L55 and the research note. `refine-draft`: re-source Response 2 to what the paper argues (two-premise reconstruction; GC defeaters; located souls; (Glued)), keep the haecceity dilemma as the Map's own extension, unattributed, and cite the nomic origin-pairing suggestion where the developmental paragraph needs a source. Fix the sibling strings in the same pass.

4. **Two physics-register sentences in §The Map's Position contradict Tenet 2 or its own analogy.** (a) L125 "which remains true whether the underlying mechanism is coherent or classical": a classical mechanism leaves no "otherwise indeterminate outcomes" (Tenet 2, `tenets.md` L65) to select among, so a classical interface is not an option the Map holds; the intended contrast is coherent-superposition versus post-decoherence selection ([P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1)). Hasker's magnet/field analogy, already at L67, is the one that does the job. `refine-draft`: rewrite (a) to the [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1) contrast and replace (b) with the Hasker analogy or drop it. ## Executive Summary

The article is well-organised and its citation surface is sound at the metadata and verbatim-quote levels (re-checked, see *Suspicions checked and cleared*). Its weakness is calibration and attribution. It announces a dissolution the Map's positions register explicitly declines to claim; its central mechanism, developmental co-construction, grounds the possibility of interaction in interaction, the circularity its own principal source names and avoids; and the "haecceity response" it attributes to Bailey, Rasmussen & Van Horn is a Map reconstruction the paper never states, while the paper's actual positive proposal — nomic origin-pairing — is the resource the developmental paragraph needs.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would note that the article has already conceded her point in its own terms: a mind "located where its quantum interface operates", individuated by "essential causal connections to particular bodies" (L73), built by "activity-dependent plasticity" (L75), persisting only if the interface persists, is a mind with no property a neuroscientist cannot track. The "metaphysical distinctness" that remains (L69, L119) is a label with no further commitment attached. She would ask what the article's dualism now excludes that a vocabulary of neural self-models would not.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would accept the 07-17 ledger's framing that the illusionist exchange is a framework-boundary standoff — but he would object to L135's description of his position: "this amounts to denying that consciousness exists". Illusionism denies *phenomenal* properties, not consciousness; Frankish's whole programme is that consciousness exists and is misrepresented by introspection. He would also repeat the 04-15 point against L133: "two functionally identical systems would generate two viewpoints — but nothing in the functional description explains which is which from the inside" presupposes a perspectival fact over and above the two systems' indexical self-reference, which is the thing in dispute.

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark would go straight to L125: if the article is prepared to say the pairing answer survives "whether the underlying mechanism is coherent or classical", it has detached the answer from the quantum story altogether — and a classical brain has no slack for a non-physical selector. He would add that L119's field analogy gets the physics backwards (particles are field excitations), and that L111's list "microtubules, synapses, or other quantum-sensitive regions" still carries the Orch-OR site the Map's own register ranks below post-decoherence selection (P-Q-level demotion in `positions/quantum-interface` L102).

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch would observe that the article's L123 concession — that "which subsystem is the subject's depends on a chosen tensor-product factorisation" — is the same problem Everettians are told they cannot solve for branches. If a preferred factorisation is available to pair a subject to a brain, it is available to individuate worlds; if it is not, the pairing answer and the No-Many-Worlds tenet fall together. The article books the debt as [P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2) but does not notice the symmetry.

### The Empiricist
Popper's ghost would credit the PEAR threshold (L145) as a real falsifier and the 08-22 repair as honest. He would then ask what falsifies *developmental co-construction specifically*: falsifier 4 (minds "jumping" between brains) tests specificity-over-time, not the claim that the developmental history "constitutes" the pairing. A view on which pairing is primitive and merely *stable* predicts the same absences. The co-construction thesis adds no risky content over the bare location thesis.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would say the article's own best paragraph is L75: a pairing that is "built through billions of iterative interactions", with no moment at which an already-constituted mind was assigned to a body, is dependent origination described in the vocabulary of substance. The residual insistence on "a particular consciousness" present at the first iteration (the onset the register leaves undated) is the reification he would remove; without it, the problem does not arise and neither does the regress.
