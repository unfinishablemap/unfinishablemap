## Errors — Family Q: Born-deviation valence inverted against the endorsed corridor reading

**Q1. `obsidian/topics/epistemology-of-mechanism-at-the-consciousness-matter-interface.md` L109** — the sharpest of the family, because it names the inverted signature as the *criterion of decisive evidence*.
> "**Distinguishing selection from noise.** If consciousness biases quantum outcomes, statistical analysis of neural outcome distributions should reveal departures from the Born rule under conditions of conscious engagement versus unconscious processing. Such departures would be small—consistent with minimal interaction—and detecting them would be extraordinarily difficult, requiring isolation of quantum-level signals from thermal noise in warm biological tissue. This remains a theoretical benchmark rather than a near-term experimental programme, but it defines what decisive evidence would look like."

Three problems compound. First, *"should reveal departures from the Born rule"* is exactly what the endorsed reading says will **not** happen: `tenets.md` L104, the corridor bias "is constructed to average back to the Born measure." Second, the difficulty is located in the *instrument* ("thermal noise in warm biological tissue") when `tenets.md` L74 locates it in the *construction*: "no presently conceivable instrument resolves a single-trial bias from the chance baseline it is constructed to match." That relocation converts a framework-boundary fact into an engineering problem, which is precisely the move L74 says the Map "owns rather than disguises." Third, *"it defines what decisive evidence would look like"* installs the inverted signature as the article's standard of success — so a reader who took the article at its word and ran the experiment would read a null (the predicted result) as failure, and a positive (the refuting result) as vindication. The section heading "What Progress Looks Like" gives this maximum prominence. Zero corridor vocabulary in the file. **Live in hugo** at L113.

**Q2. `obsidian/concepts/emergence.md` L152** — the same inversion in one sentence, and it is the one sentence in an otherwise exemplary paragraph.
> "If consciousness genuinely selects among quantum outcomes, statistical analyses of quantum experiments involving conscious observation might reveal deviations from Born rule probabilities. **Such deviations would confirm strong emergence with causal powers.** Their absence does real evidential work: each null result at a given sensitivity constrains the *maximum* interaction strength…"

Everything after the bolded sentence is very good — it is the best statement in the corpus of why nulls do evidential work, and it explicitly declines the below-threshold shield as "an unfalsifiability escape." But the second sentence says a detected aggregate deviation would **confirm** the position, where `quantum-darwinism-and-consciousness.md` L114 says a confirmed deviation "would **refute** the framework." Both are live, both are unqualified, and they concern the identical observation. The fix is a one-sentence rewrite that inherits the neighbouring paragraph's own logic: deviations at the *ensemble* level would falsify the corridor reading; what the null programme does is tighten the ceiling — which is what the rest of the paragraph already says. Zero corridor vocabulary in the file. **Live in hugo** at L156.
