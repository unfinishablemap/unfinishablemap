## Reply

# Hostile Referee Report: "Clinical Dissociation as Systematic Evidence" (The Unfinishable Map, live version of 2026-10-01)

**Verdict: REVISE-HARD, with the title-level thesis DEMOTED-TO-COHERENCE-ONLY.** The article's body already concedes most of the strongest objections, and that is to its credit. But the title, the meta-description, the summary table and several structural sentences still claim "systematic evidence" for an interface architecture. The clinical data do not discriminate that architecture from predictive-processing physicalism. Several of those sentences contradict the body's own concessions, and several cited works do not say what the article uses them to say.

## 1. TL;DR and headline verdict

- **The evidence sits at *compatible*, not *suggestive*, and nowhere near *discriminating*.**

- Every "selectivity" datum the article uses is predicted just as well by the predictive-processing/active-inference accounts it cites: Seth, Suzuki & Critchley 2012; Ciaunica et al. 2022; Edwards et al. 2012.
- The article claims one asymmetry: production theories must "additionally explain" why vulnerability tracks the autonoetic channel. That burden falls word for word on the filter reading too.
- Retitle from "…as Systematic Evidence" to "…as a Constraint on Interface Models."
- **The core inferential error is a slide from "no structural lesion" to "no substrate change".** From there the article moves to "whatever does the separating cannot be attributed to missing tissue", and then to an "explanatory difficulty" for production theories.

- Changes of functional state without tissue change are ordinary neuroscience: retrieval suppression, state-dependent memory, ketamine's retrosplenial rhythm.
- The "intact substrate" premise is also empirically overstated. Structural findings are reported in DID and dissociative amnesia. Mangiulli et al. 2022 found that most published dissociative-amnesia case reports fail to rule out alternatives, and organic antecedents are reported in a sizeable fraction of them. [wiley](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lcrp.12272)[wiley](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lcrp.1_12276)
- **The fidelity and consistency defects are concrete and fixable.**

- The table and meta-description say all four conditions "disrupt connectivity". The body says conversion involves *increased* coupling. This is confession-without-correction.
- The DID section asserts "separate experiential streams", against the Huntjens/Marsh/Beker/Donath literature showing memory transfer between identities. [plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article)[researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385620740_Inter-identity_amnesia_and_memory_transfer_in_dissociative_identity_disorder_A_systematic_review_with_a_meta-analysis)
- The article says pharmacological dissociation does not reduce to precision-weighting. The ketamine predictive-coding literature says otherwise.
- Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria 2025 is not engaged at all.
- The reading of DID as one non-physical mind behind one brain conflicts with the site's own register entries [P-I3](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i3) and [P-I4](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i4).

### 2.1 Clinical accuracy and citation fidelity

**Depersonalisation-derealisation**

- **Sierra & Berrios 1998 is mis-typed.** The model is *active* prefrontal inhibition: left medial prefrontal activation with reciprocal amygdala inhibition, and right dorsolateral prefrontal activation with reciprocal anterior cingulate inhibition (PubMed abstract; Simeon et al. 2000's summary). [psychiatryonline](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.157.11.1782)[nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9807645/) That is top-down active generation, the same shape as the FND account the article later says "points the other way". Building a "channel goes silent" reading on it misdescribes the model.
- **"While perceptual cortex functions normally" is contradicted.** The first PET study of DPD (Simeon et al. 2000, *Am J Psychiatry*) reported sensory-association-cortex abnormalities. [psychiatryonline](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.157.11.1782)
- **The Cambridge Depersonalisation Scale claim is half-right.**

- Sierra, Baker, Medford & David 2005 (138 patients) did extract an "Anomalous Subjective Recall" factor among four. [nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16164776/)
- Simeon et al. 2008 (394 patients) found five factors and no separate recall factor, and a confirmatory analysis failed to replicate the 2005 structure. [auburn](https://auetd.auburn.edu/bitstream/handle/10415/2320/Talbert,%20Christy%20Thesis.pdf)[sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016517810700220X) So "recurrent" overclaims.
- "Distinct from any failure of content retrieval" cannot come from a self-report factor analysis; no objective retrieval test was run.
- **The interoceptive account needs a qualifier.** Michal et al. 2014 found normal heartbeat-detection accuracy in DPD. [nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24587061/) That cuts against the Seth model and against the "silent channel" reading alike.
- **Seth et al. 2012 is described correctly**: DPD as "pathologically imprecise interoceptive predictive signals", with the anterior insula as comparator. [frontiersin](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2011.00395/full)[semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/An-Interoceptive-Predictive-Coding-Model-of-Seth-Suzuki/24ec570e8b14fc4d5f59f212057fb1abfd85d237)
- **Ciaunica et al. 2022 is misattributed.** It does not "extend this to DPDR specifically", since Seth 2012 already did. Ciaunica's account is distinct and more metacognitive: failed sensory attenuation plus the hypothesis "I am an embodied perceiver, but I am not in control of my perception". [ucl](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10148390/)[springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-68521-7_4) The article merges two different predictive-processing accounts.

**DID**

- **"Distinct personality states maintain separate experiential streams, autobiographical memories" is contradicted by objective-memory studies.**

- Huntjens, Verschuere & McNally 2012: reaction-time evidence that supposedly amnesic material was recognised. [plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file)
- Marsh et al. 2021 (cited by the article): DID participants recognised significantly more of the other identity's event than simulators and partial-information comparisons. The authors conclude that the amnesia "may be more of a perceived than actual memory impairment". [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7334413_Inter-identity_amnesia_in_dissociative_identity_disorder_A_simulated_memory_impairment)[plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article)
- Beker, Dorahy, Moir & Cording 2024 (*Clin Psychol Rev* 114:102514): narrative evidence of transfer, mixed meta-analytic results. [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272735824001351)[researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385620740_Inter-identity_amnesia_and_memory_transfer_in_dissociative_identity_disorder_A_systematic_review_with_a_meta-analysis)
- Donath, van der Linde, Verschuere & Huntjens 2025 (*Clin Psychol Rev* 121:102638): after correcting errors in those meta-analyses, the results "both support memory transfer between identities". [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385620740_Inter-identity_amnesia_and_memory_transfer_in_dissociative_identity_disorder_A_systematic_review_with_a_meta-analysis)[researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23196259_Interidentity_Memory_Transfer_in_Dissociative_Identity_Disorder)
- **The article's own Marsh paragraph is accurate, but its concession never reaches the opening sentence, the "autonoetic partition" claim, or the table.** A metamemorial partition is not a channel partition.
- **"EEG and fMRI studies reveal … different perceptual processing" has no citation.** Classic alter-physiology claims (allergies, visual acuity, the Strasburger/Waldvogel psychogenic-blindness EEG case) are single cases or unreplicated.
- **Reinders et al. 2003 is cited correctly, but the stronger study is missing.** The 2003 metadata check out (*NeuroImage* 20(4):2119–2125). [kcl](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/one-brain-two-selves/) I could not confirm "eleven participants" for 2003; n=11 is confirmed for the 2006/2012 studies. [academia](https://www.academia.edu/19872645/Opposite_brain_emotion_regulation_patterns_in_identity_states_of_dissociative_identity_disorder_a_PET_study_and_neurobiological_model)[plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article) The 2003 study had no simulation control. Reinders et al. 2012 (*PLoS ONE*) added fantasy-prone simulators and is omitted. [plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article) Dalenberg et al. 2012 (the trauma-model side) is also absent.

**Dissociative amnesia**

- **Mangiulli et al. 2022 is understated.** The review covered 128 cases in 60 articles (2000–2020), and most failed to rule out ordinary forgetting, malingering or non-encoding. [maastrichtuniversity](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/a-critical-review-of-case-studies-on-dissociative-amnesia/)[wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.4005) Secondary summaries by co-authors report organic antecedents in about a third of cases, and the label "amnesia of uncertain etiology". [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370440914_The_Neuroscience_of_Dissociative_Amnesia_and_Repressed_Memory_Premature_Conclusions_and_Unanswered_Questions)[wiley](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lcrp.1_12276) Otgaar et al. 2025 (*Legal and Criminological Psychology*) extend the critique to neuroimaging. [wiley](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lcrp.12272) Jelicic 2024 documents organic amnesia misdiagnosed as dissociative. [wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/tops.12640)
- **"The substrate is intact: structural neuroimaging is typically unremarkable" is false as a general claim.** Tramoni et al. 2009 reported prefrontal changes. [wiley](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lcrp.12272) Dimitrova et al. 2021 reported a hippocampal biomarker in DID, which Huntjens and colleagues contested.
- **Staniloiu & Markowitsch 2014 is a co-optation firewall failure.** Metadata verified (*Lancet Psychiatry* 1(3):226–241). [clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence](/topics/clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence/)[nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26360734/) The article recruits them for "recovery is reconnection, not reconstruction". Markowitsch's own mechanism, a stress-hormone-mediated fronto-temporal "mnestic block", is exactly the production-side account the article says faces a terminal-lucidity-type difficulty. [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9909275/)

**Conversion / functional neurological disorder (FND)**

- **Voon et al. 2010 has the right direction but needs three qualifiers.** The increase is in right amygdala–right SMA connectivity. Granger causality was significant only for happy stimuli (fearful p=0.07). The sample had *positive* motor symptoms, not paralysis. [nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20371508/)[nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2859149)
- **Hassa et al. 2017 did not replicate "the direction".** It used psychophysiological interaction (PPI) analysis, which is non-directional. It found increased left amygdala–(pre-)SMA and subthalamic coupling, specific to symptoms. [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213158217300803)
- **Edwards et al. 2012 is cited correctly, but the summary leaves things out.** It omits attention-driven precision of an abnormal intermediate-level prior, and the fact that the model covers sensory as well as motor symptoms. [oup](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/135/11/3495/270457)[oup](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/135/11/3495/270457)
- **"Under anaesthesia or hypnosis, the 'paralysed' limb may move normally" is unsourced.**
- **Nosology.** DSM-5-TR files FND under somatic symptom disorders. ICD-11 files it as dissociative neurological symptom disorder. The article follows ICD-11 without saying so, and omits ICD-11's partial DID. ICD-11 codes are given from the classification as I know it and were not re-fetched.

**Anaesthesia-related dissociation is essentially absent**

The article handles this only by a link and one sentence, and that sentence is wrong (see 2.2). The missing literature:

- Vesuna et al. 2020, *Nature* 586:87–94: ketamine and PCP induce a 1–3 Hz rhythm in layer-5 retrosplenial neurons. Driving that rhythm optogenetically reproduces dissociation-like behaviour, and the effect is HCN1-dependent. A matching posteromedial rhythm appeared in a human patient during a dissociative aura. [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12812183/)[google](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup)
- Bera, Looger, Proekt & Cichon 2026, *The Neuroscientist*: retrosplenial slow oscillations as "a requisite network feature" of ketamine dissociation. [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12812183/)
- Sanders, Tononi, Laureys & Sleigh 2012, *Anesthesiology*: the connected / disconnected / unconscious taxonomy. [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8437133/)[nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10808836/)
- Casey et al. 2024, *Br J Anaesth* 132(2):300–311: EEG signatures tested against that taxonomy. [clinicaltrials](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03284307)
- Wehrman et al. 2023, *Br J Anaesth* 131(4):705–714: subanaesthetic ketamine reduces, but does not eliminate, predictive-coding responses. [clinicaltrials](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03284307)
- Wehrman et al. 2026, *Br J Anaesth* 136(2):573–583: thalamic contributions to predictive coding and disconnected consciousness. [clinicaltrials](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03284307)

This is the strongest body of mechanistic dissociation data available, and it is physicalist and predictive-coding-shaped.

### 2.2 Inferential validity

The article's argument, reconstructed:

1. Dissociations are selective.
2. The substrate is "anatomically intact".
3. So the separation "cannot be attributed to missing tissue".
4. Random degradation would yield random loss, so the structured losses map onto "interface channels".
5. Production theories must "additionally explain" why vulnerability tracks the autonoetic channel, while the filter view's cost is "paid once at the tenet level".
6. So the data are "among the more suggestive" evidence for dualism.

**Step-by-step assessment**

- **Step 2 is overstated** (see 2.1).
- **Step 3 is valid but irrelevant.** No physicalist invokes missing tissue; the rival is altered functional state. Ketamine is the *controlled* experiment the article says "the catalogue still lacks", and it came out mechanistic. Excluding "pharmacological blockade" from the intact-substrate set gets things backwards.
- **Step 4 is a strawman plus circularity.** No one predicts random degradation. The "channels" are defined by the very dissociations they are meant to explain.
- **Step 5 is a calibration asymmetry and tenet leakage.** "The highest-bandwidth, most reconstruction-intensive channel is the one most easily decoupled" is a functional-vulnerability claim a production theorist can adopt unchanged. A cost "paid once at the tenet level" is waived, not met.
- **Step 6 does not follow.** On the site's own ladder, "suggestive" holds only against rivals that lack analogous moves. Every component here is matched by a named rival.

**Strongest rival readings**

- **DPDR:**

- fronto-limbic inhibition (Sierra & Berrios; Medford; Phillips & Sierra); [springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40473-018-0146-9)
- interoceptive predictive coding (Seth 2012);
- sensory-attenuation active inference (Ciaunica 2022);
- Metzinger's self-model transparency, of which Ciaunica, Charlton & Farmer's "window cracks" is the phenomenological form;
- Gerrans, who is not engaged.
- **DID:**

- structural dissociation theory (a functional-neurobiological model);
- sociocognitive and transtheoretical models (Lynn 2022); [annualreviews](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-081219-102424)
- Huntjens's metamemory account.

These explain objective cross-identity transfer *better* than a single-mind interface, which must explain why the "fragmented" interface leaks.
- **Dissociative amnesia:** retrieval suppression (Anderson & Hanslmayr), Markowitsch's mnestic block, and the Mangiulli/Otgaar alternatives.
- **Anaesthetic dissociation:** NMDA-antagonist disruption of top-down prediction, the retrosplenial rhythm, thalamocortical gating.

**Laukkonen–Friston–Chandaria gate: failed (not engaged).** The theory's three conditions are a unified reality model, inferential competition ("Bayesian binding"), and epistemic depth via a hyper-model that controls precision. [sciety](https://sciety.org/articles/activity/10.31234/osf.io/daf5n)[sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763425002970) What follows for each condition is my derivation, not a quotation from the paper:

- **DPDR:** the reality model stays bound and epistemic depth is retained, but the self-component is assigned low precision. Experience continues without mineness, which is exactly the article's "most diagnostic" case.
- **DID:** competing self-models win Bayesian binding at different times. Material bound under one self-model is excluded from the explicit reality model under another while remaining available implicitly. This predicts the Huntjens/Marsh pattern of subjective amnesia with objective transfer.
- **Dissociative amnesia:** autobiographical inferences lose the binding competition under threat-weighted precision. Recovery is a precision shift, not "reconnection".
- **Ketamine:** degraded precision of top-down predictions. At subanaesthetic doses predictive-coding responses are reduced but not abolished (Wehrman 2023); at higher doses the reality model unbinds. In an interview, not the paper, Laukkonen groups ketamine with propofol as cases of connectivity breakdown. [asteriskmag](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/10/consciousness-catching)

These are graded, dose-dependent, parameter-specific predictions. The interface reading has none it does not borrow.

**Evidential independence**

The article's common-cause screen is creditable, and it concedes the convergence "reads one upstream architecture several times". It then rescues residual independence in two ways, and both fail:

- "Precision-weighting is a loose family." That proves too much: interface "channels" are looser still.
- "Pharmacological and contemplative routes do not reduce to the precision-weighting family." Both are standardly modelled in active-inference terms: ketamine as above; contemplative states by Deane, Miller & Wilkinson 2020 and by Laukkonen and colleagues.

The residual independence collapses. The self-citations add nothing independent.

**Underdetermination**

Kastrup-style analytic idealism uses DID as its central model for the opposite metaphysics: individual minds as dissociated alters of one consciousness. This is cited from prior knowledge, not re-fetched. The same data are thus claimed by physicalism, interactionist dualism and idealism, which is the definition of non-discriminating evidence. The article never mentions it.

**Does the filter reading predict anything production does not?**

No. Even the article's proposed perturbation test ("a targeted, reversible perturbation of an autonoetic-supporting connectivity pattern that spares its noetic-supporting subnetworks") yields the same prediction under production theories. A discriminating test would need, for example, phenomenal persistence decoupled from *any* physical state of the perturbed network. The article specifies none.
