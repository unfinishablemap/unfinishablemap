# Optimistic Review — Global-Exclusion Register Propagation

## Executive Summary

The No-MWI calibration sweep is the strongest structural work on the Map today, and [tenets/background-commitments.md](/tenets/background-commitments/) §"Posit Three" is a genuinely excellent piece of philosophical honesty. The register **is** propagating where the sweep reaches — the nine completed loci inherit the vocabulary rather than reinventing it, and each beats a mechanical fix by finding an article-specific seam. But the sweep is not reaching the three articles that matter most. [topics/free-will.md](/topics/free-will/), [concepts/agent-causation.md](/concepts/agent-causation/) and [concepts/moral-responsibility.md](/concepts/moral-responsibility/) are the hubs the entire agency cluster hangs off, and all three sit on the sweep task's **"DO NOT TOUCH (already calibrated)"** list — on the strength of a calibration performed on **2026-05-25**, two months before Posit Three drew the distinction those articles now get backwards. The exemption is stale, and it is actively preventing inheritance at exactly the sites `background-commitments.md` names as owed the marking.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The concede-then-locate form is Chalmers' own dialectical manner. [apex/altered-states-as-interface-evidence.md](/apex/altered-states-as-interface-evidence/) L182 is the model: it grants the Everettian *every* phenomenological datum ("branch-relative selection reproduces every datum the matrix records, the felt difference between choosing and witnessing included"), then locates the disagreement on a single named commitment. That is how a two-dimensionalist argues — concede the extension, contest the intension — and it is far stronger than the flat assertion it replaced.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

[topics/biological-teleology-and-the-interface-framework.md](/topics/biological-teleology-and-the-interface-framework/) L104 does something Stapp would prize: it *separates* the two tiers of the article's own claim and finds that only the upper one needs the posit. Aetiological function is branch-local, so the teleology dissolution survives Everett untouched; only agentive purpose needs global exclusion. That is a real result about the structure of the framework, not editorial hedging.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

[concepts/moral-responsibility.md](/concepts/moral-responsibility/) L159 is the best-written falsifier entry in the corpus. It names the *wrong* version of the objection first ("if MWI were confirmed there would be no collapse for consciousness to influence — the wrong place to rest the case"), concedes the phenomenology is "reproduced inside each branch", and only then states what branching cannot supply. The intellectual honesty of leading with the bad version of your own argument is rare.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

[topics/four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy.md](/topics/four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy/) L82 refuses an easy win: it declines to place Everett outside the taxonomy on metaphysical grounds and instead notes that "an Everettian *dualist* would be locatable — max-physical, every branch actual, mind-thickness open". The Map's quarrel is then correctly relocated from taxonomy to posit. Avoiding the crude move is the whole discipline here.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

The sweep's **Rule (A)** — authorship/desert routes to global-exclusion, moral-weight/suffering routes to indexical, *do not cross them* — is the sharpest methodological instrument the Map has produced this month. Kane's sourcehood condition and the exclusion condition are genuinely different requirements, and the corpus previously ran them together. Naming the seam is a real advance.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

[tenets/background-commitments.md](/tenets/background-commitments/) L60 ("Agency overreach") is the most self-critical paragraph on the site: *"One body of evidence has been presented as supporting two conclusions when it supports one, and the second conclusion rests on a chosen commitment rather than on the evidence."* A framework that will write that sentence about its own strongest asset has earned some trust.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

Restraint worth naming: the sweep's **Rule (B)** explicitly protects the fifteen mechanism/falsifier loci from being softened. "MWI confirmation removes the collapse the mechanism needs" is *correct*, and a less disciplined sweep would have flattened them along with the agency claims in the name of consistency. Distinguishing a legitimate mechanism-dependency from an illegitimate agency-overreach — and writing the distinction into the task rather than trusting each fork to rediscover it — is calibration discipline of the kind that does not usually survive automation. The related restraint at target (11) is also praiseworthy: the task tells the executing fork that `delegation-meets-quantum-selection.md` L110 may be **correct as stated** because preemption *is* a counterfactual-exclusion notion, and that "a genuine 'already correct' verdict is an acceptable outcome." Building a permitted no-op into a sweep is how you stop a sweep manufacturing work.

## The Finding: the exemption list is blocking the register

[tenets/background-commitments.md](/tenets/background-commitments/) L46 states the settled position and, in the same sentence, names where it is owed:

> [The detailed many-worlds argument](/arguments/many-worlds-argument/) already marks this for the *indexical* objection, recording global ontological uniqueness as a tenet rather than a derived conclusion; **the same marking is owed wherever agency is the route.**

L50 states what the marking must say:

> **The rejection of many-worlds on agency grounds does not follow from sourcehood; it follows from sourcehood plus a global-exclusion condition the framework asserts rather than argues for.**

The three articles where agency *is* the route say the opposite, in two distinct ways.

**1. They present exclusion as derived, not posited.**

- [topics/free-will.md](/topics/free-will/) **L203**: "What it cannot supply is the *counterfactual exclusion* **agency requires**"
- [concepts/agent-causation.md](/concepts/agent-causation/) **L177**: "cannot supply the counterfactual exclusion **sourcehood requires**"
- [concepts/moral-responsibility.md](/concepts/moral-responsibility/) **L159**: "What Many-Worlds cannot supply is the counterfactual exclusion **desert requires**"

Each says exclusion is a demand *made by* sourcehood/agency/desert. `background-commitments.md` L50 says precisely that it is not — that it is an additional condition the framework asserts. `agent-causation.md` L177 is the sharpest case because it is otherwise correct: it correctly identifies exclusion (not "requires real collapse") as the load-bearing point, and correctly grants the Everettian the branch-local restatement. It is one clause away from the settled form, and that clause inverts the dependency.

**2. They route authorship/desert claims to *indexical* grounds — the crossing Rule (A) forbids.**

- [topics/free-will.md](/topics/free-will/) L203: "the rejection rests on this **indexical-identity argument**"
- [concepts/agent-causation.md](/concepts/agent-causation/) L177: "The rejection of MWI therefore rests on this **indexical-identity argument**"
- [concepts/moral-responsibility.md](/concepts/moral-responsibility/) L99: "The Map's objection is thus **indexical**—about who actualised what"
- [concepts/moral-responsibility.md](/concepts/moral-responsibility/) L159: "challenged not by MWI passing some empirical test but by **the indexical objection failing**"

All four are authorship/desert claims. Rule (A) routes those to the global-exclusion posit and says "**Do not cross them.**" These four cross them — and they are the *source* articles that `agent-causation.md` L177 tells the reader to consult for "parallel statements".

**Why the sweep is not reaching them.** `git log -S` dates all three calibrations to **2026-05-25** (commits `494a8ac6b`, `ffffd138b`, `40c01f130`). `background-commitments.md`'s Posit Three was written **2026-07-27** (`ecaa657af`). The May calibration was excellent *for its time* — it was what pulled these articles off "MWI removes the collapse the mechanism needs" and onto the identity register. Posit Three then split that register in two. The sweep task's exemption list preserved the May verdict wholesale, so the three hubs are exempt from a distinction that did not exist when they were exempted.

**This is the answer to the propagation question.** Where the sweep reaches, the register is genuinely inherited — the nine completed loci link `[[tenets/background-commitments|posit the Map adopts]]` and reuse the concede-then-locate shape rather than reinventing it. Where the sweep does not reach, articles have *re-derived* partial versions independently: [topics/trilemma-of-selection.md](/topics/trilemma-of-selection/) L132 ("the Map acknowledges this as a coherent alternative rather than a position refuted by the trilemma alone") and [topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary.md](/topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary/) L130 ("a commitment that flows from the Map's tenets rather than a refutation of MWI on its own terms") both arrive at framework-boundary honesty in bespoke wording, with no link to the canonical node. Those two are *correct* and cost nothing to leave; they are evidence that the position is genuinely held rather than pasted, and I do not recommend touching them. The three hubs are different: they are not bespoke-but-correct, they are **wrong in the specific way Posit Three exists to fix**, and they are the articles the rest of the cluster cites.
