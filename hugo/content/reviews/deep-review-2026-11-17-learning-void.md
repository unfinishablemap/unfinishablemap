## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Remedy claim said more than its citation supports.** The lead ran "has a plain remedy: test yourself later … A meta-analysis found that delaying the judgement brings 'robust benefits to relative accuracy'". Rhodes and Tauber measure delayed *judgements*, scored by item-by-item gamma correlation. They do not measure self-testing and do not bear on which method a learner rates as better. Resolution: the lead now separates the two claims (a later test is the way round; delay also improves the judgement, with relative accuracy defined in a clause). The part about test experience rests on Koriat and Bjork 2006 (Experiments 2 and 3, read in full this run) and stays in Limits. The description field's "that delayed self-testing remedies" was changed, as was the same phrase in the voids index.
2. **Sample description was false.** "Most of the evidence is laboratory work with university students in the United States." Koriat and Bjork 2006 ran all five experiments with University of Haifa undergraduates; Deslauriers et al. is a classroom study; six of Yan et al.'s seven experiments used online workers. Resolution: sentence rewritten to what was checked.
3. **Simon and Bjork figures attached to the wrong moment** (table row 13). Corrected; exact figures printed.
4. **Self-exemption paraphrase** (row 8), **experiment counts** (rows 7, 20, 21), **Experiment 6 n** (row 11), **novice inference** (row 17): each corrected as in the table.
5. **Lead universal.** "nothing in view shows how much of what they are doing will last" claimed more than the sources, which call performance "often an unreliable index". Now "what is in view is often a poor guide to how much … will last". The definitional point survives in the body's first part, where it is marked as close to definitional.
6. **Xie et al. attributed as read** (row 28). Now attributed through the carrier.
