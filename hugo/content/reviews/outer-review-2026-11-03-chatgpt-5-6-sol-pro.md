# Independent critical review: “Consciousness and Motor Selection”

**Assessment date:** 3 November 2026  
**Recommendation:** Major revision

I reviewed the live article and the site changelog, including changes made after the article’s stated substantive revision date of 27 October. That revision materially improved the page: it now identifies Sjöberg’s contribution as a short clinical essay rather than an experiment, scopes Hagan’s calculation to microtubules, acknowledges the absence of a coupling model, and gives more space to decoherence, the anti-Zeno effect and the energy-conservation problem. The changelog nevertheless records unresolved numerical and interpretive residue, while a later site audit corrected Stapp’s supposed measurement cadence in connected pages. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/)

## Overall judgment

The article supports one important negative conclusion:

> The classic Libet experiment does not establish that conscious agency is merely an after-the-fact narrative.

It does **not** support the stronger positive conclusion that neuroscience has located a causal opening for a nonphysical selector. Neural competition, accumulation-to-threshold and attention–motor overlap identify functional stages at which selection occurs. They do not show that those stages are causally incomplete, still less that an extra-physical cause operates there.

The quantum-Zeno section is now comparatively candid about its difficulties, but remains a **mechanism sketch without a quantitative physical implementation**. It acknowledges the decoherence objection; it does not answer it. Its strongest defensible claim is that decoherence is not a completely general theorem prohibiting every conceivable open-system Zeno effect. That is far weaker than showing that attention can repeatedly project a biologically relevant motor subspace.

In direct answer to the requested questions:

- **Libet:** The headline timings are represented accurately, but the article overstates what their reinterpretation establishes.
- **Schurger and the readiness potential:** Materially overconfident and insufficiently current. The stochastic-accumulator account is important, but neither unique nor a settled resolution.
- **Quantum Zeno and decoherence:** The objections are now honestly stated, but not technically confronted with a system-specific model.
- **Citations:** I found no evident fabricated quotation. There is one definite citation misattribution, one conspicuous missing central reference, several scope distortions and important omissions from the 2020s literature.
- **Tenet-protective bracketing:** Yes. The article repeatedly turns evidence compatible with dualism into positive support while quarantining contrary evidence as bearing only on one optional mechanism.
- **Integration:** Hyperlinking is strong, but substantive integration is weak: linked articles contain incompatible timing claims, different accounts of what Stapp’s intervention controls, and stale numerical assertions.

## 1. Libet and the readiness potential

### What the article gets right

The report of the classic Libet sequence—readiness potential beginning roughly 550 ms before movement and reported conscious intention roughly 200 ms before movement—is broadly accurate. It is also correct that the readiness potential’s early onset cannot straightforwardly be identified with the time at which an unconscious brain “decides.” Schurger’s accumulator model demonstrated that movement-locked averaging of ongoing fluctuations can produce an RP-shaped signal even when there is no discrete decision at the apparent beginning of the averaged potential. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/)

That substantially weakens a once-common argument:

1. the RP starts before reported intention;
2. therefore the brain has already made the decision;
3. therefore conscious intention is epiphenomenal.

The article is entitled to reject that inference.

### Where it overstates Schurger

The phrase that “the answer has largely arrived” is not justified. Nor is it sufficiently accurate to characterize the RP simply as an “artifact of averaging neural noise rather than a decision process.” [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/)

Three distinctions are needed:

1. **The averaged waveform can be an averaging effect without the underlying activity being causally irrelevant.** In Schurger’s model, ongoing activity contributes to threshold crossing and hence to movement timing.
2. **Schurger primarily addresses arbitrary, weak-imperative decisions about when to move.** This is not a general model of deliberative choice, option valuation or reason-responsive action.
3. **The model undermines the inference from RP onset to prior commitment; it does not establish that commitment occurs only at threshold crossing or only after reported awareness.**

Schurger and colleagues themselves subsequently called for a reassessment of the RP rather than announcing a settled account. Later work has also shown that deterministic input can explain similar averaged signals, that expectation and planning contribute to RP morphology, and that the relationship between RP, meaningful choice and reported intention remains unsettled. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33931306/)

The recent evidence is not moving in one simple anti-Libet direction. A 2025 rewarded-choice study reported stronger readiness potentials for meaningful than arbitrary decisions, while another 2025 study suggested that RP-related activity may help determine or trigger when an intention is reported. A 2026 prospective-probe study found conscious intention reports more than a second before action in some conditions, further undermining use of a universal 200 ms “conscious window.” [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40799214/)

The article should therefore say:

> RP onset is not a reliable timestamp for an already-completed unconscious decision.

It should not say, without substantial qualification:

> The RP has been shown to be mere noise and the real decision is left open until the final conscious interval.

The former is a defensible conclusion from the literature. The latter imports precisely the dualist interpretation that the evidence is supposed to establish.

### The reused 200 ms window is especially problematic

The article first uses Schurger to reject the Libet timeline as a measure of decision onset, but later treats Libet’s approximately 200 ms W-time as a meaningful interval in which an agent could intervene. Those moves do not sit comfortably together.

Once the clock-report methodology, retrospective reconstruction and probe dependence of W-time are acknowledged, 200 ms cannot be retained as a robust neurophysical “slot” for conscious intervention. At most, it is one task- and method-dependent estimate of reported awareness. The newer prospective and probe-based studies make a fixed 200 ms window increasingly difficult to defend. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34325185/)

### Sjöberg is now represented fairly, but contributes little evidential weight

The article’s revised treatment of Sjöberg is much improved. It correctly identifies the source as a short clinical argument based on neurosurgical experience, not a controlled study. Sjöberg’s claim that supplementary motor-area damage or resection can disrupt execution without eliminating the patient’s experienced effort is relevant against a simple identity between the RP-generating system and conscious will. It does not independently validate Schurger, establish an extra-physical cause or show that the experience of effort is causally efficacious. [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11224596/)

**Verdict on this section:** The article successfully defeats a crude anti-consciousness interpretation of Libet. It then converts that negative result into a positive opening for dualism without sufficient argument.

## 2. Attention, motor competition and the alleged selection point

### A definite Müller–Rabbitt citation error

The page’s comparison between voluntary attention at approximately 300 ms and reflexive attention at 100–175 ms is not supported by the cited Müller and Rabbitt paper.

Their 100–175 ms figures concern peripheral-cue effects. Their central symbolic-cue effects reached maxima over substantially later intervals, roughly 400–725 ms depending on condition. The approximately 300 ms “optimum” appears in their discussion of earlier literature; it is not a measurement of freely willed attention in their experiment. The site’s own changelog has already recognized this problem, but the target article retained the stale formulation. [Interruptions+2](https://interruptions.net/literature/Muller-JEP_HPP89.pdf)

This matters because the article does more than report a latency. It aligns an alleged 300 ms voluntary-attention interval with an alleged approximately 280 ms motor-commitment interval and treats the alignment as suggestive of a common selection process. Once the source attribution fails, so does that timing-based convergence.

More generally, average latencies from differently designed experiments cannot be aligned as though they measure successive stages of one common mechanism unless they share event anchors, operational definitions and comparable task demands.

### Rajan et al. do not restore the convergence

The Rajan study provides more relevant evidence for a distinction between instructed and freely chosen attentional allocation. Its frontal-theta activity occurred at roughly half a second, and its later alpha-lateralization measures showed slower allocation in the willed condition than in the instructed condition. The increased reciprocal frontoparietal coherence is evidence for different network dynamics under the two task demands. It is not evidence that a nonphysical selector entered the network. Granger-directed connectivity is still a statistical relationship among neural signals. [eScholarship+1](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2p21x3sp)

The strongest warranted interpretation is:

> Internally selected attention imposes different and often greater network-processing demands than externally instructed attention.

The article instead moves too quickly from a neural difference to the location of a psychophysical interface.

### Premotor theory supports overlap, not identity

The premotor theory of attention is relevant because covert attention and action preparation share circuitry and representational resources. But recent population-level work continues to find both common and dissociable signatures. Overt motor preparation can occur without the same attentional state, and covert attention can be experimentally separated from particular motor plans. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41271438/)

The article often uses conditional language—“if attention is motor planning”—which is appropriate. Elsewhere, however, it treats the attention–action unity as established architecture. It should distinguish:

- shared coding;
- functional coupling;
- partial dependence;
- strict identity.

Only the last would strongly support the article’s proposed single interface locus, and the cited evidence does not establish it.

### Cisek’s competition architecture supplies no causal gap

Cisek’s work supports parallel preparation and competition among potential actions. That is useful for rejecting a simplistic serial picture in which an abstract decision is first completed and then passed to a motor executor. It does not show that the competition is underdetermined by physical dynamics.

The article itself concedes that a classical accumulator or competition network can be causally closed. That concession should prevent the subsequent claim that neuroscience “positions the agent” at the competition point. Neural competition identifies **what must be explained**, not **an unoccupied causal role**. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/)

### Desmurget is overgeneralized

Desmurget and colleagues showed striking dissociations in seven neurosurgical patients: stimulation of parietal regions could evoke intentions or beliefs that movement had occurred, while premotor stimulation could produce movement without corresponding awareness. This is important evidence about the distributed construction of motor intention and awareness. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19423830/)

“Conscious motor intention originates in parietal cortex” is nevertheless too strong. Direct stimulation establishes causal involvement under unusual conditions; it does not establish a unique origin, a normal processing sequence or the site at which a nonphysical mind enters. A network-level formulation would be more defensible.

### Cai’s dopamine result is accurately summarized but philosophically overused

Cai and colleagues provide evidence that fast dopamine transients are not necessary for spontaneous movement initiation, while remaining important for reward-oriented behaviour and vigour. That qualifies simple dopamine-as-go-signal accounts. It does not show that physical processes specify only motivation while leaving action identity for consciousness to determine. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08038-z)

The article’s three-layer division—valuation, motor competition and conscious selection—is a proposed interpretation of multiple findings, not a result obtained by Cai or the other cited experiments.

### The 10-bit claim is an analogy, not confirmation

Zheng and Meister’s approximately 10-bit-per-second estimate concerns the rate of high-level human behavioural output or conscious task performance. It is not a measurement of total sensorimotor or neural information processing. A contemporaneous critique explicitly argues that sensorimotor throughput is far higher and that the estimate applies, at most, to a narrow high-level bottleneck. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39694032/)

The claim that 10 bits per second “matches” a policy-level conscious selector therefore has no independent evidential force. A sufficiently abstract selector can always be described at a rate similar to overt choice output. The fit becomes informative only if the dualist model predicts the rate before it is read from behavioural data.

## 3. The quantum-Zeno proposal

## What has improved

The article now does several things well:

- It does not claim that Hagan directly vindicates Stapp.
- It admits that no neural projector, physical measurement interaction or quantitative coupling has been supplied.
- It notes that repeated projection need not conserve energy.
- It recognizes Georgiev’s same-basis objection.
- It admits that rapid intervention can produce acceleration rather than inhibition.
- It explicitly says that failure of Stapp’s proposal would not by itself refute interactionist dualism.

These are substantial improvements over presenting “attention repeatedly observes the brain” as though it were an established physical mechanism. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/)

The difficulty is that the article then continues to treat the proposal as a scientifically live explanation without providing what would make it one.

### The decoherence arithmetic remains confused

Tegmark’s calculations gave a broad range of approximately $10^{-13}$ to $10^{-20}$ seconds for different proposed neural quantum degrees of freedom. The article’s phrase “femtoseconds—a twelve-order gap from milliseconds” is arithmetically unstable:

- $10^{-13}$ seconds to $10^{-3}$ seconds is ten orders of magnitude;
- $10^{-20}$ seconds to $10^{-3}$ seconds is seventeen;
- approximately twelve orders is obtained only by comparing $10^{-13}$ seconds with a tenth or several tenths of a second, not with milliseconds.

The changelog itself records that this “twelve-order” formulation was not checked. [APS Journals+1](https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194)

The calculation based on Hagan’s $10^{-5}$–$10^{-4}$-second microtubule estimate is arithmetically correct: maintaining a process across 300 ms would involve approximately 3,000–30,000 intervals at or below those timescales. But that result concerns a particular Orch-OR microtubule model. It is not a decoherence calculation for Stapp’s proposed ion-channel or electromagnetic “template for action” degrees of freedom. Hagan’s paper cannot simply be used as a friendlier generic neural timescale. [arXiv+2](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0005025)

The statement that Tegmark’s approximately $10^{-20}$-second ion-related estimate “stands unrevised” is also too categorical. It is a model calculation, not a measured universal constant. The responsible formulation would be:

> The article cites no later, mechanism-specific calculation showing that Stapp’s proposed neural degrees of freedom remain projectable on the required timescale.

### Tegmark is missing from the reference list

Despite Tegmark’s calculation being central to the section, the article’s reference list does not include Tegmark 2000. This is a straightforward bibliographic omission. The reader is given exact timescales and a description of a major objection without the corresponding source entry. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/)

### Stapp’s own proposed cadence worsens the problem

A later site audit checked Stapp’s cited example and found an illustrative interval of about 10 ms between probes, corresponding to approximately 100 Hz—not the approximately 1 kHz or roughly 1,000 probes per decision window that had propagated through several Map pages. At 100 Hz, only about 30 probes occur in 300 ms. [reviews/deep-review-2026-11-01-quantum-zeno-effect](https://unfinishablemap.org/reviews/deep-review-2026-11-01-quantum-zeno-effect/)

That is:

- 100 times fewer than the lower Hagan-derived burden of 3,000 events;
- 1,000 times fewer than the upper burden of 30,000 events;
- vastly slower still if Tegmark’s shorter estimates apply to the relevant variable.

The target article no longer repeats the erroneous 1,000-event figure, but it also does not compare its calculated burden with Stapp’s actual illustrative rate. That comparison should be central.

### Decoherence is not a universal no-go—but this does not rescue the proposal

Modern mathematical work shows that Zeno limits can exist in open systems under repeated, physically specified quantum operations, including for mixed states. It would therefore be too simple to claim that environmental decoherence automatically makes every QZE impossible. [DOI+1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00023-021-01075-8)

But this formal result sharpens rather than removes the article’s burden. A viable brain proposal must identify:

1. the relevant system and environment;
2. the Hamiltonian or open-system generator;
3. the subspace or projector;
4. the physical operation implementing repeated projection or control;
5. the interval between operations;
6. the bath correlation or relaxation timescale;
7. how the intervention survives coarse-graining into neural dynamics;
8. the measurable difference from ordinary feedback control.

“Attention asks the same question repeatedly” supplies none of these. In laboratory QZE experiments, an apparatus performs a defined physical interaction. Calling attention an observation restates the interaction problem rather than solving it.

### Georgiev’s objection is stronger than the article suggests

The article emphasizes the point that repeated projection in a basis already selected by environmental decoherence may be redundant. Georgiev’s criticism is broader: under realistic open-system evolution, the relevant Zeno effect can fail beyond the decoherence timescale; local projections do not automatically reduce the entropy of the unconditional state; and Stapp’s proposal would require something analogous to a protected or decoherence-free subspace. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4741)

This is not merely a question of whether consciousness chooses the “same basis.” It is a challenge to whether the proposed repeated interventions can preserve a controlled macroscopic alternative at all.

### “Acceleration is more common” is too broad

The anti-Zeno effect is a genuine problem, but the article’s statement that acceleration is generally more common is insufficiently scoped. Whether repeated intervention inhibits or accelerates a transition depends on the measurement interval, the bath spectral density, the system–bath coupling and the overlap between the control spectrum and the environmental response. The site’s dedicated sign-problem article is more careful about these conditions than the target page. [concepts/sign-problem-for-conscious-observation](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/sign-problem-for-conscious-observation/)

The article should not use a general slogan where a model-specific calculation is required.

### Stapp does not straightforwardly supply outcome selection

In Stapp’s von Neumann-inspired account, mind chooses or poses a question—formally, a projection or decomposition—and controls whether or how frequently it is posed. The Born-rule answer is not chosen by the mind. Repeatedly posing the same question may stabilize an already occupied “yes” subspace, but it does not by itself allow an agent to choose which one of several motor outcomes actualizes. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0010029)

That creates a significant tension with the Map’s wider agent-causal picture:

- **Stapp-style Zeno control:** choice of question, basis or repetition rate;
- **the Map’s stronger thesis:** the agent selects which possibility becomes actual.

A separate outcome-biasing or post-decoherence selection mechanism is required to bridge those claims. Once that separate mechanism is introduced, the Zeno proposal is no longer doing the central work.

### The proposed empirical test is not yet discriminating

The suggestion to compare active and passive conditions for departures from drift-diffusion dynamics is reasonable as exploratory neuroscience. It is not yet a test of quantum-interactionist dualism. Attention, arousal, metacognitive monitoring, changing bounds, urgency, feedback and nonstationary evidence can all produce classical departures from a basic drift-diffusion model.

Likewise, “near-perfect neural prediction” is an excessively demanding weakening criterion. Imperfect prediction is expected from limited measurement, chaotic amplification and ordinary stochasticity. A dualist model needs a **positive, model-specific residual signature**, not merely the failure of neuroscience to predict perfectly. The article concedes that it cannot currently specify an effect size or unique signature. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/)

**Verdict on the quantum section:** It confronts the objection discursively but not mathematically. The appropriate status is “underspecified speculative possibility,” not “candidate mechanism with empirical support.”

- The Schurger source exists and is relevant, although its implications are overstated.

### Definite or substantial problems

2. **Tegmark:** centrally discussed but missing from the bibliography.
3. **Schurger:** “largely solved” and “noise rather than a decision process” are interpretive overstatements, not faithful summaries of the current state of the literature.
4. **Desmurget:** parietal involvement is upgraded to a unique origin and potential interface location.
5. **Cai:** a negative result about fast dopamine transients is used to support a three-layer conscious-selection architecture the experiment did not test.

9. **Currentness:** the RP discussion omits important 2020–2026 work, while the quantum section relies largely on sources from 2000–2015 and omits modern open-system QZE results.

I found **no clear invented quotation or grossly false quotation** in the passages checked. The central problems are misattribution, selective interpretation, missing scope conditions and incomplete updating rather than fabricated sources.

## 5. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article’s most important structural problem is not any single factual error. It is asymmetric evidential bookkeeping.

The following pattern recurs:

- When neuroscience reveals competition or late commitment, that is said to “position the agent.”
- When classical models explain the same dynamics, the article retreats to the unresolved phenomenology of effort.
- When decoherence threatens Zeno stabilization, a different post-decoherence selection mechanism remains available.
- When Stapp’s model is physically unsuccessful, interactionist dualism is said to survive because Stapp is optional.
- When Many-Worlds would change the interpretation of projection and outcome, it is bracketed because the site rejects Many-Worlds on other grounds.
- When no distinctive effect can be predicted, only near-perfect neural predictability is offered as a weakening condition. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/)

Some modularity is legitimate. Refuting one proposed interface would not logically refute every form of dualism. The problem is that compatibility is counted positively while failures are localized and prevented from lowering confidence in the wider theory.

The article needs three evidential categories:

1. **Compatible with dualism:** competition, stochasticity, attentional modulation.
2. **Evidence favouring dualism over physical alternatives:** presently not supplied.
3. **A worked dualist mechanism:** presently not supplied.

Most of the neuroscience belongs in the first category. The prose frequently moves from the first to the second.

The opening description of the “standard materialist” position is also too narrow. Physicalism does not entail that conscious intentions are causally inert narrations. A physicalist can identify conscious intending with a higher-level neural-control state that genuinely affects later processing. The genuine dispute is whether such causal efficacy is exhaustively physically realized, not whether intentions have any causal role at all. [topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/motor-control-quantum-zeno/)

Finally, appealing to the phenomenology of effort does not by itself fill a mechanistic gap. The existence and character of effort are data to explain. They are not evidence that the physical account lacks causal closure unless the article first defeats functional, metacognitive, predictive and control-theoretic explanations of why effort is experienced.

[concepts/process-1-specification-problem](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/process-1-specification-problem/)

### Failed or incomplete integration

**1. The attention timing is internally inconsistent.**  
The current “Attention as Interface” treatment recognizes that willed and instructed attention do not share one simple 300 ms timeline. The target article retains the known stale Müller–Rabbitt formulation. [concepts/attention-as-interface](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/attention-as-interface/)

**2. “Motor Selection” is more cautious about causal gaps.**  
The linked motor-selection material says that neural competition can be classically complete and that the Zeno proposal controls basis or timing rather than the outcome itself. The target article nevertheless says that neuroscience “positions the agent” and sometimes speaks as though Zeno stabilization explained actual option selection. [concepts/motor-selection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/motor-selection/)

**3. Quantum timing claims remain numerically inconsistent.**  
A linked timing page says that approximately 1 kHz intervention is compatible with microsecond-scale decoherence. It is not: 1 kHz means one operation per millisecond, which is ten to one hundred times slower than $10^{-4}$–$10^{-5}$ seconds. Other linked material retained the old approximately 1,000-event interpretation after the canonical Zeno page was corrected to Stapp’s approximately 100 Hz example. [topics/quantum-neural-timing-constraints](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-neural-timing-constraints/)

**4. Post-decoherence selection changes the theory rather than repairing Zeno stabilization.**  
A post-decoherence mechanism may avoid the requirement to maintain microscopic coherence across a psychological decision interval. It also abandons the Zeno explanation of sustained attention and introduces a different unexplained interaction at outcome selection. The linked pages sometimes present this as an alternative; the target article uses it more like a protective fallback.

**5. Corrections are not propagated reliably.**  
The changelog is unusually transparent about known residue. That is a genuine methodological virtue. But it also shows that the same unverified numerical claim survived multiple reviews because later pages cited another Map page rather than returning to Stapp’s original source. Connected prose is not yet functioning as a controlled dependency graph. [reviews/deep-review-2026-11-01-quantum-zeno-effect](https://unfinishablemap.org/reviews/deep-review-2026-11-01-quantum-zeno-effect/)

## Final assessment

The article is a serious piece of dualist advocacy and is more self-critical than many discussions of quantum consciousness. It should nevertheless be recategorized as an exploration of where an interactionist mechanism would have to operate, not as evidence that neuroscience has revealed such a mechanism.

Its core empirical conclusion should be:

> Current readiness-potential and motor-selection research does not establish that conscious agency is causally excluded.

Its current stronger implication—

> those findings locate an incompletely physical selection point at which an agent can intervene—

does not follow.

The quantum-Zeno account fares worse. It possesses a recognizable formal inspiration but no identified neural quantum system, no physically implemented projection operation, no compatible intervention frequency, no amplification model and no distinctive empirical prediction. The article acknowledges most of these absences, but its conclusion does not sufficiently discount the proposal for them.

# Concrete potential improvements
