## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers is read at source in `colour-ontology` and would recognise himself. The Eden section (L68–72) quotes "perfect redness: the sort of property that might have been instantiated in Eden" and "the most fundamental content of perceptual experience is its Edenic content", then refuses to enlist him: the paper "declares itself neutral on whether phenomenal character is prior to representational content or grounded in it", it "even allows a type-B analogue", and "the bridge from Chalmers's content thesis to a phenomenal residue is one the Map builds, not one the paper asserts" (L72). That is the explanatory-gap structure applied to colour and then fenced at exactly the line his own paper draws. He would also take `cotard-delusion` L63 as a clean statement of what a constitutive claim about for-me-ness must survive: the falsifier is "phenomenal life continuing without any residue of for-me-ness, no position from which the lack is registered", and Cotard "is the hardest instance, because the report's content denies the position from which it is made". And in `factorisation` L68 his own collapse model with McQueen is named "the benchmark any pairing law must match or beat" — through IIT's exclusion postulate — and then charged the same Level 1 debt as everyone else.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp appears in one article and is treated fairly on a point where he is usually treated cheaply. `factorisation` L70 and L94 report that his quantum interactive dualism "answers Level 2 by stipulation: the process acts on some part of the agent's brain, and no factorisation criterion is offered", and the page then declines to make the stipulation a defect: it "shows an interactionist can proceed without a factorisation criterion; it also shows that no interactionist treatment located as of 2026-09-25 has supplied one" (L94). He would also value the form-only resemblance `influx` records between Knutzen's reply to conservation and the Map's: Knutzen "denied that the conservation law reached the soul at all" (L67), and the page marks that "the Map's own response to the conservation objection runs through the locality and symmetry-conditionality of modern conservation laws and through selection among quantum outcomes rather than injection of force ... What the two replies share is only their form" (L93). A physicist who has spent decades separating his own proposal from its cruder ancestors would want exactly that sentence.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Nagel's article is `cotard-delusion`. "The patient who says 'I do not exist' argues, resists, worries and, on the case record, feels" (L35), and the page holds to the first-person datum without letting it decide anything it cannot: "what fails is the report, not the position" (L63); "the report is internally inconsistent in part of the corpus ... a patient can claim both to be dead and to be unable to die — a datum about the kind of output the report is" (L41). He would also keep `colour-ontology` L66, which finds the one thing no colour theory disputes: "What no party disputes is that a qualitative character is *given* in experience; they disagree about whether the giving is veridical." And in `parkinsons` the felt-ness question is left standing where the data cannot reach it: "in why goal-directed control is *felt* as effortful, which neither devaluation tests nor dual-task costs address" (L97).

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would find the wing's refusal of crude substance talk in two unexpected places. `influx` L65 records that influx won by deflating "flowing" into activity — Gottsched's influx is "the power of a substance to act directly on another", Wolff's definition "is a definition of ordinary causation, not of anything flowing" — and L81 credits the pre-critical Kant with defining "force generally, as activity, rather than attributing one particular force ... to minds and bodies". Causation as activity between event-like substances is his own instinct. `factorisation` L82–86 states the consciousness-first inversion — "observers fix structure rather than the reverse" — with Tegmark and Stoica as its physicist sponsors, and then declines it: "The Map cannot help itself to the second without a pairing law." None of this praise bears on any evidential tier: no article in the wing claims that any organism or system is conscious, and the one that touches a self-model rival (`cotard`) grades the Map's reading at *compatible*.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane's page is `influx`, because it finds imputability at the root of the reversal: the Pietist case was that harmony "entails fatalism and therefore destroys the imputability of action", and "Part of why it won may be that it was the theory that let you hold people responsible — the eighteenth-century form of the connection the Map draws between interaction and [free-will](/topics/free-will/)" (L55). The page marks this strand "may" and second in confidence, which is where a historian would put it. He would also read `parkinsons` L97 as the honest version of a claim libertarians usually overstate: "On the habitual reading what survives in Parkinson's is attended, effortful control, where the Map locates [felt effort](/concepts/mental-effort/); the devaluation result complicates that comfort, since in reward learning the attended goal-directed system is the one that weakens with severity."
