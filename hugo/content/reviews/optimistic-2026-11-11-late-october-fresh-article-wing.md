## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
IDX takes the two-relata problem as the Map's own: "the indexing question is sharper for the Map than for a physicalist, since there are two relata to index rather than one object to delimit" (L112). It then notes, against its own interest, that Brenner's parody and IIT's exclusion postulate show the problem is not unique to dualism. DYN's post-review Dualism paragraph is the move Chalmers would most respect. It names him as the dualist who can *accept* the premise the Map withholds. Dualism, it says, "permits the refusal of Definition 3 without forcing it" (L106), because an organizational-invariance dualist can take Definition 3 on board. CLI keeps the hard problem where it belongs. The hard problem "applies to the felt-real signal as to any phenomenal quality, and this subject adds nothing to it either way" (L128).

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
IDX's physics section quotes Zurek's "the problem of measurement cannot be even stated without a recognition of the existence of systems" (L87; raw-verified by the 10-28 deep review in the arXiv text, not re-fetched today). It then admits that [P-Q1](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q1)'s einselected menu presupposes a factorisation the mechanism does not fix. DYN puts the Map's mechanism, "outcome selection at the improper-mixture-to-outcome transition", exactly where Kleiner and Ludwig's quantum-computing carve-out says verification "might leave enough room" (L88). It then lets [P-AS1](/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts/#p-as1), and not the theorem, do the work.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
CLI makes felt reality a variable in its own right. Its opening move is Jaspers's "a primary phenomenon which can be conveyed only indirectly" (L45; carrier-verified in Farkas's preprint by both prior reviews). It argues for presented reality from the one case where felt reality and judgement come apart the other way round, depersonalisation. TRA's limit is a Nagelian limit. The method reaches *that* one believes or sees, "but does not reach phenomenal character: what the pain or the seen red is like for its subject" (L93).

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
IDX's component 4, temporal jurisdiction (L49), treats the interface as something with an onset, persistence, suspension and end, rather than a standing tie between substances. It also names a cessation question distinct from onset as missing from the literature (L53). Component 5 finds its nearest analogue in "panpsychist combination" (L50) and borrows nothing from it. **Constraint honoured**: no page in the wing uses process-philosophical resonance to move a tier. None of the five tier labels appears in any of the four files (`grep -c` = 0 each).

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
IDX refuses to let the content clause go slack. On the semantic-ratification dilemma's first horn, where the brain computes the match and consciousness only ratifies, it says: "Given the content-sensitivity required above, this horn cannot be the whole account" (L99). That keeps Tenet 3's "toward patterns that express its content" doing work. TRA credits Moran's deliberative stance and then declines to overclaim: it "suits the Map's interest in agency without evidencing the tenet" (L103).

### The Mysterian (McGinn)
Every page leaves its open question open. IDX lists "An uncloseability result" among its own defeaters (L108), and "The corpus has not chosen; this page presents the fork rather than resolving it" (L95). DYN gives the eligibility debt "an address, not a discharge" (L82). CLI says "The two-register reading is the best fit to this record. The record does not force it." (L124). TRA says its limit is "the Map's verdict against Byrne's extension, drawn from its acquaintance view; the transparency literature neither establishes nor refutes it" (L32).

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)
This persona's verdict matters most here, because each page has an obvious opportunity to upgrade and each declines it:
