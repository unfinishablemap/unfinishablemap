## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Marmura inverted (L45).** The article attributed the "defends only the possibility of miracles / leaves genuine creaturely causality open" caution to "Marmura and others". Both SEP entries say the opposite. SEP *Occasionalism* (Lee, rev. 2019) credits the caution to Griffel (2007), with Perler and Rudolph. It says Marmura argues that al-Ghazālī "rejects the latter claim that there are real secondary causes" and that "other texts well establish al-Ghazālī's commitment to occasionalism (Marmura 2005, 145–52)". SEP *Al-Ghazālī* (Griffel, rev. 2026-01-15) says: "Marmura (1965, 183; 1981, 97) rejected the suggestion that al-Ghazālī might have held occasionalism and secondary causality as two cum-possible cosmological explanations… In Marmura's view, al-Ghazālī never deviated from occasionalism." The co-possible reading belongs to Griffel 2009 (pp. 147–73) and Moad 2023. The error was seeded by research note `non-interactionist-dualisms-2026-07-12` L42. **Resolved**: the sentence now records the reading as contested, with Marmura (2005) on the occasionalist side and Griffel (2009) and other recent interpreters on the co-possible side. The SEP phrase "some kind of genuine causality in creatures" is kept verbatim and attributed to the SEP entry. Marmura 2005 and Griffel 2009 were added to References.

2. **Arnauld misattributed the explains-too-much charge (L53, L71).** No source checked credits Arnauld with this charge:
   - SEP *Antoine Arnauld* (Kremer, rev. 2021), §4.4 "Arnauld's criticism of Malebranche's occasionalism", gives his actual objection: Malebranche's principle "destroys the nature of intelligent beings, who would not be free if they did not, as real causes, form in themselves the determination of their will toward particular goods" (grep-verified). That is a freedom and agency objection.
   - Nadler, whom the article cites (Nadler 2011 ch. 5; Nadler 1995 via SEP), reads Arnauld as *himself* an occasionalist about mind-body traffic. The Chamberlain and McDonough review (*Phil. Review* 122:1, 2013) calls him "the important exception": an occasionalist *because* a dualist.
   - In the grace dispute Arnauld insisted God acts by particular volitions, which is in tension with the attribution.
   - Control search ("Arnauld" + occasionalism + "explains nothing"/"explains too much") returned no attribution.

   **Resolved**: the explains-too-much charge is kept as a charge but no longer attributed. Arnauld's documented objection is added with the verbatim SEP fragments "destroys the nature of intelligent beings" and "as real causes". The text says his target was the global doctrine and keeps Nadler's reading attributed to Nadler. RTSP ¶2's "mirrors Arnauld's… Arnauld's explains-too-much charge" became "presses the explains-too-much charge… The charge bites hardest". Kremer SEP was added to References.

3. **The 09-30 sentence imported the dominance claim its target page declines (L59).** "Its eighteenth-century fate — displacement by physical influx in the German academy". The linked page explicitly does not assert that influx became dominant: its L33 says the dominance claim is "a stronger claim that this page has not verified at source and does not assert", and its L39 says the question is "underdetermined by the evidence this page has seen". Its L43 also names "Wolffian pre-established harmony displaced by physical influx" as the standard framing the sources correct. Its own 09-30 deep review swept "overturned", "prevailed over" and "carried the field" out of that page, but the sweep did not reach this echo. **Resolved**: "Pre-established harmony's eighteenth-century reception—the German academy's turn from it toward physical influx—is traced in [eighteenth-century-influx-debate](/topics/eighteenth-century-influx-debate/)". "Turned from … toward" is the target page's own description wording. The ambiguous "Its" was replaced by the noun, and the spaced em-dashes now match the article's unspaced style.

4. **Tenet-3 standing note not propagated (L69; calibration).** "This keeps genuine creature-level bidirectional causation" reads more confidently than `tenets#^tenet-3-standing`. That note was installed 2026-08-03, after the 07-20 review, so no earlier pass could apply it. It says downward causation is shown to be "*available* … without showing it to be *actual*", and "Articles asserting that consciousness does 'real work' or 'genuine causal work' inherit that debt rather than discharge it". A tenet-accepting reviewer would flag the sentence. **Resolved**: "If the channel does real work, this keeps creature-level bidirectional causation … ; that *if* is a debt the Map records rather than discharges, since its interface argument shows such causation to be available, not actual ([Tenet 3's standing note](/tenets/#tenet-3-standing))." The Tenet 2 route is now stated as tenets.md L71 states it: "on the Map's preferred route, the [post-decoherence](/apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme/) actualisation of one outcome". Born-statistics and energy constraints were already consistent with L69.

5. **Dropped qualifiers on La Forge (L41).** SEP: La Forge "has been suggested to have first employed the expression 'occasional cause'". The article said flatly "who first used the term". On scope, SEP says he "appears to have limited his occasionalism to extended substances and held that we as minds are causally active in producing our ideas (TEH 166)". It notes interpreters split: Prost and Bouillier for a global reading, Bardout and Nadler against. Nadler confines his occasionalism to body-body relations (2011, 106, per Chamberlain and McDonough). In Map vocabulary, "*preserved mental causation*" reads as mind→body causation, which SEP does not support. **Resolved**: "who may have been the first to use the expression … yet on the better-supported reading he kept minds causally active in producing their own ideas—a local occasionalist about bodies". Clauberg was added as the verified converse case (SEP: occasionalist about mind-body on heterogeneity grounds, with consensus that bodies act on bodies; Nadler 2011, 121). This gives the paragraph's closing sentence its exemplar.

6. **Cordemoy argument garbled (L41).** The article said "since bodies plainly do not sustain their own motion". SEP: "Based on the observation that bodies do not self-initiate movement, Cordemoy argues that bodies thus need to be set into motion by a non-material first mover … any subsequent movement … can only be continued by the causal agent that initially moved them". **Resolved**: rewritten to SEP's structure. SEP also reports the consensus that he was a global occasionalist, which the text now says.
