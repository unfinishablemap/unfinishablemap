## Overall verdict

I audited the live version identifying **13 August 2026** as its last substantive modification and checked the current site changelog rather than relying on search-indexed copies. The August revision made several worthwhile repairs: it now concedes that irreducibility alone does not secure personal persistence, qualifies the equal-capacity premise, and presents the Everettian point more accurately. The remaining problem is primarily **inferential rather than bibliographic**. I found no fabricated external reference, and the four externally cited works are substantially correct at the metadata level. But the article repeatedly makes a metaphysically distinctive description do work that can only be done by additional normative premises. The interface thesis may redescribe enhancement as modifying a subject’s instrument rather than modifying the subject. It does not by itself establish duties concerning consent, equality, identity, authenticity, responsibility, or the preservation of agency.

The article’s only potentially distinctive ethical contribution is the hypothesis that an intervention could improve cognitive performance while bypassing or narrowing the nonphysical subject’s causal interface. But that hypothesis is not currently operationalised. The positions register states that there is no worked interaction model, no subject-pairing law, no observable discriminator between selected and unselected outcomes under the default Born-preserving account, and that downstream causal claims may be cited only as **framework-internal coherence**, not as established mental causation. ## 1. Primary-source citation audit

### Bostrom and Sandberg 2009

The metadata is correct: Nick Bostrom and Anders Sandberg, “Cognitive Enhancement: Methods, Ethics, Regulatory Challenges,” *Science and Engineering Ethics* 15(3), 311–341, DOI 10.1007/s11948-009-9142-5. The article’s account of cognitive enhancement as amplification or extension of core mental capacities through improvements to internal or external information-processing systems is faithful. Its observation that the treatment–enhancement boundary is difficult to stabilise is also fair. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19543814/)

The misrepresentation comes immediately afterwards. Bostrom and Sandberg are recruited as representatives of “materialist bioethics” which allegedly assumes that cognitive ability simply is brain function. Their definition explicitly includes **external** information-processing systems and is largely operational rather than a statement of reductive metaphysics. Nothing in the cited formulation requires identity theory, neural reductionism, or the claim that improving a substrate necessarily improves a numerically identical “mind.” The article converts a technologically broad definition into a metaphysical commitment the source does not make.

The therapy–enhancement instability is also not a specifically materialist embarrassment. A dualist can equally reject normal species functioning as the ethically decisive boundary, while a physicalist may defend it for reasons concerning medicine, social institutions, or distributive policy. The source supports criticism of the treatment–enhancement distinction, not the article’s contrast between materialism and dualism.

**Verdict:** correct source and substantially correct initial paraphrase; subsequent metaphysical attribution is unsupported.

### Savulescu 2001

The metadata is correct: Julian Savulescu, “Procreative Beneficence: Why We Should Select the Best Children,” *Bioethics* 15(5–6), 413–426. The article accurately states the core principle: parents have a moral reason to select, among possible children, the child expected to have the best life on the available evidence. Intelligence is among the characteristics Savulescu discusses. [Wiley Online Library+1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-8519.00251)

But two qualifications are missing.

First, procreative beneficence is fundamentally a **welfarist expected-life argument**, not an inference from materialism. A dualist could accept that greater working memory or executive functioning tends to improve expected wellbeing; a physicalist could deny it because of trade-offs, social context, positional effects, or plural conceptions of flourishing.

Second, Savulescu’s later formulations make the principle a substantial but **defeasible moral reason**, capable of being outweighed by distributional, reproductive-liberty, risk, or justice considerations. The target article instead treats Savulescu as though he straightforwardly equates greater cognitive capacity with the best life and then moves on without confronting the strongest welfarist case. [Uehiro Centre](https://www.uehiro.ox.ac.uk/node/890271)

The phrase “which under materialism straightforwardly includes cognitive capacity” is therefore the Map’s interpolation, not Savulescu’s conclusion.

**Verdict:** metadata and statement of procreative beneficence are accurate; its classification as a materialist argument and its reduction to cognitive maximisation are not.

### Sandel 2007

The metadata is correct, and the article fairly characterises Michael Sandel’s concern that enhancement expresses excessive mastery or dominion and weakens receptivity to the “gifted” character of human capacities. [Weatherhead Center](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/case-against-perfection-ethics-age-genetic-engineering)

The further claim that this concern “sharpens” under dualism is unsupported by Sandel and under-argued by the Map. It could just as plausibly weaken:

- If enhancement leaves the fundamental subject untouched, it may look more like improving a tool than redesigning a person.
- Sandel’s concern concerns the moral stance of designers and parents toward embodied human life. Calling the body an interface does not show that mastery over it is more troubling.
- Physicalists can regard inherited embodiment as contingent and unchosen without identifying persons with isolated brain states.

The article needs an independent argument explaining why the giftedness of an interface carries greater moral weight than the giftedness of an embodied human capacity. Merely relocating giftedness from mind to interface does not sharpen anything.

**Verdict:** faithful account of Sandel; unsupported dualist extension.

### Wallace 2012

The metadata is correct: David Wallace, *The Emergent Multiverse: Quantum Theory according to the Everett Interpretation*, Oxford University Press, 2012. The revised paragraph accurately acknowledges that Deutsch–Wallace decision theory instructs rational Everettian agents to use Born-rule weights in decision-making. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/25622/chapter-abstract/193013138)

That concession largely eliminates the paragraph’s relevance to enhancement ethics. The article says that the difference concerns whether enhancement is a single-outcome wager or a portfolio across branches, while conceding that Everettians deny any resulting decision-theoretic difference. No altered duty, permissible intervention, distribution rule, consent requirement, or rational choice follows.

Unless the article derives a policy-level divergence, Tenet 4 is metaphysical scene-setting rather than part of the ethical argument.

**Verdict:** accurate after revision, but normatively idle.

### Internal Map references

The internal references appear real and correctly point to relevant Map material. They are useful for tracing framework dependencies. They cannot, however, supply independent evidence for the interface, identity, or agency claims. The Map’s own methodology register explicitly states that self-citation records framework-internal coherence rather than external corroboration. [positions/methodology-and-calibration](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/methodology-and-calibration/)

**Overall citation verdict:** no fabricated external citation detected. The more serious failure is that metaphysically neutral, welfarist, or bioconservative works are repeatedly presented as though they presupposed reductive materialism.

## 2. The normative inference from dualism does not go through

The article’s argument can be reconstructed as follows:

1. Consciousness is numerically distinct from the brain.
2. The brain is an interface through which consciousness receives information and acts.
3. Cognitive enhancement modifies that interface.
4. Therefore enhancement raises distinctive duties concerning identity, consent, agency, and equality.

The conclusion does not follow. Premises 1–3 are descriptive or metaphysical. To obtain an ethical conclusion, the article must add principles such as:

- people have a right to control conditions shaping their future agency;
- interventions should preserve authorship or reasons-responsiveness;
- parents should preserve a child’s open future;
- unequal access to valuable capabilities is unjust;
- substantial risks to identity or autonomy require precaution;
- numerical identity is morally more important than psychological continuity;
- agency has intrinsic rather than merely instrumental value.

These principles do the normative work. Every one is available to physicalists, functionalists, compatibilists, animalists, and nonreductive naturalists.

A useful test is:

> **Holding welfare, autonomy, experiential effects, personal continuity, social pressure, and distribution constant, does adding the dualist premise change the verdict?**

The article supplies no convincing case in which it does.

| Case | Article’s concern | Normative principle actually doing the work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adult memory implant | Possible loss of authorship or identity | Autonomy, informed consent, authenticity, control |
| Childhood enhancement | Subject cannot choose its future configuration | Best interests, assent, open future, option preservation |
| Anti-aggression drug | Reduced temptation may alter responsibility | Reasons-responsiveness, quality of will, achievement value |
| Unequal access | Some people receive better cognitive opportunities | Fair opportunity, priority, sufficiency, relational equality |
| BCI bypass | Performance rises while conscious control falls | Preserve agency and non-manipulated control |

Physicalists can and do endorse all of these principles. The distinctive part of the Map’s description is that the allegedly bypassed author is a nonphysical subject acting through quantum selection. The ethical injunction against bypassing a person’s deliberative control is not distinctive.

The article’s strongest defensible thesis is therefore much narrower:

> **Conditional on an interactionist interface existing, enhancement introduces a speculative additional failure mode: measured performance might improve while the interface’s contribution to authorship diminishes.**

That is a coherent research question. It is not yet a practical imperative, much less a transformation of enhancement ethics.

## 3. The interface-preservation argument exceeds the Map’s own evidence

The target article claims that artificial pathways might bypass biological sites at which consciousness acts and concludes that enhancement should be evaluated for its effect on those mechanisms. It says that this design constraint is visible only to dualism. [topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism/)

The positions register imposes four limits that the article does not inherit:

1. The preferred post-decoherence mechanism has **grade-D external evidence** and remains a programme rather than a worked model.
2. The bias-without-deviation problem is unresolved: it is unclear how selection differs causally from ordinary Born-weighted chance while leaving the same statistics.
3. No toy Hamiltonian, master equation, or simulation connects a conscious variable to a neural quantum event.
4. Under the default corridor account, competing subject counts and selection histories can be empirically indistinguishable by construction. [positions/quantum-interface](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/quantum-interface/)

The register expressly says downstream claims that consciousness “does causal work” may use the interface thesis only as framework-internal coherence until that debt is paid. The target instead proceeds from the same premise to a technology-design imperative. That is a direct calibration mismatch.

The linked BCI article also undercuts the inference. It correctly notes that intrinsic-manifold and neural-reassociation findings establish that BCI learning occurs through existing neural architecture, but explicitly concedes that a no-interface physicalist predicts the same data. Those studies therefore do not show that consciousness fails to extend into hardware or that a quantum interaction boundary has been found. [topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary/)

To make interface preservation an actionable concern, the article would need at least:

- a candidate physical variable or neural locus;
- a criterion distinguishing “inside” from “outside” the conscious channel;
- an observable proxy for conscious causal participation;
- a comparison between two interventions with equal behavioural performance but different predicted effects on that proxy;
- a probability estimate or at least an explicit precautionary threshold;
- an account of how multiple subjects or multiple neural modules compose at the interface.

None currently exists. “Bandwidth,” “fidelity,” “filter,” “range,” and “bypass” function as adaptable metaphors rather than variables in a model.

## 4. Enhancement is treated as an unjustifiably homogeneous category

The article moves among:

- temporary pharmaceuticals;
- chronic pharmaceuticals;
- non-invasive neurostimulation;
- implanted BCIs;
- sensory and motor prostheses;
- somatic genetic modification;
- germline editing;
- embryo selection;
- childhood intervention;
- adult voluntary self-enhancement.

These do not instantiate one ethical structure.

Adult nootropic use mainly raises questions about safety, voluntariness, workplace pressure, authenticity, and fair competition. An adaptive speech BCI raises control attribution, privacy, decoder error, dependence, security, and continuing consent. Embryo selection raises nonidentity and reproductive-choice problems. Editing an existing fetus or child raises best-interest and open-future questions. A reversible stimulation protocol differs morally from an irreversible developmental intervention.

The statement that enhancement is “deliberate and permanent” in contrast with temporary altered states is false across the article’s own examples. A drug may last hours; stimulation may be reversible; a decoder can be recalibrated; an implant may be removable yet dependency-forming; germline edits may be irreversible. The permanence, adaptability, reversibility, and identity effects have to be analysed intervention by intervention. [topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism/)

The article also moves too freely from reproductive genetic arguments by Savulescu and Sandel to adult pharmaceuticals and BCIs. Those sources cannot support a general conclusion about every intervention placed under “cognitive enhancement.”

Recent neuroethics has increasingly emphasised precisely these heterogeneous effects, including motivation, burnout, stigma, expectations, interpersonal consequences, uncertain real-world benefit, and variation across populations. A broad article written in 2026 cannot rely almost entirely on sources from 2001–2012 while omitting this intervention-specific turn. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11948-021-00294-w)

## 5. Identity: numerical persistence is neither established nor sufficient

### The internal inconsistency

The body of the article now correctly admits that irreducibility does not establish that the same subject survives enhancement. It says persistence requires an additional commitment: interface-independent indexical identity. Later, however, the tenet summary says Tenet 1 itself “preserves personal identity.” These cannot both be the argument.

The accurate formulation is the earlier one:

> Dualism rules out identity with the neural substrate, but it does not determine the persistence conditions of the nonphysical subject.

Substance dualism could permit:

- a subject remaining paired to a radically altered brain;
- a different subject becoming paired;
- a pairing ending;
- one subject pairing with several systems;
- several subjects interacting with one system;
- indeterminate or temporally discontinuous pairing.

The Map has no pairing law adjudicating these possibilities.

### Conflict with the positions register

The individuation register grades closed individualism and primitive subject boundaries as framework-internal, grade-D claims with no independent discriminator. The subject-census register separately says the Map has no rule determining which subject is paired with which system, how many subjects a system supports, or when a pairing begins and ends. [positions/individuation-and-subjecthood](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/)

Accordingly, the article cannot state confidently that the same indexical subject necessarily survives any “interface modification.” That conclusion assumes exactly the persistence and pairing principles the register marks as unsettled.

### The comparison with materialism is too crude

The article says dualism offers a stronger basis for continuity than “materialist views where identity reduces to psychological continuity or brain-state patterns.” This omits several major alternatives already discussed in the Map’s personal-identity article:

- animalism, on which the same biological organism persists;
- embodied continuity accounts;
- narrative identity;
- constitution views;
- nonreductive physicalism;
- four-dimensional or stage views;
- pluralist accounts distinguishing numerical identity from practical identity.

A physicalist animalist can say that an adult remains the same human organism through extensive cognitive alteration. A narrative theorist can distinguish continued existence from disruption of self-understanding. A compatibilist can preserve agency through functional integration despite substantial neural change. The comparison therefore does not establish a dualist advantage. [topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/)

### Numerical identity does not settle the ethical question

Even granting persistence of the same nonphysical subject, an intervention can transform:

- memories;
- values;
- attachments;
- practical commitments;
- emotional dispositions;
- interpersonal roles;
- narrative self-conception;
- capacities needed to endorse the intervention retrospectively.

“You remain numerically the same subject” is compatible with profound alienation, loss, manipulation, and prudential discontinuity. The article gestures at phenomenological estrangement but does not integrate it into the ethical analysis.

Recent work on memory prostheses and neurotechnology treats identity as involving memory, authenticity, agency, and narrative relations, not merely numerical persistence. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/aor.14615)

### The proposed falsifier is invalid

Reports that enhanced people feel like “a different subject” cannot falsify primitive numerical identity. Such reports provide evidence about phenomenological, narrative, or psychological discontinuity. A person can falsely believe that they are numerically different, while a new subject could inherit memories and report perfect continuity.

A metaphysical thesis deliberately insulated from physical and functional criteria cannot then be tested by self-report alone.

## 6. Consent: dualism adds imagery, not yet an argument

The custom-instrument-for-a-musician analogy is evocative, but it does not show that consent matters more under dualism. A physicalist can describe prenatal intervention as configuring the capacities, opportunities, dispositions, and experiential life of a future person. The article’s phrase that the stakes “feel different” correctly identifies an intuition but does not vindicate it. [topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism/)

### Selection and modification must be separated

Savulescu’s canonical case concerns **selection among possible children**. In many such cases, choosing a different embryo determines which person exists. That raises a nonidentity problem: there may be no already-determined subject whose interface is being configured without permission.

Editing an already-existing fetus, administering a drug to a child, or implanting a device is different. In those cases there is a stronger basis for saying that an intervention changes the future life of a particular individual.

The article’s musician analogy assumes that a particular subject is already waiting to receive a chosen instrument. That assumption is especially problematic given the Map’s admitted lack of a rule specifying when subject–brain pairing begins.

### “Open future” arguments do not uniformly oppose enhancement

The article needs to engage the counterargument that enhancement may **expand** rather than close a child’s future options. Increased memory, communication, mobility, attention, or freedom from disabling symptoms may enlarge the range of lives available. Recent debate therefore treats open-future considerations as bidirectional rather than presumptively anti-enhancement. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/theo.12349)

The morally relevant questions include:

- Does the intervention increase or decrease later choice?
- Is it reversible?
- Does it impose a narrow parental conception of success?
- Does it remove a capacity the future person may value?
- Does it make participation in ordinary institutions easier, or merely force conformity to them?
- Are benefits robust across plausible future preferences?
- Is non-intervention itself an equally consequential choice?

### Adult consent is also under-analysed

For adults, valid consent cannot be reduced to an uncoerced signature. Current autonomy analysis distinguishes decision-making capacity, actual exercise of autonomy, authenticity, relational conditions, and successful control over one’s life. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/bioe.12791)

Enhancement may also be epistemically transformative: before undergoing a major change, the person may be unable to understand what the altered cognitive or phenomenal life will be like. That complicates both prospective consent and retrospective endorsement. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11948-022-00410-4)

An adequate account should address:

- staged and renewable consent;
- trial periods where possible;
- withdrawal and reversibility;
- changing preferences after intervention;
- dependence on providers and proprietary software;
- decoder updates and model drift;
- neural-data privacy;
- cybersecurity and adversarial manipulation;
- workplace, educational, military, parental, or insurance pressure;
- the right to refuse without being blamed for remaining unenhanced.

The last concern is empirically important: BCI research has found that refusal can elicit greater blame or anger, showing that availability itself can generate coercive expectations. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36633302/)

### Direct versus indirect shaping

The article treats deliberate technological configuration as morally different from education, nutrition, upbringing, or socialisation. It does not supply a principled boundary. Recent moral-enhancement work argues that direct and indirect interventions may be practically difficult to distinguish and that uncertainty about mechanism weakens categorical ethical distinctions based on delivery route alone. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12152-026-09646-4)

A dualist version of the direct–indirect distinction would require evidence that one intervention changes the causal interface in a way another does not. The article currently lacks that evidence.

## 7. Agency and responsibility: the two-position framework is too narrow

The article contrasts:

- **interface neutrality**, under which responsibility depends on the agent’s exercise of causal power; and
- **interface dependence**, under which moral significance depends partly on the difficulty of choosing well.

This conflates several different questions:

1. Is the person an agent at all?
2. Did the person control this action?
3. Was the action reasons-responsive?
4. Did the action express the person’s evaluative commitments?
5. Was the person manipulated?
6. Is the person morally responsible?
7. Is the person praiseworthy?
8. Was the action an achievement?
9. Does the person deserve punishment or reward?

Removing an aggressive impulse could increase reasons-responsiveness and self-control while decreasing the achievement involved in resisting temptation. It might increase responsibility for subsequent aggression, decrease praiseworthiness for restraint, and improve wellbeing simultaneously. These are not one scalar “moral texture.”

The article also excludes the strongest physicalist and compatibilist positions:

- hierarchical identification;
- reasons-responsive control;
- quality-of-will accounts;
- functional integration;
- manipulation and bypass theories;
- relational responsibility;
- dynamical and temporally extended agency.

This is particularly problematic because the Map’s own agency register says that libertarianism does **not** provide unique explanatory power over sophisticated compatibilism at the level of deliberative phenomenology, praise and blame, or character formation. Its distinct residue is confined largely to ultimate desert and metaphysical alternatives. [positions/agency-and-will](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/agency-and-will/)

The target’s claim that Tenet 3 “ensures” enhanced agents remain free is also inconsistent with:

- its own suggestion that an intervention might bypass the interface;
- the register’s moderate rather than high confidence in agent causation;
- the unresolved selection mechanism;
- the absence of any means to tell whether the conscious subject causally contributed.

The defensible position is conditional: **if** agent causation exists and **if** the intervention leaves its enabling conditions intact, numerical agency may survive. That is much weaker than assurance.

## 8. Equity: the article does not yet contain a theory of justice

The equity section recognises that its strongest equal-capacity premise is unsupported, but then continues as though all persons are subjects of equal fundamental capacity operating through interfaces of unequal quality. The qualification therefore does not constrain the subsequent conclusion.

Two distinctions are needed:

- **Equal moral standing does not require equal cognitive or phenomenal capacity.**
- **Unequal capacity does not determine whether a distribution of enhancement is just.**

The article’s statement that materialists can simply regard some brains as “better” and treat enhancement as extended natural variation is a straw position. No serious physicalist theory of justice infers that natural inequality is morally legitimate merely because it is natural.

The “better interface” framing also risks reproducing the hierarchy it is meant to criticise. Describing disabled people as equal subjects operating defective or low-quality instruments can preserve metaphysical equality while expressing an ableist assessment of their embodied lives.

### Rival positions left unengaged

| Rival | Central question the article omits | Current status in the article |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Welfarism** | Which intervention maximises expected lifetime wellbeing, including risks, adaptation, relationships and externalities? | Savulescu is cited but not seriously answered |
| **Capability theory** | Does enhancement expand substantive opportunities to function and flourish, given social and environmental conversion factors? | Absent |
| **Disability rights** | Does enhancement respond to impairment or enforce normalisation, devalue disabled lives, exclude testimony, or create refusal pressure? | Absent |
| **Distributive justice** | Should access follow equality, priority, sufficiency, fair opportunity, need, contribution, or protection against positional arms races? | Reduced to generic concern about unequal access |

#### Welfarism

A serious welfarist opponent would not claim “more processing is always better.” It would compare expected wellbeing across interventions, including adverse effects, opportunity costs, social pressure, inequality, preference change, uncertainty, and effects on others. The article never establishes that its interface language changes this calculation.

#### Capability theory

Capability approaches ask what people are substantively able to do and be, rather than how powerful their cognitive instrument is. Applied BCI work has used capability thresholds, flourishing, and conversion factors to produce more operational ethical guidance than the target’s abstract equality claim. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13347-022-00597-1)

This approach might support enhancement for someone denied communication or participation while opposing an intervention that increases test performance but narrows social, emotional, or political agency. The interface premise does not decide between those cases.

#### Disability rights

Recent disability-oriented criticism argues both that enhancement can reinforce ableist oppression and that it is not inherently ableist if embedded in accessible institutions, genuine choice, and respect for disabled experience. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/bioe.12982)

Critics of procreative welfarism also object that standardised ideas of capacity and flourishing marginalise first-person disability perspectives and underestimate the role of social organisation in producing disadvantage. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bioe.13201)

The article needs to confront:

- the social model of disability;
- testimonial exclusion;
- adaptive environments;
- interdependence rather than independence as the norm;
- pressure to “correct” minority cognition;
- neurodiversity;
- the expressive effects of selecting against traits;
- the right to refuse enhancement;
- the distinction between increasing capability and enforcing conformity.

#### Distributive justice

“Access should not widen the gap” is not yet a distributive principle. The article must choose among, or compare:

- strict equality;
- Rawlsian fair equality of opportunity;
- prioritarianism;
- sufficientarianism;
- luck egalitarianism;
- relational equality;
- democratic equality;
- global justice;
- anti-domination;
- positional-goods analysis.

Recent work shows that apparently competing egalitarian, prioritarian, and sufficientarian approaches can converge on limiting unequal distribution where enhancement is positional or compounds social advantage. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11158-023-09581-8)

Equal access is not sufficient. A universally available enhancement can still create an arms race in which everyone must undergo it to remain competitive. Conversely, unequal initial provision may be justified if priority is given to people below a capability threshold.

Collective and relational critiques also reject the assumption that enhancement ethics can be reduced to a parent–child or individual-choice dyad. Social norms, group effects, institutional incentives, and community identity alter both the available choices and their meaning. [Wiley Online Library+1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/hast.1305)

## 9. Empirical claims that are insufficiently supported

### “Widening the filter”

The suggestion that a nootropic might widen the filter or expand consciousness’s expressive range is not derived from the cited literature. Improved alertness, memory performance, or signal-to-noise ratio does not tell us whether:

- more phenomenal content becomes available;
- the same content is processed more reliably;
- unconscious processing improves;
- attentional selection changes;
- reportability changes without experience changing.

The filter metaphor permits all these interpretations and therefore predicts little without an explicit mapping between cognitive measures and interface variables.

### BCI evidence and “genuine boundaries”

BCI learning within existing neural manifolds shows constraints on neural plasticity. It does not show where a nonphysical subject acts. The target’s claim that BCI adaptation indicates genuine interface boundaries is stronger than the neighbouring BCI article’s own evidential verdict. [topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary/)

### Developmental “permanent foreclosure”

The target says developmental pruning permanently forecloses perceptual territories. The linked development article is more cautious: critical-period closures can be high barriers rather than sealed doors, and experimentally induced plasticity can reopen at least some capacities in animal models. It also says the entire developmental record is equally accommodated by predictive-processing accounts. [topics/consciousness-interface-development](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/consciousness-interface-development/)

The target should say that developmental intervention may create **durable, difficult-to-reverse path dependence**, not that it necessarily closes territories permanently.

### Creative insight and biological channels

The article says creative gestalt shifts appear to require consciousness operating through specific biological channels. The linked creativity article documents neural preparations and distinctive phenomenology, but it also concedes that showing the felt qualities guide cognition rather than accompany neural processing requires a further argument. The current evidence does not identify a quantum interface site or show that artificial pathways would eliminate insight. [concepts/creative-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/creative-consciousness/)

At most, the literature supports caution that enhancement may trade one cognitive capacity for another. That is a familiar neurocognitive trade-off, not evidence for dualist channel dependence.

## 10. Tenet-protective bracketing and weak falsifiers

The article deserves credit for including a “What Would Challenge This View?” section. Unfortunately, most proposed challenges do not expose the article’s distinctive claim to a realistic test.

### “A successful materialist account”

Requiring a complete successful materialist account of consciousness is too global. The article’s local enhancement claims could fail even if the hard problem remained unsolved. For example, BCI bypass might prove incoherent, identity could follow organism continuity, or all enhancement effects could be explained without a channel variable.

### Enhancement without phenomenal change

The article says radical performance improvement without phenomenal change would count against the interface framing, but immediately observes that it could also mean processing occurs outside the conscious channel. The same result is thus both adverse evidence and compatible with the framework. This is a self-insulating structure.

A real discriminator would have to specify in advance which enhancements the model predicts should alter phenomenology and in what direction.

### Reports of identity disruption

As noted above, reports of feeling like a different person cannot test numerical identity. They test phenomenological or narrative continuity.

### Classical interaction

The article explicitly says a classical interaction mechanism would remove the quantum concern while leaving the broader interface ethics intact. It therefore is not a falsifier of the article’s central ethical framing.

### Tenet 5

Tenet 5 can block a premature inference from simplicity to truth. It cannot provide positive support for the interface interpretation. The methodology register expressly says a tenet may remove a defeater but may not upgrade an evidential level. [positions/methodology-and-calibration](https://unfinishablemap.org/positions/methodology-and-calibration/)

### Tenet 4

Once the article accepts that Everettian decision theory recovers ordinary Born-weighted recommendations, it must identify a concrete enhancement verdict that changes under single-world metaphysics. Otherwise Tenet 4 should be removed from this article.
