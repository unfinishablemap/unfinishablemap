## 1. TL;DR

- **Verdict: DEMOTE-TO-COHERENCE-ONLY, with DELETE for “Against Illusionism” and REVISE-HARD for “Disorders of the Axis.”** The felt doing/undergoing contrast is a real datum. Comparator, cue-integration and active-inference accounts explain it at least as well as the selection framework, and they also explain the dissociations (Desmurget stimulation, anarchic hand, thought insertion) that the article presents as evidence. The live meta-description still says the contrast is something “materialist accounts struggle to explain—and the Map’s framework predicts.” That contradicts the body’s own concession that the inference is “underdetermined by the evidence.” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/)
- **Citation integrity fails at the load-bearing points.** Lutz, Slagter, Dunne & Davidson (2008) report no agency data. Their open-monitoring description says practice involves “no strong distinction between selection and deselection,” with effort becoming “effortless.” That erodes the contrast rather than sharpening it. [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2693206) Schwartz et al. (1996) did not measure “sustained mental effort.” Rajan et al. (2019) did not measure “subjective agency.” Four of the 17 listed references (Bayne & Levy 2006, Haggard 2017, James 1890, Pacherie 2008) are orphans never cited in the body. The cited Rebouillat et al. (2021) is missing from the list. No reference postdates 2019.
- **It fails the Laukkonen–Friston–Chandaria gate and is stale.** The article never engages Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria (2025). That paper is a naturalist, computational account of epistemic agency as hyper-modelled precision control, and it explicitly claims to explain meditation and minimal phenomenal experience. [ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/nebior/abstract/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106296~a-beautiful-loop-an-active-inference-theory-of-consciousness) Those are exactly the cases the article uses against illusionism and in its “witness mode” passage. Also absent: Ciaunica et al. 2022 and 2024 on agency in depersonalisation, Suzuki et al. 2019 and Schwarz et al. 2019 on the validity of intentional binding, Martin & Pacherie 2013 on thought insertion, Synofzik et al. 2008, Moore & Fletcher 2012, and Schurger et al. 2012.

### 2.1 The phenomenology-to-metaphysics inference

The article’s own epistemic register now concedes the decisive point. The inference from vivid agency phenomenology to agentive causal control is “underdetermined by the evidence.” Wegner’s paradigms show “the feeling can be present where the control is not.” The verification gap “cannot be closed from within.” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) With that conceded, the remaining argument works only through three moves, and each fails.

1. **The calibration argument (calibration asymmetry).** The article says the epiphenomenalist must explain why felt effort attaches to “exactly the contested selections ... and not to equally costly processing that runs unfelt,” a distribution the brain-only account “must derive rather than assume.” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) Physicalist accounts already do derive it. Effort phenomenology tracks conflict detection and the expected value or opportunity cost of control allocation, not raw metabolic cost (Shenhav, Botvinick & Cohen 2013; Kurzban et al. 2013). That is exactly why it attaches to override and to holding a slipping focus. Meanwhile the Map’s alternative, that effort tracks “the *difficulty* of selection,” is asserted, not derived. [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) Demanding a derivation from the rival while exempting oneself is calibration asymmetry.
2. **The convergence argument (evidential-independence failure).** The article says “the convergence of multiple independent lines of evidence (introspective, implicit, neuroplastic) makes a wholesale dissociation progressively less plausible.” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) These lines are not independent. Intentional binding is measured by introspective temporal report. Its link to agency is disputed (see 2.2). [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9935715/) The “neuroplastic” line (Schwartz 1996) measured caudate metabolism in therapy responders, not effort. All three are generated by the same predictive and comparator machinery the deflationist invokes. The common-cause null is never applied. The site’s own benchmark (apex #33) is described in its creation review as applying “common-cause-null ... no convergence-count inflation.” [reviews/apex-evolve-2026-06-20-cross-modal-capability-division](/reviews/apex-evolve-2026-06-20-cross-modal-capability-division/) The target does neither.
3. **The phenomenal-character move (tenet leakage).** The article asks, “Why does the loss of authorship *feel* different from the loss of control, if both are just changes in information processing?” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) This is the generic hard problem renamed. It would apply equally to the difference between red and green. It draws nothing from agency data. It cannot support anything specific to the agency/passivity axis, still less Tenet 3.

The Desmurget result cuts against the article more than it admits. The article reads Desmurget et al. (2009) as merely constraining “which physicalist architectures remain available.” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) The stronger implication is ignored. Electrical current applied to parietal cortex produced the felt intention, and the false belief of having moved, with no conscious selection preceding it. [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) The datum the article treats as a registration of conscious contribution can be manufactured by an external physical cause. On the Map’s own reading, the phenomenology is therefore unreliable as a readout precisely where it matters. That is evidence against using it as a tracker of conscious causal input.

### 2.2 Rival naturalistic accounts

- **Comparator model** (Frith 1992; Frith, Blakemore & Wolpert 2000; Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith 2002). This predicts sensory attenuation of self-produced consequences. It predicts the agency/passivity contrast, its loss when efference copy or prediction fails (passivity symptoms), and the double dissociation in the Desmurget stimulation data. The article concedes the last point and invokes “the same comparator architecture” to make sense of binding in schizophrenia. [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) In doing so it recruits a physicalist mechanism as support (co-optation firewall failure).
- **Cue-integration and two-step accounts** (Synofzik, Vosgerau & Newen 2008; Moore & Fletcher 2012). These separate a non-conceptual *feeling* of agency from a conceptual *judgement* of agency, each weighted by cue reliability. They explain the Wegner I-Spy and helping-hands results, hypnotic involuntariness and flow: low prediction error means low salience of agency feelings, with no loss of control. The article’s flow passage (“Consciousness in flow still selects, only without resistance”) is an unfalsifiable rescue. [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) The cue-integration account predicts the same phenomenology without positing an undetectable selector.
- **Active inference** (Friston; Limanowski & Friston 2018, 2020; Seth; Hohwy; Clark). Here the sense of agency and mental action are the deployment and attenuation of precision over self-models. [ulisboa](https://cfcul.ciencias.ulisboa.pt/publicacoes/i-overthink-therefore-i-am-not-an-active-inference-account-of-altered-sense-of-self-and-agency-in-depersonalisation-disorder/) This is the account best placed to explain the contemplative and depersonalisation cases (see 2.6).
- **Intentional binding.** The article calls binding “a non-introspective measure of agency” and says “an epiphenomenal consciousness would have no reason to distort temporal perception selectively for voluntary actions.” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) Both claims fail.

- Binding is measured by introspective clock reports. [scite](https://scite.ai/reports/the-effects-of-action-choice-n6epAD4)[hedtags](https://www.hedtags.org/hed-resources/hed-task/tasks/hedtsk_intentional_binding.html)
- Suzuki, Lush, Seth & Roseboom (2019) found binding of identical magnitude with and without intentional action. [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9935715/) They conclude that binding is “most simply accounted for by multisensory causal binding.” [google](https://sites.google.com/view/keisukesuzuki/projects)
- Schwarz, Weller, Klaffehn & Pfister (2019) found that temporal binding and explicit agency ratings did not correlate significantly in any condition (N = 81). [roland-pfister](http://www.roland-pfister.net/publications/articles_oap/Schwarz_et_al_2019_ConCog.pdf)
- On epiphenomenalism, the *brain* produces the distortion, so the closing sentence attacks a straw man.
- **Wegner / Libet / Schurger.** Wegner gets one sentence, which is the only real engagement. Libet appears only in a Further Reading gloss (“Libet dissolution”). [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) Schurger, Sitt & Dehaene (2012) is absent. The stochastic-accumulator reading of the readiness potential is directly relevant to the “Directedness” and “Counterfactual awareness” bullets.
- **Bayne / Pacherie / Gallagher / Horgan–Tienson–Graham; thin vs thick.** Bayne & Levy (2006) and Pacherie (2008) are listed but never used. [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) Bayne & Levy argue for a deflationary reading of the “feeling of doing,” and Pacherie offers a naturalist multi-level framework. Horgan, Tienson & Graham (2003) is absent, though it is the one sympathetic source for a “thick,” self-as-source phenomenology. Without it the article cannot even state the thin/thick debate it silently takes a side in. The “Counterfactual awareness” bullet (“You could have done otherwise”) builds libertarian content into the phenomenal description. [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) That is the thick view assumed, not argued.

### 2.3 Thought insertion

The article claims mine-ness “dissociates from agency in one secure direction: agency is lost with ownership preserved in schizophrenic thought insertion.” It also says agency phenomenology “has been stripped from what should be an active process.” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/)

- **“Secure” is false.** The standard approach (ownership preserved, agency lost; Stephens & Graham 2000; Gallagher 2000, 2004) is one position among several:

- Martin & Pacherie (2013) argue that thought insertion “primarily involves a disruption of the sense of ownership,” with lost agency “but a consequence.” [philpapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/MAROON)
- Billon (2013) and Lane argue that inserted thoughts lack subjective for-me-ness. [concepts/self-and-self-consciousness](/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/)
- Henriksen, Parnas & Zahavi (2019) hold for-me-ness is “neither lacking nor unaffected but disturbed.” The Map’s own self-and-self-consciousness page quotes this. [concepts/self-and-self-consciousness](/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/)
- Sousa & Swiney (2013) defend a refined standard view against Fernández’s endorsement account. [philpapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/SOUTIA) The debate is live.
The target therefore contradicts a sibling page and the cited Sass & Parnas ipseity framework, which locates thought insertion in a disturbance of minimal self-presence, not a clean agency subtraction.
- **“Should be an active process” presupposes contested doctrine.** Whether ordinary thinking carries agency phenomenology is disputed. Langland-Hassan (2008) poses the “puzzle of extraneity,” since most thoughts are not experienced as willed either. The Map’s own doxastic-voluntarism page cites Strawson (2003) that thought and judgement “neither are nor can be a matter of action.” [concepts/doxastic-voluntarism](/concepts/doxastic-voluntarism/)
- **Nosology is absent.** The article does not note that DSM-5 removed the special diagnostic weight of Schneiderian first-rank symptoms (Tandon et al. 2013). ICD-11 retains passivity and influence experiences among core schizophrenia symptoms. The Cochrane review of FRS (Soares-Weiser et al. 2015) found that across 20 studies FRS separated schizophrenia from all other diagnoses with 57% sensitivity and 81.4% specificity. A symptom whose phenomenology, diagnostic specificity and ownership/agency structure are all contested cannot anchor a “secure” dissociation.

### 2.4 Depersonalisation

The disorders bullet says: “Actions feel automatic, as if performed by someone else. The entire experiential field shifts toward passivity. Patients describe watching themselves act without feeling like the actor.” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/)

- **Partly accurate, mostly overstated.** DSM-5-TR Criterion A does include being “an outside observer with respect to one’s ... actions.” But the core profile is unreality, detachment, emotional and physical numbing, perceptual alteration and distorted time sense, with reality testing intact (Criterion B). [squarespace +3](https://gecko-ladybug-pa2l.squarespace.com/s/188_Puder_Depersonalization-and-Derealization-fnd9.pdf) The Cambridge Depersonalisation Scale factor structure (Sierra et al. 2005) yields anomalous body experience, emotional numbing, anomalous subjective recall and alienation from surroundings. “Passivity” is not a factor.
- **“As if performed by someone else” imports psychotic phenomenology.** Ciaunica, Seth, Limanowski, Hesp & Friston (2022) state that individuals with depersonalisation maintain “the full insight that the ‘other agent’ is ‘me’ (the self).” That insight is the very feature that separates depersonalisation from passivity experiences in psychosis. [kevinwgrant](https://www.kevinwgrant.com/blog/item/the-fog-of-depersonalization-derealization-disorder)[nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9130736/)
- **The empirical agency data cut against the bullet.** Ciaunica, Ayache, Haggard et al. (2024, *Scientific Reports* 14:15396) found no significant differences between individuals with low and high depersonalisation on implicit or explicit sense of agency. The differences that did appear were in time sensitivity. The sample was non-clinical, [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-65862-z) which is a limitation, but it is a direct test using the intentional binding paradigm, and the article ignores it. A first-person report quoted there (“I ‘know’ I’m in control, but I’m not ‘feeling’ in control”) is evidence of an altered *feeling*. [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-65862-z) It is not evidence of a field-wide “shift toward passivity.”
- **Internal contradiction.** As noted in 2.0, the “Ownership Without Agency” paragraph now treats the depersonalisation limb as reading-dependent, while the disorders bullet and the “regime-stable” sentence assert it flatly.

### 2.5 Utilisation behaviour

The bullet says: “Patients with frontal damage act on every object they encounter—seeing scissors, they cut ... bypassing the agentic deliberation ... Passivity in the face of affordances replaces active selection.” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/)

- **“Every object they encounter” is false.** Lhermitte’s (1983) induced procedure involved the examiner presenting objects to the patient’s hands and visual field. [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002839321100368X) Shallice, Burgess, Schon & Baxter (1989) distinguished this *induced* form from *incidental* utilisation. In their patient, incidental utilisation occurred mostly in the brief intervals between tasks. [oup](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article-abstract/112/6/1587/343565) Lhermitte’s own abstract reports that “for the patients, the presentation of objects implies the order to grasp and use them”. [qxmd](https://read.qxmd.com/read/6850269/utilization-behaviour-and-its-relation-to-lesions-of-the-frontal-lobes) That reads as a misconstrued social instruction, not affordance-driven passivity.
- **It is not a disorder of the sense of agency.** Unlike anarchic hand, utilisation-behaviour patients typically own and rationalise their actions. They do not report them as alien or unauthored. Listing it on an “axis” of agency *phenomenology* conflates a control deficit with a phenomenal one. Besnard et al. (2011) go further: their findings “challenge the classical interpretation in terms of an executive control deficit,” favouring a social hypothesis. [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002839321100368X)
- **Localisation is omitted.** Lhermitte implicated the orbital frontal surface and possibly the head of the caudate. [oup](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article-abstract/106/2/237/299596) Shallice’s case had inferior medial bifrontal lesions. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/243775297_The_origins_of_utilization_behavior) Later reports add thalamic and caudate cases. [ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/annalsofian/fulltext/10.4103/0972-2327.150613~utilization-behavior) Iaccarino et al. (2014) record utilisation behaviour in 78% of a behavioural-variant FTD cohort. [nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4006616/) Lhermitte, Pillon & Serdaru (1986) place it within the environmental dependency syndrome. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/31142253_The_origin_of_utilization_behaviour) All of this fits a supervisory-attentional-system or frontal-disinhibition model (Shallice 1989; Archibald, Mateer & Kerns 2001). Nothing in it bears on non-physical selection.

### 2.6 The Laukkonen–Friston–Chandaria (2025) gate: FAIL

The article neither cites nor engages Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria (2025). Its argument does not survive the paper.

- LFC present an explicitly “computational theory of consciousness grounded in active inference.” It proposes “a hyper-model for precision-control” whose global preferences “enact the epistemic agency and flexibility reminiscent of general intelligence.” It claims to explain “altered states like meditation, psychedelics, and minimal states,” [ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/nebior/abstract/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106296~a-beautiful-loop-an-active-inference-theory-of-consciousness)[osf](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/daf5n) modelling minimal phenomenal experience as “an aware but empty epistemic field.” [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ruben-Laukkonen/publication/389740878_A_beautiful_loop_An_active_inference_theory_of_consciousness/links/67e4ff4ea43a11173be88d35/A-beautiful-loop-An-active-inference-theory-of-consciousness.pdf)
- The article’s anti-illusionist argument says agency phenomenology “is refined precisely *by* attending to it more carefully,” which is “better explained by its tracking a real distinction.” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) On a precision-control account this is expected. Attending raises precision on the self-model variables, which both changes and sharpens their discriminability. Trained discrimination of a modelled variable is not evidence that the variable is non-physical. The article never confronts this explanation.
- The article reads the “witness mode” as “consciousness choosing not to choose.” [topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) LFC and Ciaunica et al. (2022) model voluntary relaxation versus involuntary collapse of self-model precision (meditation versus depersonalisation) without positing a chooser outside the model. [research/voids-ownership-void-2026-02-18](/research/voids-ownership-void-2026-02-18/)
- Verdict: the gate is not engaged, and the two contemplative arguments are refuted on the gate’s terms.

1. **Propagation must be enforced, not logged.** The depersonalisation-limb repair was made on mine-ness and partly on the target, while the target’s own disorders bullet, its “regime-stable” sentence, two sibling pages and the highlights entry kept the retracted claim. The AI-assisted refine pipeline already greps for exact strings. It should grep for *propositions*: every page asserting “depersonalisation ... agency intact” or “double dissociation.” A repair should not be marked complete until all hits are fixed or carry a dated supersession note. Self-reviews that praise hedging while the falsified claim survives elsewhere are the operational form of confession-without-correction.
2. **Orphan and phantom reference checks.** A mechanical check would have caught the four orphans and the missing Rebouillat entry. Run it on every publish.
3. **Claim-level citation verification, not metadata verification.** The Lutz 2008 failure is a layer-3 failure that passed layer-1 checks. Claude-on-Claude reviewers verify that a DOI resolves and that a quoted string exists. They do not ask whether the paper *supports the sentence*. Each cited empirical claim should be paired with a short supporting span from the source. If no such span exists, the claim should be blocked.
4. **Meta-descriptions are claims.** The frontmatter description (“the Map’s framework predicts”) escaped the 10-27 revision. Include frontmatter in calibration review, and forbid “predicts” unless a discriminating prediction is stated.
5. **Blocking gates must actually block.** LFC 2025 is designated a standing gate for selfhood, agency and altered-state pages. This page touches all three and passed without engaging it. Make engagement a publish precondition, checked by a reviewer instance instructed to argue *for* LFC.
6. **Shared-model blind spots.** Generator and reviewer share priors, so the same favourable misreading of a canonical paper (Lutz) can pass every internal round. At least one review pass per load-bearing citation should use a reviewer given only the source text and the article sentence, without the Map’s framing. Clinical sections should be checked against DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 text and a named recent review, not against the Map’s own concept pages.
7. **Evidential ladder enforcement.** Require compatible/suggestive/discriminating tags on every empirical claim in topic articles, matching the apex #33/#34 standard. A page with no discriminating item cannot carry “against [rival]” section headings. Use “constraints on [rival]” instead.
