# Audit of “Panpsychism”

**Overall finding: major revision required, not rejection.**

The article’s descriptive core remains serviceable. It correctly presents panpsychism as an attempt to avoid brute emergence of consciousness, identifies Russellian monism and panprotopsychism as important variants, explains the classic subject-combination problem reasonably well, and acknowledges that the Map’s quantum-interactionist alternative is speculative, vulnerable to decoherence objections, and unsupported by decisive experimental evidence. It also concedes that panpsychism may initially look more parsimonious and that some philosophers reject the hard problem altogether. ## 1. Which central claims still hold?

### Panpsychism addresses a real explanatory pressure

The article is right that panpsychism is motivated partly by dissatisfaction with an apparently brute transition from wholly non-experiential fundamentals to consciousness. It is also right that constitutive Russellian micropsychism faces especially difficult questions about how numerous microexperiential subjects or properties could constitute a single macro-subject.

The opening definition is nevertheless too narrow. Saying that all matter has experience and illustrating this with an electron having “something it is like” characterises a familiar panexperientialist micropsychism. It does not neutrally characterise every position subsequently grouped under panpsychism, including panprotopsychism, cosmopsychism, emergent panpsychism, monadic proposals, or views that deny sharply bounded subjects. The article therefore begins by identifying the genus with one particularly vulnerable species of it. [concepts/panpsychism](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/panpsychism/)

### “Solves the hard problem” is too strong

Panpsychism can block one version of the emergence problem by placing experiential or proto-experiential reality at the fundamental level. That is not yet an explanation of why particular microfeatures yield a unified human experience, why those features combine or ground macroexperience, or why specific structural relations correspond to specific phenomenal characters.

Nagasawa distinguishes an ingredient problem from the process by which ingredients supposedly yield consciousness and argues that constitutive Russellian panpsychism can leave investigators at a cognitive dead end. Goff’s recent positive work is expressly an attempt to explain more carefully how panpsychism is supposed to improve on physicalism—evidence that the explanatory bridge cannot simply be presumed complete. [OUP Academic+2Ingenta Connect+2](https://academic.oup.com/aristoteliansupp/article/95/1/25/6312907)

The defensible formulation is therefore:

> Panpsychism avoids positing consciousness as emerging from an entirely non-experiential base, but it relocates much of the explanatory burden into constitution, grounding, structure and unity.

### The combination problem remains serious, but not uniform

The article’s account of the classic problem is broadly sound. Subject summing, the mismatch between microqualities and macroqualities, and the structural organisation of a unified field remain formidable problems for bottom-up constitutive micropsychism. Chalmers’s own taxonomy distinguishes multiple kinds of combination and multiple kinds of response rather than treating the issue as a single undifferentiated objection. [Consciousness Network+1](https://consc.net/papers/combination.pdf)

What has become untenable is using that classic problem as a verdict on panpsychism without distinguishing current alternatives:

| Current approach | Bearing on the article |
| --- | --- |
| **Monadic or non-combinatory panpsychism** | Kadić expressly constructs a form of panpsychism that is neither emergent nor combinatory. It incurs other explanatory costs, but it cannot be criticised as though it literally summed micro-subjects. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-023-04464-0) |
| **Cosmopsychism** | It replaces bottom-up combination with individuation or decombination. Shani disputes the assumption that the latter is simply a mirror image of the former or demonstrably insurmountable. [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/SHACCA-9) |
| **Continuism** | Roelofs questions the assumption that subjects have perfectly sharp boundaries, changing rather than straightforwardly solving the combination problem. The costs concern individuation and counting, but the position deserves separate treatment. [Imprint+1](https://www.imprint.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Roelofs_Open_Access.pdf) |
| **Weak emergent and panprotopsychist views** | Recent assessments continue to find some variants viable despite the standard objections. [Crítica](https://critica.filosoficas.unam.mx/index.php/critica/article/view/1748/) |
| **Quantum panprotopsychism** | Gambini and Pullin argue that classical assumptions underlie some palette, grain and subject-summing objections and explore quantum resources for answering them. This is particularly relevant because it contests the Map’s implicit monopoly on quantum responses to consciousness. [PhilSci Archive+2arXiv+2](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/23857/) |
| **Very recent monadic panmentalism** | Ioannou’s July 2026 proposal addresses the ingredient problem without conventional micro-to-macro combination. It appeared only shortly before the revision, so its omission is understandable, but it now belongs in a current review. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-026-05697-5) |

None of these approaches is an accepted solution. Their importance is taxonomic: the page cannot infer that panpsychism as such requires literal subject summing.

### The combination problem provides comparative pressure, not evidence for dualism by itself

The Map’s dualism does avoid one specific problem: it does not construct a subject out of many independently existing micro-subjects. A single subject is already present. This is a genuine disanalogy, and the article is entitled to count it in the Map’s favour.

It does not follow that the Map “doesn’t face this problem” without qualification. The remaining questions include:

- why one subject is paired with one particular body;
- how that subject acts through many distributed neural or quantum sites as one agent;
- what psychophysical law maps an intention onto particular collapse possibilities;
- how spatial and temporal targeting works;
- and how the intervention remains both causally efficacious and compatible with observed quantum statistics.

These are not literally the subject-combination problem. Calling them identical would be unfair. They are, however, a parallel unity and interface debt. The revised combination article now concedes exactly this and says the primitive unity assigned to the dualist interface has a symmetry with the primitiveness attributed to phenomenal bonding. Yet the same page still retains older statements that the Map avoids combination “entirely,” producing an internal contradiction even within the neighbouring article. [concepts/combination-problem](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/combination-problem/)

The appropriate comparative conclusion is:

> The Map avoids constitutive subject summing but exchanges it for subject–substrate pairing, interface unity and psychophysical-law problems.

That is weaker than the article’s present table, but stronger and more defensible than claiming that dualism simply has the combination problem under another name.

## 3. Specific factual and attribution problems

### Goff’s cosmopsychism is conflated with Kastrup’s analytic idealism

The statement that “the later Goff” treats individual minds as dissociated fragments is inaccurate. Goff’s cosmopsychist work is framed through priority, grounding and constitution relations between cosmic and individual consciousness. Dissociation is characteristic of Kastrup’s analytic idealism.

The site’s own neighbouring pages already make this distinction: its cosmopsychism article describes Goff in grounding and priority terms, while its idealism article associates dissociated alters with individual minds or living organisms. [concepts/idealism](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/idealism/)

The further suggestion that analytic idealism includes “particle-experiences” as dissociated fragments is also misleading. On the neighbouring idealism page, particles and ordinary physical objects belong to the represented appearance of a more fundamental mental reality; they are not each identified as an individual dissociated subject. [concepts/idealism](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/idealism/)

This is a substantive theoretical conflation, not merely an imprecise citation.

### The IIT description is outdated

The article uses the older slogan that any system with non-zero Φ is conscious. IIT 4.0 is more discriminating. Many overlapping candidate systems may have positive system integrated information, but the exclusion postulate selects a maximal substrate or complex. Positive Φ by itself is therefore not sufficient for every overlapping candidate system to count as a distinct conscious entity. [PLOS+3PLOS+3PLOS+3](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article)

The article may still discuss the counterintuitive possibility that very simple systems qualify under IIT. It should not present the non-zero-Φ rule as the current theory without explaining maximality and exclusion.

A current treatment should also acknowledge the 2025 adversarial collaboration testing biological implementations of IIT and global neuronal workspace theory. Its results supported some predictions while substantially challenging central expectations of both frameworks. That experiment did not test panpsychism, so it should not be used as a direct verdict on panpsychism, but it is now part of the evidential setting for claims made under the IIT heading. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1)

### The C. elegans Φ claim is unsupported as written

I could not verify an established whole-animal IIT 4.0 calculation showing that *C. elegans* has positive Φ in the sense implied by the article. Older work has applied integrated-information-like network measures to simplified connectome models, while recent reviews treat the worm’s possible minimal consciousness as an open, theory-dependent question. Exact Φ calculations also remain computationally feasible only for very small model systems, far below a full brain or nervous system. [PubMed+3ar5iv+3SciELO Colombia+3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1510.07260)

The claim should either be sourced to a precisely specified model and metric or replaced with:

> Some simplified models of the *C. elegans* network have been assigned positive integrated-information-related measures; this does not establish that the organism is conscious under IIT 4.0.

### Panpsychism does not by itself solve prebiotic collapse

The article first states the point conditionally—if consciousness participates in collapse—but then generalises that prebiotic collapse is “no problem” for panpsychists. Panpsychism alone is an ontology of mentality or experience. It does not entail that consciousness collapses wave functions.

A panpsychist consciousness-collapse theory could posit ubiquitous experiential participants and thereby avoid a period with no collapse-capable observers. But that requires an independent collapse thesis. Many panpsychists reject consciousness-caused collapse entirely.

The Map’s current prebiotic-collapse article has itself moved toward objective reduction plus later consciousness-dependent modulation. It explicitly acknowledges a remaining completeness tension: if objective collapse already determines an outcome completely, consciousness must either alter outcome statistics or be causally redundant. The panpsychism article is not aligned with that newer formulation. [concepts/prebiotic-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/prebiotic-collapse/)

## 4. Missing counterarguments from the 2020s

The article mentions illusionism but does not engage Frankish’s panpsychism-specific objection. Frankish argues that panpsychism may be an answer to a badly framed question and that relocating phenomenal properties to a metaphysical base risks detaching consciousness from its scientific, personal and ethical significance. Whether persuasive or not, that is stronger than merely noting that some philosophers deny the hard problem. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/aristoteliansupp/article/95/1/51/6312909)

It also omits current debate over whether Russellian panpsychism has a genuine advantage in explaining mental causation. Baysan argues that the alleged advantage is illusory. This matters because the article’s comparative ledger implicitly allows panpsychism to inherit physical causal efficacy through intrinsic properties while presenting dualist interaction as the alternative empirical burden. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10670-024-00816-5.pdf)

Conversely, the article does not engage the strongest recent positive accounts on their own terms. Goff’s 2024 work asks how panpsychism is supposed to explain consciousness rather than merely assuming that putting consciousness at the base ends the inquiry. Quantum panprotopsychist work also matters especially to this site because it contests the claim that quantum theory primarily supports a dualist interface. [arXiv+3Ingenta Connect+3Ingenta Connect+3](https://www.ingentaconnect.com/content/imp/jcs/2024/00000031/f0020003/art00003)

A fair current audit should therefore oppose the Map not only to classic constitutive micropsychism, but to the strongest versions of:

- Russellian constitutive panpsychism;
- cosmopsychism;
- non-combinatory and monadic panpsychism;
- continuism;
- emergent panpsychism;
- panprotopsychism;
- and quantum panprotopsychism.

## 5. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article is not wholly tendentious. Its concessions about parsimony, decoherence, lack of empirical support and disagreement over the hard problem are real and substantial. Those passages make it more balanced than a straightforward advocacy document. [concepts/panpsychism](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/panpsychism/)

The problem is that several concessions are quarantined rather than allowed to change the eventual verdict.

### The hard problem is disputed, then treated as settled

Early in the article, Dennett and Frankish are acknowledged as rejecting the hard problem. Later, under “What Panpsychism Gets Right,” the page states that physicalism fails and that the hard problem is real. The latter may accurately express the Map’s foundational commitments, but it should be marked as a tenet-relative judgment:

> Given the Map’s rejection of physicalism, panpsychism correctly recognises that consciousness cannot be reduced to ordinary structural-functional description.

Without that qualification, a concept article oscillates between external exposition and internal doctrine. [concepts/panpsychism](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/panpsychism/)

### Primitive unity is assessed asymmetrically

The page treats phenomenal bonding or constitutive unity as a major debit when panpsychists regard it as primitive. The Map’s own unified subject, however, is permitted to be fundamental, and until the 23 July revision its distributed physical interface was treated as unproblematic.

There is a legitimate distinction: primitive existence of one subject is not primitive composition of many subjects. But once that subject must select among possibilities across many physical loci, the site still owes a unity relation at the interface. The neighbouring revision now recognises that both positions eventually place explanatory weight somewhere near bedrock. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

### “Empirical rather than conceptual” is treated as a victory too early

The article suggests that the interaction problem is preferable because it is empirical whereas combination is conceptual. That could become a genuine advantage if the quantum proposal supplied a formal coupling law, discriminating prediction and feasible test.

At present, locating a possible intervention point in quantum indeterminacy is not yet a mechanism. The neighbouring universal-coupling page claims a sharp predictive difference from threshold approaches while also saying that systems such as *C. elegans* may fall below an architectural threshold whose boundary remains vague. That weakens the claim that the Map couples to all suitable systems “without arbitrary thresholds.” [concepts/universal-coupling-response](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/universal-coupling-response/)

An empirical promissory note should not be scored as more tractable than a conceptual problem until it is operationalised.

### The closing choice is artificially narrow

The article largely reduces the field to a choice between panpsychist combination and dualist interaction. That is useful as a comparison between two selected theories, but not as an exhaustive map of positions. Illusionism, non-Russellian physicalism, idealism, neutral monism, non-combinatory panpsychism and other views do not fit that forced choice. [concepts/panpsychism](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/panpsychism/)

## 6. Integration with neighbouring articles

The article’s link architecture is comparatively good. It sends readers to pages on the combination problem, Russellian monism, cosmopsychism, idealism, IIT, universal coupling and prebiotic collapse. The problem is semantic synchronisation rather than navigability.

The most important inconsistencies are:

1. **Combination Problem:** the 23 July revision concedes interface unity, while both the panpsychism article and residual passages in the combination article still say the Map avoids the problem entirely. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)
2. **Russellian Monism:** the Map is still presented as a stable endpoint or as straightforwardly sidestepping combination, without importing the newer interface-unity qualification. [concepts/russellian-monism](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/russellian-monism/)
3. **Universal Coupling Response:** the panpsychism page promises coupling without arbitrary thresholds, whereas the neighbouring page invokes architectural adequacy and says some systems may be below the relevant threshold. [concepts/universal-coupling-response](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/universal-coupling-response/)
4. **Prebiotic Collapse:** panpsychism is said to remove the problem, while the dedicated page now relies on objective reduction and leaves consciousness’s additional causal contribution unresolved. [concepts/prebiotic-collapse](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/prebiotic-collapse/)
5. **Cosmopsychism and Idealism:** the panpsychism page merges Goff’s grounding model with Kastrup’s dissociation model, contrary to the dedicated pages. [concepts/idealism](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/idealism/)
6. **IIT:** the summary relies on an older non-zero-Φ formulation and an unsupported *C. elegans* example rather than current IIT 4.0 terminology. [PLOS+1](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article)
7. **In-depth combination topic:** the site already has a richer dedicated treatment, but it has not been fully synchronised with the 23 July interface concession or the broader current taxonomy. [topics/panpsychisms-combination-problem](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/panpsychisms-combination-problem/)

The changelog suggests a procedural reason for this drift. Earlier review notes classified the combination-versus-interaction framing as effectively settled and discouraged future reviews from repeatedly flagging it. The 23 July correction then showed that the supposedly settled framing contained an unresolved double standard. The site has also proposed propagation, freshness and opponent-parity safeguards, but as of 21 July those process changes had not been enacted. [GitHub+2GitHub+2](https://github.com/unfinishablemap/unfinishablemap/commit/2f8ea3d877e6b209439c9d51361209ff0a2b13e1)

That is a concrete instance of premature convergence becoming tenet-protective: a core comparative claim was shielded from renewed scrutiny because the workflow, rather than the argument, had declared it stable.

## Bottom line

The article’s strongest surviving conclusion is that **constitutive micropsychism has a severe unity problem and that the Map’s single-subject ontology does not inherit that exact many-to-one constitution problem**.

Its strongest unsupported conclusion is that **the Map therefore avoids the relevant explanatory difficulty altogether**. The current site has already begun retracting that claim elsewhere.

The article should no longer be presented as a current field-level assessment until it:

- differentiates the major families of panpsychism;
- distinguishes subject combination from the Map’s interface-unity debt;
- corrects the Goff/Kastrup attribution;
- updates IIT;
- removes or substantiates the *C. elegans* claim;
- makes prebiotic-collapse claims conditional;
- and incorporates the 2020s literature.

### To the site’s methodology

1. **Enact a dependency-propagation system.** When a central claim such as “avoids combination entirely” is revised, automatically reopen every article containing that phrase or depending on that comparison.
2. **Put expiry and reopening conditions on convergence locks.** A framing should cease to be “settled” when a neighbouring article changes, new literature appears, or a semantic contradiction is detected.
3. **Use three-stage citation verification:** first confirm that a source exists; then verify that it entails the exact local claim and is attributed to the correct author; finally check whether newer literature materially changes the claim.
