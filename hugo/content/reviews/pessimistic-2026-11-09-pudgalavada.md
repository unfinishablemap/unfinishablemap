# Pessimistic Review

**Date**: 2026-11-09
**Content reviewed**: `obsidian/concepts/pudgalavada.md` (2,849 body words by `analyze_length`; concepts hard limit 3,500; headroom 650). Prior review: `deep-review-2026-09-28-pudgalavada.md` (read in full; its Stability Notes are honoured below). No prior pessimistic review of this page.

## Executive Summary

The page is well sourced and its source/Map separation is unusually clean; every quotation I could re-open checks out. The weaknesses are in the Map's own comparison, not in the scholarship. Three are structural: the page puts the Map's subject on two different horns of Vasubandhu's own dilemma (the "eternal soul" horn at L73/L83, the caused-and-must-name-its-causes horn at L79), so "eternal" is being used in two senses; it accepts Vasubandhu's criterion that the real is the causally efficient while the tenets page registers the subject's causal efficacy as an unsecured posit, which makes the subject's reality hostage to Tenet 3 without saying so; and it contrasts the Map's subject with an "unchanging" Nyāya *ātman* that the Map's own Indian-philosophy page describes as a substance bearing changing qualities. The Carpenter "individuation" match is also a match to the synchronic half of haecceity that the haecceity page itself says cannot do the No-Many-Worlds work. Everything else is attribution precision and pointer hygiene.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would say the page has quietly conceded her point and not noticed. It accepts Vasubandhu's test, "everything that is real or substantial (*dravya*) is causally efficient" (L63, L79), and then owes a causally efficient subject. The Map's tenets page says the interface argument shows downward causation to be "available ... without showing it to be *actual*" and that genuine non-epiphenomenal causation is "a posit the interface argument leaves open, not a result it secures" (`tenets.md` L95). On the criterion the page adopts, a posited-but-unsecured cause is not yet a *dravya*; the page should say the subject's reality under this test rises or falls with Tenet 3. *Defect* (Finding 2), because it is the page's own criterion that generates the exposure. Her further claim that the vocabulary of "person" will be eliminated is a framework disagreement; the page already marks the completeness of the causal chain as bedrock.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett's attack lands on L81. The page says the *Kathāvatthu*'s charge "applies to a subject given as the for-me-ness of experience rather than observed as an object", and in the next sentence that "Map pages should not say the subject is 'found' in experience". A subject *given* in experience is a subject found there under a different verb; introspection is exactly the faculty whose report is in dispute. The corpus is more careful than the page: Tenet 3 is held "not as a directly introspectible datum" (`tenets.md` L93) and the persisting subject is "posited rather than inferred" from felt mine-ness (`where-the-substance-commitment-enters.md` L73). *Defect* (Finding 6): the page should use the corpus's register. His broader claim that "neither same nor different" shows the Personalists were on the way to his own view is a framework disagreement.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark has little purchase on a page about Indian Buddhism and should not be given more than he has. His one real point is parasitic on Finding 2: the page's reply to Vasubandhu's agent argument is "the chain is not sufficient at the point of selection" (L79), and the tenets page concedes that selection within the Born distribution is "empirically indistinguishable from chance" under unconditioned tests (`tenets.md` L75). So the "principal cause" question the page leaves "unsettled" is not merely unsettled but, on the Map's own account, undetectable by the tests so far available. That is already recorded at the tenet; the page need not re-run it. Not a defect here.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch's point is the sharpest of the six and it is a defect. L69 says Carpenter's reading, "some causal streams are really individuated", "matches more closely still" and "is close to the particularity claim in [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/)". But the haecceity page records that causal-history individuation "runs symmetrically across" post-branch continuants and "picks out neither as mine", and that the indexical objection "needs a subject that persists *across* the measurement", a fact the process reading "leaves ... to be posited outright" (`haecceity.md` L171, L173). Branch-relative Everettian identity *satisfies* Carpenter's individuation thesis. So the closest structural match the page finds is to the half of the Map's subject that does no anti-many-worlds work, and Carpenter's Personalist explicitly disclaims the "further fact" the other half needs (L51). The page should say so (Finding 4). Deutsch's claim that the indexical question is confused is a framework disagreement, handled at the tenet.

### The Empiricist

Popper asks what the page's own comparison could get wrong. The honest answer is in L73 and L79, and they disagree. L73 has the Map "take as a horn what the Personalist took as a refutation" and "inherit the arguments aimed at the eternal soul"; L83 records a cost "under the eternalism horn". L79 has the Map take the *caused* sub-horn, where Vasubandhu says the entity "will be produced by causes and then it will not be eternal", and owe its causes. A subject cannot be on both. *Defect* (Finding 1). A second Popperian point: L93 gives the Map's Tenet 5 reply to an argument that the page itself classes (L79) as a causal-closure argument; Tenet 5 blocks a parsimony inference, it does not answer a completeness claim. The reply the page owes there is Tenet 2's, and the paragraph should say Tenet 5 is doing only the defensive job the dependency matrix assigns it ("Defensive" in the Agency row, `tenets.md` L157). *Defect* (Finding 5). The underlying unfalsifiability of the corridor reading is a framework disagreement already registered at the tenet.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nāgārjuna's challenge is pointed to `buddhism-and-dualism#emptiness-challenge` (L87) rather than re-run, and the deep review installed that pointer; honoured. What he would add is narrower and is a defect of calibration rather than doctrine: the Dualism paragraph says the pudgala "shares the Map's Dualism tenet only in its negative claim, that the person is not reducible to its constituents" (L87). Tenet 1's negative claim is that *consciousness* is not reducible to *physical* processes; the pudgala's irreducibility is to aggregates four of which are mental. The page corrects itself in the next sentence but the first sentence conflates two different negatives (Finding 7). His substantive claim, that the pudgala and the Map's subject both reify, is a framework disagreement.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The Map's subject sits on two horns of Vasubandhu's dilemma — TASK
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/pudgalavada.md`
- **Location**: L73: "The Map takes as a horn what the Personalist took as a refutation, and so inherits the arguments aimed at the eternal soul as well." L83: "for persistence within a life it answers "same", at the cost already recorded under the eternalism horn." Against L79: "The Map must "name its causes", and on the subject's origin it holds only a candidate, Hasker's [emergent-dualism](/concepts/emergent-dualism/), as a cousin rather than a commitment. And a caused subject "must change as they change"".
- **Problem**: Vasubandhu's entity horn (quoted at L61) subdivides: the entity "will be produced by causes and then it will not be eternal as you say, and you will have to name its causes; or it will be unconditioned". L79 puts the Map on the caused, non-eternal sub-horn. L73 and L83 put it on the eternal-soul horn. "Eternal" is doing two jobs: in L73/L83 it means "non-momentary, numerically the same across moments" (the Abhidharma sense in which any persisting subject is eternal); in L79 it means Vasubandhu's "unconditioned". A reader cannot tell which refutations the page thinks reach the Map, which is the section's whole purpose. This is the epistemic/metaphysical-style equivocation the skill asks for, applied to "eternal".
- **Severity**: High
- **Persona**: Empiricist (Popper)
- **Recommendation**: At L73 replace "and so inherits the arguments aimed at the eternal soul as well" with "and so inherits the arguments aimed at a self distinct from the aggregates, though not those aimed at its permanence, since the Map's subject is caused and changes (below)". At L83 replace "under the eternalism horn" with "under the entity horn". Net +18 words.

### Issue 2: Accepting the causal-efficiency criterion exposes the subject's reality to Tenet 3's unsecured posit — TASK
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/pudgalavada.md`
- **Location**: L79: "The entity-or-designation dilemma also reaches the Map, which takes the entity horn and accepts Vasubandhu's criterion that the real is the causally efficient."
- **Problem**: The criterion (Gold's paraphrase, correctly attributed at L63) makes reality conditional on causal efficacy. The tenets page records that "Genuine, non-epiphenomenal downward causation is therefore a posit the interface argument leaves open, not a result it secures" and that [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3) "sits genuinely close to epiphenomenalism" (`tenets.md` L95). The page accepts the criterion and never says what it costs: on Vasubandhu's own test the Map's subject is real only if Tenet 3 is actual, not merely available. Articles asserting the subject does "real work" are told at the tenet to "inherit that debt rather than discharge it"; this page inherits it silently.
- **Severity**: High
- **Persona**: Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)
- **Recommendation**: After "causally efficient." add: "That acceptance has a price the Map records elsewhere: its subject's causal efficacy is a posit the interface argument leaves open rather than secures ([Tenet 3's standing](/tenets/#tenet-3-standing)), so on Vasubandhu's own test the subject is real only if that posit holds." Net +45 words.

### Issue 3: The Nyāya *ātman* is not "unchanging" on the Map's own account; the contrast is eternality and origin — TASK
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/pudgalavada.md`
- **Location**: L35: "the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika *ātman*, which the Map also declines because it rejects an unchanging core." L73: "The Map declines that too, because the *ātman* is unchanging and the Map's subject is not ... The honest placement is between: ... unlike both in persisting through change."
- **Problem**: [topics/indian-philosophy-of-mind.md](/topics/indian-philosophy-of-mind/) L73–75 describes the Nyāya self as "a non-physical substance" whose consciousness "is an *adventitious* quality", "closer to an Aristotelian substance that bears properties", "the seat of desire, aversion, effort, pleasure, pain, and knowledge"; "Consciousness comes and goes; the self persists." A substance that bears changing qualities while remaining itself *persists through qualitative change*, which is the feature the page reserves for the Map. The genuine contrasts are two, and the page has them in hand elsewhere: the Nyāya self is eternal and uncaused, whereas the Map's subject owes its causes (L79); and the Map's subject changes intrinsically, "as an organism remains numerically the same while every cell is replaced" (`self-and-self-consciousness.md` L174), whereas the Nyāya self changes only in its adventitious qualities. "Unchanging" collapses both into a contrast the Map's own page does not support. (IEP's Priestley entry never mentions Nyāya or Vaiśeṣika; the placement is the Map's, as the page says, so the Map's own description governs.)
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Persona**: Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett), pressing the "which is it?" question
- **Recommendation**: L35: "because it rejects an unchanging core" → "because it rejects an uncaused, intrinsically immutable bearer". L73: "because the *ātman* is unchanging and the Map's subject is not" → "because the *ātman* is eternal and changes only in its adventitious qualities, whereas the Map's subject is caused and changes in itself"; "unlike both in persisting through change" → "unlike both in being a caused bearer that itself changes". Net +22 words. Best bundled with Issue 1 in one refine pass, since both turn on the same horn.

## Counterarguments to Address

### "Everything real is causally efficient" cuts against the Map, not for it
- **Current content says**: the Map "accepts Vasubandhu's criterion that the real is the causally efficient" (L79) and treats the entity horn as a position it can hold.
- **A critic would argue**: the Map's own register says the subject's efficacy is a posit left open and observer-indistinguishable from an unselected outcome; by the accepted criterion the subject is at best provisionally real.
- **Suggested response**: say so, with the pointer to `tenets#^tenet-3-standing` (Issue 2). The page cannot discharge the debt; it can stop hiding it.

### Branch-relative identity satisfies Carpenter's Personalist
- **Current content says**: Carpenter's individuation thesis is the closest match to the Map's subject (L69).
- **A critic would argue**: "some causal streams are really individuated" is true in an Everettian multiverse too; the match buys the Map nothing against many-worlds.
- **Suggested response**: scope the match to the synchronic half and name the diachronic further fact as the point where the Personalist and the Map part (Issue 4).

### Which self do the eternal-soul arguments hit?
- **Current content says**: the Map inherits "the arguments aimed at the eternal soul" (L73) and pays a cost "under the eternalism horn" (L83), while owing its causes (L79).
- **A critic would argue**: a caused, changing subject is not the Vaiśeṣika permanent self; only the agent/controller argument among the late-chapter arguments reaches it, and the page should not claim more exposure than it has, nor less.
- **Suggested response**: disambiguate "eternal" (Issue 1) and let the reach taxonomy follow.
