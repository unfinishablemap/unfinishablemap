## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found
- **Vedāntic term credited to Patañjali.** The Yoga paragraph read "yoga is defined (1.2) as … and *nirvikalpa samādhi* is absorption without conceptual modification". The GRETIL Sanskrit text of the *Yoga Sūtras* has zero occurrences of *nirvikalp-*; the term belongs to later Vedānta. The YS's own terms are *nirbījaḥ samādhiḥ* (1.51) and *tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe 'vasthānam* (1.3, "then the seer abides in its own form"). The article's claim that *puruṣa* remains had a direct anchor in 1.3 that it never cited. **Fixed**: the sentence now cites 1.51 (seedless *samādhi*) and 1.3 (the seer abiding in its own form). The parent [comparative-phenomenology-of-meditative-traditions](/topics/comparative-phenomenology-of-meditative-traditions/) L67 has the same imprecision; it is out of scope here and listed as a follow-up.
- **Yoga folded into "full".** "At the limit, awareness *persists* and is full, not stopped" credited Yoga with plenitude. In Yoga, every modification is stilled in seedless *samādhi* and the seer stands alone (*kaivalya*), so it persists without being "full". **Fixed**: "awareness *persists* rather than stops—in Advaita as fullness, in Yoga as the seer standing alone once every modification is stilled." This keeps the article's actual claim (both traditions agree that awareness persists) without the overreach. The rewrite also drops one intensifier use of "load-bearing" ("the load-bearing point" → "the decisive point").
- **Dropped qualifier on an internal quote.** The lede said the parent names this divergence "the most significant" of three. The parent (L123) says "*Perhaps* the most significant divergence". **Fixed**: the lede now reads perhaps "the most significant". The Further Reading gloss, which had escalated this to "the most load-bearing of its three named divergence zones", now reads "it calls cessation-versus-plenitude perhaps the most significant of its three named divergence zones".

### Medium Issues Found
- **Pronoun in today's insertion bound to the wrong subject.** "Christian tradition describes the beatific vision as …, but **it** supplies doctrine rather than a report from practice."
