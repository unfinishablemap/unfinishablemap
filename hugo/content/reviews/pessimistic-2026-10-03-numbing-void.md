## Executive Summary

The sourcing is unusually clean. Every quoted span was checked against a source fetched in this run, and all of them match verbatim in both subject and predicate. The writer's correction of Lifton to "rescue workers" is what Slovic (2007) actually says. The article has four real defects. (1) The writer's own move in *Where the Map Stands* claims more than [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) licenses. Closed individualism is common ground with Lewis and Taurek, so it cannot be what refuses Lewis's inference. The additive step belongs to [P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3)'s counting rule, which [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) alone does not supply. (2) The "resists knowledge" paragraph reports Small et al.'s debiasing finding without saying that Maier et al., cited one paragraph earlier, found "no support for debiasing". It also reads a giving result as a feeling result, which breaks the helping/concern distinction the article itself draws from Butts. (3) The void's central "since" clause equivocates. "Felt measures are one-subject-sized" is either trivially true or an empirical capacity ceiling, and the article's own regulation face and its "grant perfect affect" thought experiment both undercut the second reading. One sentence also keeps residue of the dropped "calculation with no subject" line. (4) Two of the integration seams describe the page wrongly, by turning the structural face into the cause of the empirical plateau. A third upgrades Cameron and Payne's "suggested" to "shows". All four items fit easily in the headroom: together they cost +51.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

"No one undergoes the sum" is true of every aggregate. No one undergoes a continent's total rainfall either, and nothing is hidden there, because there is nothing there to find. The magnitude face describes compressive valuation curves, and the regulation face describes prefrontal down-regulation. The article itself concedes that "Physicalist accounts of emotion regulation describe the case equally well". What remains to call a *void*? The article's best answer is that the void proper is the undecidability between the deficit and tracking readings. As written, though, that undecidability rests on the instrument being "one-subject-sized", which is a fact about affective range that neuroscience could in principle measure. The answer the article needs is that feeling has no calibration target at the aggregate, because no felt total exists anywhere. That is the item-3 fix.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

"A felt grasp of the *sum* would still need a subject who undergoes the sum." Why? Empathy for one person does not require undergoing that person's toothache. The [empathy](/concepts/empathy/) page says as much ("one cannot undergo the other's states"). So why would concern for a million require a subject who undergoes the million? The article moves from *feeling about* the many, which is possible and could in principle scale, to *undergoing* the total, which is impossible but irrelevant to empathy even at N = 1. Its heterophenomenology is also thin. "From inside, nothing seems missing" is contradicted by the article's own two witnesses: Szent-Györgyi names the gap, and Dillard's irony ("See? Nothing to it") shows she knows something is missing. Reports of felt inadequacy in the compassion-fatigue literature are the ordinary case, not the rare one.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

The article correctly says that Minimal Quantum Interaction "is not engaged". The Bidirectional Interaction paragraph is weaker. It calls instructed down-regulation "a modest instance" of the tenet. At the level the article describes, though, the case runs mental to mental ("a mental act shapes what becomes available to feel"), and any instance of Tenet 3 would have to run mental to physical. Behavioural affect ratings cannot show that. The paragraph's own next sentence ("describe the case equally well ... adds nothing to the tenet's standing") is right, and "instance" contradicts it. It should read "illustration".

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

The symmetric caution is well judged. But consider the Map's own case against many-worlds ("suffering is never reduced but redistributed"). If it is read aggregatively, it treats the cross-branch total as what moral choice should reduce. On the tracking reading, which the article says the Map "has not refuted", redistribution among branch-persons is no objection at all, because each branch-person's suffering is what it is. [phenomenal-value-realism](/topics/phenomenal-value-realism/) partly protects itself by putting the point indexically ("*this* suffering, in *this* branch"). The numbing void, however, says such arguments run through "the indexical-identity premise, where the Map's additive verdict already rests". That is the conflation [ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness](/topics/ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness/) explicitly warns against: rejecting MWI and settling the copy count are "logically orthogonal", and "The tenets are *consistent with* the Duplication View; they do not *force* it." The phrase should read "partly rests" (item 1).

### The Empiricist (Popper's Ghost)

The magnitude face is falsifiable, and the article calibrates it well against Butts, Lee and Feeley, and Maier et al. Two other claims are weaker. First, "The limit also resists knowledge" stands on a finding whose replication failed, and the failed replication is cited in the very same section. Second, calling the regulation face *occluded* rests on an untested step from timing to awareness. Cameron and Payne's time-course data concern *when* affect ratings diverge. They do not show whether participants noticed any damping. What observation would show that people *do* notice it? The article proposes none. The structural face cannot be falsified by design. The article admits this ([P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3)'s discriminability is *none*), which is acceptable so long as the face is never counted as evidence. The article does not count it so.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

The Map's reply to Lewis is that "a sum no one suffers is still a sum of sufferings, each of which someone suffers". That reply needs sufferings to *add* although no one bears the total. So the additive verdict needs value to aggregate with no owner of the aggregate, while closed individualism insists that value needs an owner. If the total can count without a bearer, what work does the bearer do? And if all the value lies in each sufferer, as the article's Occam paragraph says ("the value is had in each subject, separately"), what does the sum add beyond the each? That second question is Lewis's and Taurek's point. The [ownerless suffering](/concepts/the-ownerless-suffering-argument/) comparison in Neighbouring Voids is well placed, but the article does not see that its own reply leans toward Śāntideva's side.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Closed individualism cannot by itself refuse Lewis's inference

- **File**: [numbing-void](/voids/numbing-void/)
- **Location**: L74, "Closed individualism grants Lewis's premise, since no trans-subject subject exists to undergo the total, and refuses his inference: a sum no one suffers is still a sum of sufferings, each of which someone suffers." Also L76 ("is what the void predicts") and L98 ("where the Map's additive verdict already rests").
- **Problem**: Lewis's own example has two separate sufferers ("you, who are seated beside me"). Taurek's standard statement presupposes five separate losses that are "summed in no sufferer". Both positions accept that subject boundaries are real. What they deny is that separate bads *add*. Closed individualism is therefore common ground, and the premise that actually refuses the inference is an aggregation principle. That principle appears in [P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3) ("so the moral weight is *additive*") and in the ethics page ("the Map adopts the Duplication View on independent grounds"). It does not appear in [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1). As written, the article credits [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) with a step it does not take. The writer's move is coherent as a *refusal*: it restates the arithmetic and denies that the sum's lack of a bearer cancels it. It overstates only in what it attributes the refusal to. The guard in the driver's brief otherwise holds. The Map takes the additive side on framework grounds, "not on felt evidence". The tracking reading "remains a rival the Map does not adopt and has not refuted". No sentence reads the plateau as evidence for or against [P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3), and L76 says explicitly that "The felt plateau is no evidence against [P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3)". One residual issue: "is what the void predicts" (L76) makes the void *predict* a calibration value for a position. The void constrains only measurement of *feeling*, while [P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3)'s "none" covers every empirical measurement. "Fits" is the accurate verb.
- **Severity**: High. This is the critical guard named in the driver's brief.
- **Recommendation**: Item 1 of the priority list. **Out of scope here and not minted**: [P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3) itself carries the same gap ("under closed individualism each copy is a *numerically distinct subject*, so the moral weight is *additive*"), and its *Would shift if* list does not include anti-aggregationism (Taurek, or Lewis's no-sum view). A positions-evolve pass should add an aggregation premise to *Depends on* and an anti-aggregationist defeater to *Would shift if*. [phenomenal-value-realism](/topics/phenomenal-value-realism/) L181 ("your particular suffering is not interchangeable with a qualitatively identical suffering in someone else ... token-based") is the Map's own resource that leans toward Taurek, and that pass should take it into account.

### Issue 2: A debiasing result is cited without its failed replication, and giving is read as feeling

- **File**: [numbing-void](/voids/numbing-void/)
- **Location**: L52, "The limit also resists knowledge. As Maier et al. report the original, Small et al. (2007) found that teaching participants about the identifiable-victim effect equalised giving ... Knowing about the limit levelled feeling down and did not carry it out to the many."
- **Problem**: The Maier et al. abstract (Crossref, checked against the Warwick full text) says: "We found no empirical support for the identifiable victim effect ... and subsequently no support for debiasing such a phenomenon". The article reports the original's debiasing pattern as a finding and draws a general conclusion from it ("Knowing about the limit ..."), one paragraph after citing the replication that did not reproduce it. Maier et al.'s description of the original matches the paraphrase: "the effect was diminished not because participants gave more to the statistical victims, but because they gave less to an identified victim". Small et al. measured *giving*, however, and L40 and L50 tell the reader that helping and concern come apart (Butts: "significant effects were not found for empathetic concern"). "Levelled feeling down" therefore breaks the article's own distinction. Three companion fixes on the same evidence base: L48, where "proportion carried the feeling, the absolute number did not" goes beyond Slovic, whose sentence is that "the proportion of lives saved often carries more weight than the number of lives saved"; L80, where "it leaves no felt mark" reads an awareness claim into timing data; and L100, where "finds a modest instance" contradicts the paragraph's own hedge.
- **Severity**: High for L52. Low-medium for the companions.
- **Recommendation**: Item 2.

### Issue 3: "One-subject-sized" equivocates, and "exists for calculation" keeps residue of the dropped line

- **File**: [numbing-void](/voids/numbing-void/)
- **Location**: L38, "Felt magnitude cannot decide between them, since a mind's only first-person measure of others' suffering is felt, and felt measures are one-subject-sized." L70, "runs through feeling, which is subject-sized". L64, "The aggregate exists for calculation."
- **Problem**: The epistemic/metaphysical equivocation check fires, though only mildly, because the article's conclusion is itself epistemic ("cannot decide"). "One-subject-sized" has two readings. (a) Every feeling is one subject's feeling. That is true but does not show that feeling cannot scale: one subject's concern could in principle track N. (b) Feeling has a single-subject *range*. That is an empirical capacity ceiling. The regulation face weakens it, since Cameron and Payne attribute the "collapse" to motivated regulation and explicitly reject the view that "emotions are not triggered by aggregates". The article's own "Grant perfect affect" sets it aside. The argument that actually works is about calibration: whatever feeling does, plateau or linear scaling, there is no felt total anywhere to check it against, so feeling cannot certify whether the additive figure measures anything felt. That is the structural face doing epistemic work, which is what the void needs. L64, "The aggregate exists for calculation", in the article's own voice, is residue of the dropped "calculation with no subject" line. Read metaphysically, it asserts the tracking reading ("the additive figure is an artefact of calculation", L68), which the Map does not adopt. Read epistemically ("reached only by calculation"), it is right.
- **Severity**: Medium. The conclusion survives, but the stated reason is the wrong one.
- **Recommendation**: Item 3. Item 3-opt also narrows the void to the part of the record that both readings genuinely leave open (see Counterarguments).

## Counterarguments to Address

### "Closed individualism is the anti-aggregationist's premise"
- **Current content says**: closed individualism refuses Lewis's inference.
- **A critic would argue**: the separateness of persons is the standard *anti*-aggregative premise. Taurek's argument and Lewis's toothache both start from separate sufferers.
- **Suggested response**: attribute the refusal to [P-AC3](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac3)'s counting rule, not to [P-I1](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i1) (item 1).

### "Parts of the record are errors on both readings"
- **Current content says**: "Unit-sensitivity fits both readings ... A mind cannot settle the matter from inside."
- **A critic would argue**: not everything in the magnitude face is undecidable. Proportion dominance (Slovic's reading of Fetherstonhaugh: "saving 9,000 lives in the “smaller” population as more valuable than saving ten times as many lives in the largest") is endorsed by neither Lewis nor Taurek. No non-aggregative view prefers fewer lives because they form a larger fraction of some population. A mind *can* see that much is error from inside, by consistency reasoning. The void is confined to the flat plateau.
- **Suggested response**: item 3-opt. This sharpens the void and does not weaken it.

### "Feeling-about need not be undergoing"
- **Current content says**: a felt grasp of the sum would need a subject who undergoes the sum.
- **A critic would argue**: empathy never requires undergoing, even for one.
- **Suggested response**: make the structural face do calibration work, with no felt total to check feeling against, and not capacity work (item 3).

### "The Map's anti-MWI moral argument is aggregative"
- **Current content says**: arguments about branch-suffering "must run through the indexical-identity premise, where the Map's additive verdict already rests".
- **A critic would argue**: the indexical premise and the additive verdict are distinct, and the ethics page says so.
- **Suggested response**: "partly rests" (item 1c).
