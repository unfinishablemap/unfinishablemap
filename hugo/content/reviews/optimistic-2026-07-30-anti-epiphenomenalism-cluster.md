## Executive Summary

This cluster contains the single best-calibrated concession in the corpus: [arguments/epiphenomenalism-argument.md](/arguments/epiphenomenalism-argument/) argues four ways against epiphenomenalism and then states, in its own summary table, that the sophisticated phenomenal-concept version *survives* — the argument "decisively burdens bare-correlation epiphenomenalism" only. Paired with that, [concepts/epiphenomenalism.md](/concepts/epiphenomenalism/) builds a three-way distinction (causally coupled / report-grounded / inherited-discourse) whose entire purpose is to show that self-stultification establishes an *existential* claim ("some consciousness is report-grounded") where Tenet 3 asserts a *universal* one, and then says so in the Assessment: the tenet "outruns the available epistemic arguments."

That scoped verdict is stated in **four live articles, including the tenets page itself**, where it is hard-coded into the tenet-dependency matrix as three separate rows. The positions register contains **no first-order entry for any of it**. Worse, two registered positions already *consume* it: `P-AC1` imports the conclusion mid-paragraph and keys a shift condition to it, and `P-D2` outsources its entire selection step to "a separately-supported argument (the self-stultification objection to epiphenomenalism and the evidence for mental causation)" — an argument the register never assesses, and whose scope the corpus has since narrowed twice. ## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers is handled with unusual fairness here — and quoted *against* the Map's own convenience. `the-epiphenomenalist-threat.md` L103 gives him the microphone: "epiphenomenalism may be counterintuitive, but it is not obviously false, so if a sound argument forces it on us, we should accept it," and then draws the correct conclusion — naturalistic dualism is "coherent, but conceding the threat rather than defeating it." The article does not pretend Chalmers has been refuted; it locates him at a structural distance from the well's centre and explains what commitment would move him. Chalmers would recognise the *Degrees of Proximity* taxonomy (centre / one step / two steps / three steps) as an honest map of the dialectic rather than a scoreboard, and he would note that the article grants his central point — that the explanatory gap is real and that ignorance of mechanism is not evidence of non-existence (`epiphenomenalism.md` L84).

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp gets the thing he most wants: a *specific* location for the causal contribution, plus an honest account of what it costs. `the-epiphenomenalist-threat.md` L133–135 states the escape precisely — physics is incomplete at quantum indeterminacies "not merely epistemically (we don't know the cause) but ontologically (there is no sufficient physical cause)" — and then explains why that defeats Kim rather than merely resisting him: "Kim's exclusion argument does not apply where physics is incomplete — there is no physical cause to exclude the mental one." That is the structural point, not a hand-wave.

He would be more impressed by L139, where the article generates its own sharpest objection unprompted: if selection preserves Born statistics over any ensemble, "its influence leaves no statistical trace at the aggregate level — raising the question of whether single-event efficacy that washes out in aggregate is genuine causal work." The article names this `ensemble-level-epiphenomenalism`, calls it "arguably the sharpest open question the corridor reading faces," and routes it to the register as the Map's mechanism debt (P-Q3), "held open rather than resolved." A framework that invents the best objection to its own escape route and then logs it as a debt is doing physics-adjacent philosophy properly.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Nagel would single out `epiphenomenalism-argument.md` Argument 4, which does something the literature usually skips: it makes introspection's *causal* structure the issue rather than its reliability. "When you see a red apple, the apple causally contributes to your experience. But when you introspect your pain, the pain contributes nothing — the phenomenal object is causally disconnected from the report about it" (L129). The asymmetry is stated in one sentence and it is the whole objection. He would equally appreciate `epiphenomenalism.md` L94, where the inertness is followed through into intentionality: "a desire that does nothing cannot genuinely aim at restructuring the world, so its apparent directedness toward a not-yet-actual state would be illusory." That is a first-person structure being taken seriously as a constraint on metaphysics.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

`epiphenomenalism-argument.md` has a dedicated *Process Philosophy Perspective* section (L167–169) and it is exactly right about the diagnosis: "Concrescence (the occasion's self-determination) *is* causation; there is no gap between experience and causal efficacy for epiphenomenalism to exploit. The epiphenomenalist's picture ... assumes the Cartesian split that process philosophy rejects." Whitehead would be pleased to be read as offering a *structural* dissolution rather than a competing hypothesis.

**And — critically for this skill's calibration discipline — the article firewalls that resonance out of its own count.** The Cumulative Case table (L135–140) lists exactly four arguments; the Whitehead section sits *after* the table and contributes nothing to it. Process-philosophical compatibility is presented as a framework in which the problem does not arise, never as a fifth converging line of support. This is the honest pattern: Whitehead-friendliness is praised on the compatibility axis and kept off the evidence axis. See the Hardline Empiricist below, who is praising the same restraint from the other side.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane would head straight for `the-epiphenomenalist-threat.md` L157, which states the stake plainly: "If deliberation and choice cause nothing, they cannot ground freedom or responsibility. The phenomenology of volitional control — agency, veto, selection — would be entirely illusory." He would then note approvingly that the article refuses to convert that into an argument: "These consequences do not refute epiphenomenalism — a theory is not false because its implications are unwelcome" (L162). Naming the stakes and then declining to use them as premises is the discipline that separates Kane's project from wishful thinking.

He would also note that `epiphenomenalism.md` L177 supplies the piece bare indeterminism cannot: agent causation, with the luck objection stated as the reason it is needed — "without genuine agent causation, indeterministic choices would be arbitrary; with it, they are authored."

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

Two things earn McGinn's approval. First, `epiphenomenalism-argument.md` carries a *What Would Challenge This View?* section (L185–193) listing three concrete defeaters, including one that would gut the flagship argument: "If neuroscience provided a complete account of consciousness reports without invoking consciousness itself, the self-stultification argument would lose force." Second, and better, `epiphenomenalism.md` L201 closes the whole article by conceding the limit rather than declaring victory: the Map "holds that causal coupling is intrinsic to consciousness wherever it occurs, **while acknowledging this as a metaphysical commitment that outruns the available epistemic arguments**." That is a framework marking the boundary of its own warrant in its final sentence, which is McGinn's preferred register — and it is precisely the sentence the register has never absorbed.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This persona's verdict is load-bearing here, and the cluster earns it in three separate places.

**1. The scope concession is real, unforced, and against interest.** `epiphenomenalism-argument.md` L75 concedes that the phenomenal-concept strategy (Frankish 2016, Papineau 2002, Loar 1990) "handles the objection more directly," that "the self-undermining charge dissolves," and that the disagreement therefore "*relocates rather than ends*." The summary table (L137) then records the narrowed verdict rather than the headline one, and L142 states outright that the Map's commitment "is held on that calibrated basis, not on a claim that the argument refutes every form of epiphenomenalism from inside." An article titled *The Cumulative Case Against Epiphenomenalism* telling the reader in its own summary table that the sophisticated version survives is the register discipline working under maximum temptation.

**2. Tenet-as-evidence-upgrade is praise-worthily *not* done — twice, explicitly.** The structural opportunity was wide open: self-stultification proves *some* consciousness is efficacious, Tenet 3 asserts *all* consciousness is coupled, and the cheap move is to let the tenet's coherence quietly upgrade the existential result to a universal one. The cluster declines it in writing. `epiphenomenalism.md` L128 and L130: "The self-stultification argument alone cannot establish universal causal efficacy ... Bidirectional Interaction asserts that all consciousness is causally coupled; self-stultification establishes only that some consciousness is report-grounded. The first is a metaphysical commitment about the nature of consciousness; the second is what we can prove case by case." [apex/machine-question.md](/apex/machine-question/) L175 repeats it: the universal assertion rests "on the quantum-interaction mechanism and the evolutionary evidence, not something self-stultification alone delivers." This is *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating*, named at the level of the tenet itself.

**3. Precedent is refused as licence, locally and correctly.** `epiphenomenalism-argument.md` L179 declines to convert a rebuttal-of-a-rebuttal into a result: Reimers and McKemmish "disputed Hagan's parameter choices, so this exchange remains live rather than settled; the Map's case here is that the original Tegmark figure cannot be cited as a closed rebuttal, **not that Hagan closes the question in the other direction**." L181 does the same for quantum biology: avian magnetoreception leaves "the door open ... though cryptochrome's specific molecular architecture does not by itself license neural-scale coherence." That is P-Q8's precedent-not-licence discipline applied by an article that never cites P-Q8 — arrived at independently, which is evidence the discipline is genuinely held rather than pasted.

Birch's one reservation is not a calibration failure but a *bookkeeping* one, and it is the finding below: every one of these restraints is currently invisible to a register audit.

## The Finding: the argument P-D2 leans on is registered nowhere

### What is stated, and where

The Map's considered verdict on epiphenomenalism has two scoping clauses, both arrived at deliberately and both stated repeatedly:

1. **Self-stultification burdens *bare-correlation* epiphenomenalism only.** The phenomenal-concept version survives by relocating the dispute to whether the first-person mode of presentation can be exhaustively physically characterised. (`epiphenomenalism-argument.md` L75, L137, L142)
2. **Self-stultification establishes an existential claim where Tenet 3 asserts a universal one.** It shows *some* consciousness is report-grounded; Tenet 3 asserts *all* consciousness is causally coupled. The gap is closed by the quantum-interaction mechanism and the evolutionary evidence, not by the argument. (`epiphenomenalism.md` L100, L114, L128, L130, L201)

Clause 2 appears in **four live articles**: [concepts/epiphenomenalism.md](/concepts/epiphenomenalism/), [apex/machine-question.md](/apex/machine-question/) (L175), [apex/open-question-ai-consciousness.md](/apex/open-question-ai-consciousness/) (L91), and [tenets/tenets.md](/tenets/) (L157, L166) — where it is not merely stated but *structurally encoded*, splitting the machine-consciousness cluster into bare-phenomenality / report-grounded / bidirectionally-coupled rows with different tenet burdens, and calling the previous conflation "the error the split corrects."

### What the register contains

Nothing first-order. `grep -rn 'stultif' obsidian/positions/` returns exactly **two** hits, both of them incidental mentions inside the `Asserts` paragraph of some *other* position:

- **`P-D2`** ([positions/arguments-for-dualism.md](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/) L55) makes the self-stultification argument the load-bearer for its entire selection step: Bidirectional Interaction "selects dualism from within that set, **on the strength of a separately-supported argument (the self-stultification objection to epiphenomenalism and the evidence for mental causation)** rather than a bare appeal to commitment." The register never assesses that argument, states its scope, or bands its confidence.
- **`P-AC1`** ([positions/ai-consciousness-scope.md](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/) L56) imports clause 2 as a parenthetical dependency — "That exclusion is conditional on the strong reading, **which is more than the self-stultification argument delivers**" — and then keys a formal shift condition to it: "or Tenet 3 were weakened from universal actual efficacy to a dispositional or existential reading, re-opening epiphenomenal AI experience internally."

So the register has a position whose shift condition depends on which reading of Tenet 3 the Map holds, and no position anywhere states which reading the Map holds or how confidently. An audit of P-AC1 cannot check its own dependency.

### The judgement call this raises for P-D2

State the reasoning either way rather than asserting a defect. P-D2's grounding clause cites "the self-stultification objection to epiphenomenalism and the evidence for mental causation" without distinguishing what each delivers. Two readings:

- **P-D2 is fine as written.** It needs Tenet 3 only as a *selector* among irreducibility-respecting rivals, and for that a separately-motivated commitment suffices; the clause names the sources of motivation, and the second conjunct ("the evidence for mental causation") is doing the work the first cannot. On this reading nothing moves and the new entries simply supply the detail P-D2 gestures at.
- **P-D2's clause is imprecise.** As phrased it lets a reader take the self-stultification objection to support the universal reading Tenet 3 asserts, which the corpus has explicitly denied in four places. A one-clause fix — naming the argument as supporting the existential claim, with the mechanism and evolutionary evidence carrying the universal one — would align P-D2 with `epiphenomenalism.md` L128 at no cost to its conclusion.

My reading is the second, but it is a precision fix inside a grounding clause, **not** a retraction and **not** a band move: P-D2's conclusion (the two earnings do not compound) is untouched either way, and its `credence high` is about a counting rule, not about epiphenomenalism. Whoever executes should say explicitly if they leave the calibration unchanged.
