## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would accept the whole formal apparatus and attack the antecedent. The article concedes that public data currently support "at most a nominal classification of phenomenal states, and only via correlation" — but *correlation is what identification looks like before the reduction matures*. Heat was "only correlated" with molecular motion until it wasn't. The article's cross-subject impossibility is explicitly conditional on first-person-only access, and the history of science is a graveyard of such antecedents. To its credit the article states the conditionality plainly and twice; what it does not state is that the conditional's *track record* favours the physicalist — every prior "in principle unobservable" attribute that mattered scientifically eventually got a proxy. The article should not concede this, but it should name it as the shape of the strongest objection.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett's attack lands on the ladder's lower rungs, and it is the sharpest critique available (developed as Issue 3): what satisfies weak-order or difference-structure axioms is a corpus of *judgments* — button presses, verbal comparisons. Heterophenomenology grants those judgments full authority as data *about what subjects believe about their experiences* and no authority about phenomenal magnitudes. The article itself makes exactly this move at the ratio rung — Narens's separation of representation from veridical numerals, "ratio-like structure without veridical numerals" — and then declines to iterate it downward: why do ordinal and interval judgments get taken as tracking phenomenal structure when ratio numerals do not? A principled answer exists (monotone distortion preserves order but not ratios), but the article does not give it, and without it the ladder's within-subject rungs rest on an unargued asymmetry of trust in introspective report.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark would enjoy this article against its own project. The meaningfulness discipline says an ordinal-only phenomenal variable "fixes no functional form at all," and the article's own survey concludes that the empirically earned structure is "at most ratio-like structure without veridical numerals" in restricted perceptual domains — for the pain-and-agency contrasts the interface programme actually cares about, nothing above ordinal is even tested. So by the article's own standards, the [psychophysical control law](/topics/the-psychophysical-control-law/) currently has no determinate mathematical form: the Map's Minimal Quantum Interaction tenet owes a coupling law whose phenomenal side the Map's own measurement theory says is mathematically underdetermined. The article gestures at this ("the interface programme's progress conditions should say which strength they need") but does not state the uncomfortable corollary: on present evidence the answer to "which strength do you need?" is *more than we have earned*. Honest, but it should be said in the article, not just derivable from it.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch would note a methodological asymmetry. The decision-theoretic derivation of the Born rule (Deutsch 1999; Wallace 2012) is precisely a representation-and-uniqueness argument: preferences satisfying axioms are representable by probabilities, uniquely. The Map embraces axiomatic representation theorems when they *constrain* phenomenal measurement and rejects the same argument-form's most celebrated recent application when it *supports* Everettian rationality. There is a principled reply — the Born-rule derivation's axioms are themselves contested, and the Map's MWI rejection runs through indexical identity, not through doubting representation theorems — but the article's Relation to Site Perspective, which happily inherits the Luce constraint for two tenets, is silent on why the No Many Worlds tenet is immune to the same formal machinery. A one-clause acknowledgment would close the flank.

### The Empiricist

The Popperian verdict is mixed and mostly favourable: the meaningfulness discipline is genuinely testable methodology — declare the scale type, exhibit the axioms, run the difference-scaling and conjoint designs — and the article correctly bills it as the constraint [judging-the-map-as-science](/apex/judging-the-map-as-science/) owed. Two residues. First, the fallback "holistic" form of the unit argument (the unity of the experiential field) is not testable as stated; the article moves the unit argument off the ground Luce and Tukey took away and onto ground no experiment reaches — an improvement in honesty, not in falsifiability, and it should be labelled as such. Second, the premise "no spanning relation is observable even in principle" is itself unfalsifiable: no observation could refute an in-principle-unobservability claim. The article correctly derives rather than asserts the impossibility, but the Popperian will insist the derivation's engine is metaphysics doing methodological work, and the article's phrase "here the neutral mathematics ends" concedes exactly this — the concession could be one shade more explicit.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna's objection is the deepest unaddressed one: the within/across asymmetry presupposes that a *single subject over time* is a unified measurement domain for free. Every within-subject comparison the article's ladder rests on is diachronic — this pain now against that pain remembered — and intensity memory is a proxy with its own distortions. If the "subject" is a construction over momentary experiences, then comparing my pain now with my pain yesterday is structurally the same problem as comparing my pain with yours, and the article's sharp fifth-rung discontinuity dissolves into a continuum of calibration failures. A Parfitian reductionist reaches the same conclusion without the emptiness metaphysics. The article never argues that diachronic within-subject calibration is better off than interpersonal calibration; it assumes it. This is a real gap, not a persona flourish — see Counterarguments.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Cross-subject rung engages the weakest naturalist rival
- **File**: concepts/scale-types-for-phenomenal-quantities.md
- **Location**: "The Cross-Subject Rung" — "A physicalist who identifies phenomenal magnitudes with neural magnitudes gets a spanning relation for free"
- **Problem**: The only rival considered at the decisive rung is identity physicalism, which the article correctly notes assumes what the explanatory gap contests. But the standard naturalist route to interpersonal comparison is weaker and harder to dismiss: *proxy-based spanning relations over behavior* — common-stimulus anchoring, cross-subject matching tasks, discrimination-based scaling that counts just-noticeable differences (the Fechnerian tradition; in its modern form, Dzhafarov's Fechnerian scaling). RTM never requires direct observation of the measured attribute — temperature is measured via mercury columns — so "no observable relation connects two subjects' experiences" invites the reply that no observable relation connects anyone to temperature either, and proxies served. The Map has the answer in its corpus (the proxy discipline: behavioral structure measures behavior; promoting it to phenomenal structure needs exactly the bridging assumption in dispute), but the article does not deploy it at the rung where the objection bites.
- **Severity**: High (it is the article's central claim, and the strongest objection to it is unanswered on the page)
- **Recommendation**: One paragraph at the fifth rung: state the proxy/JND route, then apply the article's own nominal-rung observation — behavioral spanning structures earn a representation of *discrimination behavior* across subjects; reading it as a representation of phenomenal magnitude is the bridging assumption, not a theorem. This converts the objection into a restatement of the article's thesis.

### Issue 2: "Meaningful under no structure" — epistemic/metaphysical equivocation risk
- **File**: concepts/scale-types-for-phenomenal-quantities.md
- **Location**: "The Meaningfulness Discipline" — "'A suffers more than B,' across subjects, is meaningful under no structure the axioms deliver — which is why cross-subject welfare aggregation inherits the opacity that [moral-census-opacity](/concepts/moral-census-opacity/) describes"
- **Problem**: RTM "meaningfulness" is technical — invariance under admissible transformations of a representation. But the sentence reads naturally as semantic: that "A suffers more than B" lacks a truth value. The Map is realist about phenomenal states; on its own commitments there presumably *is* a fact about whether A suffers more than B — the claim it needs is that no representation the axioms license can decide it (epistemic), not that the comparison is contentless (metaphysical). The distinction is not pedantic: [moral-census-opacity](/concepts/moral-census-opacity/)'s moral force *presupposes* a hidden fact (opacity is about facts we cannot read), so citing it in the same breath as technical meaninglessness creates the exact equivocation the skill's evidential-status check exists to catch. The article is careful everywhere else ("consistent with irreducibility without upgrading it"), so this is a local phrasing repair, not a structural failure — but it sits at the article's most quotable sentence.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Split the readings in place: the comparison may be true or false, but its truth value is invariant under no admissible pair of representations — empirically undecidable, not contentless. One sentence.

### Issue 3: Judgment-structure promoted to phenomenal-structure on the lower rungs
- **File**: concepts/scale-types-for-phenomenal-quantities.md
- **Location**: "Ordinal, within-subject" ("Pairwise introspective comparisons... plausibly satisfy weak-order axioms") and "Interval, within-subject" (the Ho/Landy/Maloney sentence)
- **Problem**: Two related gaps. (a) What the cited experiments axiom-test is an empirical structure of *comparison responses about stimuli* — Ho, Landy, and Maloney's observers judged gloss and bumpiness of *surfaces*; the additive representation is of stimulus-judgment structure, and MLCM-style work is model-fitting rather than direct cancellation-axiom testing (Trendler 2019 presses exactly this against the conjoint programme, and the article cites Trendler without noting that the objection also deflates its own "partial existence proof"). Calling it an existence proof that "the axioms are checkable in practice" is fair for psychophysics and over-strong for *phenomenal* quantities. (b) The article's own ratio-rung lesson — representation without veridical numerals — is not iterated downward: if numerals can distort, orderings can too, and the article gives no reason for trusting introspective order-judgments while distrusting introspective ratio-judgments. There is a good reason (order survives any monotone response distortion; ratios do not), and stating it would *strengthen* the ladder considerably.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Add the monotone-distortion rationale for the asymmetric trust, and re-frame Ho et al. as an existence proof for axiom-testing *of perceptual judgment structures*, flagging the judgment-to-phenomenal step as the same bridging inference the article polices elsewhere.

### Issue 4: Diachronic calibration assumed free
- **File**: concepts/scale-types-for-phenomenal-quantities.md
- **Location**: The within/across-subject architecture throughout
- **Problem**: The fifth rung's discontinuity ("nothing in any axiom on a single subject's structure constrains the alignment between subjects") applies with only slightly less force *within* a subject across time: comparisons of present with remembered experiences route through memory, a proxy whose fidelity no within-occasion axiom certifies. The Parfitian/Buddhist objection (personae above) has a measurement-theoretic form the article does not consider: the "single subject's empirical relational structure" is itself an idealisation gluing together occasion-indexed structures with unearned diachronic calibration.
- **Severity**: Medium (it does not break the argument — the asymmetry survives, since within-subject comparisons are at least *made*, whereas cross-subject ones are not — but the article should say why the asymmetry survives)
- **Recommendation**: One or two sentences acknowledging the diachronic proxy and stating the surviving asymmetry: within-subject comparison judgments exist as data; cross-subject phenomenal comparisons have no data-generating act at all.

### Issue 5: Reference hygiene — two dangling entries
- **File**: concepts/scale-types-for-phenomenal-quantities.md

- **Problem**: Neither is cited inline. Stevens 1946 is defensible (the prose name-checks "Stevens's famous scale types"; the entry anchors it). Browning & Veit 2020 is never mentioned in body prose at all — a reader cannot tell what work it did. (The research note used it; the article inherited the entry without the citation.)
- **Severity**: Low
- **Recommendation**: Either cite Browning & Veit where the cross-subject/welfare material appears (their "measurement problem of consciousness" is directly on point and already cited by [moral-census-opacity](/concepts/moral-census-opacity/)'s cluster) or move the entry to Further Reading.

### Issue 6: Two SEP quotes doing slightly relocated work
- **File**: concepts/scale-types-for-phenomenal-quantities.md
- **Location**: "What the Representational Theory Says" and "From the Ferguson Committee..."
- **Problem**: Both quotes are verbatim-verified; the framing shifts are small but real. (a) SEP's "scale types are individuated by the families of transformations..." appears in Tal's exposition of *Stevens's own* classification ("As Stevens notes..."), while the article deploys it as the uniqueness-theorem gloss ("Stevens's famous scale types just *are* the resulting invariance classes"). The claim is standard RTM doctrine, but the quote is being used to certify a framing its source sentence does not carry. (b) "The physicists' verdict" compresses the Ferguson committee: the 1940 final report was a divided committee's output, and SEP frames the quoted sentence as the position of "those objecting to the measurability of sensation, such as Campbell" — the objectors' reasoning, not a committee verdict. The article's own apposition ("in Tal's summary of Norman Campbell's position") is accurate; "verdict" is the word too many.
- **Severity**: Low

## Counterarguments to Address

### Proxy measurement (the temperature analogy)
- **Current content says**: No observable relation spans two subjects' experiences, so no cross-subject representation theorem can be stated.
- **A critic would argue**: No observable relation spans anyone and temperature either; measurement always routes through proxies, and behavioral proxies (matching, discrimination counts) span subjects trivially.
- **Suggested response**: Already latent in the article's nominal rung — proxies deliver a representation of the proxy structure; promoting it to the target attribute needs a bridge, and for phenomenal attributes the bridge is the contested identity itself. State it at the fifth rung. (Issue 1.)

### Diachronic reductionism
- **Current content says**: Within-subject structure is empirically testable; cross-subject structure does not exist.
- **A critic would argue**: The within-subject domain is glued together by memory-mediated comparisons whose calibration is exactly as unearned as interpersonal alignment; the discontinuity is a continuum.
- **Suggested response**: Concede the proxy status of diachronic comparison; retain the asymmetry on the ground that within-subject comparisons are performed acts producing data, while cross-subject phenomenal comparisons have no performing act. (Issue 4.)

### The Born-rule symmetry
- **Current content says** (Relation to Site Perspective): Two tenets inherit the Luce constraint; RTM is ontologically silent.
- **A critic would argue**: The same axiomatic-representation methodology yields the Deutsch-Wallace Born-rule theorem; accepting it here while holding the No Many Worlds tenet is selective.
- **Suggested response**: The Map's MWI rejection disputes the decision-theoretic axioms' premises (indexical identity), not the representation-theorem method; a clause suffices.
