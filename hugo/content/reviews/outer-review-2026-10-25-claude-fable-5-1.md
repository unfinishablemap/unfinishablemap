## Reply

# Referee Report: “Consciousness and Agency” (apex/consciousness-and-agency, last modified 2026-10-16)

The article does not establish what its abstract claims. By its own Evidence and Dependency section and by its Born-rule sibling apex, agent-causal quantum selection “grounding free will and moral responsibility” is a framework-internal coherence result with no tenet-independent anchor for any agency-specific claim. The apex must be demoted to coherence-only and hard-revised before it can stand as a flagship synthesis.

### 2.1 Question 1 — Do the luck and exclusion sections survive?

#### Luck

Under **Meeting the Luck Objection**, the apex offers three features: “First, selection is *reasons-guided* … Reasons do not *determine* the outcome but they *guide* it”. Second, “selection carries the phenomenology of effort”. Third, “selection has neural signatures of its own”. It concludes: “Together these distinguish selection from reception, but they say *who* settles the outcome, not why it went one way.” [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

1. **None of the features separates agent-causal settling from event-causal or compatibilist agency.** A Kane self-forming action is reasons-guided on both branches and effortful by construction, and it has whatever neural signature willed choice has. So does a deterministic reasons-responsive mechanism. The claim that the features show “who settles” therefore goes unearned. That would need a feature present when an agent-substance settles and absent when agent-involving events do, and under Born preservation (2.3) no such feature could be observed.
2. **Mele’s present luck reapplies to agent causation.** Roll back with the agent-cause in place: identical powers, reasons and effort, yet A in some replays and B in others. Mele (*Free Will and Luck*, 2006), Haji, and Levy (*Hard Luck*, 2011) press exactly this point. Levy argues that present luck leaves libertarians, agent-causal ones included, worse off than compatibilists. The apex’s answer is that “the settling is the agent’s and nothing more basic accounts for it—a primitive whose modal structure is owed rather than supplied”. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/) That relabels the indeterminism; it does not answer the contrastive demand. The concession “not why it went one way” is followed by a section header, abstract and moral section that behave as though the objection had been met. This is inoculation-by-confession.
3. **Clarke is absent.** Clarke’s *Libertarian Accounts of Free Will* (OUP 2003) is the canonical attempt to show that agent causation adds control beyond event-causal indeterminism. In the same book Clarke defends an integrated agent-causal view “but argues that we should find the substance causation required by any agent-causal account to be impossible”, and concludes that under broad incompatibilism “no libertarian account is entirely adequate” (per the OUP/Google Books summary). The apex’s central claim, that agent causation supplies what event causation cannot, is made without its best defender or that defender’s retreat.
4. **Pereboom is reported accurately and then evaded.** Under **The Authorship Problem** the apex correctly says Pereboom’s case is that agent-caused choices “would have to match the statistics physics records by *wild coincidence*”. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/) Pereboom writes (2001, p. 85) that this “involves wild coincidences that make it incredible”, and (2014, p. 67) that “this conformity would be a wild coincidence, which we would not expect and would have no explanation”. [springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-023-04117-2)[nd](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/living-without-free-will/) Both page references come via published secondary sources and were not checked against the books. The apex then “carries the second as a cost”. But Tenet 2’s Born-preserving default *requires* exact frequency matching; the apex itself says “the Map’s Born-preserving design sits on it”. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/) A defeater written into the design cannot be overhead. This is calibration asymmetry.
5. **Kane is suppressed.** Under **Agent Causation: The Agent as Source**: “The seam shows in the tradition’s leading figure: Kane (2024) now rejects the event-causal *label* … yet still declines to posit an agent-substance cause.” [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/) Kane did more than drop a label. David Palmer’s NDPR review (2025) reports that “Kane denies that his view is an event-causal one. Instead, he argues … that it is both event-causal *and* agent-causal”. It also reports that Kane holds the causation of free action “must bring in reference to agents, qua substances, understood as complex dynamical systems, exercising teleological guidance control” (p. 84). A PhilArchive review records that “On the AC/EC account that emerges, Kane thinks there is no longer any worry about the ‘disappearance’ of the agent”. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/CYRTCT) Whether it works is disputed. The *Philosophical Review* notice (135(2):183–192, April 2026) calls the hybrid a “rather surprising revelation” and says “It is unclear to me … how AC is supposed to eliminate these concerns”. Either way it is a rival answer, not a seam. **Relation to Site Perspective** repeats the unscoped “answering the disappearing-agent objection event causation cannot”. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/) This is tenet leakage the site already corrected elsewhere on 2026-10-20.
6. **The akrasia concession is fatal to feature one.** The apex says “The first feature also fails to reach akrasia … and the Map leaves that case open”. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/) Yet akratic acts are paradigm objects of blame, and the moral section later asserts retributive desert without restriction.
7. **The neural signatures are a category error.** Rajan, Thura & Cisek and Desmurget are signatures of *physical* selection, so they cannot bear on whether a *non-physical* agent selected. [cell +2](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(14)00062-2) The apex concedes “a physicalist decision-load account predicts them too”. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/) They should leave the luck section.

**Rival libertarians.**

- Balaguer’s non-agent-causal libertarianism is absent from the apex.
- Steward’s agency incompatibilism is absent. It defends sui generis animal “settling” without substance dualism.
- O’Connor is uncited, and his agent causation is an *emergent* organismic power, which the site’s own causal-closure page acknowledges (“Timothy O’Connor and Hong Yu Wong argue that emergent properties possess ‘fundamentally novel’ causal powers”). [concepts/causal-closure](/concepts/causal-closure/)
- Tse’s criterial causation, a physicalist account of non-random self-shaping choice, is absent, though it fills the “fourth place” the trilemma paragraph admits exists.

Steward and O’Connor refute the Relation-to-Site claim that “agent causation requires consciousness to be ontologically distinct from physics—otherwise it inherits determinism or random indeterminism”. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

**Compatibilists.** The apex says: “Fischer’s account explains why an action is appropriate given the agent’s reasons; it does not explain why those reasons are *the agent’s* if the responsive mechanism is itself the product of causes the agent never chose.” [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/) This is a straw man in two ways.

- Guidance control has a second condition, mechanism ownership (“taking responsibility”), designed to say exactly when the mechanism is the agent’s own. The apex omits it.
- The charge applies equally to the Map. The agent-substance’s powers and character were not self-chosen either. This is Galen Strawson’s Basic Argument, which the apex never engages.

Dennett and Wolf are named but not engaged. Vihvelin’s new dispositionalism is not engaged in the apex. Frankfurt cases are the direct challenge to the “wide” (leeway-retaining) half of the Map’s source incompatibilism, and they get no reply.

#### Exclusion

Under **Mental Causation and the Exclusion Problem**: “Where the closure premise fails locally there is no second sufficient cause to compete with, so the overdetermination worry dissolves rather than needs answering.” [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

1. **Probabilistic exclusion goes unanswered.** If physics supplies a complete chance distribution and selection preserves it exactly, then on any probabilistic or interventionist account the mental factor makes no difference to any probability. Kim’s exclusion returns in probabilistic form. The apex notes “Born-preserving selection leaves probabilistic closure intact” but never draws the consequence. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)
2. **Interventionism is left to the sibling apex.** The Born apex states the requirement: “If conscious selection has interventionist efficacy, then for some context X and intention C, P(O | do(C), X) ≠ q(O | X)”. It then proves a trilemma. Either the conditionals differ (empirical exposure), or they never differ (“the epiphenomenalism horn in its exact form”), or they cancel under a balancing law “the framework would have to state and motivate”. [apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/) The agency apex uses none of this. Woodward, List & Menzies’ proportionality and Baumgartner’s argument that interventionism cannot rescue supervenient causation are all absent. The apex declares instead that the Anscombe dispute “bottoms out in whether content, *as content*, can be causally relevant under supervenience”, which is exactly the question that literature addresses. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)
3. **Papineau is unanswered.** His completeness argument is inductive: no anomalous forces have been found in physiology. The Born-preserving default concedes that inductive base at every aggregate grain, so denying closure “at quantum indeterminacies” leaves no trace that Papineau’s induction could miss.
4. **The “two independent responses” are not independent.** Saad’s preemption (“experiences preempt rather than overdetermine the default causal profile of brain states”) is the machinery of the Born apex’s Route 1 for the quantum response itself. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)[apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/) The Born apex records costs that this apex omits. Under trumping, “‘consciousness is a cause’ and ‘consciousness is epiphenomenal’ make no predictively distinct claims at any scale”. And “Preemption is symmetric: a physicalist can run the mirror move”. [apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/) Saad’s own abstract says experiences “uphold causal responsibilities ‘delegated’ to them by physical states”. [nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12062107/) Delegated profiles supply neither leeway nor sourcehood, and they are compatible with determinism. This is an evidential-independence failure and, for agency, a co-optation.
5. **The conservation paragraph misuses its source.** The apex argues that Born-preserving selection “leaves the ensemble energy expectation intact … tested at the ensemble level, not trial by trial”. It then cites Cucu & Pitts (2019) only “dialectically”. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/) Their paper is about how dualists should *not* respond. It favours the “conditionality response”, on which “energy is conserved when and where minds do not act on bodies, but is not conserved when and where minds act on bodies”. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1909.13643) And because the Map’s thesis is *token* causation (“fails at the token level”), the token energy accounting is the one that matters. Appealing to the ensemble changes the subject. Only the energy-degenerate clause actually answers the objection.

**Verdict on Q1:** neither section survives the strongest replies.
