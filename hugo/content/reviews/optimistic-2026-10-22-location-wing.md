## Expansion Opportunities

### High Priority

#### Consciousness and the epistemic condition on moral responsibility
- **Builds on**: `alief`'s noticing condition (L109–L113: implicit influence "not introspectively known by the actor"), `concepts/moral-responsibility`, `topics/responsibility-gradient-from-attentional-capacity` (attentional capacity grades desert; its "negligent depletion" case at L108 is a tracing case) and `topics/reactive-attitudes-and-strawsonian-responsibility`.
- **Would address**: Whether an agent is responsible for what unnoticed attitudes do. Neil Levy's consciousness thesis (*Consciousness and Moral Responsibility*, OUP 2014; Crossref chapters include "The Consciousness Thesis", pp. 14–37, and "The Global Workspace", pp. 38–69) holds that responsibility requires awareness of the facts that give an action its moral significance. Attributionists deny this: Sher (*Who Knew? Responsibility Without Awareness*, OUP 2009) and Smith ("Responsibility for Attitudes", *Ethics* 115(2), 2005, 236–271). Implicit bias is the standard test case, and `alief` has already sorted the states involved.
- **Estimated scope**: Long. Research first; read Levy's argument at source.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 3, at the level of compatibility. A selector model suggests a consciousness condition on responsibility, but Levy argues for one from a global-workspace account that a physicalist can accept. The page must say "tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating" and must not cast Levy as an ally of dualism.

#### Emotion regulation and where control enters
- **Builds on**: `self-control`'s hot/cool hinge case (L72) and situational strategies (L46, Duckworth, Gendler and Gross 2016); `alief`'s retraining of aliefs "under conditions of full disclosure" (L119); `voids/numbing-void`'s motivated down-regulation (Cameron and Payne 2011, L56, read there as "an illustration, not an instance", L100); `mental-effort`'s CBT reappraisal record (L106).
- **Would address**: Gross's process model ("The Emerging Field of Emotion Regulation", *Review of General Psychology* 2(3), 1998, 271–299) orders regulation strategies along the emotion's own time course: situation selection, modification, attentional deployment, cognitive change and response modulation. Gross and John (*JPSP* 85(2), 2003, 348–362) contrast reappraisal with suppression. This gives the self-control relocation a second, finer-grained domain. - **Estimated scope**: Medium–long.
