## Why This Wing

These four are not a topical cluster — they are a *methodological* one. Each article's
distinctive move is to **bound its own claim**: to say what the Map is committed to, what
it is not committed to, and which layer the commitment enters at.

- `where-the-substance-commitment-enters` localises the Map's substance-leaning to the
  agent-causal step and explicitly refuses to let the Dualism tenet carry it.
- `supervenience` concedes the empirical covariance in full and then denies the inference
  physicalists draw from it.
- `mind-arena` declares itself a posited framework construct and removes itself from the
  five-tier evidential scale rather than claiming a tier on it.
- `status-of-content` presents the strongest self-refutation argument against eliminativism
  and then declines to lean on it.

That shared discipline is the wing's strength, and it is also where the expansion room is:
every one of these articles names a boundary it does not then walk.

The meta-problem / illusionism cluster was excluded by the driver brief (saturated this
session, and every member is within ~350 words of its ceiling). `concepts/phenomenal-depth`
was set aside as belonging to a phenomenology wing rather than this one; it remains a good
target for a future run at 2186 words of headroom.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`supervenience` is the wing's clearest Chalmers-friendly article, and for the right reason:
it does not deny supervenience, it *deflates* it. The lead gets the whole thesis in one
sentence — **"Supervenience describes a pattern; it does not explain it."** — and the article
then supplies the missing explanandum as psychophysical law rather than as identity. The
disanalogy paragraph is the sharpest paragraph in the wing: temperature "is reductively
identified with mean molecular kinetic energy — once the identity is grasped, the explanatory
work is done", whereas "grasping every physical detail leaves the phenomenal character
unexplained." That is the *Conscious Mind* argument stated without borrowing its vocabulary.

Chalmers would also approve of the register discipline in `where-the-substance-commitment-enters`:
a property dualist can read almost the whole Map without conceding anything, and the article
says so in a table rather than making the reader find out.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

`supervenience`'s treatment of Kim's exclusion argument is the wing's best piece of
constructive physics-facing philosophy. It accepts Kim's diagnosis in full — non-reductive
physicalism really does collapse into reduction or epiphenomenalism — and then relocates the
faulty premise to causal closure rather than to mental causation. The formulation is precise:
"at those junctures, there is no sufficient physical cause to compete with." Stapp would
recognise this as the correct shape of the reply, and would note approvingly that the article
does not need the mental to *overpower* physics anywhere.

`mind-arena` then supplies the term that makes this cash out downstream, and does so without
inflating it: the arena's operative property is **"structural unmodellability from physical
state"**, which is a claim about what is derivable, not a claim about how much force
consciousness exerts.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`status-of-content` is the Nagel-friendly member, somewhat unexpectedly. Its subject is
content rather than phenomenal character, but its structural point is Nagelian: any
successor vocabulary "inherits the same semantic burden." The first-person is not argued for
directly; instead the article shows that the eliminativist's attempt to get rid of it cannot
state itself. Nagel would especially like the three-part account of *why* Boghossian's version
is the sophisticated one — in particular "the eliminativist is hoist with their own arguments
rather than confronted with a premise they reject."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

`mind-arena` avoids crude substance dualism by construction: it is defined *extensionally*,
by outcomes, not by an inventory of things. "A purely epiphenomenal mind would have a rich
subjective domain but an *empty* mind-arena" is the line that earns this — the arena is a
causal footprint, so the article never needs to posit a mental *stuff* to make its argument
work.

Whitehead would also find `where-the-substance-commitment-enters` congenial for declining
full Cartesian substance: "the relevant subject is *substance-leaning*, not necessarily full
Cartesian substance."

**Constraint honoured.** None of this praise is used below to justify a tier-upgrade. See
the Birch section — in this wing the two personas converge rather than conflict.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`where-the-substance-commitment-enters` does the job Kane's own position needs and rarely
gets: it explains *why* agent causation requires an ontological upgrade rather than treating
the upgrade as a stipulation. The argument is compact and correct — "If there is no thing that
does the causing, only properties co-occurring with neural events, then 'the agent originated
the action' loses its referent; what is left looks like event causation under another name."
Kane's own event-causal libertarianism would resist the conclusion, but he would concede that
the dilemma is put fairly.

The Chisholm immanent/transeunt framing is the right historical anchor, and the article
correctly presents it as a distinction rather than as an argument.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

Three of the four articles end by marking what they have not established. `status-of-content`
is the strongest case: it spends 1500 words building the best available self-refutation
argument and then says the Map's actual case "rests primarily on the substantive claim that
phenomenal evidence has an epistemic status incompatible with eliminativism ... not on a
formal-incoherence verdict." An argument the article rates highly is deliberately kept off
the load-bearing path. That is epistemic humility with a cost attached, which is the only
kind worth praising.

`mind-arena`'s two "honest caveats" do the same job, including the concession that the
construct "shares the empirical exposure of Tenet 2."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This wing is where the Birch persona has the least to complain about anywhere in the corpus,
and the reason is worth naming precisely.

**`mind-arena` performs the exact move the evidential-status discipline exists to protect.**
It had every structural opportunity to place itself on the five-tier scale — it is a Map-coined
term, it is central to the AI-risk argument, and tenet-coherence would have supplied an easy
argument for "realistic possibility." It declines: the arena "is not itself a claim placed on
the Map's five-tier evidential scale; **its credence tracks the tenets it presupposes rather
than any independent evidence**." That is *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating*, written
out in full, about the article's own central term. It then states the falsifier from the
physicalist's side — "for the physicalist every outcome is in principle physical-state-computable
and the arena collapses to the empty set" — which is how you tell a framework construct from a
discovered one.

**`supervenience` keeps the same registers apart on the modal axis.** The Map's position is
stated as nomological rather than metaphysical necessity, and the article says plainly that it
accepts the covariance as *empirical* — "neuroscience confirms extensive psychophysical
correlation" — while denying the inference. The parsimony paragraph is the best single
sentence in the wing on this: "determination-without-explanation is not ontological parsimony —
it is an unresolved mystery wearing a formal label."

**`where-the-substance-commitment-enters` refuses a tenet-load upgrade explicitly.** Its
closing move is that Tenet 5 blocks parsimony from settling the question — and then, critically,
it does not convert that defeater-removal into a positive reason: "The Map adopts the heavier
reading where agency requires it and declines to treat its weight as a strike against it — but
only where agency requires it. Elsewhere, the lighter reading and the heavier reach the same
place, and the Map asserts no preference." Removing a defeater without upgrading is the whole
of the discipline, and this is a clean instance of it.

Verdict: **no calibration concerns found in this wing.** The Process Philosopher and the
Hardline Empiricist praise the same passages here for different reasons, which per the skill's
own criterion means the articles have resolved the tension honestly. No `refine-draft` task is
minted on calibration grounds.

### 1. `supervenience` — add Horgan's superdupervenience: a materialist making the Map's argument

- **Article**: `obsidian/concepts/supervenience.md` (2000 words of headroom)
- **Insertion locus** (verbatim, verified unique on disk this run): insert a new `###`
  subsection immediately after the paragraph beginning
  *"Physicalists sometimes respond that other inter-level relations (temperature supervening
  on molecular motion) involve similar explanatory "gaps.""*
- **What to add**: Terence Horgan's argument that bare supervenience is explanatorily idle and
  that materialism owes *superdupervenience* — supervenience that is itself explainable in
  materialistically acceptable terms. The value is dialectical: this is a **materialist**
  conceding precisely the article's lead claim, which converts "the Map says supervenience
  doesn't explain" into "even materialism's own house standard says supervenience doesn't
  explain." Pair it with the article's existing nomological-necessity position: the Map can
  accept Horgan's demand and answer it with psychophysical laws, whereas the physicalist owes
  a materialistically acceptable explanation and has not supplied one.
- **Coverage check**: "superdupervenience" appears in **0 files** corpus-wide (`grep -rcF`
  across `obsidian/`). `obsidian/concepts/physical-completeness.md` and
  `obsidian/concepts/type-specificity.md` each use the phrase "explanatory demand" once but
  contain **0 occurrences of "superven"** — neither runs this argument. Not a duplicate.
- **Size**: ~350 words. Verify the Horgan citation at the publisher before it lands.

### 3. `supervenience` ↔ `where-the-substance-commitment-enters` — the missing reciprocal

- **Measured gap**: `supervenience.md` contains **0 occurrences of "agent"**.
  `where-the-substance-commitment-enters.md` contains **1** occurrence of "supervene" and
  **0** wikilinks to `[[supervenience]]`.
- **Why it matters**: these two articles are one question apart and neither asks it. The
  supervenience page's lead commits the Map to "no change in experience without some change in
  the brain"; the substance page's agent-causal reading has a volition with "no *event*-cause
  but does have an *agent*-cause". Does an agent-caused volition have a supervenience base?
  A reader who takes both pages seriously will ask, and the wing's own commitment-scoping
  ethic says the answer should be stated rather than left to inference. The likely answer is
  benign — supervenience constrains *which experiences are possible given* a physical state,
  and says nothing about what caused the physical state to be that way — but "likely benign
  and never stated" is exactly what this wing exists to eliminate.
- **Where**: a short `###` subsection in `supervenience.md` under "Supervenience and the
  Exclusion Argument", plus a piped wikilink `[[supervenience|supervene]]` in the substance
  article's existing sentence *"A property-only reading—on which mental properties supervene
  on a physical substrate without any substance bearing them—struggles to supply it."* — that
  second half is a **zero-word-cost** reciprocal.
- **Size**: ~250 words in `supervenience.md`; 0 words in the substance article.

### 4. `mind-arena` — work the boundary the article concedes is fuzzy

- **Article**: `obsidian/concepts/mind-arena.md` (2119 words of headroom)
- **Insertion locus** (verbatim, verified unique): the caveat beginning
  *"First, the boundary of the arena is not sharply operationalised"*. Follow it with a worked
  `###` subsection rather than leaving the concession bare.
- **What to add**: three or four concrete boundary cases decided out loud, e.g. a coerced
  human acting at a "manifest physical bottleneck" (the article's own example, decided);
  an anaesthetised or dreamlessly sleeping human, where the interface is presumably quiescent;
  an outcome mediated by a non-conscious biological system; an AI-to-AI transaction with
  downstream human effects. The payoff is that the AI-risk argument's "mind-arena avoidance"
  strategy is only as sharp as the boundary an AI would have to draw, so this section makes
  the term's primary application testable rather than merely stated.
- **Caveat**: this one is genuinely harder than the other three and may not resolve cleanly.
  A section that lays out the cases and reports that two of them stay undecided would still be

  article's honest "fuzzier than a clean partition" into a false precision.
