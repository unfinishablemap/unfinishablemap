### 1. The probability leg's strongest form is now on disk in a sibling that did not exist on 2026-08-25 — and it lands on the apex's own outcome-uniqueness conjunct

The apex gives the probability argument one paragraph. Verbatim, `obsidian/apex/one-world-wager.md` **L56**:

> **The probability argument** targets technical adequacy rather than coherence. A theory in which all outcomes occur must still recover the Born statistics we observe, and the derivations on offer — decision-theoretic, self-locating, envariance-based — remain contested within the Everettian literature itself: Mandolesi (2018, 2019) formalises a circularity charge against Wallace's decision-theoretic proof, and Dawid and Friederich (2022) argue that the Sebens–Carroll self-locating derivation smuggles in the amplitude-sensitivity it was meant to derive. [The probability-problem article](/topics/probability-problem-in-many-worlds/) carries the full case. The Map's use of this argument is calibrated: the derivations are contested, not refuted, and the argument's force is the structural asymmetry — collapse readings postulate the Born rule honestly, while Everett must earn it from a theory in which everything happens.

`envariance` appears there once, as a bare hyphenated adjective with no wikilink. It is the article's only occurrence. `concepts/envariance` (created 2026-09-03, 19.8KB, `last_deep_review` 2026-09-04) states the result the apex is reaching for and does not have. **L36**:

> envariance fixes the *form* of the probability measure—if outcomes carry probabilities at all, they must be Born-weighted—but it never converts probabilities into actualities. Zurek's own scaffolding concedes the point in advance: outcomes enter through his "facts" and, in the 2022 restatement, through the repeatability postulate, before any probability is derived over them. What the derivation presupposes is exactly what the Map's framework treats as the open question—why one outcome becomes actual.

And **L78**, where the same point is derived rather than asserted, from Mertens & van Wezel (2023):

> This is the measure/actuality distinction derived from inside the formalism: envariance guarantees that a Born-weighted *description exists*, per state, not that actual measurements instantiate it.

**Why this is the top finding, and not merely a missing crosslink.** The apex's §"What the Tenet Actually Demands" (**L76**) names *outcome-uniqueness* — "exactly one outcome is globally actual" — and says it is "what an interpretation must satisfy." The apex's probability leg (L56) is filed under "technical adequacy," a *separate* concern. The envariance result joins them: the best-developed Born-rule derivation on offer presupposes outcomes-as-outcomes before it can weight them, which is to say it presupposes precisely what outcome-uniqueness asserts and what the apex says the physics does not supply. That is a synthesis-level connection between two of the apex's own sections, sourced to a primary-text concession (Zurek's "facts") rather than to a contested verdict — **and it survives the derivations succeeding**, where the apex's current framing ("remain contested") does not.

That last clause is the real value. The apex currently rests the leg's force on a live technical dispute it correctly declines to adjudicate. The envariance material lets it rest the leg on something the Everettian grants: form-fixing is not actuality-fixing. The sibling `topics/probability-problem-in-many-worlds` **L136** already runs the counterfactual version of exactly that move —

> Suppose, counterfactually, that the in-framework objections were all answered... The Deutsch-Wallace program would then have delivered its full result... But it would be a claim about rational agency and self-location, not about physics.

— which is the argument the apex should be carrying at L56 and is not. **Actionable, minted below.**

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The apex refuses the one move that would make the hard problem disappear by fiat, and says why in the article's own terms rather than by tenet assertion. L46: branching "is the sharpest available way to *dissolve* that question... the question is declared confused rather than answered. What earns Chalmers' assent is the next step rather than that one — L80: "Choosing one world therefore does not *answer* the vertiginous question... The tenet protects the explanandum; it does not supply the explanation." A framework that distinguishes protecting an explanandum from explaining it has understood what the explanatory gap is.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The article's cleanest structural insight, and one Stapp would take straight: L78 separates the demand for a single actual outcome from any particular mechanism for producing it — "single-outcome actuality is what the tenet requires of an interpretation, collapse is one way of supplying it" — with Griffiths's consistent histories admitted as "an ally against branching and a rival on mechanism in a single stroke." That is a taxonomy of the interpretive landscape by *what the tenet needs*, not by *who agrees with us*, and it is why the survey can name allies it disagrees with.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The article centres the first-person question without letting felt singularity do work it cannot do. L76 is the discipline: "branch-local experience does not discriminate between the readings, since each branch-successor anticipates one outcome and gets one outcome either way." Nagel's own move — that objective description leaves something out — is stated here as a *posit the Map adopts* rather than as a datum extracted from phenomenology, which is the honest version of the claim.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**Load-bearing here, and the verdict is favourable with one reservation.**

What earns praise is the grading. §"Evidence and Dependency" (L102–L106) separates registers instead of blending them: the Everettian probability disputes are "externally evidenced: live controversies in the technical literature that hold independently of the Map's framework, **though what they establish is that the derivations are contested, not that they fail**"; the indexical argument "carries weight independent of Tenet 4 and none independent of Tenet 1"; the consciousness argument "grades split," only its explanatory-gap clause counting; and the modal-realism rejection is "inherited from Tenet 4 itself." The section closes: "Coherence among tenet-derived clauses contributes no independent weight, and this synthesis claims none for it."

Two things that are praise-worthily *not* done. The apex declines to press ontological extravagance, L60: "the Map cannot disavow simplicity arguments against dualism while wielding one against Everett" — a defeater it holds against itself. And L98 refuses to convert its own candour into standing: "That provenance is a fact about the registration, not extra warrant for the tenet — what survives the discounts is all the tenet has." That sentence is the correct answer to the 08-25 review's sharpest counterargument, and the article gives it rather than deleting the offending claim.

**The reservation, and it is a calibration concern rather than a praise.** The probability argument is graded *flatly* external at L102, and its sibling grades the same argument as **split** — `probability-problem` L142: "the support is not independent of the indexical objection: branching indifference, the premise the Map declines here, is declined for the same reason it declines branch-relative indexicality... Both refusals descend from one root, so the objections converge rather than accumulate." Both can be true: the *technical disputes* are externally evidenced, while the *Map's declination of a premise* is tenet-driven and convergent with the indexical root. But the apex does not draw that distinction, and it is exactly the distinction §"Evidence and Dependency" exists to draw — the apex already performs it for the consciousness argument (external gap-clause vs Tenet-3 clause) and has the same structure available and ungraded for probability. Since the article's signature move is its count of independent lines, this belongs in the audit. **Filed as part of the minted refine task, not as an expansion opportunity.**
