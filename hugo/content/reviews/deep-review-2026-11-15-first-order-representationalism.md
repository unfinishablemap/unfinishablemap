## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Misclassification: Dennett filed as a first-order representationalist. FIXED.** L66 read "**First-order (Tye, Dretske, Harman; often Dennett)**: phenomenal character is the first-order content of the experience." Reference-work text contradicts this on both axes. SEP *Higher-Order Theories of Consciousness* files Dennett as a higher-order theorist ("the conscious status of a perceptual state consists in its availability to higher-order thought (Dennett 1978a; Carruthers 1996, 2000, 2005)"), and lists Dennett 1978a and 1991 among those who tie consciousness to higher-order representations. SEP *Representational Theories of Consciousness*, the article's own References entry, puts Dennett (1991) among those who deny the qualities outright ("It is possible simply to deny the existence of WIL properties, as do Dennett (1991) and Dretske (1995) … To do that is of course not to defend a representational theory"). It also leaves him off its list of representationalist adherents. The bullet's gloss ("phenomenal character is the first-order content") is a thesis Dennett denies. Provenance: the fresh create (`6c4a793bef`) copied an unsourced aside from the research note ("Daniel Dennett is often grouped here"), and no review since has examined it. Fix: "; often Dennett" deleted (−2 words). Dennett's actual neighbourhood is already reachable through the See-Also [illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/) entry.
2. **Calibration: transparency called "arguably even suggestive of" dualism. FIXED.** L112 ended "the datum representationalism leans on is compatible with—arguably even suggestive of—the Map's dualism." This passes the diagnostic test: a reviewer who accepts the tenets would still flag it, and the Map's own sibling page does. [naturalist-relationalism](/concepts/naturalist-relationalism/) states that "The transparency datum the Map relies on underdetermines dualism: a wholly naturalist theory can honour it" and that "The Map's reply is therefore not 'transparency proves dualism'". This article's own §Colour Relationalism (L100) concedes that perceptual relationalism reaches world-directed experience "with no inner medium … without any non-physical ingredient". Datum-compatibility had been upgraded to datum-support. The tension arose on 07-28, when the relationalism material was added after the 07-11 transparency paragraph, and the 07-29 pass did not catch it. Rewritten: "…is compatible with the Map's dualism but does not favour it; perceptual relationalism honours transparency equally." Pronoun check: "it" refers to dualism, and the second clause names "transparency" explicitly so that the two referents do not collide.
