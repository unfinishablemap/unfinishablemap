## Executive Summary

Both articles run the same move: **they recruit a critical criterion to attack a rival and never turn it on the Map's own commitments.** `pragmatism.md` deploys Peirce's maxim — *a difference with no practical consequences is merely verbal* — against materialism, while the Map's own tenets page concedes at two separate loci that Minimal Quantum Interaction is "*empirically indistinguishable from chance*." `idealism.md` cites Nāgārjuna against idealism's single primitive and never against the Map's two categories, and deploys "more natural explanation" for dualism three sections after conceding that parsimony cuts against it.

Separately, `pragmatism.md` is **an un-propagated summary of its own sibling**. On 2026-06-01, commit `dce148141` fixed the van Fraassen epistemic/metaphysical equivocation and the un-conceded reflexive argument in [topics/pragmatisms-path-to-dualism.md](/topics/pragmatisms-path-to-dualism/). Both defects are still live, verbatim, in [concepts/pragmatism.md](/concepts/pragmatism/) — and the 2026-06-25 deep-review of that file **cleared the van Fraassen line on a false premise**, recording that the inference is "correctly framed as the Map's move" when it is not framed as the Map's move at all. This is a dated, fully-attested instance of cluster **C1** from [reviews/outer-review-synthesis-2026-08-03.md](/reviews/outer-review-synthesis-2026-08-03/) ("correction lands in one place and binds nowhere else"), including the ratification step.

`idealism.md`'s central positive argument is a cumulative case assembled from three considerations, each of which the article itself concedes the idealist answers — and all three are answered by the *same* dissociation move. Three arguments that fail for one reason do not accumulate.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

`pragmatism.md` L70 is the whole eliminativist target in one sentence: "The felt sense that one framework illuminates while another obscures — cognitive phenomenology — is what gives pragmatic assessment its normative force." The argument *presupposes* that the felt sense is the normative force rather than its correlate or its report. Strip the folk-psychological vocabulary and the sentence says: brains that model problem-resolution get a characteristic signal. Nothing in the article shows the signal is not the mechanism. Worse, the article never engages a single naturalist account of normativity — Millikan, Dretske, Papineau, Boghossian are all absent, and they exist precisely to block this step. An argument that its targets have spent forty years answering, presented as though unanswered, is not a strong argument.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

You have written an article (`idealism.md`) whose case for genuine otherness is: things feel resistant (L125), things feel thing-like (L129), and regularities feel law-like (L123). This is exactly the inflation of introspective report into metaphysics I have spent a career on. And you *admit* it: L127 concedes "the Map's preference for dualism rests partly on taking the phenomenology at face value... this is a wager, not a proof." Then L155 cites the same passage as evidence for a "cumulative case." You cannot bank a concession and spend it.

On `pragmatism.md`: the Dewey passage (L54) is remarkable. Dewey was a naturalist. Putnam declined the conclusion. Rorty rejected the framing. The article's response in all three cases is that the philosopher failed to see the implication of his own view — "the logic of his position outruns his commitments." Four of five founders, all of whom understood their positions rather well, are read as insufficiently attentive to them.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

Neither article does quantum work, so the direct attack does not land — but the *indirect* one does, and it is the review's sharpest finding. `pragmatism.md` L38–40 endorses Peirce's maxim: "If two positions have identical practical consequences, the difference between them is merely verbal," then asserts that the materialism/dualism dispute is genuine. Fine. Now run the maxim on Tenet 2. `tenets.md:75` states the mechanism is "*empirically indistinguishable from chance*," that "no presently conceivable instrument resolves a single-trial bias from the chance baseline," and `tenets.md:107` repeats it. `positions/quantum-interface.md:75` adds that the position "sits genuinely close to epiphenomenalism." By the article's own imported criterion, Minimal Quantum Interaction is merely verbal. The article does not run the check. The Map's registers already supply the verdict — the concept page just never asks.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

`idealism.md` L121 announces a cumulative case, then L123 tells me what it is worth: "positing a mental substance that behaves exactly like an independent physical world becomes a verbal choice rather than a substantive claim." Note what that concedes. It is a *parity* argument. If the dualism/idealism dispute is verbal once the idealist's structure does the work physical law does, the argument does not favour dualism — it dissolves the disagreement. The article files its own deflationary result as "the strongest consideration" *for* its side.

### The Empiricist (Popper's ghost)

`pragmatism.md` L48: "when a choice is forced, living, and momentous... we may choose based on our nature and needs rather than waiting for conclusive evidence. The consciousness question meets all three conditions." Two problems. First, the second sentence is asserted with no argument in an article that argues for everything else. Second, "forced" is the condition that fails: James's sense of forced is that suspension carries the same cost as choosing wrongly, and the consciousness question is the paradigm case where suspension is cheap — the dualist and the materialist raise their arms identically. Third and worst: a page that licenses belief-by-need sits inside a corpus that has built a positions register, confidence bands, and `evidential-status-discipline` to do the opposite. `idealism.md` hedges ten times; `pragmatism.md` hedges zero times about any dualist conclusion.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nāgārjuna)

`idealism.md` L59 cites me correctly and then stops exactly where it becomes inconvenient: "Madhyamaka Buddhism rejects both materialism *and* idealism as extreme positions... nothing—including consciousness—has inherent existence. This challenges idealism's claim that consciousness is the 'sole ontological primitive' just as it challenges materialism's claim about matter." *Just as it challenges* — and substance dualism, which posits **two** inherently existent categories, is not mentioned. The Mūlamadhyamakakārikā's critique of svabhāva applies with more force to two primitives than to one. I am recruited against the rival and dismissed from the home position in the same paragraph.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Epistemic→metaphysical equivocation on van Fraassen, live in the summary, fixed in the sibling, and cleared by a deep-review on a false premise

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/pragmatism.md`
- **Location**: L80, "Relation to Site Perspective" → Bidirectional Interaction
- **Problem**: The line reads: *"If explanation is agent-relative — as van Fraassen's pragmatic theory holds — then conscious evaluation has causal consequences."* Van Fraassen's pragmatics of explanation is an **epistemic/semantic** claim about a speech act (answering a why-question is interest- and context-relative). The conclusion drawn is **metaphysical/causal**. The article's own sibling, `topics/pragmatisms-path-to-dualism.md:79`, states the correction explicitly: *"Two readings must be kept apart here, because the dualist payoff depends on the stronger one and van Fraassen licenses only the weaker... That is an epistemic and semantic claim about an act, and it is all van Fraassen commits to."* That correction landed 2026-06-01 in commit `dce148141`; the 2026-06-02 deep-review logged it as "van Fraassen two-readings separation (epistemic/metaphysical equivocation FIXED)". It never propagated. Compounding it: `reviews/deep-review-2026-06-25-pragmatism.md:39` **cleared this exact line**, recording that the "'causal consequences' inference [is] correctly framed as the Map's move, not van Fraassen's." It is not framed as the Map's move — the sentence is a bare conditional whose antecedent is attributed to van Fraassen. The same review made zero enhancements on the ground that the piece was "already-stable, well-calibrated."
- **Severity**: **High** (textbook instance of the skill's Epistemic/Metaphysical Equivocation check, with the correct reading independently attested in-corpus)
- **Recommendation**: Import the sibling's two-readings distinction in compressed form. One sentence suffices: mark that van Fraassen commits only to explanation-as-act and that the causal step is the Map's own extension. Do **not** delete the passage — the material is legitimate; the framing is not.

### Issue 3: Boundary-substitution in the Rorty reply

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/pragmatism.md`
- **Location**: L62
- **Problem**: *"Rorty's move works only if consciousness* can *be dissolved — if phenomenal experience is just one more vocabulary rather than a datum that any adequate vocabulary must accommodate. But the hard problem persists precisely because consciousness is not a description of the world but the condition under which description occurs."* The reply's premise — that phenomenal experience is a **datum** constraining any adequate vocabulary — is precisely what Rorty denies; his whole position is that "datum" is itself a vocabulary with no privileged standing. The passage is presented as an in-framework refutation ("The Map disagrees") but its reasoning depends entirely on a Map commitment Rorty rejects. Per `direct-refutation-discipline`, this is **boundary-substitution**. It is also a second instance of the sibling's already-corrected pattern: the 2026-06-02 deep-review logged "functionalist section demoted from 'forecloses both responses' overreach to honest boundary-marking (boundary-substitution FIXED)" in `pragmatisms-path-to-dualism.md`.
- **Severity**: **High**
- **Recommendation**: Either supply the in-framework argument (Rorty's own pragmatism has a standard pressure point: the vocabulary of vocabularies must itself be assessable, and assessment is what the reflexive argument targets — that route *is* internal to Rorty), or honestly downgrade to framework-boundary marking: *"this runs counter to the Map's foundational commitment that phenomenal experience is a datum, and is noted as such rather than refuted on Rorty's terms."*

### Issue 4: The reflexive self-defeat argument runs un-conceded here and conceded in the sibling

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/pragmatism.md`
- **Location**: L70, L72, L78
- **Problem**: L70 concludes *"The pragmatic criterion cannot validate itself from within a framework that reduces it to physics"*; L78 hardens it to *"materialism about consciousness fails pragmatism's own test... practically self-defeating by the pragmatist's own criterion."* The sibling `pragmatisms-path-to-dualism.md:91` states the concession the concept page omits: *"This reflexive argument is powerful but incomplete on its own. A functionalist might concede the point while insisting that the relevant evaluative capacity is a computational role, not an irreducible phenomenal property."* There is also an internal tension the article does not notice: the demand that a criterion **validate itself** is a foundationalist demand, and pragmatism's defining move is to reject foundational self-certification. ### Issue 5: The cumulative case for genuine otherness is one argument counted three times

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/idealism.md`
- **Location**: L121–129 (§ "The Case for Genuine Otherness"), cashed in at L155
- **Problem**: L121 announces: *"No single argument is decisive, but together they create a cumulative case."* Three considerations follow, and the article concedes each one *at the point it is made*, in each case to the **same** idealist move:
  - **Regularity** (L123): the idealist "invokes intrinsic structure of universal consciousness" — conceded, and conceded so thoroughly the article calls the dispute verbal.
  - **Phenomenology** (L127): *"The idealist can accommodate this: resistance is one portion of universal consciousness constraining another, and dissociation produces precisely this sense of independence... a wager, not a proof."*
  - **Thing-likeness** (L129): *"The answer—that physical appearances are stable dissociative structures while thoughts are ephemeral processes—works."*

  Three arguments defeated by one manoeuvre are not three pieces of evidence; they are one dialectical position stated three times. Cumulative-case reasoning requires that the considerations fail *independently*, and these do not. Then L155 spends the concession: *"These practical difficulties compound the cumulative case for genuine otherness developed in the phenomenological argument above"* — citing as support an argument the article had already granted the opponent accommodates. This is convergence double-counting outside the altered-state register, structurally identical to what the `calibration-audit-triple` Audit Two discipline targets.
- **Severity**: **High**
- **Recommendation**: Keep all three considerations — they are well-written and the concessions are honest. Replace the "cumulative case" framing with the accurate one: the three considerations expose a **single** cost the idealist pays (a mental structure that must behave exactly as an independent physical world), the idealist has a **single** unified answer (dissociation), and the Map's preference is a wager on taking phenomenology at face value rather than a weight of independent evidence. Then repair L155 so it does not re-bank the conceded argument.

### Issue 10: Putnam's model-theoretic argument glossed as its own target

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/pragmatism.md` L66; `obsidian/topics/pragmatisms-path-to-dualism.md` L127
- **Problem**: Both read: *"reference cannot be fixed by physical-causal relations alone; a conscious interpreter is needed to pin down what our words mean"* (concept page) / *"What pins down meaning is the perspective of a conscious interpreter"* (topic page). ## Counterarguments to Address

### The idealist's reply to the interface question

- **Current content says** (`idealism.md` L135): idealism must "either explain why certain appearances function as interfaces while others don't (which smuggles physical realism back in) or deny that any localized interface exists (which conflicts with the phenomenology of embodiment)."
- **A critic would argue**: This is a false dilemma. Kastrup's third option is that the dissociative boundary *is* the interface — the alter's boundary is not an extra posit smuggled in but the very thing the dissociation model already contains. The article states the dissociation model at L73–81 and then argues as if it were unavailable at L135.
- **Suggested response**: Engage the third horn. The Map's real objection is not that idealism lacks an interface but that its interface does no *causal* work in the Map's sense — which is a substantive disagreement worth stating instead of the dilemma.

### The naturalist reply to the reflexive argument

- **Current content says** (`pragmatism.md` L70): "If consciousness reduces to physical processes, evaluation reduces to mechanism, and 'what works' collapses into 'what happens to occur.'"
- **A critic would argue**: Teleosemantics is the standing answer — selection history distinguishes what a mechanism is *for* from what it merely *does*, so "works" does not collapse into "occurs" even on a wholly physical story. The article names no naturalist theory of normativity.
- **Suggested response**: Name the reply and say why the Map thinks it under-delivers (the Map's line is presumably that selection history grounds function but not the *felt* demand-character the argument needs). The sibling article already runs the parsimony version of this at L113; the concept page can inherit it.

### Interventional asymmetry

- **Current content says** (`idealism.md` L103): "Correlation is symmetric; causation claims require additional argument."
- **A critic would argue**: The production theorist's core evidence is not correlational. Anaesthesia, lesion studies and direct stimulation are interventions, and interventions break the symmetry the sentence relies on.
- **Suggested response**: The filter theorist's genuine reply exists (a damaged receiver equally explains degraded output). State it, rather than resting on a symmetry claim that does not survive contact with the interventional literature.

## Strengths (Brief)

**`idealism.md`** is the better-calibrated of the two and much of it should be preserved intact. L71 is a model concession — it names parsimony as a real cost, cites the tenet that licenses paying it, and flags the decoherence objection in the same breath. L153 repeats the discipline ("*speculative, faces the decoherence objection, and has no direct experimental support... a research programme, not a proven mechanism*"). L127's "*this is a wager, not a proof*" is exactly the register the corpus asks for. The idealist's replies are stated fairly and in their strongest form throughout — the article never straw-mans Kastrup, and L87's handling of the dissociation-vs-combination asymmetry is genuinely even-handed.
