# External audit: “Dualism”

**Article reviewed:** “Dualism,” substantively modified 16 October 2026  
**Overall judgment:** **Major revision required**

The article works reasonably well as a concise declaration of The Unfinishable Map’s position. It does not yet work as a dependable core concept article. Its principal weakness is not that it advocates dualism, but that it repeatedly moves from “this is one of the Map’s foundational commitments” to “the alternatives have failed” without supplying the intervening argument. The strongest physicalist, illusionist and Russellian-monist positions are either compressed below steelman level or described in terms those positions explicitly reject.

The article also has a version-control problem. Several linked articles revised between 19 and 22 October now acknowledge that modal rationalism is contested, Russellian monism is not demonstrably unstable, cross-traditional evidence is not independent evidence for dualism, neural quantum relevance is unproved, and the phenomenal-concepts debate remains open. The 16 October hub continues to state stronger conclusions than those dependencies support. The most recent modification to the hub itself was narrowly confined to adding the lawful-correlation reply to its epiphenomenalism paragraph. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

## What the article does well

Several improvements made during the site’s recent review cycle are visible.

First, the article no longer treats eight arguments as eight independent confirmations. In “The Convergence” it concedes that the explanatory gap, Mary, zombies and Kripke share substantial premises, and that failure of their common starting point would make them fail together. That is a valuable correction to simple argument counting. It also says that no single argument conclusively establishes dualism and characterises the combined case as “substantially stronger,” not overwhelming. [dualism](/concepts/dualism/)

Second, the article correctly reports Levine’s own explanatory-gap argument as epistemic rather than as an explicit refutation of materialism. It also now acknowledges that lawful common-cause correlation is available to the epiphenomenalist, rather than presenting consciousness-to-report causation as the only imaginable grounding relation. [dualism](/concepts/dualism/)

Third, it is commendably explicit when a rejection is partly tenet-level. Readers are told that the Map commits to interactionism, minimal quantum interaction and two-way influence. That candour makes it possible to distinguish the framework’s starting assumptions from its evidential case. The problem is that the distinction is not maintained consistently later in the article.

## 1. “What Dualism Claims” and “The Main Varieties”

### The criterion for dualism is underspecified

The article says that what matters is “the irreducibility claim: consciousness cannot be identified with or derived from physical processes.” That does not cleanly distinguish dualism from every form of non-reductive physicalism. Contemporary physicalists can deny type identity or reductive derivation while maintaining physical grounding, realization, constitution or metaphysical dependence. A core article therefore needs to say whether dualism requires:

1. non-identity,
2. failure of physical grounding,
3. failure of metaphysical necessitation by the complete physical truth,
4. possession of fundamentally non-physical properties, or
5. a second substance or causal domain.

Current reference treatments of physicalism explicitly distinguish reductive, non-reductive, identity, realization, grounding and fundamentality-based formulations. Treating “irreducible” as sufficient leaves the boundary between property dualism and non-reductive physicalism unstable. [dualism](/concepts/dualism/)

This also matters for the Map’s bi-aspectual ontology. The linked bi-aspect article presents mind and matter as two aspects of one reality and acknowledges that “aspect” language can merely rename, rather than explain, the relation. The core page should state why that proposal counts as dualism rather than Russellian monism, neutral monism or a strongly non-reductive one-category view. [bi-aspectual-ontology](/concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology/)

### The “subject combination problem” is assigned too broadly

Under “Substance dualism,” the article says property dualism struggles to explain how micro-experiences combine into macro-subjects, whereas substance dualism starts with unified subjects. That is chiefly the **subject-combination problem for constitutive panpsychism and some Russellian-monist variants**, not a generic problem for property dualism. Emergent property dualists need to explain emergence and subjecthood, but they need not posit micro-experiences that combine.

The formulation gives substance dualism an advantage by contrasting it with a particularly burdened subtype of property dualism. It should also state the reciprocal costs: substance dualism faces individuation, pairing, interaction and empirical-disunity problems. Zimmerman’s argument for substance dualism concerns what bears phenomenal properties and whether subjects can be reduced to organisms or events; it is not simply an inference from failed micro-subject combination. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/panpsychism/)

### Chalmers’s naturalistic dualism is described too emergently

The article says that, on Chalmers’s early view, phenomenal properties are fundamental features that “arise from physical complexity.” That wording risks assimilating naturalistic dualism to emergent property dualism. Chalmers’s framework adds fundamental phenomenal or protophenomenal properties and fundamental psychophysical laws; it does not derive phenomenal character from complexity in the ordinary reductive or mechanistic sense. “Are lawfully associated with certain forms of physical organisation” would be more accurate. [Consciousness Network](https://consc.net/papers/precis.html)

## 2. “Arguments for Dualism”

### The explanatory-gap inference is announced, not defended

The article accurately notes that Levine left materialism open, but then says, “the Map infers further that physical science’s concepts cannot capture subjective character.” Levine’s primary argument explicitly separates the epistemic gap from a demonstrated ontological gap. The additional inference therefore requires a bridge principle—for example, a version of phenomenal revelation, transparency or modal rationalism. The article supplies none at this point. [Information Philosopher+1](https://www.informationphilosopher.com/solutions/philosophers/levine/Explanatory_Gap.pdf)

This is a recurring pattern: a source establishes explanatory opacity; the Map then treats ontology as the best explanation of that opacity. That may be defensible, but it is an inference to the best explanation, not something already contained in Levine’s conclusion. The core article should name the inference and compare it with physicalist competitors such as phenomenal-concept accounts, cognitive limitation and future-concept views.

### Mary is presented as though the disputed premise were the conclusion

In “The Knowledge Argument,” the article moves from Mary learning something on first seeing red to learning a fact “beyond the physical.” The latter follows only if Mary gains new propositional knowledge of a new fact, rather than:

- a new ability,
- acquaintance with an already known property,
- a new recognitional concept,
- an indexical or demonstrative mode of presentation, or
- a new epistemic relation to an old physical fact.

The linked phenomenal-concepts article now recognises the Carruthers/Veillet-style reply as a serious live rejoinder and describes the debate as open. The core page instead presents the anti-physicalist interpretation as the unqualified result of the thought experiment. [phenomenal-concepts-strategy](/concepts/phenomenal-concepts-strategy/)

The Jackson quotation—“should be addressed really seriously if you are a physicalist”—is also not properly sourced. I located the 2023 interview episode in which Jackson discusses his recantation, but the article gives neither a transcript nor a timestamp and cites only Jackson’s 1982 paper. The wording may be genuine, but it cannot be checked against the bibliography as presented. [Podtail+2](https://podtail.com/en/podcast/mind-chat/frank-jackson-the-greatest-argument-against-materi/)

### The conceivability argument is stated as a result despite the site’s newer modal analysis

The section says:

> If zombies are conceivable, they are metaphysically possible.

It then invokes phenomenal concepts as directly revealing their referents. These are not neutral premises. They are substantive commitments to modal rationalism and some form of phenomenal revelation. The site’s 22 October article, “Modal Epistemology Beyond Conceivability,” now explicitly says that the zombie and Kripke arguments depend on contested answers to precisely these questions, and that the major alternative accounts neither independently establish nor independently refute the conceivability-to-possibility inference. The changelog describes the Revelation debt as open. [dualism](/concepts/dualism/)

This is the clearest example of the core page becoming stale within its own link graph. It should say that the Map endorses the inference, state the required modal bridge, and identify the objections. It should not present the bridge as an agreed rule of modal reasoning.

### Kripke’s argument receives the same unearned transparency premise

The statement that there is “no hidden essence to pain beyond what we experience” is not simply Kripkean semantics. It adds a thesis about first-person access to phenomenal essence. Type-B physicalists deny that phenomenal acquaintance exhaustively reveals metaphysical constitution. The newer modal article recognises this as a contested Revelation premise; the core page does not. [dualism](/concepts/dualism/)

### Nagel is recruited too straightforwardly into the anti-physicalist case

The article says Nagel showed that objective physical description cannot capture subjective character and describes the modes of access as “categorically different.” Nagel certainly argued that current objective concepts fail to supply the bat’s point of view. But he also said we might have reason to believe sensations are physical while lacking the concepts needed to understand how, and he proposed the development of a more objective phenomenology rather than treating dualism as established.

The article therefore turns Nagel’s challenge to existing conceptions of physicalism into a more decisive ontological conclusion than the source supports. Nagel is evidence for a serious explanatory and conceptual problem, not a straightforward citation for interactionist or property dualism. [University at Buffalo+2](https://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~dgthomas/Nagel_Whatisitliketobeabat.pdf)

### The intrinsic-character argument suppresses its main rivals

The claim that physics describes only relations while phenomenal redness is an intrinsic quality depends on at least two controversial premises:

- that physics is exhausted by relational or dispositional structure; and
- that phenomenal character is non-relational rather than representational, functional or constituted by subject–world relations.

Russellian monists accept much of the first premise and use it against standard physicalism without accepting dualism. Dispositional essentialists and ontic structural realists reject the assumption that physics leaves a categorical intrinsic base waiting to be filled. Representationalists reject the simple contrast between relational physical descriptions and intrinsic phenomenal paint.

The article’s intrinsic-nature argument therefore cannot simultaneously serve as an argument for dualism and then receive only a brief dismissal of Russellian monism later. Its strongest immediate competitor is built from the same premise.

### The unity argument omits both empirical disunity and the dualist burden

The Hasker paragraph says no physical mechanism explains how physical parts compose a unified subject, while dualism treats unity as intrinsic to a non-physical subject. This states the dualist answer at the same level at which it demands a mechanism from physicalism. “The subject is intrinsically one” is not yet an explanation of:

- why phenomenal unity tracks particular neural integration conditions,
- why unity can fragment or partially dissociate,
- how one subject is paired with one changing organism, or
- how the proposed subject’s unity produces the detailed structure of a unified field.

Hasker’s treatment is considerably more sensitive to empirical disunity and to the problems facing Cartesian substance dualism than this summary suggests. The article should distinguish feature binding, access unity, phenomenal co-consciousness and numerical unity of the subject rather than allowing the word “unity” to move among them. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/William-Hasker/publication/290270352_Persons_and_the_Unity_of_Consciousness/links/58765a9f08aebf17d3b99f29/Persons-and-the-Unity-of-Consciousness.pdf)

### The intentionality argument substitutes lack of consensus for failure

The article says that no reductive causal, informational or teleological theory of intentionality has achieved wide acceptance. Even if sociologically correct, lack of consensus does not show that intentionality is non-physical. Philosophy lacks wide acceptance on dualist intentionality as well.

A stronger argument would identify a specific adequacy condition—misrepresentation, normativity, original rather than derived content, determinacy or abstract-object reference—and show why leading naturalistic approaches fail it. As written, the paragraph reports disagreement and treats that disagreement as anti-physicalist evidence.

### “The Argument from Reason” contradicts its linked article

The core page says that physicalism makes beliefs fully explicable by “nonrational physical causes,” and calls the self-defeat structure “decisive.” This creates a false dichotomy between causal and rational explanation. A physicalist can hold that one event has both neurophysical causes and rationally assessable content, or that reasons are causally relevant higher-level or structuring properties.

More importantly, the linked argument-from-reason article itself discusses reliabilism, Anscombe, Davidson, Yablo-style proportionality and real-pattern accounts, and concludes that the argument is not a decisive disproof of physicalism. It also acknowledges that the Map’s proposed quantum interface remains an open explanatory burden. The core page is therefore stronger than its own detailed source. [argument-from-reason](/topics/argument-from-reason/)

### “The Convergence” remains too strong across traditions

The article commendably discounts dependence within the analytic arguments but then says Sāṃkhya, Buddhism, Islamic philosophy, African traditions and the Kyoto School “independently arrive at irreducibility,” suggesting that they track a real feature rather than a shared assumption.

The linked cross-traditional article is now more qualified. It acknowledges that some Indigenous and Chinese traditions do not fit dualism; that Buddhist and Kyoto positions require careful qualification; that the claimed Islamic independence is weaker; and that a common source in introspection may generate correlated conclusions without independent evidential support. It further says that any convergence supports irreducibility more readily than dualism, because idealism, neutral monism and process views can accept much of the same phenomenology. [cross-traditional-convergence-on-consciousness-irreducibility](/topics/cross-traditional-convergence-on-consciousness-irreducibility/)

The core wording should therefore be changed from independent convergence on dualism to cross-traditional recurrence of a problem for reductive description, with the common-source and underdetermination qualifications made explicit.

## 3. “Physicalist Responses and Their Limits”

This is the article’s weakest major section.

### The strongest physicalist argument is largely absent

The core page presents physicalism primarily as a set of responses to the hard problem: Type A denial, Type B a posteriori identity, illusionism and the phenomenal-concepts strategy. It does not fairly state the strongest positive argument against **causally efficacious dualism**:

1. physical effects appear to have sufficient physical causes or physically fixed chances;
2. systematic causal overdetermination is implausible;
3. if mental causes are distinct additional causes, they appear excluded or redundant;
4. interventions on brains systematically alter consciousness and behaviour;
5. a separate interaction law therefore inherits a large explanatory and empirical burden.

Chalmers’s Russellian-monist work treats the causal argument for materialism as one of the two principal pressures a theory of consciousness must reconcile. Contemporary mental-causation treatments distinguish completeness, exclusion, conservation, pairing and proportionality objections. The core page mentions closure later, but never gives the physicalist argument its strongest connected form before announcing that quantum indeterminacy undermines it. [emmind.net+2](https://emmind.net/scien/cau/Files/%28530%29%20panpsychism.pdf)

### Type-A physicalism is reduced to denial plus an unexplained “seeming”

A serious Type-A or illusionist account does not simply say that the hard problem is unreal and stop. It proposes that introspective and metacognitive systems generate representations, judgments and dispositions that philosophers interpret as revealing ineffable phenomenal properties. The meta-problem is then a research programme about report, attention, metacognition, concept formation and self-models.

The Map may argue that such an account leaves genuine experience untouched, but it must engage the proposal on its own terms. Merely asking why the hard problem seems real does not defeat a view whose central project is to explain exactly that seeming.

### The Type-B objection begs the generation question

The article objects that Type-B physicalism provides no positive account of “how physical processes generate experience.” A Type-B identity theorist denies that one entity generates a second entity: the conscious state just is a physical state, known under epistemically different concepts. The right objection is that the identity or necessitation relation appears brute or epistemically opaque—not that an identity theory lacks a production mechanism.

This matters because wording the challenge as “generation” builds dualism’s two-item picture into the question. The current physicalism literature includes identity, grounding and realization formulations that must be distinguished before the article can claim that they merely relocate the burden. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/)

### The illusionism objection equivocates on “experience”

The article says illusions presuppose experience and later argues that the discovery of experiential misrepresentation would itself be experiential and therefore confirm what illusionism denies. Frankish’s position expressly distinguishes:

- functional or access-conscious experience, which he accepts;
- introspective representations and quasi-phenomenal properties, which he accepts;
- irreducible phenomenal properties, which he denies.

Thus “there is an experience in the functional sense” does not entail “there are phenomenal properties in the realist sense.” The core rebuttal moves between those senses without argument. [pdf.stafforini.com+1](https://pdf.stafforini.com/frankish-2016-illusionism-as-theory.pdf)

The site’s dedicated illusionism page now acknowledges that the bare “seeming requires phenomenal seeming” regress assumes the disputed point and does not answer Frankish’s functional-seeming proposal. The core page nevertheless retains essentially that bare reply. [illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/)

A fair core treatment should state the stronger objection: illusionism owes a non-phenomenal model of why introspective representations have exactly the apparent immediacy, privacy, qualitative structure and epistemic force they do. That is a substantial research burden. It is not, by itself, a contradiction.

### The phenomenal-concepts strategy is treated as defeated when the linked page calls it open

The article says the strategy must explain why phenomenal concepts are special and suggests their isolation simply restates the knowledge argument. That is a legitimate challenge, not a demonstrated failure. Recognitional, demonstrative, quotational and acquaintance-based accounts attempt to explain the special epistemic role without positing a non-physical referent.

The linked page now identifies a serious physicalist response and treats the outcome as unsettled. The core’s final sentence—“Each response either denies the hard problem … or relocates the explanatory burden without discharging it”—therefore overstates the site’s own detailed assessment. [phenomenal-concepts-strategy](/concepts/phenomenal-concepts-strategy/)

## 4. Russellian monism is not stated or answered fairly

The section “Alternatives to Dualism” says Cutter and Kind argue that Russellian monism is unstable, that anti-physicalist arguments push toward outright dualism, and that the Map occupies a more stable endpoint. This is the most substantial source-fidelity failure in the article.

### The positive Russellian-monist case is omitted

Russellian monism is attractive because it attempts to reconcile:

- the causal and empirical case for physical completeness,
- the structural or dispositional character of physical description, and
- the apparent failure of structural description to entail phenomenal character.

On many versions, consciousness or proto-conscious intrinsic nature constitutes the categorical basis of physical dispositions. This avoids cross-domain causal interaction because the phenomenal and physical are not separate causes linked from outside. The core article mentions combination difficulties but does not state this principal advantage. [emmind.net+1](https://emmind.net/scien/cau/Files/%28530%29%20panpsychism.pdf)

### Cutter’s conclusion is made unconditional

Cutter’s argument is conditional: roughly, philosophers who reject physicalism using familiar epistemic or modal arguments may find that those same commitments push them beyond Russellian physicalism toward dualism. A Russellian monist can instead reject or restrict those anti-physicalist premises. The article omits this fork and presents Cutter as showing that Russellian monism simply collapses into dualism. [achrafkassioui.com](https://www.achrafkassioui.com/library/Brian%20Cutter%20-%20Against%20the%20Middle%20Ground%2C%20Why%20Russellian%20Monism%20is%20Unstable.pdf)

### Kind is materially misattributed

The cited Kind discussion does not establish that Russellian monism is false, unstable or forced into dualism. Her point is that familiar problems and divisions reappear inside the Russellian framework, so the label does not automatically dissolve the physicalist–dualist dispute. That is a more modest and different conclusion. [www1.cmc.edu](https://www1.cmc.edu/pages/faculty/AKind/Monism-final.pdf)

### The combination problem is variant-specific

Panpsychist and panprotopsychist Russellian views face different combination, constitution and intelligibility problems. Not every Russellian view posits micro-subjects that must sum into a macro-subject. The article needs a variant-sensitive taxonomy rather than one generic combination objection.

### “Stable endpoint” is unsupported by the Map’s own ontology articles

The updated comparison article now describes the movement toward dualism as an organisational or dialectical trajectory, not a proof, and declines to declare a parsimony winner. The bi-aspect article also concedes that “actuality” names a proposed role rather than a mechanism and that the coupling between phenomenal actuality and physical transition remains open. The Map’s endpoint is therefore not demonstrably more stable; it relocates the unexplained relation into a psychophysical control law. [russellian-monism-versus-bi-aspectual-dualism](/topics/russellian-monism-versus-bi-aspectual-dualism/)

## 5. “Dualism and Interaction” and “Quantum Mechanical Considerations”

### Quantum indeterminacy does not by itself create a causal vacancy

The article says quantum indeterminacy leaves room for consciousness to influence outcomes without injecting energy. This is too quick. A physical theory can be indeterministic while still fully specifying the objective chances of outcomes. A non-physical intervention must then do one of three things:

- alter those chances,
- select individual outcomes while preserving all observable distributions, or
- affect which measurement/question is physically realised.

Each option incurs a different burden. Chance alteration should in principle produce statistical deviations. Undetectable token selection risks making the mental contribution empirically idle. Question selection still requires a lawful route from consciousness to the neural process that sets the question.

The linked causal-closure article now acknowledges that chance-fixing physical closure can survive indeterminism and that interaction requires mind-conditioned probabilities to differ in a causally relevant way. The core article should import that concession. [causal-closure](/concepts/causal-closure/)

### The argument is interpretation-dependent

Quantum mechanics does not uniformly supply undetermined single outcomes. The proposed opening depends on an interpretation involving genuine outcome selection or collapse. Bohmian, Everettian and some relational or epistemic interpretations locate the apparent indeterminacy differently. The Map is entitled to reject Many Worlds as a tenet, but rejection by tenet does not constitute an empirical refutation of it or positive evidence for consciousness-driven selection.

The linked quantum-consciousness article now admits that the selector is a philosophical commitment and that rival interpretations can deny the supposed opening. [quantum-consciousness](/concepts/quantum-consciousness/)

### Stapp’s model is misdescribed

The article says Stapp proposes that attention-driven observations “bias superposition collapse.” In Stapp’s formulation, conscious effort is associated mainly with choosing or repeatedly posing a Process 1 question and controlling its timing or rate; nature supplies the probabilistic answer under the quantum rule. That is importantly different from consciousness choosing the outcome.

The Map’s linked interactionist article has already adopted this correction and says the Map’s own outcome-biasing proposal goes beyond Stapp. The core page has not. [eScholarship+1](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5zv0k0kk/qt5zv0k0kk.pdf)

### “Quantum biology” is not evidence for the proposed neural mechanism

The article argues that quantum biology shows warm biological systems can sustain quantum effects. That establishes only a broad possibility. It does not show that cognition-relevant neural degrees of freedom preserve the required coherence, that consciousness accesses them, or that they support a controllable psychophysical influence.

The linked quantum article now says the neural quantum case is unproved, removes photosynthetic coherence as a precedent, describes avian magnetoreception as unsettled in its relevance, and concedes that no concrete consciousness-selection model is empirically established. The core page’s general appeal to quantum biology suppresses those distinctions. [quantum-consciousness](/concepts/quantum-consciousness/)

Tegmark’s 2000 decoherence calculation remains relevant as a historical objection, but it cannot carry a 2026 empirical section by itself. The bibliography needs current work on neural decoherence, radical-pair mechanisms, nuclear-spin proposals, non-equilibrium open quantum systems and the absence of demonstrated psychophysical control. [Tegmark](https://www.tegmark.org/brain.pdf)

### Avoiding energy injection answers only one objection

Even if a proposed interaction conserves energy, it must still answer:

- the pairing problem,
- the form and parameters of the psychophysical law,
- compatibility with Born-rule statistics,
- no-signalling constraints,
- why influence occurs at some physical transitions and not others,
- why neural intervention evidence has its observed specificity, and
- how mental content maps to a particular physical question or outcome.

The article’s opening sentence—classical objections seemed devastating under determinism, but quantum mechanics reopens possibilities—makes the interaction problem appear chiefly energetic. Current mental-causation literature treats completeness, exclusion and pairing as distinct objections, none of which disappears merely because the underlying dynamics are stochastic. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mental-causation/)

### The neural-correlation paragraph is too strong and non-discriminating

“Every mental state correlates with brain states” is an unsupported universal. More importantly, saying interactionism “predicts exactly these findings” does not make the findings evidence for interactionism. Physicalism, epiphenomenalism, parallelism and many Russellian views also predict close neural dependence.

The dedicated NCC article now describes correlates as philosophically neutral and stresses that current findings do not discriminate between the Map and major materialist alternatives. It also distinguishes correlational, reporting and intervention evidence. The core page should say that interactionism can **accommodate** neural dependence, not that generic correlation confirms it. [neural-correlates-of-consciousness](/concepts/neural-correlates-of-consciousness/)

## 6. “The Parsimony Objection”

The article’s treatment is partly successful but overstates its sources.

Lycan’s description of parsimony as a “very posterior reason” is accurately quoted. Zanotti also argues that familiar simplicity considerations do not straightforwardly favour physicalism over dualism. [New Dualism Archive+1](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/W.Lycan/Du.htm)

Three qualifications are needed:

1. **Huemer and Sober are being recruited too specifically.** Their works address the justification and role of simplicity more generally; only Zanotti is directly focused on the mind–body comparison. Saying all three “independently conclude” that standard justifications fail in this debate overstates the shared conclusion.
2. **Churchland is not traceable from the references.** The text says Churchland qualifies his simplicity argument, but no Churchland source appears in the bibliography.
3. **“Explanatory parsimony favours dualism” is not established.** Dualism may take phenomenal data at face value, but interactionist dualism adds psychophysical laws, a pairing relation, an interface location and a selection mechanism. Whether that is explanatorily simpler depends on how explanatory fit and ontological or nomological complexity are weighted.

The statement that parsimony arbitrates only among theories of equal explanatory power is also too restrictive. Simplicity can be weighed against fit rather than applied only after exact explanatory equality. The Map can reasonably argue that physicalism’s apparent economy has been overstated; it has not shown that explanatory parsimony positively favours its own full five-tenet package.

## 7. “The Eliminativist Challenge” and idealism

### Eliminativism and illusionism are conflated

The article says the discovery that experience is a misrepresentation would itself be an experience, confirming the phenomenon it sought to eliminate. This may be effective against a crude view denying all cognition, representation and access consciousness. It is not effective against Frankish-style illusionism, which accepts those functional phenomena while denying irreducible phenomenal properties.

The sentence “the misrepresentation itself would be phenomenal” is the disputed conclusion, not an independently established premise. The article should distinguish eliminativism about all conscious mentality from illusionism about phenomenal properties.

### Idealism is excluded by stipulation, not answered

The article says idealism is rejected partly because Bidirectional Interaction requires two domains. That is transparent as a description of the Map’s framework, but circular as an objection to idealism:

- the tenet requires two domains;
- idealism has one fundamental domain;
- therefore idealism is rejected.

This shows incompatibility, not inferiority. An idealist could represent apparently bidirectional mind–world influence within one mind-like ontology. The article should label idealism as outside the stipulated framework and then separately present substantive objections—such as individuation, intersubjective constraint, the structure of the physical world, dissociation or explanatory relocation.

## 8. “What Would Challenge This View?” does not make the core view genuinely vulnerable

The article lists several possible challenges, including explaining consciousness physically, exposing a common failure in the eight arguments and ruling out biologically relevant quantum coherence. But it immediately allows that loss of a quantum mechanism would require replacement rather than defeat dualism. The linked interactionist article goes further: the Map’s preferred post-decoherence selection route is not touched by the usual coherence objection. [dualism](/concepts/dualism/)

Accordingly, “biologically relevant coherence is ruled out” is not presently a failure condition for the preferred framework. At most it would eliminate a subset of candidate implementations. The article’s claim that the view is “empirically vulnerable, not metaphysically protected” is too broad unless it supplies a predicted observation that would count against:

- the Dualism tenet itself,
- Bidirectional Interaction,
- Minimal Quantum Interaction, or
- the specific post-decoherence control law.

A useful revision would separate **metaphysical underdetermination** from **mechanism-level falsifiability**. Dualism as a broad ontological family may survive many mechanism failures; a proposed interaction mechanism should not.

## 9. “The Dualist Perspective”

### “Materialism in all its forms fails” exceeds the article’s argument

The article has not examined all forms of materialism. It gives short treatments of Type A, Type B, illusionism and the phenomenal-concepts strategy, while largely omitting grounding physicalism, constitutive Russellian physicalism, sophisticated identity views, non-reductive causal accounts and mysterian physicalism. The universal conclusion is therefore not earned.

“Each major form faces a residual explanatory debt that the Map judges serious” would be defensible. “Materialism in all its forms fails” is not.

### Irreducible consciousness does not entail irreducible value

The article says that because consciousness is ontologically irreducible, phenomenal value is irreducible as well. That inference needs an additional metaethical premise. A dualist about experience could still be:

- a naturalist about value,
- a constructivist,
- an expressivist,
- a response-dependent realist, or
- an error theorist.

The linked phenomenal-value article presents separate identity, transparency, coherence and explanatory-economy arguments. Their existence confirms that value irreducibility is not a simple consequence of consciousness irreducibility. The core should say dualism **makes room for** or **motivates** phenomenal-value realism, not that it entails it. [phenomenal-value-realism](/topics/phenomenal-value-realism/)

### “Concrete metaphysical content” overstates the bi-aspect proposal

The closing paragraph says the framework has concrete metaphysical content because consciousness “actualises” one among physically available outcomes. But the linked bi-aspect article explicitly concedes that actuality currently names a functional role rather than a worked-out mechanism, and that the identity-or-interaction and coupling questions remain open. Calling that concrete content is fair only in the minimal sense of specifying a role; it is not yet a concrete dynamics or psychophysical law. [bi-aspectual-ontology](/concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology/)

## 10. Tenet-protective bracketing

The site is entitled to begin from explicit tenets. The methodological problem arises when a tenet performs argumentative work and the resulting survival is then counted as evidence for the framework.

| Location | Protective move | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Dualism and Interaction” | Epiphenomenalism is rejected as a tenet-level commitment | Legitimate as framework definition; not a refutation. The new lawful-correlation clause improves this. |
| “Alternatives to Dualism” | Idealism is rejected because the interaction tenet requires two domains | Circular if presented as an objection rather than an exclusion rule. |
| “Quantum Mechanical Considerations” | Many Worlds is excluded in advance, allowing a single-outcome selector | A framework condition, not evidence that selection occurs. |
| “The Parsimony Objection” | Occam’s Razor is limited whenever physicalism appears ontologically cheaper | Risks asymmetric standards unless the extra psychophysical laws are costed explicitly. |
| “What Would Challenge This View?” | Failure of one quantum implementation triggers replacement rather than tenet revision | Protects the metaphysical commitment while advertising mechanism-level vulnerability. |
| “The Dualist Perspective” | Irreducible value and agency are presented as implications of dualism | Imports further tenet-compatible conclusions without supplying their independent premises. |

The appropriate discipline is simple: mark each statement as one of **stipulation**, **argument**, **evidence**, **research hypothesis** or **downstream implication**. A tenet can define the explored region without also counting as evidence that the region contains the truth.

## Changes to site methodology

27. **Introduce claim-level citations.** Each consequential historical, empirical or interpretive sentence should point to a source and locator; quotations should require a page, section, transcript or timestamp.
28. **Maintain a source-fidelity ledger.** For each cited work, record the article’s attributed claim, the source passage, whether the claim is direct or inferred, and whether important qualifications were omitted.
29. **Add dependency-aware review.** When a linked specialist article weakens or withdraws a claim, automatically flag stronger formulations in hub, apex, tenet and position-register pages.
30. **Give core hubs precedence rules.** A core article should not state a conclusion more strongly than the dedicated article it links to unless it explicitly explains the disagreement.
31. **Tag epistemic status in prose or metadata.** Distinguish tenet, definition, argument, empirical evidence, compatibility claim, speculative mechanism and research programme.
32. **Require an opponent steelman block.** For every major rival, record its strongest argument, best primary source, the Map’s answer and the unresolved residue. “Relocates the burden” should not count as an answer unless the comparative burden is actually assessed.
33. **Use symmetrical burden accounting.** If physicalism must explain why identities or grounding relations hold, dualism must explain why psychophysical laws, pairing relations and selector mappings hold; if one side may use brute laws, state why the other may not.
34. **Separate accommodation from confirmation.** Generic compatibility with neural dependence, quantum indeterminacy, dissociation or phenomenology should not be described as evidence unless a rival predicts the observation less well.
35. **Separate framework exclusions from arguments.** Excluding Many Worlds, idealism or epiphenomenalism by tenet is legitimate cartography, but those exclusions must not later be counted as evidential victories.
36. **Require mechanism-specific failure conditions.** Each proposed interface should say what result would lower confidence in that mechanism, what would lower confidence in Bidirectional Interaction, and what—if anything—would lower confidence in dualism itself.
37. **Add citation-age and revision-age warnings.** Empirical claims based mainly on sources more than ten years old, and hub pages older than materially changed dependencies, should receive automated review flags.
38. **Track argument dependence formally.** Extend the convergence analysis into a premise graph so arguments sharing Revelation, modal rationalism, phenomenal realism or anti-structuralism are not counted as independent support.
39. **Apply a no-universal-conclusion rule.** Claims such as “materialism in all its forms fails” should require demonstrated coverage of the recognised taxonomy or be rewritten as a bounded Map judgment.
40. **Schedule hub reconciliation after every review cycle.** Reviews of modal epistemology, illusionism, Russellian monism, quantum interaction and cross-traditional convergence should culminate in a mandatory pass over “Dualism,” the tenets page and the arguments-for-dualism position register.
