## Executive Summary

All three pages do the same hard thing well: they hold an eminent interactionist as provenance and refuse to let eminence become evidence. The influx page says "compatibility, not support"; the Sherrington page answers "Is distinguished dissent evidence?" with "No"; the Popper page's verdict is "provenance without inheritance". The Hardline Empiricist and the Process Philosopher praise the same passages, which is the review's sign that the calibration is honest.

The main opening is a provenance upgrade on the Sherrington page, found by reading the 1947 foreword in raw scan for the first time. The foreword verifies the Tenet 5 sentence verbatim ("That our being should consist of two fundamental elements offers I suppose no greater inherent improbability than that it should rest on one only", p. xx), and it does not contain the second sentence the page assigns to it ("not merely unsolved, but still devoid of a basis for its very beginning"). That sentence belongs to Sherrington's 1950 contribution to the BBC symposium *The Physical Basis of Mind*, where a later contributor quotes it back at him (p. 69). The page's correction of a "standing misattribution" is therefore half right, and the half it gets wrong is the half the Map leans on most (the interface-problem gap). The foreword also carries three passages the page has never used: an explicit "at times interaction between them", a quantum-order "amplification" remark, and Sherrington's own anticipation of the energy-monist reply that Pepperell (2018) later made.
