## Errors — Family R: Tenet 4's subsidiary rationale reported as case-work

**R1. `obsidian/concepts/tenet-falsification-conditions.md` L77** (hugo L81) — the higher-leverage of the two, because this article's entire job is to state accurately what each tenet does and does not rest on.

> "Currently, [many-worlds](/concepts/many-worlds/) and collapse interpretations make identical predictions for all feasible experiments. **The rejection is motivated primarily by philosophical concerns—indexical identity, ontological proliferation** (see [the cumulative case against many-worlds](/arguments/many-worlds-argument/))—rather than empirical evidence."

Two members, one conjunction, no ranking, and the adverb *primarily* attaches to both. `tenets.md` L114 ends its indexical paragraph "**This is the load-bearing argument**"; L116 opens the multiplicity paragraph by conceding "the Map cannot invoke simplicity against many-worlds while disarming parsimony arguments *against* dualism" and closes "**subsidiary—a registered cost, not a refutation**." The sentence therefore promotes to co-motivation exactly what the tenet demotes.

The compounding problem is the citation. The article sends the reader to `many-worlds-argument.md` for "the cumulative case," and that article's treatment of ontological extravagance is a section headed *"A Non-Decisive Cost"* whose closing sentence is "**not where the case against MWI rests**." The parenthetical points at the text that contradicts the clause it supports. This is a citation-framing failure as much as a tenet one: real source, correctly linked, framed as endorsing a ranking it explicitly refuses.

Aggravating context: the same file's *Occam's Razor Has Limits* entry twelve lines below (L83) is impeccable — "Individual cases where parsimony succeeds do not threaten the tenet, which claims parsimony is *unreliable*, not that it always fails." The article knows the doctrine; the No-Many-Worlds entry does not apply it to itself.

**R2. `obsidian/topics/probability-problem-in-many-worlds.md` L142** (hugo L147) — the same inversion, with the demoted member listed *first*.

> "The probability crisis provides strong independent support for the Map's [No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds) tenet. **Alongside the [broader case against MWI](/concepts/many-worlds/)—ontological extravagance, the indexical identity problem, the failure to explain consciousness—**the probability problem adds a distinct and arguably more damaging objection…"

Three members in apposition, extravagance in first position, "the broader case against MWI" naming the set. The link target is [concepts/many-worlds.md](/concepts/many-worlds/), whose own extravagance section (L89–96) ends "extravagance is a registered cost of MWI rather than a refutation of it." The article states a membership its own link target denies, in the same clause that makes the link.

The irony is sharp and is also the reason the fix is trivial: two paragraphs further down (L148) the same article handles Tenet 5 with full care — "MWI's defenders argue that eliminating the collapse postulate makes the theory simpler… The Map's first response is internal: the elaborate decision-theoretic or self-locating machinery needed to recover probability… is a hidden complexity that offsets the saved postulate." That is precisely the non-parsimony reply the tenet wants. The defect is confined to one appositive in the section above it.

## Warnings

- **`obsidian/topics/vertiginous-question.md` L188** (hugo L191) — *"The tenet's rationale explicitly cites the indexical problem: many-worlds leads to vast ontological proliferation and raises unanswerable indexical questions."* The colon promises a report of the indexical rationale and then delivers both rationales joined by *and*, so the ranking vanishes at the exact point the sentence claims to be quoting the tenet. Warning rather than error because the remainder of the paragraph is among the most careful No-Many-Worlds treatments in the corpus — it separates branch-egalitarian from first-personally-centred MWI, concedes that List's variant answers the vertiginous question, and closes "The indexical case against many-worlds is decisive against branch-egalitarian views and supplementary, not sufficient, against first-personally centred ones." The paragraph earns its conclusion; only the reporting clause is loose.

- **`archive/voids/indexical-void.md` L113**, served at `hugo/content/archive/voids/indexical-void.md` L117 — a **quotation of a retired tenet formulation**, and the ellipsis falls exactly on the qualification.
  > The tenet's rationale explicitly cites the indexical problem: *"Many-worlds leads to vast ontological proliferation... it raises unanswerable indexical questions: why am I* this *instance and not another?"*

  A grep for `it raises unanswerable indexical questions` against `obsidian/tenets/tenets.md` returns **0**. A reader following the quotation marks to the tenet page finds neither the wording nor the ranking. Warning rather than error because it is an archive body — but archive bodies are fully served, and this one is quoting the Map's most foundational page. Note that `vertiginous-question.md` L188 above carries a near-identical sentence with the quotation marks removed: the two share an ancestor in an older `tenets.md`, which is why the same flattening appears in a live topic and an archived void.

- **`archive/voids/scale-void.md` L97**, served — *"The rejection of many-worlds is warranted not merely because branching is conceptually strange but because the resulting structure involves magnitudes (infinitely many equally-real observers) that human cognition cannot genuinely grasp."* This makes ungraspable magnitude a *warrant* for the rejection — the multiplicity objection restored to case-work, and in a form Tenet 5's own reasoning should block symmetrically: the tenet's central move is that apparent simplicity may reflect our conceptual limits rather than reality's structure (`tenets.md` L138, "The apparent simplicity of physicalism could reflect ignorance rather than insight"), so a cognitive limit cannot then be run in the other direction as evidence about what is true. The article's closing sentence half-sees this — "Our commitment to singular, indexical identity *may partly reflect* the cognitive impossibility of genuinely conceiving ourselves as infinitely duplicated" — which is the honest version, and undercuts the warrant claim two sentences earlier.

- **`obsidian/topics/parsimony-case-for-interactionist-dualism.md` frontmatter L3** (hugo L17) — a navigation-surface warning, and the cheapest fix in this report.
  > `description: "Interactionist dualism may be simpler than physicalism once all explanatory costs are counted—human+AI analysis of hidden complexity, dimensional trade-offs, and the positive simplicity argument."`

  The body is the corpus's best statement of Tenet 5's symmetry and says so four times, most directly at L41: *"The Map does not endorse the inference simpler-therefore-truer here… that self-binding applies to parsimony arguments* for *dualism exactly as it applies to those* against *it."* L41 even anticipates the misreading and defuses it for the **title** — "The title, then, should be read for what the article does." The `description` was not given the same treatment. It drops the conditional the body insists on (*if* simplicity is the measure) and ends on "**the positive simplicity argument**" in the Map's own voice. Under the site's LLM-first writing convention this line is the most truncation-resilient text on the page and the one most likely to be read alone by a fetching model — so the one surface where the disclaimer cannot follow is the one surface that carries the undisclaimed claim. Scope it to the internal-critique reading the body actually defends.
