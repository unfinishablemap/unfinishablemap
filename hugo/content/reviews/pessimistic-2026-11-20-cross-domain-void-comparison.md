## Critiques by Philosopher

Each critique is labelled **[tenet-level]** (it attacks a chosen starting point; noted, not tasked) or **[internal]** (it identifies a defect the page can fix on its own terms).

### The Eliminative Materialist
**[tenet-level]** Churchland would say the signature inventory is a catalogue of folk-psychological reports. Ineffability, epistemic vertigo and dissolution under attention are what people *say* at limits. A void catalogue built from those reports will cluster around "consciousness" as a matter of vocabulary. The article half-concedes this through the by-construction discount (L96). **[internal]** She would also note that "phenomenal voids" form a class whose membership is fixed by the Map's own individuation. The article admits as much at L110, where they are "precisely the voids where dualist and physicalist or illusionist readings are evidentially tied". That admission is a strength, not a defect.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
**[internal]** Dennett would aim at L108: the phenomenal-core voids "are not self-referential obstructions at all." The article's own physicalist alternative (L81) explains consciousness voids as consequences of "the one fact that it models itself with limited, evolved equipment". On self-model accounts ([attention-schema-theory](/concepts/attention-schema-theory/), [self-model-theory-of-subjectivity](/concepts/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity/), and the [meta-problem-of-consciousness](/concepts/meta-problem-of-consciousness/) framing), the hard-problem intuitions are produced by a self-model. That puts them exactly on the self-reference spine the article says they stand outside. So the flat "not … at all" assumes the point in dispute. L128 hedges correctly ("may simply reflect that the phenomenal core has not yet been formalised"), but the body sentence does not. See Language Improvements L1.

### The Quantum Skeptic
**[tenet-level, partly internal]** Tegmark would say the measurement problem is not a principled limit at all: unitary dynamics plus decoherence explains the appearance of definite outcomes, and "structural gap" is an interpretive choice. **[internal]** He would also catch a tension in L49. The article calls the measurement problem "a structural gap, not missing knowledge". The same paragraph then says objective-collapse rivals make "distinct, in-principle testable predictions" and that Diósi–Penrose "has already been experimentally constrained" (Donadi et al. 2021, metadata verified on Crossref). Part of the problem is therefore missing knowledge in the plain sense. See L2, and Priority 4, which uses this to break the "flatness" finding.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
**[tenet-level]** Deutsch would say the article treats the measurement problem as a void only because the Map rejects Everett (Tenet 4). On Everett there is no gap, only branching, and "interpretive proliferation" is a sociological fact, not a signature of a limit. The article honestly lists many-worlds among the predictively equivalent interpretations, so this critique marks a boundary and does not find a defect. One internal point survives: if the measurement problem counts as an *aporia* (Priority 3), Everett's move becomes visible as giving up one horn (unique definite outcomes). That is a cleaner way to present the dialectic than "matches nothing cleanly".

### The Empiricist
**[internal]** A Popperian would note that the article has exactly one test whose result was not fixed in advance: do the four signatures that are not subject-bound (self-refutation, aporia, systematic illusion, ineffability) appear in physics and mathematics? Everything else the article itself calls "close to analytic" (L96). That one test is where a falsifier could fire, and it does. By the Map's own definition of aporia, "equally plausible yet inconsistent premises" ([compound-failure-signatures](/voids/compound-failure-signatures/) table; apex L151), the measurement problem matches. In its standard formulation, completeness, linear dynamics and unique definite outcomes cannot all hold, and the rival approaches are sorted by which one they give up (SEP, Myrvold 2022, §4.2, verified; Bell's dilemma quoted there verbatim). So "incompleteness alone matches one" (L98) is false by the Map's definitions, and the residue the article says survives the discount is thinner than stated. **[tenet-level]** The comparison is hand-classified and not pre-registered. The article says so (L37), so this is noted, not tasked.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
**[tenet-level]** Nagarjuna would say the "inquirer-equals-inquired" framing still reifies an inquirer. In Madhyamaka, every view collapses under its own analysis (*prasaṅga*), so the self-referential obstruction is the signature of all positions, the phenomenal core included. Treating the phenomenal core as standing "apart … entirely" from the fixed-point spine (L108) grasps at an intrinsic nature (*svabhāva*) for experience. This converges with Dennett's internal point (L1) from the opposite direction: both deny the phenomenal core a privileged exemption from self-reference.

## Counterarguments to Address

### "Physics voids have no subject in the loop" (L94)
- **Current content says**: the four subject-requiring signatures cannot appear in physics or mathematics because no one is attending.
- **A critic would argue**: every void, as the article defines it at L41 ("a place where understanding breaks down"), has an inquirer. Physicists report vertigo at the measurement problem, and mathematicians reported it at Cantor's infinities. The article separates *subject in the subject-matter* from *subject as inquirer* only for construction-mistaken-for-observation and dissolution. For epistemic vertigo the separation is asserted, not shown. The Map's own table defines vertigo as "destabilizing awareness of the fragility of one's rational position", which a physicist can have.
- **Suggested response**: one clause at L94 restricting "epistemic vertigo" to *meta-cognitive* vertigo about one's own cognition (the apex's "meta-cognitive limits"), or conceding that vertigo can attach to any domain's inquirer. Not tasked.

### "The phenomenal core is not self-referential" (L108)
- **Current content says**: the phenomenal core stands outside the fixed-point spine.
- **A critic would argue**: self-model theories put the hard-problem intuitions inside a self-model, which is a self-referential structure. See Dennett and Nagarjuna above.
- **Suggested response**: Language Improvements L1.

### Everett dissolves the measurement "void"
- **Current content says**: the measurement problem is a principled structural gap.
- **A critic would argue**: on unitary-only QM there is no gap.
- **Suggested response**: this marks a Tenet-4 boundary, and the page already lists many-worlds among the predictively equivalent interpretations. The Priority 3 rewording makes Everett's move legible as giving up one horn. No further change needed.
