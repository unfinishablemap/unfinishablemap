### 2.2 The argument reconstructed in premise–conclusion form

- **P1.** Integral feedback control / robust perfect adaptation is a substrate-indifferent dynamical property (Yi et al.; Barkai & Leibler; Aoki et al.). *[Established, verbatim-sourced.]*
- **P2.** The same property is fully realised in devices no one credits with experience (op-amp integrator, PID loop). *[True.]*
- **P3.** Therefore any inference "system X regulates robustly → X experiences" over-generates to the op-amp and is invalid. *[Valid — the "integrator floor" reductio. This is the article's one genuinely load-bearing move.]*
- **C (Orthogonality).** Control-theoretic competency is *orthogonal* to phenomenality — it gives "no purchase" on the phenomenal question in either direction.

**The gap:** C does not follow from P1–P3. P3 refutes only the *upward* inference (regulation → experience). Orthogonality is the far stronger bidirectional claim that regulation is *evidentially inert*. That stronger claim is supplied not by the control theory but by the "Relation to Site Perspective" section, which states plainly: **"Tenet 1 (Dualism) grounds the orthogonality."** The work is done by the dualist tenet, then read back into the control-theory data, whose subsequent "silence" is counted as vindication. This is the **constitutional-attractor effect** and the **epistemic-to-metaphysical slide** operating in plain sight.

### 2.3 Equivocation, proving-too-much, and the two unanswered replies

**Equivocation via the "domain-general ladder."** The article's rhetorical engine is a ladder from op-amp to physiological allostasis with "no joint at which the control mathematics changes character." Individually the article is careful — it insists every term ("senses," "set-point," "adapts") "names a dynamical feature; none names anything felt." But the ladder device does the equivocating work the individual sentences avoid: by asserting the ladder is *unbroken in the control mathematics*, it implies the phenomenal verdict fixed at the op-amp rung is inherited all the way up to allostasis. The predictive-processing rival's entire claim is that the relevant joint is **not** in the control mathematics — it is in embodied, self-jeopardising, predictive architecture, and that is exactly where valence is said to emerge. The ladder assumes away the rival's thesis by choosing the one axis (control math) on which it is trivially continuous.

**Proving too much / calibration asymmetry.** The "explanatory closure" argument — the control loop "leaves no functional residue that an experiencer is required to fill" — generalises without limit. If explanatory closure of the functional story drains regulation of phenomenal significance, it drains *every* functional signature of phenomenal significance, including the functional dissociations (blindsight, the cross-modal capability division) the site elsewhere treats as evidence *for* its interface. The article installs a partial mirror ("mechanistic completeness no more settles the phenomenal question downward than robust regulation settles it upward") but applies it only to the downward inference about humans; it never turns the mirror on the site's own positive functional-marker arguments. This is an **evidential-independence failure**: the same acid that dissolves the opponent's inference dissolves the Map's, and the article does not book the cost.

**Functionalist reply (steelmanned), unanswered.** The strongest functionalist does not hold "robust regulation → experience." She holds that experience tracks a *specific, complex functional organisation*, and predicts — as confidently as the article does — that a three-component op-amp integrator feels nothing. So the integrator floor refutes a naive vitalist over-attribution, not functionalism; it is dialectically inert against the actual rival. The article treats the floor as "philosophically decisive" when it is decisive only against a strawman. Orthogonality of *simple* integral control to experience is exactly what functionalism predicts and establishes nothing about complex functional organisation — the article's own thesis, handed back to the opponent.

**PP / active-inference reply (steelmanned), unanswered.** Allostatic regulation and interoceptive inference are precisely where predictive processing *locates the origin* of valence and minimal selfhood (Seth & Tsakiris; Barrett; Solms; Damasio). The article's top-of-ladder examples are therefore **contested territory, not neutral ground for a possibility proof**. The article's reply — "the restriction relocates the gap without closing it" — is its best move and a legitimate restatement of the hard problem. But it is a *stalemate*, not a refutation: it reasserts the explanatory gap rather than defusing the rival's positive constitutive claim. It earns "perpetually contested," not "settled primitive."

**Internal model principle — a trap avoided.** The task flagged risk of equivocation on Francis & Wonham (1976) and Conant & Ashby (1970) — the "good regulator" theorem, whose actual claim is narrow and structural: "any regulator that is maximally both successful and simple must be isomorphic with the system being regulated…Making a model is thus necessary" (Conant & Ashby 1970, *Int. J. Systems Science* 1(2), 89–97, DOI 10.1080/00207727008920220). This is a theorem about linear regulator *structure*, carrying no cognitive/phenomenal sense of "model." The article does **not** invoke it, and so does not commit the feared equivocation between the theorem's sense of "model" and a representational/phenomenal one. It uses "models its own viability" loosely in the rival section but does not rest weight on the theorem. Credit where due — this is the single most common way such arguments smuggle representation into control theory, and the article dodges it.
