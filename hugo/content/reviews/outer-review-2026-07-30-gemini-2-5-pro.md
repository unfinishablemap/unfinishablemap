# Referee Report: "Open Individualism and the De-Combination Problem"

## Introduction and Structural Diagnosis

The manuscript under review advances an ambitious structural disambiguation within the contemporary metaphysics of consciousness. Its stated remit is to untangle "open individualism"—a thesis regarding the numerical identity of persons—from the "de-combination problem," which operates as a grounding dilemma for cosmopsychism and priority monism. Upon this disambiguation, the manuscript attempts to construct a wide-ranging defense of interactionist dualism. The overarching architectural ambition is to demonstrate that interactionist dualism, specifically a "closed-individualist" framework bounded by a primitive haecceity or "thisness," successfully sidesteps the mereological paradoxes that currently plague both physicalist and monist ontologies. By mapping the mereology of mind onto these twin failures of upward composition (the combination problem) and downward individuation (the de-combination problem), the manuscript concludes that consciousness is fundamentally a non-mereological entity, inherently resisting both physical assembly from micro-experiential parts and cosmic fragmentation from a macro-experiential whole.   

The analytical intent of separating open individualism from the de-combination problem is philosophically legitimate, identifying a genuine conflation in the literature where identity theses are routinely mistaken for grounding mechanics. However, the manuscript’s execution of this disambiguation is fatally compromised. The text exhibits a systemic reliance on dogmatic presupposition, severe omissions of the relevant 2020–2025 philosophical literature, and the flagrant misappropriation of formal physicalist ontologies to artificially prop up dualist conclusions. The manuscript consistently substitutes stipulative axioms—which it refers to as "tenets" or "framework boundaries"—for rigorous, dialectical philosophical argumentation.   

When the text confronts robust physicalist, animalist, or panentheist counterarguments, it routinely retreats into declaring a "framework-boundary disagreement" rather than engaging the competing premises on neutral analytical ground. A careful audit of recent changelogs regarding the broader project's methodological evolution reveals a persistent systemic vulnerability: the framework historically relies on redefining external empirical literature to fit its axiomatic priors, an error that structurally manifests in this specific manuscript through its manipulation of mereological formalisms. Most egregiously, the manuscript mishandles the formal mathematical criteria of Integrated Information Theory (IIT 3.0) and contemporary information-compression models of mereology, falsely abstracting these rigorously physicalist frameworks into ad hoc support for irreducible dualism.   

The resulting text identifies a valid structural parallel between upward combination and downward de-combination but completely fails to secure its desired metaphysical conclusions. It relies on invalid logical inferences, false trilemmas, and a highly gerrymandered reading of the contemporary state of the art. The following sections provide an exhaustive forensic reconstruction and critique of the manuscript's central argumentative pillars, demonstrating why the text falls drastically short of the standards required for peer-reviewed academic publication.

## The Methodological Collapse of the Indexical Objection

The manuscript's foundational maneuver in its treatment of personal identity is to evaluate Daniel Kolak’s well-known trichotomy—closed, open, and empty individualism—and to reject open individualism on the grounds that it violates the "distinct-subjects fact". Open individualism, as championed historically by Schrödinger and modernized by Arnold Zuboff (1990) and Kolak (2004), posits that there is numerically only one subject of experience appearing as many, arguing that the separation between individuals is an illusion of perspective. The manuscript counters this by invoking what it terms the "vertiginous question" or the indexical void: the first-person realization of "why am I *this* one?".   

According to the manuscript's logic, this vertiginous question is meaningful only if there exists a plurality of distinct subjects and a thick, non-deflationary ego whose "thisness" (haecceity) picks out a determinate, bounded subject. Because open individualism dissolves the plurality of subjects and denies the metaphysical weight of individual boundaries, the manuscript argues that the theory "collapses exactly the distinct-subjects fact" that the dualist framework presupposes to exist.   

This argumentative structure is demonstrably circular and constitutes a textbook example of begging the question against the open individualist. The manuscript evaluates a rival ontological claim and rejects it solely because it conflicts with the manuscript's own unargued presupposition (closed individualism and primitive thisness). An open individualist does not view the denial of the distinct-subjects fact as a fatal theoretical cost; rather, the denial of distinct subjects *is* the thesis itself. Zuboff and Kolak construct their arguments specifically to demonstrate that the intuition of "thisness" is a cognitive illusion generated by the immediate, localized nature of sensory inputs, not a metaphysical reality. By declaring that the vertiginous question "defeats" open individualism, the manuscript assumes the veridicality of the exact indexical illusion that open individualism explicitly sets out to diagnose and dismantle.   

If there is only one cosmic subject experiencing the illusion of fragmented perspectives, the question "why am I *this* one?" is exposed as a malformed linguistic artifact born of epistemological limitation, not a metaphysical defeater that proves the existence of multiple discrete subjects. The manuscript acknowledges this severe structural weakness but attempts to mask it as a feature of intellectual transparency. It concedes that its commitment to closed individualism is "best read as tenet-driven rather than empirically compelled" and that an open individualist could "read the same first-person data the other way without contradiction". It further categorizes this as a "framework-boundary disagreement registered as such, not a refutation of open individualism from inside its own resources".   

While transparency regarding one's axioms is generally preferable to obfuscation, simply admitting that an argument rests on dogmatic stipulation does not magically transform that stipulation into a valid philosophical critique. In a peer-reviewed academic context, a metaphysical framework that can only defend itself against coherent rivals by retreating behind impenetrable axioms fails the basic standard of dialectical engagement. If the manuscript concedes that it cannot refute open individualism using neutral conceptual resources, then it has no analytical justification for declaring that interactionist dualism is the superior or more explanatorily powerful paradigm. The text uses the vertiginous question as a weapon while simultaneously admitting the weapon only fires if one already accepts the conclusion it is meant to prove.

## The False Trilemma of Individuation and the Evasion of Animalism

A recurring architectural failure of the manuscript is its reliance on false trilemmas to aggressively force the reader toward its interactionist conclusions. This fallacy is most glaring in the manuscript’s treatment of subject individuation, which asks the downward mereological question: what makes a subject *one*, and what draws a definitive boundary around a single conscious entity?.   

The text relies exclusively on Kolak’s taxonomy, insisting that the boundaries of personal identity must be explained by either empty individualism, open individualism, or closed individualism. The manuscript quickly dispenses with empty individualism—associated with Derek Parfit's reductionist view that the self is a fleeting conceptual aggregate based on psychological continuity—by asserting it clashes with the subjective certainty that *this* subject experiences *this* moment. Having ostensibly defeated both empty individualism (via the certainty of the present moment) and open individualism (via the vertiginous question), the manuscript concludes that closed individualism, grounded in a primitive, non-physical indexical boundary (haecceity), is the only remaining logical option for individuation.   

This argument operates in a state of willful ignorance regarding the dominant physicalist framework for personal identity in contemporary philosophy: Animalism. Animalism, defended historically by Eric Olson and Paul Snowdon, and heavily refined in the 2020–2025 literature via hylomorphic biology, argues that the criteria for individuation are strictly biological. According to contemporary hylomorphic animalism, the boundary of the subject is neither a fleeting psychological convention nor an immaterial Cartesian haecceity, but the continuous, integrated biological life of the human organism.   

To illustrate the severity of this omission, consider the following structural comparison of the individuation criteria that a comprehensive literature review must address:

Recent peer-reviewed literature, particularly programmatic essays and analytical defenses published in *Synthese* (2024/2025), demonstrates exactly how hylomorphic accounts of persons resolve the boundary disputes that the manuscript claims defy physical mereology. The manuscript repeatedly invokes extreme neurological anomalies—such as split-brain patients and conjoined twins—to argue that there is no objective, anatomical way to read off how a conscious whole partitions. However, hylomorphic animalism provides a robust, empirically grounded answer to these precise downward questions of individuation. What makes a subject *one* is the integrated metabolic, immune, and biological persistence of the animal itself.   

By grounding the "distinct-subjects fact" in biological continuity, animalism entirely bypasses the manuscript's forced choice between a single cosmic mind (open individualism) and a mysterious, immaterial dualist ego (primitive thisness). The manuscript's failure to even mention biological animalism or hylomorphic continuity as a viable criterion for individuation exposes a massive literature gap. A minor internal review note in the project's changelog fleetingly mentions animalism as a "cluster gap" missing from the site's personal-identity shelf, yet the manuscript proceeds to build its entire taxonomy on the premise that no robust physicalist boundary exists. The author attacks the extremes of psychological continuity (Parfit) and universal monism (Kolak), while systematically ignoring the biological center of the debate. Consequently, the manuscript’s central negative claim—that physical criteria universally fail to settle the boundary of the subject—is an unearned conclusion built on the deliberate suppression of the opposing hylomorphic literature.   

## The Anachronistic Freezing of Cosmopsychism

The manuscript's treatment of the de-combination problem is similarly crippled by a selective, anachronistic reading of the philosophical literature, functioning to freeze its opponents in an outdated paradigm. The de-combination problem—which the manuscript identifies as the downward structural inverse of panpsychism’s upward combination problem—asks how a single fundamental cosmic subject could successfully ground, decompose, or fragment into multiple discrete macro-subjects (human minds). The manuscript rightly identifies Gregory Miller's (2018) formulation as the canonical articulation of this dilemma, noting that the problem hinges on the sheer conceptual impossibility of subjects acting as proper spatial-mereological parts of other subjects.   

In recounting the cosmopsychist defense, the manuscript surveys early replies by Philip Goff (grounding by subsumption), Itay Shani (partial grounding), and Miri Albahari (perennial idealism). The text treats Albahari’s 2019 "dissolve, don't solve" move—which posits an aperspectival cosmic consciousness that does not require literal de-combination—as the terminal state of the debate. By framing cosmopsychism as hopelessly trapped by the de-combination problem and reliant on spatial-mereological assumptions that inevitably fail, the manuscript sets up a second false dichotomy: the reader must either accept the catastrophic grounding failure of priority monism, or embrace the manuscript's preferred interactionist dualism, which "structurally avoids" the problem by treating consciousness as non-composite.   

This narrative entirely ignores the profound advancements in cosmopsychist and panentheist metaphysics published between 2020 and 2025. Specifically, the literature has shifted aggressively away from the spatial-mereological "parthood" models that Miller's 2018 critique relies upon. For example, recent developments in Vedantic cosmopolitanism and cross-cultural revisionary metaphysics have formalized what Swami Medhananda (2022) terms "Nondual Panpsychism". This contemporary framework explicitly sublates the de-combination problem by rejecting the foundational premise that the fundamental reality is an undivided, monistic "One" that must literally fracture, divide, or decompose into distinct spatial parts.   

As contemporary literature demonstrates, nondual panpsychism maintains that reality is neither plural nor dual, but crucially, it is also never wholly "One" in the simplistic mereological sense attacked by Miller. By refiguring the cosmic foundation as multidirectional, reflexive, and cognitively systemic rather than spatial, these recent frameworks explicitly overcome cosmopsychism's de-combination problem without abandoning the priority of the whole, and most importantly, without paying the severe ontological costs associated with interactionist dualism. Furthermore, panentheistic iterations of cosmopsychism bypass the spatial-parthood dilemma entirely by grounding human freedom and distinct subjectivity within a divine or cosmic consciousness that does not require literal mereological decomposition to host distinct loci of awareness.   

The manuscript's failure to engage with this post-2020 literature is fatal to its central thesis regarding monism. If the de-combination problem has been robustly addressed and sublated by contemporary nondual panpsychism and panentheism, then the manuscript's claim that dualism is uniquely positioned to avoid mereological paradoxes loses its primary dialectical leverage. The manuscript cynically uses a 2018 problem to dismiss a complex, evolving philosophical lineage, willfully ignoring the 2022–2025 systemic solutions that invalidate its foundational dichotomy.

## The Misappropriation of Formal Mereology: Integrated Information Theory

The most alarming scholarly malpractice in the manuscript occurs in its attempt to hijack formal mathematical and physicalist accounts of mereological composition to support an irreducible, non-spatial dualism of mind. In its analysis of the upward mereological dynamic, the manuscript examines Peter van Inwagen’s "Special Composition Question" (SCQ), which asks when objects genuinely compose a further object. The text correctly notes that traditional answers fall into universalism (composition always occurs), nihilism (composition never occurs), or restricted composition (composition occurs only under specific conditions). The manuscript then claims that the most serious contemporary proposals for restricted composition—such as van Inwagen’s organicism, Trenton Merricks’ eliminativism, and Integrated Information Theory (IIT)—"cluster around the domain where consciousness tends to occur".   

To substantiate this extraordinary claim, the manuscript leans heavily on Kelvin McQueen and Naotsugu Tsuchiya's 2023 paper, "When do parts form wholes? Integrated information as the restriction on mereological composition," published in *Neuroscience of Consciousness*. The manuscript abstractly summarises McQueen and Tsuchiya's rigorously physicalist, information-theoretic criterion, emphasizing that they "reach an information measure they refuse to identify with experience," and abruptly concludes that this convergence provides "suggestive support" for a dualist picture where consciousness is entirely non-mereological.   

This represents a profound distortion of the cited material. McQueen and Tsuchiya (2023) utilize the formal mathematical calculus of IIT 3.0 not to point toward an irreducible, non-spatial Cartesian subject, but to provide a precise, mathematically objective restriction on physical composition. They argue that non-trivial composition occurs exactly when integrated information (Φ) is maximized across a physical system.   

To understand the severity of the manuscript's mischaracterization, one must contrast the manuscript's metaphysical conclusions with the actual mechanics of IIT as deployed by McQueen and Tsuchiya in the cited 2023 paper. The mathematical architecture of IIT 3.0 relies on generating Transition Probability Matrices (TPMs) to map the cause-effect structures of discrete physical components (e.g., logic gates or neurons). The calculus of Φ determines the exact boundary of a macro-object by finding the precise physical partition that yields the maximum irreducible cause-effect power.   

McQueen and Tsuchiya explicitly decouple their compositional criterion from phenomenal consciousness in this specific paper precisely to avoid the kind of circular mysticism the manuscript peddles. By defining physical composition through the structural maximization of integrated information, they provide a fully naturalist, non-dualist, computable answer to the Special Composition Question. Their model implies that a system is a whole because of its dense physical causal interrelatedness, not because a non-physical haecceity is hovering over it.   

The manuscript performs an invalid, sleight-of-hand logical maneuver: it observes a physicalist theory (IIT) mathematically defining the objective physical boundaries of complex informational systems (like brains), notes that these are the exact same systems that happen to generate consciousness, and subsequently concludes that the boundary *is* the irreducible consciousness. This is akin to observing that a thermometer accurately tracks the boiling point of water and concluding that the thermometer is constructed out of heat.

Furthermore, the manuscript completely ignores the profound mathematical challenges facing IIT when one attempts to use it as an interactionist dualist interface. As McQueen himself notes in a separate 2023 preprint ("Quantum Superpositions of Conscious States in a Minimal Integrated Information Model"), utilizing IIT's Φ-structures to dictate quantum collapse models—which the manuscript's "minimal quantum interaction" tenet explicitly requires—leads to a catastrophic "operator proliferation" problem. The collapse dynamics become mathematically intractable because the conscious states in IIT are individuated by counterfactual causal structures encoded in the TPM, leading to an explosion in dynamical complexity. The manuscript ignores this fatal mathematical roadblock, instead co-opting McQueen and Tsuchiya's 2023 formalism as "suggestive support" for an interactionist framework that inherently rejects the very concept of upward composition. This is not merely a misreading; it is the active subversion of a cited source to argue the exact opposite of its published, physicalist intent.   

## The Evasion of Objective Information Compression

The manuscript’s mishandling of physicalist mereology extends to its treatment of recent breakthroughs regarding information compression ontologies. The text briefly references Alexander Bird's (2023) and Majid Beni's (2025) advancements in the philosophy of composition but summarily dismisses them to protect its dualist thesis.

Bird’s 2023 paper, "Restricted Composition is Information Compression," offers a groundbreaking, non-conscious solution to the Special Composition Question: entities are parts of a composite object when that object allows for the efficient, if lossy, compression of information about those parts. This model relies on the physical concept of "fastenation," where the physical conjoinment of parts establishes objective, verifiable correlations among their locations and motions. When parts are fastened, tracking the macro-object compresses the data required to track the individual micro-parts, establishing an objective, mind-independent boundary for the whole. Beni (2025) further refines this by introducing thermodynamic depth to constrain the compression, ensuring the compositional boundaries are objectively rooted in physics and thermodynamics, completely independent of observer perspective or conscious unity.   

These papers represent the most formidable contemporary threat to the manuscript's thesis that only consciousness can resolve the composition question. If Bird and Beni are correct, then the physical world possesses its own fully objective, non-arbitrary criteria for drawing boundaries around composite objects—criteria that have absolutely nothing to do with dualistic consciousness, haecceity, or first-person indexical voids.

Faced with this robust physicalist literature, the manuscript simply surrenders intellectual rigor. It openly admits that it "does not defeat [these rivals] on neutral ground". Instead, it retreats once again into declaring a "framework-boundary" dispute, pressing what it calls the "standing burden of explaining why our compositional intuitions cluster so persistently around organised [sic] life".   

This is an astonishingly weak defense. Bird’s entire project is designed to explain exactly *why* our intuitions cluster the way they do: because human cognition is highly evolved to detect and exploit efficient information compression in the physical environment. By demonstrating that information compression is an objective, thermodynamic feature of the physical universe—not a matter of subjective perspective, interest, or dualistic primitive unity—Bird and Beni eliminate the mereological explanatory gap that the manuscript claims only interactionist dualism can fill. The manuscript acknowledges Bird (2023) and Beni (2025) but completely fails to mount a philosophical counterargument against the mechanics of their theories, opting instead to preserve its own tenets through sheer declarative fiat. To cite the bleeding-edge literature of one's physicalist opponents only to explicitly refuse to engage with its logical mechanics is a fatal flaw in a peer-reviewed submission.   

## The Interactionist Paradox: Relocating the Mereological Gap

Ultimately, the manuscript's defense of interactionist dualism hinges on what it characterizes as a "standard, honest trade". The author argues that by positing consciousness as an ontologically basic, non-composite, and non-divisible entity, interactionist dualism successfully avoids both the upward combination problem and the downward de-combination problem. The price for this theoretical avoidance, the manuscript concedes, is the classic interaction problem: how a non-physical, non-spatial mind exerts precise causal influence on a physical, spatial brain.   

However, a rigorous logical analysis reveals that this is not an "honest trade" at all; it is a metaphysical shell game that merely relocates the paradox. The manuscript attempts to resolve the mereology of mind by declaring that the mind possesses no mereology. Yet, the interaction problem—the causal pairing of mind and matter—fundamentally requires a mapping interface. The manuscript explicitly endorses a "Minimal Quantum Interaction" tenet, proposing that the indivisible mind biases specific quantum indeterminacies (such as wave-function collapse) within the brain to author physical action.   

This creates a devastating structural paradox that the manuscript leaves entirely untested. If a unified, non-composite, primitive subject must target and bias specific microscopic quantum states within the highly composite, spatially extended, and mereologically vague neural architecture of the brain, *how does it individuate its target?* The physical side of the causal interface is subject to the exact physical mereology that the manuscript claims is completely insufficient for establishing boundaries.

In other words, the combination and de-combination problems are not structurally avoided; they are simply offloaded onto the causal interface. If the brain is a highly complex sum of overlapping parts (molecules, cells, neural assemblies, hemispheres), and the mind is an indivisible, non-spatial unity, the mapping function between the two inherits the combination problem all over again. How does a radically indivisible unity interface with a divided biological multiplicity without fracturing its own causal agency or requiring a one-to-many causal mapping that dualism cannot explain?

The manuscript provides no formal mechanism for this mapping, relying instead on positing a "causal-interface void" at the mind-body junction. To claim total victory over the combination problem while simultaneously defining one's own theory as possessing an impenetrable "void" at the exact locus where combination must conceptually occur is philosophically bankrupt. The manuscript trades a well-defined conceptual problem in panpsychism for an undefined, structurally identical conceptual problem in dualism, and attempts to pass this sleight of hand off as theoretical progress.   

## Specific Weaknesses and Verdict

The manuscript’s failure to achieve academic readiness is most clearly demonstrated by its systematic omission, contradiction, and mishandling of recent peer-reviewed literature. The following five specific weaknesses mandate outright rejection:

1. **Omission of Nondual Panpsychism’s Solution to De-Combination:** The manuscript asserts that cosmopsychism is fatally trapped by Miller’s (2018) de-combination problem, forcing a false choice between grounding failure and dualism. This completely ignores Medhananda (2022, *Swami Vivekananda's Vedantic Cosmopolitanism*), which explicitly demonstrates how nondual panpsychism sublates the de-combination problem by rejecting the spatial-mereological parthood assumptions the manuscript relies upon.
2. **Misrepresentation of Integrated Information Theory (IIT 3.0):** The manuscript cites McQueen and Tsuchiya (2023, *Neuroscience of Consciousness*) to suggest that restricted composition converges on, and provides "suggestive support" for, dualist phenomenal consciousness. This severely misrepresents the source; McQueen and Tsuchiya explicitly formulate IIT’s compositional restriction (Φ maximization) as an objective, causal-informational architecture strictly decoupled from phenomenal claims, solving the composition problem physically rather than supporting dualism.
3. **Mishandling of Information Compression Ontology:** The manuscript waves away Alexander Bird (2023, *The Philosophical Quarterly*) and his thesis that restricted composition is information compression, dismissing it merely as a "framework-boundary" dispute. It fails to logically engage Bird’s rigorously physicalist proof that objective correlations (fastenation) inherently define compositional boundaries without any need for the manuscript's proposed dualistic "primitive thisness".
4. **Omission of Contemporary Hylomorphic Animalism:** The manuscript establishes a false trilemma for subject individuation (open, empty, or closed/dualist individualism) by entirely ignoring the dominant biological alternative. Recent peer-reviewed work in *Synthese* (2024/2025) applying robust hylomorphic animalist accounts to personal identity provides a strictly physicalist, biological grounding for the "distinct-subjects fact," directly invalidating the manuscript’s claim that physical mereology universally fails to bound a subject.
5. **Failure to Account for Thermodynamic Constraints on Mereology:** By attempting to declare consciousness as the only non-arbitrary boundary for macroscopic objects, the manuscript explicitly contradicts Beni (2025, *The Philosophical Quarterly*). Beni formally demonstrates that thermodynamic depth mathematically constrains information compression, yielding an observer-independent, non-conscious physicalist restriction on composition—a major literature advancement the manuscript notes but explicitly refuses to dialectically engage.

**Verdict:** Due to its reliance on circular axioms, severe misrepresentation of formal informational mereology, and systemic omission of contemporary panentheist and animalist literature that directly invalidates its central dichotomies, this manuscript is categorically unfit for academic publication and should be rejected.
