## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **The Parity Reply was weakened by its own ellipsis, inside a section titled "Rivals
  at Full Strength."** The block quote elided *"And since action is conventional, and is
  undertaken by conventional agents, there is no reason to think that merely conventional
  distinctions are irrelevant to action and its goals."* The elision was not neutral: it
  removed the half of the reply that presses **against the Map** (conventional
  distinctions are action-relevant, so impartial benevolence can be rebuilt on
  conventionally real streams with no appeal to ownerlessness) while keeping the half the
  article uses **in the Map's favour** under Tenet 5 (deflation reaching persons reaches
  suffering too). *Resolution*: restored the elided sentence verbatim from the chapter,
  attributed the quote to Garfield, Jenkins and Priest in their own voice (it was
  previously unattributed in-text), and added a two-sentence gloss naming which half cuts
  which way, with the first half marked as the harder one. The Relation-to-Site-Perspective
  paragraph now says "the Parity Reply's *second half*" so it no longer implies the whole
  reply favours the Map.

- **Over-concession to Harris.** The article said that if the verses are skilful means
  rather than a proof, "the finding above loses its target along with the challenge." That
  concedes more than Harris's position delivers, and it is the documented shape where
  over-claims running *against* the Map collect ratification instead of correction.
  *Resolution*: scoped the concession using the article's own naming point — the
  "Ownerless Suffering Argument" is a modern label for a reconstruction. What dissolves if
  Harris is right is the *exegetical* target (that Śāntideva himself grounded impartiality
  on ownerlessness); the reconstruction survives, because Goodman advances it in his own
  voice, and the finding constrains that inference. Harris's own abstract fixes the scope:
  the reconstructions he rejects are those of Williams, Siderits and Pettit.

- **Misattribution of an identity claim to the positions register.** The article wrote that
  "this feeling-moment is bad" and "this feeling-moment is bad for its locus" may be one
  fact under two descriptions "as [P-VS3](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs3) holds." [P-VS3](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs3)
  asserts the identity of *felt badness and badness*, not the identity of *bad* and *bad
  for a locus*. The sibling article states this correctly as an analogy ("as [P-VS3](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs3) says
  felt badness and badness are"); this article asserted it as [P-VS3](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs3)'s content.
  *Resolution*: reworded to "on the pattern [P-VS3](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs3) sets for felt badness and badness, which
  is the identity that entry actually asserts."

### Medium Issues Found

- **The load-bearing sentence was an assertion, not an argument.** "A distinction can be
  idle within a metaphysics and decisive within an argument, and this one is" carries the
  whole piece, and a reviewer accepting the Map's tenets can still press it: a genuinely
  idle distinction should not be inferentially decisive. *Resolution*: supplied the
  argument — the two descriptions are interchangeable for the *valuation* (neither makes
  the moment worse) but not for the *ontology* (the second entails a bearer, the first does
  not), and Śāntideva's premise quantifies over bearers rather than over badness.

- **Partial verse quoted as whole.** The Garfield–Jenkins–Priest rendering of 102 was given
  as three of its four lines with no ellipsis, dropping "What restriction can be made in
  that case?" — the closing rhetorical question that parallels Crosby and Skilton's "Why is
  any limitation put on this?", which the article does quote. *Resolution*: line restored.

- **A hedge that the primary text resolves.** The article flagged as odd that Finnigan's
  survey groups Williams with the consequentialist interpreters while Garfield, Jenkins and
  Priest cite him as the argument's critic, and conjectured "Both may hold." The chapter
  settles it: they credit the abhidharma *reading* of the passage to Williams (1997) and
  then cite him among those who find that reading "at least prima facie fallacious from a
  Madhyamaka standpoint." *Resolution*: conjecture replaced with the sourced statement —
  he supplies the reading and doubts it.

### Counterarguments Considered

- *Eliminative materialist / hard-nosed physicalist*: the whole dispute presupposes
  phenomenal badness. Bedrock — the disagreement is at the tenet boundary and the article
  does not overclaim past it.
- *Empiricist (Popper's ghost)*: "idle yet decisive" looks like description-sensitivity
  smuggled in. This one bit, and is answered in the Medium issues above rather than
  dismissed as bedrock — a tenet-accepting reviewer would still have pressed it.
- *Buddhist philosopher (Nāgārjuna)*: the Parity Reply is his, and it is now stated whole.
- *Many-Worlds defender*: no purchase; the article deliberately declines to draw on Tenet 4
  and says why.
