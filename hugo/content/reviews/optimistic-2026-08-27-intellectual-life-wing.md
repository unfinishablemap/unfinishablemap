## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The wing's best single move is `irreducibility-of-thought`'s Mary variant (L77–81). Mary knows everything physical about the neuroscience of mathematical understanding — which populations fire during verification versus insight, which neurotransmitters modulate the "aha" — but has never grasped why √2 is irrational. The article then does what a naturalistic dualist most wants: it names the physicalist's best reply (if she knows *everything* physical, perhaps she already understands) and argues that the reply is *weaker* for cognition than for colour, because "no amount of neuroscientific description of proof-processing seems to *constitute* the grasp of why the conclusion follows." That is the explanatory gap relocated to the one domain where physicalism thought itself safe, and the article says so: "Materialists have a standard response to the hard problem: treat it as a problem about sensory qualia and hope that cognition is easier." Closing that retreat is the wing's thesis, and this article states it in one paragraph.

`cognitive-phenomenology` earns Chalmers-praise for a different virtue: it will not let the abductive case masquerade as a proof. The section "Constitutive as Kind, Not Degree" (L188) concedes that Sass and Parnas's ipseity-disturbance data show cognitive phenomenology *admits of degrees*, then draws the correct line: "The conservative needs not gradients but zeros — a clear case of understanding with no phenomenal character whatsoever." And "The Methodological Asymmetry" (L137) — why trust human introspection while dismissing LLM self-report — ends with "This is a defence of consistency, not a positive case for phenomenal trust on its own." A property dualist who has watched the field over-claim for thirty years will recognise an article that has learned the lesson.

The hub's PCT paragraph (L185, "Constitution vs reliable correlation") deserves mention because it lets the weaker rival stand: "A correlationist who accepts epiphenomenalism can hold the weaker thesis coherently, at the cost of these explanatory burdens." Three burdens are named; the rival is not declared incoherent.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The wing's physics-facing content is mostly honest *silence*, and Stapp would approve of that. Two members flag Tenet 2 as a non-link in so many words: `cognitive-phenomenology` L212 ("This concept does not bear directly on MQI. Semantic disambiguation has adequate classical predictive-coding explanations"), and `irreducibility-of-thought` L133, which goes further and names the temptation it is refusing — "an honest non-link rather than smuggling in a structural-metaphor bridge ('choosing among neural possibilities mirrors selection') that would only repeat in cognitive vocabulary the same speculative move the `neural-implementation-specifics` article handles directly." An article that can describe the bad argument it is declining to make has understood why it is bad.

Where the wing *does* engage the mechanism, `phenomenology-of-cognitive-capacity` L141 offers the wing's one genuinely prediction-shaped Tenet 2 claim: if conscious selection is a scarce resource, then "any competence that can be delegated to deterministic procedural systems *should* be, freeing the scarce quantum-selection capacity for domains where genuine indeterminacy remains." That is a functional rationale for automatisation with a testable corollary the article states at L109 — "the tasks that resist automation share a feature: they involve genuine novelty, ambiguity, or evaluative judgement" — and it is the one place in the wing where the interface model earns its keep by predicting a pattern rather than re-describing one. `constraint-satisfaction` L56 pays the same bill from the other side, importing the log₂(N) ceiling from `selection-only-mind-influence` and naming what the framework still owes: "the selection-criterion problem ... without pretending to resolve the primitive the framework still owes a story about."

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The wing is, at bottom, nine sustained answers to "what is it like to —?", and the best of them are precise where the tradition is often vague. `phenomenology-of-cognitive-capacity` distinguishes early, intermediate and near-capacity load by *what maintenance feels like* ("from grasping to juggling"), and then the overflow: items "*drop*", the gap is "specific — you often know what shape the gap has, even when you can no longer access the content." The sentence at L75 — "Consciousness must actively resist its own withdrawal" — is the wing's most quotable line and a genuine phenomenological observation: automatisation has *momentum*, and staying in contact with an automating skill costs effort.

`intellectual-courage` L82–86 gives the holding-on / standing-in contrast: "Stubbornness feels like holding on. The phenomenal character is of grip ... Courage feels like standing in. The phenomenal character is of occupation." The distinction is drawn from the inside, admitted to be invisible from the outside, and used for exactly the purpose a Nagelian would want — to show a functional description that lumps both as "persistent belief maintenance" leaves a remainder. L108's "temptation to perform confidence" is a small original observation: dismissal pressures the dissenter toward *overstatement*, and calibration under that pressure is part of what courage is.

`translation` is the phenomenological centrepiece. The "split attentional field", the pre-linguistic hold, the "awareness of loss" as "not an inference but a felt quality" — and then the five faces of operational opacity (L106–118), which turn the article from a description of what the translator *receives* to a description of what the translator *cannot see*: "The translator experiences having translated; the translator does not experience the translating." That is a Nagelian point sharpened by Hofstadter-Sander and Gentner: the analogical core is sub-personal, and the felt-fit is the output, not the operation.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Two members are process-friendly in the right way. `phenomenology-of-cognitive-capacity` L119 describes consciousness as a builder that departs: "Consciousness actively constructs the procedural systems that will replace it, then departs. The resulting systems bear the imprint of conscious selection even after consciousness withdraws." Mind and matter as phases of one process, with the de-automatisation mismatch as the felt trace of a structure consciousness built and no longer fits — that is process metaphysics doing explanatory work without substance-dualist furniture. The hub's five modes of meaning (L147–171) are likewise all *activities* — "consciousness generates meaning through temporal binding, evaluative perception, anticipatory orientation, and recursive self-awareness" — events rather than states.

**Constraint applied.** The Process Philosopher's praise stops at `deliberation-under-uncertainty` L97 and L129. L97: "In process-philosophical terms, the commitment is the subjective-aim achieving satisfaction where inherited data is insufficient — consciousness directing the outcome rather than receiving a verdict." L129: "the agent bringing something to the situation that was not already present in the inputs ... The Map proposes that consciousness provides this determination." Here Whiteheadian vocabulary is not decorating a calibrated claim; it is carrying the claim that consciousness *generates* rather than *selects*. Two members of this same wing say otherwise — `constraint-satisfaction` L56 imports the selection-only reading on which consciousness "contributes nothing to the candidate set the brain physically prepares and only chooses" — and the register's [P-A1](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a1)/[P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2) commit to agent causation, not to generation from nothing. The process resonance is praiseworthy; using it to license "something not present in the inputs" is the tier-slippage the skill warns about, in the agency register rather than the organism register. The Hardline Empiricist takes this up below.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`deliberation-under-uncertainty` is the wing's Kane article and, apart from the two lines just flagged, a good one. Its structure is the one Kane would choose: a taxonomy of felt uncertainty (Knight's risk/uncertainty distinction *as* a phenomenological distinction — "risk feels like difficult arithmetic; genuine uncertainty feels like standing at the edge of something unmapped"), then deliberative vertigo as "ignorance combined with necessity", then commitment as leap. The article puts the strongest computational rival *first* ("The Metacognitive Monitoring Objection", L115) and concedes its force — "much of what the article describes *could* be produced by a sophisticated self-modelling system" — before replying that functional self-monitoring does not entail phenomenal self-awareness. L81 lets the deflationary reading of expert intuition stand ("not ruled out by the phenomenology alone"), and L127 gives the epiphenomenalist the common-cause reply and admits that expert testimony "sits uncomfortably with this prediction" rather than refutes it. A libertarian who has learned that Libet-style data are not the only threat will find the right opponents engaged here.

`intellectual-courage` L134–136 is the Kane-adjacent passage a libertarian would *want* to be true and the one the Hardline Empiricist must send back: "The courageous thinker's neurons fire differently than they would have if social conditioning alone determined the outcome. This is bidirectional interaction in action." The felt effort of dissent is genuine data; the counterfactual about neurons is not something the phenomenology can deliver.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`cognitive-phenomenology`'s "Introspective Limits" (L192–202) is the most McGinn-friendly section in the corpus, and it is the *concept* article — the wing's definitional node — that carries it. "Three faces of irresolvability" (L198): the introspective face (the first-person question cannot be settled from inside introspection), the dialectical face (Fürst: "introspective judgments about phenomenology are regimented by the view initially held"), and the empirical-architectural face (aphantasia, neuroimaging and dissociation cases "admit liberal and conservative re-descriptions"). Then the Würzburg–Titchener precedent (L196): fifteen years of rigorous laboratories, identical methods, opposite conclusions; "the controversy did not resolve — it was abandoned." And the conclusion, which is the exact shape McGinn recommends: the qualified-liberal verdict "is offered as interpretive choice under that limitation," and "this honest limitation does not strengthen the dualist commitment, but it does not weaken it either."

`philosophical-disagreement` L104 applies the same humility reflexively: "The Map cannot claim immunity from the dynamics described here. Its own commitments structure its perception of evidence, generate its sense of what counts as intelligible, and produce the felt conviction that dualism is correct." And L62 dispatches the bias hypothesis with a genuinely good argument — "If biases explain the other side's error, they equally explain one's own conviction. The hypothesis dissolves philosophical positions into psychology without remainder, a self-undermining move." The cognitive-closure reading of persistent disagreement (L98: introspective access partial, or communicative transfer lossy, or both) is McGinn's own diagnosis, arrived at from the phenomenology of the dispute rather than from the metaphysics.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

No member of this wing touches minimal-organism consciousness, so the five-tier organism scale is not in play. The wing's tier question is the *evidential* one — what can first-person texture establish? — and the Empiricist's verdict is the review's headline, so it is given in full.

**What the wing praise-worthily does not do.** Three members hold the line that phenomenology *constrains* and does not *establish*, and each says so in its own idiom:

- `translation` L100: "The translation-phenomenology evidence therefore constrains the picture — it makes the simple container model untenable — without establishing the dualist reading over its computational rival; the data is underdetermined by the evidence available from inside the translator's first-person situation." L118 repeats it for the five faces: "None of these dissociations on its own establishes the dualist reading; each removes the simplest identity claim ... and leaves the deeper question ... open."
- `cognitive-phenomenology` L192–202: "the Map's stance is *interpretive*, not evidential — abductive convergence raises the credence of the liberal interpretation without breaking the symmetry."
- `irreducibility-of-thought` L97: PCT "is contested even among cognitive-phenomenology liberals ... The Map adopts PCT abductively, so the rabbits/undetached-rabbit-parts conclusion and the symbol-grounding application below are conditional on it."

The Empiricist also praises `constraint-satisfaction` L102, in isolation: "This remains a suggestive convergence between first-person texture and the Map's mechanism, not a demonstration that the phenomenology proves the metaphysics." And `phenomenology-of-cognitive-capacity` L129–133 for a "What Would Challenge" section whose three falsifiers are all *empirical* — felt capacity failing to track actual capacity; expert meditators keeping the limit while losing the phenomenology; total automation proving achievable — with the honest note that "more rigorous testing would be valuable."

**Where the register slips — three spokes, one class.** The wing's own standard is stated at the tenets page: Bidirectional Interaction is "a metaphysical commitment supported by self-stultification and indirect evidence, not as a directly introspectible datum," and [P-A4](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a4) holds at high credence that "no agent can certify its own causal efficacy by introspection, because every such check is performed by the faculty whose causal status is in question." Against that standard:

- `intellectual-courage` L36 (lead): the phenomenology "is irreducible to physical description and reveals consciousness doing normative work that mere computation cannot." L134: "The phenomenology is not an epiphenomenal gloss on neural activity but the very medium through which epistemic norms exert their authority." L136: "The courageous thinker's neurons fire differently than they would have if social conditioning alone determined the outcome. This is bidirectional interaction in action." The third sentence is a first-person certification of a counterfactual about neurons.
- `constraint-satisfaction` L36 (lead): "constraint satisfaction feels like something *because* consciousness is doing genuine causal work at the interface between mind and brain." L108: "The Map's dualism explains this naturally." The same article's L102 disclaims exactly this ("not a demonstration that the phenomenology proves the metaphysics") — the lead asserts what the tenet section withdraws, and a chatbot truncating at the lead never reaches the withdrawal.
- `philosophical-disagreement` L98: "If consciousness were epiphenomenal — present but causally idle — this shaping influence would be inexplicable. The phenomenology of philosophical disagreement reveals consciousness doing causal work at the foundations of thought." The epiphenomenalist's common-cause reply — which `deliberation-under-uncertainty` L127 states correctly — is not considered.

These three are precisely the wing members with one prior optimistic mention each. The two spokes that have been refined against the register (`translation`, `irreducibility`) and the concept that has been deep-reviewed three times have the honest register; the three that the review pipeline has touched least do not. An open task minted 2026-08-26 (tenet-check Warning 5) targets the identical defect class in the psychophysical-laws pair ("ground Tenet 3 in first-person evidence the tenets page says cannot verify it"); this wing supplies three more loci of the same class that no task covers.

**The one Process/Empiricist conflict.** `deliberation-under-uncertainty` L129, "Commitment generates determination": "Stochastic algorithms handle this by randomising, but the phenomenology of commitment is not one of randomness — it is of *resolution*, of the agent bringing something to the situation that was not already present in the inputs." The felt difference between resolution and randomness is real data. But "not already present in the inputs" is a generation claim, and the selection-only reading that `constraint-satisfaction` L56 adopts from `selection-only-mind-influence` says consciousness contributes *nothing* to the candidate set. The honest pattern is available one article over: the leap is a high-N candidate set collapsing toward one actualised option, and what the agent "brings" is the *choice*, not new content. Per the skill's rule, when the two personas conflict the output is a `refine-draft`, not an expansion — and this one folds into the Tenet 4 task below, since L129 and L141 are eleven lines apart in the same file and the same pass should harmonise both.

**Honest framework-stage calibration.** `cognitive-phenomenology` L196's Würzburg precedent is the wing's clearest pre-Keplerian self-location: it says the current debate "reincarnates" a controversy that empirical psychology *abandoned*, and does not pretend the Map's abductive tilt escapes that history. That is calibration the Empiricist can endorse without reservation.

### `topics/phenomenology-of-philosophical-disagreement`

### `topics/phenomenology-of-intellectual-courage`
- **Strongest point**: "What Would Challenge This View" L152 — "If heterodox thinkers were no more accurate than conformists — if resisting consensus correlated with no improvement in tracking truth — the case that courage reflects consciousness responding to reasons would be harder to make." That falsifier would actually bite, and it is a real empirical question.
- **Notable quote**: "Being dismissed — treated as though your position does not merit engagement — feels categorically different. It is experienced as exclusion from the epistemic community, not defeat within it."
- **Why it works**: "Openness within firmness" (L60) distinguishes courage from both dogmatism and anxiety by how *challenges* feel — tests rather than threats — which is a finer cut than the virtue-epistemology sources it cites make.

### `topics/consciousness-and-the-phenomenology-of-constraint-satisfaction`
- **Strongest point**: L70 — "The computational difficulty of a constraint problem does not predict the phenomenal character of solving it" — followed by the materialist's salience-computation reply given honestly (L72) and the correct response: it "explains *which* constraints feel heavy ... What remains unexplained is why this valuation has a felt quality in the first place."
- **Notable quote**: "An optimiser benefits from knowing *that* a trade-off was made; consciousness additionally feels *what it is like* to have made it."
- **Why it works**: The automatisation boundary (L94) is used as a three-phase *pattern* — engagement, delegation, interference — that a spectator model and a sole-processor model each fail to predict.

### `topics/phenomenology-of-cognitive-capacity`
- **Strongest point**: "What Remains After Withdrawal" (L79–81) — automatisation as *transformation* into anoetic, articulacy-resistant phenomenology rather than disappearance, set against Dreyfus's representation-free coping with a specific disagreement.
- **Notable quote**: "You don't infer overload from noticing errors; you experience the strain before errors occur."
- **Why it works**: Every phenomenological claim is paired with the third-person literature it exceeds (Cowan 2001, Rouder 2008, Fitts-Posner 1967, Baddeley 2012), and the "exceeds" is stated as a claim about what the models omit, not as a refutation of them.
