## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found — 2 corrected

**1. Internal contradiction: the compression reply said the Map and Bird agree about pebbles (L125).**

- **What the article said.** It held that over "pebbles, molecules" the compression answer and the Map's convergence argument "are observationally matched", and that "the disagreement still bites where consciousness enters".
- **Provenance.** The sentence's original form, quoted in [reviews/pessimistic-2026-06-07.md](/reviews/pessimistic-2026-06-07/) L60, made the intent explicit: "both correctly count pebbles and molecules as wholes". The archived predecessor still read "pebbles and molecules count as wholes on each".
- **Why it is wrong.** The Map's own lead makes consciousness the mark of genuine composition, where simples form "a real whole rather than a mere collection". On that criterion a pebble is a mere collection. The same holds on van Inwagen's criterion: the article's own L59 calls him "near-nihilist: only living things compose". Bird counts the pebble a whole (L81, L87). The two views therefore *disagree* about the central inanimate cases. They agree only that no observation separates the verdicts.
- **Why earlier reviews missed it.** Every prior review (06-07 pessimistic, 06-15, 06-25, 08-18) argued only about the *margins*: shipstars, and the Bird/Beni divergence on scope. None noticed that the centre was misstated.
- **Why it is critical.** A tenet-accepting reviewer would flag it. It contradicts the lead. It also makes the disagreement look smaller than it is, which hid how far the consciousness criterion is a revisionary, restrictive answer.
- **Correction.** The two views are now "observationally matched but split on the verdict: Bird's pebble is a whole, while on the Map's, as on van Inwagen's, it is a mere collection". "Still bites where consciousness enters" became "the disagreement does not wait for consciousness to enter". The 08-18 margin correction (Bird undecided; the distant rock pair) is kept verbatim. The concession now runs in the honest direction: more disagreement is admitted, and none of it is said to be won.

**2. Attribution error and internal contradiction: deflationism cast as a parsimony preference (Relation to Site Perspective, L133).**

- **What the article said.** "Deflationism is, in one light, an extreme application of parsimony: it does not merely prefer the simpler ontology, it dissolves the dispute..." The "not merely X" construction presupposes that deflationism prefers the simpler ontology.
- **Thomasson's raw text contradicts this:**
  - "the kind of simple realism one gets from the easy approach to ontology incurs no such ontological disadvantages, and that those who accept the truth of the undisputed claim do not truly offer a more parsimonious ontology by denying (or avoiding commitment to) the truth of the ontological claim";
  - easy arguments license "the existence of mereological sums, Van Inwagen's gollyswoggles ..., Hirsch's incars";
  - "It may, however, be worth mentioning a point in favor of plenitudinous ontologies."
- **It also contradicts the article.** L55 says the easy approach yields "*simple realism* about the disputed entities — tables and numbers exist".
- **It breaches the brief.** Tenet 5 constrains only the Map's own reasoning, and this sentence filed the deflationist under parsimony just before the fifth-tenet refusal.
- **Correction.** "Deflationism's economy is metaontological, not first-order: Thomasson's easy ontology admits tables, numbers and mereological sums, and she denies that withholding them yields a sparer ontology; what deflationism economises on is the dispute itself, dissolved as empty". The sentence that follows ("the simplicity of declaring a question shallow") now matches it.
