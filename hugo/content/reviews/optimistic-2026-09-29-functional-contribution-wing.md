## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`surprise-prediction-error-and-consciousness` L105 is the explanatory gap stated the way Chalmers states it, after the 2026-09-26 refine removed the strawman: "But grant PP every parameter it assigns each case. The remaining question is why the library's parameter set feels like *this*—or like anything at all." L119 does the same for affective predictive processing ("What it does not say is why that difference is felt as fear versus wonder rather than as nothing") and L121 for global workspace ("GWT, like PP, is silent on why the broadcast is felt"). Three rival frameworks, each granted its full explanatory reach, each left with the same residue: that is the hard problem argued rather than asserted. `curated-mind` L98 earns the same praise by giving the workspace theorist the last word — "A workspace theorist could reply that 'experiential coherence' is itself just behavioural coherence redescribed — a response the presupposition argument must answer rather than assume away."

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp would notice that the wing does not lean on his mechanism where it cannot bear weight. `types-of-consciousness` L118 says of the Map's own preferred route that "A mechanism that specifies no timing predicts no temporal signature, and therefore yields no phenomenal profile of its own", and then states the consequence against interest: "the mechanisms with the most vivid phenomenal profiles are the ones it holds least firmly". `cognitive-integration-and-the-self` L127 refuses to let the self-forming picture discharge anything: any causal claim "inherits the Map's mechanism debt rather than discharging it". `consciousness-and-cognitive-distinctiveness` L143–145 turns the selection mechanism into a statistical prediction (the residual-selection test) and then says why it cannot be run — "no measurement establishes a negative of that form". A quantum-mind theorist who wants the programme taken seriously should want exactly this: the mechanism stated precisely enough to fail, and the reasons it has not yet been tested stated without excuse.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The wing's phenomenology is at its best when it describes the threshold rather than the peak. `surprise-prediction-error-and-consciousness` L127: "There is something it is like to notice the wrong tone of voice, and nothing it is like to have room temperature corrected" — the selective consciousness of prediction error (L125, "Prediction error occurs constantly, but most of it never reaches conscious experience") is a datum Nagel's question fits precisely, because it asks what it is like *at the edge* where processing becomes experience. `cognitive-integration-and-the-self` L133 separates two questions phenomenology tends to run together: "integration does not reach it, because drawing a boundary does not supply the indexical fact the boundary encloses". Composition (which experiences are one stream) and ownership (whose stream) come apart, and only the first is a binding question.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would recognise his own picture in the wing's insistence that consciousness is something *done* rather than something *had*. `consciousness-and-cognitive-distinctiveness` L110 reads the threshold as "integration as selective activity—integration as something consciousness *does*", and `cognitive-integration-and-the-self` L36 opens with "The self is not a static object but an ongoing achievement." `curated-mind` L55's thermostat contrast ("A thermostat corrects temperature drift without distinguishing modes that serve different experiential purposes") is process-friendly in the right way: the argument turns on the *organisation* of processing around a recipient, not on a substance behind it. None of this praise is used below to move any organism or system up the evidential-status scale, and the wing gives no occasion to: no article in it grades a boundary case.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane's interest is in the wing's account of what unconscious processing cannot do. `conscious-vs-unconscious-processing` L97–107 ("Spontaneous Intentional Action") assembles the dissociations — blindsight patients who "cannot spontaneously use blind-field information to guide self-initiated action" (L104), primes that "cannot initiate decisions de novo" (L105), disorders of volition that track "disrupted conscious access, not motor function per se" (L106). L157's graded framing is the honest version of the ape–human contrast: "A graded gap carries the argument as well as a categorical one would: the capacities that thin out first are precisely those requiring conscious access." Kane would also endorse `cognitive-integration-and-the-self` L183's refusal to inflate diachronic agency past the register's grade: "this section reads no more confidently than the register does."

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`consciousness-and-cognitive-distinctiveness` L147 is the wing's most McGinn-shaped sentence: "the disagreement between phenomenal causation and its two rivals sits closer to bedrock than the comparative framing implied", and the paragraph before it explains why the paradigms that could decide it are not available. `surprise-prediction-error-and-consciousness` L185 declines the easy impossibility claim about machine framework-transcendence: "Whether that is a principled limit is an open research problem, not a demonstrated impossibility." `cognitive-integration-and-the-self` L189 keeps the many-worlds disagreement in its place — "many-worlds denies there is a further fact here rather than failing to accommodate one" — and L191 catches the intuition-pump the Map's own readers reach for: "treating the feeling as confirmation reads felt weight as evidential weight."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This persona's verdict is load-bearing for the wing, because the wing's subject is a causal claim about phenomenality drawn from functional data.

**Praised restraint.** `consciousness-and-cognitive-distinctiveness` L114–147 is the model. It lists six co-varying factors, notes that the comparative record "speaks to five of these" and "is silent on the first" (L125), names the accompaniment and common-cause readings (L130–131), concedes both "predict everything this article has assembled: the selectivity of the gap, the clustering of capabilities, the working-memory expansion, the failure of the cultural ratchet" (L133), and grades the Map's reading as "adopted on tenet-level grounds, coherent with the framework, consistent with everything surveyed here—and still without a discriminating result from comparative cognition" (L147). L165 extends the same restraint to the evolutionary argument: the by-product reply "remains available, which keeps the argument suggestive rather than decisive". This is *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating*, done in full.

`types-of-consciousness` L94 corrects a figure the Map elsewhere leans on: the 10–50 bits/s bandwidth "is a bound on *serial behavioural throughput* … not a measured bandwidth of phenomenal experience itself". L106 keeps AI consciousness at "unlikely for current architectures", and L124 keeps access consciousness on the physical side of the ledger ("A complete taxonomy must therefore distinguish types that require the non-physical … from types that do not"). `curated-mind` L43 and L102 label the whole argument correctly ("the inference is suggestive rather than forced"; "The prediction is real but soft"). `cognitive-integration-and-the-self` L121 states a datum against interest: "the best-supported consolidation window is the one from which dream reports are sparsest". `conscious-vs-unconscious-processing` L117 keeps the functional evidence tied to access consciousness, and L217–227 ("What Would Challenge This View?") names four falsifiers.

**Where the two personas conflict.** The Process Philosopher above praises `conscious-vs-unconscious-processing` for treating consciousness as an activity that enables functions. The Hardline Empiricist cannot praise the inference the article draws from that. L173–175 runs "1. Consciousness is functionally necessary for specific cognitive operations … 3. Therefore, consciousness makes a causal difference to cognition and behavior"; L193 concludes the evidence "does refute the claim that consciousness is causally idle"; L235 reasons that "if consciousness were reducible to neural processing, we would expect unconscious neural processing to achieve the same functions"; L241 says the three functions "match the kinds of cognitive capacities that distinguish human-level cognition from great ape baseline". Each of these is the inference the sibling's audit retracted on 2026-08-17. A reductionist who identifies consciousness with global broadcast does *not* expect non-broadcast processing to achieve broadcast's functions, and the accompaniment reading predicts every dissociation the article lists while leaving phenomenal experience idle. The article's own L117 and L169 ("neutral on whether consciousness is physical or non-physical") already contain the concession; L173–175, L193, L235 and L241 do not honour it. This is a calibration concern, not a strength, and it is minted below as a `refine-draft` rather than an expansion (Concern 1). The same by-product reply the sibling concedes at L165 is denied without argument by `surprise-prediction-error-and-consciousness` L141 ("An epiphenomenal accident would not be so well-tuned"), a locus the 2026-09-26 pessimistic review flagged under *Counterarguments to Address* and the same-day refine did not action (Concern 4).
