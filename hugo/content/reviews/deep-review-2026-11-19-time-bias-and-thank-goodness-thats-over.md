## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Attribution overreach, Hoerl 2014 (item 1).** The page said that "deflationists such as Christoph Hoerl (2014) … would deny" that relief has a tensed phenomenal character beyond the belief. Hoerl's paper explicitly puts Prior's relief case outside its scope. Footnote 5 says that "arguments of this type (and reductionist rejoinders to them) are outside the scope of the present paper" (grep −c = 1, WRAP accepted version). Footnote 29 says: "I have not tried to address such attempts in this paper" (1). The same footnote also says that the paper "can be seen as providing an extension of their approach", meaning the replies of Mellor, Maclaurin & Dyke and Suhler & Callender. **Fixed.** "Would deny it" now belongs to deflationists about passage experience in general. Hoerl is cited only for what the paper says: perception of change involves no phenomenology of passage, the relief case is set aside, and his argument is suggested to extend the replies to Prior.
- **Uncredited coinage, Hoerl 2015 (No Many Worlds).** The rewritten paragraph's key distinction, *temporal* versus *counterfactual* relief, comes from Christoph Hoerl, "Tense and the Psychology of Relief" (*Topoi* 2015). He writes "which I call temporal relief" in the abstract, and in §2: "We could call the type of relief at issue here counterfactual relief". He also argues that temporal relief "seems to be a sui generis phenomenon, which cannot be reduced to counterfactual relief of a certain type" (grep −c = 1). The page used both terms as if they were generic. **Fixed.** The paragraph now opens by crediting Hoerl with the distinction and with the claim that the first does not reduce to the second. The paper also strengthens the page's split: temporal relief survives many-worlds and counterfactual relief is what the tenet bears on. The surname-level orphan check would have missed this second Hoerl work, because Hoerl 2014 was already cited.
