## Executive Summary

The page is carefully sourced and candid about what was not read. Its main weakness is the **occasion face**, which the lead, the section and the tenet paragraph all present as "the crier has no privileged access to what her tears are *about*". The evidence offered shows something narrower: that she cannot see *what produced* them. The lead case is a deep-brain-stimulation patient whose reported content ("I'm crying over myself, of course") was the accurate content of an induced depression. The Map's own phenomenal-authority account rates content reports high and causal reports beyond authority. The page's L99 moves from the first to the second inside one paragraph. The **relief face** uses "recovery" in two senses, and so asserts in two places a separation that a third sentence says one small study cannot make. Two citation framings in the function paragraph misdescribe verified abstracts. The "possibly unexplorable" Plessner core rests on an inference that fails even if Plessner is right. ## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would welcome the opening. An electrode in the subthalamic region produced, within five seconds, a mood, tears and a fluent first-person story, and switching it off removed all three within ninety seconds. "What the tears are about" is folk-psychological talk laid over a circuit that the case shows can be driven directly. She would press the page's own concession that a physicalist and a dualist "describe the same blank". On her view, the blank is just the predictable failure of a folk theory of lacrimation, and it closes as affective neuroscience matures. The page has no reply. It does not need one, because it claims framework-independence. But the function face's "it is shrinking" (L55) concedes her trajectory without saying why the other faces should not follow it.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett would say this is Nisbett and Wilson (1977) applied to tears. People "tell more than they can know" about causes, and here they do it while crying. He would then build a dilemma from the Map's own commitments. Elsewhere the Map protects first-person reports with the process/content distinction. `topics/phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence` rates "Attended phenomenal content (what it is like)" at **High** warranted trust and "Causal processes behind experience" as **Beyond authority**. Its L158 says Nisbett and Wilson's critique "concerns *causal processes*, not *phenomenal content*". Either the distinction holds, and the occasion face shrinks to cause-opacity, which every page already concedes. Or the crier's report of what her tears are about really is unprivileged, and the distinction the Map uses to fence off Schwitzgebel has a hole exactly where emotion meets expression. The page takes the second horn in L99 ("her report of what the tears are about ... carries no privileged weight") while citing the first ("places the causal processes behind experience beyond first-person authority"). Dennett would take the concession and ask what else falls with it. This is the review's main finding; see Issue 1.

### The Quantum Skeptic

There is nothing to attack, and the page says so: "Minimal Quantum Interaction and No Many Worlds have no connection." Tegmark might add that the Bejjani case is a macroscopic current producing a full affective episode in seconds, so it leaves no explanatory work for a sub-threshold selection mechanism. The page does not claim one. The discipline is correct.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Not engaged, correctly. Deutsch has no purchase here.

### The Empiricist

A Popperian would sort the faces by what could refute them. The **relief face** is testable and has been tested. The 2015 study found an above-baseline gain at 90 minutes, and its authors read it as one that "corresponds to the results of retrospective studies". In other words, the criers' retrospective sense of relief was tracking a real later change. The page reports the gain but frames the episode around the 2014 review's *misperception* hypothesis, which the 2015 design was "specifically designed to evaluate". The **occasion face** is testable too, but not by the evidence used. Misattribution-of-arousal paradigms test whether people misidentify the *object* of an emotion; a stimulation case with mood-congruent reports does not. The **Plessner core** is framed so that nothing could bear on it: "every method reaches them through a later articulate report". A claim that defines every possible probe as a contaminated probe is unfalsifiable, which the "possibly" softens without changing. See Issue 4.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would fix on "I'm crying over myself, of course". The self over which she weeps is what the induced depression constructed, a global "everything is useless" self-story assembled in five seconds and dissolved in ninety. On that reading the case shows the conventional self being fabricated on demand. It does not show a stable crier who has or lacks access to her tears. He would also find the occasion face over-reified from the other side. "What the tears are about" assumes a determinate intentional object waiting to be read. The rival theories (being moved, kama muta, helplessness) might instead be different conventional designations of a dependently arisen episode, with no fact of the matter for the crier to be wrong about. Plessner's "the person who yields remains a person" is, for him, the clinging the page never examines.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The occasion face slides from cause to content

- **File**: [voids/weeping-void.md](/voids/weeping-void/), lead L43, section L59, tenet paragraph L99. The same claim appears on three navigation surfaces: the page `description`, [voids/voids.md](/voids/) L124 and L226.
- **Location**: L43 "What an episode is *about* is not privileged to the crier, since an electrode can supply tears and reasons together"; L59 "The crier's access to what her tears are about is not privileged, and it can be manufactured."; L99 "her report of what the tears are about, and whether they helped, carries no privileged weight".
- **Problem**: the page's own careful sentence at L45 states what the stimulation case shows: "a felt reason is not a certificate of its cause". That is a claim about **cause**. The headline claims are about **content**, and none of the cited evidence supports the move:
  - *Bejjani via Schildkrout.* Schildkrout's text, read raw, says that "A psychiatrist can recognize in this presentation the hallmarks of major depression: tearfulness, depressive affect and mood ... feelings of hopelessness and worthlessness". The patient's words ("I'm crying over myself, of course"; "Everything is useless") report the content of that induced state, and nothing suggests the report was wrong. What she could not see was the current. She misattributed the despair's *grounds*, which is the cause-level confabulation the Map's process row already concedes. She did not misreport what the tears were about. The disanalogy with the mirth sibling is real. In Fried et al. (1998) the laughter came first and a *trial-varying external object* was invented afterwards ("a different explanation on different trials although the stimulus was the same", `mirth-void` L41). Bejjani has no invented object. So L47's "As there, a physically caused expression arrives with sincere reasons" overstates the parallel.
  - *Parvizi et al. 2001.* The full abstract sentence the page quotes continues: PLC "is thus distinct from mood disorders in which laughter and crying are associated with feelings of happiness or sadness". The Bejjani episode is mood-congruent crying. Parvizi's own distinction places it on the mood side, where tears and feeling *do* go together, and not in the expression/feeling dissociation.
  - *Aragón & Bargh 2018* study observers. The same abstract reports that when participants described their *own* tears in positive contexts, those expressions "represented positive ... emotional experiences". That is evidence of the crier knowing her tears' valence.
  - *Being moved / kama muta* is a dispute between theorists about how to classify the state. That is unexplored territory, not occluded access (see Issue 1d below).
  - What remains for genuine content-opacity is the V&B feedback hypothesis, which they call "plausible".
- **Map-internal conflict**: `topics/phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence` L148 gives "domain-variable reliability (strongest for attended content, absent for causal processes)". Its gradient table rates "Attended phenomenal content" **High** and "Causal processes behind experience" **Beyond authority**. Weeping-void L99 cites that account as placing causes beyond authority, "and tears fall under that limit". In the next sentence it extends the limit to aboutness, which the cited account places on the other side.
- **1d. Folk vocabulary**: L67 says the crier's own words "name none of them". Zickfeld et al. (2019) is titled "the experience **often labelled being moved**". Its abstract opens: "English-speakers sometimes say that they feel 'moved to tears,' 'emotionally touched,' 'stirred'". Cova & Deonna's paper is titled "Being moved". The vernacular word is the very label both accounts set out to analyse. The page's two example phrases ("I'm so happy"; "I don't know why I'm crying") are the exceptions. What survives is that the theories disagree, which makes this part of the face *unexplored*. Yet L67 ends "This face is *occluded*."
- **Severity**: High. The claim is in the lead, the tenet section and three navigation surfaces, and it sets the face's classification.
- **Recommendation**: re-scope the occasion face to cause, which the evidence supports and the Map's framework predicts. Keep content-opacity only as the V&B "plausible" hypothesis. The edits are near length-neutral (Priority 1).

### Issue 2: "Recovery" means two things, and the page contradicts itself about what the 2015 study separated

- **File**: [voids/weeping-void.md](/voids/weeping-void/) L73, L75, L89.
- **Problem**: L73 defines recovery by the 2014 review: mood "reaches a nadir" and "the return to baseline 'may be misperceived as a real mood improvement'". The same paragraph then asks of the **above-baseline** 90-minute gain: "Whether crying or recovery produced it, one small study cannot settle." A return to baseline cannot by definition produce a gain above baseline. The 2015 abstract keeps the two apart: "it apparently takes some time for the mood, not just to recover, but also to become even less negative than before". L75 and L89 then assert the separation L73 denies. L75: "separating them took a 90-minute delay and a control group". L89: delayed measurement "is the only one found that separates relief from recovery". The study's comparison group cannot separate them either way. The abstract says that in non-criers mood "did not change", so they were never brought low. They control for the film, not for the low, and the low is the confound in question.
- **Also missing**: the 2015 authors' own reading of the gain, that it "corresponds to the results of retrospective studies". In that reading the crier's later sense of relief was right. That weakens the face's "occluded" label for relief, and the page should report it next to the 2014 misperception hypothesis it currently leads with.
- **Severity**: Medium-High. This is an internal contradiction on the face's central empirical claim.
- **Recommendation**: say what the design does separate (the 20-minute return to baseline from the 90-minute gain) and what it cannot (whether crying, rather than having been brought low, produced the gain). Optionally add the authors' reconciliation reading (Priority 2).

### Issue 3: Two citation framings in the function paragraph (L53)

- **3a. "A rival review treats crying as self-soothing (Gračanin, Bylsma & Vingerhoets 2014)."** The 2014 and 2018 reviews have the same three authors. The 2014 abstract concludes "that, **in addition to** the importance of socially mediated mechanisms for the mood-enhancing effects of crying, there is converging evidence for the direct, self-soothing effects of crying". The 2018 abstract makes mood effects follow "primarily as a consequence of fulfillment of the proposed signaling function". This is one group shifting emphasis over four years, and the earlier paper is explicitly complementary. "Rival" tells the reader there are two camps.
- **3b. The chemosignal sequence omits a null on the very outcome Agron measured.** The page reads: Gelstein 2011 positive; Gračanin 2017 "failed to replicate"; Agron 2023 "reduced human male aggression by 43.7%". The 2017 abstract also says: "In addition, sniffing tears did not affect measures of connectedness, **aggression** and pro-social behaviour." As written, a reader infers that aggression was first tested in 2023. In fact the dispute contains a direct null on aggression, though the paradigms differ.
- **Severity**: Medium. Both are verified against the raw abstracts, and both are cheap to fix.
- **Recommendation**: Priority 3.

### Issue 4: The "possibly unexplorable" Plessner core fails even if Plessner is right

- **File**: [voids/weeping-void.md](/voids/weeping-void/) L85; carried by L101 and by [voids/voids.md](/voids/) L322 ("supplies a possibly unexplorable core").
- **Location**: "The tears answer where articulate answers have run out, so a full articulate account of what they said would be the response whose unavailability they answer, and every method reaches them through a later articulate report. This is the Map's inference, not a finding, and it stands or falls with Plessner."
- **Problem**: in Rasini's presentation, Plessner's "fitting response" is a response *to the situation*, conduct addressed to what has become impossible. A later articulate *account* of what the tears answered is addressed to an inquirer. It describes the response and does not replace it. "She wept because the news admitted no answer" is articulate, and it is not the fitting answer to the news. So the conclusion does not follow from Plessner's premise without a further premise: that what the tears "said" has no content apart from the answering. That premise is not argued. The Map's own sibling page draws exactly this distinction. `voids/emotional-epistemology-void` L88: the articulation gap "shows that the experience exceeds what can be said of it, not that its evaluative content cannot be stated: 'It was beautiful' might state in full what was disclosed while conveying nothing of what the disclosing was like." This is a cousin of epistemic/metaphysical equivocation: a *situational* unavailability (no fitting conduct was open to her *then*) is recruited for an *in-principle* one (no articulate account is possible *ever*). "Stands or falls with Plessner" misdescribes the dependency. The inference can fall while Plessner stands.
- **Severity**: Medium. The page hedges heavily ("possibly", "the Map's inference"), but the classification propagates to the index.
- **Recommendation**: name the missing premise. Change "stands or falls with Plessner" to a sentence that says what more is needed (Priority 4). Note also that once Issue 1 is fixed the occasion face concerns cause, so "part of the occasion face is unexplorable" (L85) should read "part of the void".

### Issue 5 (off-list): The tenet paragraph's Occam inference misreads its quote

- **File**: L97. "Vingerhoets and Bylsma (2016) conclude that crying 'should rather be considered as a behavior that obeys the laws of operant conditioning ... than as a reflex-like symptom'. If so, the single-function question was itself the simplification."
- **Problem**: the quoted contrast is **learned behaviour vs reflex**, which concerns mechanism type. It says nothing about **one function vs many**. Operant behaviour can serve a single evolved function, and the same group's 2018 "main hypothesis" is exactly one function: "crying evolved as an emotional expression that signals distress". So "If so" does not follow, and the page's "clearest" tenet connection rests on it. Minor point: "conclude" is wrong for a sentence stated as a thesis in the introduction ("which, as we will demonstrate, should rather be considered...").
- **Severity**: Medium-Low. The page already says the case "illustrates the tenet without arguing for it".
- **Recommendation**: replace "If so, the single-function question was itself the simplification." with "Yet the same group still proposes one main function, signalling (Gračanin et al. 2018), so the case shows a search qualified at each step, not a refuted simplicity." (+15 words). Change "conclude" to "argue". This is next in line after the priority list.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The crier's authority over content
- **Current content says**: the crier's report of what her tears are about carries no privileged weight (L99).
- **A critic would argue**: the Map cannot give this up for tears without giving it up for emotion generally, and its process/content distinction is what protects layer-2 reports from Schwitzgebel. The Bejjani patient's report was accurate about content and blind only to cause.
- **Suggested response**: concede the point. Locate the void at cause and at the theory-level classification, and keep content-opacity as V&B's "plausible" feedback hypothesis only.

### The 70% / 50% gap is not by itself an unreliable instrument
- **Current content says**: more people say crying generally helps than say their last cry did. L93 presents this as what "Aggregation also sees what no crier can".
- **A critic would argue**: the two figures are compatible with accurate self-knowledge. Suppose 70% of people find most of their cries help and the rest find few do. The share whose *last* cry helped can then be 50% with nobody misjudging anything. "Generally" and "last time" are different questions.
- **Suggested response**: one clause, e.g. "a gap that recall bias or lay theory may explain, though the two questions differ" (+12 words). Otherwise, lean on the lay-theory explanation (Gračanin 2014, citing Cornelius 1997) rather than on the bare gap.

### The repeated stimulations
- **Current content says**: the tears and reasons "had a cause the patient could not have known about".
- **A critic would argue**: per Schildkrout, "With the patient's permission, the researchers twice repeated stimulation of contact 0 and demonstrated that the patient's depression was turned on and off each time". On those trials she knew the cause. If the despair came anyway, knowing the cause did not dissolve the content, which suggests a state-driven content she reported accurately rather than an inference she got wrong. Schildkrout does not report her words on the repeats.
- **Suggested response**: none needed in the article. This bears on Issue 1 and is noted for the refine pass.

### The function face as a void
- **Current content says**: the face belongs in the catalogue "because nobody who weeps can tell from inside which of these functions, if any, her tears are performing".
- **A critic would argue**: nobody can introspect the function of their kidneys either. Functions in the evolutionary sense are not introspectible by definition, so this justification would turn every open question in physiology into a void.
- **Suggested response**: the page already says "This is ordinary scientific ignorance". Either leave it as an unexplored face without the "from inside" rationale, or say what is specific to tears (that the crier's felt sense of relief *invites* a functional reading that may be wrong, which links the function face to the relief face).
