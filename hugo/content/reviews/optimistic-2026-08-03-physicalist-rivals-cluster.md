That is the review's finding.

## Executive Summary

This is the most consistently well-calibrated cluster reviewed to date. All five articles independently implement the same discipline — **stating precisely what an argument earns, and refusing to bank more than that** — and they do it against their own interest, repeatedly: the functionalism article grades its five arguments down to "two legs" in a table; the materialism article grades its own reply to Dennett as the weakest of three; the self-representation article declines to recruit two sympathetic authors because their primitivism is a naturalistic monism rather than a dualism; the PCT article announces in its second paragraph that "PCT" is the Map's own coinage that no major figure uses.

The structural finding is that **this discipline has no shared home and no register anchor**. The five articles form a **zero-edge graph** — not one links to any other — and **none cites a single `positions/` entry or `project/` discipline page**, against a corpus baseline of **199 of 799 live articles (24.9%)** that do. The omission runs both ways: **no position in the 14-file register cites `arguments/functionalism-argument` or `arguments/materialism-argument`**, though [positions/arguments-for-mental-causation.md](/positions/arguments-for-mental-causation/) cites their sibling `arguments/epiphenomenalism-argument` twice — so this is a selective gap, not a section-wide convention. The register entry **[P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1)** ("The anti-physicalist arguments are premise-sharing, not evidentially independent") is argued in prose more explicitly by `functionalism-argument` than by any of the four articles its `Argued in` field actually lists.

Nothing here needs rewriting. The cluster needs wiring.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would find his own dialectic reproduced with unusual fidelity — including the parts that go against the Map. `materialism-argument` L72 states his master dilemma against the phenomenal-concepts strategy in its proper two-horn form ("either C is physically explicable, in which case zombies have C and the apparent gap is reproduced inside C itself; or C is not physically explicable, in which case the strategy has helped itself to a non-physical feature"). Then `functionalism-argument` L167 adds what most secondary treatments omit: "This has not closed the debate, and the Map does not present it as having done so; Carruthers and Veillet (2007) dispute the explanandum it turns on."

Better still, `materialism-argument` L88 reports Chalmers as the **dissenter** against an argument the Map relies on — Elitzur's inference from the fact that we discuss consciousness to interactionism — noting he "holds epiphenomenalism counterintuitive but not obviously false, and judges that few arguments do it serious damage." Citing your most useful ally's disagreement with your own supporting argument, in the paragraph where you deploy it, is the behaviour of an article that expects to be checked.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp gets a careful and notably modest hearing. `materialism-argument` L98 sets out the von Neumann–Wigner–Stapp line and then declines to lean on it: "Whether Stapp's specific proposal is correct matters less than what it demonstrates: quantum mechanics does not close the door on mental causation." The load is carried by the measurement problem's openness, not by Orch-OR or by the Zeno mechanism — so the section survives Stapp being wrong.

The decoherence objection is given its own labelled treatment rather than a footnote, with three distinct replies and an explicit concession of the timescales at issue. What earns the most credit is `functionalism-argument` L129, which does the same job in one sentence and then books the cost: "[decoherence](/concepts/decoherence/) and [quantum-biology-and-neural-mechanisms](/concepts/quantum-biology-and-neural-mechanisms/) set out the objection, the Map's replies, and the biological evidence. Neither treats it as settled, so Premise 3 rests on a claim under active dispute." The article's own premise is marked as resting on contested ground, in the section that asserts it.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Nagel's question is kept as the fixed point across all five articles, and `logical-behaviourism` L76 gives it the cluster's sharpest formulation — a reversal of Ryle rather than a rebuttal: "Ryle does not so much refute the ghost as decline to look for it. His method dissolves the inner theatre by redescribing mind-talk as disposition-talk — but it never confronts the datum that motivated the inner in the first place." The category-mistake charge is then turned around with a precise counter-charge: "to treat the felt quality of an experience as *nothing but* a disposition to behave is itself a category error — mistaking a report of what-it's-like for a summary of behavioural tendencies."

`self-representational-theory-of-consciousness` L79 sides with Zahavi against Kriegel on pre-reflective self-awareness — "treating a state as even a secondary object of itself already objectifies consciousness and reopens the regress" — while crediting Kriegel's structural analysis of the datum. Taking the phenomenologist's side without discarding the analytic rival's contribution is exactly the reading Nagel's own position licenses.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would approve of what the cluster refuses. `logical-behaviourism` L78 makes the concession that keeps the Map out of crude substance dualism, and marks it as a concession: "Honesty requires a concession, and the Map makes it. Ryle was right that the mind is not a second *substance-thing* sitting behind the body like a pilot in a machine... The Map's dualism is a claim about the irreducibility of *phenomenal character*, not a defence of a Cartesian inner mechanism."

`phenomenal-constitution-thesis` L85 does the finer-grained version, scoping which reading of Tenet 1 the thesis needs: "The Tenet 1 commitment PCT requires is the bare irreducibility reading, not the agency cluster's substance-leaning sub-reading; an article invoking PCT should not import persisting-subject commitments it does not need." An instruction to *downstream articles* not to over-import from this one is a rare and valuable form of hygiene.

**Boundary observed.** Per this skill's standing constraint, none of the above is offered as evidence-elevating. The cluster's process-friendliness removes a defeater; it does not move anything up the five-tier scale, and — see the Hardline Empiricist below — the cluster is conspicuously careful not to let it.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane's interest is served indirectly but soundly. `materialism-argument` L92–108 does the work his position needs — establishing that causal closure is a claim about classical physics that quantum mechanics does not underwrite — and then declines to convert the opening into a result: "This is a constrained philosophical hypothesis: consciousness might bias quantum indeterminacy in the brain without violating conservation laws or injecting energy." *Might*. The modal register holds across the whole section.

L108's historical observation is the strongest thing in it: "Much of materialism's intuitive appeal still rests on the older deterministic picture — atoms bouncing like billiard balls... Materialism drew its confidence from 19th-century determinism, and that confidence is no longer warranted." That is a claim about the *warrant* for an intuition, not about the truth of a metaphysics, and it is correctly scoped as such.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The cluster's humility is structural rather than decorative. `functionalism-argument` L179 states an outstanding debt as a debt: "That second horn shifts a burden rather than closing a case... The debt is outstanding, not unpayable." L191 does the same for the verdict on illusionism: "The Map's verdict is that the bridge is not yet built — a verdict on a live programme, not a defeated one."

`materialism-argument` L74 goes further and grades the Map's own three replies against each other, naming the weakest: "The Dennett case is weaker by the Map's own accounting, since it comes to rest on a methodological dispute about what first-person reports can evidence, which heterophenomenology settles the other way." An article that tells you which of its own arguments to trust least has done the reader's adversarial work for them.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This persona's verdict is the review's centre of gravity, and it is strongly positive.** None of the five articles touches minimal-organism consciousness, so the usual boundary-case test does not apply — but the *tenet-as-evidence-upgrade* test applies everywhere, and the cluster passes it four times over on four different mechanisms.

**1. Tenet 5 is explicitly denied evidential force.** `materialism-argument` L114 is the cleanest statement of the discipline anywhere in the corpus: "The Map does not then press explanatory simplicity as a criterion of its own. The fifth tenet does not license dualism on grounds of superior simplicity; it only refuses to let parsimony *veto* the posit." A veto-blocker, not an argument. This is *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating* stated in the Map's own words, and it is the exact discipline whose violation is the subject of the currently-open P2 on the competency cluster — where three articles run Tenet 2 minimality as truth-tracking parsimony *against* experience. The materialism article shows the corpus already knows how to say this correctly.

**2. The count is deflated rather than inflated.** `functionalism-argument` L43, in the **third paragraph of the article** — front-loaded, truncation-resilient, exactly what the style guide asks: "They are not five independent confirmations: Arguments 1 and 2 share a contested modal premise, Argument 5 restates Argument 1 without the modality, Argument 3's reach beyond semantics is a conjecture, and Argument 4 states the Map's alternative rather than a refutation." The closing table (L221–229) then carries a **"Rests on"** column that itemises each argument's dependency, resolving five arguments into "two legs sharing no premise." A cumulative-case article that publishes its own dependency ledger in the lead is doing the reviewer's job before the reviewer arrives.

**3. Friendly authors are not recruited past what they concluded.** `self-representational-theory-of-consciousness` L85 is the cluster's finest single passage: "What Williford and Giustina establish is that *acquaintance is non-representational*... That is a weaker claim than *acquaintance is non-physical*, and weaker still than *acquaintance is a genuine causal relatum in a mind-world interaction*. Giustina and Williford are acquaintance **primitivists**, and primitivism about acquaintance is standardly offered as a broadly naturalistic monism, not a dualism; neither takes the further metaphysical steps the Map does." The Map has found two authors whose conclusion points its way, and has publicly measured the gap between their conclusion and its own rather than closing it silently. `phenomenal-constitution-thesis` L59 does the same in reverse: "**Chudnoff supplies the distinction but is a *critic* of content-PCT, not a proponent.**"

**4. An anti-recruitment guard against a live misreading.** `functionalism-argument` L187 anticipates a specific bad citation of the literature and forbids it: conceivability is widely granted while the modal step is denied, and "That position rejects Premise 3 rather than supporting the conclusion, and **should not be recruited as support**." An instruction to future editors not to miscount an ally — written into the article.

**Framework-stage calibration** is honest throughout: `phenomenal-constitution-thesis` L31 and L89 label PCT "a *contested constitutive thesis the Map holds*, advanced abductively against live and capable opponents, not an established finding," and L81 propagates the conditionality downstream: "downstream applications that lean on it — the symbol-grounding diagnosis, the Chinese Room verdict, and AI-understanding skepticism — are *conditional* on PCT. A reader unpersuaded by the abductive case should treat those conclusions as conditional too."

**Process Philosopher / Hardline Empiricist verdict: convergent, not conflicting.** The two personas praise the same passages for different reasons — L78 of `logical-behaviourism` and L85 of `phenomenal-constitution-thesis` are both Whitehead-friendly *and* evidentially disciplined. Per this skill's rule, convergence means the cluster has resolved the tension honestly. **No calibration concern is raised and no `refine-draft` is minted on calibration grounds.**

## Content Strengths

### [arguments/functionalism-argument.md](/arguments/functionalism-argument/)
- **Strongest point**: The dependency accounting at L43 and L219–231. The lead states the discount; the table itemises it; the conclusion states what survives ("two legs sharing no premise") and what that licenses ("held on that calibrated basis, not on a claim that five arguments have independently closed the question").
- **Notable quote**: "The extension is a conjecture, not a corollary. If implementing the right program fails to fix even semantic content, the functionalist owes an account of why it should fix phenomenal character. That shifts a burden without discharging one." (L107)
- **Why it works**: The article separates *what an opponent's own commitments generate* from *what the Map additionally proposes*, and it does this typographically — Argument 4's substrate claim is quarantined under a subheading reading "The Substrate May Matter — **The Map's Alternative**" (L125), opening "Everything above in Argument 4 is a demand the functionalist's own commitments generate. What follows is the Map's rival hypothesis, which the functionalist need not accept."
- **Also outstanding**: the illusionism treatment (L171–181), which distinguishes Frankish's hard end from what Graziano (2024) calls the subtle end, notes that "Objections calibrated against the hard end do not automatically reach the subtle end," and then commits to answering the *harder* one: "It is the second charge the Map must meet." The bare regress objection is explicitly disowned in the same breath as it is reported — "Taken bare it proves nothing, and the Map does not run it as though it did."

### [arguments/materialism-argument.md](/arguments/materialism-argument/)
- **Strongest point**: L86, where the Map concedes that the phenomenal-concept strategy defuses the self-stultification charge against epiphenomenalism — the Map's own favoured argument — and relocates rather than declares victory: "The Map holds Bidirectional Interaction on that calibrated basis rather than on a claim that the argument refutes epiphenomenalism from inside the epiphenomenalist's framework."
- **Notable quote**: "Materialism's failure does not by itself yield dualism, and the Map does not claim otherwise." (L132) — followed immediately by panpsychism, neutral monism, Russellian monism and mysterianism named as live rivals that also reject materialism, with the comparative work explicitly delegated to two other articles.
- **Why it works**: the article blocks the false dichotomy its own title invites. It also declares a dependency most such articles hide: L144 notes the anti-closure argument "is available only to those who have already rejected MWI" — Tenet 4 is named as a *precondition* of the quantum section rather than as independent support for it.
- **Also notable**: the Contemplative Perspectives section (L136) concedes that the tradition it cites cuts against the use it is put to — "the tradition's own analysis of *vedanā* points toward *anattā* rather than toward a stable qualitative substrate."

### [concepts/logical-behaviourism.md](/concepts/logical-behaviourism/)
- **Strongest point**: the historiographical calibration at L52, which corrects the textbook lineage against the Map's own convenience. Filing Carnap and Hempel as strict analytic behaviourists would make the "materialism keeps conceding" narrative tidier; the article refuses: "Later scholarship contests this: both, on a closer reading, allowed reference to internal physiological states and treated the psychology-to-physical links as *synthetic and a posteriori* rather than as analytic definitions. Hempel himself later disowned the reductive reading of his 1935 paper... ascribing the full translation programme to them wholesale is a retrospective simplification the honest account flags rather than repeats."
- **Notable quote**: "Logical behaviourism did not fall to dualists. It fell to the materialists who succeeded it." (L56)
- **Why it works**: attribution discipline is enforced per-objection. L58 closes with "The super-Spartan case is Putnam's, and should be attributed to him alone," and L62 pre-empts a live mis-citation risk: "The sharpest versions of this residue objection — Thomas Nagel's 'what is it like' in 1974, Ned Block's cases against functionalism in 1978 — were aimed at behaviourism's *successors*, and the credit for those refinements belongs to them."
- **Structural role**: this is the corpus's answer to the single most-cited objection to interactionist dualism, and it says so plainly: "The Map cannot dodge this charge; it has to answer it" (L72). It is also **181 words below its soft threshold** — the cheapest expansion headroom in the cluster.

### [concepts/self-representational-theory-of-consciousness.md](/concepts/self-representational-theory-of-consciousness/)
- **Strongest point**: L85's refusal to recruit Williford and Giustina (quoted in full under the Hardline Empiricist above).
- **Notable quote**: "The Map's verdict: self-representationalism is the best structural map yet drawn of the fact *that* experience is for-a-subject, and it fails, informatively, to explain *why*." (L95)
- **Why it works**: the objections are bundled and *graded* rather than stacked — L71: "the Map regards them as jointly sufficient to decline the reduction — not as a single knock-down, but as a bundle... Two of them... are avowedly defeasible: Weisberg's dilemma cuts both ways, and Levine's gap rests on a conceivability intuition." L89 then declares the argument's conditionality outright: "The fork therefore refutes self-representationalism *given* phenomenal realism rather than compelling a committed reductionist to abandon the reduction on its own terms."
- **Also strong**: L48's provenance caution on Brentano ("the tradition is *standardly claimed* as the lineage, not that Brentano held the modern theory") and L61–67's refusal to flatten the third-camp-versus-HOT-variant taxonomy dispute, which correctly identifies the substantive question underneath the labels ("is the awareness-conferring component itself conscious?").

### [concepts/phenomenal-constitution-thesis.md](/concepts/phenomenal-constitution-thesis/)
- **Strongest point**: the house-term disclosure at L31, in the article's second paragraph: "'PCT' is the Map's house term — a compression of the strong cognitive-phenomenology liberalism and the phenomenal-intentionality programme, defined here against the established literature rather than borrowed as an external term of art. **No major figure uses this exact phrase as a named position.**" A site that coins vocabulary and labels the coinage where a retrieving LLM will see it is protecting every downstream citation of the term.
- **Notable quote**: "The two deployments share a verb and nothing else; conflating them inverts the thesis." (L63)
- **Why it works**: the article builds a homonym firewall. L63 separates PCT's direction of constitution from the *inverse* direction used by grounding-and-constitution physicalists; L65 then rules out two further collisions (the temporal constitution thesis, and governance constitutions). This is the least glamorous and most useful kind of precision, and it is done pre-emptively rather than after someone got it wrong.
- **Also strong**: L35–43 imports the constitution relation with its three technical properties (non-identity, asymmetric non-causal dependence, hyperintensionality) sourced to Johnston 1992 and the SEP, so "constitutes" is doing defined work rather than serving as an intensifier.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

### The cluster is a zero-edge graph

A 5×5 link matrix over the five articles — full text, both wikilinks and prose mentions — returns **zero edges in every cell**. This is the same structural pattern the 2026-08-02 review found in the single-case dissociation cluster (open P3, `obsidian/topics/anarchic-hand-and-action-ownership.md`), and it has one especially sharp instance here:

- `phenomenal-constitution-thesis` L81 names "the Chinese Room verdict" as a conclusion **conditional on PCT**.
- `functionalism-argument` L107 runs the Chinese Room and concludes that its reach past semantics into phenomenal character is "a conjecture, not a corollary."

PCT is precisely the bridge premise whose absence makes that extension a conjecture. Each article states one half of a single thought; neither links to the other. Wiring these two is the highest-value edge in the cluster and costs two sentences.

### No article cites a position or a discipline page

None of the five contains a wikilink to any `positions/` entry, to `project/evidential-status-discipline`, to `concepts/possibility-probability-slippage`, or to `concepts/concession-convergence` — against a corpus baseline of **199 of 799 live articles (24.9%)** that cite at least one. These are the five articles in the corpus that most conspicuously *practise* those disciplines in prose.

### The register omits them in return

**Zero of the 14 files in `obsidian/positions/` reference `arguments/functionalism-argument` or `arguments/materialism-argument`.** The gap is selective rather than sectional: of the five live `arguments/` files, `epiphenomenalism-argument` is register-cited twice and `many-worlds-argument` once, while these two — the flagship anti-physicalist pair — are cited zero times.

The mismatch is substantive, not clerical:
