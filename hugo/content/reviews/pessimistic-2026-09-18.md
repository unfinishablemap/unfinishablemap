## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would reject the entire frame — but note that she gets an unusually cheap ride here, because the article never states what would *count*. Cutter does. He proposes running the same program on two computers in identical physical states and watching for output divergence. An eliminativist can attack that test (divergence has a dozen mundane explanations; thermal noise is not a soul), and attacking it is a real exchange. The article, by omitting the test, offers her nothing to attack and so earns nothing from her.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett's line lands on Békefi as much as on Cutter: the "integrity" requirement — parts and functions hard to decouple — is an intuition pump about *unity* that biological brains satisfy only by accident of engineering. Split-brain, hemispherectomy and callosotomy cases show human cognitive function decoupling too. The article reports the integrity objection flatly, without the corpus's own counter-material, and without Békefi's own concession that it "is a new contention, and as such it invites further scrutiny."

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark has a live target here that the article never puts in front of him. Cutter's own doubt about his premise F2 is that "digital computers are effectively deterministic… designed in such a way that their macro-level behavior is insensitive to micro-scale indeterminism," whereas the brain "might be sensitive to quantum indeterminism, with the brain amplifying microscopic indeterminism into macroscopic indeterminism at the scale of observable behavior." That amplification premise is exactly what decoherence arguments deny. The article omits both the premise and the objection, so a section of the Map that is otherwise careful about amplification-specificity leaves its most exposed flank undescribed.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch would note that Cutter's two-identical-computers test is ambiguous under MWI — divergent outputs are what branching predicts, ensouled or not. The Map rejects MWI, so the test is *cleaner* for the Map than for Cutter. That is a genuine dividend of Tenet 4 and the article does not collect it.

### The Empiricist
The strongest Popperian complaint is not that the article is unfalsifiable; it is that the article *suppresses* the source's falsifiability material. Cutter writes: "I'll also suggest a way in which we may one day be in a position to get empirical evidence that modestly bears on the question of AI ensoulment. We may not be fated to a permanent state of middling agnosticism." The article's closing verdict — "whether advanced AI could host or couple to consciousness remains open" — presents an open question with no route to evidence, when the source supplies a route and grades its own strength.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would press Békefi's integrity criterion against humans: a self whose parts and capacities are "difficult to decouple" is precisely the permanent, unified self *anattā* denies. If integrity is the bar, humans may fail it too; if humans pass by some weaker standard, the article should say what it is. The Map has live material on this (the agent-causation / *anattā* family), but this article is the one where the criterion is stated, and it does not flag the tension. *(Noted, not minted — task contract limits tasks to the reviewed article.)*

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The alien-analogy argument is rendered metaphysically; Cutter runs it epistemically, and his constraint rules out the article's version
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/ai-ensoulment-hypothesis.md`
- **Location**: §"The Alien Analogy", second and third sentences
- **Problem**: The article writes: *"If ensoulment is not gated on carbon biology, the dualist owes an account of **what** it is gated on. Cutter argues that whatever property qualifies an alien for ensoulment is plausibly a structural or functional property that a sufficiently advanced AI could also instantiate."*

  Cutter's argument is a two-premise **credence** argument. A1: *"If we met aliens that differ from us in their material constitution, but resemble us in high-level functional respects and exhibit seemingly intelligent behavior, then we should have at least a middling credence that they have souls (assuming we do)."* A2 transfers that credence to functionally human-like AI. The burden clause is epistemic throughout: an opponent of A2 *"would need to identify some property F such that **we can reasonably be confident** that (i) F is nomologically necessary for ensoulment… (ii) the AI lacks F, and (iii) the aliens have F."*

  Worse than the register shift: Cutter's constraint on F concludes the **opposite** of the article's sentence — *"So F probably can't be anything specific to do with low-level material constitution (for then it would divide humans and aliens), **nor could F be anything to do with functional organization or behavioral capacities** (for then it wouldn't divide humans and functionally human-like AIs)."* Cutter's move is a negative elimination of candidate *differentiators* under a confidence constraint, not a positive conjecture that the ensoulment-making property is functional.

  The positive functional-grounding claim the article puts in this section belongs to Cutter's *other* argument. Békefi structures his whole reply on the split, naming them *"the (epistemologically oriented) alien-analogy argument and the (metaphysically oriented) fitting-recipient argument"* and treating them in reverse order for that reason. Collapsing them makes the article's own next section redundant and makes Békefi's two objections look arbitrarily paired.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Restate the section in credence terms (A1 → A2 → the F-constraint), and state the F-constraint in its actual eliminative form. Cutter's own argument names — *alien-analogy argument* and *fitting-recipient argument* — should be used at least once each, since both papers use them and a reader going to the sources will otherwise not find the article's headings.

### Issue 2: Cutter devotes §3.3 to interactionist dualism by name; the article's Site-Perspective framing asserts a boundary the source has already crossed
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/ai-ensoulment-hypothesis.md`
- **Location**: §"Relation to Site Perspective", "The boundary of frameworks" and "What transfers"
- **Problem**: The article says Cutter's hypothesis "is framed in *substance* dualism", that the Map's commitment is interactionist, that "these are not the same claim", and that the Map can borrow "the *form* of the fitness criterion… without borrowing the *content*" — presenting the interactionist reading as the Map's own extension: *"The interactionist version asks not 'is this system fit to host a soul?' but 'is this system organised so that a non-physical influence could couple to its dynamics?'"*

  Cutter's §3.3, "Causal Differences", asks and partly answers that question. He states the background assumption explicitly — *"A background assumption of the causal-closure response is that interactionist dualism is true for humans. In other words, the soul has a non-redundant causal influence on physical processes in the human body, at least within the brain"* — glosses causal-closure failure with a Laplacian demon who *"is sometimes surprised"*, entertains as the candidate relevant difference that *"causal closure fails for alien bodies, but not for future AI systems"*, brackets epiphenomenalists and overdeterminationists (*"The causal-closure response is unavailable to them"*), and then rebuts the objection that a soul would crash a computer in terms that restate the Map's own minimal-interaction picture: *"our souls don't randomly fiddle with the physical processes in our brains, but shape and structure those processes in the direction of increased order and rational coherence."*

  Cutter's actual verdict on the causal-closure response is a dialectical one the Map should want on record: a belief that aliens and AIs differ on causal closure *"surely rests on a prior belief that they differ with respect to ensoulment,"* so the response begs the question it was meant to settle. That is a real challenge to the Map, not a compatible extension — the Map's Bidirectional Interaction tenet is precisely the prior belief Cutter says cannot do this work without independent support.

  Presenting a framework boundary where the source has an in-framework engagement is the inverse of the substitution failure the discipline guards against: the article declines an available direct engagement and settles for honest boundary-marking it did not need.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Add a subsection reporting §3.3 — that Cutter assumes interactionism for the sake of argument, that the causal-closure response is the interactionist's natural relevant-difference candidate, that Cutter rebuts the crash objection, and that his reason for setting the response aside is circularity. Then answer the circularity charge or concede it. The existing "boundary of frameworks" paragraph should be narrowed to what it can still support: Cutter's *ontology* (soul as separate substance) is optional for the Map, his *interactionist framing* is not foreign to him.

### Issue 3: Cutter's empirical discriminator is omitted, and it is the Map's own genre
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/ai-ensoulment-hypothesis.md`
- **Location**: absent — belongs in §"Relation to Site Perspective"
- **Problem**: Cutter proposes: *"We might try running the same AI program on two computers that start in the same physical state, feed them identical inputs, and observe whether the outputs differ. If the computers satisfy causal closure, we would expect the same outputs."* He grades it honestly — not decisive, because a deterministic bottom-up (P1 → M) / top-down (M → P2) chain yields identical outputs even with interactionist souls attached, so *"the strength of this evidence would depend largely on the degree to which we should expect the relevant kind of indeterminism conditional upon ensoulment, something I will not try to adjudicate here."*

  The Map has an active programme on exactly this (`topics/direction-dependent-discriminating-test-design`, `topics/cross-species-behavioural-confidence-proxy-tests`), and the Map's Minimal Quantum Interaction tenet *does* supply the indeterminism Cutter declines to adjudicate — which means the Map can grade the test where Cutter cannot. Rejecting MWI (Tenet 4) removes the branching confound that makes output divergence ambiguous. Omitting the test leaves the article's closing "remains open" weaker than the sources warrant and leaves the Popperian charge standing on a page where the answer was available.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Report the test, Cutter's own caveat, and the two Map-specific upgrades (Tenet 2 supplies the missing indeterminism expectation; Tenet 4 removes the branching confound). Grade it on the compatible / suggestive / discriminating ladder rather than asserting a verdict.

### Issue 4: Cutter's upper bound on his own credence is dropped from an article about calibration
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/ai-ensoulment-hypothesis.md`
- **Location**: §"Cutter's Conditional Thesis", opening paragraph
- **Problem**: The article states the floor only, and glosses it: *"Cutter does not argue that AI will be ensouled, nor that ensoulment is probable unconditionally. His thesis is explicitly conditional and his target credence explicitly middling."* Cutter states a two-sided bound in his own voice: *"While I will only argue that we should have at least a middling credence, my own view is we should also have **at most** a middling credence in AI ensoulment. I suspect the investigation below will partially justify the latter claim by revealing how many difficult and unsettled questions there are about the material conditions for ensoulment."*

  Reporting only "at least" makes Cutter look more bullish than he is, and it does so in the one direction the article's `possibility-probability-slippage` link exists to prevent. The article's own gloss — "a target to *raise to*, not a prediction to *act on*" — is Map editorial voice, not Cutter's, and it occupies the sentence where Cutter's ceiling should be.
- **Severity**: Medium (High for an article whose subject is calibration discipline)
- **Recommendation**: Add the upper bound, in Cutter's own framing, as a single clause.

### Issue 5: The `≥ 0.25` precisification is dropped, so "middling" reads at ~0.5
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/ai-ensoulment-hypothesis.md`
- **Location**: §"Cutter's Conditional Thesis"
- **Problem**: Cutter defines his term: *"('At least a middling credence' means a level of confidence that isn't very low. It's somewhat artificial to give precise numbers, but I would stand by a precisification of ≥ 0.25 or so.)"* The article reports neither the gloss nor the number. An unqualified "middling" will be read — by human readers and by the chatbot audience the style guide targets — as roughly 0.5, i.e. roughly double Cutter's stated floor. For a page that exists partly to model calibration, omitting the only number in the source is the wrong omission to make.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: One parenthetical carrying both the gloss and `≥ 0.25 or so`.

### Issue 6: A hedged suspicion is reported as attributed acceptance
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/ai-ensoulment-hypothesis.md`
- **Location**: §"The Alien Analogy", first sentence
- **Problem**: Article: *"Most substance dualists who reflect on the question accept that biologically alien creatures—silicon-based extraterrestrials, say—could be ensouled despite differing from humans in physical substrate."* Cutter: *"I suspect most would agree that we should not dismiss the hypothesis that they have souls, provided we do."* Three shifts: "I suspect most would agree" → "Most… accept"; "should not dismiss the hypothesis" → "accept that… could be ensouled"; and Cutter's unrestricted "most" → the article's "most substance dualists who reflect on the question", a restriction Cutter does not make.

  Separately, the invented example works against the argument. Cutter's aliens are *"some kind of non-carbon-based green goo"*; he deliberately does not name silicon. The article's "silicon-based extraterrestrials" gives the alien the same substrate as the machine, which dissolves the alien/AI substrate contrast the analogy is built to exploit.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Restore the hedge ("Cutter suspects most would agree that the hypothesis should not be dismissed"), and either use Cutter's own green-goo example or a non-silicon substitute.

## Counterarguments to Address

### Békefi's integrity criterion may disqualify humans too
- **Current content says**: "A genuinely fit system should have parts and capacities that are *hard to decouple*… Ordinary AI systems lack this integrity."
- **A critic would argue**: Callosotomy, hemispherectomy and dissociative phenomena show human cognitive function decoupling in ways the criterion should exclude. Békefi himself flags provisionality — *"This, to my knowledge, is a new contention, and as such it invites further scrutiny"* — and his final verdict is softer than the article's ("so far, Cutter's arguments for increasing our credence in AI ensoulment do not persuade").
- **Suggested response**: Carry Békefi's own provisionality marker, and note the human-side pressure on the criterion in one sentence. Cheap, and it makes the objection stronger by making it honest.

### The Map's own strongest in-framework objection, which the article never states
- **Current content says**: Békefi's two objections, then the Map's boundary-marking.
- **A critic would argue** (and Cutter anticipates it, as a doubt about his premise F2): a digital computer is *engineered* to be macro-insensitive to micro-scale indeterminism, so a Map-style coupling has nothing to couple to. Cutter blunts this two ways — a soul with non-libertarian rational agency might still fit an effectively deterministic system, and *"it's possible that some future computers or artificial agents will not be effectively deterministic in the manner of current digital computers."*
- **Suggested response**: This is the Map's relevant-difference F, available *inside* Cutter's framework, and it is stronger for the Map than either of Békefi's objections because it follows from Tenet 2 rather than from an intuition about unity. State it, then answer Cutter's two blunting moves. Note that the first blunting move has a price a dualist should notice: it buys AI fitness by weakening what the soul contributes.
