## Executive Summary

The wing's strength is that it holds four *different* honest postures toward four different rivals, and each posture is stated as a posture rather than smuggled in as a verdict: endorse-the-physics/reject-the-interpretation (Zurek), mine-the-insight/decline-the-frame (Fuchs), borrow-under-conditions (GRW/CSL), and closest-competitor-not-defeated (Kastrup). ## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The analytic-idealism article does the thing Chalmers's own underdetermination arguments demand and almost nobody performs: it grants that "the phenomenal data that motivate the mind-centric turn do not adjudicate" between idealism and dualism, and books the Map's preference to "theoretical-virtue and tenet-grounded reasons" rather than to evidence (L90). It even concedes the tie-breaker experience — stone resisting foot — "is available to the idealist" under a different reading (L136). That is the hard problem taken seriously *without* the false comfort of thinking it settles which non-physicalism wins. The QBism article earns matching praise for the co-optation firewall at L103: Fuchs's primitive agent is "not a dualist subject in disguise," and the Map's reading is "an avowed reinterpretation, not an extraction of something QBism already holds." Chalmers would also note the quantum-darwinism article's care to keep his explanatory gap distinct from the outcome gap rather than borrowing one to inflate the other (L102).

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The spontaneous-collapse article's three-pictures taxonomy (L44–48) is the cleanest statement in the corpus of why the Map's consciousness-*modulated* middle differs from von Neumann–Wigner consciousness-*caused* collapse: "remove all minds from the universe and physics still produces definite outcomes. Consciousness is a participant, not a prerequisite." And the quantum-darwinism article gives Stapp the constraint architecture his own programme lacked: einselection fixes the menu, the Born rule fixes the statistics, redundant encoding fixes intersubjective agreement, so "the proposed intervention is minimal precisely *because* quantum Darwinism has done most of the work" (L80). Stapp would also record, with professional grace, that his Zeno route is kept live as the named fallback if collapse models are falsified (stakes L90) — demotion with a re-elevation condition, exactly how the register (P-Q4) books it.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The QBism article's experience presupposition is a what-it-is-like argument compressed to one clause: agents "must *find* something, not merely exist in correlation with outcomes" (L61). That is the difference between registration and experience stated without jargon. The idealism article's "What's at Stake" section keeps the first-person where Nagel insists it belongs — "Why does *your* suffering matter distinctly from suffering-in-general?" (L124) — and its handling of witness-consciousness reports shows the filter framework absorbing contemplative phenomenology without surrendering the individual (L126). Nagel would also endorse the quantum-darwinism article's refusal to let objectivity-as-emergent swallow the subject: redundant encoding explains why observers *agree*; it does not explain why there is anyone for whom the agreement is an experience.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Einselection is the most Whiteheadian result in modern physics — classicality not as given substance but as *achievement*, states surviving by ongoing environmental process, objectivity constituted by proliferating records — and the quantum-darwinism article presents it exactly so, down to the Darwinian vocabulary of fitness-relative-to-environment (L44–46). The QBism article's Jamesian genealogy (pragmatism, participatory realism, experience as the working ground of physics) extends the same sensibility into epistemics. Per this persona's constraint, none of that resonance is evidence for anything, and the wing already knows it: the quantum-darwinism article marks the selector reading "a contested interpretive commitment, not an uncontroversial consequence of the formalism" at both ends of the article (L38, L72), which is the "tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating" pattern applied to process-friendliness itself. Nothing here needs flagging; the constraint is being honoured unprompted.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

The stakes article hands Kane his physical precondition in one disciplined paragraph: GRW/CSL stochasticity "provides the openness that libertarian free will requires. Consciousness does not need to override deterministic laws; it modulates genuinely open processes" (L60) — indeterminism sourced from a live physics programme, not from wishful reading of quantum folklore. The same article immediately prices the ad-hoc-parameters objection against the very models it just leaned on (L62), which is how a libertarian keeps the physics honest. One caution Kane should hear from the register rather than from this wing: the causal work these paragraphs describe is graded coherence-only under the mechanism-debt convention (`positions/quantum-interface` P-Q3/P-Q10), and the article does not yet say so — see Calibration Concerns §3.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The quantum-darwinism article closes its falsifiability section with a sentence McGinn could have written: "the consciousness-selection hypothesis sits at the boundary of empirical accessibility — motivated by a real gap in physics, but not yet subject to decisive experimental adjudication" (L118). The idealism article ends on the same note of principled undecidability: the dualism-versus-idealism disagreement "turns on theoretical commitment rather than empirical verdict" (L136). Neither article converts humility into surrender — both name what *would* move the question (a no-go theorem closing the improper/proper gap; a coherence result) — which is humility of the working kind, not the decorative kind.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This wing carries the Map's most tenet-loaded physics claims, so this persona's verdict matters, and it is largely favourable — with the exceptions priced in Calibration Concerns. Three pieces of restraint deserve naming. First, the quantum-darwinism falsifiability paragraph states the confirmation asymmetry outright: "refutable (a deviation kills it) but not symmetrically confirmable... the empirical content lives entirely on the refutation side" (L114) — the vacuity worry and the falsifiability claim held as "two faces of one fact" instead of the usual move of citing one while suppressing the other. Second, the stakes article's stakes are explicitly conditionalised: "The conditional is load-bearing... the stakes the Map draws are therefore conditional on an interpretive choice the evidence does not yet decide" (L54) — an article about philosophical payoff that declines to bank the payoff. Third, the idealism article relabels its own third diagnostic from finding to commitment: "this is not a neutral diagnostic — it reflects the Map's commitment that indexical identity matters. Frameworks that dissolve individual identity are not thereby refuted" (L114). That is a tier-honesty move performed on the article's own taxonomy, unforced by any reviewer. The debit side: the stakes article's Chalmers–McQueen enthusiasm and parameter-modulation framing predate the register and now read one band more confident than P-Q1/P-Q2 license, and its Relation section asserts "a concrete mechanism for consciousness causally influencing the physical world" without the mechanism-debt grade the register imposes downstream. That is pre-register calibration, not fresh slippage — the fix is an alignment pass, minted below, not a rewrite.

### concepts/qbism
- **Strongest point**: the structural version of the agent problem — not "QBism leaves consciousness unexplained" (true of everything) but "QBism alone *needs* experiencing agents to make sense... the gap isn't incidental but structural" (L133), with the Copenhagen/MWI/Bohm contrast doing real work.

- **Why it works**: "located the mystery" is precisely calibrated — it credits QBism with progress (location) while withholding what QBism has not earned (dissolution), and the L103 firewall stops the Map from claiming Fuchs as an ally.

### topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse
- **Strongest point**: the flash-versus-matter-density ontology section (L64–72) — the only place in the corpus that asks what the Map's interface claim *means* under each GRW ontology ("If reality consists of flashes, consciousness selects *which events occur*. If reality is a density field, consciousness selects *where density concentrates*").
- **Notable quote**: "These are not just different equations. They embody different answers to the question of whether minds matter to the physical world."
- **Why it works**: it converts a technical underdetermination (Bell flashes vs Ghirardi density) into a live question for the Map's own semantics, which is exactly what a stakes article is for.

### topics/analytic-idealism-and-mind-centric-metaphysics

- **Why it works**: naming the strongest rival as undefeated ("a coherent alternative the Map has not empirically defeated," L136) costs the article nothing and buys it the credibility every over-claiming survey forfeits.

## Calibration Concerns

These are flagged as concerns, not strengths, per the skill contract; the first two are minted as tasks below.

1. **Zurek 2009 quote fails the arXiv source** (`topics/quantum-darwinism-and-consciousness` L54). Article: "The state of the system can be found out independently and indirectly by many observers, who will agree about it" (cited Zurek 2009, p. 183). The arXiv source of that paper (0903.5082, `QDnat44.tex`, figure caption — the only "agree" locus in the file) reads: "The state of the system can be found out **indirectly and independently** by many observers, who will agree about **their conclusions**." Two deviations in one span: adverbs transposed, tail rewritten. The published *Nature Physics* text was not reachable this run, so publisher-side confirmation is still owed before correcting — but a span that fails its preprint at two points is presumptively unfaithful.
2. **Zurek 2003 quote absent from the cited work** (same file, L62). Article: Zurek acknowledges that decoherence "does not, by itself, solve the measurement problem" (Zurek 2003). The arXiv source of the cited RMP paper (quant-ph/0105127, final 2005 revision) contains no such span; its only nearby sentence leans the other way: "while decoherence — through einselection — helps solve the measurement problem, it is also a major obstacle to quantum information processing." The *substance* attributed to Zurek is defensible from his other writings and from Schlosshauer, so the fix is re-anchoring, not deletion — but the verbatim-plus-citation as it stands fails verification.
3. **Register drift in the stakes article** (`topics/philosophical-stakes-of-spontaneous-collapse`, last substantive pass 06-05, deep review 07-16 — the whole `positions/quantum-interface` register as currently written postdates both). The Relation section's "concrete mechanism for consciousness causally influencing the physical world" lacks the mechanism-debt citation grade (P-Q3/P-Q10: coherence-only downstream until the toy-model desiderata are met). None of the four wing files links `positions/` at all; this is the file where it costs.
