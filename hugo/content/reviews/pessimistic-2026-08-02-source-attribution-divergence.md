## Executive Summary

The article's calibration apparatus is genuinely good — it marks the option-2 cost pre-emptively, concedes its own membership in the cluster it cites, and defers the cluster-level discharge to the apex on common-cause-null grounds. The defects sit underneath that apparatus, in the argument the apparatus is calibrating. Three are structural and mutually reinforcing: (1) the article asserts matched task performance while its Empirical Signatures section documents the opposite, breaking the parallel with `aphantasia` and `synaesthesia` on which the whole cluster membership depends; (2) *both* surviving branches of its trichotomy fail to deliver the anti-functionalist conclusion the article draws from them — option 1 has phenomenology co-varying with function (the functionalist's prediction), and option 3 converts an access limit into a metaphysical falsification; (3) the article erects an explicit methodological firewall ("our evidence is external cohort-level performance measures, not first-person reports") and then lists a pure self-report study on the evidence side of it. A fourth, quieter defect: the article's foundational claim that the spread is a stable trait rather than noise is asserted at the lead and never evidenced by any cited work.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

"You have written a very careful article that says: people are bad at knowing where their thoughts came from, they are bad in ways that vary, and this variation is measurable in the third person. I agree with all of it. Then you appended the word *phenomenal* to 'source-tag' fourteen times and declared victory over functionalism.

Notice what the source-monitoring framework you cite actually says: memories carry no native source tags, and origin is *inferred* from contextual features. That is a complete, mechanistic, tag-free account. Johnson and colleagues built it without your posit. Your 'phenomenal source-tag' is a theoretical entity introduced solely to have something for a dualist story to be about; the framework you lean on is the strongest available evidence that no such entity is needed. You have cited the demolition of your own posit as support for it."

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

"Option 3 is mine, and you have handed it to me while calling it a defeat. You write that under option 3 'the simple identification of phenomenal source-content with functional source-tagging fails ... from the introspective-access side.' That inference is invalid on its face. If introspection is an unreliable guide to source-tagging, we lose the ability to *check* whether phenomenal source-content matches functional source-tagging. Losing the ability to check a claim is not falsifying it.

Worse for you: unreliable introspection is exactly what my position predicts. Introspection is itself a further functional process — a downstream consumer with limited and lossy access to the machinery it reports on. It misreports for the same reason any monitoring subsystem misreports. Your option 3 is my house. You cannot list it as one of two roads to my defeat.

And your option 1 is worse still. You define it as 'phenomenal source-tags differ across the spectrum, *and accuracy tracks this phenomenal difference*.' Phenomenology co-varying with functional competence is my prediction, not my counterexample. The aphantasia wedge works because performance is *matched*. Yours is not. You have built a trichotomy in which one branch confirms functionalism, one branch is compatible with it, and the third branch you have already conceded is 'the case under examination.'"

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

"No quantum claims here to check, which is to your credit — the article stays in its lane. But apply the same standard you would demand of me. I am told decoherence calculations must be *done*, not gestured at. So: where is the psychometric calculation? You claim a population spread that is 'not noise around a uniform competence.' That is a variance-decomposition claim. It requires test-retest reliability coefficients, a between-subject variance component, and a demonstration that the reliable variance is not measurement error.

You cite Loftus and Palmer, Johansson, Brown and Murphy, Marsh and Bower. Every one is a group-mean condition effect. None reports a reliability coefficient. There is a known trap here: experimental tasks optimised to produce robust group effects systematically minimise between-subject variance, and therefore have poor test-retest reliability — the very tasks that most reliably show an effect are the worst instruments for individual differences. Your entire article is an individual-differences claim built on a scaffold of group-effect paradigms, and you have not addressed the objection that the scaffold cannot bear it."

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

"Little contact with my position, but one methodological remark. You are impressed by convergence across three cases — aphantasia, synaesthesia, source attribution. You then concede, admirably, that you are *inside* the cluster you are counting and defer to the common-cause null at the apex. Having made that concession, you should notice it applies one level down as well. Within this single article you list five 'Empirical Signatures' and treat them as five signatures. Reality-monitoring, imagery correlations, and the aphantasia covariance are not independent; they are one imagery-related finding entered three times. Your count of the evidence is inflated by the same double-counting the apex taught you to distrust."

### The Empiricist (Popper's ghost)

"State the falsifier. You write that the wedge 'survives both available readings' and that 'either way, the simple identification ... fails.' An argument constructed so that every branch of its own trichotomy terminates in the same conclusion has not been tested by the trichotomy. What finding about source attribution would count against you?

You come close to an answer and then walk away from it. If the source-monitoring framework is right that source is *inferred* from ordinary contextual features by ordinary cognitive machinery — encoding depth, attention, working memory, all of which you name — then the finding is fully explained without phenomenal source-tags. You concede at the Site Perspective section that 'the data here are also fully compatible with reconstructive-cognition physicalism.' Good. But then the earlier sentence — the finding *establishes*, not merely suggests — cannot stand. You cannot say the data establish a conclusion in section six and are fully compatible with its denial in section eight."

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

"This article comes nearer my position than any other in your catalogue, and then flinches. Your source-monitoring framework says there are no native tags; origin is a construction, assembled after the fact from whatever features are to hand. That is exactly the analysis of the self I would give: no owner, only a process of attribution running continuously and mistaking its own output for a discovery.

You have found that the sense of *mineness* is manufactured. The correct conclusion is that the manufacturer is empty too. Instead you propose that the manufacturing varies because minds differ in their *phenomenal source-tag configuration* — you have replaced one reified tag with a distribution of them. And in the closing speculation you go further, suggesting the variation tracks 'architectural differences in interface configuration.' You began with the discovery that thoughts do not come stamped with an owner and ended by proposing a subtler owner behind the stamp."

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The matched-performance premise contradicts the article's own Empirical Signatures section
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/source-attribution-divergence.md`
- **Location**: lead §2 (line 41) and "Why Single-Species Variation Matters" (line 115) vs. "Empirical Signatures" (line 77)
- **Problem**: The lead claims cluster membership on the basis of "same biological species, same neural architecture, **same external task performance at standard behavioural grain**, divergent inner architecture", and line 115 repeats it as "approximate task performance at standard behavioural grain". Line 77 states the opposite: "Population scores form a distribution, not a uniform high-accuracy band; healthy adults vary from near-perfect source recovery to near-chance."

  This is not a hedging mismatch, it is a structural break. The wedge at the sibling articles is earned *by* behavioural matching, and both siblings say so explicitly — `aphantasia`: "the same task performance ... They differ in inner life"; `synaesthesia`: "missing phenomenal content under matched task-level performance ... *extra* phenomenal content under matched representational input". In source-attribution divergence the measured dependent variable **is** task accuracy. There is no matched-performance baseline against which a phenomenal difference could stand out, because the accuracy difference is the phenomenon. The hedge "at standard behavioural grain" cannot rescue the claim: source-monitoring accuracy *is* standard behavioural grain.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Do not paper this over with a stronger hedge. Either (a) state plainly that this leg differs in kind from its siblings — the divergence is in *calibration*, and the article's contribution to the cluster is a claim about introspective access rather than a matched-performance phenomenal contrast — and rewrite the lead's cluster parallel accordingly; or (b) identify a genuinely matched sub-case (equal source-monitoring accuracy, divergent confidence profiles) and rest the wedge only on that. Option (a) is the honest and cheaper route, and it is the route `synaesthesia` already models when it concedes the van Leeuwen pipeline-identity caveat costs the wedge "its cleanest premise".

### Issue 2: Option 1 does not run the wedge the article says it runs
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/source-attribution-divergence.md`
- **Location**: "The Function-Phenomenology Wedge—Stress-Tested", options list (line 95) and conclusion (line 107)
- **Problem**: Option 1 is defined as "The phenomenal source-tags differ across the spectrum, **and accuracy tracks this phenomenal difference**." Line 107 then concludes: "Under the option-1 reading, source-attribution divergence runs as direct phenomenal divergence and the wedge against functionalism runs against the simple identification."

  It does not. Phenomenal difference accompanied by a matched functional difference is precisely what functionalism predicts. The anti-functionalist wedge requires phenomenal divergence *under functional equivalence*; option 1 supplies phenomenal divergence under functional *divergence*, which is the functionalist's own account of why the phenomenology differs. Option 1 is the pro-functionalist branch of the trichotomy, and the article has scored it as an anti-functionalist one.

  Note the knock-on: the article's careful treatment of option 2 (line 99, correctly identifying that uniform phenomenology plus varying machinery decouples phenomenology from function) makes option 2 the branch that actually presses functionalism — yet it is labelled "the default functionalist response". The trichotomy's scoring is inverted at two of three branches.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Re-score the trichotomy. Option 2 is the branch that costs the functionalist (and the article already identifies the cost correctly); option 1 as currently worded is a functionalist-friendly reading and should be presented as such. If the intent was that phenomenal tags differ while *task-level* performance is matched, that must be stated in the option's own wording — but see Issue 1, since the article's evidence base does not supply matched performance.

### Issue 3: Option 3 converts an epistemic limit into a metaphysical falsification
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/source-attribution-divergence.md`
- **Location**: lines 105 and 107
- **Problem**: This is the equivocation the [evidential-status-discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/) check is designed to catch, and it is load-bearing. Option 3 is an *epistemic* claim: introspective reports about source-attribution structure are unreliable. Line 107 cashes it as a *metaphysical* result: "option 3 [falsifies the simple identification] from the introspective-access side."

  Unreliable access to X does not falsify a claim about X — it removes our warrant for claims in either direction. And the specific position on offer is hospitable to the functionalist rather than damaging: if introspection is itself a further functional process with lossy access to the machinery it monitors, systematic misreport is exactly what a functionalist expects. The article half-sees this at line 101 ("option 3 is *the case under examination*") but discharges the knot in the wrong direction.

  The firewall the article builds at line 103 is the right *kind* of move — separating first-order phenomenal reports from second-order cohort-level calibration measures — and it does successfully protect the *empirical* finding (confidence dissociates from accuracy in externally measurable ways). What it cannot do is bridge from that finding to a conclusion about phenomenal *content*. Confidence-accuracy dissociation is evidence about the calibration of a metacognitive judgement. The conclusion drawn is about what phenomenology contains. The bridging argument is not supplied, and line 105's italicised "*establishes*, not merely suggests" is the point where the missing bridge is papered over with emphasis.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Split the readings explicitly. The defensible claim is: *cohort-level confidence-accuracy dissociation shows that introspective reports about source are at best partial guides to the machinery* — an access claim, and a real one. The claim that must be dropped or separately argued is that this falsifies the identification of phenomenal source-content with functional source-tagging. Drop "*establishes*, not merely suggests". Then reconcile with line 123, which already concedes the data "are also fully compatible with reconstructive-cognition physicalism" — the two sentences cannot both stand.

### Issue 4: Dawes et al. (2020) is mis-framed, and the mis-framing breaches the article's own methodological firewall
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/source-attribution-divergence.md`
- **Location**: "Empirical Signatures", Imagery-spectrum correlations (line 83)
- **Problem**: The article writes: "**Aphantasia-correlated reality-monitoring differences (Dawes et al. 2020)** and imagery-vividness correlations with false-memory susceptibility connect the imagery spectrum to the source-attribution spectrum at population scale."

  Verified against the published abstract (OpenAlex, DOI 10.1038/s41598-020-65705-7): the study administered multi-sensory imagery measures, autobiographical-memory and imagined-future-scenario richness, dream reports, spatial ability, and trauma symptomatology. It measured **no** reality-monitoring task, **no** source-monitoring task, and **no** false-memory susceptibility. Every relevant finding is reported as self-report — "aphantasic individuals **report** decreased imagery", "they **report** less vivid and phenomenologically rich autobiographical memories".

  Two defects follow. First, the construct attributed to the source is not in it: "reality-monitoring differences" is not a Dawes finding. Second, and more damaging, this is one of five items in the section the article's firewall (line 103) characterises as "*external, cohort-level performance measures* ... **not** first-order phenomenal reports". Dawes et al. is exactly a first-order phenomenal-report study. The firewall that discharges the option-3 knot is breached by the evidence listed behind it.

  For contrast, line 73 uses the same source correctly ("aphantasics often report reduced autobiographical-memory richness") — the defect is localised to line 83, which makes it a clean fix.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Re-frame, do not delete (the source is real and the covariance is real). Restate line 83 as a self-report covariance between imagery vividness and remembered-experience richness, and either (a) drop it from the performance-measure firewall's support, or (b) locate an actual imagery-vividness × reality-monitoring-accuracy or × false-memory-susceptibility study and cite that for the performance claim. The "imagery-vividness correlations with false-memory susceptibility" clause currently carries no citation at all and needs one either way.

### Issue 6: The trait premise — the article's foundation — is asserted and never evidenced
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/source-attribution-divergence.md`
- **Location**: lead (line 39), typology close (line 61), cryptomnesia signature (line 81)
- **Problem**: Line 39 states flatly: "The variation is not noise around a uniform competence." Line 61 goes further: "Strong performance on one is weak prediction of strong performance on others" — a claim about inter-task correlations. Line 81 asserts that cryptomnesia susceptibility "varies across individuals and tracks broader source-monitoring competence."

  Every cited empirical work is a group-level or condition-level demonstration. Loftus and Palmer (1974) is a misinformation-effect demonstration. Johansson et al. (2005) — abstract verified — reports that participants "failed to notice" manipulations, with no individual-differences or stability analysis. Brown and Murphy (1989) and Marsh and Bower (1993) manipulated cognitive load. Nisbett and Wilson (1977) reviews aggregate dissociations. Johnson, Hashtroudi and Lindsay (1993) is a theoretical framework paper. None reports test-retest reliability, a between-subject variance component, or a cross-task correlation matrix.

  This matters more than a routine missing-citation flag, because the standing psychometric objection cuts directly against the inference: experimental paradigms optimised to produce robust *group* effects systematically suppress between-subject variance and consequently show poor test-retest reliability. "Population scores form a distribution" is true of any noisy measure; the article needs the distribution to be *reliable* variance, and that is exactly what its sources do not report. Line 81's attribution of an individual-differences correlation to two condition-manipulation studies is the sharpest instance.
- **Severity**: Medium-High
- **Recommendation**: Either cite psychometric / latent-variable individual-differences work on source monitoring that reports reliability, or downgrade the framing throughout: the cited literature establishes that source attribution is *fallible and condition-sensitive*, which is enough for much of the article, and stop short of the trait claim. Line 61's inter-task independence claim needs a source or should be marked as a conjecture. Line 81's "tracks broader source-monitoring competence" clause should be cut unless sourced. Adding the reliability objection and answering it would strengthen the article considerably — it is the objection a hostile psychologist reaches for first.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The source-monitoring framework is a tag-free account, and the article leans on it
- **Current content says**: source attribution is "irreducibly inferential, probabilistic" (line 125), citing Johnson, Hashtroudi and Lindsay (1993) for the claim that "memories lack native source tags; origin is inferred from contextual, sensory, semantic, emotional, and cognitive-operation features" (line 69).
- **A critic would argue**: that framework is a complete mechanistic account of the phenomena the article catalogues, and it posits no phenomenal source-tags at all. The article's central theoretical entity is one its own primary source dispenses with. Every finding listed under Empirical Signatures is predicted by the framework without the posit.
- **Suggested response**: this is answerable and the answer is already half-present at line 127 — the framework explains the *inference* and its variation; it does not address whether the resulting confidence is *experienced*, which is where the Map's interest lies. But the article must make the concession explicit rather than leaving the framework standing as unqualified support. As written it reads as citing the framework in its own favour when the framework is neutral at best.

### The reliability paradox
- **Current content says**: "The variation is not noise around a uniform competence" (line 39).
- **A critic would argue**: the cited paradigms were designed to maximise group-level effects, which suppresses between-subject variance and yields poor test-retest reliability. Observed spread in such tasks is substantially measurement error. The article's entire individual-differences reading may be reading noise as architecture.
- **Suggested response**: cite reliability evidence, or restrict the claim to the paradigms where stable individual differences have actually been demonstrated, and state the restriction.

### Within-article convergence double-counting
- **Current content says**: five "Empirical Signatures" are listed as distinct lines of evidence (lines 77–85).
- **A critic would argue**: reality-monitoring variation, imagery-spectrum correlations, and the aphantasia covariance are not independent signatures; they are one imagery-related finding entered three times. The article applies the common-cause-null discipline scrupulously at the cluster level (line 111) and not at all at the signature level one layer down.
- **Suggested response**: apply the same discipline internally — group the imagery-related signatures as one line of evidence, and say so. This costs the article nothing and is consistent with the calibration standard it already meets elsewhere.
