## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
The page treats "the arriving is never observed" as marking a place where consciousness reaches something beyond it. Its own best empirical source says the suddenness is a feature of the report, and that the lead-up is a measurable series of ordinary brain states. A void that neuroscience has already begun to chart from the outside is a gap in introspection, which is what the eliminativist said all along. (Bedrock as to the framing; the misdescription of the source is the actionable part, item 2.)

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
"Surprise at one's own thought" is offered as paradoxical "if they produced them". It is paradoxical only on the assumption that the producer and the reporter are one transparent thing. Nisbett and Wilson, cited two paragraphs later, is the standard evidence that they are not. The page uses the same paper as support for a void and ignores that it dissolves the paradox.

### The Quantum Skeptic
The Minimal Quantum Interaction paragraph proposes that "representing the interaction might interfere with it" at femtosecond timescales. It is flagged as speculative, and that is the most that can be said for it: no quantity is named that would be disturbed, and insight research measures effects 0.3 s wide.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
"When Poincaré's solution arrived, it arrived to *this* consciousness" is true on every interpretation, each branch included. The paragraph then concedes that imagination's unreliability "cuts both ways". With that concession the paragraph claims nothing against branching; it only says the Map's reading needs definite outcomes.

### The Empiricist
"Persistence despite effort" counts centuries of criticism and "millennia of introspective practice" as tests the void has survived. No study is cited, and the one experimental literature the page does cite reports progress on exactly the process it calls inaccessible. The claim "Novel qualia: impossible. … The failure is total" has no source at all; Hume, the page's own authority, held that a never-seen shade can be raised by imagination.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
The page reads felt externality as "reception from a source consciousness cannot access", which keeps a receiver and a source. The contemplative reading of the same report is that no owner was ever producing thoughts; the surprise marks the absence of a producer, with nothing hidden behind it.

## Counterarguments to Address

### Process opacity as evidence of a void
- **Current content says**: selection happens unobserved, so the creative mechanism is constitutively hidden, possibly because observing would interfere.
- **A critic would argue**: opacity of process to introspection is the general condition of cognition (retrieval, parsing, face recognition), and the insight literature the page cites tracks the hidden stage from outside. A limit on first-person access to a process is an epistemic fact; "constitutively hidden" and "a source consciousness cannot access" are metaphysical readings the evidence does not reach.
- **Suggested response**: say which reading each piece of evidence supports. Poincaré and Kounios and Beeman support the access limit. The stronger reading rests on the Map's commitments and is already marked as such at L186; the body should carry the same marking.
