# External referee report: “The Consciousness-Value Connection”

**Recommendation: major revision.**

The article has a defensible and philosophically interesting core: **some phenomenal states plausibly instantiate intrinsic value rather than merely represent or cause it**. Rawlette is genuine support for that proposition, and recent work such as Dimitrov strengthens one version of it. But the article repeatedly moves from that comparatively local thesis to a much stronger exclusivity claim—**only consciousness can ground any intrinsic value**—without supplying the additional argument needed. It then makes further moves from necessity to sufficiency: that every conscious being thereby has moral status, and even that wherever experience exists, value exists. Those are distinct theses. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

The current changelog is live through 8 August and reports zero *direct* contradictions with the five tenets across its latest corpus-wide check. That is compatible with my findings: the problems here are principally invalid implication, asymmetric burdens of proof, underpowered rival selection, and semantic drift between neighboring articles—not an article explicitly denying a tenet. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

## 1. The central argument does not establish its advertised conclusion

The strongest part is the phenomenal-value claim. Rawlette's book is real and its published description explicitly presents pleasure and pain as **instantiations**, not merely indicators, of value and disvalue. Dimitrov's 2025 argument likewise claims that introspection of suffering can acquaint us directly with intrinsic badness. But Dimitrov carefully makes the case depend on controversial assumptions, including rejection of strong representationalism. [Google Books+2OUP Academic+2](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Feeling_of_Value.html)

That supports something like:

**Some phenomenal properties are intrinsically evaluative.**

It does **not** by itself support:

**Nothing non-phenomenal can possess intrinsic value.**

The article slides between them. Its phenomenal-value-realism route says that *some* phenomenal properties just are value properties, then immediately concludes that consciousness supplies "the very stuff of value" and hence is necessary for all value. No exclusion argument has appeared. Showing that pain contains intrinsic badness does not show that truth, beauty, achievement, ecological integrity, relationships, or other states of affairs cannot possess value independently of being experienced. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

The two-step "mattering" argument fares worse. "Value requires mattering"; "mattering is a relation"; therefore intrinsic value requires a perspective. But the controversial premise is hidden in the definition of *mattering*. An objective-value theorist precisely denies that all goodness simpliciter must reduce to goodness-for-a-subject. The article therefore does not refute objective value; it defines "value" so that objective value is excluded before the argument begins. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

G. E. Moore's famous beautiful-world comparison is almost tailor-made for this article and is considerably stronger than the generic "Platonic Form of the Good" objection it chooses instead. Moore asks us to compare an exceedingly beautiful and an exceedingly ugly world that can never be contemplated by anybody, and argues that the beautiful world can still be better in itself. Whatever one thinks of Moore's intuition, it attacks exactly the disputed premise rather than positing abstract Platonic objects. [Project Gutenberg+1](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/53430/53430-h/53430-h.htm)

Contemporary alternatives need not be Platonist either. Fitting-attitude/buck-passing views analyse goodness in terms of there being reasons to favour something. Such theories provide an obvious route to evaluative objectivity without postulating a causally active "Form of the Good." [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/6243/chapter/149872279)

There is a second conflation around "good for." The article says functional interests are not genuine mattering and that there can be no good or bad "for" a non-conscious system. David Benatar's 2026 treatment of sentience reaches a sentience-friendly conclusion while explicitly rejecting that argument: he distinguishes functional, biotic and conscious interests, allows that things can objectively be good or bad for a non-conscious car or plant, and argues separately that those interests are not the *right kind* to confer moral standing. [Cambridge University Press+1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cambridge-quarterly-of-healthcare-ethics/article/sentience-and-why-it-matters/4225ECED8AA5DF00B7959E1BFCB0CF13)

That distinction would substantially improve the Map's position. It could concede objective functional/biotic "good-for" relations while maintaining that **welfare in the morally relevant sense requires experience**. The present formulation needlessly makes the much more vulnerable semantic claim that nothing can be good or bad for anything unconscious.

## 2. The article reverses its own implication

This is the clearest formal problem.

Its advertised thesis is:

**Value → consciousness**: if something has intrinsic value, consciousness must be involved.

But several implications use:

**Consciousness → value**.

The ethics section says "Conscious beings matter"; the panpsychism section says that if micro-experience exists then value begins at the fundamental level and concludes that "wherever experience goes, value follows." Neither follows from consciousness being **necessary** for value. They require consciousness to be **sufficient** for value. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

Recent literature makes this distinction especially hard to ignore. Joshua Shepherd's 2024 paper explicitly distinguishes valence sentientism, broad sentientism and views on which consciousness is not necessary for non-derivative moral significance. Mattia Cecchinato's June 2026 paper argues more specifically that *affective* consciousness—pleasure, pain and emotion—rather than phenomenality as such grounds welfare and moral status. The hypothetical conscious-but-affectless "Vulcan" is therefore an obvious stress test for the Map's inference from consciousness simpliciter to moral status. [Springer Link+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-023-01835-6)

This matters especially for panpsychism. Even if an electron had some extremely thin micro-experience, it does not follow that its experience is valenced, beautiful, meaningful, agentive, or otherwise valuable. The article's panpsychism paragraph establishes almost the converse of the thesis it says it is illustrating.

## 3. Citation integrity: real, but not adequately current or well deployed

I found **no fabricated item in the six-entry bibliography**. Rawlette 2016, Wolf 2010, Nagel 1986, Metz 2013, Frankish 2016 and Chalmers 1996 are all genuine works, and the basic bibliographic metadata is substantially correct. The live article also correctly no longer puts quotation marks around the Rawlette paraphrase; the Map's July 16 review identified that earlier quote-fidelity defect and removed them. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

The more important problem is **source role**. Rawlette's experiential realism is presented immediately alongside beauty, knowledge, love and meaning as candidate intrinsic values. Yet the Map's own Phenomenal Value Realism article explicitly says Rawlette defends a hedonistic view and that the Map itself extends this into phenomenal pluralism; the current changelog likewise records Rawlette as rejecting the Map's pluralist extension. The focal article should make that boundary explicit rather than allowing Rawlette's authority to bleed into the Map's additional six-dimensional value theory. [topics/phenomenal-value-realism](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenal-value-realism/)

Wolf is even more awkward. The article cites *Meaning in Life and Why It Matters* but tells the reader that, given the consciousness-value connection, "meaning is grounded in conscious experience itself." Wolf's own account famously requires not just subjective attraction but **objective attractiveness** plus active engagement. In other words, one of the article's references is a natural source of pressure against the reduction it makes in the body. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

Metz similarly develops explicitly objectivist theories of meaning and ultimately a fundamentality account structured around intelligence engaging fundamental conditions of human existence. Listing him without explaining how the Map rejects or absorbs that objectivist component is not substantive engagement. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/10038)

The handling of Nagel is loose. The article calls the "view from nowhere" the nihilist's abstraction. Nagel's project is not a defence of nihilism; OUP describes *The View from Nowhere* as investigating humanity's divided **subjective and objective** standpoints. The phrase can certainly be repurposed rhetorically, but Nagel should not appear to underwrite the identification of objectivity with nihilistic value-elimination. [Oxford University Press](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-view-from-nowhere-9780195056440)

Most importantly, **the bibliography is stale for a July 2026 revision**. Its newest item is from 2016 even though highly pertinent literature now includes Kriegel's 2019 taxonomy of the value of consciousness, Shepherd 2024, Dimitrov 2025, Cecchinato 2026, Benatar 2026, and Kammerer's 2026 book-length defence of illusionism. The surrounding Map already knows some of this literature—the Evaluative Phenomenal Character article cites Dimitrov 2025—so this is a synchronization failure rather than a lack of available material. [OUP Academic+3OUP Academic+3The Unfinishable Map+3](https://academic.oup.com/analysis/article-abstract/79/3/503/5529320)

## 4. The illusionism response still attacks the convenient version

There has been a genuine improvement since the earlier Map review. The present article now distinguishes practical/motivational "mattering" from intrinsic mattering, so the former defect—where quasi-phenomenal functional states appeared to concede the thesis—has been repaired. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

But the principal response still says an illusion requires someone who is deceived and presses "Who is having the illusion?" Frankish's 2016 article explicitly has a section titled "Who is the audience?" in which he considers exactly that objection and rejects the internal Cartesian spectator model. The illusionist identifies the organism/system, not a phenomenal inner observer, as the subject of the relevant misrepresentation. [Keith Frankish](https://keithfrankish.github.io/articles/Frankish_Illusionism%20as%20a%20theory%20of%20consciousness_eprint.pdf)

The current literature has moved further. Kammerer's 2026 *House of Mirrors* develops illusionism through an Argument from Mysteries and an Argument from Irrelevance: roughly, phenomenal realism must earn its explanatory and evidential commitments rather than being established by the apparent immediacy of phenomenal data. Kammerer also explicitly treats illusionism's ethical consequences without inferring that well-being or moral status simply vanish. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/62981/chapter/565378110)

The Map therefore needs to answer the **non-phenomenal account of seeming and representation**, not insist that seeming must already contain phenomenal appearance. Otherwise its rebuttal has the form: "illusionism is false because genuine seeming is phenomenal," when that is exactly what illusionism denies.

Interestingly, the normative consequences of illusionism could be one of the Map's better lines of attack. Kammerer's earlier "normative challenge" asks illusionists to explain what becomes of the apparent relation between consciousness and value. That is much stronger dialectically than the audience regress because it pressures illusionism from a domain where the Map has independent argumentative interests. [PhilArchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/KAMTNC-2)

## 5. The defeat conditions are tenet-protective

The "What Would Challenge This View?" section sounds unusually exposed to criticism, but two of its three tests are structured to make serious rivals almost incapable of qualifying.

The first requires experience-independent value to be **"demonstrated."** Yet questions of intrinsic value are not normally settled by demonstration in anything like an empirical or mathematical sense. Benatar's 2026 discussion is instructive: even on the narrower question whether sentience is necessary for moral standing, he explicitly says decisive proof is unlikely and argues instead from the balance of considerations. Requiring demonstration from the opponent while treating the Map's own introspective argument as sufficient is a burden asymmetry. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

The third condition is worse: abstract value must become "a force like gravity" and exert causal influence. But an objective-value theorist need not claim that goodness is a physical force. Moore's beautiful-world argument is precisely an assertion about comparative intrinsic value, not a hypothesized new causal field. Requiring objective value to push matter before it is allowed to count as value confuses **normative/evaluative reality with efficient causation** and conveniently imports the Map's own causal metaphysics into the admission test for rivals. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

A serious defeat condition would instead ask what considerations would lower credence in the exclusivity thesis: robust intuitions about observer-independent goods; a convincing fitting-attitude analysis; cases of non-conscious welfare or moral status; evidence separating valence from phenomenal character; or a successful illusionist account retaining normative reasons.

"The connection stands" is consequently too strong. "The connection remains the Map's working hypothesis" would better reflect what the arguments establish.

## 6. Some of the five-tenet integration is grafted on rather than earned

The **dualism** subsection says that if consciousness grounds value, value therefore becomes irreducible. Yet the neighboring Phenomenal Value Realism article correctly acknowledges that phenomenal value realism can be held within physicalism. What the Map needs is the conjunction: phenomenal value identity + irreducibility of the relevant phenomenal property. Consciousness-grounded value alone does not establish dualism. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

The **bidirectional-causation** subsection makes an analogous mistake. From "consciousness causes" and "value is a property of consciousness" it infers that value influences physical outcomes. But a property's bearer being causally efficacious does not establish that every property of that bearer is causally efficacious *qua that property*. The sibling Phenomenal Value Realism article is more careful: it explicitly admits that if the evaluative dimension is causally idle, value's non-epiphenomenality is weaker. The focal article itself half-recognizes the problem one sentence later when it says whether value enters selection directly remains open. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

The **No Many Worlds** subsection is the clearest case of tenet protection. The consciousness-value thesis itself has no obvious implication for Everettian quantum mechanics. A phenomenal axiology can simply assign value to branch-relative experiences, perhaps weighted by branch measure. The focal article instead announces that Many-Worlds "dilutes value" and that suffering is merely distributed. Yet the newer Moral Architecture apex already gives the Everettian opponent the stronger answer—branch-relative significance and measure weighting—before explaining why the Map nevertheless rejects those responses on indexical grounds. The focal article should inherit that argument rather than present the tenet's verdict as an implication of value theory. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

The latest apex is also methodologically healthier in explicitly announcing that its unification is **framework-internal and conditional** rather than independent evidence for dualism. The concept article needs the same discipline. [apex/moral-architecture-of-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/moral-architecture-of-consciousness/)

## 7. Integration is rich in links but weaker in propositions

The article is not poorly connected in a navigational sense. It links extensively to phenomenal value realism, evaluative phenomenal character, environmental ethics, moral architecture, meaning, alignment, valence and other neighboring material. [concepts/consciousness-value-connection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/consciousness-value-connection/)

The weakness is **semantic synchronization**.

Evaluative Phenomenal Character is more cautious than its parent concept. It says introspection supplies "evidence but not proof"; cites the 2025 Dimitrov argument; and treats cross-traditional recurrence with explicit independence discounts. The focal article compresses this into structural features supposedly "confirmed by cross-traditional convergence." [concepts/evaluative-phenomenal-character](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/evaluative-phenomenal-character/)

Phenomenal Value Realism explicitly distinguishes Rawlette's hedonism from the Map's phenomenal pluralism, while the focal page blurs them. It also openly states that phenomenal value realism is compatible with physicalism and acknowledges uncertainty about value-sensitive causation. [topics/phenomenal-value-realism](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenal-value-realism/)

The newer Moral Architecture page correctly marks its synthesis as conditional and gives Many-Worlds defenders their branch-relative and measure-weighted answers. The focal article retains the stronger, less qualified formulations. [apex/moral-architecture-of-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/moral-architecture-of-consciousness/)

The environmental article at least engages Holmes Rolston's ecocentric argument specifically, whereas the focal article answers the easier claim that an ecosystem must literally possess a perspective. Here the problem is not identical strings but argument families: the newer qualification exists somewhere in the cluster without propagating to every page making the parent claim. ## Overall assessment

There is a worthwhile article inside this one, but it should defend a narrower hierarchy of claims.

The strongest claim presently supported is that **certain valenced phenomenal states are plausible loci of intrinsic value**. There is significant philosophical work supporting that position. The much stronger thesis that **all intrinsic value must be phenomenal** remains under-argued. Still stronger claims—that every phenomenal subject has moral status, that experience wherever found carries value, that value qua value is causally efficacious, and that Many-Worlds undermines value—do not follow from it.

The central methodological defect is therefore not straightforward dualist bias. It is **implication expansion**: a comparatively plausible phenomenal-value thesis gets used as a license for increasingly ambitious conclusions, while the points at which additional premises enter are obscured.
