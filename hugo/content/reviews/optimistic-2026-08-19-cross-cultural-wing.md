## Executive Summary

The wing's strength is not that it argues for cross-traditional convergence; it is
that it argues **against its own convergence claim, repeatedly, in the articles that
would benefit most from not doing so**. The common-cause discount registered as
[P-M2](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#p-m2) is applied at least **seven** times
across this wing — not the four identified going in — and in three of those cases
the article discounts *the very strand it exists to present*. The Islamic article
links the audit that grades its own tradition worst; the African article turns
Hountondji's ethnophilosophy critique against the Map's own counting; the Indian
article concedes that its schools lacked the reducing science that would have made
a reduction verdict available at all.

The proposed opportunity — adding these demonstrations to [P-M2](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#p-m2)'s `Argued in` — was
checked and **does not hold**. Its premise is literally true and its conclusion is
wrong; the register's parent/instance architecture already routes content citations
to [P-D3](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d3), which carries two of them. A **narrower, genuine** gap sits one level down
at [P-D3](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d3) and is recorded below.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`cross-traditional-convergence-on-consciousness-irreducibility` L88 states the
explanatory gap in its cleanest structural form and grounds it in a reason rather
than an intuition-pump: "Structure specifies relations; quality specifies character.
No amount of relational specification entails intrinsic character, just as no amount
of information about a map's topology tells you what the terrain looks like from the
ground." What earns Chalmers' assent is the sentence *after* it — that the gap
surfaces "under vocabularies as different as *guṇa* theory, *dharma* taxonomy, and
functional-role analysis." The article treats vocabulary-independence as the datum
requiring explanation, which is exactly the shape of the hard problem rather than a
restatement of it.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Thin, honestly. This wing engages the physics-consciousness interface only through
`african-philosophy-of-consciousness` L59, and it engages it in order to *refuse* a
connection: the structural parallel between the Akan *sunsum* interface layer and the
Map's quantum-indeterminacy interface "is a comparison rather than a connection — the
Akan framework predates quantum mechanics and is independent of it." That is the
right call and it is the interesting one. A wing under pressure to find allies
declined a flattering anachronism. Stapp gets no new mechanism here; he gets a
demonstration that the Map will not manufacture one.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`cross-traditional-convergence` L76 is the wing's best Nagel sentence: "A rock does
not reveal itself to itself. A neural firing pattern does not illuminate its own
character. Consciousness does." It is doing real work — self-intimation as the
structural asymmetry the traditions independently locate — and it is immediately
followed by the concession that a shared introspective architecture could still
generate it. Centring first-person experience while naming the way first-person
method could be the artefact's source is the phenomenologist's position held with
its own defeater in view.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

This wing is the most Whitehead-friendly material the Map owns — Nishida's pure
experience prior to the subject-object split, absolute nothingness as a formless
field, Advaita's nondual Brahman, the Bantu/Akan vital-force ontologies. Every one
of these avoids crude substance dualism, and the Kyoto article's rendering of the
"self-identity of absolute contradiction" (L46) is a genuinely good exposition of a
process metaphysic on its own terms.

**And the wing banks none of it as support.** See the Hardline Empiricist below;
this is the case where the two personas converge rather than conflict.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`indian-philosophy-of-mind` L151 gives Kane a real find: Nyaya's explicit causal
chain from self through *manas* to the sense organs, "a form of [agent-causation](/concepts/agent-causation/)
articulated independently of the Western tradition." The article does not stop at
the resonance — it registers Samkhya's passive *purusha* as "a genuine tension" and
states which side the Map takes and why ("consciousness acts, it does not merely
witness"). Naming the tradition that *cuts against* the tenet, in the tenet-alignment
section, is the move that makes the supporting citation worth anything.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`voids/question-formation-void` L92 is the wing's humility high-water mark, and its
third concession is the one that costs something: "The cleanest empirical probe is
precisely the method the apparatus cannot self-administer: exposing the question-set
to minds whose generativity differs." The article identifies the test that would
settle the matter and concedes the apparatus cannot run it on itself. McGinn's
cognitive closure, stated as a methodological limit with a named unreachable
experiment rather than as a mood.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This persona's verdict is load-bearing here, and it is favourable.**

The structural opportunity in this wing is enormous: seven articles' worth of
traditions that all find consciousness irreducible, in a framework whose Tenet 5
explicitly weakens parsimony objections. The tenet-as-evidence-upgrade move is
available on every page. **It is declined on every page**, and declined with the
mechanism named:

- **`indian-philosophy-of-mind`** — the description itself pre-commits: "convergence
  within one culture, not independent confirmation." L129 grants the deflationary
  reading force in the deflater's own voice, then L155 adds a discount I have not
  seen elsewhere in the corpus: the three schools "lacked the reducing apparatus,"
  so "none possessed a reducing science (neural correlates, computational models)
  that would have made a reduction verdict a live option to begin with." That is a
  **selection-effect** discount layered on top of the common-cause discount. The
  traditions could not have found reduction, so their failure to find it is worth
  less. This is a stronger self-limitation than [P-M2](/positions/methodology-and-calibration/#p-m2) requires.
- **`african-philosophy-of-consciousness`** — L73 turns the field's own foundational
  critique inward: counting "Bantu, Akan, Yoruba and Zulu as separate votes for
  irreducibility, as [[cross-traditional-convergence-on-consciousness-irreducibility|the
  convergence argument]] does, performs that operation" — *unanimism*, "on the
  standard reading of Hountondji the founding error of ethnophilosophy." The article
  names its own wing's flagship as committing the error, then states what survives
  (a count of named philosophers disagreeing with one another). Its description
  closes the loop: "suggestive context for dualism, not independent evidence."
- **`islamic-sufi-philosophy-of-consciousness`** — L43 links
  [[neoplatonist-common-cause-weight-for-cross-tradition-introspection-architecture-parallels|the
  common-cause audit]] that grades *its own* tradition's transmission channel at
  **moderate-to-substantial** contamination while clearing Advaita and Stoicism. The
  article hosting the strand carries the finding that its strand is the weakest.
- **`cross-traditional-convergence`** — the whole "Engaging the Common-Cause Null"
  section (L126–138), and its verdict is **calibrated rather than flat**:
  "premise-level common causes substantially weakened; residual
  introspection-architecture common cause acknowledged." L118 then demotes its own
  summary table's Islamic row on the audit's finding, and L138 closes with "The
  convergence does work; it does not do unlimited work." A discount applied
  *differentially per strand*, rather than as a uniform haircut, is materially harder
  to do and materially more honest.
- **`evaluative-qualia-...`** — five traditions "deflate to at most three
  near-independent lineages," and the deflated count is then carried consistently
  into the tenet sections (L122, L130 both say *three*, not five). The discount
  survives into the sections where the temptation to quote the raw tally is greatest.
- **`eastern-philosophy-consciousness`** L73 — Advaita "is better read as a nondual
  anti-physicalist rival than as a dualism-friendly ally—with the Map against
  reduction, against it on the structure interactionism requires." **Reclassifying an
  apparent ally as a rival** is the most expensive form of this discipline: it does
  not discount a supporting observation, it moves it to the other column.
- **`japanese-philosophy-of-mind-kyoto-school`** L82 — "the Kyoto case only
  **strengthens rather than confirms**" the convergence argument, because the school
  "reaches its conclusions by the same broadly introspective and contemplative
  method" the other traditions share.

**The Kyoto article deserves separate credit for a device the register does not yet
have a name for.** L80 defines a *basin edge*: "a tradition that shares the human
introspective starting point and pursues it deeply, yet does *not* land on the Map's
reading of the result." This is not a discount — it is a **disconfirming-instance
protocol**, a standing search for traditions that should have converged and did not.
The article names three (Kyoto, `chinese-philosophy-of-mind`, `jain-philosophy-of-mind`),
and `cross-traditional-convergence` L135 puts the Chinese case to work as "a partial
discharge of the common-cause objection" — traditions sharing the architecture but
not the framework produce *different* outputs. The wing built itself a natural
experiment out of its own negative cases. L86 states the payoff without inflating it:
"neither a defeat nor a vindication."

Finally, the Whitehead/Birch convergence: the nondual material is where
process-philosophical resonance is strongest, and it is precisely where the wing is
hardest on itself (Advaita reclassified as a rival; absolute nothingness read as "a
genuinely third framing" the Map "does not conscript"). Kyoto L48 states the mismatch
as the finding — "Neither of the Map's ready categories fits, and that mismatch is
itself the datum." Process-friendliness is treated as tenet-coherent and
**not** evidence-elevating, exactly as the discipline requires.

## The Real Gap — [P-D3](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d3)'s Two Dangling References

One level down, a genuine and narrower gap **does** hold. [P-D3](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d3)'s `Asserts` leans on
two bodies of material the Map has dedicated articles for, and cites neither. Both
articles are cited by **zero** files in `obsidian/positions/` (verified by grep).

**(1) The Neoplatonist audit is relied on but uncited.** [P-D3](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d3) asserts "the
Neoplatonist common-cause audit assigns moderate-to-substantial shared-source weight
to the Ibn Sīnā strand" — the finding that downgrades the Islamic strand. That audit
is a full article,
`obsidian/topics/neoplatonist-common-cause-weight-for-cross-tradition-introspection-architecture-parallels.md`.
[P-D3](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d3)'s `Depends on` (L69) lists "the historical record of Indo-Greek transmission;
the textual assessment of the early-Chinese candidate" — both *unlinked prose
referents* — and omits the audit entirely. The position depends on a worked result
whose home article the register never points at, so a reader cannot reach the
evidence for [P-D3](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d3)'s own Islamic verdict from the register.

**(2) West African ontologies are named as an open candidate the Map has already
assessed.** [P-D3](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d3) says "Indigenous American or West African ontologies are
candidates" for the missing independent third lineage, and calls establishing one
"open work rather than a completed step." Meanwhile
`obsidian/concepts/african-philosophy-of-consciousness.md` has assessed the West
African material and returned a *self-limiting* answer — "suggestive context for
dualism, not independent evidence," with the unanimism objection at L73 explaining
why the multi-tradition tally cannot be counted as separate votes. The register
holds the question open while the corpus holds a partial, negative answer to it.

Secondary, same task: the **basin-edge** apparatus (Kyoto / Chinese / Jain) is the
Map's worked machinery for testing candidate lineages, and no positions file cites
any of the three. [P-D3](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d3)'s `Would shift if` turns on whether "a genuinely independent
third lineage were established" — the basin-edge cases are precisely the instrument
that would adjudicate that, and the register does not know they exist.

This is an `Argued in` / `Depends on` completion on an existing entry, not a new
position. `positions/` stands at **15/80** (measured live via
`tools.evolution.state.check_section_caps`), so it is also the only section with
room — but no new slot is consumed.
