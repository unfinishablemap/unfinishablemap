## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Possibility/probability slippage in "Counterfactual Reasoning as Evidence for Bidirectional Interaction".** The section said counterfactual reasoning "may provide some of the strongest evidence" for Tenet 3. Its deliberation argument is a conditional whose antecedent is the conclusion. A reductive physicalist accepts the conditional, because conscious deliberation is a neural process on that view. The ape-gap argument ("these physical differences *track* differences in conscious processing… why does counterfactual sophistication correlate so precisely with conscious workspace expansion") was presented as defeating the accompaniment reading. The Map's own audit (`topics/consciousness-and-cognitive-distinctiveness` L133) says plainly that it "cannot defeat the second and third readings from comparative data". A reviewer who accepts the tenets would still flag this. **Fix**: heading → "Counterfactual Reasoning and Bidirectional Interaction" (no inbound anchor links; grep of obsidian + hugo found 0). The lead now says counterfactual reasoning supplies "a picture of how the tenet would operate if it holds rather than independent evidence that it does". A sentence after the conditional notes that a reductive physicalist accepts it. The ape paragraph now concedes that the accompaniment reading predicts the same pattern (linked to the audit anchor) and says what actually tilts the Map: its case for mental causation, quoting the amplifier page's own wording, not the gap.
2. **Map-internal misattribution (three cases).**
   - (a) "as the concept article on [counterfactual-reasoning](/concepts/counterfactual-reasoning/) notes, the inference depends on premises a physicalist need not grant". The concept page says nothing of the kind (grep `need not|grant|premises|physicalist` → 0 relevant hits). The clause is now in the Map's own voice.
   - (b) "The consciousness threshold article argues that counterfactual reasoning is one of a cluster of capacities—alongside logical reasoning, cumulative culture, and declarative metacognition". That page's cluster is working memory, metacognition, cumulative culture and logical reasoning. Counterfactual reasoning appears there only in frontmatter, Further Reading and a "counterfactual simulation" phrase at L184. The page *interprets* the cluster, and since its audit it concedes the rival readings. Rewritten to report the page faithfully, including the concession.
   - (c) "The agent-causal framework proposes that consciousness evaluates quantum possibilities through counterfactual simulation". `concepts/agent-causation` has 0 hits for "counterfactual". The evaluation-by-simulation proposal lives in `concepts/counterfactual-reasoning` L94. Rewritten: agent-causation locates selection at quantum indeterminacies (its L107), and the concept article proposes counterfactual evaluation. The Further Reading label for agent-causation is corrected to match.
3. **Result-direction / framing error on Van Hoeck et al. (2013).** This was cited against predictive processing as "consistent with elaborated phenomenal construction rather than minimal prediction-error correction". The paper tests no such contrast. Its finding is that counterfactual thinking shares the core episodic memory network used for remembering the past and imagining the future, and recruits some regions "more strongly and extensively". That is the simulation account's own evidence. The citation moved to the simulation paragraph, where it supports the physicalist's account.
4. **Internal contradiction in the lead.** The lead said that representing a counterfactual requires that the dual status "must be *phenomenally present*", then said computers represent counterfactuals routinely. It also contradicted L70's concession that phenomenal grasp may not be necessary. **Fix**: "in conscious reasoning this dual status is *phenomenally present*". Two categorical claims were also brought into line with L70's "has not been shown to share" register: "nothing in their processing is known to involve" and "has not been shown to provide". L92 and the Dualism tenet line got the same treatment.
5. **Misattributed coinage: "what Byrne (2005) calls the tendency toward 'close counterfactuals'".** In this literature "close counterfactuals" is the term for near misses (e.g. McMullen & Markman 2002, *Affective impact of close counterfactuals*, which is in the précis reference list). It is not Byrne's name for the minimal-departure default. The Cambridge précis page has 5 hits for "close counterfactual", all in the reference list, and 0 in the abstract. Byrne's own verbatim term is "fault lines" (précis abstract). The book's full text could not be searched: archive.org lending copy 403, Google Books quota exhausted, core.ac.uk 403, scispace empty. So the phrase was replaced with what the abstract supports: the fault lines (actions over inactions, controllable events, the most recent event in a sequence).
6. **Cited-author stance (Byrne) and a non-sequitur in "The Counterfactual Void".** The section argued that "if counterfactual reasoning were merely computational… it should have no inherent boundary". From this it concluded that the nearest-world constraint shows counterfactual reasoning to be "grounded in phenomenal experience rather than formal computation". A resource-bounded system with models learned from experience predicts the same boundary. Byrne's own explanation of the fault lines is representational: "The sorts of possibilities that people envisage explain the mutability of certain aspects of mental representations" (précis abstract). **Fix**: the section now concedes that the limit does not by itself favour a phenomenal account. It states Byrne's explanation and draws a narrower Map inference: the thinning is a loss of phenomenal content, while formal methods still represent distant alternatives. That inference is consistent with `voids/counterfactual-void` L58 as revised on 2026-11-18. The section also takes over the void page's "bias rather than ceiling" distinction (void L56). Previously it said research on the void "reveals" architectural limits, which overstated what the void page now claims.
7. **Cited-author stance and attribution (Suddendorf & Corballis 2007).** These authors were cited for apes "not entirely confined to the present", with "tool caching… and anticipation of others' actions". Their abstract concludes "there is as yet no convincing evidence for mental time travel in nonhuman animals". "Anticipation of others' actions" probably draws on the post-2007 false-belief work (Krupenye et al. 2016, cited on the threshold page), not on this paper. **Fix**: the tool-saving finding is now cited to its source (Mulcahy & Call 2006), and Suddendorf & Corballis are quoted for their actual conclusion.

### Medium Issues Found

- **Physicalist accounts not at their strongest (brief focus).**
  - Simulation: now names the strongest version, a recombined episodic network (Van Hoeck 2013), plus Byrne's mental-model account of the dual status itself (Byrne & Tasso 1999: models make explicit "the suppositional case" and "the presupposed case"). The Map's residue is stated as phenomenal.
  - Predictive processing: the old reply ("phenomenally rich in ways that exceed any learning function—a simple error signal would suffice") was unsupported. Developed PP theories make counterfactual modelling central. Seth (2014) uses "counterfactually-rich" generative models to explain perceptual *presence*, the felt marker of reality and its absence, which bears directly on the page's puzzle. The elaboration of counterfactuals also has a functional rationale (Roese 1997: counterfactuals yield causal inferences, "net effect… is beneficial"). The rewrite concedes both points. The Map's residue is now how a scenario can be vivid while felt as unreal, and why either marker is felt at all, and the rewrite says honestly that whether this residue goes beyond the hard problem is what the physicalist disputes. L60's framing sentence, which said each account "faces a difficulty that goes beyond the general hard problem", was softened to "on the Map's reading, leaves a difficulty specific to the *dual-status* character".
  - Causal modelling: Pearl (2009) added. Counterfactuals need an explicit causal model, which machines implement, so this demand is not yet a demand for consciousness.
- **Computational-account non-sequitur.** The old line was "If human counterfactual reasoning is just computation, the felt awareness of non-actuality is epiphenomenal". That holds only if the awareness is something over and above the computation. It is now a disjunction: either identical to the computation (denied by the Dualism tenet) or epiphenomenal (rejected by Bidirectional Interaction). It is flagged as a conflict with the Map's commitments, not a refutation.
- **"Two incompatible epistemic attitudes".** Supposing P and believing not-P are opposed in content but not incompatible as attitudes; that is what supposition is. Changed to "opposed" at L62 and L68. The 2026-06-25 cliché ruling on L62 ("not just X… it is Y" kept as a substantive escalation) is respected; that sentence is otherwise unchanged.
- **Occam tenet line strawmanned the rival** ("neural pattern matching… fails to explain… the qualitative difference between human and non-human primate counterfactual capacities"), contradicting L78's own concession. It now credits neural simulation with the functional structure and, plausibly, the primate gap, and locates the residue in the phenomenology.
- **Unsourced ape working-memory claim.** "Great ape capacity is substantially smaller still" now cites Read, Manrique & Walker (2022), marked "contested" as on the concept and amplifier pages. Note that Read et al. compare their 2±1 with Miller's 7±2, not with Cowan's 4; the page attributes only the 2±1 to them.
- **Currency wording.** "Current research estimates… (Cowan, 2001)" became "Cowan (2001) puts human capacity at about four chunks (three to five)", which removes the "current" superlative attached to a 2001 source.
