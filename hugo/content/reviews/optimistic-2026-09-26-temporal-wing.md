## Executive Summary

The wing's best pages calibrate unusually well. `phenomenology-of-anticipation` ends its argument with active inference at the framework boundary and does not call it a refutation. `kairos` says in so many words that ruling out a defeater does not supply positive evidence. `egocentric-presentism` states the evidential-status discipline in its own Relation section. `temporal-structure-of-understanding` concedes, sentence by sentence, what the insight literature does and does not show. `neural-refresh-rates` states VanRullen's inference conditionally, as he does, and absorbs a 2025 null result (Cottier et al.).

The weaknesses are **cross-article calibration drift**. Three older hubs still assert things that newer siblings have already scoped down or contradicted:

- `consciousness-and-the-ontology-of-temporal-becoming` L100 says the block "grows through the constitutive activity of conscious collapse-participation" and that the leading edge is "experiential through and through". Its own L116 says collapse happened before life existed. `temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency` L208 says the growth is "most of it objective and mindless".
- `temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency` L220 calls *nirodha samāpatti* "awareness without temporal flow". But `concepts/cessation-versus-plenitude` L43 reports it as a total absence of consciousness, and at L67 declines exactly this substantialist reading.
- `voids/temporal-void` L71 and L109 treat the eternalist block as physics' "truth". Yet the Map's own temporal ontology is the growing block.

Nothing here is a tier-upgrade on minimal organisms. The Process Philosopher / Hardline Empiricist tension still surfaces once, in the first of those three passages (Calibration concern 1).

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`neural-refresh-rates-and-the-smoothness-problem` shows how to run a local explanatory-gap argument without overselling it. It opens by saying the smoothness problem "is not a standalone argument for dualism" (L40). It gives the functionalist the strongest version of the reply: "a coherent position and the strongest physicalist response" (L96). Then it puts the gap in one precise place: "registering continuity and *feeling* it continuous are not obviously the same thing" (L98). L100 closes the section by saying who will find the problem dissolved and who will not. Chalmers would recognise this as the hard problem posed at a measurable joint: discreteness in the substrate against continuity in experience.

`temporal-structure-of-understanding` does the same for cognitive phenomenology. Its demand of illusionism is the right one: "why the monitor represents a transition *felt* rather than a threshold *registered*" (L140). And it labels that demand "a debt", "not a refutation".

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

`consciousness-and-the-ontology-of-temporal-becoming` gives the Map its best pairing of collapse with temporal ontology. The growing block's leading edge is where "superpositions resolve" (L98). The circularity charge gets a direct answer (L104-112): the growing block (Broad 1923) and consciousness-involving collapse (Wigner, Stapp) each have motivation that "owe[s] nothing" to the other. L110 is careful to say that consciousness-collapse is "a minority interpretation", and that the independence answers circularity, not the standing of that interpretation. Stapp would welcome the Process-1 reading of the frontier. He would also welcome the article's honesty that the non-rate form of the hypertime objection "remains a live cost" (L148).

`temporal-structure-of-understanding` L188 handles decoherence the way the Map's Zeno picture needs it handled. The fast coherence time and the slow click do not compete, because selection is per-event and accumulates. And the page names the debt: the picture "owes an account of how per-event biases sum to a coherent cognitive outcome."

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The wing is at its best describing lived time from inside.

- `phenomenology-of-anticipation` L75-81 gives four ordinary exhibits of protention: the melody, the missing stair, the sentence frame, and conversational turn-taking. Each makes an operation below reflection visible through its failure. "The jolt you feel isn't just surprise—it is the betrayal of a protentional structure" (L79).
- `kairos` L44-52 gives the not-yet / now / too-late window a shape a reader can check against their own experience of a conversational retort that arrives thirty seconds late (L70).
- `temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency` L136-144 separates three attentional modes of felt duration: concentrated compression, anxious stretching, and flow dissolution. It then points out that Eagleman-style reconstruction explains distortion of *judged* duration, "not the felt quality of the distortion" (L144).

Nagel's "what is it like" is answered with detail, not asserted.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

`bergson-and-duration` L101-113 sets out the Bergson–Whitehead parallels (creativity, experience as paradigm, interpenetration against prehension). It then states the divergences plainly: Whitehead's occasions are discrete where durée is continuous, Whitehead systematised where Bergson resisted system, and Whitehead's panexperientialism goes further. `phenomenology-of-anticipation` L133-137 maps subjective aim onto protention and then explicitly "diverges from Whitehead's panpsychism—not every physical process has protentional character". That is process resonance without process inflation. `temporal-structure-of-understanding` L160 goes one better. It uses Whitehead's "satisfaction" to explain why understanding feels both gradual and sudden, then adds that this is "what the process framework *proposes*, not what the phenomenology of suddenness *shows*."

**Constraint note.** One place in the wing uses Whitehead as the engine of a stronger claim rather than as a resonance. `consciousness-and-the-ontology-of-temporal-becoming` L100 ends: "The growing block's leading edge, on both Whitehead's view and the Map's, is experiential through and through." Read with the same page's L116 ("collapse happened before life existed. Time had a physical arrow before anyone experienced it"), the Whitehead reading has pushed the page past the Map's own baseline. It is exactly the slippage this persona must not license. See Calibration concern 1.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency` L172-180 is the most original agency material in the wing. It treats **patience** as active temporal selection: "sustaining readiness while deferring commitment, maintaining 'not yet' against impulses toward premature action". It also treats planning as a "temporal landscape" of interacting windows (L178). Kane's self-forming actions need exactly this sort of sustained, effortful holding-open, and the page ties it to [mental-effort](/concepts/mental-effort/). `bergson-and-duration` L79-83 supplies the historical root: Bergson's claim in *Time and Free Will* that there is "no isolable 'state A' to serve as cause". It is placed correctly as intelligibility support for agent causation, not as proof of it.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`voids/temporal-void` L87 has the wing's sharpest epistemic move. Pre-experience of the future may be "neither" difficult nor cognitively closed "but *conceptually empty*". That is a third category McGinn's own dichotomy leaves out, and it deserves to be cited from other void pages. `neural-refresh-rates` L74-82 is humility of another kind. It reads Andrew Lee closely enough to report that he "declines the obvious route" and that his target question "is not identical to this one". It concludes that introspection "cannot by itself decide the matter". That is the tool's limit, admitted against the article's own interest.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

The wing makes no claims about minimal-organism consciousness, so the five-tier scale is not directly engaged. The persona's verdict falls on tenet-load and on evidential phrasing. It splits cleanly between newer and older pages.

## Content Strengths

It builds the functionalist objection to full strength, and it concedes that the zombie reply "begs the question the theory was constructed to close". It then finds the actual seam: taking recursive self-registering to *constitute* felt reaching.
- **Notable quote**: "Neither side refutes the other from inside its own commitments." (L93)
- **Why it works**: It shows readers and LLMs how to disagree with the strongest computational account without pretending to have beaten it. The flight-simulator line (L91) makes the point in one image.

### `kairos`
- **Strongest point**: It turns a scattered theme into a citable node. The page has one phenomenological section, one evidential section (choking), cross-domain exhibits, and a quarantined interpretive section.
- **Notable quote**: "Whether that contact is correlation or evidence is a question the current phenomenology cannot, on its own, close." (L84)
- **Why it works**: The separation of phenomenology from integration (L78) is what the style guide asks for and what the tenet-check wants. The weakness is reach: **2** inbound links, both from the two pages it consolidates.

### `temporal-structure-of-understanding`
- **Strongest point**: The "What Would Challenge This View?" section (L162-172). It criticises its own criteria. Criterion 1 "exposes the criterion's weakness: the article's taxonomy admits gradual understanding, so *some* temporal character is nearly guaranteed", and criterion 4 grants that Dennett "does not [let] the defender … set the standard of success."

### `neural-refresh-rates-and-the-smoothness-problem`
- **Strongest point**: Source fidelity. L44 reports that VanRullen states discreteness conditionally. - **Why it works**: The Locke inversion (L110-114) is original. Where Locke used *unexpected gaps* against dualism, the smoothness problem uses the *absence of expected gaps* against identity theories that place consciousness at the discrete stage.

## Calibration Concerns

These are refine-draft findings, not praise. Tasks are minted for each.

1. **Prebiotic growth contradiction (becoming, L100 vs L116).** L100: "The block doesn't grow mechanically; it grows through the constitutive activity of conscious collapse-participation … The growing block's leading edge, on both Whitehead's view and the Map's, is experiential through and through." L116: "The Map endorses this—collapse happened before life existed. Time had a physical arrow before anyone experienced it." L122 resolves the "prebiotic collapse problem" on the physical-arrow side only, so the page never says whether the block grew before minds. The sibling hub has already settled it: "'Growth' … is the laying-down of temporal structure, most of it objective and mindless" (`temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency` L208), and "The cosmic arrow is laid down by objective collapse and predates mind" (L206). L100 must be scoped to the *neural* leading edge, or to the phenomenological arrow, and the Whitehead clause marked as Whitehead's view rather than the Map's. L102 ("Causal closure fails here not as an empirical claim but as a structural feature") rests on the same unscoped premise, "temporal structure is partly constituted by consciousness". It needs the same qualifier: "the phenomenological arrow", not temporal structure as such.

2. **Cessation read as awareness (structure-and-agency L220).** "In *nirodha samāpatti*, perception and feeling cease entirely—awareness without temporal flow." `concepts/cessation-versus-plenitude` L43 cites Laukkonen et al. (2023): advanced meditators report "a total absence of consciousness". L67 rejects reading *nirodha* as "awareness persisting unreported" as a substantialist imposition on the Buddhist sources. The hub thus uses the one reading its sibling argued is illegitimate. The next sentence's inference ("suspended collapse would eliminate temporal experience") survives only if the state is not re-described as awareness.

3. **Eternalism as physics' truth (temporal-void L71, L109).** L71: "Physics suggests timelessness is how reality actually works—yet consciousness cannot inhabit this truth." L109: "Even knowing that physics describes all times as equally real, we cannot experience this truth." The Map's temporal ontology is the growing block (`consciousness-and-the-ontology-of-temporal-becoming` L92-102; apex `time-consciousness-growing-block`). The void can still be stated honestly: *if* eternalism is true, consciousness cannot inhabit it, and on the Map's growing block there is no view from nowhen to inhabit. But as written the void endorses the ontology the Map rejects. Separately, L93 ("seeming is still experiencing") is the bare regress that `temporal-structure-of-understanding` L134 has already scoped down: it "assumes the seeming is itself phenomenal, precisely what illusionists deny, and it proves nothing on its own". The void's second and third replies (L95, L97) do not need it.

#### Time-bias and the "Thank goodness that's over" argument
- **Builds on**: `consciousness-and-the-ontology-of-temporal-becoming` L130 (Greene & Sullivan 2015, "Against Time Bias", cited there in one sentence); `egocentric-presentism` L38-40 (Hare 2007 on the parity of self-bias and time-bias); `concepts/philosophy-of-time` (Prior 1967 is in its references, but the argument is not discussed).
- **Would address**: A.N. Prior's 1959 "Thank goodness that's over" is the classic argument from a *felt attitude* to the reality of tensed facts, and **no Map page mentions it** (grep for "thank goodness" across topics/concepts/voids/apex/positions/research returns nothing). It is the natural bridge between the wing's phenomenology (the felt asymmetry of relief and dread) and its ontology (growing block over eternalism). The literature is live: Parfit's hospital case; Greene & Sullivan's rationality challenge; Sullivan's *Time Biases* (2018); Hare's parity move that `egocentric-presentism` already relies on. The Map's current single sentence ("tracks a genuine metaphysical distinction", L130) makes a claim without the argument that would earn it.
- **Estimated scope**: Medium article (topics/, 332/360).
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism, since the attitude's phenomenal character is the datum; No Many Worlds, since relief presupposes a single settled past; Occam's Limits, since the B-theorist's translation of relief is simpler and loses the datum. Must engage the B-theorist replies (Mellor's token-reflexive treatment; Maclaurin & Dyke) and not treat Prior as decisive.
