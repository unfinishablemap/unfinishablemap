## Executive Summary

`observational-closure.md` is the article the Map's whole Tenet-3 defence rests on — 22 inbound links from concepts, topics, apex *and* `project/` — and it is also the corpus's cleanest example of a discipline the Map finds hard: it states a result that would be enormously convenient to run forward as evidence for dualism, and it declines to do so, in every one of the seven places it could have. Its Saad quotation is character-exact against the primary text. The opportunity is not correction but proportion: at 1357 words it is the shortest thing in the defensive core, less than half its own soft threshold, and its answer to the objection it exists to answer (epiphenomenalism-in-disguise) is currently *stronger and less honest* than the answer the Map already carries in `delegatory-causation.md`.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The article's structural insight is that the exclusion argument is not one argument but two arguments sharing a name, and that the empirical one cannot reach the metaphysical conclusion. L44: *"The argument needs universal closure to exclude non-physical causes, but its empirical support delivers only observational closure."* This is the right shape of reply to Kim — it does not deny any finding, it identifies where a finding was silently promoted into a thesis. Chalmers would note approvingly that the article locates the gap *inside the opponent's premise* rather than asking for an exemption from it.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The Domain Specificity section (L64) is the best short statement of the interface case in the corpus: consciousness *"fills a gap that physics explicitly acknowledges rather than overriding a domain where physics makes determinate predictions."* Stapp's own framework gets the correct treatment two paragraphs earlier — real influence, zero statistical signature, and the reason given is that the invisibility is *structural* (Born-rule noise the theory already predicts), not stipulated. And the MWI dependency is declared inline at L64 rather than buried: *"(This mechanism presupposes collapse interpretations of quantum mechanics; under the [Many-Worlds Interpretation](/concepts/many-worlds/), all outcomes occur and there is nothing for consciousness to select.)"* A framework-relative premise, flagged as one.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The epiphenomenalism rebuttal (L74) gets the first-person stakes right: observational closure lets consciousness cause *"everything it appears to cause—the avoidance behaviour, the verbal reports, the neural downstream effects."* The distinction drawn is between what laboratories can detect and whether causation occurs — precisely Nagel's objective/subjective asymmetry, applied to causation rather than to knowledge.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

The article avoids the crude picture entirely. On the delegation route there is no mental substance shoving a physical one: an experience *inherits* a causal profile the physical state already had and exercises it. L56 — *"Because the experience causes exactly what the physical state would have caused by default, no experiment can detect the substitution."* Mind and matter are two descriptions of one causal channel, which is as close to Whitehead as an interactionist framework can honestly get.

**Held to its axis, per this skill's constraint**: none of the above is offered as reason to think the interface is more *likely*. The article gives the Process Philosopher nothing to over-spend, because it never states a probability claim at all.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Domain Specificity is Kane's opening, stated economically: physics predicts distributions and not individual results, and *"the gap is intrinsic to quantum mechanics."* Agency does not need a hole to be drilled; the theory already has one.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The finest section in the article is the one that could most easily have been an equivocation. The Map holds mysterian sympathies *and* holds observational closure, and the words "closure" in each are unrelated. L82 separates them explicitly — cognitive closure is *"an epistemic claim about the limits of human understanding"*, observational closure *"an empirical claim about the limits of physical detection"* — then notes how they could interact, then refuses to let the interaction become an inference: *"But the two theses stand or fall independently."* Naming a possible interaction and then declining to bank it is exactly the discipline the Map's own [epistemology-of-limit-knowledge](/concepts/epistemology-of-limit-knowledge/) articulates at L129.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This is the load-bearing verdict, and it is unusually positive.**

Observational closure is a *defeater-removal* result: it shows that the causal-closure objection does not go through as stated. The standing temptation is to spend it as positive evidence — "physics might be incomplete beyond experimental reach, therefore the Map's incompleteness is likely." The article never does this. Grepped every calibration verb in the file (`support|evidence for|prove|establish|demonstrat|confirm|favour|more likely|probable`); seven loci, and every one of them is either about the *opponent's* evidence or is explicitly capped:

- L42 — *"The empirical evidence—centuries of scientific success—establishes observational closure."* The factive verb is attached to the **weaker** thesis, which the evidence really does establish. Correct register.
- L42 — *"The inference to universal closure requires an additional step that the evidence alone does not compel."* "Does not compel", not "is refuted".
- L32 / L44 — *"the scientific evidence supports only observational closure"*, *"its empirical support delivers only observational closure"*. Both scope the word "support" to the opponent.
- L70 — *"The general claim that some mental causation exists somewhere is indeed unfalsifiable under observational closure."* The article **concedes the objection** at full strength before answering the answerable part.
- L86 — the thesis is *"the Map's most important argumentative move **against** the causal closure objection"*. Defensive framing, not evidential.
- L88 — *"The tenet does not claim physics is causally complete; it claims physics **appears** complete where we can check."*
- L92 — *"Observational closure sets a floor, not a ceiling."*

Three further pieces of restraint deserve naming because they are things the article had every opportunity to do and did not:

1. **No parsimony forward-run.** Grep: `parsimon|simplic|occam` — **absent**. The article had an obvious route to Tenet 5 (the closure inference is an unwarranted simplicity extrapolation, therefore the Map wins on the same currency). It stays off that ground entirely and lets `causal-closure.md` L127 own the honest version: *"Both positions extrapolate from evidence to metaphysics."*
2. **Real falsifiers, named and specific** (L70): quantum coherence proving impossible in neural tissue; consciousness effects violating Born-rule statistics; classical mechanisms explaining reportability. Three named ways the position loses, in an article whose subject is empirical undetectability.
3. **Domain specification defended on precedent, not on convenience** (L78). Conservation of energy, general relativity, thermodynamics — real cases where scientific theories legitimately restrict scope. And per the corpus discipline, a precedent is not treated as a licence: the paragraph claims only that *"Domain specification is standard scientific practice"*, not that this particular specification is thereby vindicated.

The honest summary of the article's register is the pattern this skill names: **tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating**. Both this persona and the Process Philosopher can praise this file without conflict, which per the skill's own test means the article resolved the tension rather than papering it.
