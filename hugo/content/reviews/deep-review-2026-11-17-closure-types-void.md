### Critical Issues Found

- **Quotation given a false sentence boundary (Kriegel)** — row 3 above. Resolved.
- **Position overstated (Kriegel)**: "Kriegel argues that mysterianism is incoherent." His abstract targets McGinn's closure thesis and ends by formulating "a form of mysterianism which is in some sense more optimistic and in another more pessimistic than McGinn's". Resolved: now "McGinn's closure thesis is incoherent", with his alternative mysterianism stated.
- **Unverifiable paraphrase presented as Kriegel's reasoning**: "he takes questions to be individuated by the set of statements that would answer them. To pose a question Q is already to possess the conceptual resources to frame potential answers to Q" (note L53-54). The abstract supports only an appeal to "certain accepted assumption in the logic of questions, developed in the early sixties, mainly by Stahl"; Crossref's deposited reference list confirms Hamblin 1958 and Stahl 1962. Resolved by paraphrasing at the grade the abstract supports. Unverified at full text.
- **Map construction presented without a label (source/Map conflation)**: (a) the "assembly capacity" gloss on psychological closure. Vlerick & Boudry's psychological closure is the lack of an intuitive sense of grasping, and on their account a theory can be correctly represented and used without it. (b) "Each of Vlerick and Boudry's cases looked, to contemporaries, like something the mind could not take in": they say only that these theories overthrew commonsense. (c) The three-pile sorting of the catalogue. (d) The claim that Kriegel "collapses" the two readings, a distinction drawn fourteen years after he wrote. All four now marked as the Map's.
- **Calibration, overclaims**: "Representational closure is, by its own definition, undetectable" (contradicted by McGinn, who argues from within that we are closed and says a mind can recognise a problem it cannot solve, and by the page's own "plausibly representational" pile); "false positives for representational closure outnumber false negatives" (a frequency claim with no countable second term); "most of which are probably psychological with pockets of genuine representational limit" (asserts genuine closures exist). All three regraded. The 2026-06-26 review filed the asymmetry claim under a Dennett counterargument judged "adequate for genre"; a tenet-accepting reviewer would still flag an uncountable comparison, so it is a calibration matter and was reopened.
- **Tenet section presented closure as dualism's ally**: "Under Tenet 1 — Dualism, the distinction sharpens… The Map's dualism gives the representational reading a natural home", with no statement that cognitive closure is a naturalist thesis. McGinn's text calls his approach "naturalistic but not constructive" and lists Cartesian dualism among the supernatural answers. Resolved: paragraph rewritten on the pattern of [meta-epistemology-of-limits](/voids/meta-epistemology-of-limits/) L162 (no support; persistence does not discriminate; the conditional is the tenet applied; [P-V3](/positions/voids-as-evidence/#p-v3) cumulative step suspended). Minimal Quantum Interaction, Bidirectional Interaction and No Many Worlds stated as not engaged. Tenet 5 paragraph now says the page illustrates the tenet without adding support.
- **"Mysterianism is partially rehabilitated"** read as if the representational thesis had been vindicated. Now states that only Kriegel's objection is answered and that Vlerick & Boudry's stands.
- **Reference cited nowhere**: Chomsky 1976 had no inline citation. Removed (the 2026-06-26 review had waved it through as "Further-Reading-grade background").
- **Banned word**: "'Provisionally' is load-bearing". Replaced.
