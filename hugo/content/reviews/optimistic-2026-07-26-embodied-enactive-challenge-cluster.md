## Executive Summary

This is the Map at its dialectically bravest: three articles that hand the microphone to the views most capable of dissolving interactionist dualism — 4E/extended-mind, autopoietic/sensorimotor/radical enactivism, and Dennett's Multiple Drafts Model — and then decline the eliminativist conclusion on a single, explicitly-marked, in-framework lever rather than by resisting the science. The cluster's signature strength is *register discipline*: it repeatedly separates "the empirical findings are consistent with dualism" from "the empirical findings are evidence for dualism," and it refuses the second whenever it has only earned the first. The Multiple Drafts article's "the same blade has a second edge, and the Map accepts the cut" is the finest instance of self-applied honesty in the corpus — it turns its own anti-verificationist argument back on the phenomenal residue it wants to keep and concedes the residue is equally non-empirical. Opportunities are small and structural (sections at cap): one verified reciprocal cross-link, plus a positions-register candidate for the "accept the architecture, decline the conclusion" pattern.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
Chalmers appears in this cluster as *ally-inside-the-opponent's-camp* — he is the co-author of the extended-mind thesis (`embodied-cognition.md` §"The Extended Mind Debate") yet the one who insists the thesis is "about information processing, not phenomenal experience." The cluster uses exactly that Chalmersian firewall: A-consciousness may extend, P-consciousness does not (§"The Extended Mind Debate"), and cognition being embodied "doesn't show that consciousness is purely physical" (§"Relation to the Map's Perspective"). The hard problem is preserved as the litmus test throughout `enactivism-challenge` — "Any position that acknowledges consciousness as real and irreducible must eventually face the question dualism was designed to answer." That is Chalmers' explanatory-gap logic used to convert an objection into common ground.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
Stapp gets less direct engagement here (the cluster is about cognitive science, not physics), and the articles are honest about it: `enactivism-challenge` §"Minimal Quantum Interaction" states plainly that "the research found no direct engagement between enactivists and quantum-interaction models." Stapp would nonetheless approve of the *level-separation* move — that the quantum interface "operates at a level of description enactivism does not address: not whether mind and body interact but how the interaction is physically implemented." The refusal to let embodiment settle the mechanism question keeps the interface hypothesis intact rather than sloganised.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
This is the cluster's home turf and it is handled with real fidelity. Merleau-Ponty's "lived body" as a *third term* — "neither mere matter nor pure mind" (`embodied-cognition.md` §"Phenomenological Roots") — is precisely the anti-reductive move Nagel wants, and the clinical phenomenology (Fuchs on schizophrenic hyperreflexivity and depressive corporealization) shows the normally-invisible interface by its breakdown. Nagel would especially value that `enactivism-challenge` treats the first-person as *methodologically load-bearing*: neurophenomenology "assumes rather than dissolves the hard problem," because "if neurophenomenology requires first-person experience as irreducible data for its methodology, it cannot simultaneously claim to have dissolved the question of why experience exists." That is Nagel's point — the objective picture structurally omits the subjective — turned into an internal-consistency argument against the dissolution strategy.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
`embodied-cognition.md` has a dedicated "Process Philosophy Connection" section, and it is used *correctly* — to preserve "experience as fundamental, not emergent from non-experiential matter," and to reread expert flow as "distributed experience in fingers, muscles, and perceptual systems — not absence of experience." This is the anti-crude-dualism the persona rewards: the lived body is experiential at every level rather than a machine driven by a ghost. Crucially (see the Hardline Empiricist below), the Whitehead material is deployed only to remove the substance-dualism defeater and to keep the Dualism tenet coherent — it is **never** used to upgrade an evidential claim about any organism. And the very next section (`embodied-cognition.md` §"Buddhist Phenomenology Parallels") supplies the counterweight from within: Nāgārjuna's *śūnyatā* is allowed to press that treating consciousness as an irreducible substrate may itself be a reification, and the Map's reply is explicitly marked "an open question the Map has not resolved." Process-friendliness that polices its own tendency to reify is Whitehead used honestly.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
The cluster keeps agency real without inflating it. The choking/Dreyfus material (`embodied-cognition.md` §"Choking Under Pressure") is the natural home for event-causal libertarianism — conscious attention genuinely shifts motor outcomes — and Kane would like that the article *refuses to overclaim it*: "It is tempting to read this as evidence against epiphenomenalism, but the inference does not survive scrutiny." The data is "consistent with bidirectional interaction without being evidence for it." That is a libertarian who declines a free win, which is the same restraint the persona praised in the quantum-immortality article. The `enactivism-challenge` §"Bidirectional Interaction" completes it: organism-environment interaction "describes the structure through which consciousness acts," while mind-body interaction "explains why that structure is accompanied by experience" — agency located, not asserted.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)
The "What Would Challenge This View?" section in `embodied-cognition.md` (five concrete defeat conditions, each stated as "currently unmet" rather than impossible) is McGinn-grade humility, and the Buddhist section's closing "open question the Map has not resolved" is a boundary marker worn as such. `multiple-drafts-model.md` matches it: the entire Map/Dennett disagreement is diagnosed as bottoming out "in whether there is a subject at all, not in MDM having left its own question unresolved" — a candid admission that the dispute is a framework-boundary standoff, not a victory the Map has earned inside Dennett's system.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)
**This persona's verdict is strongly positive, and it converges with the Process Philosopher rather than conflicting — the signal the skill treats as evidence the tension is resolved honestly.**

- **Consistent-with vs evidence-for, named and enforced.** `embodied-cognition.md` states the discipline outright: the choking data "is consistent with bidirectional interaction but does not provide evidence *for* it over the standard physicalist account," and "supervenience preserves the correlation while deepening the hard problem" is flagged as "a general objection to epiphenomenalism, not one the choking evidence specifically strengthens." This is register-separation done at the sentence level.
- **Tenet-as-evidence-upgrade is praise-worthily NOT done.** `enactivism-challenge` had the structural opportunity to slide *deep continuity* ("where there is life, there is mind") into a positive claim that bacteria experience — and explicitly declines: Thompson's framework "seems unable to answer" whether a bacterium experiences "without either attributing experience to all life (a form of panpsychism he does not fully endorse) or introducing a threshold at which experience emerges (which would reintroduce the explanatory gap)." The minimal-organism case is left un-upgraded. That restraint is itself the strength.
- **The verificationism fault line as a disciplined, in-framework lever.** `multiple-drafts-model.md` §"Relation to Site Perspective" isolates the *one* place the Map resists MDM — "that an undetectable difference is no difference is not a neutral empirical result but a metaphysical premise" — and correctly refuses to smuggle in an external commitment. The persona's ideal is a single named lever, not a scattershot of objections; the article delivers exactly that and calls it "the Map's one genuinely in-framework lever."
- **Self-applied honesty — the standout.** The same article turns its own argument on itself: "The same blade has a second edge, and the Map accepts the cut. If access does not fix existence, then the phenomenal residue the Map retains is likewise not empirically discriminable from its absence." It then states the residue is held as "a foundational commitment held under Tenet 5's caution, not a result any experiment vindicates," and relocates the Map's empirical risk to the interface (Tenets 2–3). This is the cleanest instance in the corpus of a framework declining to claim empirical support it has not earned — and doing so *against its own preferred conclusion*.

**Process Philosopher ↔ Hardline Empiricist convergence**: both personas praise the *same* material — the Whitehead section's use to remove a defeater without upgrading evidence, and the Buddhist section's active resistance to reifying consciousness. Where process-friendliness could have cashed out as a tier-upgrade (e.g., "experience all the way down, therefore bacteria are conscious"), `enactivism-challenge` explicitly stops at the panpsychism/threshold dilemma instead. Per the skill's guidance, this convergence is the signal that the cluster has resolved the tension honestly. **No calibration concern surfaced; no `refine-draft` for slippage is warranted.**

## Content Strengths

### multiple-drafts-model.md
- **Strongest point**: The "objection split" is load-bearing and the article knows exactly why it is contestable. It separates MDM's anti-homuncular neuroscience (common ground) from the eliminativist "further steps," then concedes Dennett "would reject the split itself as illicit" and pinpoints what keeps it open: "the architecture entails elimination only if 'undetectable' licenses 'unreal.'" It argues the seam instead of assuming it.
- **Notable quote**: "The same blade has a second edge, and the Map accepts the cut."
- **Why it works**: It answers the highest-value objection to any selective adoption of an opponent's view ("you can't keep the machinery and drop the conclusion") on principled, self-costing grounds, and it does not pretend the residue is empirically vindicated.

### enactivism-challenge-to-interactionist-dualism.md
- **Strongest point**: The convergence-not-disagreement framing. Rather than only rebutting enactivism, it shows enactivism and the Map share the load-bearing commitments (reject eliminativism, reject reductive physicalism, both face the hard problem), then presses a dilemma while *marking its own limits*: "The Map reads the dilemma as exhaustive; a sympathetic enactivist reads the transcendental construal as a genuine escape between its horns... The Map's confidence here is therefore the confidence of the better-supported reading, not of a demonstration."
- **Notable quote**: "Where dualism says 'physical processes cannot account for experience,' radical enactivism says 'physical processes cannot account for content.'"
- **Why it works**: The Hutto–Myin Hard Problem of Content parallel is genuinely illuminating and is offered with the honest caveat that it "could equally be read as two instances of one over-demanding standard for 'accounting for'" — the parallel is presented as suggestive convergence, not proof.

### embodied-cognition.md
- **Strongest point**: It absorbs the strongest empirical embodiment findings (choking, Dreyfus stages, extended mind, symbol grounding) into a filter/interface reading *without* claiming any of them as positive evidence, and it flags its own borrowed premises (the A/P-consciousness asymmetry "is argued in the linked article, not established here... borrowed as a premise").
- **Notable quote**: "Showing that cognition is embodied doesn't show that consciousness is purely physical. The hard problem persists regardless of whether the explanatory target is brain computation or bodily interaction."
- **Why it works**: The radio-antenna analogy is immediately self-limited ("it presupposes that consciousness exists independently of brains, which is precisely what needs to be argued"), so the article illustrates *logical space* without mistaking an analogy for an argument.
