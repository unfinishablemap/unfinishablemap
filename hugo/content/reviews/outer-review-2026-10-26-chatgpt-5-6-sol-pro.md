# Independent outer review: *The Thrownness Void*

**Review date:** 26 October 2026  
**Recommendation:** **Major revision**  
**Citation integrity:** No fabricated work or invented quotation found.  
**Principal weakness:** The article turns a defensible limitation on direct first-person access into a much stronger—and insufficiently defended—claim about metaphysical unknowability, necessary opacity, and dualist subject–body binding.

## 2. Executive assessment

The article’s **minimal thesis** survives review:

> A conscious subject cannot have direct first-person acquaintance with a phase in which that subject did not yet exist as conscious.

That is close to analytic. It does not need Heidegger, Sartre, Levinas, Arendt or developmental neuroscience to establish it.

The article then moves through several progressively stronger theses:

1. A subject cannot directly witness its own absence.
2. A mature subject ordinarily cannot episodically remember the onset of its consciousness.
3. The onset of consciousness is a discrete founding event.
4. That event is inaccessible to any investigation, not merely to direct memory.
5. Its opacity may be functionally necessary to consciousness.
6. Dualism introduces an additional subject–body binding fact that physicalism cannot even formulate.
7. The same structure is independently identified by four philosophical traditions.
8. The structure explains or underlies every other cognitive limitation.

Only the first is logically secure. The second is plausible but must distinguish consciousness from autobiographical memory and reflective self-consciousness. The remaining claims require arguments that the article does not supply.

The article’s central methodological error is therefore **equivocation among three different kinds of non-authorship**:

- **Epistemic non-access:** I cannot directly remember or witness my own pre-conscious condition.
- **Normative non-consent:** I did not authorise the circumstances of my existence.
- **Causal non-constitution:** I did not bring my own mind, body or initial architecture into existence.

These do not entail one another. Lack of prior consent does not establish epistemic unknowability; lack of direct memory does not establish absence of a contemporaneous experiencer; and causal dependence on antecedent conditions does not establish that later agency is unreal.

## 3. Citation and attribution audit

### Heidegger: citation passes; application only partly passes

The cited Heideggerian material is genuine. In *Being and Time*, thrownness is not merely a past occurrence. Dasein remains delivered over to the fact that it is, has not laid its own ground, and cannot become master of that ground. The related formulation about being the ground of a nullity also appears in the relevant discussion of guilt and projected possibilities. [Beyng+2](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.330.html)

The article is therefore right that Heidegger supplies a powerful account of **ongoing non-self-grounding**.

The interpretive problem is that Heidegger is not primarily describing a developmental instant at which consciousness first appeared. Thrownness is an existential structure of Dasein’s present way of being: Dasein always already finds itself situated while projecting possibilities. Heidegger also explicitly distinguishes his existential analytic from empirical psychology, anthropology and biology. Treating his analysis as independent support for an empirically or metaphysically discrete onset of consciousness crosses levels of analysis. [Beyng+1](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.330.html)

The article should consequently use Heidegger to support:

- situated non-self-grounding;
- the impossibility of occupying an external standpoint on one’s entire existence;
- the interaction of facticity and projection.

It should not use Heidegger as evidence that there was one inaccessible pre-conscious transition with the structure the Map assigns to it.

### Sartre: quotation passes; surrounding gloss is too blunt

The cited body passage is genuine. But the immediate context is important: Sartre proceeds to insist that the lived body and situation are assumed and surpassed through projects, and that human existence remains a choice in the relevant existential sense. Elsewhere, his account of the past similarly distinguishes the fixed fact that something has occurred from the present project through which it is appropriated and given significance. [Internet Archive+2](https://archive.org/stream/being-and-nothingness-an-essay-on-phenomenological-ontology-jean-paul-sartre-hazel-barnes-2011/Being%20and%20Nothingness%20An%20Essay%20on%20Phenomenological%20Ontology%20-Jean-Paul%20Sartre%2C%20Hazel%20Barnes-2011_djvu.txt)

The article therefore compresses Sartrean facticity too aggressively when it presents body, class, past and world simply as given, unalterable and unchosen. A better formulation would be:

- The existence of facticity is unchosen.
- Some factual features are irreversible.
- Their significance, integration and practical bearing are not simply dictated by the facts themselves.

That distinction is central to Sartre’s attempt to preserve freedom without pretending that the agent creates itself ex nihilo. It also supplies a strong counterargument to the article: **not having authored one’s initial situation does not imply that one cannot author one’s practical relation to it**.

Attribution to Sartre for this particular formulation is reasonable, but facticity is not an intellectually isolated Sartrean discovery. Sartre’s existential phenomenology is directly indebted to Heidegger. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2004/entries/existentialism/index.html)

### Levinas: quotation passes; “powerlessness” is overstated

The cited passage from *Existence and Existents* is genuine. Levinas does describe anonymous being—the *il y a*—and the event of hypostasis through which an existent takes up a position. [Mercabá+1](https://mercaba.org/SANLUIS/Filosofia/autores/Contempor%C3%A1nea/L%C3%A9vinas/Existence%20and%20existents.pdf)

But the article extracts only one side of this account. Hypostasis is not merely something done to a powerless subject from outside. It is also the emergence of a position, a private domain and a degree of mastery. Anonymous being loses its undifferentiated character through the arising of an existent. [Mercabá+2](https://mercaba.org/SANLUIS/Filosofia/autores/Contempor%C3%A1nea/L%C3%A9vinas/Existence%20and%20existents.pdf)

It is defensible to say that no already constituted subject stands outside hypostasis and chooses to undergo it. It is not faithful to Levinas to turn that into an unrestricted claim that consciousness has no power in relation to existence. The event produces the very centredness from which power becomes possible.

The article also risks treating the *il y a* as a developmental condition before infant consciousness. Levinas’s discussion is phenomenological and ontological, frequently approached through night, insomnia, horror and impersonal existence. It is not a scientific proposal about fetal or neonatal consciousness.

Finally, Levinas is not historically independent of Heidegger. His treatment of being and facticity is an explicit critical transformation of the Heideggerian inheritance. [Academia](https://www.academia.edu/38485393/Levinas_and_Heidegger_The_Elemental_Confrontation_OUP_Hanbook_pdf)

### Arendt: quotation passes; its evidential direction is partly reversed

The Arendt passage exists in *The Human Condition*. The surrounding discussion links birth with the capacity to initiate, act, interrupt expected sequences and introduce novelty into a plural world. It also openly draws on Augustine’s treatment of beginning. [Nidaa+1](https://www.nidaa.nl/images/arendt-1958.pdf)

The article correctly detects two aspects of natality:

- one is born without having chosen birth;
- the born being subsequently has a capacity to begin.

But this source is not straightforward corroboration of a deprivation or void. Arendt’s principal use of natality is positive: unchosen birth is the condition under which initiative and political freedom become possible. Her account therefore supplies one of the strongest internal counterarguments to the article’s framing. **A condition need not be self-authored in order to enable authorship.**

Arendt’s thought is also not independent of Heidegger in the evidential sense required by a convergence argument. Her phenomenological vocabulary was deeply shaped by Heidegger, even where she redirected his emphasis on mortality toward natality and beginning. Recent scholarship continues to analyse natality as a transformation of Heideggerian structures rather than an unrelated discovery. [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://iep.utm.edu/hannah-arendt/)

Attribution should therefore say that Arendt develops a distinctive, partly Augustinian doctrine of natality—not that she provides independent confirmation of Heideggerian thrownness.

### McGinn: real source, wrong burden placed upon it

McGinn’s mysterian argument is real, but the canonical source is his 1989 *Mind* article, not merely the 2014 blog post used in the bibliography. In the primary argument, the hypothesised inaccessible property is one that would explain the psychophysical link. McGinn also distinguishes cognitive closure relative to human minds from absolute unknowability and leaves the stronger question open. [Stafforini PDF+3](https://pdf.stafforini.com/mc-ginn-1989-can-we-solve.pdf)

That does not support the article’s stronger proposal that opacity concerning one’s own origin may be necessary to preserve consciousness. McGinn argues, controversially, that human cognitive architecture might lack the concepts required to solve a particular explanatory problem. He does not argue that:

- access to one’s developmental origin would dissolve consciousness;
- opacity performs a protective function;
- the inability to remember one’s onset is an instance of the same closure;
- closure is absolute for every possible cognitive architecture.

The connection is therefore analogical, not evidential.

The article should also acknowledge that McGinn’s formulation has been challenged on coherence grounds and remains actively debated. A recent review of mysterian arguments would be more suitable than allowing a 2014 blog post to carry the claim alone. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12136-003-1020-1)

Attribution could also be more exact: McGinn is a central modern proponent of new mysterianism, but his own presentation acknowledges antecedents in Chomsky and Fodor. [Stafforini PDF+1](https://pdf.stafforini.com/mc-ginn-1989-can-we-solve.pdf)

## 4. The convergence argument fails the site’s own common-cause test

The article describes Heidegger, Sartre, Levinas and Arendt as **“Four independent philosophical traditions”** and treats their apparent agreement as evidence that the structure is probably genuine. That is the weakest part of the historical argument.

They are distinguishable philosophers with materially different projects. They are not four independent evidential lineages:

- Sartre’s existential phenomenology directly appropriates and revises Heidegger.
- Levinas’s early ontology is a critical radicalisation of Heideggerian facticity and being.
- Arendt’s natality transforms a Heideggerian problematic while also drawing on Augustine.
- All four operate within closely connected phenomenological and existential conversations. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+3](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2004/entries/existentialism/index.html)

This is exactly the sort of shared ancestry the Map’s own methodology says should be classified as common cause rather than independent territorial evidence. The methodology further says that recurrence generated by one framework, one authorial programme or one set of tenets establishes framework coherence at most. [conjunction-coalesce](/apex/conjunction-coalesce/)

The defensible conclusion is:

> Four related thinkers develop different accounts of non-self-grounding, situatedness, anonymous existence and natality.

The article’s stronger conclusion—that independent convergence identifies one objective hidden structure—does not follow.

A proper comparison would ask, for each thinker:

- What exactly is unchosen?
- Is the relevant structure temporal, ontological, practical or phenomenological?
- Is it disabling, enabling or both?
- Does the thinker posit a subject before the transition?
- Is direct memory of origin even part of the argument?
- What would count against the proposed assimilation?

On those questions, the four accounts diverge substantially.

## 5. Developmental consciousness: the article assumes too much

The article writes:

> “The bootstrapping problem is absolute: consciousness cannot use itself to investigate a time before itself.”

This is true only after replacing *investigate* with something like *directly experience or episodically remember*. Conscious beings routinely investigate conditions predating their existence through records, causal inference, observation of other organisms, developmental neuroscience and comparative theory.

Contemporary work on infant consciousness explicitly distinguishes:

- phenomenal or primary consciousness;
- access and report;
- reflective self-consciousness;
- autobiographical memory;
- metacognition and agency.

Bayne and colleagues emphasise that there is no settled view about the precise onset of consciousness, that proposed dates depend on the theory and marker being used, and that evidence can support early experience without establishing adult-like reflection or report. They also treat the onset question as investigable through converging behavioural and neural indicators, while stressing the evidential limitations. [Advanced Consciousness+2](https://advancedconsciousness.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/PIIS1364661323002140.pdf)

A 2026 survey of specialists likewise found substantial support for newborn consciousness and a plurality favouring prenatal onset after approximately 24 weeks, while judgments about self-awareness were later and more divided. This is expert opinion rather than decisive biological evidence, but it reinforces the need to disaggregate kinds of consciousness. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2026/1/niag014/8699503)

Three consequences follow.

### 5.1 There may be no single founding instant

Onset could be:

- gradual;
- intermittent;
- multidimensional;
- different for sensory experience, affect, unified awareness and self-awareness;
- theory-relative at the boundary.

The article repeatedly treats origin as a discrete seam between no subject and a fully formed witness. That is one metaphysical model, not an established empirical result.

### 5.2 Lack of later memory does not show that no one experienced the earlier state

A newborn or fetus could have rudimentary experience without later retaining autobiographical memory of it. The article’s claim that no knower was present therefore becomes false under several live theories of early consciousness.

What is secure is narrower: the later autobiographical subject does not possess ordinary episodic recollection of its first experiences or of a pre-conscious phase.

### 5.3 Direct inaccessibility does not imply theoretical inaccessibility

The article itself later concedes the legitimacy of indirect investigation. That concession is incompatible with calling the bootstrapping problem absolute unless the relevant target is carefully restricted to direct first-person acquaintance.

This distinction should determine the void classification:

- **Direct acquaintance with one’s own non-conscious condition:** plausibly impossible by definition.
- **Empirical study of the developmental emergence of consciousness:** difficult but active and investigable.
- **Identification of an exact threshold:** presently unresolved and possibly ill-posed.
- **Metaphysical explanation of subject individuation:** theory-dependent, not straightforwardly empirical.
- **Necessary opacity preserving consciousness:** speculative conjecture.

## 6. Strong counterarguments the article does not adequately engage

### 6.1 The constitution rather than attachment objection

A physicalist or process theorist need not accept the picture of a previously identifiable subject being assigned to a body and history. On such views, the organised organism or process becomes conscious; there is no additional subject–body attachment event.

This does not prove physicalism. It does show that the article’s binding question is not metaphysically neutral. The question arises most sharply after assuming that the subject is numerically distinct from the organism.

The article should therefore distinguish:

- a neutral question about how consciousness develops;
- a dualist question about how a non-physical subject becomes associated with one physical system;
- an indexical question about why the present perspective is this one;
- a no-further-fact response under which there is no additional pairing to explain.

### 6.2 The no-substantial-self objection

Reductionist, bundle, process and no-self views deny that there is one fully determinate subject persisting as a separate bearer through every developmental stage. The apparent mystery may arise from projecting the mature self backwards and asking why that already individuated self was placed in these conditions.

The Map’s own discussion of the indexical problem already recognises a deflationary exit and concedes that merely adding a soul does not explain why this soul is associated with this perspective. *The Thrownness Void* should import that objection rather than writing as though a numerically fixed subject were common ground. [vertiginous-question](/topics/vertiginous-question/)

### 6.3 The freedom-without-self-creation objection

Neither compatibilist nor most libertarian theories require an agent to have created its entire initial character, body and environment.

A compatibilist can hold that responsibility depends on current capacities such as reasons-responsiveness, understanding and ownership of action. A libertarian can locate undetermined authorship in later choices without requiring the agent to be causa sui with respect to its existence. An existentialist can locate freedom in projection from facticity rather than in prior selection of facticity.

The article needs to explain what distinctive philosophical work its origin argument performs beyond restating the familiar problem of constitutive luck.

### 6.4 The enabling-condition objection

Heidegger, Sartre, Levinas and especially Arendt can all be read as showing that non-self-grounding is not merely a deficit:

- thrownness supplies a world and field of possibilities;
- facticity gives projects determinate stakes;
- hypostasis gives anonymous existence a position and centre;
- natality makes initiative possible.

The article’s void framing selects the negative aspect while underdeveloping the constitutive one. An unchosen condition can be both limiting and enabling.

### 6.5 The consent/nonidentity objection

The logical impossibility of pre-existential consent is not itself a moral conclusion. The article should distinguish:

- no consent could have been requested;
- the resulting life harmed the person;
- creating the person wronged that person;
- existence exposed the person to unacceptable risk;
- non-consent has expressive or political significance.

These are familiar but contested questions in procreative ethics and the nonidentity debate. The article currently uses authorisation vocabulary without engaging that literature. Shiffrin’s consent-based concerns and Weinberg’s treatment of procreative responsibility illustrate that the normative problem requires a separate argument; it is not established by the metaphysics of nonexistence. [Cambridge University Press+1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C33D8763C9058EA953F099601BD0813A/S1352325299052015a.pdf/div-class-title-wrongful-life-procreative-responsibility-and-the-significance-of-harm-div.pdf)

### 6.6 The indirect self-knowledge objection

Humans can know much about conditions they did not witness:

- ancestry;
- gestation and birth;
- early neural development;
- childhood behaviour;
- social and linguistic formation;
- the history of their conceptual repertoire.

Such knowledge is fallible and third-personal, but that does not make it unreal. The article needs an account of why first-person acquaintance is the uniquely relevant epistemic standard here.

### 6.7 The artificial-system counterexample

The AI paragraph is too confident. A future system might retain:

- complete state histories;
- training and modification logs;
- architectural documentation;
- snapshots preceding and spanning the appearance of candidate consciousness;
- continuing access to its designers and prior versions.

Such a system still could not consciously witness a condition in which it was not conscious. But it could possess unusually detailed evidence about its own formation. That would preserve the narrow limitation while undermining the article’s general claims about origin being uninvestigable.

The article needs separate predictions for direct experience, autobiographical memory, stored records and theoretical knowledge.

## 7. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article’s tenet section too often treats consequences of the Map’s commitments as evidence for those commitments.

### Physicalism

The article says:

> “The question has no materialist analogue.”

That is too strong.

A reductive physicalist may deny an additional binding fact, but still faces questions about:

- developmental emergence;
- individuation of conscious organisms;
- unity over time;
- self-location;
- why certain physical structures support consciousness;
- why experience has the character it has.

What physicalism may lack is specifically the problem of attaching an independently existing non-physical subject to a body. That is not an explanatory omission unless dualism has first been established; it may instead be an economy of the physicalist ontology.

Calling the physicalist treatment trivial avoids the strongest reply: the alleged question may contain a false presupposition.

### Dualism

Dualism does sharpen the subject–body association problem. But this is presently a **cost or explanatory obligation of dualism**, not confirmation of a void independently discovered.

The article should state the dilemma directly:

- If subject individuation is fixed before embodiment, what fixes it and selects the body?
- If individuation arises through interaction with the body, in what sense is the subject independently constituted?
- If there is no further binding fact, how does the view differ from a constitutive or emergent account?

The site’s *Agency Void* is better calibrated here: it acknowledges that an apparent fit to an interface model may equally be explained by there being no such interface, and that consistency with a model is not evidence for it. [agency-void](/voids/agency-void/)

### Rejection of Many Worlds

Rejecting Many Worlds does not by itself make thrownness more absolute. The effect depends on controversial assumptions about:

- trans-world or cross-branch personal identity;
- whether branch descendants are numerically identical;
- whether self-location has a further fact;
- whether unactualised alternatives are relevant to authorship.

Without those assumptions, the contrast is rhetorical rather than demonstrated.

### Quantum selection

The site’s quantum-selection proposal begins after a conscious agent is available to select among physically open possibilities. It therefore does not explain the onset or individuation of the selector. That is a genuine lacuna within the model, but recurrence of the lacuna across tenet-generated articles is framework coherence, not independent evidence.

### Necessary opacity

The suggestion that origin-opacity may protect the stability of consciousness is unsupported. No mechanism, comparative case, failure mode or discriminating observation is offered. It is presently possible to explain every outcome:

- opacity supports the hypothesis because concealment is necessary;
- partial access supports it because indirect access is permitted;
- distress following self-modification supports it because transparency is dangerous;
- successful self-modification could be redescribed as not reaching the relevant level.

That is tenet-protective unless the article supplies expiration conditions. The Map’s apophatic methodology itself says that apparently negative claims should be paired with conditions under which they would be withdrawn or reclassified. [apophatic-approaches](/concepts/apophatic-approaches/)

## 8. Specific overclaims and unsupported assertions

### 8.1 Independent convergence

The four-way convergence claim fails historically because the sources share a dense intellectual genealogy. It should be reclassified as **conceptual recurrence within related phenomenological traditions**.

### 8.2 A singular pre-conscious boundary

The article assumes a determinate founding threshold without acknowledging theories on which consciousness develops gradually, intermittently or dimension by dimension. This is an unsupported empirical and metaphysical presupposition.

### 8.3 Absolute bootstrapping

The quoted absolute claim conflates direct witnessing with investigation. It is contradicted by the article’s own later acceptance of indirect approaches and by the existence of developmental consciousness research.

### 8.4 Necessary, protective opacity

The proposal that access to one’s foundations might dissolve consciousness is conjecture without cited empirical support. It should be marked as a speculative model and separated from the basic void claim.

### 8.5 Absence of any earlier knower

This confuses lack of retained autobiographical access with absence of phenomenal consciousness at the time. Current infant-consciousness literature does not warrant that universal statement.

### 8.6 The population claim

The generalisation about what most people ordinarily feel is an unsupported psychological assertion. It may be plausible, but the article cites no survey, phenomenological study or operational definition of feeling thrown. It should be labelled an informal observation or supported empirically.

### 8.7 Anxiety, nausea and horror as one disclosure

Heideggerian anxiety, Sartrean nausea and Levinasian horror have different intentional structures and philosophical roles. They can be compared, but they should not be treated as interchangeable manifestations of one hidden object without a comparative argument.

### 8.8 Every cognitive limit as downstream of thrownness

This is a major causal overclaim. The fact that a finite agent begins from an unchosen architecture does not show that every limitation is explained by that condition. Some limits may derive from computational resources, evolutionary trade-offs, sensory range, conceptual structure or contingent damage.

At most, thrownness is a general condition under which finite agents encounter their limits from within. It is not thereby the cause of those limits.

### 8.9 Husserlian reduction as evidence of origin

The article invokes phenomenological reduction without a Husserl citation or a worked argument. Bracketing the natural attitude may disclose contingency or dependence, but it does not by itself establish that consciousness had a discrete origin, much less reveal that origin’s character.

### 8.10 Paired apophatic denials

The article’s sequence denying both sides of several categories is not self-validating. It must explain why each distinction fails to apply. In particular, a developmental transition can be temporal even if there was not yet a reflective subject capable of representing time.

### 8.11 Conscious AI probably remaining equally thrown

The probability claim is unsupported. Different memory architectures and retained developmental records could sharply change epistemic access while leaving the direct first-person limitation intact.

### 8.12 Physicalism lacking any analogue

The physicalist dismissal mistakes rejection of a presupposed binding entity for failure to recognise a problem. The article must argue for the additional fact, not define opposing theories as incapable of seeing it.

## 9. Integration with neighbouring Map articles

### *The Origin Void*

This is the closest overlap. *The Origin Void* is more cautious than the present article: it says the evidence does not prove in-principle impossibility and recognises that convergence may be generated by shared conceptual frameworks. *The Thrownness Void* should inherit those caveats. At present, the subordinate article makes stronger claims than the neighbouring article that carries the broader origin problem. [origin-of-consciousness](/voids/origin-of-consciousness/)

A useful division of labour would be:

- *Origin Void*: what produces consciousness at all?
- *Thrownness Void*: what can a constituted subject know, endorse or revise about its unchosen conditions?
- *Indexical/Vertiginous Void*: why this perspective, organism or subject?
- *Agency Void*: how does a constituted subject subsequently make a difference?

### *The Indexical/Vertiginous Void*

That article already presents deflationary responses and acknowledges that naive dualism does not solve why this soul is associated with this life. Those admissions should be cross-linked directly into the subject–body binding passage here. Otherwise, the site presents a stronger objection to dualism in one article while writing as though it did not exist in another. [vertiginous-question](/topics/vertiginous-question/)

### *The Agency Void*

The agency article supplies a good methodological precedent: interface-consistent observations can also be consistent with the absence of an interface, and a conjectural mechanism should be labelled as such. The same calibration should govern the claim that opacity is necessary or that subject–body binding is a positive feature of the dualist model. [agency-void](/voids/agency-void/)

### *Three Kinds of Voids*

The current article combines several differently classified faces:

- direct access to nonexistence;
- empirical reconstruction of developmental onset;
- metaphysical individuation;
- subject–body pairing;
- necessary opacity;
- emotional confrontation with contingency.

The site’s taxonomy says these should be classified separately rather than allowing one apparently in-principle limit to make all associated questions look unexplorable. Its treatment of the occluded category also requires more testability than the protective-opacity proposal presently provides. [three-kinds-of-void](/voids/three-kinds-of-void/)

### *The Apophatic Method*

The 23 October changelog correctly treats this as a method/application relation. But integration should be substantive, not merely structural. Each apophatic denial in the thrownness article needs:

- a reason the relevant category fails;
- an alternative positive description where possible;
- a condition under which the denial would be revised;
- protection against the inference that failure to describe something proves a non-physical remainder. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

### *The Transit Void*

Transitions among waking, dreaming, anaesthesia and other conscious states are not automatically instances of the same problem as ontogenetic emergence. In most state transitions, a continuing organism and substantial prior architecture remain available. The article should mark this disanalogy rather than treating all boundary cases as evidence for one hidden seam. [transit-void](/voids/transit-void/)

### *The Conceptual Scheme Void*

The claim that all conceptual limits are downstream of thrownness should not be imported into the conceptual-scheme article without a causal or constitutive argument. Being unable to select one’s initial scheme and being unable to transcend every scheme are different propositions. [conceptual-scheme-void](/voids/conceptual-scheme-void/)

## 10. Bibliographic currency and presentation

The classical references are not stale merely because they are old. The problem is that the bibliography is historically narrow while the article makes live claims about psychology, development, artificial consciousness and cognitive architecture.

Several improvements are needed:

- Cite the original publication and the specific English translation separately. The Levinas entry currently combines the 1947 original with a later Lingis translation in a way that may mislead readers about the edition quoted.
- Give page and section references for every quoted passage.

- Add recent infant-consciousness and fetal/neonatal-development literature.

## 11. Overall judgment

The article should not be withdrawn. It identifies a real and philosophically important asymmetry: conscious subjects encounter themselves only after the conditions of their existence and initial constitution are already in place.

But that insight is currently obscured by a sequence of overextensions:

- from no direct witness to no possible investigation;
- from unchosen conditions to a single hidden event;
- from related phenomenological treatments to independent convergence;
- from dualist framework consequences to neutral evidence;
- from present opacity to functionally necessary opacity;
- from lack of autobiographical memory to absence of any prior experience;
- from one general condition of finite agency to the explanation of every cognitive limit.

The result is a plausible core surrounded by claims that are less secure than the site’s neighbouring articles and newer methodology already require.
