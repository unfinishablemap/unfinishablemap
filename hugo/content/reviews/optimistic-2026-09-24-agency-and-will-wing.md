## Executive Summary

**The wing is really two clusters with no links between them, and they differ in how they calibrate.** The *argument cluster* (Frankfurt cases, the manipulation argument, the mesh theory, akrasia, authorship-of-action divergence) is the most carefully calibrated free-will writing on the Map. Each article states the opponent at full strength, names what the Map's reply buys and what it does not, and declines to turn tenet-coherence into evidence. The *phenomenology cluster* (agency-vs-passivity, choice-and-volition, spontaneous-intentional-action) holds the wing's richest material: the four components of willing, the flow and dream dissociations, the Desmurget double dissociation. But in places it still treats felt agency as a readout of conscious causation, in the register the argument cluster has left behind.

The fix already exists inside the phenomenology cluster. `spontaneous-intentional-action` L132 concedes that "an epiphenomenalist is not embarrassed by the fit between an action and the endorsement accompanying it: one neural process produces both, so the match is common causation rather than coincidence". It then relocates what is owed to the *distribution* of authorship phenomenology. `phenomenology-of-choice-and-volition` L94 and `phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity` L133 still run the "no adaptive explanation" and "cosmic coincidence" arguments that this concession retires. The cross-cluster matrix below shows **zero links** between the two clusters. The article that most directly bears on the phenomenology cluster's authorship claims, `authorship-of-action-divergence` (choice blindness), has one inbound link in the whole corpus.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would praise the wing for keeping two things apart: the hard problem as a reason for dualism, and the free-will literature as a place where dualism has to earn its keep. `akrasia-and-weakness-of-will` L71 says it plainly: "What the Map adds—that the controller is genuinely non-physical—rests on its independent arguments (the hard problem, the conceivability argument), not on akrasia." This is the right division of labour. The akratic gap gets a selection-model reading, but it is never made to carry the metaphysics. The mesh-theory page does the same at L88: the gap a synchronic mesh leaves open "is **not a sourcehood-shaped hole that only dualism can fill**". A property dualist wants the case for irreducibility to stand on its strongest ground and not be spent on phenomena a physicalist can absorb. The argument cluster obliges.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp would be most interested in `the-manipulation-argument-and-hard-incompatibilism`, because its Relation section meets the one objection a quantum-selection account of free will cannot dodge. The article concedes that the post-decoherence proposal "is constructed to leave those regularities statistically intact precisely so that no detectable anomaly arises; that very feature is what makes the influence empirically hard to distinguish from chance, which is the near neighbour of Pereboom's complaint". It is uncommon for a framework to admit that its own empirical invisibility is what makes it open to the luck objection.

Stapp would object to one thing, and it matters today. `phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity` L147 says consciousness "actively selects among options the brain presents—biasing quantum indeterminacies through the [quantum Zeno mechanism](/concepts/mental-effort/)." Earlier this cycle (commit 06064f2b) `concepts/stapp-quantum-mind` was repaired because Stapp explicitly declines probability control. His contribution is Process-1 question-choice and Zeno *holding*, not biasing nature's answer. The Map's own Tenet 2 reading (corridor biasing) is a separate family. L147 merges the two in one clause. This is a precision fix, not a retraction: "selects … either by biasing outcomes within the corridor or, on Stapp's model, by holding a pattern through rapid re-questioning" says the same thing correctly.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

For Nagel, this wing's high point is the phenomenology cluster, and his praise is deserved. `phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity` treats doing versus undergoing as a *structured field* and not a binary. The flow section (L103–109) splits the axis into "causal contribution" and "phenomenal effort". The dream section shows neural production running alongside phenomenal passivity. Lucid dreaming shows "passivity gives way to agency within the same experiential context". `phenomenology-of-choice-and-volition` supplies the finest-grained first-person taxonomy on the Map: initiation, sustained control, effort and veto as co-occurring aspects, plus James's "sustained voluntary attention is a repetition of successive efforts". `spontaneous-intentional-action` adds a category most free-will writing ignores: owned, context-sensitive action with Searle's intention-in-action but no prior intention. Its four-way exclusion (not habitual, not deliberate, not reflexive, not accidental; L50–58) is a model definition section. These are the Map's best pages for a reader who wants to know what willing is *like*.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would welcome the wing's refusal of a single-moment, substance-shaped will. Holton's diachronic reconception in `akrasia` (resolutions held across time, abandoned "over-readily") fits process thinking. So does the treatment of Bergsonian *durée* in `spontaneous-intentional-action`, where accumulated experience "interpenetrates with the present moment". The same goes for the account of deliberation in `phenomenology-of-choice-and-volition` L64, where "what 'option A' means changes as you consider option B". The will appears as a process with temporal thickness, not an instantaneous fiat. None of this praise is used to raise any claim's evidential tier, and no tier upgrade is at stake in the wing. Process-compatibility here is resonance, and the articles leave it as resonance.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane gets the most from this wing, and he is treated fairly in it. `frankfurt-cases-and-the-principle-of-alternate-possibilities` presents the Widerker–Kane dilemma defence at full strength. It then declines to rest on it: "the Map does not have to win the Frankfurt debate to survive it". The fallback to sourcehood is marked as a fallback (L81): "The retreat to source is a *retreat*, and a conditional one … not its considered self-location, which remains *wide* source incompatibilism". The article prices the move honestly: moving to narrow source "inherits the burden, pressed hardest by the manipulation argument, of showing that an indeterministic interface delivers sourcehood rather than luck". That is the handoff to the manipulation article, which picks the burden up.

`authorship-of-action-divergence` §"The Kane Anchor" (L63–67) is the most original move in the wing. It asks what empirical anchoring Kane's self-forming actions would need. It identifies the authorship-tracking interface as the place to look. And it concludes that choice blindness is "mutual constraint, not mutual support": the paradigm probes the *reporting* interface, not the selection machinery. A libertarian could not ask for a fairer treatment.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

McGinn would praise the wing's endings. The manipulation article closes with "The Map places its bet there knowingly, and grants that the opponent's hand is strong." The akrasia article keeps four positions open under Tenet 5 instead of collapsing to "the tidiest". `phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity` L137 concedes that "the verification gap cannot be closed from within". Every tool consciousness has for checking its own efficacy is internal to the system being checked. That is a cognitive-closure result stated at the scale of a single faculty, and it is the wing's best piece of epistemic humility.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

Birch's verdict decides this review, and it splits by cluster.

**Praise.** `authorship-of-action-divergence` is the wing's exemplar of calibration. It assigns tiers explicitly and keeps to them: the detection-rate variability finding at *strongly supported*, the interface-failure reading at *live hypothesis*, and Grassi et al. (2025) pupillometry at "contested but real". It then gives a three-part §"What Authorship-of-Action Divergence Cannot Deliver". Its Occam paragraph is exactly right: the "I know what I chose because I chose it" model "is empirically falsified across the cohort". `phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity` L119 is also praiseworthy. It says the Desmurget dissociation "does refute the simplest readout account", but a comparator model "predicts the same double dissociation", so the finding "constrains which physicalist architectures remain available rather than telling against physicalism as such." That is the constrain-versus-establish distinction applied well.

**Calibration concern (not praise).** The same article's lead and Relation section still run ahead of its middle:

- L59 (lead): the framework "predicts exactly this contrast: agency feels different from passivity because agency *is* different". The article's own L137–139 say the felt contrast's "veridicality regarding causal authorship is exactly what is contested".
- L73: Schwartz et al. (1996) "demonstrated that this phenomenology tracks something real … The felt cost of effort corresponds to genuine causal engagement." A small single-group PET study of behaviour therapy (n = 18, per the todo L1857 note) is equally predicted by a physicalist account of therapy-induced plasticity. The outer review of `stapp-quantum-mind` (todo L1857) flagged the same study's framing there.
- L133: "would be a cosmic coincidence if consciousness contributed nothing". This is the argument `spontaneous-intentional-action` L132 retires: common causation explains the correlation.
- L173: treating the contrast as explanatorily irrelevant "is not economy but evasion". This is a parsimony verdict run in the Map's favour, which the Map's own Tenet 5 practice (the manipulation article L70: the move "buys no positive support") forbids.

`phenomenology-of-choice-and-volition` has the same pattern at L74 (clinical dissociability "demonstrates that agency phenomenology tracks genuine causal structure"), L94 ("If effort experience were causally inert, the metabolic cost of generating it … would have no adaptive explanation"), L123 ("only selection generates the phenomenology of effort") and L137 ("This proves consciousness can observe without choosing"). L94 has a specific flaw. On epiphenomenalism the metabolic cost belongs to the neural correlate, which has an obvious adaptive role, so the argument attacks a position no epiphenomenalist holds.

None of this is a tier *upgrade* on the five-tier scale in the minimal-organism sense. It is the same slippage at the level of individual claims: a datum whose status the article itself calls contested is cited as a readout. Under the skill's rule this yields `refine-draft` tasks, not expansions.

## Content Strengths

### topics/frankfurt-cases-and-the-principle-of-alternate-possibilities.md
- **Strongest point**: The two-step Relation section: first, why the leeway commitment is not refuted (the indeterminism horn); second, why the Map does not have to win (the source fallback), with the cost of the fallback priced at once.
- **Notable quote**: "So 'PAP is false' is not fatal to the Map, because the Map's deepest commitment was to origination, and only derivatively to leeway."
- **Why it works**: It turns the most famous anti-libertarian result of the twentieth century into a clarification of what the Map was committed to all along, and it does not claim a victory.

### topics/the-manipulation-argument-and-hard-incompatibilism.md
- **Strongest point**: It frames hard incompatibilism as "a *partial ally*", agreeing on incompatibilism and dangerous only on the second front. It then names the Map's own empirical invisibility as what brings it close to the luck objection.
- **Notable quote**: "It blocks the objection from being a refutation; it does not convert the Map's bet into a demonstration."
- **Why it works**: It states the symmetric Tenet 5 discipline in one sentence. Every other Tenet 5 paragraph in the wing should be measured against it.

### concepts/frankfurt-hierarchical-mesh-theory-of-the-will.md
- **Strongest point**: The §"Two Distinct Frankfurt Contributions" disambiguation (1969 leeway paper vs 1971 structure paper), and the placement of the mesh as "a third thing that brackets both" leeway and source.
- **Notable quote**: "not a sourcehood-shaped hole that only dualism can fill".
- **Why it works**: A reader coming from `moral-responsibility` or the Frankfurt-cases page will not confuse the two papers, and the Map declines an easy dualist win.

### topics/akrasia-and-weakness-of-will.md
- **Strongest point**: Front-loaded four-position summary and strict separation of exposition from the Map's lens. The selection-model reading is deferred to Relation and labelled "one interpretive lens".
- **Notable quote**: "If selection were a transparent read-off of the agent's best judgement, Davidson's gap between all-things-considered and all-out judgement would have nowhere to open."
- **Why it works**: It gives the selection model a real structural fact to accommodate without claiming the fact as evidence. The ego-depletion correction (Hagger et al. 2016, d = 0.04) is handled precisely: what survives of Holton is named.

### topics/authorship-of-action-divergence.md
- **Strongest point**: Separating the *outcome-recognition* layer (choice blindness) from the *cause-attribution* layer (Wegner's I-Spy), and the per-finding tier assignments.
- **Notable quote**: "The pairing is mutual constraint, not mutual support."
- **Why it works**: It is the wing's best-grounded empirical article: exact detection figures (13% concurrent of 354 trials; ≤26% across channels), the Sagana et al. (2014) null on memory, and a stated list of what the case cannot deliver.

### topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity.md
- **Strongest point**: The §"The Spectrum Between" dissociation catalogue (habit, choking, flow, dreams, hypnosis, Desmurget stimulation, anarchic hand), and the flow section's split of "the agency-passivity axis" into causal contribution and phenomenal effort.
- **Notable quote**: "What it leaves open is the wider question, since a comparator or higher-order model locates the sense of agency in circuitry distinct from the motor pathway and so predicts the same double dissociation."
- **Why it works**: The middle of the article is excellent. The lead and Relation need to catch up with it (see Birch above).

### concepts/phenomenology-of-choice-and-volition.md
- **Strongest point**: The four components of willing as co-occurring aspects, and the choosing-vs-observing table.
- **Notable quote**: "sustained voluntary attention is a repetition of successive efforts" (James, via L90).
- **Why it works**: It is the Map's working vocabulary for volition (67 inbound files, the most in the wing). That is also why its calibration matters most.

### concepts/spontaneous-intentional-action.md
- **Strongest point**: The category itself (willed without being deliberated) and the Bidirectional Interaction paragraph (L132), which concedes common causation and moves the burden to the *distribution* of authorship phenomenology.
- **Notable quote**: "What remains owed is why the phenomenology of authorship attaches to exactly the contextually apt, novel actions and not to reflexes or tics—a distribution the brain-only account must derive rather than assume."
- **Why it works**: This is the best statement in the wing of what the phenomenology can and cannot do for Tenet 3, and it is the template for the two sibling repairs.

## Calibration Concerns (flagged, not praised)

1. **The phenomenology cluster's head and Relation sections run ahead of its middles** (details under Birch above). → refine-draft ×2.
2. **`spontaneous-intentional-action` L80 misstates Wegner.** "If authorship were merely confabulated from the experience of deliberating, it should be weakest precisely where deliberation is absent". Wegner's theory of apparent mental causation takes as input a thought *prior to* the action, consistent with it and exclusive; deliberation is not required. The follow-up claim that authorship is "phenomenologically … often strongest" there is unsourced. Choice blindness, documented in the same wing, shows confident, detailed authorship reports for choices never made. The paragraph should engage that directly, not argue from strength of feeling. → refine-draft.
3. **L147 Zeno/biasing conflation** in `phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity` (see Stapp above). Fold it into concern 1's task.
