**C1. James quotation from the wrong work.** Before: "Every conscious state has a centre surrounded by a fringe that 'shades insensibly into a subconscious more.'", with only *Principles* (1890) in References. The string is absent from both volumes of *Principles* (archive.org `theprinciplesofp01jameuoft`, `principlesofpsyc02jameuoft`; positive controls "psychic overtone" and "steeped and dyed" found). It is in *A Pluralistic Universe* (1909), Lecture VII, p. 288, verified in the 1909 Longmans, Green printing (`pluralisticunive00jameuoft`) and the 1920 impression (`pluralisticunive033539mbp`). James's subject there is "My present field of consciousness". After: the sentence is in James's own first-person framing, cited (1909, p. 288), with James 1909 added to References. **Seed**: `research/voids-attention-created-voids-2026-01-29` L118, which lists it among "key points" from the PhilArchive MANCFC source (Mangan). That source does not contain it either (grep 0).

**C2. Commentator's gloss credited to James.** Before: "The fringes modify the theme and bridge gaps between thoughts but cannot be the primary focus of attention. Attempting to focus on a fringe transforms it into a theme while generating new fringes." "primary focus", "bridge gaps" and "modify a theme" each have 0 hits in *Principles* vols 1–2, in *A Pluralistic Universe*, and in Mangan (2007). The "cannot be focused" claim is Mangan's reading: "James constantly notes the elusive character of fringe experiences: they cannot be turned into nuclei by an act of attention" (Mangan 2007, p. 680). "while generating new fringes" is a Map extension. After: James's own words ("psychic overtone", "aware of relations and objects but dimly perceived", vol. 1 p. 258; "seizing a spinning top to catch its motion", vol. 1 p. 244), Mangan's reading credited to Mangan, and the new-fringe step marked as inference ("Since the new nucleus has its own fringe…"). **Seed**: research note L115–L116 (quotation marks around strings that occur in no fetched source), repeated at L167.

**C3. Gurwitsch's condition dropped (installed by the 10-26 repair).** Before: "A marginal item that is singled out becomes a new theme and is, phenomenally, no longer the same item". Zaner (2010) reports Gurwitsch's thesis "We deny that an item which is susceptible of being singled out remains phenomenally the same when it is singled out" (p. xxvi, quoting "Thematics"). It is aimed against Husserl and Stumpf and concerns a part isolated from its whole: the case at issue is Husserl's horse's head (p. xxv). Zaner separately says marginal items "could become themes" (p. xxviii). The 10-26 fold welded the two into one claim about marginal items and credited it to Gurwitsch. After: the thesis is quoted with its target and case ("against Husserl and Stumpf, he holds that a part isolated from its whole becomes 'a new theme'"), and the margin application is marked "Extended to the margin". **Seed**: `research/gurwitsch-thematic-field-2026-10-26` L41 ("Marginal items **can** become thematic; when they do the item changes qualitatively") and L129 premise (c).

**C4. Müller & Kleinschmidt: condition dropped.** Before: "neural activity in regions representing nearby-but-unattended locations is actively reduced below baseline." **C6. Simons & Chabris: population dropped and a reading stated as fact.** Before: "subjects focused on a counting task do not experience a gorilla-shaped gap … The gorilla is genuinely absent from experience — never entering awareness at all." In the paper (chabris.com PDF), across all 192 observers 54% noticed the unexpected event, and the Gorilla was noticed by 44% (n = 96), so 56% missed it. "Never entering awareness" is the authors' reading, which they defend against Wolfe's (1999) inattentional amnesia. The 2025 evidence against it at the level of perception is in Nartker, Firestone, Egeth & Phillips (eLife 13, RP100337): "as a group, inattentionally blind participants can successfully report the location, color, and shape of stimuli they deny noticing". After: the figure is 56%, the reading is credited to the authors, Nartker et al. is added, and the void is restated as a limit of report. **Seed**: research note L79–L80. **C7. Hyperfocus stated as an established feature of ADHD.** Before: "ADHD produces a striking paradox: the same person who cannot sustain attention on routine tasks can become utterly absorbed in intrinsically motivating activities." The evidence is self-report. Hupfeld, Abagis & Shah (2019; PubMed 30267329): "Adults with ADHD often report episodes of long-lasting, highly focused attention"; higher ADHD symptom scores predicted more reported hyperfocus. Groen et al. (2020, *Res Dev Disabil* 107, 103789): "ADHD patients and matched controls did not differ in the occurrence, frequency, duration and pervasiveness of hyperfocus". After: reported, self-report, mixed, both studies cited, and the interface reading made conditional ("If the reports hold"). **C8. Neglect described as a visual-field loss.** Before: "Patients with right parietal damage lose awareness of the left visual field — not just the ability to attend to it, but apparently the experience of it altogether … an entire dimension of experience vanishes without leaving a trace in phenomenology." Corbetta & Shulman (2011; PMC3790661) describe neglect as following "predominantly right hemisphere injuries", with lesions in "right parietal, temporal, and frontal cortex", and as "an inability to attend and report stimuli on the side opposite the lesion (contralesional) despite apparently normal visual perception". Their patient's "visual fields were normal", and "when asked, he was able to move his eyes to the left and reported seeing the object". Severity "can be modulated by behavioral interventions over very short timescales (e.g. seconds)". After: the left side of space rather than the visual field, right-hemisphere injury, cue-dependent and fluctuating loss, and non-lateralised deficits (Husain & Rorden 2003, publisher abstract: "deficits that are not spatially lateralized"). The interface sentence now says the region can return with the lesion unchanged. **Seed**: no research note; `archive/voids/attention-disorders-and-consciousness` L117. `research/hemispatial-neglect-2026-09-16` L44 flagged this exact sentence on 09-17, but nothing repaired it.
