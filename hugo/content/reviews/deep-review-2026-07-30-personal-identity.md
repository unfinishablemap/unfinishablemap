## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. Citation-framing error — Santander et al. 2025 cited for a claim it does not support (FIXED).** The split-brain section read: *"That some unity persists suggests consciousness has intrinsic unity physical disconnection cannot fully sever. A 2025 PNAS study sharpens the point: as little as a centimetre of intact callosal fibres can maintain full neural synchronisation between hemispheres."*

The study does not sharpen that point; it cuts against it. Santander et al. found that partial-callosotomy patients retain full interhemispheric integration *because* ~1 cm of splenium is spared, while **complete-callosotomy patients showed significant disruption to interhemispheric network organisation**. That is a *physical* explanation of residual integration, and it is precisely the deflationary reading the surrounding argument was using the study to resist. Two further drifts in the same sentence: "intact" for the paper's "spared", and "full neural synchronisation" (implying temporal phase-locking) for the paper's "full interhemispheric integration" measured as functional connectivity.

This is the citation-framing-accuracy failure mode — real, verbatim-faithful, correctly attributed, and pointed the wrong way. It survived eleven reviews because every prior pass verified the *metadata* and stopped there.

Fixed by re-framing the study as a constraint the Map must absorb rather than support it recruits, and relocating the argument onto the ground that actually holds it — first-person unity reported despite perceptual fragmentation, which is not a connectivity measure:

> If consciousness were merely brain processes, complete disconnection should produce complete division. A 2025 PNAS study constrains how far that reading can be pressed: a centimetre of spared posterior callosal fibre sustains full interhemispheric integration, while complete section does disrupt network organisation (Santander et al. 2025). Residual integration is thus physically explicable, so the argument cannot rest on connectivity measures. What survives is narrower: despite perceptual fragmentation, patients report a single first-person perspective—suggesting indexical identity is a different thing from the computational binding physical connections enable.

The resulting argument is weaker in reach and stronger in honesty, which is the correct trade.
