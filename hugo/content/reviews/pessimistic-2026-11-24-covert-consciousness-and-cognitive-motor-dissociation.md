## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would accept nearly all the data and reject the vocabulary. "Bedside behaviour misses volitional cognition" credits the patient with *volition* on the strength of a classifier separating SMA from parahippocampal activation. The neural description, task-locked activity in specific circuits with a damaged route to motor cortex, says everything the data say. The page's discipline helps her: it calls the step to consciousness "an inference". But it then measures a "floor" of the gap between behaviour and *experience* (Occam paragraph). What was measured is a floor of the gap between behaviour and *neural response*, and "experience" is carried over by inference.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett would press point 5 of the page's own steelman, which overstates his side's case and so hides a weakness the Map could have named. The anaesthesia and sleep evidence for production does not read consciousness "on neural measures". It reads it on unresponsiveness and on what subjects report after emergence or waking, and neural measures such as PCI are calibrated against those reports (Casarotto et al. 2016 set the cutoff on "a benchmark population who could confirm the presence or absence of consciousness through subjective reports"). Retrospective report fails under amnesia, which anaesthetics produce, as the Map's [anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface](/topics/anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface/) says ("converge on an empty report, which need not mean extinction"). The physicalist can repair the point. The page as written lets him claim more than the record gives him, and its "limited" reply never notices. Dennett would also attack the §What Would Discriminate design itself. "Verified, content-specific recall after recovery" is a later report. On a multiple-drafts view there may be no fact about whether the recalled content was experienced at the time or reconstructed at retrieval from material encoded without awareness. The page's second caveat (capacity measures can miss consciousness) does not cover this third reply.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark has little to attack. The Minimal Quantum Interaction paragraph says "No bearing" and gives the right reason (the data concern macroscopic thalamocortical integrity). His only point is Popperian: the page's own minimal-interaction commitment "forecasts little or no detectable residual", so the single discriminating design it offers cannot test the Map's view in either direction.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

No content engages No Many Worlds, and the page correctly leaves the tenet out. Deutsch has nothing to say.

### The Empiricist

The Popperian's question is what result would count against the Map's interface reading. The page answers honestly, and the answer is "nothing here". Prevalence is "evidentially neutral", and the recall design "could embarrass production without confirming the interface". That candour is a strength. But it means the section titled "What Would Discriminate" names only a test between production and a filter view on which experience can outrun its neural support. The Map's own minimal-interface view cannot be distinguished from production by any test the page describes. The page should not claim more, and it does not. Report-only.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would question the Cotard sentence: "a report of absence from a subject who persists, where CMD is an absence of report from one who probably does." The data show task-specific processing over 30-second blocks, not a unified, persisting subject. "Probably persists" asks more of a classifier output than "probably conscious" does. Low; report-only.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Bodien et al. (2024) is quoted from a superseded manuscript, and the dependence on test modality goes unreported
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/covert-consciousness-and-cognitive-motor-dissociation.md`
- **Location**: §The Numbers and Their Denominators, Bodien paragraph (L50); §How Detection Fails (L58, L60); References (L119).
- **Problem**: Three linked defects.
  - *(a) Superseded wording.* The page says the "other figures and wording come from the authors' manuscript, not the journal text", and quotes "5-10 percent higher than previously reported" and "ranging from 2% to 45%". Both are verbatim only in the Europe PMC funder manuscript EMS197475, which is what the deep review read through BioC. The current PMC record (PMC7617195.2, NIHMS2018757; its abstract matches the published NEJM abstract word for word) reads "The percentage of participants with cognitive motor dissociation is 5 to 10 percentage points higher in our study than in previous studies" and "(range, 2 to 45%)". "Percent" and "percentage points" differ by roughly a factor of four at a 25% base rate. On a page about figure drift, that correction should be carried. Two other quoted spans also have current-version forms that differ in punctuation: "intentionally designed to minimize the potential for a false-positive result, which may increase the likelihood of a false-negative finding" (now hyphenated) and kappa "from 0.02 to 0.04" (now in place of "0.02-0.04").
  - *(b) Dropped condition.* Table 2 of the current version gives CMD in 36 of 79 participants assessed with both fMRI and EEG (46%), 11 of 61 with fMRI only (18%) and 13 of 101 with EEG only (13%) (percentages derived here). The authors add that the rate "may have been even higher if all the participants had been assessed with both imaging techniques." The page's "depending on cohort and method" gestures at this. But the best cohort's headline 25% blends a ~46% rate under multimodal testing with ~13–18% under one test. That supports the page's own "floors" reading, and the page does not report it. Testing was not randomised (the site range was 2–45%), so 46% must not be offered as a better prevalence estimate.
  - *(c) "The authors do not state them."* The main text indeed does not state the 20% and 32% splits, but it says "Table S8 shows the percentage of participants with a diagnosis of coma or vegetative state or of minimally conscious state-minus … who had a response". The supplement was not obtained, so "the authors do not state them" is unverified as written.
- **Severity**: Medium-High.
- **Recommendation**: Re-source the Bodien paragraph and the two §How Detection Fails spans to the current PMC version, and update the References evidential note. Add one sentence giving the modality split as a Map derivation, with the non-randomisation caveat and the authors' "may have been even higher" sentence. Change "the authors do not state them" to "the main text does not state them". **Task minted** (P2).

### Issue 3: The clinicians' stance is misdescribed as "graded", and the founding case's claim is softened
- **File**: same
- **Location**: §From Activation to Consciousness, L66 opening and L70 opening.
- **Problem**: L70: "The clinicians' own position is graded." The support is Aubinet's "likely conscious" and Kazazian, Monti & Owen's caution "for passive paradigms". But Kazazian, Monti & Owen (PMC12233511) write of *command-following*: "it has been consistently demonstrated that ∼20% of both chronic and acute DoC patients who cannot behaviourally command follow remain covertly aware", and "∼20% of patients who cannot reliably follow commands behaviourally are, in fact, aware". - **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: Report the split accurately. Some clinicians state the active-paradigm finding as demonstrated (Kazazian, Monti & Owen; Owen et al. 2006 "to demonstrate"), and others grade it (Aubinet). - **File**: same

- **Problem**:

## Counterarguments to Address

### Recall after recovery as the discriminating criterion
- **Current content says**: verified, content-specific recall of events from a period when every capacity measure was far below threshold would embarrass production. Two caveats follow (negative CMD tests miss consciousness; capacity measures can miss consciousness).
- **A critic would argue**: recall is a later report. Material encoded without awareness can be reconstructed as an episodic memory at retrieval (Dennett's Orwellian/Stalinesque point), so recall dates the report, not the experience.
- **Suggested response**: one clause in the second caveat ("or that the content was encoded without awareness and experienced only at retrieval"). Report-only, because the section is already caveated and the issue is philosophical, not a fidelity error.

### Stender's threshold as an "independent" capacity measure
- **Current content says**: point 4 treats PCI, metabolism and CMD as "independent capacity measures" that "converge", and §What Would Discriminate uses metabolism as one of the thresholds.
- **A critic would argue**: Stender et al. (2016) fit the 42% threshold against *behavioural* awareness ("accounted for the current level, or imminent return, of awareness in 94%"). It is calibrated on the very behavioural criterion the page says misses ~20% of conscious patients, so it is neither fully independent of behaviour nor a clean "below threshold" marker for covert cases.
- **Suggested response**: none needed in the article now (it is the physicalist's argument, stated as theirs). Note for the next deep review of §What Would Discriminate.
