## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. O'Brien-Kop over-recruited — citation-framing defect (line 50).** Article read: "Karen O'Brien-Kop (2023) argues **explicitly** … that Sāṃkhya **anticipates** Chalmers' hard problem." Verified against the Cambridge open-access PDF of the paper itself. She makes no anticipation claim. Her abstract says "there are **resonances** with Chalmers's notion of consciousness as fundamental"; her stated method is bringing Sāṃkhya "into a contemporary **dialogic frame**" with Chalmers. Decisively, her footnote 32 states that on Chalmers' 2020 restricted formulation ("how and why physical processes give rise to consciousness") "**Sāṃkhya is somewhat outside**" the problem — the article asserted the opposite of a qualification the author makes explicitly. She also *shifts* the hard problem from "subjectivity as conscious experience (Chalmers)" to "subjectivity as the capacity to consciously recognize experience". **This was also an internal corpus contradiction**: [samkhya-three-way-distinction](/concepts/samkhya-three-way-distinction/) (L95) and [epistemology-of-convergence-arguments](/topics/epistemology-of-convergence-arguments/) (L96) already carry the correct calibrated reading — "O'Brien-Kop's own analysis resists the strict identification rather than confirming it" — so this article was the outlier. **Fix applied**: re-framed to dialogue-and-resonances, with her own narrower-formulation caveat stated. State: real-correct-source → **re-framed** (not deleted).

**2. Nishida second sentence — secondary-source prose laundered as primary verbatim (line 108).** The 07-12 pass replaced a Viglielmo-translation quote with wording it believed was verbatim Abe & Ives, citing SEP as the verification source. Re-verified: **sentence 1 is genuinely verbatim Abe & Ives, p. 3** (independently reproduced on two Kyoto School quotation pages and in secondary literature). **Sentence 2 is not.** Its only occurrence anywhere is the SEP Nishida entry, where the quotation marks enclose **only** the fragment "The moment of seeing a color or hearing a sound" and the remainder ("is prior not only to the thought that…") is SEP's own expository prose closing on a page-cite. Two Kyoto School pages that reproduce the surrounding p. 3 material extensively do not contain the sentence at all. The article printed the whole of SEP's sentence inside quote marks attributed to Nishida. **Fix applied**: sentence 1 retained as the verbatim quote and its translation now named inline ("trans. Abe and Ives, 3"); sentence 2 **de-quoted** and rendered as attributed exposition, preserving the claim intact. State: commentator's phrasing as author's verbatim → de-quoted + translation attributed.

**3. *NTU* misattributed to Tempels — term-of-art misassigned across authors (line 122).** Article read: "vital force (*NTU*), as articulated by Placide Tempels". Tempels' concept in *Bantu Philosophy* (1945/1959) is **force vitale / vital force**; he does not operate an "NTU" concept. The *-ntu* categorial framework (Muntu/Kintu/Hantu/Kuntu, derived from Bantu linguistic morphology) is **Alexis Kagame**'s (1956), and **NTU** capitalised as "the universal force as such, of which the four categories are manifestations" is **Janheinz Jahn**'s (*Muntu*, 1961), built on both. The article attached a third author's term to the first author's thesis. **Fix applied**: re-predicated — *force vitale* attributed to Tempels, the *-ntu* categories to Kagame and Jahn. State: real-thesis-wrong-term → re-predicated.

**4. Transliteration inconsistency (line 169).** *sakṣin* → ***sākṣin***. The article uses full diacritics throughout (*Puruṣa*, *Prakṛti*, *ahaṃkāra*, *śūnyatā*, *svasaṃvedana*); this was the corpus's only half-diacriticked form (retroflex ṣ present, long ā missing). The undiacriticked `saksin` in five other files is internally consistent with those files' own convention and is **not** a defect.
