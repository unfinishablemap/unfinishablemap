## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This persona's verdict is the load-bearing one here, and it is strongly positive. Three things earn it.

**A discipline that states its own bounds as part of what it commits to.** `architecture-vs-significance-two-tier-discount.md` carries a section titled "What the Discipline Does Not Claim" with three named overreaches it refuses. On the one that matters most for tier discipline:

> **It does not deliver tier-graduation.** Splitting a finding into two claims does not assign either a tier; that work belongs to [evidential-status-discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/). The two-tier discount is a strength-indicator discipline, in the precise sense that page fixes: it indicates *which tier of a finding* is doing inferential work, leaving the tier *label* to the single-claim discipline downstream.

And the anti-slippage clause, stated as a prohibition on precisely the inference the Map is most tempted by:

> The larger discount on the significance tier records *whose framework supplies the reading*, not that the reading is mistaken. [...] A reader is free to grant both tiers — what the discipline forbids is granting the second *because* the first was robust.

That last sentence is the general form of "tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating," and the corpus derived it independently and made it a rule. P-F1 carries the same bounds into the register rather than registering a flattering summary of the discipline: it records that the discount delivers "**no quantitative discount factor**", "**no tier-graduation**", and "**does not hold that the significance-reading is false**", plus a scope condition that it "applies **differentially, not universally**" and "should not be invoked" where both tiers are equally framework-shaped. A register entry that writes down where its own position stops doing work is the behaviour to reward.

**Tier assignments that come out against the framework's interest.** `concession-convergence.md` runs the Map's own meta-argument through the scoring and splits the verdict three ways:

> - **Architectural finding** [...]: *strongly supported*.
> - **Interpretive significance** (the narrowing constitutes concession of the dualist claim): *realistic possibility, contested*.
> - **Cumulative meta-argument weight** (the pattern constitutes positive evidence for dualism): *live hypothesis* in its full positive form; *strongly supported* in the weaker form that the pattern *constrains* physicalist explanation. The full form needs the cross-tradition discriminator to land; the constraint form is what the architectural finding already supplies, and is what the article should be read as claiming.

The article tells the reader to read it at the weaker claim. It then states the rule that forced the result:

> The asymmetry is the discipline's point: load-bearing claims must be labelled at the tier the lowest-scoring relevant criterion supports, not at an average across criteria.

Lowest-scoring rather than averaged is the whole ballgame for tier discipline, and it is stated as policy rather than applied silently.

**Reporting a contrary data point found while hunting for a supporting one.** The cross-tradition discriminator section goes looking for non-Western physicalisms that narrow the same way, does not find them, and reports what it did find:

> Cārvāka/Lokāyata is the one genuinely indigenous, pre-contact physicalist tradition [...] But it is a *broad* emergentism that never narrows toward type-specificity [...] This inverts the concession-convergence dynamic: an independent physicalism that met absent-qualia-style pressure and did not ratchet inward.

It then declines to overread its own contrary evidence in either direction — noting the source record is "gappy" and the case is "best read as a suggestive nuance, not a firm disconfirmation" — and closes: "The discriminator therefore remains a *gap*, not a failed test; looking harder confirmed the absence without filling it." Searching for support, finding an inversion, and publishing the inversion at its correct (low) weight is unusual and worth naming.

`epistemology-of-convergence-arguments.md` does the same at the clinical convergence, refusing the upgrade explicitly:

> The compound signature is therefore best read as *compatibility*-grade, not *vindication*-grade, for the interface reading: it appears fully compatible with the Map's reading and not adjudicated in its favour over the functionalist rival, because both readings seem to predict the identical dissociation phenomenology.

**Tenet-as-evidence-upgrade is praise-worthily *not* done here.** Every one of these passages had the structural opportunity to let tenet-coherence carry a tier and declined it in writing.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

The cluster is process-friendly in the way that matters — it treats a finding as an event with two separable claims riding on it rather than as a bare fact — and, critically, its friendliness is kept off the evidential axis. The architecture tier is defined by survival across rival vocabularies, including illusionist and identity-theoretic ones, so nothing is upgraded for being congenial to the Map.

**The two personas converge here rather than conflict**, which by this skill's own design criterion means the cluster has resolved the tension honestly. The two-tier discount is the mechanism that lets process-philosophical resonance be praised on the coherence axis while explicitly barred from the evidence axis. **No calibration concern to flag in this cluster** — which is itself the finding, given how often that flag is warranted.

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The explanatory gap is preserved rather than papered over. The discipline places the Map's own dualist readings at the *larger* discount and says why:

> A reader inside a rival framework grants the architecture tier and declines the significance tier; that asymmetry of assent is exactly what the larger discount records.

Chalmers would recognise this as taking seriously that the gap is where the disagreement actually lives, instead of claiming the data settle it.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The cross-vocabulary translatability test — "does the claim survive restatement in functionalist, identity-theoretic, illusionist, predictive-processing, and dualist terms?" — is a disciplined way of asking which parts of a first-person-adjacent finding are framework-independent. The mine-ness exhibit is the strongest case: the for-me character is treated as a separable phenomenal feature at the architecture tier, with the dualist reading of that separability held apart at the larger discount.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The "Honest Limitation" section concedes the recursive bind without either dramatising or dissolving it:

[...] The bind is genuine; the response is asymptotic — adversarial cross-framework auditing through the [three-service outer-review programme](/project/outer-reviewer-service-calibration/) tightens the placement of the line without eliminating the framework-relativity of where it falls.
