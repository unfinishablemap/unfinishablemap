## Executive Summary

The article is careful, well sourced and honestly graded. Its weaknesses are structural, not evidential. (1) The framing claim that sham neurofeedback "removes the physical link between the patient's conscious effort and the brain signal" and holds "the conscious contribution fixed" misdescribes what a sham removes. It removes the signal-to-display link, and it changes the experience of control. (2) The proposed two-by-two design does not discriminate as claimed. The article's own definition of the expectancy reading includes "a sense of doing something", and the Occam paragraph says the dispute is about classification. Both undercut the claim that an instruction effect would favour the intention reading. (3) The opening promises that Tenet 3 "must price" the result, but the Relation section records only compatibility and a removed embarrassment. The genuine cost, that directed effort left no trace on the trained rhythm in either Schabus 2017 or Maaz 2026, is never charged against the neuroplasticity article's selection claim, which still stands unqualified at `concepts/neuroplasticity` L117.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
"You call the sham arm an 'intention condition' as though intention were a variable you can hold fixed. What you actually hold fixed is a protocol: instructions, a screen, a clinician. Whatever 'effort' names is a family of prefrontal and arousal processes. Those plainly differ between arms, because one arm gets contingent reinforcement and the other gets noise. Your own best datum (Schabus: the trained band did not move) says the folk-psychological target, 'I am changing my brain rhythm', failed to pick anything out. That is a point for elimination: the patients' self-description of what they were doing was false." The article concedes the Schabus result limits the intention reading (L65) but does not register that it falsifies the patients' *own* description of their effort. That tells against treating their reported effort as the active ingredient.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would target L43: "Because conscious experience is present in both arms, the design is the mirror image of the consciousness placebo." Presence is not sameness. Participants in the real arm experience contingent success, since the display moves when they try. Sham participants experience a display that ignores them. Good blinding shows that their *guesses about arm* match. It does not show that their *experience of control* matches. So the design does not hold "the conscious contribution fixed" (L43). It varies the phenomenology of agency along with the contingency. That makes the "mirror image" framing an overstatement. The stability note on effort-as-neural is respected here: this critique is about what the design varies, not about what effort is.

### The Quantum Skeptic
Nothing to attack. The article explicitly declares Tenet 2 not engaged (L91) and makes no mechanism claim. That restraint is correct and should be preserved. Tegmark would add only that the Map's quantum-selection story, if it applied anywhere, should apply to the real-arm case where effort is aimed at a specific signal, and there it produced no measurable change in the target (Schabus 2018 reply; Maaz 2026). Any future article linking attention-based Zeno selection to neurofeedback self-regulation must reckon with this.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Not engaged, correctly. No critique.

### The Empiricist
The article earns credit here: it names a design and its predictions (L73). But the predictions are less crisp than they look. (a) The expectancy reading, as the article defines it at L61, includes "a sense of doing something". Active instruction supplies exactly that and passive instruction withholds it, so the expectancy reading *also* predicts an instruction effect unless "sense of doing something" is somehow matched. (b) The Occam paragraph (L89) says "whether effort is a nuisance variable or the active ingredient is a choice about which arm counts as the control". It then asserts in the next sentence that "that disagreement is empirical, it has a design that could decide it". A classification choice is not settled by data. If the two-by-two found an instruction effect, Thibault, Lifshitz and Raz (who already list "motivation and effort" among placebo factors) would score it as placebo and the interactionist would score it as treatment. The article needs to say which of the two disagreements the design settles: whether *effort* carries the benefit (empirical, and decidable) or whether effort *counts as specific* (classificatory, and not decidable). As written, the Occam section runs them together.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would press on the passive arm. The article's design treats "watch it attentively because exposure is therapeutic" as a no-intention control. The Map's own framework says attention is the interface: `concepts/neuroplasticity` L117 says "when consciousness directs attention to specific content, it selects which neural patterns receive reinforcement", and the contemplative articles treat effortless open-monitoring awareness as a distinct and neurally marked mode (Garrison 2013's PCC deactivation). On the Map's own theory, then, attentive passive watching engages the interface too. The intention reading has to say whether its active ingredient is *effortful trying* or *directed attention*. If it is attention, the passive arm is not a control for it, and the predicted main effect of instruction does not follow from the Map's own view. If it is effort, the article should say why the Map's attention-as-interface account puts the causal weight on effort rather than on attention.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The framing misdescribes what the sham arm removes
- **File**: `topics/sham-controlled-neurofeedback-and-the-consciousness-comparator`
- **Location**: L43, "it removes the physical link between the patient's conscious effort and the brain signal the effort is aimed at, while leaving the effort itself in place. Because conscious experience is present in both arms, … the conscious contribution is held fixed and the specific physical pathway is varied."
- **Problem**: A sham decouples the *display* from the signal. Whatever link runs from effort to the signal is untouched. Sham participants can still modulate their EEG; they just don't see it. The claim that the conscious contribution is "held fixed" also fails, because the experience of contingent control differs between arms. Blinding matches beliefs about arm assignment, not phenomenology. This is an epistemic/metaphysical slide: matched *reports* (belief, blinding guesses) are recruited to assert matched *experience*. Since this sentence sets up the article's "mirror image of the consciousness placebo" framing, the error colours the whole page.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Rewrite L43 to say the sham removes the *feedback contingency* (signal→display→reinforcement) and keeps the effort, the belief and the setting. Replace "held fixed" with "matched in belief and effort instructions, though not in the experience of contingent control". One clause suffices, and the change is length-neutral.

### Issue 2: The discriminating design does not discriminate as claimed, and the Occam section contradicts itself
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L61 (expectancy ingredients include "a sense of doing something"); L73 (the expectancy reading predicts "no main effect of instruction once expectation is controlled"); L89 ("a choice about which arm counts as the control" followed by "That disagreement is empirical, it has a design that could decide it").
- **Problem**: (a) Under the article's own definition, the expectancy reading predicts an instruction effect, because active instruction supplies "a sense of doing something". (b) The Occam paragraph calls the dispute classificatory and then empirical in consecutive sentences. The design can settle whether effort carries the benefit. It cannot settle whether effort counts as a specific or a non-specific factor.
- **Severity**: High (it touches the article's central constructive contribution)
- **Recommendation**: At L73, have the expectancy reading predict an instruction effect *fully mediated by measured expectancy and self-efficacy/engagement*, and the intention reading predict a residual effect after that mediation. Alternatively, drop "a sense of doing something" from the L61 list and justify the omission. At L89, separate the two questions in one sentence: the design decides whether effort carries the benefit; whether effort then counts as "placebo" is a classification the data do not fix, and the Map declines to let parsimony fix it.

### Issue 3: Tenet 3 is promised a price and given only compatibility
- **File**: same, plus `concepts/neuroplasticity` L117
- **Location**: L35, "the result is a test that Tenet 3 must price"; L85, the Relation §Bidirectional ends on "at most it removes one apparent embarrassment".
- **Problem**: The article reports two findings that count *against* the selection mechanism the neuroplasticity article asserts. In Schabus 2017/2018, sustained directed effort *with* contingent reinforcement left the trained band unchanged. In Maaz 2026, alpha rose whether or not participants tried. The neuroplasticity article's L117 still states without qualification that attention "selects which neural patterns receive reinforcement. Repeated selection produces plasticity." The Relation section tallies only the favourable side, "no embarrassment", and never enters the debit. That is the asymmetry the evidential-status discipline exists to catch. The fMRI self-regulation finding (Thibault et al. 2018) offsets it only partly, since it shows short-term BOLD control and not lasting plasticity.
- **Severity**: Medium-High
- **Recommendation**: Add one or two sentences to Relation §Bidirectional. In the EEG trials, deliberate effort aimed at a specific rhythm, even with contingent reinforcement, did not produce lasting change in that rhythm. That counts against reading neuroplasticity's selection claim as a mechanism of *targeted* self-directed change, even though it leaves Tenet 3's general claim untouched. Separately, qualify `concepts/neuroplasticity` L117 with a short clause and a piped link to the sham article (the page has ample headroom). The current reciprocal sits on L140 "fair test" instead, which leads to Issue 4.

### Issue 4: Inbound anchor texts overstate what the page shows

### Issue 5: The passive-viewing arm is not a no-intention control on the Map's own theory
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L73 (passive instruction); L63 (the intention reading)
- **Problem**: See the Buddhist critique above. The Map's attention-as-interface account locates the causal act in attention, and attentive passive watching is attention. The intention reading does not say whether its ingredient is effortful trying or attention. If it is attention, the design's key contrast is not the one the Map's theory cares about.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Add one sentence to the intention-reading paragraph or to the design section's cautions. On the Map's attention-as-interface view, passive attentive watching is not a zero-attention condition. The design therefore tests *effortful trying*, not attention as such, and a null instruction effect would count against the effort version of the intention reading only.

## Counterarguments to Address

### "Sham-equivalence is compatible with Tenet 3"
- **Current content says**: The part of the treatment that survives subtraction "is the part that involves the patient's own directed effort" (L85).
- **A critic would argue**: What survives subtraction is *everything* non-specific, including expectation, clinician care, regression to the mean, and repeated measurement. Singling out effort among the survivors is selective description that tilts toward the tenet.
- **Suggested response**: Rephrase to "the part that survives includes the patient's own directed effort, together with expectation and care". The compatibility claim then stands without seeming to pre-select the favoured ingredient. This change is length-neutral.

### "Contingency adds nothing"
- **Current content says**: Across the tested outcomes, contingent feedback added nothing detectable.
- **A critic would argue** (from the other side, a neurofeedback proponent): Theta/beta and SMR protocols may simply be the wrong targets, and the Westwood standard-protocol SMD of 0.21 plus the NCG medication result hint at a small specific effect the trials were underpowered to detect.
- **Suggested response**: The Status section already scopes this (L81). No change needed. This is noted as a strength.
