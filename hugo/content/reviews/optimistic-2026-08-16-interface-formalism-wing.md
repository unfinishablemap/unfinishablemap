## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The wing takes the explanatory gap seriously by declining to let a formalism close it by notation. `agency-budget` builds an exact channel between mental and physical variables and then states what the channel does not buy: "a proof that the interface can be perfectly secure is simultaneously a proof that it can never be witnessed from outside." Chalmers would recognise the move — a structural result about consciousness's physical traffic that leaves the phenomenal question exactly where it was. `geometric-model-of-mind` presses the same point against the best contemporary opponent: Duch's identity reading "is stipulated closed, not shown closed", because "the framework nowhere argues that the qualitative character of experience is thereby *captured* rather than merely *co-present* with the dynamical state." That is the explanatory gap stated as a demand on a rival's argument rather than as an assertion of the Map's own.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp would be startled and, I think, satisfied. `sign-problem` is the Map turning its sharpest instrument on the mechanism Stapp gave it, and the objection is a genuine contribution rather than a retreat: Kofman and Kurizki's result that "the inhibitory quantum Zeno effect may be feasible in a limited class of systems," whereas accelerated decay "appears to be much more ubiquitous," is not a refutation of Zeno-based interaction but a specification of what such a proposal now owes. Better still, the article extracts a *sharper prediction* from the objection — "not merely non-linearity but *non-monotonicity* — enhanced decay at intermediate rates, suppression only above a threshold set by the bath correlation time. A classical Hebbian selector predicts smooth saturation with total attention and cannot produce a turning point." A discriminator that a rival account structurally cannot mimic is worth more to an interactionist than the comfortable reading it replaces.

`bohm` is the wing's other Stapp-adjacent success, and its discipline is in the accounting. The active-information channel is borrowed because "what steers the particle is the shape of the information, not its magnitude" — exactly a conservation-respecting influence — and the borrowing is immediately priced: "Bohmian mechanics is deterministic and hidden-variable ... Adopting Bohmian ontology wholesale would therefore *undercut* the Map's selection mechanism, not support it." Two candidate seams are named, and both are costed: "Neither seam is free, and the Map should not pretend otherwise."

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Nagel's demand is that the third-person account not quietly stand in for the first-person one, and `agency-budget` honours it in the least convenient place. The article's rival-readings section lets the objection speak in its own voice: "a channel whose only competent decoder already possesses the mental facts is not obviously a mental-to-physical channel at all." The Map does not answer this; it records it as "the sharpest rival and the Map does not claim to have answered it." `sign-problem` does the same with felt effort — noting that the physics and the phenomenology now make incompatible predictions, since "[felt effort](/concepts/mental-effort/) scaling with observation rate predicts monotonic benefit from trying harder, while the physics predicts a turning point." First-person data is kept on the table as data *and* allowed to conflict with the model, which is the treatment Nagel asks for and the Map's own introspection-scepticism makes hard to give.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Bohm is Whitehead's natural ally and the wing reads him well. The holomovement, enfoldment, and soma-significance are presented as a serious metaphysics of process — "everything is enfolded into everything else", "separateness is a secondary appearance abstracted from a prior wholeness" — and the Map's engagement is a genuine borrowing rather than a dismissal: active information is "the single most borrowable piece of Bohm's apparatus for the Map." The article also avoids the crude-dualism trap Whitehead attacks, since what is borrowed is a *relation* (form directs energy) rather than a substance pushing another substance.

Per this persona's standing constraint, none of this praise upgrades an evidential tier. The one place where process-friendly resonance threatens to become evidence — `local-tomography`'s reading of interface holism as "the formal fingerprint of dualist holism" — is flagged below as a calibration concern rather than praised, because the theorem it leans on does not license the step. Note that the article *itself* mostly holds this line ("The signature reading is speculation the Map finds attractive, not a result"); the failure is one connective in a numbered inference, not a failure of intent.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane's problem has always been that indeterminism buys randomness, not authorship, and `agency-budget` is the first article in the corpus to price the difference rather than assert it. The conservation law — "Selection may correlate arbitrarily with C; what it may not do is disturb the o-marginal within any publicly assemblable ensemble" — makes *reasons-correlated* selection a quantity with a ceiling: "at most min(H(conscious source), H(Born distribution)) bits of reasons-correlated selection per event, at exactly zero statistical signature." And the Landsman bookend closes the loophole Kane's critics usually exploit from the other side: a policy that "imprinted any computable pattern — Morse-coding reasons into a spin sequence — would yield a compressible string, failing randomness tests while passing every frequency test," and the coupling construction rules it out by inheriting the covertext's randomness. Authorship is given a budget and a price rather than a slogan.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The wing is unusually willing to end an article without a result. `sign-problem` closes: "The Map does not resolve the dilemma here. Recording it as unresolved is the point." `local-tomography` says its contribution "is to *name the exact axiom* whose interface-status is unknown ... rather than supplying any evidence for the interface." `agency-budget` ends its own construction with a list of what a worked model would still fail to deliver — "It states no [selection principle](/concepts/selection-criterion-problem/) ... It does not by itself demonstrate token counterfactual dependence, since a coupling is a distribution rather than a causal structure." McGinn's humility is here not as a mood but as a section heading.

Two smaller instances deserve naming because they are the kind of restraint that usually goes unrecorded. `sign-problem` computes ħ/k_BT at 310 K and then refuses to let the number harden: "**This is arithmetic performed for the Map, not a measured or published neural parameter**, and no such parameter exists in the literature." And it declines to convert a failed search into a finding: "a search for anti-Zeno results in biological systems returned only Zeno-side work; that absence is weak evidence rather than a finding, since the search was not exhaustive." A bare zero is not evidence of absence, and the article says so unprompted.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This wing is where the Map's evidential discipline is most tested, because a formalism is the easiest thing in the world to over-read, and four of the five articles pass.**

The load-bearing case is `agency-budget`, and the reason it matters is structural. The 2026-08-13 external review charged that Born-preserving efficacy was "only a verbal conjunction of two desiderata" and demanded a conservation law. The article *delivers* the law — and then declines the upgrade the delivery invites:

> "The honest verdict is that the budget converts a suspicion of incoherence into a priced concession, and does not convert either into a demonstration of efficacy."

> "That is progress on exactly one axis. It answers the *impossibility* form of the ensemble-level worry and leaves the *idleness* form standing."

This is tenet-as-evidence-upgrade praise-worthily *not* done. Every incentive ran the other way: an outside reviewer set a bar, the article cleared it, and a less disciplined pass would have banked "the objection is answered." Instead the article names the concession it bought and concedes that its own construction "is uncomfortably close" to defining the observable consequences away. It also front-loads the register separation before any reader can misread it: "**the mapping onto mental causation is the Map's own construction, not a published result**," citable "as framework-internal coherence arithmetic and never as established mental causation." Scope conditions on borrowed theorems are stated rather than dropped — Valentini's result is flagged as being "about *deterministic hidden-variables theories*, not about every possible framework"; Landsman's "target is *determinism*, not agency; the extension below is the Map's inference and not his claim"; Aaronson is reported with "the abstract's own verb". And a disanalogy that a promotional pass would have buried is given its own paragraph: the steganographic construction needs a shared key and a receiver, and "what does not survive is the picture of a message successfully sent to someone."

`sign-problem`'s discipline is of a different kind and is worth measuring, because it is a fresh create and fresh creates carry defect tails. I checked its inline attributions separately from its reference entries, against the publisher-verified research note it inherits ([research/anti-zeno-effect-and-sign-of-conscious-observation-2026-08-05.md](/research/anti-zeno-effect-and-sign-of-conscious-observation-2026-08-05/)):

- The Virzì et al. τ_c criterion is reproduced verbatim.
- The Denton et al. (2024) quote is reproduced verbatim, and — importantly — is used *against* the Map: "The one warm-biology precedent the Map holds for Zeno dynamics illustrates the reading of 'observation' that Stapp's proposal cannot use." No "first biological precedent" over-claim appears.
- The Fischer et al. 1 μs / 5 μs crossover is reproduced correctly, and the article **does not import** the paper's separate 30 μs Bloch-period figure that the research note explicitly warned downstream articles not to conflate with the crossover. A named trap, correctly avoided.
- The note's three flagged gaps (unverified Weizmann compact form, unread Kaulakys abstract, non-existent neural bath correlation time) all propagate into the article as caveats rather than being silently dropped.

`bohm` and `geometric-model-of-mind` both keep the registers apart. Bohm's borrowing is explicitly partial — "What survives the borrowing is the form-directs-energy idea; what does not is the determinism, the no-collapse ontology, and the single-ground monism" — and the second seam is marked as resting on "an unsettled foundational dispute." `geometric-model-of-mind` runs the two-tier appropriation under [framework-stage-calibration](/project/framework-stage-calibration/) by name, separating empirical-stage results (appropriable) from metaphysical-stage interpretation (declined), and marks the bedrock disagreement as bedrock under [bedrock-clash-vs-absorption](/project/bedrock-clash-vs-absorption/) instead of staging a refutation inside the opponent's framework. ## Calibration Concern

### `local-tomography`: a theorem's conjunction is stated as a disjunction, in the Map's favour

The article's inferential spine is Galley and Masanes (2018). The reference entry reproduces the title exactly:

> "Any modification of the Born rule leads to a violation of the purification **and** local tomography principles."

The published abstract states the result twice, both times as a conjunction: "We prove that in all these theories the purification principle is violated" and "We also prove that in all such modifications the task of state tomography with local measurements is impossible."

The body then converts the connective:

> "Galley and Masanes (2018) proved the tightness of this: any modification of the Born rule violates purification **or** local tomography. Local tomography is therefore one of exactly two doors through which a non-Born interface could formally enter without signalling."

And the conversion becomes load-bearing at step 3 of the interface argument:

> "If (B + S) is *not* locally tomographic, then by Galley and Masanes the door is open: the interface's outcome-selection could deviate from the Born rule without signalling, because the derivation that closed that door assumed the axiom that would now fail."

On the theorem as published, modified-Born entails that *both* principles fail. Contraposing: if *either* purification *or* local tomography holds, the Born rule is forced. So local-tomography failure is **necessary but not sufficient** for a non-Born interface — purification must fail too. The "two doors" image has it backwards: an interface must pass through both, not either.

Three things about this flag matter:

1. **It is a framing defect, not a fabrication.** The paper is real, the title is transcribed correctly, the quotations from Hardy-Wootters and Barnum-Wilce are verbatim, and the surrounding discipline ("this is a coherence move within the framework, not framework-independent support") is intact. This is the citation-framing lens: a correctly cited source recruited slightly beyond what it shows. The fix is a re-frame, never a deletion.
2. **The correction runs against the Map, which is why it is worth making.** Stated correctly, the Map's Born constraint is *more* robust than the article says — it survives as long as either axiom applies. That is good news for the "As a problem" reading (a smaller liability) and bad news for the "As a signature" reading (dualist holism now needs two axioms to fail, not one). The article currently gets both halves slightly wrong in the same direction.
3. **The sibling should be checked in the same pass.** [concepts/generalised-probabilistic-theories.md](/concepts/generalised-probabilistic-theories/) states the same result as "The Born rule becomes forced only once **purification and/or local tomography** are added to no-signalling" — looser rather than wrong, but "and/or" leaves the sufficiency question open where the theorem settles it. [apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy.md](/apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy/) L211 says "no-signalling *under purification* forces Born-preservation", which is correct. Fixing the concept file alone would leave the imprecise sibling live.

Per the skill's standing rule, this is filed as a calibration concern rather than praise, and the resulting task is a `refine-draft` on the reviewed article rather than an expansion opportunity.
