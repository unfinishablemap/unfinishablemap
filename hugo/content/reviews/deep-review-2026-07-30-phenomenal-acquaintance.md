## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Citation-framing: Earl Conee enlisted against his own position (FIXED).** In "The Three Epistemic Categories," Conee was the named authority establishing acquaintance knowledge as an irreducible third category — a thesis the article's lead then treats as load-bearing for dualism ("Its irreducibility is central to the Map's case … for substance dualism"). The attributed claim is faithful to Conee; the *framing* was not. Conee (1994) is a **physicalist** who presses the acquaintance/propositional distinction precisely to *defuse* the knowledge argument: if what Mary gains is non-propositional acquaintance rather than a new truth, she learns no new fact and physicalism survives. Verified at IEP's knowledge-argument entry, which files him under strategies that "reject the assumption that phenomenal knowledge is propositional knowledge … (Conee 1994, Bigelow and Pargetter 1990)" — i.e. among the *rejections* of the inference to physicalism's falsity. Per re-frame-don't-delete: the attribution is kept and Conee's actual commitment stated. Added — "Conee is no ally of dualism, and that matters for how much weight the third category can bear: he presses the distinction *in defence of* physicalism … The category is common ground; what the Map contests is the further claim that what acquaintance presents is physical." This strengthens rather than weakens the article: Conee grants *more* than Loar does (the full third category) and still denies dualism, so he is the sharper illustration of the two-step architecture the article already advertises, and the honesty now arrives where the recruitment happened rather than 35 lines later in the Loar section.

- **Misattribution inside "Russell's Original Distinction" (FIXED).** The section stated: "Descriptive knowledge is third-personal, communicable, and inherently general—it characterises things as falling under types." Both flagged features are non-Russellian:
  - *"Inherently general / falling under types"* inverts Russell's category. Russell's knowledge by description is knowledge of a **particular** as "the so-and-so" — "To know some thing or object by a definite description is to know that it is the so-and-so or that the so-and-so exists" (*Problems of Philosophy* 82-3, quoted at SEP); the 1910 paper turns on **definite** rather than ambiguous descriptions, "one object, and no more, having a certain property." His paradigms are all particulars: Bismarck, the man in the iron mask, the author of *Waverley*, Jack the Ripper.
  - *"Third-personal"* is likewise not Russell's axis. SEP: the distinction is "**not primarily** drawn along first-person/third-person lines. Rather, it concerns the **directness** of awareness."
  - The sentence also contradicted its own example *in situ*: "Paris is the capital of France" is a definite description of a particular, not a characterisation of things falling under a type.

  Diagnosis: the article had imported its own later thesis — the concepts-generalise/acquaintance-is-particular contrast, correctly deployed in the fineness-of-grain section ("Concepts generalise; acquaintance is irreducibly particular") — back into the exposition of Russell's 1910 position. This is exactly the "stated as later authors redrew it rather than as Russell drew it" failure. Rewritten to state the category as Russell drew it ("the so-and-so," the unique satisfier, how we reach particulars we never encounter), keeping "communicable" and adding "mediated." Note the Map's third-personal gloss survives where it legitimately belongs, one section later, on *propositional* knowledge in the explicitly *contemporary* taxonomy — the fix separates Map gloss from Russell exegesis rather than deleting the gloss.
