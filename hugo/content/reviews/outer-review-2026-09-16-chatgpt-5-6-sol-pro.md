## Reply

# External referee report: *Lucid Dreaming and the Evidence for Dualist Rendering*

## Recommendation: **major revision**

### Overall verdict

The article has an interesting and potentially defensible core: lucid dreaming shows that richly structured conscious experience can occur when external sensory constraint is greatly reduced, and this prevents any simple equation of perception with passive sensory reception. It also illustrates that the empirical facts of dreaming can be redescribed under more than one metaphysical framework.

It does **not**, however, provide empirical evidence that a non-physical consciousness supplies dream content, nor that the brain contributes only the “architecture” while consciousness performs the “rendering.” The decisive equivocation is between:

1. **experience occurring without current external sensory input**, which the literature strongly supports; and
2. **experience occurring without neural content-generation**, which the literature does not support and in several respects counts against.

Lucid-dream research verifies that subjects can become reflectively aware during REM sleep, signal that fact, perform tasks, receive limited external information and respond to it. Neuroimaging and electrophysiology identify systematic neural activity associated with dream occurrence, particular contents, metacognition and lucid-dream signalling. None of this resolves the hard problem, but none of it isolates a non-neural renderer either.

The article should therefore be recast as **a dualist interpretation of empirical underdetermination**, not as “evidence for dualist rendering.”

## Version audited

I audited the repository version carrying the 9 September 2026 substantive modification rather than the older indexed HTML. The September 9 commit itself recognised that the former description asserted that the evidence “reveals” or “converges on” mind-side generation while the body repeatedly treated that conclusion as merely compatible with the evidence. The revised description is better, but the mismatch persists in the body through expressions such as “mental causation made visible,” “exactly what dualism predicts,” “structurally simpler” and “cleaner account.”

# 1. What the laboratory evidence actually establishes

## 1.1 Signal verification is real and important

LaBerge and colleagues established the foundational result in 1981. Five selected subjects made prearranged eye-movement signals while polysomnography showed unequivocal REM sleep. This turned lucid dreaming from a retrospective report into a temporally localisable laboratory phenomenon. The result verifies that reflective awareness, memory for an instruction and voluntary signalling can occur during REM sleep. It does not identify the ontological source of either awareness or dream content. [Sage Journals+1](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.2466/pms.1981.52.3.727)

The later two-way communication work is even stronger methodologically. Konkoly and colleagues obtained 29 correctly answered questions from six participants across four independent laboratories, using eye movements or selective facial contractions. This establishes a limited but reproducible channel through which information can enter a dream and an answer can leave it. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33607035/)

These findings are important for the science of consciousness. They support:

- reflective cognition during verified REM;
- limited working-memory and language/arithmetic operations;
- intentional action under REM atonia;
- real-time investigation of dream phenomenology.

They do **not** distinguish neural causation from non-physical mental causation.

## 1.2 Lucid REM is not a neurally impoverished blank

The article repeatedly contrasts phenomenal richness with “reduced neural resources” or describes there being “more experience when the system is doing less.” That description is not supported by the best signal-verified physiology.

Baird, Tononi and LaBerge analysed 14 signal-verified lucid dreams from six participants. Lucid periods occurred during relatively activated, often phasic REM, with increased eye-movement density and markers of cortical and subcortical activation. After correction for ocular muscle artefacts, the earlier reported frontal 40-Hz effect disappeared. Their conclusion was not that lucid dreaming uses unusually little brain activity, but that it occurs during activated REM within the normal physiological range of REM sleep. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/45/4/zsab294/6528977)

This also corrects the older Voss et al. picture. Voss and colleagues’ 2009 six-subject study characterised lucid dreaming as a hybrid waking–REM state, partly on the basis of frontal/frontolateral gamma activity. That finding was historically important but is no longer safe to present without the 2022 artefact correction. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19750924/)

The 2025 pooled multi-laboratory EEG study by Demirel and colleagues found a more complex pattern: relatively small sensor-level differences, reduced beta power in right central and parietal sources including the temporoparietal junction, increased alpha connectivity, and transient right temporo-occipital and precuneus gamma changes around initial lucid signalling. These findings indicate dynamically altered neural network activity, not a generic “resource reduction.” [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40258661/)

## 1.3 Dream contents have content-specific neural correlates

The broader dreaming literature places important pressure on the article’s clean division between brain-provided architecture and mind-provided content.

Siclari and colleagues found that reports of dream experience were associated with local reductions in low-frequency activity in posterior cortical regions during both REM and NREM sleep, while high-frequency topographies correlated with reported classes of dream content. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.4545)

In lucid dreamers, dreamed hand movements recruited corresponding sensorimotor regions. Dresler’s later EEG–fMRI case study found increased activity during lucidity in areas including precuneus, parietal cortex, anterior prefrontal cortex and occipitotemporal regions, although it was effectively a one-subject, two-episode result and must be treated accordingly. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22036177/)

These studies do not establish identity between neural activity and phenomenal content. But they make the proposed allocation—

> brain: architecture; consciousness: content

—empirically unmotivated. Neural activity covaries not only with the existence of a dream but with its motor, perceptual and metacognitive content.

## 1.4 The sensory channel is attenuated, selective and state-dependent—not closed

The article calls the brain’s normal informational channel “closed” and later says that dream experience occurs “without any corresponding sensory transduction.” That is too strong and is directly contradicted by the literature the article itself cites.

Konkoly’s dreamers heard novel spoken questions during REM, processed them and answered. Koroma and colleagues found that informative speech continued to be discriminated during REM, with flexible amplification and suppression depending partly on the timing of rapid eye movements. REM therefore involves selective sensory gating, not total sensory disconnection. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33607035/)

The accurate formulation is:

> During dreaming, exteroceptive precision and behavioural responsiveness are substantially reduced, while some external signals continue to be neurally processed and can sometimes enter dream content.

That correction matters philosophically. The dream state is not an uncontaminated experiment in what consciousness can do when the brain receives no information. It is a state in which internally generated neural activity dominates a selectively permeable input channel.

# 2. Critical empirical and inferential findings

The claims discussed below occur in the current September 9 article.

## Finding 1 — The Baird 2018 claim is factually wrong

The article states that frequent lucid dreamers’ connectivity suggests that “practice in deploying this capability reshapes neural structure,” and then argues that the capability must have existed before this “neural accommodation.”

Baird et al. found the opposite of the structural premise:

- 14 frequent lucid dreamers were compared with 14 matched controls;
- frequent lucid dreamers showed increased resting-state functional connectivity involving left anterior prefrontal cortex;
- **no significant structural brain differences were found**;
- the study was cross-sectional;
- participants were selected for spontaneous lucid-dream frequency, not randomised to lucid-dream practice.

Nothing in the study establishes practice, structural reshaping or temporal precedence of mental capability over neural accommodation. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-36190-w)

A 2026 exploratory multimodal MRI study has since reported associations between self-reported lucid-dream frequency and distributed grey–white matter components. But it too is correlational and exploratory. It cannot retrospectively validate the attribution to Baird or show that mental practice caused structural change. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.70305)

**Required correction:** replace the entire “neural structure” and temporal-precedence argument with:

> Frequent lucid-dream occurrence is associated with differences in resting functional connectivity. The direction of causation is unknown: connectivity might facilitate lucidity, repeated lucidity might alter connectivity, or both might reflect another trait.

This error has propagated to neighbouring articles and requires a corpus-wide correction.

## Finding 2 — “Sensory channel closed” is false

The article’s opening description presents lucid dreaming as a particularly clean case because sensory input has been stripped away. That is not the experimental condition studied by Konkoly, targeted memory reactivation research or REM sensory-processing studies.

The channel is heavily gated, but sounds, speech, touch and other stimuli can be neurally processed and incorporated. The 2021 interactive-dream study depends on precisely this fact. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33607035/)

This is not a minor verbal correction. “No sensory input” makes dreaming appear to isolate an independent renderer; “low-precision but selectively permeable sensory input” instead fits a hierarchical generative model naturally.

## Finding 3 — Konkoly 2026 does not establish phenomenal causation

The article reports that puzzles incorporated into dreams were solved at 42% versus 17% and argues:

> If dream phenomenology were epiphenomenal, incorporation should be irrelevant to outcomes.

The descriptive association exists, but the inferential use is substantially overstated.

The primary study recruited 20 participants over 39 nights and obtained 13 signal-verified lucid reports from six participants. Auditory cues reliably increased incorporation of associated puzzles. However:

- the planned model found only a nonsignificant trend for incorporation predicting solving, p=.057;
- there was no significant overall main effect of cueing;
- the planned mediation analysis could not be performed;
- the positive cueing-by-outcome result came from a post-hoc division into 12 “targeted dreamers” and eight nonresponders;
- lucid puzzle incorporation was not demonstrably superior to non-lucid incorporation;
- the authors explicitly reported numerically better outcomes for incorporated puzzles in non-lucid dreams and said that there were too few lucid cases for a robust comparison.

The study itself characterises the causal conclusions as preliminary and calls for larger samples. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2026/1/niaf067/8456489)

More fundamentally, a correlation between reported phenomenal content and later performance does not discriminate epiphenomenalism. On a physicalist or epiphenomenalist model, the neural process that both constitutes or accompanies the dream content can also affect later problem solving. To challenge epiphenomenalism, one would need evidence that variation in phenomenal content itself changes the outcome while relevant physical processing is held fixed. This study does not approximate that intervention.

**Verdict:** the 42%–17% statistic is not fabricated, but its argumentative role in the article is invalid.

## Finding 4 — “Physicalist concession” misrepresents the cited authors

The Hobson–Hong–Friston quotation about an innate virtual-reality generator is accurate. The article’s representation of it as a concession that points toward an independent consciousness is not.

Their paper advances a detailed neurocomputational model in which dream and waking contents arise from hierarchical inference implemented through neuronal message passing. It explicitly presents the brain’s generative model as producing virtual realities and discusses consciousness in functionalist, reductive or non-reductive physicalist terms. It even proposes that Cartesian dualism can be addressed through biophysical inference. [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01133/full)

Calling this a “physicalist concession” exploits an ambiguity in *generation*:

- Hobson et al.: the **brain’s generative model** produces internally organised experiential contents;
- the Map: **consciousness itself**, ontologically distinct from brain activity, supplies phenomenal content.

The source accepts the first and rejects or seeks to dissolve the need for the second. It is evidence for the strength of the rival model, not a reluctant step toward the Map’s model.

Revonsuo’s simulation theory is similarly not a concession that dreaming lacks adaptive explanation. Threat Simulation Theory is expressly evolutionary: offline simulation is proposed to have adaptive value. Saying that adaptive pressure is “minimal” because the sleeping organism is currently paralysed confuses an activity’s immediate behavioural output with the evolutionary value of maintaining or rehearsing capacities.

## Finding 5 — “The system is doing less” is not established

The article rhetorically intensifies the hard problem by asking why there is “more experience when the system is doing less.”

Three different quantities are being conflated:

- absence or attenuation of external sensory drive;
- reduced activity in some executive and neuromodulatory systems;
- total neural processing or representational activity.

REM is not globally quiescent. Lucid REM is often especially activated within the REM distribution. Visual-association, limbic, motor-simulation and posterior cortical systems may be strongly engaged while other systems are relatively suppressed. [OUP Academic+2PubMed+2](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/45/4/zsab294/6528977)

The article may legitimately say that dreaming occurs with a **different allocation and mode of neural processing**. It may not infer a phenomenal surplus over diminishing neural resources without specifying and measuring the relevant resource.

## Finding 6 — Bilzer and Monzel do not demonstrate a general “vividness surplus”

The article now reports the study’s modality-specific findings accurately: emotional and visual dream imagery was rated more vivid than voluntary waking imagery, while auditory, olfactory, gustatory and tactile imagery showed the reverse.

But the article then overinterprets those data.

Bilzer and Monzel:

- surveyed 226 participants;
- compared remembered dream imagery with **voluntary waking mental imagery**, not waking perception;
- did not measure brain activity or neural resources;
- did not exclusively study lucid dreams;
- found a selective multisensory profile, not globally richer dream experience.

Their result supports a difference between two forms of offline imagery. It does not establish that a sleeping brain produces richer output with fewer resources, nor that visual/emotional channels are supplied by an independent mind. [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2411-5150/9/2/37)

The predictive-processing explanation is straightforward: voluntary imagery competes with high-precision waking sensory input and executive task demands, whereas dream imagery dominates the currently active model when exteroceptive precision is low. That explanation may leave the hard problem untouched, but so does the assertion that consciousness “renders” content.

## Finding 7 — The architecture/content division is stipulated, not discovered

The article claims:

> the brain provides rendering architecture while consciousness provides rendering content.

No cited experiment separates these contributions.

The evidence instead shows:

- content-specific activity in posterior sensory and association regions;
- dreamed movement recruiting sensorimotor systems;
- lucidity associated with altered prefrontal, parietal, precuneus and connectivity patterns;
- external cues changing dream contents;
- lesions and individual sensory history constraining dream imagery.

A dualist may interpret those findings as interface conditions. But the same evidence is directly predicted by a neural generative model in which learned cortical hierarchies encode both the available representational space and the content instantiated within it.

To make the architecture/content proposal scientific, the article would need a discriminating prediction—for example, a class of dream-content variation that remains after complete specification of relevant neural state and cannot be attributed to stochastic neural dynamics, unmeasured memory or report error. At present, “architecture” means what the article grants to the brain, while “content” means the unexplained phenomenal remainder. That is a philosophical partition, not an empirical result.

## Finding 8 — The purported blindsight/dreaming double dissociation does not hold

The article presents:

- blindsight as neural processing without experience;
- dreams as experience without input;
- therefore, a double dissociation between processing and phenomenal rendering.

This is not a genuine double dissociation.

Blindsight involves residual neural processing of externally presented stimuli after particular visual-system damage, with absent, degraded or disputed awareness. Dreaming involves extensive endogenous neural processing in sensory and association systems without ordinary retinal stimulation. The contrast is therefore:

- external-input processing with deficient conscious report;
- endogenous processing with conscious report.

It is not:

- neural processing without consciousness;
- consciousness without neural processing.

Moreover, the neighbouring vision article itself recognises that perceptual integration can occur without conscious access and that some blindsight may involve degraded experience rather than none.

The phrase “perceptual organisation fails in blindsight” is also too categorical. Blindsight subjects retain various forms of localisation, orientation, movement, shape, affective and visuomotor processing, with considerable variation between patients and paradigms. The boundary between unconscious organisation and phenomenal organisation is precisely what remains disputed.

## Finding 9 — Lucid control does not reveal non-physical mental causation

It is empirically credible that expectations and intentions can influence lucid-dream contents. It does not follow that the causal transition is non-physical.

A physicalist account can represent:

- a metacognitive state encoding “I am dreaming”;
- a goal or intention state;
- top-down modulation of imagery-generating networks;
- altered expectations changing subsequent dream content.

The article replies that this leaves the hard problem unresolved. Correct—but that is not evidence for bidirectional interaction. The hard problem concerns why the process is experienced. The causal question concerns what changes the subsequent physical and phenomenal state. Showing that the neural description does not explain phenomenality does not show that phenomenality supplied an additional causal influence.

The same problem affects the phrase “mental causation made visible.” What is visible in the experiment is **intention-responsive state transition**. The ontological nature of the intention remains the point at issue.

Imperfect dream control is equally non-discriminating. It can be described as consciousness struggling with interface constraints, or as goals competing with strong generative priors, affective salience, memory fragments and neural attractor dynamics. Both models expect partial and variable control.

## Finding 10 — The predictive-processing rival is not given a fair hearing

The article mentions disinhibition, metabolic cost and avoidance of waking hallucination. These are not the strongest contemporary physicalist responses.

Simor, Bogdány and Peigneux offer a lucid-dream-specific framework in which prediction errors can arise at different levels of the hierarchy; a higher-order self-model resolves the mismatch by representing the current state as dreaming; multisensory integration helps maintain lucidity; and attentional control alters the precision balance between bottom-up and top-down information. The framework produces testable predictions concerning induction, individual differences and dream maintenance. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36279459/)

A serious predictive-processing steelman explains the article’s main phenomena as follows:

| Phenomenon | Predictive-processing account |
| --- | --- |
| Immersive dream world | High-level generative models dominate when exteroceptive precision is low |
| Dream bizarreness | Fewer high-precision sensory prediction errors correct the generative trajectory |
| Lucidity | A higher-order self-model resolves discrepancies between dream events, memory and interoceptive or proprioceptive signals |
| Dream control | Intentions alter high-level priors and precision weighting, thereby biasing lower-level simulated content |
| Imperfect control | Existing priors, affective salience, unstable attention and internally generated errors constrain the intended trajectory |
| External cue incorporation | A sufficiently salient external signal gains precision and is assimilated into the active model |
| Waking sensory degradation | Priors cannot recover the missing, presently unknown fine detail of the external environment without generating confabulation |
| Vivid dreams but weak voluntary imagery | The imagery model dominates during sleep but competes with precise sensory evidence and executive control during waking |

The article can still argue that this account does not explain why any of the processing is phenomenal. But that establishes only the general explanatory gap. It does not create differential evidence for the filter interpretation.

## Finding 11 — The degradation asymmetry is not a successful discriminator

The article asks why a brain capable of dreaming a detailed street cannot compensate for macular degeneration, cataract, cochlear damage or retinal scotomas.

The comparison overlooks the difference between:

- generating a plausible but unconstrained scene; and
- accurately reconstructing missing current environmental information.

A dream can feel detailed while containing unstable, generic, misplaced or retrospectively confabulated detail. A predictive model may generate “a face,” “a street” or “text” without possessing the information needed to reconstruct this person’s face, that moving vehicle or the letters currently obscured by a scotoma. Filling the gap with a plausible guess would often reduce perceptual accuracy and create hallucination.

Charles Bonnet syndrome shows internally generated visual contents correlated with content-specific ventral extrastriate activity. It does not show the visual system generating reliable *compensatory* imagery that restores the lost scene. Calling these hallucinations “compensatory imagery” imports the very function the evidence does not demonstrate. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10196592/)

The filter account also inherits a version of the problem. If independent consciousness possesses rich rendering capacities, why can it not supply the missing veridical content? The answer must again be that the required current-world information is unavailable. Once that distinction is introduced, the asymmetry ceases to favour either ontology.

## Finding 12 — The colour-blindness claim is unsupported

The article asserts that colour-blind people dream in the same restricted palette. I found no robust modern primary study supporting that generalisation.

The closest directly relevant indexed paper is a 1982 psychoanalytic case series of three colour-blind patients. It reported the use of colour in their dreams and interpreted it through denial and psychodynamic conflict. Whatever one thinks of that interpretation, it does not support the article’s population-level claim. [OpenAlex+1](https://openalex.org/W2415509229)

More generally, reported dream colour is method-sensitive and influenced by recall, imagery style and prior media exposure. [Heidelberg Journals+2Sage Journals+2](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/9179)

**Required action:** either provide a suitable contemporary primary source with an adequately characterised colour-vision sample, or remove the sentence.

## Finding 13 — “Phenomenal completeness” overstates the evidence

Dream experience can be richly multisensory, but “phenomenally complete” and “the full qualitative spectrum” go beyond what the evidence establishes.

Dream modalities are uneven. Visual and emotional imagery dominate many reports; smell, taste and detailed touch are less frequent. Reports are vulnerable to awakening delay, reconstruction and demand effects. Bilzer and Monzel’s own selective results count against a simple blanket-completeness claim. [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2411-5150/9/2/37)

A more accurate claim would be:

> Dreams can instantiate integrated and compelling experiences across multiple sensory and affective modalities without contemporaneous stimulation matching those contents.

That remains philosophically significant without implying exhaustive or waking-equivalent phenomenology.

## Finding 14 — Prefrontal “reactivation” requires qualification

The broad idea that lucidity involves renewed metacognitive and frontoparietal engagement is reasonable. But the evidence remains heterogeneous:

- the Dresler fMRI result was a tiny case study;
- Baird 2018 concerned waking resting-state connectivity in frequent lucid dreamers, not within-dream activation;
- Voss’s frontal gamma localisation was later substantially attributed to eye-muscle artefact;
- Demirel 2025 found source-level and connectivity changes but not a simple global prefrontal reactivation signature.

The article should not present “the prefrontal cortex partially reactivates” as a settled unitary finding. It should distinguish state activation, trait connectivity and scalp-EEG artefacts. [PubMed+3PubMed+3Nature+3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22754049/)

“Neurochemistry is fundamentally altered” is also too unspecific. REM sleep has a characteristic neuromodulatory profile, but the article provides no primary evidence identifying a lucidity-specific neurochemical transition.

# 4. Does lucid dreaming support dualist rendering?

## The necessary evidential comparison

For lucid dreaming to support dualist rendering rather than merely be compatible with it, the article needs some evidence E such that:

P(E∣dualist rendering)>P(E∣neural generative model)

The article instead mostly identifies evidence predicted by both:

- vivid internally generated experience;
- intention-responsive content;
- metacognitive lucidity;
- individual variation in dream control;
- external cue incorporation;
- neural correlates of lucidity and content;
- differences between dreaming and voluntary imagery.

The dualist framework can accommodate all of these by positing an interface and filter. A neural predictive-processing model can accommodate them through hierarchical priors, precision weighting, recurrent cortical activity, memory, metacognition and attentional control. Compatibility with both models is not differential support for either.

## The “rendering dilemma” is not exhaustive

The article frames the alternatives as:

1. the brain possesses a powerful renderer whose non-use in sensory impairment is puzzling; or
2. consciousness supplies dream rendering through brain architecture.

There is at least one omitted middle account:

1. the brain implements a probabilistic generative model capable of producing subjectively compelling but informationally unconstrained simulations; accurate waking perception additionally depends on presently available sensory evidence.

That third account predicts both vivid dreams and inability to reconstruct missing current-world detail. It therefore dissolves much of the proposed dilemma without committing to identity theory or claiming to solve the hard problem.

## The hard problem does not supply positive evidence for this mechanism

The article repeatedly makes a move of the following form:

1. a neural model explains functional organisation;
2. it does not explain why the organisation is accompanied by experience;
3. therefore, a non-physical consciousness probably supplies the experience.

Step 3 does not follow without a positive account of how the proposed consciousness generates or selects content, interfaces with neural systems, preserves content-specific correlations and affects physical processing.

Dualist rendering relocates the hard problem into at least three further problems:

- **allocation:** why does this neural state receive this phenomenal content?
- **interface:** how does non-physical content influence neural dynamics?
- **coordination:** why do detailed content-specific neural and phenomenal structures remain aligned?

The article is entitled to argue that physicalism leaves the explanatory gap open. It is not entitled to count naming an independent renderer as closing that gap.

## Best defensible conclusion

The strongest conclusion supported by the evidence is:

> Lucid dreaming demonstrates that conscious perceptual worlds need not be driven by matching contemporaneous external stimuli. The empirical findings constrain theories of consciousness but presently underdetermine whether phenomenal content is produced by neural generative processes, instantiated through a mind–brain interface, or related to neural activity in some other way.

That conclusion is significant, compatible with the Map’s commitments and more defensible than the present claim of comparative explanatory advantage.

# 5. Fairness to rivals and tenet-protective bracketing

## Honest hedges

The article does contain several genuine concessions:

- dream phenomenology alone does not force either model;
- physicalists can appeal to disinhibition and suppression of hallucination;
- the proposed double dissociation may be read differently;
- degradation evidence does not settle the issue;
- dream control is imperfect;
- the production horn remains open.

These are improvements over a simple advocacy piece.

## The “hedge-and-bank” pattern

The difficulty is that uncertainty is conceded locally and then the desired conclusion is banked globally. Examples include:

- “not forced” followed by “less explanatory debt”;
- “the production theorist can redescribe this” followed by “structurally simpler”;
- “if it holds up” followed by treating the double dissociation as support;
- “arguably cleaner” followed by “exactly what dualism predicts”;
- “speculates” followed by presenting variable control as expected under quantum interaction.

This creates the appearance of calibration while leaving the argumentative architecture nearly unchanged.

## Minimal quantum interaction is tenet-protective speculation

The article suggests that unreliable lucid-dream control may reflect consciousness biasing quantum-indeterminate neural outcomes.

No lucid-dream study cited or omitted here measures quantum-sensitive neural events, distinguishes quantum from classical stochasticity, or derives a quantitative prediction from the Map’s model. Variable control is what virtually every psychological and neural-control model already predicts.

The passage therefore has no empirical support from lucid dreaming. Its role is to absorb an apparent weakness—imperfect control—into the theory after the fact. Unless the Map specifies what distribution of success, temporal profile or neural signature would differ under quantum selection, this is accommodation rather than explanation.

## The Many-Worlds paragraph has no connection to the evidence

Lucid dreaming supplies no experimental leverage over Everettian quantum mechanics. An Everettian can describe dream intentions and actions as ordinary branch-relative causal processes; they need not regard agency as phenomenologically unreal merely because other outcomes occur in other branches.

The paragraph derives a tenet from prior commitment rather than from lucid-dream findings and should be removed from the empirical argument.

## Methodological evidence of a tenet firewall

The site’s internal review history is revealing. An earlier deep review treated the quantum-interaction claim, the Many-Worlds discussion and the production/filter dispute as “bedrock framework-boundary disagreements” and instructed that they should not be reflagged as critical defects.

That is precisely where external review is most needed. A claim does not cease to be evidentially assessable because it follows from a tenet. The correct distinction is:

- **framework commitment:** the Map may openly adopt dualism;
- **empirical support claim:** whether a particular experiment favours that commitment remains reviewable;
- **internal interpretation:** the Map may state how it interprets the result, provided it does not describe compatibility as confirmation.

The present review process appears better at checking whether a statistic or quotation exists than at checking whether the source plays the argumentative role assigned to it.

# 6. Integration with neighbouring articles

## *Dream Consciousness*

This article gives broader context and contains valuable material absent from the target page, including signal verification and neural-state discussion. But it propagates the Baird error by stating that frequent lucid dreamers have larger anterior prefrontal cortex and that practice shapes structure. Baird 2018 found no significant structural differences. It also needs to incorporate the Baird 2022 correction to frontal gamma and distinguish the Demirel 2025 results from the older hybrid-state account.

The Konkoly 2026 problem-solving result is also overinterpreted there. Both articles should use a common evidence summary noting the planned-model, mediation and post-hoc limitations.

## *Predictive Processing*

This is the most important missing reciprocal integration. The Map’s predictive-processing article already says that dreaming does not straightforwardly decide between production and filter models. It describes dreams as internally dominated generative processing and presents a significantly more sophisticated rival than the target article acknowledges. Yet it too repeats the false “larger aPFC” claim and gives an oversimplified description of the Demirel findings.

The target article should directly engage Simor’s lucid-dream-specific framework and link to this article in both directions.

## *Mental Imagery*

The mental-imagery article is better calibrated: it explicitly states that imagery does not uniquely establish dualism and treats neural and dualist interpretations as underdetermined. It is the natural home for interpreting Bilzer and Monzel’s comparison of dream imagery with voluntary imagery. The target article currently uses that comparison without integrating the neighbouring article’s caution.

## *Filter Theory*

The filter-theory article contains a methodological warning that should govern the lucid-dream piece: sophisticated production and filter accounts often accommodate altered-state findings through parallel moves, so multiple examples should not be counted as independent confirmations. It also concedes that altered-state evidence tells mainly against naive production models and that the filter interpretation derives much of its appeal from prior hard-problem arguments rather than discriminating experiments.

The target article currently violates that discipline by counting intention-responsiveness, vividness, phenomenal integration, metacognition, sensory degradation and cue incorporation as multiple “signatures,” although most arise from the same fact: internally dominated conscious processing during REM.

## *Capability Division in Vision*

This neighbouring article provides the architecture/content division on which the lucid-dream article relies. But it also derives the reality of that division from the site’s tenets: dualism establishes that the division is ontological, bidirectional interaction ensures that the mind-side is causally nonempty, and quantum interaction explains why the boundary is difficult to observe. That makes it unsuitable as independent support.

The lucid-dream article cites the vision article as if visual neuroscience had empirically located a transition from brain computation to mind-supplied phenomenal organisation. It has not. The relation is circular:

1. the tenets establish the division;
2. the vision article interprets neuroscience through that division;
3. the lucid-dream article cites the vision article as evidence for the division.

## *Mind–Brain Separation and the Division of Faculties*

This article contains two caveats that should be imported into the target:

- the rendering-engine analogy risks a separate-viewer regress;
- neurological and dream findings can often be matched by neural reorganisation or production accounts.

Yet it also repeats the claim that two-way dream communication supports mental causation. The experiments establish causal integration of information and action during dreaming; they do not determine whether the relevant causal state is non-physical.

# 7. Referee decision

The article is not ready to stand as an evidential article under its current title.

### Strengths

- The rendering dilemma is an engaging way to organise questions about endogenous experience.
- The revised text contains more concessions than earlier Map articles of this type.
- The selective Bilzer result is now stated accurately.
- Imperfect dream control is acknowledged rather than ignored.
- The distinction between architecture and content is philosophically fertile, even though it is not empirically established.
- The article identifies a real limitation of purely functional accounts: modelling dream construction does not by itself explain phenomenal presence.

### Decisive weaknesses

- A direct factual error concerning Baird 2018 underwrites one of the four principal “capability signatures.”
- The article calls a selectively permeable sensory channel closed.
- It omits the foundational signal-verification paper and major neurophysiological corrections.
- It reverses the argumentative role of Hobson’s predictive-generative model.
- It treats correlational and post-hoc results as causal pressure against epiphenomenalism.
- It does not engage the strongest lucid-dream-specific predictive-processing account.
- Its architecture/content division is stipulated by the Map’s tenets.
- It mistakes failure to solve the hard problem for positive evidence that a separate renderer exists.
- Its quantum and Many-Worlds sections are not connected to the lucid-dream evidence.

A more accurate title would be:

> **Lucid Dreaming and the Underdetermination of Dream Rendering**

or, if the advocacy role should remain explicit:

> **A Dualist Interpretation of Lucid Dreaming**

## Site methodology

1. **Create a claim–source ledger.** For every empirical claim record the exact claim, primary source, sample, design, verification method, result, limitations and the argumentative role assigned to the source.
2. **Enforce primary-source precedence.** Reviews such as Baird et al. 2019 may organise a section, but all load-bearing experimental claims should point to the underlying study.
3. **Label inferential status.** Every result should be marked as descriptive, correlational, longitudinal, experimentally manipulated, post-hoc, mediation-supported or merely theoretically compatible.
4. **Audit argumentative-role fidelity.** Verifying that a quotation exists is insufficient. Reviewers must ask whether the cited author used the finding in the same direction and for the same purpose. Hobson 2014 is the paradigmatic failure here.
5. **Require a strongest-rival reconstruction.** Before claiming explanatory advantage, each article should state the most developed rival account in terms its proponents would accept. For lucid dreaming, that requires Simor’s predictive framework, not only generic remarks about disinhibition.
6. **Separate compatibility from confirmation.** Articles should use “supports” only where the observation is more expected under the preferred theory than under named alternatives. Otherwise use “is compatible with” or “can be interpreted through.”
7. **Retire the framework-boundary exemption.** Tenet-connected claims should remain open to critical review. A tenet may determine the Map’s eventual position, but it cannot determine whether an experiment supports that position.
8. **Add corpus-level propagation checks.** A correction to a source interpretation—such as Baird 2018—should automatically identify every article repeating the same claim.
9. **Maintain an evidence-status block for fast-moving topics.** Record foundational result, strongest replication, strongest correction, largest current sample and unresolved methodological problem. For lucid dreaming these include LaBerge 1981, Baird 2022 and Demirel 2025.
10. **Require statistical-context review.** A numerically correct percentage should not pass review without planned-versus-post-hoc status, sample size, uncertainty, model result and whether the proposed mediation or causal test succeeded.
11. **Count common-cause evidence only once.** Vividness, metacognition, control and multisensory integration may all reflect the same altered generative regime. They should not be presented as independent convergence without an independence argument.
12. **Publish discriminating predictions for dualist rendering.** Specify what observable result would be more likely if consciousness supplies content than if neural generative models do—and what result would lower confidence in the dualist account.
