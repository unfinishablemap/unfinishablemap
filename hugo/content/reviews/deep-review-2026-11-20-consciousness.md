## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Possibility/probability slippage: the arguments "jointly establishing" dualism** (L137). The tenets page says the Dualism rationale is "a commitment the Map owns, not a result it reports … it does not report that dispute as won". [P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1) puts the cumulative force at "real but modest". A reader who accepts the tenets would still flag "establishing". OLD: "The Map takes the arguments as jointly establishing that consciousness is not reducible". NEW: "The Map takes the arguments as jointly supporting its commitment that consciousness is not reducible".

2. **Stale cluster map, inconsistent with [P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1) and `concepts/dualism`** (L137). The hub put the knowledge argument, conceivability, the gap and inverted qualia into one cluster, and said "only the argument from reason begins elsewhere". The cluster map was restated on 2026-10-23 (`concepts/dualism` L134, `topics/the-convergence-argument-for-dualism` L79–87) and registered on 2026-11-10 ([P-D1](/positions/arguments-for-dualism/#p-d1)). Under it, Mary's Room and the qualia arguments form the phenomenal-intuition cluster, while the gap and conceivability form the explanatory-gap cluster. The argument from reason is "a separate self-defeat charge rather than a fourth cluster". The old wording also clashed with the hub's own next sentence, which locates the force "*across* clusters". NEW: "The knowledge and inverted qualia arguments share one cluster, treating first-person phenomenal reports as revealing real features; the conceivability argument and the explanatory gap share another, resting on the premise that the gap between physical description and phenomenal character is real rather than apparent. If a cluster's common starting point fails, its members fail together rather than confirming one another. The argument from reason presses a separate self-defeat charge, from the normativity of inference."

3. **The page contradicted itself on epiphenomenalism** (L176–180). The 2026-11-02 fix at L117 says that whether a lawful parallel yields knowledge is "the contested premise" (tenets L101). The Causation section still said reports "require" consciousness entering the physical domain, and that under epiphenomenalism "the baseline cognition hypothesis would make no sense". The tenets grade baseline cognition as "suggestive but contested corroboration". `concepts/conscious-vs-unconscious-processing` L50 says the functional findings show that "conscious access makes a causal difference … whether phenomenal experience does is left open". Changes:
   - Lead-in: "Evidence includes:" → "Three considerations support it, none decisively:"
   - Reports: inserted "on its face," and appended "The epiphenomenalist's lawful-parallel reply, noted [above], contests that step."
   - Cognitive effects: "functions unavailable to unconscious processing" → "functions unconscious processing cannot match" (the sibling's wording; it concedes durable maintenance is a difference of degree). "If consciousness were epiphenomenal, the baseline cognition hypothesis would make no sense." → "This shows conscious *access* at work; whether phenomenal experience does the work is a further step, to which the baseline cognition hypothesis lends suggestive but contested support."

4. **Possibility/probability slippage on the meta-problem** (L70). OLD: "The Map treats the persistence of these intuitions as evidence of something real rather than a quirk of cognitive mechanism." `concepts/meta-problem-of-consciousness` L137 says the Map's moves "*neutralise a threat*; neither adds independent positive evidence for dualism". That is the defeater-removal-as-evidence pattern. NEW: "On the Dualism tenet our problem intuitions are accurate, giving the meta-problem a face-value solution; that neutralises a debunking threat rather than adding evidence for dualism."

5. **Block misattributed, and the cited source contradicted** (L80, L85). OLD: "Ned Block introduced this distinction to isolate what physicalist theories can explain (access mechanisms) from what remains puzzling (phenomenology)." Block 1995's stated target is a fallacy: "an obvious function of the machinery of access-consciousness is illicitly transferred to phenomenal consciousness". Block's own project is physicalist; he looks for "a neural realizer" of overflow (Block 2007). So the old sentence presented the Map's purpose as Block's. Separately, L85 said "[blindsight](/concepts/blindsight/) demonstrates access without (or with degraded) phenomenology". The abstract of the paper the page cites says "stimuli in the blind field are both access-unconscious and phenomenally unconscious". In his Response (R4) Block says A-without-P cases are "conceptually possible, but I knew of no actual ones". The sibling grade is "may demonstrate" / "suggests" (`concepts/access-consciousness` L41, L111). NEW L80: "Ned Block drew the distinction to expose a fallacy, in which a function of the access machinery is "illicitly transferred to phenomenal consciousness". Block himself seeks a neural basis for phenomenality; the dualist reading below is the Map's." NEW L82–85: "For the Map, the distinction matters because:", "Physicalist theories make headway on access." (was "Physicalism succeeds at explaining access."), "The two may dissociate. The Map reads [blindsight](/concepts/blindsight/) as partial access with minimal or absent phenomenology, though Block counts blind-field stimuli as both access- and phenomenally unconscious; the [overflow argument](/concepts/phenomenal-overflow/) suggests phenomenology exceeding access, an open hypothesis." The overflow grade follows `concepts/phenomenal-overflow` L166 as revised today: "an open hypothesis … at most compatible with the dualism tenet". The Map's blindsight reading follows `concepts/access-consciousness` L71.

6. **Inline cite with no reference**: "Chalmers later introduced the meta-problem (2018)" had no References entry. Added: Chalmers, D. J. (2018). "The Meta-Problem of Consciousness." *Journal of Consciousness Studies*, 25(9–10), 6–61.
