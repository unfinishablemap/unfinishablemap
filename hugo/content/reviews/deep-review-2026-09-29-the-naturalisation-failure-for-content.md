## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Fabricated rationale attributed to Mann & Pain 2022 (CRITICAL, fixed at root and across the family).** The article stated that Mann and Pain hold teleosemantics "is not required to meet [the intensionality criterion], since intensionality is a personal-level desideratum rather than a requirement on sub-personal cognitive states." The word *personal* occurs **zero times** in the paper. Their actual argument (§4, "Teleosemantics need not meet the intensionality criterion") is Russellian: following Heck (2012, "Solving Frege's Puzzle", *J. Phil.* 109: 132–174) and Millikan's (2017) theory of concepts, the folk-psychological work modes of presentation were meant to do — explaining why Fred, who already knew Mark Twain had died, is newly upset to hear that Sam Clemens has — is done by beliefs employing different *vehicles* (Millikan's "unicepts") while sharing one purely referential content, so no theory of content owes intensionality. The paper *does* grant that functional norms fail the intensionality criterion ("Invoking functional norms accounts for the truth-evaluable criterion … but not the intensionality criterion", §4.1), so the article's "concedes" was fair while its stated reason was invented. Root cause: [research/hutto-myin-hard-problem-of-content-2026-04-27.md](/research/hutto-myin-hard-problem-of-content-2026-04-27/) recorded the paper as "paywalled; abstract-level summary only" and glossed the abstract's "not required to meet" with a level-split the Map supplied itself; the gloss then propagated into this article, the archived `concepts/hard-problem-of-content` page (still a public URL), `concepts/teleosemantics` (where the 2026-09-28 todo task *instructed* the writer to build on the personal-level framing), and [research/teleosemantics-2026-07-11.md](/research/teleosemantics-2026-07-11/). All five files corrected this pass; the sibling concept page now marks its level-split extension as "the Map's construction, not the paper's". The research note's "paywalled" claim was itself wrong — the paper is hybrid CC-BY OA — and is corrected with the mirror URL.
