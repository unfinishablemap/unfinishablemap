## 2. Primary-source and attribution audit

### Nagel

The article is right that Nagel treats subjective character—what an experience is like for its subject—as central to consciousness, and that he regarded the available forms of objective reduction as inadequate. The page nevertheless strengthens this into an in-principle claim that subjective experience is accessible *only* through the first person. Nagel’s conclusion is more guarded. He explicitly entertains the development of an “objective phenomenology” capable of describing aspects of subjective character to beings unable to undergo the experience themselves. His argument establishes a limitation of current concepts and reductive methods, not a theorem that third-person understanding is permanently impossible. [UConn Philosophy Department+1](https://philosophy.uconn.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/365/2020/03/Nagel-What-is-it-like-to-be-a-bat.pdf)

**Verdict:** accurate historical association; overstated modal conclusion.

### Jackson

The presentation of Mary is faithful to Jackson’s 1982 argument: Mary possesses the complete physical information while confined, learns something upon seeing colour, and Jackson originally inferred that physical information is incomplete. The article’s further claim that this demonstrates “two fundamentally different epistemic channels” is an interpretation of the case, not something established by the scenario alone. Ability, acquaintance, new-mode-of-presentation, representationalist, social-externalist and Russellian responses all dispute what kind of epistemic gain Mary undergoes and what follows metaphysically. [Simon Fraser University+2Simon Fraser University+2](https://www.sfu.ca/~jillmc/JacksonfromJStore.pdf)

The presentation is also historically incomplete. Jackson later wrote from a physicalist and representationalist standpoint, while contemporary defenders such as Torin Alter continue to argue that the knowledge argument survives physicalist replies. That continuing disagreement is exactly why a 2026 hub cannot use the 1982 paper as though it settled the ontology. [Cambridge University Press+2PhilPapers+2](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/428B619967A0F8F9D287ADB8A6D457F9/S1358246100008365a.pdf/mind_and_illusion.pdf)

**Verdict:** accurate account of the original argument; inadequate account of its dialectical status.

### Levine

The attribution of an *epistemological* explanatory gap to Levine is accurate. Levine deliberately reformulates a Kripkean metaphysical argument as an epistemological problem: physical identities involving consciousness lack the explanatory transparency found in paradigmatic reductions. But Levine also explicitly cautions that an epistemic gap does not by itself establish a metaphysical possibility of separation or show that materialism is false. [Information Philosopher+1](https://www.informationphilosopher.com/solutions/philosophers/levine/Explanatory_Gap.pdf)

The article invokes Levine in a cumulative anti-physicalist case without clearly preserving that restriction. This is a significant contextual distortion even though the citation itself is correct.

**Verdict:** attribution accurate; inferential use insufficiently faithful to the source.

### Chalmers

The article’s short quotation that physical accounts leave us “entirely in the dark” about why experience accompanies processing is accurate. Chalmers also treats experiences, rather than merely verbal judgments about them, as the ultimate explananda. [Personal LSE+1](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/ROBERT49/teaching/ph103/pdf/Chalmers_The_Conscious_Mind.pdf)

The problem is that the article subsequently slides among four different things:

1. phenomenal experience;
2. an introspective judgment about experience;
3. a verbal or behavioural report;
4. publicly assessable evidence derived from that report.

Chalmers distinguishes phenomenal seeming from psychological seeming and judgment. His formulation therefore does not license the page’s claim that first-person reports themselves “constitute the explanandum.” Experience may be the explanandum; reports are normally evidence about it, and the reliability and interpretation of that evidence remain open questions.

**Verdict:** quotation accurate; the surrounding evidential formulation conflates distinct levels.

### Dennett and heterophenomenology

The claim that heterophenomenology “reduces first-person data to behavioural evidence” is recognizably the Map’s criticism, but it is not an opponent-endorsable description of Dennett’s method. Dennett treats subjects as indispensable collaborators whose reports are used to construct a neutral catalogue of how things seem to them. The method brackets whether those reported objects and properties exist as described; it does not deny that the reports or seemings are data. Dennett also presents heterophenomenology as an organization of the explananda from which inquiry begins, not as a completed theory of consciousness. [Tufts Digital Library+2Tufts Digital Library+2](https://dl.tufts.edu/downloads/br86bg072)

**Verdict:** materially slanted attribution.

### Frankish and illusionism

The rival-position matrix is an improvement over simply saying that illusionists deny consciousness. Frankish does deny that experience possesses the intrinsic, private qualitative properties posited by robust qualia theories. But he accepts introspection, introspective seemings, reports and ordinary conscious life; his claim is that introspection systematically misrepresents the nature of the properties involved. He also argues that introspection alone cannot adjudicate the ontology and must be assessed alongside broader explanatory and causal considerations. [Keith Frankish+2Keith Frankish+2](https://keithfrankish.github.io/articles/Frankish_2022_Illusionism_eprint.pdf)

Thus the matrix is acceptable only if “phenomenal data of the relevant kind” is explicitly defined as *intrinsic nonphysical qualitative properties*. Without that qualification, it makes illusionism appear to reject the evidence rather than dispute the Map’s interpretation of it.

**Verdict:** partially corrected but still asymmetrical.

## 3. Are the central claims sufficiently supported?

### 3.1 “The consciousness debate is at bottom an epistemological crisis”

This is the article’s organizing thesis, but it is asserted rather than demonstrated. Nagel, Jackson, Levine and Chalmers establish that important consciousness arguments contain epistemic premises. They do not establish the comparative claim that epistemological assumptions do more work than *any empirical finding*, nor that disagreement over ontology is fundamentally reducible to disagreement over evidential standards. [concepts/epistemology](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/epistemology/)

To support the stronger thesis, the article would need at least one of the following:

- a comparative analysis of major empirical research programmes showing that their results underdetermine ontology;
- an argument that the interpretation of all relevant neural and behavioural evidence necessarily depends upon one of the disputed epistemologies;
- a historical case showing that convergence on empirical findings has left only epistemic disagreement;
- or an explicit transcendental argument establishing that first-person warrant is prior to all relevant third-person evidence.

None is supplied. At present, “epistemological crisis” functions as the site’s preferred framing, not as a conclusion earned by the cited evidence.

### 3.2 The two-channel model is too coarse

The page presents first-person acquaintance and third-person description as two fundamentally different channels. That binary compresses several separable questions:

- Who has direct causal or informational access?
- What method produces the belief?
- What is the content of the resulting evidence?
- Is the evidence private or publicly communicable?
- What makes the resulting belief justified?
- How can its reliability be calibrated?

Contemporary work on introspection distinguishes introspection as a method of inquiry from introspective access as a psychological capacity, and examines privacy, interference, reliability and epistemic value separately. That more granular treatment makes the Map’s channel metaphor look undertheorized. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/55721)

A subject may have privileged access to an experience while producing a publicly assessable report; a third party may acquire testimonial evidence of the same occurrence; and both forms of evidence may be defeated by independent information. “First-person” and “third-person” therefore identify positions in an evidential system, not necessarily two autonomous epistemologies.

### 3.3 Epistemic asymmetry does not establish ontological nonidentity

The page says direct acquaintance warrants the claims that an experience exists, has a qualitative character and “is not identical to any third-person description.” The final step is unsupported. In its literal form it is trivial: an experience is no more identical to a description than a brain state is identical to a sentence describing it. In its intended form—presumably that the experience is not identical to any physical state picked out by third-person description—it is a substantive metaphysical conclusion that does not follow from a difference in modes of access. [concepts/epistemology](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/epistemology/)

The site’s own Phenomenal Authority article now states the objection correctly: an identity theorist can grant first-person incorrigibility under one mode of presentation and physical description under another, and epistemic asymmetry does not itself entail ontological difference. It explicitly calls the direct inference to dualism unsupported. [topics/phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence/)

There is also a Sellarsian problem. Sensing or undergoing a state does not automatically amount to propositional knowledge of its metaphysical nature. A self-authenticating, non-inferential awareness cannot simply be assumed to supply all the conceptual content required by the conclusion “this property is nonphysical.” [Pitt Sites+1](https://sites.pitt.edu/~rbrandom/Courses/2023%20Sellars/Sellars%20texts/SellarsEmpPhilMind.pdf)

The warranted conclusion is thinner:

> There is an experience, and it seems or presents itself to the subject in a certain way.

Moving from that to intrinsic qualitative properties, irreducibility or nonphysicality requires additional premises.

### 3.4 Experience, report and evidence are repeatedly conflated

“Phenomenological evidence constitutes the explanandum itself” contains a defensible insight and an important confusion. The experience is what a theory of phenomenal consciousness is supposed to explain. But evidence *for claims about* the experience may consist of introspective judgment, memory, discrimination, report, behaviour, physiology or experimentally controlled correlations. Those evidential vehicles are not identical to the experience. [concepts/epistemology](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/epistemology/)

The further claim that without first-person reports there would be no consciousness problem is too broad as a methodological proposition. No-report paradigms attempt to isolate awareness from the cognitive and motor processes involved in reporting, and recent work has decoded or discriminated conscious contents without requiring a report on every trial. Those methods remain contestable and ordinarily require report-based calibration, so they do not eliminate the epistemic importance of first-person evidence. They do, however, show that *report* and *experience* cannot be treated as interchangeable. [OUP Academic+3Nature+3OUP Academic+3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-28897-2)

The better claim would be historical and epistemic rather than absolute: first-person reports are a principal source through which consciousness becomes identifiable and experimentally calibratable.

### 3.5 The parsimony criticism attacks an unduly strong principle

The page is right that simplicity is not an infallible guide to truth and that incomplete knowledge can favour a superficially simple but false theory. That does not show that parsimony has no evidential role. It only refutes the principle that the simpler theory must always be true. [concepts/epistemology](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/epistemology/)

A serious treatment would distinguish:

- parsimony as a truth guarantee;
- parsimony as a defeasible prior;
- reduced parameterization as protection against overfitting;
- explanatory unification;
- ontological economy;
- and predictive compression.

Interactionist dualism then has to be assessed not merely by counting entity types, but by asking whether its additional psychophysical structure improves prediction, explanation or unification enough to compensate for the added freedom. The present discussion exempts dualism from a crude razor rather than supplying a positive model-selection standard.

### 3.6 The closure discussion understates the physicalist case

The article says causal closure is treated as an empirical discovery but functions as a methodological stipulation, and it presents quantum indeterminacy as leaving room for mental influence. That formulation omits the strongest mainstream argument. Papineau expressly treats closure as a historically and scientifically motivated proposition rather than an a priori axiom. He also formulates a probabilistic version: even when quantum theory does not determine a unique result, the physical state and laws may fix the probability distribution. A nonphysical cause that systematically selects outcomes would then change those probabilities and violate probabilistic closure. [DAVID PAPINEAU+1](https://www.davidpapineau.co.uk/uploads/1/8/5/5/18551740/the_casual_closure_of_the_physical_and_naturalism.pdf)

Quantum indeterminacy therefore supplies logical room only in a weak sense. It is not positive evidence of mental intervention. The Map would need to specify:

1. where standard physical probabilities cease to be sufficient;
2. what consciousness-dependent deviation is predicted;
3. how that deviation differs from noise, selection effects or ordinary physical dynamics;
4. and which interpretations of quantum mechanics permit or exclude the proposed mechanism.

The interpretive status of quantum theory remains open, and not all interpretations contain the sort of stochastic event-selection structure the Map requires. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61879/chapter/547555640)

### 3.7 Agency phenomenology does not establish mental causation

The page treats the phenomenology of agency as support for bidirectional interaction. First-person experience can support the claim that actions *seem authored, effortful or controlled*. It cannot by itself establish that a nonphysical subject changes quantum probabilities. The causal interpretation lies outside the content to which introspective authority is strongest. The Map’s own Phenomenal Authority article now recognizes that causal process and metaphysical interpretation exceed first-person authority. [concepts/epistemology](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/epistemology/)

Without an independent causal bridge, this is a transition from:

> I experience myself as acting

to:

> a nonphysical consciousness makes a physical difference.

That transition is one of the main matters under dispute. Treating the phenomenology as evidence for the second proposition risks assuming the reliability of precisely the causal appearance that rivals seek to explain.

### 3.8 The convergence claim is not settled

The article says its arguments are partially independent and refers readers elsewhere for a settlement of how much genuine independence exists. The linked convergence article is now appreciably more cautious: it discusses common cognitive architecture, cultural divergence, Bayesian dependence and the possibility that several arguments share the same disputed intuitions or bridge principles. It says conditional independence does much of the formal work and is the hardest premise to establish. [topics/epistemology-of-convergence-arguments](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/epistemology-of-convergence-arguments/)

The changelog subsequently changed the site-wide formulation from multiple independent arguments to arguments that are “not all independent,” with a case stronger than any single argument but not overwhelming. The Epistemology hub has not absorbed that recalibration. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

### 3.9 The proposed falsifier is potentially self-sealing

The proposed falsifier is a third-person account that predicts reports “without remainder” and leaves “no qualitative residue.” This combines two different standards:

- predicting behaviour and reports;
- metaphysically entailing or explaining experience.

A theory could predict every report while leaving the hard problem untouched. Conversely, a physicalist might maintain that a complete identity explanation leaves no residue while a dualist continues to insist that something qualitative remains. Unless “residue” is identified independently of allegiance to dualism, the criterion permits the site to preserve the theory by redescribing every successful physical account as explanatorily incomplete. [concepts/epistemology](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/epistemology/)

The linked Tenet Falsification article uses stronger and somewhat different criteria: physical entailment of experience, a successful resolution of Mary, failure of the relevant conceivability arguments, and evidence bearing directly on the proposed quantum interaction. That is better than report prediction alone, but those conditions also require operational decision rules. [concepts/tenet-falsification-conditions](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/tenet-falsification-conditions/)

## 4. Serious mainstream counterarguments omitted or understated

### 4.1 Externalism, reliabilism and knowledge-first epistemology

The article largely assumes an internalist picture in which the phenomenological accessibility of a state gives it foundational evidential priority. Externalist approaches instead ask whether a belief is produced by a reliable or appropriately connected process, even when the subject cannot internally certify that process. Knowledge-first approaches treat knowledge as explanatorily prior to justification or evidence rather than beginning with incorrigible appearances. Knowledge-centred approaches remain influential enough that recent Oxford work describes a broader “factive turn” in epistemology. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/9070)

This matters because privileged access and certainty are not the only available measures of epistemic standing. A private belief may feel maximally secure while being produced by an unreliable process; a publicly mediated belief may count as knowledge despite lacking introspective luminosity.

### 4.2 The Myth of the Given and the normativity of acquaintance

The article treats acquaintance as though its mere occurrence supplies justification. The omitted objection is that acquaintance is a psychological relation, whereas justification is normative. The page needs to explain how being conscious of a quality places a subject in a position to know propositions about that quality, especially propositions concerning its intrinsic or nonphysical nature. Sellars’s critique of self-authenticating awareness is the classic version of this problem. [Pitt Sites+1](https://sites.pitt.edu/~rbrandom/Courses/2023%20Sellars/Sellars%20texts/SellarsEmpPhilMind.pdf)

Recent work makes the issue especially current. Kammerer questions whether the felt certainty or specialness of acquaintance provides the epistemic relation dualist arguments need. Pallagrosi’s 2025–26 work develops a normativity challenge and an “acquaintance trilemma”: strong combinations of knowledge, constitutive consciousness and necessarily conscious qualities threaten either inconsistency or trivialization of acquaintance’s epistemic significance. [Springer+2Springer+2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10670-026-01081-4)

### 4.3 Phenomenal concepts and two modes of presentation

A physicalist can grant that phenomenal and physical concepts differ radically while maintaining that they co-refer. Mary may gain a new concept, recognitional capacity or mode of presentation without discovering a new nonphysical property. Chalmers himself treats the phenomenal-concept strategy as a serious way of developing type-B materialism, even while arguing that it ultimately does not provide the required physicalist explanation. [Consc.net](https://www.consc.net/papers/pceg.pdf)

The hub names stronger physicalist positions but never presents this response in enough detail to show where the Map thinks it fails. Its own Phenomenal Authority page already concedes the logical point: distinct epistemic modes do not automatically imply distinct entities or properties.

### 4.4 Representationalism and transparency

Representationalists argue that phenomenal character supervenes on, or is constituted by, representational content rather than by intrinsic private qualities. On such a view, introspection may reveal how the world is represented, not an extra nonphysical item. Recent work continues to refine representationalism, including stronger identity versions, rather than treating it as a discarded twentieth-century position. [Cambridge University Press+1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-philosophy/article/abs/representationalism-perceptual-distortion-and-the-limits-of-phenomenal-concepts/C8549B4F9A64A8B9EC41BE500DD28753)

This directly challenges the article’s assumption that awareness of qualitative character is already awareness of a metaphysically autonomous phenomenal property. The article needs to explain what introspection discriminates beyond representational content and why that discrimination supports dualism.

### 4.5 Defeaters, cognitive penetration and higher-order evidence

Even where an experience initially justifies a belief, that justification may be defeated by evidence about unreliability, bias, expectation, pathology, disagreement or the subject’s poor discriminative track record. Work on higher-order evidence shows how information about one’s own epistemic performance can substantially alter the rational standing of a first-order belief. [Cambridge University Press+1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/FEAB79DBDE02329F572D90BFD011E8E1/S1742360000001362a.pdf/evidentialism_higherorder_evidence_and_disagreement.pdf)

This creates an asymmetry in the article. It emphasizes the defeasibility of physicalist methodological assumptions but treats the nonphysical interpretation of phenomenology as comparatively insulated from higher-order correction. A consistent epistemic humility principle must apply to both.

### 4.6 Contemporary objections to phenomenal conservatism

The linked Phenomenal Conservatism article is more careful than the hub. It says that a physicalist can accept phenomenal conservatism and that the principle by itself does not establish irreducibility. Contemporary objections also include bootstrapping, reflective defeat and questions about whether seemings possess inherent justificatory force; recent work develops phenomenal explanationism as an alternative. [concepts/phenomenal-conservatism](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/phenomenal-conservatism/)

The Epistemology article nevertheless treats direct phenomenological warrant as if it reaches all the way to nonidentity. It fails to import its child page’s thin/thick distinction.

### 4.7 Social and testimonial epistemology

The publicity issue is framed as a choice between accepting private evidence and wrongly requiring that every datum be publicly observable. Mainstream scientific epistemology has a middle position: the phenomenal object may be private while reports, discrimination patterns, interventions and reliability estimates become intersubjectively accessible evidence. Heterophenomenology and no-report approaches differ on how this mediation should work, but neither requires direct public access to another person’s experience. [OUP Academic+3Tufts Digital Library+3Tufts Digital Library+3](https://dl.tufts.edu/downloads/br86bg072)

The serious question is therefore not “must evidence be public?” but:

> Under what calibration, testimonial and experimental conditions should a private report alter another investigator’s credence?

The article does not answer that question.

### 4.8 Causal exclusion and probabilistic closure

The omitted physicalist objection is not merely that physics appears complete. It is that if physical causes already fix either an outcome or its objective probability, an additional mental cause is redundant, overdetermining or probability-altering. A quantum interactionist must say which horn it accepts and what evidence would distinguish its answer. [DAVID PAPINEAU+1](https://www.davidpapineau.co.uk/uploads/1/8/5/5/18551740/the_casual_closure_of_the_physical_and_naturalism.pdf)

The article’s appeal to indeterminacy bypasses rather than answers this argument.

### 4.9 Common-cause and debunking explanations of convergence

Several anti-physicalist arguments may feel mutually supporting because they arise from the same cognitive architecture: introspective opacity, essentialist intuitions, limitations in imaginative recombination, or a shared tendency to reify modes of presentation. If so, their agreement is correlated evidence rather than independent confirmation. The linked convergence article now acknowledges this possibility, but the hub does not integrate it into its confidence level. [topics/epistemology-of-convergence-arguments](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/epistemology-of-convergence-arguments/)

## 5. Where dualism is protected by bracketing

Several passages are formally conditional but function rhetorically as support for the framework.

1. **The epistemic asymmetry is treated as neutral evidence, while the physicalist interpretation is made to bear the entire explanatory burden.** The possibility that the asymmetry arises from concept structure, representation or cognitive architecture is mentioned only at the level of rival labels.
2. **“Science is optimized for the physical domain” presupposes a prior division into physical and nonphysical domains.** It cannot then serve as independent evidence that science is constitutionally unable to explain consciousness. [concepts/epistemology](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/epistemology/)
3. **Closure is downgraded to a stipulation before its empirical and probabilistic formulations are considered.** This brackets the strongest version of the causal-exclusion challenge rather than answering it.
4. **The phenomenology of agency is admitted as evidence for interaction without an independent test of causal veridicality.** The experience is treated as trustworthy precisely where the rival account says it may be systematically misleading.
5. **The convergence question is delegated to another page and described as settled.** The linked page actually treats dependence as unresolved and potentially substantial. [topics/epistemology-of-convergence-arguments](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/epistemology-of-convergence-arguments/)
6. **The falsifier depends upon “qualitative residue,” a notion already interpreted through the Map’s ontology.** A successful physical account can always be declared residually incomplete unless an external decision procedure is adopted.
7. **The internal review system designates the central framing as bedrock.** Once those claims are protected from renewed scrutiny, later reviews can test only local consistency within assumptions that remain untested. [reviews/deep-review-2026-07-25-epistemology](https://unfinishablemap.org/reviews/deep-review-2026-07-25-epistemology/)
8. **Epistemic humility is applied asymmetrically.** Incompleteness of physical explanation is treated as a reason to suspend physicalist conclusions, whereas incompleteness of the proposed psychophysical mechanism does not similarly suspend dualist conclusions. The site-wide outer review has separately identified this pattern of imposing stricter evidential standards on physicalist rivals than on the Map’s own mechanics. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

The page is entitled to develop the consequences of dualism conditionally. It should, however, label those consequences as framework-internal. They cannot then be counted again as independent evidence for the framework.

## 6. Currency of the references

The bibliography contains Nagel 1974, Jackson 1982, Levine 1983 and Chalmers 1996, plus an internal 2026 page. These are appropriate canonical starting points, and none should be removed merely because of age. But a page presenting itself as the epistemological foundation of a current research framework cannot stop there. The bibliography is stale as *coverage*, not because the classics have ceased to matter. [concepts/epistemology](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/epistemology/)

A minimally current treatment would engage:

- **Introspection:** Spener’s 2024 distinctions among introspective inquiry, access, privacy, interference and reliability. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/55721)
- **Acquaintance:** Kammerer’s 2021–23 critiques and Pallagrosi’s 2025–26 normativity and trilemma work. [Springer+2Springer+2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10670-026-01081-4)

### Improvements to the site’s methodology

1. **End bedrock immunity.** Load-bearing propositions should receive periodic adversarial re-audit precisely because many downstream pages depend on them.
2. **Use a four-level citation check:** bibliographic existence; quotation accuracy; attribution in context; and whether the source supports the inference for which it is cited.
3. **Build bidirectional dependency tracking.** When a child article weakens a claim, every parent and synthesis page using that claim should be automatically flagged.
4. **Add semantic tests, not merely link checks.** A valid link does not establish that the parent accurately inherits the child’s confidence level or conclusion.
5. **Require opponent-endorsable summaries.** A rival-position description should pass a test of whether a leading proponent could recognize it before criticism begins.
6. **Maintain a claim ledger** distinguishing observation, report, interpretation, framework commitment, auxiliary assumption, prediction and falsifier.
7. **Introduce a recency floor for live debates.** A foundational hub should cite at least two substantial sources from the previous five years for each actively developing dispute, alongside the classics.
8. **Apply epistemic humility symmetrically.** Uncertainty about physical explanation and uncertainty about psychophysical interaction should lower confidence by comparable rules.
9. **Treat intra-site agreement as correlated evidence.** Repetition across sibling pages must not count as independent confirmation, particularly when all inherit the same source summary.
10. **Sample primary sources during every deep review.** The Levine propagation incident demonstrates that internal consistency checks alone can stabilize an error.
11. **Preregister falsification criteria.** Specify in advance what probability shift, explanatory achievement or empirical result would reduce confidence in each tenet and by how much.
12. **Ban self-authenticating residual categories.** Terms such as “qualitative residue” should not function as permanent escape clauses unless independently measurable or tied to a publicly evaluable argument.
13. **Do not lower review priority merely because a page has been reviewed repeatedly.** Repeated reviews conducted under the same protected assumptions are correlated checks, not accumulating independent assurance.
14. **Separate conditional and evidential pages.** Pages describing what follows *if dualism is true* should be visibly distinguished from pages claiming to supply evidence that dualism is true.
15. **Propagate uncertainty numerically or categorically.** When a supporting article changes from “independent evidence” to “partially dependent evidence,” all cumulative confidence claims should be recalculated or explicitly downgraded.
