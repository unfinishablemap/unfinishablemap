## Executive Summary

The page is carefully sourced and honest about its tenet relations. But two of its four faces are contradicted by the very sources it cites.

**Face 1.** The page argues "in principle, on one model" that an exploration signal cannot name its target (L48, L50). The cited model computes the value of exploration *per option*: VOI(s, a_k). Its worked example is exploration of a known, already-sampled arm.

**Face 2.** The page's "unexplored margin" is whether the two MAC causes can be told apart from inside (L58, L89, L94). The cited MAC paper's own Study 2 manipulated the two deficits separately. Each manipulation moved a different set of the subjects' self-reported boredom dimensions.

**Face 4.** It is stated as an empirical tendency ("tends to end it", L40, L70). It is derived only from a definition, and L90 concedes the empirical question is untested.

What survives the repairs is narrower but still a void. On the model, boredom is felt as a cost on the current task, while the computation behind it concerns particular alternatives. That is an occlusion. In its current form the page puts face 1's objectlessness in the signal's content (L50), when its own source puts it in what the feeling displays.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Patricia Churchland)

"Boredom" is a folk term that the page's own sources are already taking apart. The MAC model splits it into an attentional mismatch and a meaning mismatch. Westgate and Wilson's Study 2 then shows the two produce different measured profiles. In their words, "the attention manipulation (but not the meaning manipulation) significantly predicted the Inattention subscale … In contrast, the meaning manipulation (but not the attention manipulation) significantly predicted the Disengagement, Dysphoric Affect, Agitated Affect, and Time Perception subscales." Agrawal et al. write the signal down as an equation. Churchland would say the "void" of not knowing which cause is operating (L58) is just what happens when a folk word covers two mechanisms. It is a lexical blur, not a cognitive dark space. The page's best reply is its own L94 concession that the void is framework-independent. The page should then say plainly that face 2's margin is about whether a person can *attribute* their boredom to the right cause, not whether the causes feel different. On its own cited evidence, they do feel different.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Daniel Dennett)

L84 contrasts the artificial explore signal, whose specification is "open to the designer", with the human one, "felt and its specification hidden". Dennett would call that the user illusion working as designed. Every interface hides its specification from the user and not from the engineer, and the engineer here is neuroscience. He would press three points.

- **Face 2's starting claim is grammar.** "Boredom arrives as a predicate of its object" (L52) is a report shaped by the word *boring*. English speakers say "I'm bored" at least as readily as "this is boring". Eastwood's clause (b), quoted at L72, has the bored person "focused on the fact that we are not able to engage attention", which is a self-directed state. The page's own Pascal sentence (L56) is weariness "without any cause", and the page's own profound boredom "has no object at all" (L80). So the universal claim at L52 is contradicted three times on the page.
- **Face 4 is a definitional artefact.** "By the attentional definition the bout is then over, and what she studies is its memory, or a new curiosity about it" (L72). Whether the bout "ended" or was "redescribed" has no answer apart from the definition chosen. The page half-concedes this: "The inference is definition-relative" (L74).
- **The face 1 fix makes the void more Dennettian, not less.** Once the model is read correctly, an option-specific computation sits under a felt cost that does not display the option. That is a gap between the system's representation and the user's display, which is exactly his picture.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Max Tegmark)

The page rightly says Minimal Quantum Interaction has no bearing (L100). Two sentences earlier, though, L98 "reads boredom as the felt cost where attention is selected" on the [attention-as-interface](/concepts/attention-as-interface/) hypothesis. Tegmark would object to the timescale. Boredom accumulates over minutes: Witowska's 7.5-minute wait, Chin's 30-minute sampling windows, and Agrawal's value of information, which grows "over prolonged experience with the stationary one". A slow, integrative cost signal is the least likely candidate for a selection event at a microscopic, decoherence-limited site. He would ask the page either to say what that reading predicts, or to mark it as an interpretive gloss that adds nothing testable. This is a low-severity point, because the page does not enlist boredom for the interface. But the clause sits uneasily beside L100's "no bearing".

### The Many-Worlds Defender (David Deutsch)

Deutsch would accept the page's verdict that No Many Worlds has "no bearing" (L100). Explore–exploit reasoning is decision theory under uncertainty, and the Deutsch–Wallace programme reproduces it inside a branching universe without change. His one objection would be to the L98 phrase "where attention is selected". If "selected" means selection among outcomes, the phrase quietly assumes a single outcome, which the page claims to be setting aside. This is low severity. A neutral verb would remove it.

### The Empiricist (Karl Popper's Ghost)

- **Face 1 is refuted by its own model.** The "in-principle on one model" claim (L50) can be tested against the model, and it fails. The page says "Exploration value is the value of options not yet sampled. A signal of it cannot name its target, because a named target would be a known option, and choosing a known option is exploitation" (L48). Agrawal et al.'s formal definition is "V OI(s, ak ) = Gain(s, ak ) × N eed(s, ak )" (Eq. 9), which is indexed to a particular action. Their worked case is a two-armed bandit in which the agent keeps the stationary arm while "the uncertainty about the first arm's value increases — as does the VOI for resolving this uncertainty — and choosing it once can give valuable information about which arm to choose in subsequent trials" (Figure 5 discussion). The option explored there is known, named and already sampled. Their abstract speaks of "the competing value of particular options".
- **The Fox reconciliation fails under the page's own definition.** L64 says curiosity about the shock "is what an exploration signal would produce". But the reply the page cites says "all participants had already experienced the shock" (Wilson et al. 2014b, PMC4260463). Under L48's definition, an option already sampled and named is exploitation, so the reconciliation fails. Under the model's actual definition it works, because a known option with residual uncertainty has positive VOI. Correcting L48 therefore also rescues L64, but it costs face 1 its in-principle form.
- **Face 4 cannot fail.** It follows from the definition (L72), so the heading's "Tends to" (L70) claims an empirical tendency nothing has tested. The page's own Approaches list says so (L90).
- **The self-sealing forecast has not been tested.** The page proposes a loop for the forecast (L66, L102) and does not test it, although the mechanism invites a test. Everyday life forces waiting on people constantly, in queues, commutes and waiting rooms. That is exactly the corrective experience the loop says avoidance removes. If forecasts stay low despite involuntary waiting, the persistence is a memory or forecasting bias, not a self-sealing loop. *Unverified suspicion*: Hatano et al.'s six experiments collected forecasts and then experiences, so their data may already show whether experience updated later forecasts. Only the abstract was read.

Credit where due. The Approaches section (L86–L90) is the kind of operational content Popper wants. One of its three proposals, however, has already been run (Issue 2).

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

Vronsky's "desire for desires" (L46) is not an oddity of one signal's design. It is craving at its plainest: the thirst for becoming, which supplies stand-in objects because no object was ever the point. The page treats objectlessness as a fact about an exploration signal. The Madhyamaka reading is that boredom exposes what every want has always been, and the dualist's "felt verdict" (L42) is one more construction mistaken for a thing. On face 4, noting practice takes boredom as its object over long sessions without becoming absorbed in it and without ending it. That is a sustained first-person reading of a bout that the attentional definition does not exclude (Issue 3). The page's "untrained default" fence (L68) is right. But it rests the whole contemplative question on one correlational datum (L90), when contemplative traditions have reported on exactly this for centuries. Finally: who is bored? The phrase "the mind acts on an underestimate of its own capacity" (L102) reifies an owner of the verdict that the page never examines.

On the model, then, the computation is about particular alternatives, and the felt state is a cost on the current task. L50 asserts the opposite, *metaphysical* reading (the target is absent from the content). The evidence supports one reading and the conclusion asserts the other, which is the skill's interpretive-equivocation case. The corrected reading suits the void better: something is computed and not displayed. The L64 dilemma (above, under Popper) falls away once L48 matches the model.

## Counterarguments to Address

### Objectlessness
- **Current content says**: An exploration signal "cannot name its target" in principle, on one model (L48–L50).
- **A critic would argue**: The model names targets: VOI is computed per option. Westgate and Wilson also say the MAC model explains "when boredom motivates people to seek interesting versus enjoyable activities" (abstract), so boredom carries at least a rough direction.
- **Suggested response**: Locate the void in what is displayed. The computation is option-specific; the feeling is a cost on the current task, with at most a rough direction. What is hidden is which alternative carries the value.

### Inside legibility of the two causes
- **Current content says**: Whether the causes can be read from inside is open, and no study has tested it (L58).
- **A critic would argue**: Study 2 of the cited paper tested it, and the self-reported profiles separated the two causes.
- **Suggested response**: Concede the profiles. Move the open question to attribution: can a person say *why* they are bored?

### The self-sealing forecast
- **Current content says**: Avoidance removes the corrective experience, so the forecast "seals itself" (L66, L102).
- **A critic would argue**: Involuntary waiting supplies corrective experience every day. If forecasts persist anyway, the cause is biased memory or forecasting, not avoidance.
- **Suggested response**: Keep the loop as a labelled Map inference. Add that involuntary waiting is the natural test, and that persistence despite it would point to a forecasting bias instead.

### Projection as grammar
- **Current content says**: "Boredom arrives as a predicate of its object" (L52).
- **A critic would argue**: "I'm bored" is as common as "this is boring". Eastwood's clause (b) is self-focused, and the page's own Pascal and Heidegger cases have no object.
- **Suggested response**: Restrict face 2 to situational boredom, and rest the occlusion claim on L58's fitting-versus-projected access claim.
