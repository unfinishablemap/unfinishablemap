## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

The phenomenal layer rests on one premise, stated at L47: "a judgment is an instance of the very state the proposition denies". That is a folk-psychological stipulation, not a structural fact. Cognitive neuroscience routinely finds judgment-like discriminations, error signals and decisions formed outside report. The page says the failure is "structural to judgment as such", which treats a contested premise as definitional.

Strip the premise and what is left is the doxastic layer, and that layer is neutral, as L97 itself grants. Churchland would add that "I am not now conscious" being "fully graspable from outside" (L91) is just a sentence that a self-monitoring system flags as inconsistent with its own activity. No metaphysics follows.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

Dennett's target is L97. Every reporting device has the asymmetry the Map calls distinctive. A thermostat cannot validly report "I am off", because reporting is something only an operating thermostat does, and every report it issues verifies "I am on". The page's line that the thermostat's inability is "a limit of its self-model, not a property of the fact" gets this backwards. The inability is a property of what reporting is, exactly as in the phenomenal case.

Dennett would also point to Dretske's own footnote on the same issue. Beliefs in "a physical condition necessary for life--and, therefore, necessary for belief" are beliefs that "can't be false". Dretske adds that "The belief that one has conscious experiences, unlike the belief that one has beliefs, is not self-verifying." So the page's own source denies the self-verification the positive mirror needs.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

The page makes no quantum claim and says so (L101). Tegmark's point is about distinctiveness. Positional closure of the Moore and Fitch kind is reproducible in classical devices: a machine asked to output a correct prediction of an output it is built to negate cannot do so. Nothing here needs, or bears on, a non-physical interface. That supports the page's own caution at L97 and weakens its L95 framing of the void as an "unusually clean" Occam example.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

L99 says that under branching, "the question of whether a branch-relative self is the same bearer of a blindspot becomes open". The page's own L81 already makes blindspot-bearers time-indexed: a later self knows what the earlier self could not. Branch-indexing adds nothing the temporal case lacks, and self-locating uncertainty in many-worlds is handled with the same indexicals. The page concedes "This is a coherence point, not an argument against branching", which is honest. Deutsch would say even the coherence point is idle.

### The Empiricist (Popper)

The doxastic and global layers are theorems, so they are not empirical claims and Popper has nothing to falsify there. The phenomenal layer is where he presses. L75 says a trained denial would be "undetectable from the reports themselves". The page rightly scopes this to reports, and the substantive verdict is sent to other pages. But the Dualism reading (L97) names no observation that would count against it. The page calls the reading "modest and conditional", which is the right grade. Issue 2 shows that the condition, self-verification, is not even met as stated.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

The positive mirror leans on Advaita self-luminosity (Ramm). Madhyamaka is the tradition that most famously denied reflexive awareness: the lamp that does not illuminate itself, and the sword that cannot cut itself. (This is a persona lead, not verified at source this run.) A Mādhyamika would also say that the "I" which each blindspot indexes is a conventional designation, so the indexical shape the page calls evidence about "what a first-person perspective is" (L37) may show only how the designation works.

The page applies this doubt to AI at L75: "Whether an AI has a stable 'now' and unified 'I' for a blindspot to index at all is left open." It does not apply the same doubt to humans.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The negative phenomenal blindspot does not survive illusionism, and the page's own antecedent shows it

- **File**: `obsidian/voids/blindspot-void.md`, L55. Upstream loci are L39 and L47. The frontmatter description is a sibling locus.
- **Location**: L55 reads "The blindspot itself survives illusionism—a system that judges "I am not conscious" still cannot be validly right in whatever sense of "conscious" the judgment invokes—but…". `The blindspot itself survives illusionism` occurs ×1, and `in whatever sense of "conscious" the judgment invokes` occurs ×1.
- **Problem**: The same paragraph opens with "If phenomenal consciousness is an introspective misrepresentation, then 'I am conscious,' read as a claim about phenomenal properties, is a false judgment". On that antecedent, "I am not (phenomenally) conscious" is true of everyone. The illusionist judges it of herself, and by her lights she is right. The Map's own `concepts/illusionism` L139 says illusionism "denies phenomenal consciousness exists".
  - The blindspot requires that the judging be an instance of the property denied. That holds only on a functional reading of "conscious", on which judging is itself a case of being conscious. So "in whatever sense" is false for the one sense the page cares about.
  - Two consequences follow. First, L39's "Its positive mirror ... is contested by [illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/)" understates the contest: the negative half is contested too. Second, L47's "the failure is structural to judgment as such" turns that premise into a definition.
  - The description also says the phenomenal case is "a proved, indexical limit" that "reveals what a perspective is". L39 says the phenomenal layer rests on a preprint and on the Map's own extension, and L97 calls the gain "modest and conditional".
- **Severity**: High. It misstates which part of the void is secure.
- **Recommendation**: Restrict the survival claim to the functional sense. Name the negative half as needing the phenomenal reading. Make L47 conditional. Recalibrate the description. Exact text is in Priority 1.

### Issue 2: The Dualism disanalogy (L97) is refuted by its own example and by its own Dretske source, and it misdescribes Ramm

- **File**: L97. The affected Ramm sentence is at L53.
- **Location**:
  - `No physical fact about a system is verified by a report that is an instance of it` ×1.
  - `which is the self-luminosity thesis Ramm defends` ×1.
- **Problem**: There are three faults.
  - **(a) The thermostat.** Every report a thermostat issues is issued by an operating thermostat, so "I am on" is verified by any report and "I am off" is self-defeating. The structure is exactly the one L51 gives the phenomenal case. The universal negative is false, and the example the page chose is a counterexample to it.
  - **(b) Dretske's footnote 15.** The source the page cites says this in so many words. Beliefs in "a physical condition necessary for life--and, therefore, necessary for belief" are beliefs that "can't be false", and the next words are "but they aren't, not for that reason, knowledge". The same footnote says "The belief that one has conscious experiences, unlike the belief that one has beliefs, is not self-verifying". (Both strings occur ×1 in the consc.net draft. The footnote number is the draft's.)
  - **(c) Ramm.** "The judgment is itself an instance of the property" is not Ramm's thesis. His abstract defines self-luminosity as "(1) I am implicitly aware of my awareness and (2) I am phenomenally aware of a distinct phenomenal character of my awareness". He argues that "the mere fact that I am perceptually conscious is not sufficient to allow me to know that I am perceptually conscious". The page quotes that sentence at L53, so L53 and L97 contradict each other. Mere instantiation is exactly what Ramm says does not suffice.
- **Severity**: High. This is the page's only Tenet 1 argument. The optimistic review of 2026-09-29 ratified it.
- **Recommendation**: Concede that self-verification is common to reporting devices, and cite Dretske's footnote for it. Rest the Map's reading on Ramm's actual claim (2), the distinct phenomenal character of awareness, which is what the thermostat lacks. The paragraph's closing grade, "modest and conditional", then becomes accurate. The fix saves 8 words.

### Issue 4: The global layer is not positional, but the page says every blindspot is

- **Locations**:

  - L37: `and open to anyone differently placed` ×1, and "its shape is *indexical*: the very same proposition is accessible from the third person and closed from the first".

- **Problem**: L45 says the Fitch conjunction "*p* and nobody knows *p*" "cannot be known by anyone at any time" (`cannot be known by anyone at any time` ×1). L95 says Fitch refutes the claim that "every truth is available to some suitably placed knower". So the Fitch conjunctions are closed to *every* perspective. ## Counterarguments to Address

### "Judgment need not be conscious"

- **Current content says**: the phenomenal failure "is structural to judgment as such" (L47).
- **A critic would argue**: higher-order and global-workspace traditions allow thoughts and judgments to occur without being conscious. On such views, "I am not now conscious" can be judged, wrongly or rightly, by a system that is not conscious. Kim's asymmetry builds the opposite assumption into "valid judgment". (This is a persona lead; no source was fetched this run.)
- **Suggested response**: make the premise explicit and conditional (Priority 1, L47). The phenomenal layer is then exactly as strong as the claim that judging is a conscious act, and the page should say that premise is contested.

### "Self-verification is cheap"

- **Current content says**: "I am conscious" is special because "the judgment is itself an instance of the property".
- **A critic would argue**: every reporter verifies its own operation. So instantiation-verification cannot single out consciousness, and Dretske says so in a footnote of the paper the page cites.
- **Suggested response**: Priority 2. Ramm's distinct-phenomenal-character claim is the only premise that separates the cases, and the page should rest on it openly.

### "Indexicality is not perspectivalism about being"

- **Current content says**: "The Map reads that asymmetry as evidence about what a first-person perspective is" (L37).
- **A critic would argue**: Moore-type asymmetries arise for any agent whose attitudes are part of the world they describe, including software agents. They show that the agent is *in* the world, not that the agent is non-physical.
- **Suggested response**: the page already grades the Dualism gain "modest and conditional". After Priority 2 the grade will match the argument.
