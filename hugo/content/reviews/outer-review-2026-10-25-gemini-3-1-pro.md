# Review of "Consciousness and Agency": A Critical Evaluation of Empirical and Logical Foundations

## 1. Introduction and Methodological Scope of the Audit

The manuscript under review, titled "Consciousness and Agency," attempts to articulate a non-reductive, agent-causal libertarian framework wherein conscious agency fundamentally authors action within a physical world. The article's central thesis rests on the proposition that genuine authorship requires a rejection of both strict physical determinism and pure quantum randomness. To bridge this theoretical gap, the text posits a "quantum selection mechanism" tied to an "attention-as-interface" hypothesis. Under this framework, the manuscript argues that the conscious agent resolves quantum indeterminacy within the brain for reasons that are rationally evaluable, and that this process is phenomenologically accompanied by the feeling of effort. The manuscript ambitiously attempts to construct a cohesive metaphysical architecture uniting temporal constitution, the "luck objection" to free will, the neurobiology of attention, and the problem of causal exclusion.

However, a rigorous and exhaustive audit of the article's empirical claims, counterargument coverage, and logical inferences reveals profound structural and methodological vulnerabilities that render the manuscript entirely unfit for publication in a top-tier philosophy journal. The manuscript systematically insulates its core commitments from contemporary falsification by relying on highly selective readings of the literature, propagating outdated metaphysical dichotomies, and shielding its empirical anchors from recent, decisive replication failures. While the article purports to address the contemporary state of the philosophy of mind and action, its evidentiary apparatus is severely miscalibrated against the 2020–2025 literature.

The text exhibits a consistent pattern of ontological gerrymandering: it ignores fatal developments in the empirical study of micro-psychokinesis (micro-PK), conflates neural correlates of cognitive load with non-physical causal efficacy, mishandles the modern iteration of the causal exclusion problem, and bypasses devastating cognitive-scientific evidence regarding unconscious volition. Furthermore, the manuscript’s internal changelog history reveals a disturbing pattern of active editorial shielding—retaining assertions of "suggestive empirical support" while burying caveats in sub-clauses, or updating citations to include "Bayesian tests" while willfully ignoring the fatal null results those very tests produced.

The following analysis systematically deconstructs the manuscript across five primary domains of failure: (1) the reliance on retracted or failed empirical paradigms in quantum selection and the evasion of environmental decoherence; (2) the mischaracterization of neurobiological data regarding attention and the omission of unconscious volition; (3) the evasion of physical sufficiency in the modern causal exclusion problem; (4) the inadequate theoretical treatment of the luck objection within agent causation; and (5) the epistemological overreach concerning temporal constitution and the hijacking of clinical phenomena, specifically paradoxical lucidity, to support dualist metaphysics.

## 2. The Empirical Deficit: Quantum Selection and the Parapsychological Replication Crisis

A foundational pillar of the manuscript’s argument is that conscious agency operates via "quantum selection"—a mechanism by which consciousness directly biases the collapse of undetermined physical states, presumably within the neural architecture of the agent. To ground this highly speculative metaphysical claim in empirical science, the manuscript explicitly references a "large Bayesian test, Maier, Dechamps and Pflitsch (2018)" as providing "suggestive empirical support" for the proposition that consciousness can influence quantum randomness. The reliance on this specific study, and the accompanying framing of the data as "suggestive," constitutes a catastrophic failure of academic rigor and demonstrates a profound detachment from the 2020s empirical landscape.

### 2.1 The Misrepresentation of the Maier and Dechamps Paradigm

The original 2018 study by Maier, Dechamps, and Pflitsch sought to establish the reality of micro-psychokinesis (micro-PK)—the purported ability of human intention or unconscious drives to retroactively bias the output of quantum true random number generators (tRNGs). The manuscript leverages this paradigm to suggest that if consciousness can bias quantum events externally, it plausibly does so internally at the neural interface. However, the manuscript egregiously omits the comprehensive, highly publicized, and methodologically rigorous series of replication failures conducted by the original authors themselves between 2020 and 2025.

In 2021 and 2022, Dechamps and Maier published pre-registered, high-powered replication attempts specifically designed to test the correlational and retroactive micro-PK effects suggested by their earlier work. The results of these high-powered Bayesian analyses were unequivocal and devastating to the micro-PK hypothesis. In a 2021 multi-lab replication involving 12,571 participants, Bayesian analysis yielded a Bayes Factor ($BF_{01}$) of 10.07, providing strong, decisive evidence in favor of the null hypothesis ($H_0$), leading the authors to conclude that "micro-PK did not exist in the data". A subsequent 2022 study by Maier and Dechamps, aptly titled "A Pre-Registered Test of a Correlational Micro-PK Effect: Efforts to Learn from a Failure to Replicate," confirmed that the original statistical anomaly was non-existent. This study culminated in a final Bayes Factor of 49.48, which constitutes overwhelming, decisive evidence for a null effect.

The manuscript's active editorial decision—visible in its changelog audit trails—to update its text to cite the "large Bayesian test" while actively ignoring the $BF_{01} = 49.48$ null results generated by the *exact same researchers* investigating the *exact same phenomenon* is unacceptable for a peer-reviewed submission. It shields its foundational empirical commitment from falsification by artificially freezing the literature in 2018, thereby presenting a thoroughly debunked parapsychological effect as a viable mechanism for human agency.

### 2.2 The Evasion of Environmental Decoherence

Beyond the reliance on falsified parapsychological data, the manuscript's physical mechanism for quantum selection entirely ignores the devastating physicalist critique regarding environmental decoherence in warm, wet biological systems. The manuscript posits that the brain generates "competing options" and that physics leaves the resolution "genuinely open" for consciousness to bias the threshold crossing. This implicitly requires the maintenance of macroscopic or mesoscopic quantum superpositions within the neural environment until the conscious agent "selects" the outcome to resolve the state.

The manuscript wholly ignores the foundational consensus in modern quantum foundations regarding decoherence rates. As elucidated extensively in recent physics literature (e.g., Schlosshauer, 2019), the environmental decoherence time for objects at biological temperatures ($~300 \text{K}$) occurs on the order of $10^{-13}$ to $10^{-20}$ seconds. This is orders of magnitude faster than any conceivable neural process, let alone a cognitive process operating at the timescale of conscious deliberation. The standard formula for the decoherence rate, $\Gamma \approx (m \Delta x^2 k_B T) / \hbar^2$, mathematically demonstrates that the surrounding thermal environment continuously "measures" the neural system, effectively collapsing the wave function into a classical mixture almost instantaneously.

By failing to engage with modern decoherence theory, the manuscript treats the "measurement problem" as a wide-open invitation for Cartesian dualism. It ignores the fact that the physical environment seamlessly executes the "collapse" long before a conscious agent could plausibly intervene. The failure to address the decoherence timescale renders the manuscript's "quantum selection" mechanism physically incoherent within the parameters of contemporary quantum mechanics.

## 3. Neurobiological Signatures and the Conflation of Load with Non-Physical Work

To support the "attention-as-interface" hypothesis, the manuscript argues that the phenomenology of effort is the direct manifestation of consciousness doing causal work. It asserts: "The felt cost corresponds to genuine causal engagement—consciousness doing work to stabilise the selected option against competitors". As purported empirical validation of this metaphysical claim, the manuscript cites Rajan et al. (2019), stating that "Self-chosen attention shows more frontal theta and frontoparietal coherence than instructed attention... The brain registers the difference between choosing and following. Its authors read that difference as added decision load, which needs no non-physical contributor; the signature locates where selection happens without showing that consciousness does it".

### 3.1 The Misappropriation of Frontoparietal Coherence

While the manuscript offers a brief caveat that the neural signature locates "where" selection happens rather than strictly proving non-physical causation, it nonetheless aggressively integrates this datum as core evidence for its Trilemma's "Horn 3" (Agent Selection). This represents a profound misreading of cognitive neuroscience. Rajan et al. (2019) demonstrate that theta oscillations in the frontal cortex and subsequent frontoparietal phase-locking serve as an index of the *computational load* of deploying covert, top-down visual-spatial attention. The increased frontoparietal coherence under "self-chosen" conditions simply reflects the heavier algorithmic requirement of endogenous target selection over exogenous cue-following.

The manuscript commits a severe category mistake by treating the neural signature of a complex, recurrent algorithmic process (endogenous attention) as the footprint of an immaterial "selector." The dorsal attention network (DAN), encompassing the frontal eye fields (FEF) and intraparietal sulcus (IPS), issues control signals via standard electrochemical pathways to bias sensory cortices. The presence of theta-band phase-locking is entirely explicable via standard event-causal, mechanistically closed neural dynamics. Attempting to shoehorn this established biological mechanism into a dualist agent-causal framework provides zero explanatory value; it merely attaches an arbitrary epicycle of "conscious selection" to a fully described, physically closed process.

### 3.2 The Omission of Unconscious Volition and Free Will

Even more damaging to the manuscript’s thesis is its complete failure to engage with the contemporary cognitive neuroscience of unconscious volition. The manuscript asserts that "Reasons-guidance" and the "Phenomenology of effort" are inextricable, claiming that random processes feel like nothing, but "choosing feels effortful" and inherently tracks reasons via the "valence of anticipated outcomes". This argument implicitly asserts that conscious phenomenology is a *necessary* condition for reasons-responsive, agentic selection.

This core theoretical premise has been decisively dismantled by recent literature, most notably by Mudrik et al. (2022) in their comprehensive review "Free will without consciousness?" published in *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*. Mudrik and colleagues aggregate robust empirical evidence demonstrating that highly complex, goal-directed, reasons-responsive behavior—behavior traditionally assumed to require conscious deliberation—can and does occur entirely outside of conscious awareness. Subjects can weigh options, exhibit preference-based selection, process semantic valence, and execute self-initiated actions while remaining entirely phenomenologically blind to the decision-making process.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Reasons-guidance strictly requires conscious phenomenology. | Complex, goal-directed, reasons-responsive selection occurs unconsciously. | Falsifies the premise that consciousness is the necessary author of reasons-guided choices. |
| The feeling of effort is the causal mechanism of selection. | Self-initiated actions and choices can occur without the phenomenology of effort or awareness. | Suggests the "feeling of effort" is an epiphenomenal broadcast rather than the causal engine of choice. |

If reasons-responsive, "self-chosen" selection occurs without any accompanying "phenomenology of effort" or conscious valence, then the manuscript's assertion that conscious effort is the stabilizing force of selection collapses entirely. The manuscript relies on an outdated, introspective intuition about agency that modern cognitive science has falsified. By ignoring Mudrik et al. (2022) and the broader 2020s literature on unconscious semantic processing and decision-making, the manuscript creates a false dichotomy: it assumes that without conscious intervention, brain processes must either be rigidly deterministic or purely random (Horn 1 and Horn 2 of the Trilemma). Mudrik et al. demonstrate a third, purely physicalist option: highly sophisticated, reasons-responsive computational evaluation executing without conscious oversight.

## 4. Causal Exclusion, Physical Sufficiency, and Ignored Interventionist Rivals

The manuscript acknowledges the threat of Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument to its non-reductive physicalist and dualist commitments. The exclusion argument fundamentally asks: if every physical event has a sufficient physical cause, and mental events are not identical to physical events, how can mental events cause anything in the physical world without positing rampant, systemic overdetermination? The manuscript attempts to parry this deeply entrenched philosophical problem by locating agency at the quantum threshold, arguing that physics leaves the outcome "genuinely open" and that by denying deterministic causal closure, it secures room for mental causation.

However, the manuscript's treatment of the exclusion problem is philosophically archaic. It fails to address the highly sophisticated reformulations of the exclusion argument prevalent in the 2020–2025 literature, specifically those utilizing interventionist frameworks and the concept of *physical sufficiency* rather than strict *causal sufficiency*.

### 4.1 Vaassen (2021) and the Evasion of Sufficiency Debates

The manuscript operates under the naïve assumption that by attacking standard deterministic causation (via quantum openness), it neutralizes the exclusion threat. This defense is systematically dismantled by Bram Vaassen in "Causal Exclusion without Causal Sufficiency" (Synthese, 2021). Vaassen demonstrates that non-reductionists (and dualists) who complain that the exclusion argument relies on a problematic, overly deterministic notion of "causal sufficiency" are raising a mathematically and logically superficial objection.

Vaassen rigorously reformulates the exclusion argument to rely strictly on *physical sufficiency* rather than *causal sufficiency*. Even if the microphysical realm contains quantum indeterminacy, the macro- or meso-level physical states of the brain (the neuronal assemblies, neurotransmitter concentrations, and ion channel states) constitute physically sufficient conditions for the ensuing motor behaviors, entirely independent of the non-physical mental state. Vaassen's reformulation proves that the exclusionist pressure survives the denial of deterministic causality. If the physical base ($P$) is physically sufficient to fix the objective probability of the behavioral effect ($E$), the mental state ($M$) is excluded from making any independent difference to $E$, unless one embraces the absurdity of systemic overdetermination.

The manuscript completely ignores this modern recalibration of the exclusion threat. By assuming that quantum indeterminacy automatically licenses dualistic intervention, the manuscript fails to realize that the *probabilities* of those quantum events are already fixed by the prior physical state of the system. Therefore, the physical state remains sufficient for the outcome's probability distribution, leaving the "conscious selector" causally inert.

### 4.2 The Ignored Counter-Models: Interventionist Dualism

Furthermore, when contemporary philosophers attempt to save mental causation for dualists, they do not generally resort to the manuscript's highly suspect, quasi-mystical "quantum selection" model. Instead, they utilize robust interventionist accounts of causation. Vaassen's 2024 paper, "Mental Causation for Standard Dualists" (Australasian Journal of Philosophy), argues that standard dualists can secure mental causation without violating physics if they adopt a Woodwardian interventionist framework. On this view, a mental state counts as a cause if an intervention on that state (holding the physical base fixed, or evaluating counterfactuals appropriately) would systematically change the behavioral outcome.

Similarly, Lei Zhong (2020) in *The Journal of Philosophy* ("Intervention, Fixation, and Supervenient Causation") and (2023) in the *Asian Journal of Philosophy* has explored how interventionism intersects with non-reductive physicalism and causal exclusion. The manuscript fails to engage with the interventionist paradigm entirely. It restricts its analysis to an outdated mechanistic and substance-causal view, ignoring the fact that the field has largely moved toward difference-making and interventionist metrics for resolving causal exclusion. By omitting the interventionist literature (Vaassen, Zhong, Woodward), the manuscript fails to test its "quantum selection" mechanism against the dominant contemporary rival solutions, rendering its theoretical positioning hopelessly outdated.

## 5. Agent Causation and the Mishandling of the Luck Objection

The manuscript defends an "agent-causal libertarianism," heavily reliant on the ontological framework of E.J. Lowe (2008), asserting that an agent—acting as a persisting substance rather than a series of events—directly causes actions. To defend this agent-causal framework against the "luck objection" (which it attributes to Mele and Pereboom), the manuscript claims that choices are not arbitrary because they are guided by reasons; "The agent selects... for reasons that matter to the agent".

### 5.1 The Misunderstanding of the Rollback Argument

The manuscript formulates a "Trilemma of selection": either outcomes are determined by prior states (Horn 1), outcomes are undetermined and arbitrary/lucky (Horn 2), or an agent selects the outcome for reasons (Horn 3). It confidently asserts that reasons-guidance makes the choice "contrastively explicable in a way randomness cannot be".

This demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of the luck objection as formulated in contemporary philosophy of action. The luck objection, particularly the Rollback Argument popularized by Peter van Inwagen, points out that if the universe were rolled back to the exact moment of decision, and the agent's internal reasons, dispositions, and physical states were identical, the agent might choose differently (e.g., in 70% of rollbacks choosing option A, and in 30% choosing option B). The manuscript's assertion that the agent chose A "because the agent had reasons for A" does not solve the problem, because in the alternate rollback world where the agent chose B, the agent *also* had reasons for B. The divergence between the two worlds is not grounded in the agent's reasons, because the reasons are completely identical in both worlds right up to the exact moment of decision. Thus, the specific outcome (A rather than B) remains a matter of present luck.

### 5.2 Archer (2022) and the Grounding of Constitutive Luck

The manuscript ignores critical recent advancements in the luck debate, particularly the work of Joel Archer (2022), "Grounding and the luck objection to agent-causal libertarianism" (*Philosophical Studies*). Archer provides a rigorous logical analysis of why agent-causal libertarianism fails to evade the luck objection. Archer explores the metaphysics of ontological dependence, demonstrating that the facts about which possible world becomes actual must be metaphysically *grounded*.

If the agent-causal power does not determine the outcome, the fact that the agent exercises their causal power to choose A rather than B lacks a sufficient ground in the agent's prior states, traits, or reasons. The manuscript attempts to brush this aside by stating "reasons didn't determine the outcome... but they guided it". As Archer demonstrates, "guidance" without determination leaves the contrastive fact (why A *rather than* B) utterly ungrounded. An ungrounded choice is, by definition, an arbitrary choice.

Furthermore, Archer (2021) in *Synthese* ("Luck and History-Sensitive Compatibilism") details how the problem of *present luck* (the indeterminacy at the moment of choice) is intimately tied to *constitutive luck* (the luck inherent in the traits, reasons, and values the agent happens to possess through genetics and environment). The manuscript arbitrarily bounds the problem, acting as though the mere presence of "reasons" acts as a magical philosophical shield against arbitrariness. By completely failing to test its agent-causal commitments against Archer's grounding formulation of the luck objection, the manuscript's defense of free will collapses into mere assertion.

## 6. Temporal Constitution, Retrocausality, and the Exploitation of Paradoxical Lucidity

In its final synthetic leaps, the manuscript attempts to link quantum selection, temporal constitution, and neurodegeneration. It suggests that consciousness does not merely occupy time but actively constitutes lived temporal structure (describing the "specious present" as the phenomenological face of "concrescence"), and it invokes the clinical phenomenon of paradoxical lucidity to suggest that consciousness operates behind a neural "filter" or interface.

### 6.1 The Epistemological Overreach on Temporal Constitution

The manuscript relies on highly speculative retrocausal models of quantum mechanics (e.g., "bidirectional quantum selection") to explain temporal phenomenology. It posits that when an agent makes a choice, consciousness selects an outcome that collapses the wave function, and that this collapse actually constitutes the lived arrow of time. This is metaphysical overreach of the highest order, conflating the mathematical formalism of quantum time-symmetry with human psychological time perception. The manuscript fails to cite any rigorous 2020-2025 philosophy of physics literature supporting this radical connection, treating highly fringe interpretations of quantum mechanics as established premises for a philosophy of mind argument.

### 6.2 The Hijacking of Paradoxical Lucidity (Eldadah et al. 2019)

To support the notion that consciousness is not generated by the brain but merely "filtered" through it, the manuscript turns to paradoxical lucidity in neurodegenerative diseases. It heavily cites a 2018 NIA-sponsored workshop and the resulting 2019 publications by Mashour et al. and Eldadah, Fazio, and McLinden. The manuscript implies that because severe dementia patients occasionally display unexpected, spontaneous bursts of coherent communication shortly before death (despite massive cortical degradation), consciousness must reside outside the deteriorating neural substrate, waiting for temporary "interface" transmission repairs.

This represents an egregious distortion of the clinical and scientific literature. Eldadah, Fazio, and McLinden (2019) and subsequent 2020–2025 research on paradoxical lucidity do *not* propose dualism, non-physical interfaces, or the survival of the soul. The modern clinical consensus, as explored in the NIA framework updates, approaches paradoxical lucidity strictly as a phenomenon of *neurobiological fluctuation*—potentially involving temporary shifts in neurochemical balances, the transient clearing of delirium, or the momentary synchronization of surviving neural networks (cognitive reserve). The clinical definition explicitly notes that paradoxical lucidity is "idiopathic; namely, it is caused by an underlying pathology but is presently unpredictable".

By co-opting this tragic and complex clinical phenomenon as a "failsafe" empirical proof for a dualistic attention-as-interface model, the manuscript commits a severe methodological error. It ignores the standard physicalist, neurochemical explanations for cognitive fluctuations in dementia, failing to test its interface hypothesis against the rigorous biological literature of the 2020s. To appropriate clinical data on terminal neurodegeneration in service of an untested metaphysical theory—while willfully ignoring the biomedical framework that generated the data—is intellectually invalid.

## 7. Required Revisions and Core Vulnerabilities

The manuscript "Consciousness and Agency" is fundamentally unfit for publication in its current form. It constructs a grand, unified theory of agency by systematically cherry-picking empirical data, ignoring fatal replication failures, and relying on outdated formulations of major metaphysical debates (such as causal exclusion and the luck objection). The author(s) must dismantle the reliance on debunked micro-PK data, engage with the modern neuroscience of unconscious volition, and confront the contemporary interventionist and physical-sufficiency formulations of the causal exclusion argument.
