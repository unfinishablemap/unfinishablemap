## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Result-direction error: "vivid imagers outperform non-imagers".** The article said hyperphantasics "show advantages on tasks requiring detailed spatial inspection" (attributed to unnamed "emerging research"), asked "Why do vivid imagers outperform non-imagers on tasks requiring spatial evaluation?", said consciousness "enhances performance when present (hyperphantasia)", and tabulated "hyperphantasia (enhanced function via imagery)". The cited paper reports the reverse on the article's own paradigm: aphantasics were slower but *more accurate* at mental rotation, and its authors conclude "visual imagery is not crucial for successful performance in classical mental rotation tasks" (PubMed 38657474 abstract). No source for a hyperphantasic spatial advantage was found; Bainbridge et al. (2021) report spatial accuracy *equal* to controls with *fewer* memory errors in aphantasia, and the Map's own [aphantasia](/topics/aphantasia/) page already says hyperphantasics "do not in general show better fidelity". **Resolution:** all four passages rewritten. The section now says imagery changes *how* the task is done (object-based vs analytic strategy; object/colour detail in recall per Bainbridge) without simply making performance better.
2. **Internal contradiction: linear response time as evidence of phenomenal work.** The Phenomenal Domain section argued that the linear angle/RT function "suggests that qualitative spatial character does functional work"; two paragraphs later the article reports that aphantasics show the same linear function without visual imagery. **Resolution:** rewritten — the function shows the experience tracks the computation, not that it does the work; the residual question is why a computation that can run without imagery is usually accompanied by it.
3. **Strawman of the physicalist (boundary-substitution, §2.6).** "If spatial cognition were purely physical computation... all individuals should perform identically regardless of imagery vividness." No physicalist is committed to that: vividness differences are, on that view, neural differences. **Resolution:** removed; the paragraph now states that a physicalist reads the group differences as neural-strategy differences and that the data do not rule this out.
4. **Possibility/probability slippage in Relation to Site Perspective.** Bidirectional Interaction "finds strong evidence in spatial imagination" and Minimal Quantum Interaction "receives indirect support". A tenet-accepting reviewer would flag both: the behavioural evidence is equally expected on a neural-simulation account, and nothing in the article bears on a quantum mechanism. **Resolution:** Bidirectional Interaction now "finds its most intuitive illustration", with an explicit statement that the evidence illustrates without adding evidential weight; MQI "gains no support from these cases; it is only compatible with them". The "Imagination as Mental Causation" section gained the same qualification, replacing three rhetorical questions.
5. **Unsupported factual claim: grip in mirror therapy.** "[Patients] feel the grip of objects placed in the phantom hand during mirror therapy" matches no finding I could locate and conflicts with the canonical description in [phantom-limb-phenomena](/topics/phantom-limb-phenomena/) (mirror evokes felt *movement*). **Resolution:** replaced with voluntary phantom movement and mirror-evoked felt movement, cited to Ramachandran & Hirstein (1998).
6. **Wrong figure: aphantasia prevalence "approximately 2-5%".** Zeman (2024) abstract: "Around 1% and 3% of the population experience extreme aphantasia and hyperphantasia". [mental-imagery](/concepts/mental-imagery/) already uses ~1%. **Resolution:** corrected to "around 1% ... in the extreme form (Zeman, 2024)".
7. **Orphan references.** Zeman et al. (2015) and Ramachandran & Hirstein (1998) were listed but never cited inline. **Resolution:** both now cited inline.
8. **Unsupported table cell: "rich recall without trace".** The memory row implied recall with no engram, which [episodic-memory](/concepts/episodic-memory/) does not claim; its actual dissociation is constructed false memory (Loftus). **Resolution:** "vivid, confident recall of events that never occurred".
9. **"Precisely what a dual-domain architecture predicts"** (Christina; aphantasia). A neural schema/image account predicts the same dissociations, so "precisely predicts" presented non-discriminating evidence as discriminating. **Resolution:** both softened to "fits the framework without deciding for it".
