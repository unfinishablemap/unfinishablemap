## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Schopenhauer quotation is altered and its predicate reassigned

- **File**: `obsidian/topics/the-comic-and-humor-as-an-aesthetic-category.md`
- **Location**: line 44, "Incongruity theory" paragraph
- **Problem**: The article renders: *laughter is "the sudden perception of an incongruity between a concept and the real object"*. The canonical text (verified against SEP's "Philosophy of Humor", §on incongruity theory) reads: **"The cause of laughter in every case is simply the sudden perception of the incongruity between a concept and the real objects which have been thought through it in some relation, and laughter itself is just the expression of this incongruity."**

  Three separate defects compound here:
  1. **Wording**: `an incongruity` for `the incongruity`; `the real object` (singular) for `the real objects` (plural). The quoted span will not grep against the source.
  2. **Truncation without ellipsis**: the qualifying clause `which have been thought through it in some relation` is dropped silently. That clause is doing real work — it is what makes the incongruity a *concept-application* failure rather than a bare mismatch.
  3. **Predicate reassignment — the substantive error.** Schopenhauer says the sudden perception is the **cause** of laughter, and that laughter **is the expression** of the incongruity. The article says laughter **is** the sudden perception. This is the exact cause/identity conflation the article's own argument needs to avoid, since its thesis is that the *felt* response is a further fact beyond the structural perception. Attributing the identity claim to Schopenhauer imports the collapse the article spends two sections resisting.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Restore the full sentence verbatim, or paraphrase without quotation marks. If quoting, cite the translation (SEP's rendering is Haldane & Kemp-derived; Payne differs) — the References entry currently names only "WWR I §13, 1818", which does not identify a translation, and the research note recorded this citation as "VERIFIED (section)" only, never at the quote level.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The Acquaintance Principle as evidence for constitutively experiential knowledge

- **Current content says**: The principle is "the aesthetics-native sibling of the knowledge argument", supplying "corroborating pressure, not a demonstration", with partial independence honestly flagged.
- **A critic would argue**: Partial independence is being counted as a positive when it may be a net negative. Two arguments resting on one intuition are *worse* than one argument, because each supplies the other with the appearance of external corroboration while adding no new evidence — the reader's confidence rises while the evidential base does not. The article names the shared root and then still describes the situation as "a stronger evidential situation than either alone."
- **Suggested response**: Either drop "stronger evidential situation than either alone" or specify the condition under which two applications of one intuition *do* add evidence (they must resist *different* debunkings — the article gestures at this with "the corroboration is real to the degree the two resist a *common* debunking" but does not identify any debunking that separates them).

### Mirth as an evaluative quale

- **Current content says**: Structural theories fix the object of humor and leave felt amusement as an extra term (Clark's conditions 2 and 3).
- **A critic would argue**: Clark's conditions were designed as an *analysis of the concept of humour*, not as an inventory of ontological ingredients. Reading "enjoys perceiving it" as a residue left over by conditions (1) is a category slide from conceptual analysis to metaphysics. Clark himself would say enjoyment is a further *condition*, not a further *fact*.
- **Suggested response**: Note the analysis/ontology distinction explicitly and argue for the crossing, or restate the residue claim without leaning on Clark's numbering.

### Aesthetic exceptionalism itself

- **Current content says**: Whether the asymmetry is genuine "is the debate's central fault line", presented even-handedly.
- **A critic would argue**: The literature has moved. Shelley 2023 (which the article cites) argues the principle cannot be true on *any* traditional epistemic or usability reading — which, if right, undercuts unavailability pessimism, unusability pessimism, and the Map's sibling-to-Mary reading in one stroke. The article reports Shelley as "a further recent move" in a list and never registers that his conclusion is a threat to its own reading.
- **Suggested response**: Give Shelley a sentence in "Relation to Site Perspective" as a third defeater alongside Meskin and Nguyen.
