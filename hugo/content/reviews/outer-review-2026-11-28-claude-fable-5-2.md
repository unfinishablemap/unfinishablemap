## 2. Dimensional analysis

### 2.1 What the article argues (from the Map’s own descriptions)

- **Living with the Map (apex):** the article explains “Why AI alignment faces the meaning problem and requires human judgment, with the experiential alignment framework as its constructive proposal.” The apex summarises it as follows: if preferences are “shaped by evolution for survival rather than flourishing”, then “learning from them cannot produce genuine alignment.” [living-with-the-map](/apex/living-with-the-map/)
- **Subjective Aim (concept page):** the article holds that human purpose is “the ongoing subjective aim of each moment of experience toward maximal intensity and harmony” (Whitehead). [subjective-aim](/concepts/subjective-aim/)
- **Instrumental Convergence:** glosses it as “alignment when human purpose is uncertain; the preference-learning side of the same deference logic.” [instrumental-convergence](/topics/instrumental-convergence/)

### 2.2 Reconstructing and testing the central argument (INFERRED from those descriptions)

**Premises and conclusions**

- **P1.** Preference-based alignment infers what humans value from their behaviour or choices.
- **P2.** Humanity’s ultimate purpose or interests are unresolved.
- **P3.** Preferences are unreliable guides to a person’s good: evolved for survival, constructed, and manipulable.
- **C1.** So preference-based alignment rests on unstable foundations.
- **P4.** Value is constituted by the quality of conscious experience (phenomenal value realism, Tenet 1).
- **P5.** Purpose is internal to experience (Whiteheadian subjective aim).
- **C2.** So alignment should target predicted distributions over conscious experience, and this “does better.”

**Testing each step**

- **P1 is a strawman.** Russell’s three principles are (i) maximise the realisation of human preferences, (ii) start uncertain about them, (iii) treat behaviour as evidence. [aiandfaith](https://aiandfaith.org/book-review/human-compatible-artificial-intelligence-and-the-problem-of-control/) These make behaviour *evidence* about preferences, not what constitutes them, and the uncertainty is what produces deference. [devthrottle](https://devthrottle.com/ai-library/papers/human-compatible) CIRL and the off-switch game formalise exactly this, [lesswrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JjGh46oPtTe5xntHH/can-we-align-ai-by-having-it-learn-human-preferences-i-m) and the Map’s own Instrumental Convergence page explains it correctly. [instrumental-convergence](/topics/instrumental-convergence/) So the cluster describes Russell accurately in one article and as “thin” in another. That is an internal inconsistency.
- **P2 proves too much.** If an unresolved human purpose undermines preference-tracking, it undermines a fixed eight-dimension experiential target at least as badly. Choosing hedonic valence, meaning engagement, agency sense and the rest is itself a substantive verdict on the unresolved purpose. Nowhere in the cluster I read does the Map show that uncertainty about purpose hurts one target and not the other.
- **“Purpose” is equivocated.** The word covers three different things: cosmic telos (the “meaning problem”), individual meaning (subjective aim), and collective goals (alignment targets). The step from “the cosmic question is open” to “preferences fail as individual targets” is invalid.
- **P3 is borrowed, not owned.** The point that preferences are constructed and incommensurable is genuine. But its strongest source, Zhi-Xuan et al. 2024 (VERIFIED: arXiv:2408.16984), concludes the other way. The authors recommend aligning AI with “normative standards appropriate to their social roles,” “negotiated and agreed upon by all relevant stakeholders”. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.16984) They do not recommend aligning it with experiential quality. This is a co-optation firewall failure: an author’s critique is recruited toward a positive programme the authors do not hold.
- **C2 inherits worse measurement problems.** On the Map’s own dualism, experience is “not derivable from physical description alone” and “cannot be directly measured from outside” (Experiential Alignment page, VERIFIED). [experiential-alignment](/concepts/experiential-alignment/) Its measurement protocol (experience sampling, microphenomenology, neural and physiological proxies) therefore runs entirely on physically realised behaviour and reports. [experiential-alignment](/concepts/experiential-alignment/) These are the same evidence channels preferentism uses. The page admits triangulation “does not answer” the residual case. [experiential-alignment](/concepts/experiential-alignment/)
- **Further problems C2 faces:**

- Wireheading and experience-machine scenarios are handled by adding constraints (agency, growth, diversity). [experiential-alignment](/concepts/experiential-alignment/) Those constraints are non-experiential or preference-like values brought back in, which is Nozick’s point.
- There is no aggregation rule. The page concedes the axiology “fixes no exchange rate”. [experiential-alignment](/concepts/experiential-alignment/)
- Paternalism is not addressed. Deference to preferences respects autonomy in a way that experiential targeting does not.
- **Verdict on the argument.** At most it shows the experiential framework “is coherent given the tenets.” “Does better” does not follow.

### 2.4 Objections

- **Preferentist objections are unmet.** I could see no engagement with idealised or laundered preferences, meta-preferences, Yudkowsky’s Coherent Extrapolated Volition (CEV), Parfit’s informed-desire views, or the autonomy argument. Russell’s uncertainty over preferences answers most of the “thin proxy” charge, and the cluster ignores it.
- **The illusionist objection is half-met.** The Experiential Alignment page concedes that if illusionism is true, the framework “collapses toward the sophisticated functional-state tracking a good preferentism could also aim at.” [experiential-alignment](/concepts/experiential-alignment/) The concession is honest. But it is the defeat condition for “does better”, and it has not been converted into a status downgrade. This is confession-without-correction. Kammerer argues that phenomenal consciousness is “probably not particularly normatively significant” given materialism (JCS 29(3–4):180–204, 2022), [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00986) and that illusionism faces a “normative challenge” (Ergo 2019/2020). [researchgate + 2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339921937_The_Normative_Challenge_for_Illusionist_Views_of_Consciousness) The cluster does not engage either argument.
- **Mainstream alignment research is misdescribed.**

- Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) collects preference judgments; it does not use revealed choice behaviour. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09053)
- Constitutional AI replaces human harm labels with written principles. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073)
- Debate targets questions humans cannot directly judge. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00899)
- The January 2026 Claude constitution explicitly includes user wellbeing and “long-term flourishing”. [ai](https://ai.info/policies/anthropic/claudes-constitution)
- Corrigibility, interpretability, and AI-control work are not preference-inference at all.
- The article’s target is therefore largely the 2016–2019 inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) and CIRL programme, not the 2026 field.
