## Executive Summary

This is the Map's strongest *defensive* cluster and it is in unusually good calibration: at four separate points these articles argue against their own interest, and each time they say so explicitly. The historical survey concedes that the mainstream anti-exclusion responses (Woodward, List & Menzies, Loewer) rescue chemistry and biology but pointedly *do not* rescue the dualist; the dissolution article concedes that its headline result is empirically equivalent to physicalism on the very question it settles; the mental-causation article concedes that its no-energy-injection claim holds only in the degenerate case, and that the quantum-Zeno reply *relocates* rather than closes the decoherence timing gap. The Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist converge here rather than conflict — nothing in the cluster uses tenet-coherence to upgrade evidential status, and the survey states the non-upgrade in as many words ("Weakening the case *for* closure is not the same as establishing its denial").

Two genuine opportunities, both concrete. First, the survey critiques the arguments for closure without ever stating the best one: Papineau's physiological no-special-forces induction is present only as *history of the concept*, never as a live argument to be met, and one sentence risks reading the Map's own verdict as Papineau's. Second, `causal-exclusion-argument.md` advertises itself as "the neutral anatomy" of "the mainstream responses literature" but omits two response families its own siblings treat as standard — Shoemaker's subset account (absent corpus-wide) and Yablo's proportionality (present in both siblings, absent here). Third, a smaller calibration wobble: the mental-causation comparison table claims testability that the same article's falsifiability-dilemma paragraph withdraws sixty lines later.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The cluster gets the dialectical shape right: it treats exclusion, not the hard problem, as the objection that actually threatens the position. `causal-exclusion-argument.md` opens by naming premise 3 (non-identity) as "non-negotiable" for the dualist and premise-by-premise refuses the reductionist horn — the horn Kim himself takes — as unavailable in principle rather than merely unattractive. That is the honest statement of what a property dualist has to pay. The three-routes-out taxonomy (deny closure; keep two causes; locate the mental cause where physics is genuinely insufficient) is the cleanest map of the dualist's options I have seen in the corpus, and it correctly identifies which route the Map takes and what that route costs.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The survey's treatment of Process 0 is the best-calibrated Stapp exposition in the corpus. It states the gap Stapp identifies (physics says what happens *given* a measurement, not why *that* measurement), then immediately says that most physicists regard the framework as non-standard and that its strength is interpretation-dependent — and then, crucially, shows that the *closure* argument survives Stapp's fate: "the broader point survives independently of Stapp's particular framework." A Stapp partisan should be pleased rather than offended: the article gives the framework its due while making sure the Map's case does not stand or fall with it.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`mental-causation-and-downward-causation.md` earns Nagel's approval in its agent-causation section, where the [world-to-mind direction of fit](/concepts/direction-of-fit/) passage does real work: an intention "holds a representation of a not-yet-actual state and brings that state about, a literal directedness the exclusion argument's event-causal framing renders metaphorical." That is a first-person datum used as a *constraint on the framing of the problem* rather than as evidence for a conclusion — exactly the move Nagel makes about "what it is like." The self-stultification section does the same for reports: an epiphenomenalist's talk about qualia is only accidentally correlated with qualia.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

The cluster avoids crude substance dualism by refusing the injection model outright. The Map's mental cause does not push; it selects among a candidate set the physical process has already prepared — "Two operations, two domains, one outcome," in the dissolution article's phrase. That is a process-friendly picture: the mind's contribution is a moment in a single completed occasion, not a foreign force arriving from outside. The Whiteheadian "actual occasions" nod in the agent-causation section is appropriately brief and does no argumentative lifting.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane should be pleased that the cluster does not try to buy freedom cheaply. It locates agency exactly where quantum probabilities fail to fix outcomes and then immediately flags the luck objection by cross-link rather than pretending indeterminacy alone delivers control. The dissolution article's insistence that the mind-side contribution is *selection among brain-prepared candidates* — not the manufacture of candidates — is the version of quantum libertarianism that has a chance of answering the randomness charge, because the agent's contribution has content-dependent structure rather than being noise.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The historical survey's closing paragraph is a model of restraint: "The debate is not settled. But the history shows it was never as one-sided as textbooks suggest." The whole survey is an argument about *confidence levels* rather than about truth — its thesis is that a methodological assumption was mistaken for an empirical finding, and it repeatedly declines the stronger conclusion that would follow if it forgot the distinction. Line 116's explicit statement ("the survey claims only the former") is exactly the epistemic humility McGinn demands, stated rather than merely practised.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This is the persona with the most to say here, and the verdict is strongly positive with one flagged wobble.

**Restraint that is praise-worthily *done*.** Four separate places where the cluster had a clear structural opportunity to upgrade a claim on tenet-load and declined:

1. **The generalisation problem is not claimed as a dualist win.** The survey (§Twentieth Century, final paragraph) walks through Woodward's interventionism, List & Menzies' difference-making, and Loewer's compatibilism — the standard rescues of higher-level causation — and then says plainly that each rescues only properties that *supervene on the physical*, so "the dualist's mental causes, which are not physically grounded, receive no such protection," and "the exclusion of non-physical mental causation — the case Kim actually cared about — is left standing by these responses." An article optimising for its own side would have banked the generalisation problem as a defeat for Kim. This one hands it back.

2. **The dissolution article denies its own headline any discriminating power.** "Physicalism and selection-only interactionism are, on the overdetermination question alone, empirically equivalent: both escape Kim, by incompatible means, and the escape itself does not adjudicate between them. The dissolution is therefore conditional on the framework, not a standalone result." This is the tenet-register / evidence-register separation applied to the Map's own best argument, stated in the third paragraph rather than buried.

3. **The energy-conservation claim is split at the degenerate/non-degenerate line.** `mental-causation-and-downward-causation.md` states no-energy-injection "**(under degenerate outcomes)**", then immediately: "This exactness holds *only* in the degenerate case. Real neural superpositions need not be energy-degenerate." The fallback (Cucu & Pitts on Noether symmetry-breaking) is explicitly labelled a *second, independent* and weaker response rather than smuggled in as a continuation of the first.

4. **The Zeno reply is labelled a relocation, not a solution.** "This relocates the three-order-of-magnitude gap rather than closing it: 'can coherence persist long enough?' becomes 'can the observation cycles fire fast enough?' … the Zeno proposal is a conceptual research direction, not a mechanistic solution." And the section's coda states the pattern in the Map's own vocabulary: "Removing the in-principle defeater is not the same as supplying a working mechanism, and the Map should not present it as one." That is the *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating* formula applied to a physics claim.

**Empirical restraint on the clinical evidence.** The neuroplasticity paragraph volunteers n=18 (9 new), no independent replication, no head-to-head against exposure-response prevention, and that "the materialist reading … fits the same data." Then the route-versus-direction distinction is introduced *and immediately capped*: it "neutralises the input-modality deflation without thereby adding positive evidence for mental causation — the pathway data remain compatible with both readings." A defeater-blocker labelled as a defeater-blocker.

**Where the two personas converge.** The Process Philosopher's praise above (selection-not-injection as a process-friendly picture) is nowhere cashed out as a tier-upgrade: the cluster never argues that because the picture is metaphysically graceful, the mechanism is more likely to be real. The graceful-picture point does defeater-removal work only. That is the convergence signal.

**One flagged wobble (calibration concern, not praise).** The Classical-Emergentism-vs-Quantum-Interactionism comparison table in `mental-causation-and-downward-causation.md` scores quantum interactionism "**Testability**: Makes empirical predictions" and "**Relation to physics**: Exploits genuine incompleteness." Sixty lines later, the same article's falsifiability dilemma says: "'Selection within Born probabilities' is a metaphysical interpretation, not by itself a testable physical prediction." And the survey is careful that the incompleteness is *interpretation-dependent* — "interpretations on which it dissolves (Bohmian hidden variables, many-worlds) remain live, so the physics constrains rather than settles the closure question" — where the table asserts "genuine" flatly. Tables are read first and quoted out of context; this one currently states the un-hedged version of two claims the surrounding prose hedges. Refine, not revert: the table rows should carry the qualification the prose already supplies. Task minted below.

## Content Strengths

### [topics/causal-closure-debate-historical-survey.md](/topics/causal-closure-debate-historical-survey/)

- **Strongest point**: The generalisation-problem paragraph (§Twentieth Century, final para). It is the cluster's clearest instance of arguing against its own interest, and it is *correct* — the interventionist, difference-making, and compatibilist rescues really do presuppose physical grounding, so a dualist genuinely cannot borrow them.
- **Notable passage**: On Gibb and Saad — "This carries an acknowledged cost: the general claim that *some* mental causation occurs leaves no detectable trace in aggregate and is, to that extent, unfalsifiable — while *specific* mechanisms (neural coherence timescales, Born-rule compliance) remain testable. The interaction hides in the statistics; the mechanisms that would implement it do not."
- **Why it works**: The unfalsifiability concession is placed *inside* the paragraph making the pro-Map point, not quarantined in a "challenges" section where a skim-reader would miss it. The final clause converts the concession into a usable distinction (general claim untestable / specific mechanisms testable) rather than leaving it as damage.
- **Scholarship marker**: The Broad correction (§Nineteenth Century) — "Broad set the argument out in order to *assess* it, not to endorse it. An emergentist who defended two-way mind-body interaction, he resisted the objection." The corpus previously risked the standard misreading; the repair now names both the misreading and its cause ("whose careful statement of the argument was later mistaken for its endorsement"). This is the kind of attribution repair that is easy to skip and expensive to get wrong.
- **Also strong**: The Leibniz paragraph flags its own anachronism ("standardly if anachronistically traced back to this critique") rather than letting the vector-momentum principle drift back into 1686.

### [concepts/causal-exclusion-argument.md](/concepts/causal-exclusion-argument/)

- **Strongest point**: The drainage section, and specifically the choice to present Block's argument as pressure "from *within* physicalism, independent of any dualist commitment," with the aside "for a non-partisan reader, the most telling." The article is explicitly writing for someone who does not share its priors, and it identifies the strongest objection to Kim as one that owes nothing to dualism. It also reports Kim's reply fairly (reduction blocks the descent at physical levels) rather than leaving Block unanswered.
- **Notable structural achievement**: The premise-by-premise mapping of dualist routes onto the inconsistent set — route 1 denies premise 2, route 2 rejects premise 5, route 3 denies premise 2's *sufficiency at a specific joint*. Route 3's statement is the sharpest one-sentence version of the Map's position in the corpus: "There is nothing to exclude because the exclusion principle's 'screening-off' premise presupposes a sufficient physical cause that, at that joint, does not exist."
- **Accuracy marker**: The two-cause family is correctly split into the *overdeterminist* branch (Mills; Kroedel as one of two options) and Vaassen's *interventionist* branch, with the explicit note that on Vaassen's own terms "there is no overdetermination to excuse." This is the corrected framing following the author's own 2026-07-11 correspondence; it has landed cleanly and should not be re-flattened by a future condense pass.
- **Also strong**: The lead disambiguates itself from two adjacent things the reader could confuse it with ([galilean-exclusion](/concepts/galilean-exclusion/); the Map's own dissolution move) before stating what the page is for. Textbook truncation-resilient opening.

### [concepts/mental-causation-and-downward-causation.md](/concepts/mental-causation-and-downward-causation/)

- **Strongest point**: The decoherence-challenge section, for the reason given under Birch above — three responses offered, each explicitly scored as insufficient, and a closing paragraph that names the remaining gap rather than declaring the objection answered. The Hagan/Reimers/McKemmish exchange is reported with the *rebuttal to the rebuttal* included, which is where citation-optimistic writing usually stops.
- **Notable passage**: "Longer coherence narrows the gap; it does not close it."
- **Why it works**: The article is defending a mechanism it believes in, and it still tells the reader the mechanism's headline defence fails by three orders of magnitude. That buys credibility for the claims it does assert.
- **Also strong**: The Lowe contrast in the agent-causation section — the Map takes the *difference-making* branch of four-category ontology rather than Lowe's *fact-causation* branch, "because a purely structuring cause risks collapsing into the epiphenomenalism it rejects." A named fork in a named framework, with the reason for the choice given. That is the standard the rest of the corpus should be held to when it cites a philosopher it partly agrees with.

### High Priority

#### State Papineau's positive argument for closure, and unhook his name from the Map's verdict

- **File**: `obsidian/topics/causal-closure-debate-historical-survey.md`
- **Builds on**: §Contemporary Challenges: Philosophy, and the concluding §An Empirical Claim Hardened into an Axiom.
- **Would address**: The survey's whole thesis is that the arguments for closure are weaker than advertised — but it never states the strongest contemporary argument *for* closure in its own voice. Papineau's physiological induction (the nineteenth- and twentieth-century failure to find any special mental force operating in living bodies, combined with the modern reduction of forces to a small known set) is the live argument; Buhler's "inductive arguments commit sampling errors" is currently a reply to an argument the reader has never been shown. Papineau appears three times in the article and is each time cast as *historian* of the concept's rise, never as its leading defender.
- **Framing risk to repair while there**: the concluding sentence "As Papineau (2001) documents, each step appeared empirically motivated, but the cumulative effect was to elevate a methodological assumption into an axiom." The "as X documents" frame reaches across the "but" and can be read as attributing the Map's critical verdict to Papineau, who holds the opposite. Split the attribution: Papineau documents the sequence; the "elevated an assumption into an axiom" reading is the Map's. (Pattern: citation-framing accuracy — a citation can be real, verbatim, and correctly attributed while the article still mis-frames the cited author's conclusion.)
- **Estimated scope**: One new short subsection (~200w) plus one sentence repair. Article is ~3080w against a 3000 soft / 4000 hard topics ceiling, so a small trim elsewhere may be wanted; the Leibniz and Helmholtz paragraphs have some slack.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction) — the survey is the Map's public case that closure is not an established finding, and that case is stronger, not weaker, for meeting the best argument on the other side.

#### Add the two missing response families to the "neutral anatomy"

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/causal-exclusion-argument.md`
- **Builds on**: §The Responses Literature, which currently runs three families (Bennett's compatibilism, List & Menzies / Woodward interventionism, Kim's own reductionism).
- **Would address**: Two named positions the page's self-description ("the mainstream responses literature", "the value here is the neutral anatomy") commits it to covering:
  - **Shoemaker's subset account of realization** (Shoemaker 2001, "Realization and Mental Causation"; 2007, *Physical Realization*), with Jessica Wilson's proximate-powers variant. The causal powers of a mental property are a *proper subset* of its realizer's powers — so the mental property is efficacious without being a second competing cause, and premise 4 is answered without reduction. This is the fourth standard family in every survey treatment of exclusion, and it is **absent corpus-wide**: `grep -rl 'subset account\|subset of the causal powers' obsidian/concepts obsidian/topics` returns nothing, and Shoemaker appears only in `parfit-reductionism`, `inverted-qualia`, and three others, never on mental causation. It is also directly relevant to the Map's dialectic for the same reason the survey gives about the interventionist rescues: a subset of *physical* powers is unavailable to a dualist whose mental properties are not physically realized. The page can add it and then decline it, in the same move it already makes well.
  - **Yablo's proportionality** (1992). Present and treated as standard in *both* siblings — `mental-causation-and-downward-causation.md` §The Overdetermination Problem and `overdetermination-dissolution…` §Kim's Overdetermination Premise — but absent from the page that exists to be the canonical anatomy. `grep -c 'Yablo'` on this file returns 0.
- **Estimated scope**: ~250w, two short paragraphs in the existing three-family structure. Article is ~2070w against a 3000 soft concepts ceiling — ample headroom, no condense pressure.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1 (Dualism) and Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits) — the subset account is precisely a parsimony-driven attempt to have efficacy without extra powers, and showing why it does not extend to non-physically-realized properties is the same argument the survey already runs against the interventionist rescues.
