## Critical issue: page range in the headline citation

The article cited "Freedom and Resentment" as *Proceedings of the British Academy* 48, **1–25**. The British Academy's own scan of the bound volume runs **187–211** (first page 187; running heads 188…210; final page 211, ending "…the obscure and panicky metaphysics of libertarianism"; signature "C 187" at the foot of p. 193). The 1–25 form circulates widely — SEP uses it — because it is the pagination of the separately issued Annual Philosophical Lecture offprint. Both are in use, so the reference now gives the volume pagination, notes the offprint form, and warns that the Methuen/Routledge reprint paginates differently again. Page references were added to the two long quotations so a reader can check them against the edition actually cited.

## Argument fidelity

**Omitted second limb of Strawson's reply — added.** The article rendered the dissolution as resting on inescapability: we could not give the reactive attitudes up, so no theoretical thesis can require us to. That is Strawson's first reply (p. 198). His second (p. 199) is that *even if* we could imagine a genuine choice in the matter, we could choose rationally "only in the light of an assessment of the gains and losses to human life, its enrichment or impoverishment", and "the truth or falsity of a general thesis of determinism would not bear on the rationality of this choice". Omitting it weakened the opponent — the article's stated remit is to present the dissolution at full strength — and it made the closing Pereboom paragraph over-reach by treating inescapability as the premise "on which it rests". Both fixed: the second limb is now stated, and the Pereboom paragraph is scoped to "the premise on which its *first* limb rests". This also connects the article to the live scholarly dispute over whether Strawson's inescapability claim is psychological or metaphysical.

**Exemption scope, tightened.** The article said exemptions suspend the attitudes "more globally toward that person". Strawson's two sub-groups differ: the first is episodic, the second standing — he suspends attitudes "either at the time of his action or all the time" (p. 193, verbatim). The bullet now marks the distinction.

**Pereboom, scoped.** "A forward-looking, non-reactive stance" overstated him — his target is the *desert-based* reactive attitudes (resentment, indignation), not the reactive attitudes wholesale. Corrected, and a reference entry added (Pereboom 2014, OUP) since he was named inline with no References entry.
