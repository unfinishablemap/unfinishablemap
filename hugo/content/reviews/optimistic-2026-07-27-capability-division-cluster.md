## Executive Summary

This is the corpus's best-calibrated multi-article cluster, and its distinctive virtue is *subtractive*: the strongest content in every member is a claim about what the pattern does **not** establish. The apex's headline finding is an asymmetry rather than a recurrence; the proprioception article converts "three independent confirmations" into "one cross-substrate pattern at three sites"; the 3D-world article partitions its own falsifiability list into three grades and concludes that only one of six items is genuinely in-practice. The opportunities are all connective rather than expansive: three specific, grep-verified link gaps, one of which (pain asymbolia) would supply a datum the apex says it does not have.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The 3D-world article makes a move Chalmers would recognise as structurally his own and then does something he rarely gets from allies: it refuses to cash the gap in as dualism. §"Relation to Site Perspective" separates two layers explicitly — "what the 3D-world puzzle supports on its own" is the explanatory-gap claim, which "delivers it whether the reader's wider metaphysics is dualist, panpsychist, neutral-monist, Russellian, or sophisticated-functionalist," while "what the Map's reading adds is independent of the 3D-world puzzle and inherits from the tenets directly." Chalmers's own naturalistic-dualist argument has always insisted the gap is neutral between his view and Russellian monism; this article says so about its own argument, in its own voice, and names panpsychism and Russellian monism as co-beneficiaries by wikilink. That is the rare case of a dualist article declining to conscript the hard problem.

The vision article's *ownership* section supplies the residue Chalmers cares about in its sharpest operational form: "Conscious vision is not merely information availability — it is information available *to someone*." That distinction (availability-to-the-system vs availability-to-a-subject) is doing real work later — it is precisely the term the apex uses to locate where Global Neuronal Workspace under-describes.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The cluster is admirably restrained about where the quantum story enters. The proprioception article's Tenet 2 paragraph offers a microtubule-distribution consideration and immediately withdraws its weight: "This is speculative; the dual-domain argument rests on the explanatory gap, not on any specific mechanism." The 3D-world article routes phenomenal binding to the quantum-holism programme and the entanglement-binding hypothesis while stating "the mechanism is an open problem and the Map does not claim to have settled it," and it names panpsychism's combination problem as the structurally parallel open programme on the rival side. Stapp would want the mechanism argued harder; he would have no complaint that it is *presented* honestly.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The proprioception article does what Nagel's method demands: it starts from what it is like and lets the phenomenology set the explanandum. Merleau-Ponty's body as "vehicle of being in the world," Sacks's Christina describing herself as "disembodied" with motor function partially preserved, phantom limbs delivering felt position without a limb — these are not decorative anecdotes but the dissociation structure itself. The article's `Key Dissociation` column (Christina: no feel, some function; phantoms: feel, no receptor) is a genuine double dissociation stated in phenomenological rather than behavioural terms.

The 3D-world article's presence section is the cluster's best piece of first-person description: "Behind you right now, there is a world you cannot see... The experienced world is not a visual field surrounded by blankness. It is an all-encompassing spatial envelope that contains the perceiver." Nagel's point is that this is data, and the article treats it as data whose computational shadow (predictive maintenance of unperceived space) is a different thing.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

The cluster avoids crude substance dualism by construction: nothing here posits a mental *thing* that acts on a physical *thing*. The vision article's closing speculation is explicitly process-flavoured — "the mind-side contribution in vision may not be an additional processing step but a different mode of existence for information that the brain has already computed." The apex generalises this: the mind-side term is not one substance-like contribution but "a family of distinct contributions — object-unity (touch, hearing), owned affect (interoception), and the situatedness of the viewpoint (vestibular)."

Per this persona's evidential-status constraint, none of the above is offered here as licence to upgrade any claim's tier, and the cluster does not invite it: the apex holds the whole convergence "consonant with the interface reading and equally consistent with its strongest rival."

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

The proprioception article's §"Imagination as Mental Causation" is the cluster's clearest agency content: the sculptor's imagined form preceding the cut, the athlete's mental rehearsal transferring to physical performance, the architect revising from an imagined walkthrough. The causal chain is stated as a chain — "consciousness generates spatial content → content is evaluated phenomenally → evaluation guides physical action" — and the epiphenomenalist challenge is put as a question the epiphenomenalist has to answer ("why does the *quality* of the imagery matter?"), not as a rhetorical flourish.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The 3D-world article's §"What Would Challenge This View?" is the strongest single passage in the cluster and the one most worth reusing as a template. It sorts six potential falsifiers into three grades — in-practice, in-practice-only-conditional-on-absent-instrumentation, and in-principle-inaccessible-by-the-framework's-own-commitments — then states the arithmetic against itself: "only (2) is genuinely in-practice as stated... The framework's net falsifiability is genuinely modest — narrower than a six-item challenge list invites the reader to assume." The article notices that its *own* answer to challenge (3) is what makes challenge (1) uninterpretable — "the article's own response to challenge (3) is what installs that limitation" — which is a piece of self-auditing McGinn's cognitive-closure thesis would applaud and which most falsifiability sections never reach.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This cluster has no minimal-organism or animal boundary cases, so the persona's usual target is absent; its verdict is nonetheless load-bearing here because the cluster runs three separate *count* disciplines, each of which is a refusal to upgrade.

**One.** The proprioception article had the easiest available over-claim in the corpus — three capabilities, three dissociations, three confirmations — and declined it in the same paragraph where it would have landed: "they are not three fully independent confirmations of dual-domain architecture so much as one explanatory-gap pattern recurring across three sites." It then salvages the *correct* residual weight rather than abandoning the argument: what recurrence genuinely adds is **substrate divergence** (engrams, proprioceptive receptors, and parietal rotation machinery cannot share capability-specific mechanism, so a deflation at one site does not transfer), which is "worth the weight of one cross-substrate pattern, not three." That is the ideal shape — identify the shared upstream, discount the count, name what survives.

**Two.** The 3D-world article applies the same discipline to its own three-part structure, and its conclusion is more costly: two of its three sections are re-dress of arguments the corpus already counts elsewhere. "The construction problem is the binding problem in spatial dress... The perspective problem is the indexical-identity problem in spatial dress... Counting these as independent contributions to the case for irreducibility would double-count what the corpus already counts elsewhere." Only the presence problem is claimed as distinctive. An article volunteering that two-thirds of its own structure is non-novel is unusual.

**Three.** The apex refuses to enlist its best external evidence. Sanchez et al. 2020's supramodal decoding is exactly the datum an apex would want to headline, and the apex instead states that Global Neuronal Workspace predicts it too, that "the datum does not discriminate between them," and that "the Map must out-accommodate the workspace, not enlist Sanchez against it." It then names where its own case is weakest without being asked: "there are supramodal-access experiments but... no direct cross-modal *ownership* experiments. The ownership claim is a place the workspace looks incomplete, not a place the Map has independent evidence the workspace lacks."

Process Philosopher and Hardline Empiricist converge rather than conflict throughout this cluster: the family-of-mind-side-contributions reading that Whitehead would like is introduced *as a scope restriction* on the apex's recurrence claim, not as an expansion of it. No calibration concern is flagged in this pass.

## Content Strengths

### apex/cross-modal-capability-division.md
- **Strongest point**: the negative half of the finding — "The Brain Side Does Not Recur." The apex notices that the *labels* recur while the *machinery* does not (V1 retinotopy vs cochlear tonotopy vs a ~400-receptor combinatorial olfactory code vs mechanoreceptor populations) and draws the consequence for reduction: "A deflationary account that explained away the explanatory gap in vision by appeal to V1 feature integration would have nothing to say about the combinatorial olfactory code or about why a still finger has no roughness-quale."
- **Notable quote**: "The recurrence claim was never universal, and saying so precisely is stronger than asserting it everywhere."
- **Why it works**: the interoceptive and vestibular sections are structured as *scope discoveries* rather than as further confirmations. Interoception inverts both terms (brain side converges on the insula; mind side becomes ownership outright), which demotes "the brain side does not recur" from a structural necessity to an exteroceptive regularity — and the apex treats that demotion as the finding. Thermoception is then correctly placed *on the line* rather than on either side, turning a partition into a gradient. This is what an apex is for: none of the eight source articles can see any of it from inside its own modality.

### topics/three-dimensional-world-representation-problem.md
- **Strongest point**: the introspective-grade calibration inserted *before* the phenomenology it licenses. The article distinguishes content reports ("there is a felt depth") from structural-organisational reports ("phenomenal space involves a structural framework of three dimensions with perspective, depth gradations, spatial continuity, and an ambient envelope") and concedes that "introspective access to the structural-organisational layer is on weaker footing." It then carries that distinction forward to the colour-comparison section rather than dropping it.
- **Notable quote**: "the article's own response to challenge (3) is what installs that limitation."
- **Why it works**: it handles the Dennett objection by *reversing the burden onto itself*. Where most dualist articles treat human/machine functional similarity as an embarrassment to explain away, this one states the heterophenomenologist's reading in the heterophenomenologist's favour ("same functional organisation, same reports, same consciousness — from that vantage, the article's refusal to attribute phenomenal experience to the artificial system is the conclusion, not the premise") and then grounds its own asymmetric attribution in the interface problem's bootstrapping gap rather than in any observed difference. The Madhyamaka counter-reading of centred perspective is handled the same way.

### concepts/capability-division-in-vision.md
- **Strongest point**: the epiphenomenalism section supplies the spandrel reply *against itself* and then downgrades its own argument accordingly — "the epiphenomenalist can answer that phenomenal structure is a *necessary byproduct*... The Map's reply does not lean on the selection argument alone... and treats the evolutionary consideration as corroborating rather than load-bearing." Evolutionary arguments for consciousness's causal efficacy are the corpus's most over-claimed genre; this is the disciplined version.
- **Why it works**: Phillips (2021) is engaged as a live threat to the article's own load-bearing case rather than as a footnote. The article grants that if blindsight is degraded conscious vision, "less can be attributed to the brain alone," and identifies the narrower claim that survives (the performance limitations hold either way).

### concepts/mind-arena.md
- **Strongest point**: the disambiguation is genuinely useful editorial infrastructure. Three candidate readings of the term are laid out, the canonical usage is fixed by appeal to how the term actually behaves in its primary home, and the resolution is tested against four existing use-sites. The closing one-liner is the kind of thing the rest of the corpus can cite: "mental causation is the mechanism, the interface is the junction, the subjective is the phenomenal category, and the mind-arena is the domain of outcomes co-determined by all three."
- **Why it works**: the epiphenomenalism test is the cleanest demonstration that the term is doing work rather than relabelling — "A purely epiphenomenal mind would have a rich subjective domain but an *empty* mind-arena." The article also self-classifies honestly as "a posited framework construct, not an established entity" whose credence "tracks the tenets it presupposes rather than any independent evidence," and explicitly declines to place itself on the five-tier evidential scale on the grounds that it is definitional rather than evidential. Correct call, correctly reasoned.

### High Priority

#### Pain asymbolia as the within-modality isolation of the mind-side ownership term

- **Builds on**: [concepts/pain-asymbolia.md](/concepts/pain-asymbolia/) and [apex/cross-modal-capability-division.md](/apex/cross-modal-capability-division/).
- **Would address**: a datum the apex says it lacks. The apex's central open question is whether the mind-side term is one contribution or a family, and its answer is that the object-centred senses "never isolate" the individual members "because in them it is always fused with a felt object." Pain asymbolia is exactly that isolation, performed clinically, within a single modality: the sensory-discriminative layer survives intact (patients "identify that a stimulus is nociceptive," "localise the stimulus correctly," "describe its sensory quality") while the affective-motivational layer is abolished — Grahek's *feeling pain* without *being in pain*. That is the mattering/ownership term coming apart from the content term with the modality held fixed, which is the one experimental geometry the cross-modal survey cannot produce. It also bears on the apex's admission that there are "no direct cross-modal *ownership* experiments": asymbolia is not cross-modal, but it is a direct within-modality ownership dissociation, and saying so is more informative than the current silence.
- **Verified gap**: [apex/cross-modal-capability-division.md](/apex/cross-modal-capability-division/), [concepts/capability-division-in-vision.md](/concepts/capability-division-in-vision/), [topics/dual-domain-capabilities-in-proprioception-and-spatial-imagination.md](/topics/dual-domain-capabilities-in-proprioception-and-spatial-imagination/), [topics/interoceptive-consciousness-and-the-interface.md](/topics/interoceptive-consciousness-and-the-interface/) and [voids/capability-division-problem.md](/voids/capability-division-problem/) contain **zero** references to asymbolia (grep, 2026-07-27), despite [concepts/pain-asymbolia.md](/concepts/pain-asymbolia/) having 16 inbound linkers elsewhere in the corpus and despite nociception being named in the apex as one of interoception's afferent channels.
- **Calibration that must be preserved**: `pain-asymbolia.md` already carries the Griffith & Kind (2024) "asymbolia is not pain" objection and the Duval & Klein (2025) reply, and states the disciplined narrower claim ("not that it delivers a clean single-variable experiment isolating felt awfulness with everything else held fixed"). Any addition must inherit that hedge, not the pristine-dissociation reading. On the `common-cause-null`, asymbolia is a *sharper instrument*, not an additional confirming line — it shares the same explanatory-gap upstream as everything else in the cluster.
- **Estimated scope**: short — one paragraph plus reciprocal links. Route the substance to [concepts/pain-asymbolia.md](/concepts/pain-asymbolia/) (2292/3500 words, real headroom); the apex has only ~83 words of headroom before its 5000 hard threshold, so its side should be a single sentence or a Further Reading bullet.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1 (the affective layer as a distinct phenomenal contribution) and Tenet 3 (the layer whose absence removes the motivational force).
