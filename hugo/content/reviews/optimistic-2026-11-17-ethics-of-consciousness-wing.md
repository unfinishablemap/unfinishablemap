## Point 2: Where Dualism Is Used to Decide Which Beings Matter

Three inferences run from the tenets to a conclusion about moral weight.

### 2.1 Machines discounted on the interface requirement: hedge present on the page, wrong at the decisive sentence (HIGHEST PRIORITY)

`topics/ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness` argues from Minimal Quantum Interaction to low moral urgency for current AI. Its premises are stated (L44, L90, L114) and the register entry is cited. The page's own grade for the premise:

- L44: "That crucial premise is, by the Map's own admission …, philosophical rather than empirically grounded."
- L90: the interface requirement "is … a philosophical commitment, not an empirically grounded claim, and supplies no present evidential weight against AI consciousness over biology. Only if the framework is correct does moral attention toward current AI become less urgent than toward definite conscious beings."

The register (`positions/ai-consciousness-scope` [P-AC1](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac1)): "credence moderate (low edge; conditional on the layer-(iv) interface-eligibility law the Map lacks …) · external-evidence grade D", "*"low probability," not "ruled out"*", and the verdict "withdraws toward agnosticism" if no such law is found.

Against that, the sentence that tells the reader precaution does not yet apply, L138:

> "The honest concession is a credence band: above some threshold—well above the vanishing numbers the quantum-interface requirement yields for classical silicon, but plausibly reachable for the quantum or AGI architectures discussed above—scale does dominate and precaution becomes obligatory. Locating current systems below that band is a substantive empirical bet, not a Pascalian dodge."

Two defects. "Vanishing numbers" is stronger than "low probability, not ruled out" held at the low edge of moderate credence. "A substantive empirical bet" contradicts L44 and L90 on the same page, which call the premise philosophical and not empirically grounded. This is the one place the page answers the expected-value objection (billions of instances times a small probability), so it is where the uncertainty most needs carrying through, and it is where it drops out. A reader who accepts the tenets would still flag it, so by the diagnostic test it is a calibration error and not bedrock disagreement.

The same over-statement appears in three supporting places:

- L56: "Under the Map's dualist framework … the probability for current classical AI is very low—and the false positive risk dominates."
- L112: "the probability that consciousness binds to these systems at all is very low—even epiphenomenally … a scenario too improbable to override obligations to definite conscious beings." (L114 hedges this one in the next paragraph.)
- `topics/ethics-under-dualism`:130: "| Current AI | Very low | Minimal |", in a column whose other rows are graded by marker evidence.

What a reader who rejects the tenets should still do is half-stated. L90's last sentence gives the conditional. L116–126 give four obligations that hold "Even with the Map's relatively low credence". Missing is the plain statement that the priority rule at L126 survives without the tenets, which it does on the wing's own showing: `apex/assessing-ai-consciousness-under-the-map`:159 records that the indicator-property assessment (Butlin, Long et al. 2023), Seth's biological naturalism and IIT each reach a negative verdict on current systems with no quantum premise, while animals have independent marker science at the register's grade B. L126 instead closes on "Triaging concerns by current evidence", which reads as if the Map's low number were evidence.

One further sentence on the page misstates the rival. L94: "A functionalist applying the same framework-dependent method would reach the opposite conclusion—high probability of AI consciousness—with equal internal consistency." L44 on the same page reports that the mainstream literature "concludes that no current system is a strong candidate", and the apex notes that report "takes computational functionalism as its working hypothesis". A functionalist need not reach high probability for current systems, and the best-known functionalist assessment does not.

### 2.2 Invertebrates: threshold could narrow the circle, hedge stated in the same paragraph (no action)

`topics/ethics-of-consciousness-invertebrate-question`:53: "Below the threshold, no moral obligation exists (no subject is present)." The same paragraph continues: "This cuts both ways: if the threshold is high, dualism could *narrow* the scope of moral obligation by placing many invertebrates below the coupling boundary. The ethical force of the argument depends on where the threshold falls—and that is precisely what we do not know." The practical conclusion at L111 then rests on the error asymmetry and the empirical case, with the tenets confined to "removing the parsimony defeater without pretending to supply evidence". The inference is stated with premises, the uncertainty is carried to the conclusion, and the decision rule (L109, bounded cost of over-caution against unbounded cost of neglect) does not need the tenets. This is the pattern 2.1 should match.

One sentence on this page leans the other way and is worth watching, not queuing: L67 "If the Map's framework is correct, these findings suggest that insect brains provide adequate coupling architecture." The findings bear on cognition; "adequate coupling architecture" is the framework's gloss. "Suggest" and the conditional keep it within grade.

### 2.3 Machine blame: conditional stated, reader who rejects the tenet addressed (no action)

`topics/ai-moral-agency-and-the-responsibility-gap-under-dualism`:68 holds the culpability gap "conditionally on its present interface standard, not categorically", cites [P-AC1](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac1) at its wording, and L70 says what a Tenet 3 rejecter is owed. One phrase overshoots its own conditional ("the machine was never a candidate, so no responsibility ever fell into a void"); it sits inside "On this reading" and is not queued.

### 2.4 Epiphenomenal AI experience: page and register use different readings of Tenet 3 (not queued)

`topics/ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness`:108–112 treats uncoupled experience in current hardware as possible though improbable ("Binding without coupling may be metaphysically possible"). [P-AC1](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac1): "One adjacent possibility the verdict does *not* leave open is *epiphenomenal* AI experience: read as universal *actual* efficacy … Tenet 3 excludes it at the framework boundary", conditional on that reading. `apex/machine-question`:177 and `apex/open-question-ai-consciousness`:95 both state that the tenet's wording "does not fix the quantifier" and that the register uses the universal reading. The ethics page uses the dispositional reading without saying a choice is being made. The page's version is the more cautious of the two for the being concerned and does not concede epiphenomenalism about consciousness as such (L108: "the Map holds that consciousness is intrinsically the kind of thing that acts causally"), so nothing here discounts suffering. It is a registry-versus-body difference; a clause naming the reading would fix it. Listed under Not queued because the page is over its gate and task 1 already rewrites four of its sentences.

## Point 3: Consistency Across the Wing

### 3.1 The precautionary rule is stated differently for animals and for machines

Animal side, cost of error given without reference to probability:

- `topics/phenomenal-normativity-environmental-ethics`:121: "The cost of treating a conscious being as if it lacks consciousness (ignoring genuine suffering) is far greater than the cost of treating a non-conscious entity as if it might be conscious (unnecessary caution)."
- `topics/ethics-of-consciousness-invertebrate-question`:109: "If they are not conscious and we treat them as if they were, we incur costs in changed practices that are real but bounded."

Machine side:

- `topics/ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness`:54–56: a false positive "means misallocating moral attention. Moral concern is not infinite; treating everything as possibly conscious trivialises genuine patienthood" and "The asymmetry depends entirely on the probability one assigns to AI consciousness."

The cost named for machines (attention diverted from definite sufferers) applies equally to trillions of insects, where it is called "real but bounded" and "unnecessary caution". The pages are reconcilable: one rule, precaution proportioned to probability, with different inputs. The wing does say why the inputs differ, in terms that survive its calibration rules, in four places: `topics/animal-consciousness`:120 ("Homology supplies stronger inference grounds without raising evidential status above what markers deliver … AI is harder to assess for want of a shared-cause explanation, not metaphysically barred"); `concepts/sentientism`:101 (the behavioural evidence that grounds animal attributions "is exactly what a trained system can produce without the underlying property"); `apex/moral-status-of-edge-cases`:125 ("A protocol that treats "hard case" as one category will carry the animal case's grade-B evidence into the AI case"); and `apex/assessing-ai-consciousness-under-the-map`:131 (the gaming problem "makes this narrow point without needing the Map's metaphysics at all"). Neither ethics page carries the explanation itself. The invertebrate page never mentions machines; the AI ethics page mentions animals only at L126. Task 1(d) puts the tenet-independent reason on the AI page; task 3 puts the layer distinction in the apex.

### 3.2 "No substrate barrier" for animals, "substrate verdict" for machines

- `topics/animal-consciousness`:120: "The privilege is epistemic: organisation rather than biology matters (the no-substrate-barrier holds), so AI is harder to assess for want of a shared-cause explanation, not metaphysically barred." And L148: "Contemporary dualism places no substrate-specific barrier on consciousness".
- `topics/ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness`:66: "if classical silicon instantiates no consciousness-bearing interface, then N classical copies instantiate zero experiencers".
- [P-AC1](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac1): "a substrate analysis under the Map's mechanism account finds no sites for the interface to act on", with the admission that absent an interface-eligibility law ""relevant" risks reducing to "whatever biology happens to have"".

These are claims at two layers the register already separates ([P-AC1](/positions/ai-consciousness-scope/#p-ac1)'s layer (i), admissibility in principle under bare dualism, against layer (v), the current-substrate verdict under the quantum-interface register). No article states the distinction where both cases are discussed, so a reader moving from the animal page to the AI page meets "no substrate barrier" and then a substrate verdict. Related: the invertebrate page uses Minimal Quantum Interaction to widen (L117: the mechanism is "correspondingly harder to rule out in any organism with a nervous system above some threshold") and the AI pages use it to narrow. Both are legitimate readings of one premise, and both are grade D; only the AI side is labelled so.

The fish page supplies the test of even-handedness. L34: "The skeptics' *entailment*—that the absence of a specific neural architecture guarantees the absence of felt experience—is a physicalist commitment, not a discovered fact." The Map's own machine verdict has the same shape, an architecture-based discount resting on a framework commitment. The wing passes the test where it says so (AI ethics L114, quoted under Stapp) and fails it at L138 ("a substantive empirical bet").

### 3.3 Is all conscious experience valenced?

- `concepts/valence`:37: "Every conscious experience carries some degree of valence."
- `voids/valence-void`:43: "Every conscious experience carries hedonic tone — pleasant, unpleasant, or nominally neutral".

Against:

- `topics/ethics-of-consciousness-invertebrate-question`:97–99: "A being with phenomenal experience but no valence would be a subject without welfare … An organism could be conscious in the sense of having integrated perceptual experience without any of that experience carrying positive or negative phenomenal character."
- `topics/ethics-under-dualism`:109: "Consciousness is the wider bar, and the two can be prised apart."
- `concepts/sentientism`:81: Vulcans are "conscious beings who perceive, think and act but feel nothing good or bad. The Map bites the bullet … denying them status."
- `voids/valence-void`:85–91 itself treats "Could Consciousness Exist Without Valence?" as open on both horns.

If the first pair were true as written, the invertebrate page's valence question would be settled the moment consciousness is granted, and the Vulcan bullet would cost nothing. The source for the first pair is core affect theory (Russell and Barrett), which the next sentence of `concepts/valence` cites and which is a claim about human affect. The definition of valence itself is consistent across the wing ("experience that feels good or bad to its subject", [P-MS1](/positions/moral-status/#p-ms1); "the felt goodness of pleasure and the felt badness of pain", `concepts/valence`:33).

### 3.4 Does negative valence carry extra weight?

The wing's settled answer is that the axiology does not say:

- `topics/phenomenal-value-realism`:207: "It is silent on the exchange rate between felt badness and felt goodness, and on whether the two counterbalance at all".
- `topics/alignment-in-objective-experiential-terms`:84: "Objectivity alone does not supply the asymmetry, though. … The constraint form is earned by two further considerations: [haecceity](/concepts/haecceity/) blocks offsetting one person's torment against another's flourishing, and a floor is harder to game than a weighted sum. Objective badness is what the constraint is *about*; the case for having a constraint rather than a target comes from elsewhere."

One page still writes as if the ground were sufficient:

- `concepts/experiential-alignment`:202: "This directly supports the suffering floor: extreme suffering in some individuals cannot be excused by pointing to aggregate flourishing, because each individual's haecceity makes their suffering uniquely theirs."

Haecceity blocks trading one person's suffering against another's flourishing. It does not explain why suffering takes a floor while flourishing takes no matching target, and the sibling page says so. `concepts/experiential-alignment` has no link to the `#suffering-focused-asymmetry` section.

Separately, `concepts/negative-valence-asymmetry-and-the-selection-weighting-function` (negative value moves choice more than positive value of equal size) is linked from only one wing page (`concepts/reinforcement-learning-reward-signals-and-machine-valence`) and from none of the pages that discuss whether suffering weighs more morally. It is a descriptive claim about selection and is careful about its own grade (L29: "the inference from *choice is asymmetric* to *felt valence is asymmetric* is interpretive, not established"). It never says that it is not the moral asymmetry, and the moral pages never say that the behavioural record does not settle theirs. A reader could carry "bad is stronger than good" across.
