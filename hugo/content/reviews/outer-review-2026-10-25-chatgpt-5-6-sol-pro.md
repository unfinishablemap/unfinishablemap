# External review: *Consciousness and Agency*

**Version reviewed:** live article on 25 October 2026, stated as last substantively modified on 16 October 2026. ## Overall verdict

**Recommendation: major revision.**

The article is strongest as a candid statement of a research programme. It distinguishes sourcehood from leeway, grants sophisticated compatibilism substantial explanatory parity, concedes that the neural evidence is compatible with physicalism, identifies the Born-rule dilemma, and labels the temporal material speculative. Those are substantial improvements over a standard consciousness-causes-collapse argument.

It does not, however, establish its opening thesis that consciousness “authors action” by selecting quantum possibilities. Its own later formulation is more accurate: until there is a worked model, the selector proposal is a **framework-internal coherence result, not established mental causation**. That conclusion directly qualifies the much stronger language in the opening, the exclusion section, and the closing celebration of authorship. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

The central results of the audit are:

## 1. Version control and an important stale-synthesis problem

The apex was last substantively modified on 16 October, but the linked *Agent Causation* article now contains corrections absent from the apex:

- Kane is described in the source article as offering an **agent-causal/event-causal naturalistic rival**, not as abandoning the event-causal side of his theory.
- Lowe is now correctly presented as holding that free volitions are uncaused and that agents cause bodily results by willing them; the Map explicitly departs from him by making the volition itself agent-caused.
- The source article now identifies the pairing problem: locating an interface does not yet explain why this mental subject is paired with this brain or this physical event.
- The site’s subsequent review of libertarian theories now says agent-causal accounts answer the **disappearing-agent form** of the luck objection but do not thereby defeat the rollback form. [agent-causation](/concepts/agent-causation/)

The Kane error is particularly conspicuous because the changelog records a correction on 12 October: Kane’s 2024 position is explicitly an “agent-causal/event-causal” view in which neither side is reducible to the other. Nevertheless, the 16 October apex says Kane “rejects the event-causal label” and that the Map takes a step he will not. That is not a defensible compression of Kane’s position; it reverses an important part of it. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

This is not merely a bibliographic issue. Kane’s hybrid is a direct rival to the article’s inference that avoiding the disappearing agent requires an irreducible substance-bearing subject. A naturalistic agent understood as an information-responsive dynamical system may be able to exercise teleological guidance without being a nonphysical substance. The article must defeat that position rather than classifying it out of the relevant space.

# 2. The luck objection

## 2.1 What the article gets right

“Meeting the Luck Objection” gives a fair statement of the rollback problem: if the antecedent state, reasons and agent are identical across replays, yet A occurs in some and B in others, what explains the difference? It also makes three unusually important concessions:

1. Felt effort can be carried by an epiphenomenal psychophysical law.
2. A physical executive-control process predicts the same load-sensitive phenomenology and neural signatures.
3. Reasons-guided agent causation identifies **who settles** the outcome without yet explaining **why it was settled that way**.

The final concession is decisive. The article expressly says that the agent’s settling is primitive and that its modal structure remains owed. That is a more accurate verdict than the heading “Meeting the Luck Objection” suggests. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

The article also correctly avoids using the neural findings as direct evidence for dualism. Willed attention, premotor commitment and the stimulation dissociation are compatible with a selection architecture, but a functional or physical executive architecture predicts them too. In the Desmurget case, electrically inducing an intention-like experience is at least as natural evidence for a brain-generated representation of intention as for a nonphysical selector. The apex acknowledges this. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

## 2.2 Why the reply does not defeat present or rollback luck

The article’s three positive features—reasons, effort and neural signatures—distinguish deliberative choosing from an externally observed coin toss. They do not answer the strongest contrastive version of the objection.

Suppose the agent has substantial reasons for A and B, sincerely deliberates, and exerts effort. In one replay the agent selects A; in another, B. Those reasons and the degree of effort are present in both histories. Consequently:

- reasons explain why A was eligible or intelligible;
- effort explains why the decision was difficult;
- the agent-causal relation designates the agent as the source;

but none explains why the agent exercised the relevant power toward A in this replay rather than toward B. The apex itself arrives at exactly this point when it says the primitive’s modal structure remains unsupplied. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

Agent causation may therefore answer the **disappearing-agent objection**: the outcome is not caused solely by prior agent-involving events while the agent disappears from the causal story. It does not, without further structure, answer **present luck** or **contrastive luck**. The site’s own later changelog now makes this distinction, replacing a claim that agent-causal views defeat luck with the narrower claim that they answer its disappearing-agent form. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

Calling the exercise primitive is not automatically illegitimate. Every theory has stopping points. But two further steps are required:

- The article must compare the primitive exercise of agential power symmetrically with a primitive stochastic transition. Why is one control and the other chance rather than merely being labelled differently?
- It must provide at least dispositional or probabilistic constraints connecting the agent’s reasons, character and attention to the exercise of the power. Otherwise “the agent settled it” supplies an ontological subject but no explanatory profile.

At present, the article’s most defensible conclusion is therefore: **agent causation can represent the outcome as the agent’s act, but it has not yet shown that this representation removes contrastive luck**.

## 2.3 The compatibilist challenge is acknowledged but not answered

The article commendably concedes that Fischer and Ravizza, Wolf and other sophisticated compatibilists can reproduce deliberative phenomenology, responsibility practices and character development. It then objects that such theories do not explain why the agent’s reasons are really theirs when the reasons-responsive mechanism is itself the product of unchosen causes. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

That objection presupposes the incompatibilist standard it is meant to establish. Fischer–Ravizza-style ownership does not require the agent to have created the mechanism ex nihilo; it requires an appropriately reasons-responsive mechanism that the agent has come to regard as their own. Wolf’s account similarly locates freedom in governing action through one’s values and in being able to develop or alter those values through appropriate data, methods and reasons—not in having created the initial self from nothing. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/49444)

The article’s question—

> why are those reasons the agent’s if their mechanism was unchosen?

—therefore does not expose an omission internal to compatibilism. It imposes the Map’s further requirement of ultimate origination. To turn that into an argument, the apex needs to show one of the following:

- historical mechanism ownership cannot distinguish responsibility from manipulation;
- identification and normative competence are insufficient for desert;
- derivative agency is incoherent rather than merely less ultimate;
- or ordinary and retributive responsibility really require creation of the springs of action.

The article currently demonstrates only that compatibilism does not provide **libertarian sourcehood**. Compatibilists already agree.

## 2.4 Rival libertarian theories are not adequately confronted

The most serious omitted rival is not determinism but a naturalistic or event-integrating libertarian theory.

The apex portrays event-causal libertarianism as leaving the agent a passive channel for prior mental events. That is a genuine objection to simple event-causal models, but stronger versions make the agent’s effort, reasons, attention and self-forming activity constitutive parts of the causal process. Kane’s 2024 AC/EC theory expressly combines agent-causal and event-causal elements rather than choosing between them. The Map must explain why such an agent cannot be the source unless it is a nonphysical persisting substance. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

The linked source article itself records O’Connor’s alternative: emergent agent-causal power added to a world otherwise governed by event causation. That is another rival to the apex’s inference from agent causation to a dualist substance. [agent-causation](/concepts/agent-causation/)

Lowe cannot fill the gap. Lowe’s free volitions are wholly uncaused; the agent is a substance cause of bodily movements by willing them but does not cause its own volition, precisely to avoid a regress. The Map’s stopping point is therefore not Lowe’s stopping point. The current source article and changelog acknowledge this, but the apex does not. [Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews+2](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/personal-agency-the-metaphysics-of-mind-and-action/)

## 2.5 Constitutive luck and moral desert remain

The article moves from authorship to the conclusion that the wrongdoer “really deserves blame” and that character is partly self-created. That transition is too rapid.

Even granting libertarian selection at particular decisions, the agent’s initial evaluative dispositions, intellectual capacities, developmental environment, available reasons and opportunities for self-modification are not self-created. Later self-formation may enlarge responsibility, but it begins from constitutively lucky starting conditions. Saying that repeated choices partly form character does not establish ultimate responsibility for the chooser who made those formative choices. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

Moreover, libertarian control would at most supply one possible control condition for desert. It does not by itself justify retribution. A separate normative argument is needed to move from “the agent originated the harm” to “suffering or deprivation should be imposed because the agent deserves it.” The apex does admit that this is the thinnest part of its moral case, but “Moral Responsibility Grounded” and “retribution becomes intelligible” remain stronger than the argument supports. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

### Verdict on luck

The article succeeds against one objection: **a substance-causal model need not make the agent disappear from the causal history**. It does not succeed against rollback luck, contrastive luck or constitutive luck. Its own best passage already acknowledges this result.

# 3. The causal exclusion problem

## 3.1 What the quantum response establishes

The Map is entitled to argue conditionally:

1. assume a genuinely indeterministic, single-outcome physics;
2. assume physical antecedents determine only a probability distribution;
3. assume a conscious subject has a law-governed power to select among the available outcomes;
4. then the mental cause need not duplicate a deterministic sufficient physical cause.

This shows that Kim-style exclusion is not an immediate proof of the logical impossibility of interactionist dualism. It identifies one possible way to reject a deterministic formulation of physical causal closure. The article is also right that a single-outcome collapse interpretation provides a more natural setting for this proposal than Everettian unitary evolution. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

That is a useful result. It is not the same as showing that physics leaves a causal vacancy that consciousness fills.

## 3.2 Indeterminacy is not causal incompleteness

The key overstatement occurs in:

> “Where the closure premise fails locally … the overdetermination worry dissolves.”

A stochastic physical theory can be causally or probabilistically complete even though its laws do not determine an individual outcome. The physical state and stochastic law may completely determine the objective chances, after which an outcome simply occurs without a further determining cause. The absence of a deterministic sufficient cause does not entail the presence of unfinished causal work.

The type–token distinction does not bridge that gap. Born-rule statistics at the type level and a particular result at the token level can both be entirely physical if physical causation is probabilistic. Showing that physics does not entail which result will occur is not yet showing that a nonphysical selector caused the result. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

The article effectively concedes this two paragraphs later. If selection changes frequencies, there should be a departure from the physical probability model; if it leaves every relevant probability unchanged, what causal difference does it make? Its own conclusion—that a worked model is required and that the present result is only internal coherence—undercuts the earlier claim that exclusion has dissolved. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

A more accurate formulation would be:

> Quantum indeterminacy blocks one deterministic formulation of the closure premise. It does not by itself establish mental causation or block probabilistic physical closure.

## 3.3 The required model must be conditional, not merely Born-preserving in aggregate

The article needs a formal distinction between:

$$P(O\mid B)$$

the physical probability of outcome $O$ given brain and environmental state $B$, and

$$P(O\mid B,M)$$

the probability given both the physical state and a putatively irreducible mental state $M$.

A causally effective selector should make some counterfactual difference:

$$P(O\mid B,M_1)\neq P(O\mid B,M_2)$$

for at least some physically comparable contexts. Otherwise $M$ is not doing identifiable selection work.

Preserving the **unconditioned aggregate** Born frequency would not by itself solve the empirical problem. Intention-conditioned biases could cancel when pooled. Conversely, if Born probabilities remain exact under every publicly conditionable mental, behavioural and physical context, the selector becomes causally elusive in precisely the way the article’s “wild coincidences” horn identifies. The changelog shows that the site is already wrestling with the difference between pooled and conditionable Born exactness; that distinction belongs explicitly in the apex. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

A worked proposal therefore needs, at minimum:

- the physical variables over which the alternatives are defined;
- the mental variable that selects;
- a psychophysical transition law;
- a reason-sensitive conditional probability structure;
- a neural amplification pathway;
- consistency with no-signalling and existing interference results;
- conservation accounting for the complete brain–apparatus–environment system;
- and a discriminating prediction or a principled account of why no accessible prediction follows.

Until then, “selection” names the desired causal role.

## 3.4 The strongest physicalist answer is largely omitted

The apex presents the choice as either a quantum gap or a Saad-style dualist pre-emption model. The linked *Mental Causation* article is broader: it discusses Yablo-style proportionality, levels or descriptions, causal delegation and interventionist accounts in which mental variables can be genuine higher-level causes. [mental-causation](/concepts/mental-causation-and-downward-causation/)

A sophisticated nonreductive physicalist need not accept the principle that a complete physical explanation excludes every mental causal explanation. Recent interventionist treatments explicitly explore how physical completeness, nonreduction and mental causal explanation might coexist by rejecting or restricting exclusion. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/59296/chapter/499449385)

The apex therefore does not yet answer the strongest physicalist critic. It answers the narrower critic who accepts:

- causal explanation must be a competition between sufficient causes at a single level;
- mental and physical descriptions cannot be related as levels, realizers or intervention variables;
- and indeterminacy must be filled by another cause.

Those assumptions are precisely what contemporary nonreductive physicalists dispute.

## 3.5 The conservation argument contains a category error

The statement that conservation is “a statistical, local law” tested “at the ensemble level, not trial by trial” should be removed or substantially rewritten.

“Local” and “statistical” are different properties. Local conservation in field theory concerns continuity equations and the transport of conserved quantities through a region. It is not equivalent to preservation of an ensemble mean. Preserving expected energy across many selections does not by itself show that an individual physical interaction satisfies local energy–momentum accounting.

The energy-degenerate case is potentially cleaner: choosing between genuinely energy-degenerate outcomes need not change the measured energy. But the article has not shown that the neurally consequential alternatives it needs are always degenerate, nor that the complete measurement or collapse dynamics conserve energy locally.

Cucu and Pitts do not establish the Map’s “selection without injection” solution. Their conditionality response argues that conservation follows from appropriate symmetries and that a dualist who posits symmetry-breaking interaction may have to accept nonconservation, leaving the issue to empirical study. The apex is right to say it uses this only dialectically, but the paper cannot also serve as support for Tenet 2’s stronger conservation-preserving picture. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13643v1)

## 3.6 Saad is a rival architecture, not confirmation

Saad’s delegatory dualism is relevant and correctly cited, but its evidential role is overstated. Saad distinguishes ordinary closure from observational closure and develops a theory under which experience can do causal work without observable violations. He explicitly treats quantum interactionist dualism as a separate research project with potentially distinctive predictions that are not presently testable. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-025-02290-3)

Saad therefore supplies:

- evidence that the logical space of dualist causal models is wider than simple overdetermination;
- a worked rival using psychophysical laws and delegation;
- and a challenge to overly strong empirical formulations of closure.

He does not supply evidence that consciousness selects quantum outcomes. “Structural analogy” is the appropriate description.

### Verdict on exclusion

The exclusion response succeeds in showing that **Kim’s argument is not a direct logical refutation of every interactionist model**. It does not show that quantum probabilities contain an unoccupied causal slot, that consciousness occupies it, or that overdetermination has dissolved. The article’s later “framework-internal coherence” verdict should govern the entire section.

# 4. Citation, quotation and attribution audit

## 4.1 Major corrections

### Kane 2024 — incorrect

The statement that Kane “rejects the event-causal label” is wrong. Kane calls his position agent-causal/event-causal and says neither component reduces to the other. The current linked source and the changelog already contain the correction. This should be treated as a substantive stance error, not a wording issue. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

### Lowe — materially incomplete

It is correct that Lowe treats substance causation as fundamental. It is incorrect or seriously misleading to invoke him immediately after the claim that the agent directly causes the action or volition without noting that Lowe’s own free volitions are uncaused. The Map’s agent-caused volition is an explicit departure from Lowe, as the current source now recognises. [Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews+2](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/personal-agency-the-metaphysics-of-mind-and-action/)

### Whitehead — false equivalence

The claim that Whitehead reaches Lowe’s conclusion “from different premises” is too strong. Whitehead makes processes or actual occasions, not enduring substances, metaphysically fundamental. His account of self-determining concrescence may offer an **analogy** to primitive self-determination, but it does not reach the Lowean conclusion that all causation is substance causation. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

### Pereboom — overclassified as empirical

The apex calls Pereboom’s case against agent causation “empirical.” The site’s own later review gives a more careful breakdown:

- luck is a conceptual problem;
- the absence of positive reason is an epistemic or parsimony consideration;
- wild coincidence is a likelihood or fit problem.

Only the last two have the broad empirical flavour the apex assigns to the whole case. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

### Sjöberg 2024 — correct citation, overstated evidential description

The bibliographic citation is correct, but the article is classified by *Brain* as an essay, not a new controlled clinical investigation. It discusses SMA resection observations and earlier clinical work. “Reviews clinical observations” or “draws on resection cases” would be better than “adds clinical data.” [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

### Transactional interpretation and TSVF — conflated

The “handshake” of advanced and retarded waves is specific to Cramer’s transactional interpretation and related transactional developments. Aharonov’s two-state-vector formalism is a time-symmetrized formulation for pre- and post-selected ensembles; it should not simply be described as another absorber transaction between backward and forward waves. [APS Journals+1](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/RevModPhys.58.647)

## 4.2 Empirical references that are substantially accurate

The apex handles most of its direct neuroscience and quantum-biology references responsibly:

- Schurger et al. is correctly used to undermine the simple inference from readiness potential to an already completed unconscious decision.
- Rajan et al. is carefully limited to willed attention rather than movement.
- Thura and Cisek is used as evidence of premotor commitment, not as proof of conscious causation.
- Desmurget et al. is correctly presented as an intention/execution dissociation, with the functionalist reading conceded.
- Babcock et al. and Khan et al. are treated as evidence that biologically relevant quantum phenomena deserve investigation, not as demonstrations of cognition-scale coherence or conscious collapse.
- Tegmark’s decoherence estimates are now appropriately divided by physical target rather than represented as one universal brain timescale. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

The article’s calibration around these papers is one of its strongest features.

## 4.3 Missing primary citations

The reference list contains only 17 items despite numerous named theories, arguments and phrase-level attributions. It omits standalone references for, among others:

- van Inwagen’s rollback argument;
- Fischer and Ravizza;
- Wolf;
- Dennett;
- Frankfurt;
- Strawson;
- Anscombe;
- Lowe;
- Whitehead;
- Cramer, Aharonov and Kastner;
- Byrne’s counterfactual work;
- the clinical meta-analyses invoked in the text.

Internal links are useful for navigation but do not replace primary references in an apex article that makes direct historical and interpretive claims. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

Several slogan-like or quotation-marked expressions also need exact sources or page locations: “freedom worth wanting,” “wild coincidences,” “handshakes,” and “concrescence.” I found no clear fabricated extended quotation, but source precision is not yet adequate for the article’s synthetic role.

# 5. Quantum selection and time-collapse caution

## 5.1 Quantum selection: good caution, missing mechanism

The article appropriately states that quantum-biological observations do not demonstrate a neural selector, that amplification is unresolved and that the Born-rule dilemma remains. It also restricts the thesis to collapse-involving readings rather than claiming that all interpretations of quantum mechanics contain the same opening. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

What is still missing is a clean separation among four claims:

1. quantum indeterminacy exists;
2. quantum alternatives remain functionally relevant within a warm brain;
3. microscopic alternatives are amplified into different decisions;
4. consciousness, rather than physical stochastic dynamics, controls the amplification.

Evidence for the first does not establish the second; evidence for the second would not establish the third; and none of the first three establishes the fourth.

The article should also engage the more rigorous consciousness-collapse literature. Chalmers and McQueen illustrate what a genuine research programme requires: an explicit consciousness measure, a collapse dynamics, mathematical consequences and exposure to possible falsification. Their simple models encounter a quantum-Zeno problem, while more complex models remain research proposals rather than established physics. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.02314v1)

## 5.2 Time symmetry is stated too broadly

“Quantum mechanics is time-symmetric at the fundamental level” needs qualification. Some unitary quantum dynamics under time-reversal-invariant Hamiltonians are time symmetric. That does not make every interaction, boundary condition, measurement model or objective-collapse dynamics time symmetric.

The article should use language such as:

> Some formulations of unitary quantum dynamics are time-reversal symmetric, and some interpretations employ both past and future boundary conditions.

That is enough for the speculative argument without making a universal statement.

## 5.3 Atemporal selection does not yet resolve the timing problem

The move from time-symmetric formalism to “the selected transaction includes the neural history that preceded awareness” is not derived from transactional quantum mechanics, TSVF or the neuroscience. It is a new psychophysical proposal.

In particular, the article needs to explain:

- what the future boundary condition is;
- how a conscious intention enters that boundary condition;
- why the construction does not simply postselect a history after the fact;
- how agency is distinguished from consistency across an already fixed history;
- and how ordinary causal and counterfactual dependence are represented.

Without those steps, the account redescribes neural precursors as parts of a selected four-dimensional history. It does not yet show that consciousness authored that history.

The apex is right to subordinate this hypothesis to a forward-time mechanism and to say the central case does not depend on it. That caution should be reflected in the heading: “Speculative temporal extension” would be more accurate than presenting it as the Map’s “fuller resolution.” [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

## 5.4 Collapse and experienced time

The claim that consciousness may locally constitute lived temporal becoming is explicitly marked speculative, and the article correctly denies that consciousness creates the cosmological arrow of time. That is appropriate caution. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

The linked temporal article is less careful. It bundles transactional interpretation, TSVF and Kastner’s possibilist interpretation, and moves from collapse irreversibility to the direction of time and then to phenomenal temporal structure. These are distinct theses:

- an interpretation of quantum probabilities;
- a physical asymmetry in outcome actualisation;
- the thermodynamic arrow;
- neural temporal integration;
- and the phenomenology of retention and anticipation.

No inference from one to the next is presently supplied. The apex improves on the source article by downgrading the claim, but it should not inherit the source’s physics-language as though it supplied a mechanism. [time-collapse-and-agency](/topics/time-collapse-and-agency/)

# 6. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article is unusually transparent about its dependencies. It says:

- the libertarian framing is adopted partly for coherence with the tenets;
- the quantum model is not established mental causation;
- the time interpretation is speculative;
- the rejection of Many Worlds relies on a global-exclusion condition the Map posits rather than derives;
- and Tenet 5 does not answer the wild-coincidence problem. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

That is good methodological practice. The remaining protection occurs in subtler forms.

## 6.1 Failure of the selector is treated as failure only of an implementation

Under “What Would Challenge This View,” the article says the case survives failure of quantum interaction because it rests on the hard problem and the argument from reason. But those arguments, even if successful, would support at most:

- irreducible consciousness;
- perhaps phenomenal intentionality;
- perhaps some form of mental causal relevance.

They do not establish agent causation, open alternatives, quantum selection, a substance-bearing subject or ultimate desert. A failure of every plausible interaction mechanism would therefore leave general dualism standing while defeating the apex’s distinctive account of agency. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

This is a change of thesis under pressure: from “consciousness authors action by quantum selection” to “some arguments for irreducible consciousness survive.”

## 6.2 The falsification threshold is set too high

“Near-perfect prediction” of voluntary choice is presented as the relevant neural challenge. That is an unnecessarily protective threshold. Robust, cross-validated prediction substantially above individual baseline—especially when combined with interventions that manipulate the predicted choice—could count strongly against a selector located after the predictive state, even if prediction never approached perfection.

The classic simple-choice decoding results are indeed imperfect and task-specific; the cited “around 60%” family of findings concerns impoverished binary choices and should not be generalized to reason-sensitive decisions. But the evidential question is not binary between 60% and near-perfect prediction. It is how prediction changes when the experiment controls motives, confidence, deliberation, and opportunities for conscious reconsideration. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

The “around 60%” claim also requires a direct reference in the apex.

## 6.3 Unequal explanatory standards

The article permits “the agent selected” to terminate explanation while saying a materialist solution must “explain rather than redescribe.” This may be defensible, but only under a neutral standard applied to both sides.

The comparison should ask:

- What does each theory take as primitive?
- What counterfactuals does the primitive support?
- What unification does it supply?
- What observations would differ without it?
- Does it compress or merely rename the phenomenon?

Otherwise, a primitive agent-causal relation is accepted as explanation while primitive physical laws or stochastic transitions are dismissed as redescription.

## 6.4 Independent evidence is confused with independent provenance

The article correctly says the neural and clinical findings are compatible with physicalism and do not discriminate between mental and neural causation. It then calls these findings part of a “genuinely independent subset” supporting convergence. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

They are independent of the site’s tenets in the sense that the experiments were not designed to presuppose dualism. They are not independent **evidence for dualism or selection** if competing theories predict them equally well. Independence of provenance is not independent likelihood support.

The clinical source article itself states the point well: the evidence removes a simple epiphenomenalist defeater but does not adjudicate dualist causation against brain-to-brain causation. The apex should preserve that exact evidential grade throughout. [clinical-neuroplasticity-evidence-for-bidirectional-causation](/topics/clinical-neuroplasticity-evidence-for-bidirectional-causation/)

## 6.5 Tenet 2 is sometimes used as an answer rather than a constraint

“Minimal Quantum Interaction” can require that a proposed mechanism avoid net energy injection. It cannot establish that such a mechanism exists or that selection preserves conservation. Phrases such as “the constraint Tenet 2 fixes” risk converting a design requirement into a physical result. [consciousness-and-agency](/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

# Final assessment

The article’s **negative discipline is stronger than its positive case**. It successfully shows that:

- ordinary neuroscience has not proved that conscious awareness is causally idle;
- agent causation is a coherent metaphysical category worthy of separate evaluation;
- deterministic causal closure cannot simply be assumed in every quantum interpretation;
- compatibilism and libertarianism share more of the moral landscape than polemical presentations admit;
- and a dualist selection model is not refuted merely by pointing to microscopic indeterminacy or decoherence.

It does not yet show that:

- the agent-causal primitive defeats rollback luck;
- a nonphysical substance is required for agent causation;
- stochastic physical completeness fails at individual outcomes;
- consciousness changes any quantum conditional probability;
- Born-preserving selection can be causally efficacious while empirically inaccessible;
- conservation is preserved by the proposed process;
- atemporal selection explains neural precursors;
- or agent-causal origination grounds ultimate or retributive desert.

The defensible thesis is therefore narrower:

> Given the Map’s prior tenets, agent-causal selection at quantum indeterminacies is one conditionally coherent model of conscious agency. It currently lacks the psychophysical law, contrastive account of settling, empirical discriminator and conservation analysis required to establish it over physicalist, compatibilist or rival libertarian alternatives.

### Site methodology

23. **Dependency propagation gate:** Automatically flag any apex whose named source article has a later substantive modification date. Block publication until changed claims have been reviewed for propagation.
24. **Position-attribution registry:** Maintain one structured record per philosopher containing work, date, exact position, quotation, page or section, and the site’s interpretation. Use it to prevent Kane-, Lowe- and O’Connor-type stance drift.
25. **Primary-citation coverage check:** Require every named philosopher, direct quotation, named argument and empirical aggregate claim in an apex article to have a primary reference, even when an internal article is also linked.
26. **Claim-status labels:** Mark major assertions as established result, interpretation, conditional model, compatibility evidence, discriminating evidence, speculative extension or tenet commitment.
27. **Opponent-completeness matrix:** For every apex, track the strongest physicalist, compatibilist, illusionist, rival-dualist and rival-libertarian response, together with whether the article refutes, concedes or brackets it.
28. **Primitive-symmetry test:** Whenever the Map permits an irreducible primitive, require a side-by-side comparison with the rival theory’s primitive using the same standards of explanation, counterfactual power and empirical consequence.
29. **Conditional-probability discipline:** For causal claims, require an explicit statement of which observable or latent variables would change under an intervention and which rival models predict the same data.
30. **Evidence independence audit:** Separate independence of data collection, independence of theoretical assumptions and likelihood discrimination. Count only the third as independent evidential convergence.
31. **Prediction ledger:** Record each empirical commitment with its conditioning variables, expected effect, current evidence, possible compensating effects and an advance criterion for revision or abandonment.
32. **Physics review gate:** Require external review for claims involving conservation, decoherence, no-signalling, collapse, Born probabilities, transactional interpretations or time-reversal symmetry.
33. **Moral inference gate:** Prevent automatic movement from metaphysical agency to desert, retribution or mitigation. Require separate control, epistemic and normative arguments.
34. **Apex/source consistency test:** Compare all sentences making stronger claims than their source articles. An apex may simplify a source but should not upgrade “compatible with” to “supports,” “conditional possibility” to “mechanism,” or “analogy” to “same conclusion.”
35. **Adversarial falsification review:** For each tenet-linked theory, ask an external reviewer to identify whether the proposed falsifier is unrealistically strong, whether mechanism failure has been reclassified as optional, and whether the thesis retreats to a broader tenet when its distinctive prediction is challenged.
