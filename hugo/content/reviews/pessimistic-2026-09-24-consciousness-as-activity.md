# Pessimistic Review

**Date**: 2026-09-24
**Content reviewed**: [consciousness-as-activity](/topics/consciousness-as-activity/) (2612 words by `analyze_length`; topics soft limit 3000). Chosen because it is the oldest-deep-reviewed live article with no earlier pessimistic review. Also checked: the sibling [enactivism-challenge-to-interactionist-dualism](/topics/enactivism-challenge-to-interactionist-dualism/) L74 and the seeding research note [research/enactivism-challenge-interactionist-dualism-2026-03-21.md](/research/enactivism-challenge-interactionist-dualism-2026-03-21/), which share one citation defect with it.

**Prior-review fences respected.** The 2026-06-21 deep review's stability notes declare these points settled: the §86/§129 wording, the Nagarjuna non-self reply, the MWI indexical argument, the physicalist co-optation of predictive processing and enactivism, and the whirlpool caveat. This review does not reopen any of them on the same grounds. Issues 2–4 below come from a different angle, the article's own internal consistency. Issue 1 is a verified source defect that no earlier review checked.

## Executive Summary

The article's framing is useful, but four defects need fixing. (1) De Jaegher & Di Paolo's "Enactivism is not interactionism" is cited for a mind–body claim it never makes. The paper is a social-cognition commentary, and its "interactionism" is interaction theory as opposed to mindreading. The same misreading appears in a sibling article and in the research note that seeded both. (2) The agent-dependence commitment ("if consciousness is activity, someone is doing it") contradicts two of the article's own three "philosophical roots". James and Whitehead both hold process views that do without a performer behind the process. (3) The "irreducibility of practice" paragraph uses a descriptive-level non-capture (dancing is not captured by biomechanics) to assert an *ontological* distinction. It also concedes that conscious activity *supervenes on* neural processes, which sits badly with interactionism. (4) The Occam paragraph blames the combination problem on the property framing. But the article's own Whiteheadian root ("experience is what actuality does, all the way down") is the classic home of the combination problem.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
"Your Sciences section enlists predictive processing, enactivism and global workspace ignition as allies of the verb framing. Every one of them is a physicalist research programme, and every one describes *neural* activity. You have shown that consciousness science is activity-first. You have not shown that it is dualist-friendly. If anything, you have shown that the activity framing has already been absorbed by the neuroscience you claim it escapes. 'Brains don't *have* models, they *do* modelling' is my slogan, not yours."
(This overlaps the fenced PP/enactivism co-optation point. The fence holds for the Relation section's category argument. It is recorded here only because the 'Dualism' paragraph's "strengthens the Map's framework at every point" (L124) remains an overclaim that the category/irreducibility separation does not cover. See Language table.)

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
"'Properties don't strain. Activities do' (L77) trades on a pun. Physicalists identified mental *events and processes* with neural events and processes: Kim's event ontology, Davidson's anomalous monism, Smart's 'brain processes'. The identity theory's founding paper is 'Sensations and *Brain Processes*'. Your Property Trap attacks a target that mainstream physicalism mostly never occupied. Moving from noun to verb does not block reduction, because neural activity is also an activity. Your L126 claim that 'activities are not the kind of thing that *can* be identical to physical properties' is true and irrelevant. The physicalist says conscious activity is identical to a physical *activity*."
This is the strongest external objection, and the article never engages the process-identity version of physicalism. See Issue 5.

### The Quantum Skeptic
"You say the minimality constraint 'follows naturally — activities work through their medium, not against it' (L128). That is a slogan. Pushing a boulder is an activity and works against its medium. Nothing about the category 'activity' fixes a magnitude of influence. Your Temporal Constitution paragraph also says consciousness 'exists only as temporal flow' and cannot exist in a time-slice. Yet your mechanism places selection at quantum-indeterminate *junctures*, which are instantaneous events. Which is it?"

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Fenced: the MWI indexical argument is marked bedrock. No new point is added.

### The Empiricist
"The article admits (L55) that noun versus verb is 'underdetermined by the evidence' and earns its place by 'the puzzles it avoids'. Fine. But then the Relation section says the framing 'strengthens the Map's framework at every point'. A reframing chosen for convenience cannot also count as independent support. The four 'candidate features' of experiential activity (L99–L102) are also untestable as stated. What observation would show that an activity is self-referentially integrated but *not* experiential?"

### The Buddhist Philosopher
The non-self reply is fenced. A new point stays within the article's *own* sources: "You call Whitehead, Bergson and James the roots of the activity view. Then you add a doctrine they reject: that every activity needs a performer distinct from it. James wrote that 'the passing Thought then seems to be the Thinker'. Whitehead's subject is a *superject* that arises from the occasion's own concrescence and perishes with it. There is no enduring agent behind the occasion. 'Dancing requires a dancer' is grammar doing metaphysics, which is Lichtenberg's point against 'I think'." See Issue 2.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: De Jaegher & Di Paolo (2013) cited for a claim the paper does not make
- **File**: topics/consciousness-as-activity.md L116; also topics/enactivism-challenge-to-interactionist-dualism.md L74; seeded by research/enactivism-challenge-interactionist-dualism-2026-03-21.md L40–44, L185, L216
- **Location**: "De Jaegher and Di Paolo (2013) make this explicit: the 'interaction' in enactivism refers to organism-environment engagement, not to causal commerce between a non-physical mind and a physical body."
- **Problem**: Verified at frontiersin.org (full text, DOI 10.3389/fnhum.2012.00345). The paper is a general commentary on Bohl & van den Bos (2012), "Toward an integrative account of social cognition: marrying theory of mind and interactionism…". Its "interactionism" is the interaction-based account of *social understanding*, as opposed to mindreading. The authors say Bohl & van den Bos misattribute to them a radical claim that interaction dynamics suffice for all social cognition. The paper says nothing about mind–body dualism or non-physical causation. Term counts in the commentary body: "social" 21, "mindreading" 9, "Cartesian" 0, "mind-body" 0, "non-physical" 0. The single "dualis" hit is inside "individualism". The research note also adds a third author, Gallagher. Crossref lists only De Jaegher and Di Paolo, so the article's two-author form is correct. Metadata is otherwise fine (Front. Hum. Neurosci. 6:345; volume-year 2012, published 3 January 2013).
- **Severity**: High (citation framing: the source exists and the metadata is right, but the claim credited to it is not there)
- **Recommendation**: Remove the attribution. The substantive point is still true of enactivism and can stand without a citation, or be credited to Thompson 2007 on the dissolution of the mind–body problem (already in the References). If a citation is kept, describe the paper correctly: "the enactive approach is not the 'interactionism' of social-cognition debates either". Or drop it and the reference entry. Apply the same fix at the sibling's L74 and correct the research note (text and authorship). Sync both trees.

### Issue 2: Agent-dependence contradicts the article's own philosophical roots
- **File**: topics/consciousness-as-activity.md
- **Location**: L85 ("Activities require agents… If consciousness is activity, someone is doing it"), L116 ("Activities require agents. Dancing is not self-performing"), set against L63–L65 (Whitehead) and L73–L77 (James)
- **Problem**: The article presents Whitehead and James as roots of the activity view. Both decline to posit a performer standing apart from the activity. James does so on grounds of theoretical economy, Whitehead as a matter of doctrine. James: "The passing Thought then seems to be the Thinker"; "that thought is itself the thinker, and psychology need not look beyond" (*Principles*, Vol. 1, Ch. X; verified in the Gutenberg text). He allows that there *may* be a non-phenomenal Thinker behind it, but says the phenomena do not require one. Whitehead: the subject is the superject of its own concrescence and does not endure beyond the occasion. The inference "activity → agent" is also the grammatical-subject move Lichtenberg criticised in Descartes' "I think". The article never argues for it; it rests on the examples of dancing and running. This is not the fenced Nagarjuna point. The defect is that the article's own sources contradict it, and the enactivism reply at L116 depends on the same premise.
- **Severity**: High (internal contradiction in a premise the enactivism reply and the anti-eliminativist claim both depend on)
- **Recommendation**: Add one or two sentences noting that the process tradition itself splits on this point. James and Whitehead let the activity constitute its subject; the Map's version posits a subject that performs it. Then give the reason for the Map's choice, for example the agent-causal selection that the Bidirectional tenet requires, or the unity of a single selecting subject across occasions. Soften "Activities require agents" to a claim argued for rather than stipulated. Link [agent-causation](/concepts/agent-causation/).

### Issue 3: Epistemic-to-metaphysical slide, plus a supervenience concession, in "Irreducibility of practice"
- **File**: topics/consciousness-as-activity.md L89
- **Location**: "conscious activity supervenes on neural processes but is not captured by their description. The explanatory gap reflects a genuine ontological distinction… not a mere epistemic limitation."
- **Problem**: (a) Equivocation. The evidence offered, that dancing "is not captured by biomechanical description", is a claim about levels of description. Dancing is fully physical (the article admits as much for the whirlpool at L53), so the analogy supports only the *epistemic* reading. It is then used to assert the *metaphysical* one. The analogy cannot tell the two readings apart, because the physical case it uses fits the epistemic reading. (b) Tension with the tenets. If conscious activity supervenes on neural processes, then fixing the neural facts fixes it, and consciousness cannot select among neural outcomes the physics leaves open. That is the core of Tenet 3 as the article itself states it at L130. The claim that "you cannot reduce an activity to its material substrate" is also false as a general rule (whirlpools, digestion).
- **Severity**: High (interpretive equivocation in a premise the Dualism argument depends on; possible conflict with a tenet)
- **Recommendation**: Replace "supervenes on" with "is realised through" or "operates on", or state explicitly which supervenience relation, if any, the Map accepts. Rewrite the last sentence so that the ontological reading rests on the hard-problem arguments (link [explanatory-gap](/concepts/explanatory-gap/)), with the dance analogy illustrating the *category* point only. For example: "The dance analogy shows only that activity-talk is not biomechanics-talk; that the conscious case is ontologically, not merely descriptively, distinct rests on the arguments of the explanatory gap." Qualify "you cannot reduce an activity to its substrate" to "not every activity reduces to its substrate".

### Issue 4: The combination problem is blamed on the wrong framing
- **File**: topics/consciousness-as-activity.md L134 vs L65
- **Location**: Occam paragraph: the property framing "generates the hard problem, the explanatory gap, and the combination problem". Whitehead paragraph: "experience is what actuality does, all the way down."
- **Problem**: The combination problem is a problem for panpsychism and panexperientialism, including Whitehead's activity ontology (how micro-occasions compose a macro-subject). A property dualist who puts consciousness only at the organism level faces no combination problem. The article therefore charges its rival with a problem that belongs mainly to one of its own roots. L65 also says the hard problem "doesn't arise" for Whitehead, which clashes with L55 ("not that the hard problem dissolves entirely").
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Drop "the combination problem" from L134, or say that the activity framing inherits it in Whiteheadian form. At L65, add "on Whitehead's view" before "the hard problem doesn't arise", and note that the Map does not adopt panexperientialism wholesale.

### Issue 5: The process-identity version of physicalism is never engaged
- **File**: topics/consciousness-as-activity.md L47, L126
- **Problem**: The article's case against physicalism answers only property identity. Identity theory in its original form (Place 1956; Smart 1959, "Sensations and Brain Processes") and Kim's event ontology identify mental *processes and events* with neural ones. L126 ("activities are not the kind of thing that *can* be identical to physical properties") is valid but does not address the opponent. This is a mild case of **missed unsupported-move identification**. The in-framework reply is available: process identity still owes the bridge explaining why *this* neural activity is experiential, which is the article's own L93 reformulation.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Add two or three sentences to "The Property Trap" or the Dualism paragraph acknowledging process-identity physicalism. Then argue that the verb shift leaves the gap in place for it too ("why is *this* activity experiential?"). This makes the reframing neutral ground on which the dualist arguments still apply, not an argument against physicalism in itself. Softening L124 "at every point" follows naturally.

## Counterarguments to Address

### "Activities cannot be inert by definition" (L87)
- **Current content says**: Activities, unlike properties, cannot be epiphenomenal.
- **A critic would argue**: An activity can be causally idle *with respect to a given effect*. A shadow's moving is an activity-like process that moves nothing. Epiphenomenalists can simply redescribe their view as "experiencing is a causally idle process". The claim holds by definition only if "activity" already means "causally efficacious doing", which assumes the point at issue.
- **Suggested response**: Change "by definition" to a weaker claim: the activity reading makes epiphenomenalism *unnatural*, since performing something and doing nothing sit badly together, but not incoherent. The next sentence already calls this defeater-removal, so the change fits the article's own hedging.

### Temporal constitution vs momentary selection
- **Current content says**: Consciousness "exists only as temporal flow"; no time-slice contains it (L83). Selection happens "at quantum-indeterminate junctures" (L130).
- **A critic would argue**: Momentary selections are what the mechanism needs, and an entity that cannot exist at an instant cannot act at one.
- **Suggested response**: One sentence stating that selection is an event *within* a temporally thick activity, the way a dancer's footfall happens at a moment inside the dance. Link [temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency](/topics/temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency/).
