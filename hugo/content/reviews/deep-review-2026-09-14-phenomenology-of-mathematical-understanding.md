## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found
- **Secondary-source paraphrase presented as a primary quotation (×2).** L118 read: *Hardy and Littlewood judged some profoundly original, some already known, and "others incorrect as stated."* L120 read: *Hardy, an atheist, dismissed the Namagiri story as "a fable" and admitted he never actually asked Ramanujan about the source of his inspiration.* Both quoted phrases are verbatim from Krishnaswami Alladi's *Inference* essay "Touched by the Goddess" ("Several of the formulas … some were already well known, and others incorrect as stated"; "Hardy, a sworn atheist, dismissed the story … as a fable"), which the article does not cite. Neither phrase occurs in Hardy 1940 (checked against the IIT-Kanpur excerptise: 0 hits for "incorrect", 0 for "fable"). Hardy's own words on the letter are "(1.8) is classical … (1.9) occurs in a paper published by Rogers in 1907" and, of (1.14)–(1.15), "one is false and the other, as stated, misleading"; his own admission is "I saw Ramanujan almost every day, and could have cleared up most of the obscurity by a little cross-examination. … I hardly asked him a single question of this kind." **Resolution**: both sentences rewritten around Hardy's verbatim wording; the "never asked about the source of his inspiration" gloss is replaced by the cross-examination admission, which is what the "did not interrogate it" inference in the next sentence actually rests on. The 06-03 ledger's line ("the Hardy-as-atheist 'fable' / never-asked-the-source facts were web-verified against the literature") is the *the-secondary-source-that-seeded-the-paraphrase-ratifies-it* pattern: the facts are true, the quotation marks were not.

### Medium Issues Found
- **Internal tension between the Universality Problem and the methodological caveat.** L54 asserted that mathematicians of different traditions are "reporting qualitatively distinct experiential structures"; L58 then states that for non-Western traditions there are no first-person reports, only method texts and commentary. A tenet-accepting reviewer would flag the first as overstated relative to the second. **Resolution**: L54 now attributes the variation to "the traditions surveyed below … as far as their texts show"; the parallel "universally reported activity" in the Tenet-3 paragraph became "universally described".
- **Jain infinity overclaim.** "sophisticated treatments of infinity and transfinite numbers centuries before Cantor" — the popular-literature claim (Joseph, *Crest of the Peacock*, does make it) outruns what the Jain classification of *asaṃkhyāta*/*ananta* into orders of infinity supports; "transfinite numbers" imports Cantor's cardinal arithmetic. **Resolution**: now "a hierarchy of distinct orders of infinity centuries before Cantor", which is what the Surya Prajñapti-tradition texts actually exhibit.
- **Missing fifth convergent feature.** The 2026-08-25 formal-authority-wing optimistic review identified the "Phenomenology of the Edge" section of [formal-cognitive-limits](/voids/formal-cognitive-limits/) as first-person data of exactly the kind the Convergence Beneath Variation list collects, with no link in either direction. **Resolution**: added as the fifth bullet ("A negative phenomenology at the limits") with a heading-anchor link, plus a reciprocal Further Reading entry in the void.
