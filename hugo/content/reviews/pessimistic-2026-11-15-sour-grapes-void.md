## Executive Summary

Quotation fidelity is good: every Elster, Scheler and Sen quotation I could test matched the source text word for word, and the eleven external references that carry DOIs match their Crossref records. The defects are in what the article does with the sources.

1. **The article inverts the variable in Elster's one operational test.** It says the adapted subject is "the one for whom *x* is out of reach". In Elster's sentence *x* is the thing wanted, which the subject is free to do; what the adapted subject lacks is the freedom *not* to do *x*. Elster's next paragraph says that wanting an *x* one is not free to do is "an alternative proof" that the want is not adaptive, which is the opposite of the article's gloss.
2. **Ratings are promoted to feelings in the [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) paragraph.** Kay and colleagues measured "judgments of the desirability" and Laurin and colleagues measured how favourably restrictions were viewed and how much freedoms were valued. The article says both "found felt desirability". Its own Scheler section distinguishes talk that shifts first from feeling that shifts later, so the page contradicts its own distinction at the point where the Map's position is at stake.
3. **The "imagining is no substitute" step misreads the Laurin design.** Reactance appeared under a restriction with a small chance of lapsing. An option represented as plainly open was the control condition, with neither effect.
4. **Scheler's law is misplaced in his book.** The article says he "states a general law before it turns to the syndrome of its title". The law arrives about twenty pages into chapter I, which is on ressentiment from its first page, and Scheler introduces it as "an additional psychological law" inside that discussion.

None of these changes the page's disclaimer that the void is framework-independent and no evidence for dualism. Item 2 weakens the confound the page raises against the Map's own value-sensitive selection, so fixing it is a move in the Map's favour and should be made for accuracy, not for that reason.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would accept most of the page and ask what "want" is doing. The data are ratings moving with likelihood. That is a regularity in report behaviour with a plausible dissonance-reduction mechanism. Talk of a want with a "genealogy" that the subject "cannot see" presupposes a determinate inner item with a history. If preference talk is a coarse folk gloss on valuation circuitry, there is no hidden fact about whether *this* want was adapted, only a model of how valuation responds to feasibility. The page's own sibling, the preference void, concedes half of this.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would say the page describes ordinary heterophenomenology and then calls the result a void. The subject's report is one data stream, the cross-condition comparison another, and the second corrects the first. Nothing is occluded in a deeper sense than any other fact about one's own cognition that needs an experiment. He would press the sentence "nothing marks it": the article concedes that no study asked, so the sentence reports the limits of a literature search as a fact about minds.

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark has little to attack, since the page disclaims any bearing on Minimal Quantum Interaction. He would note the one place the page touches selection: if the options "reach it already weighted by a feasibility process", then the selector's contribution is whatever is left after a classical valuation process has run, and the page offers no way to size that remainder.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch would pass. The page says No Many Worlds has no bearing, which is correct.

### The Empiricist
The Popperian complaint is the strongest of the six. The central claim, that adaptation leaves no felt mark, rests on an untested absence, and the Colburn passage makes part of it true by definition. The page admits both. What it does not do is say what observation would count against the void. A direct awareness probe after a Kay-style manipulation is named in the research note as the cleanest test and does not appear in the article. Adjacent literature may already hold relevant data: the classic dissonance studies on recall of one's own pre-manipulation attitude (Bem and McConnell 1970; Goethals and Reckman 1973) are remembered here as finding that subjects misrecall their earlier attitude as matching their new one. That is offered as a lead from memory and was **not** verified at source in this review.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would question the contrast the page depends on. "Autonomous" wants are also conditioned: by upbringing, by what was available to be wanted, by prior wants. Elster says he cannot supply a criterion for autonomy, and the page quotes him saying so. Without that criterion the adapted want is a special case of the dependent arising of all wants, and the void is the ordinary opacity of conditioning seen at one of its joints. The page files Elster's "buddhist" character planning as the transparent alternative; a Madhyamaka reader would say the planner's second-order want has a genealogy too.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The freedom-to-do-otherwise test is glossed with its variable inverted
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/sour-grapes-void.md`
- **Location**: §The Test Changes What It Tests, first paragraph. OLD text (occurs once): "The adapted subject is by hypothesis the one for whom *x* is out of reach."
- **Problem**: Elster's sentence, quoted correctly just before, is "If I want to do x, and am free to do x, and free not to do x, then my want cannot be shaped by necessity". *x* is what the subject wants and can do. The adapted subject is the one who wants *x* and is not free not to do it. Elster continues: "An alternative proof that my want to do x is not shaped by the lack of alternatives would be that I am not free to do x." On his account an *x* that is wanted and out of reach is certified non-adaptive, so the article's gloss describes Elster's clearest unadapted case as the adapted one. The lead's phrasing ("needs the option the adapted subject lacks") is correct, because the missing option is the alternative to *x*. Only this sentence is wrong.
- **A consequence the page does not draw**: the test does certify wants. Any want for something I am free to take and free to leave "cannot be shaped by necessity". The void is therefore confined to wants whose alternatives are closed, and wants for the unattainable fall outside it. The page's "None certifies a particular want as unadapted" is true of the structural substitutes it is attached to, but a reader will take it as covering the main test as well.
- **Severity**: High. It is the page's central mechanism and the sentence contradicts the source two lines after quoting it.
- **Recommendation**: Replace with: "The adapted subject is by hypothesis the one who wants *x* and is not free not to do it; for the fox, *x* is going without the grapes. Elster adds that still wanting what is out of reach is evidence against adaptation." (+27 words.) The next sentence ("Running the test means making the option available") then reads correctly, with "the option" as the closed alternative.

### Issue 2: Rated desirability is reported as felt desirability where [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) is engaged
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/sour-grapes-void.md`
- **Location**: §Relation to Site Perspective, second paragraph. OLD text (occurs once): "Kay and colleagues found felt desirability tracking perceived likelihood, and Laurin and colleagues found it tracking the absoluteness of a constraint."
- **Problem**: This is the epistemic-versus-metaphysical check applied to a measurement. Kay et al. report that "people adjusted their judgments of the desirability of a future event". Laurin et al. report that participants "viewed the restrictions more favorably, and valued the restricted freedoms less". Both are ratings. [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1)'s currency is "the *presently felt* valence of anticipating each outcome", an occurrent state. The confound the paragraph raises needs the feeling to move, and the evidence shows the report moving. The page's own §Two Depths gives the reason the two can come apart: Scheler's "At first, such statements are only supposed to deceive the 'spectators'", which the article glosses as "Some sour-grapes talk is therefore face-saving and no evidence of an adapted want". The conditional that follows ("If felt anticipatory valence is partly a function of perceived feasibility") is properly hedged; the sentence before it has already asserted the antecedent.
- **Severity**: High for calibration, because the sentence states a finding the studies did not make, in the paragraph that bears on a registered position.
- **Recommendation**: Replace with: "Kay and colleagues found rated desirability tracking perceived likelihood, and Laurin and colleagues found stated valuation tracking the absoluteness of a constraint. Both are reports, and Scheler's first depth allows a report to shift before the feeling does." (+17 words.) The following conditional stands unchanged and has no pronoun depending on the replaced sentence.

### Issue 3: The inference from Laurin et al. to "imagining is no substitute" does not fit the design
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/sour-grapes-void.md`
- **Location**: §The Test Changes What It Tests, first paragraph. OLD text (occurs once): "Imagining the option open is plausibly no substitute, since representing it as attainable is the condition that, on that result, pushes valuation the other way."
- **Problem**: The reactance condition in Laurin, Kay and Fitzsimons was a restriction "described as nonabsolute (i.e., as having a small chance of not coming into effect)". Both effects are measured "compared with control participants", for whom there was no restriction. An option represented as attainable is the control, where valuation was the baseline. The result supports "a threatened freedom is valued more", and does not support "an option imagined as open is valued more". The word "plausibly" hedges the conclusion but the "since" clause states the support as if it were the finding. The same paragraph's earlier use of the result ("swing the counteradaptive way that Laurin and colleagues found when a restriction became merely probable") is accurate.
- **Severity**: Medium. The unexplorable residue the lead advertises depends on the test having no imaginative substitute, and this is the only argument given.
- **Recommendation**: Replace with: "Imagining the option open is plausibly no substitute, though nothing cited here tests it: the reactance condition was a restriction that might lapse, and a plainly open option was the control." (+6 words.) The closing sentence about the mutation void still follows from the paragraph's first half.

### Issue 4: Scheler's law is said to precede the treatment of ressentiment; it sits inside it
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/sour-grapes-void.md`
- **Location**: §Two Depths, first sentence. OLD text (occurs once): "states a general law before it turns to the syndrome of its title:"
- **Problem**: In the text consulted, chapter I ("On the Phenomenology and Sociology of Ressentiment") opens at about 2% of the file and the law appears at about 20%, after the page marker for p. 22. It is introduced with "Ressentiment can account for important developments in the history of moral judgments as well as for small everyday events. However, we must introduce an additional psychological law." Scheler brings the law in to explain how ressentiment falsifies values, and the first, object-level depth is itself described as a stage of ressentiment ("not yet the most characteristic achievement of ressentiment"). The research note's guard was to keep the general law separate from the syndrome. The article keeps them separate by a claim about the book's order that the book does not bear out.
- **Severity**: Medium. A reader who opens Scheler finds the structural claim false on the page cited, and the separation of ordinary adaptation from ressentiment is the article's, which should be said.
- **Recommendation**: Replace with: "introduces, well into its account of the syndrome of its title, what it calls "an additional psychological law":" (+5 words; the quoted phrase occurs once in the source text). The next paragraph's "The second depth Scheler reserves for ressentiment proper" should then be read again by the refine pass: on Scheler's text both depths belong to ressentiment and the reservation is the article's own reading.

### Issue 5 (not on the priority list): "Relief is the only event"
- **Location**: §Two Depths, end of first paragraph. "Relief is the only event, and relief ordinarily reads as resolution."
- **Problem**: Scheler's next sentence after the quoted one is "Thus our vital energy and feeling of power rises by several degrees, though on an illusory basis." He reports a second felt change. "Only" is the article's and it is an absence claim the source contradicts. It also sits awkwardly with the lead's "no source surveyed here reports a felt cue": Scheler reports a lift, and later a residue that "may still shine through".
- **Severity**: Low to medium.
- **Recommendation**: "Scheler reports relief and a lift in the feeling of power; neither is marked as an adjustment." (About +6 words.)

### Issue 6 (not on the priority list): Colburn's definition is stretched to "by definition ... character planning"
- **Location**: §The Test Changes What It Tests, last paragraph. "has, by definition, moved it into character planning".
- **Problem**: Colburn's abstract defines adaptive preferences by covert influence and says the account distinguishes them from character planning. It does not say that a noticed and endorsed adaptation becomes character planning. Elster's character planning is deliberate: "If ... I perceive that I am frustrated and understand why, I may deliberately set out to change my wants". A want that was adapted behind one's back and then noticed and endorsed was never intentionally shaped. On Colburn's definition it stops being adaptive; whether it is character planning is a further step the abstract does not take. Colburn was read at abstract level only, so "by definition" claims more than the reading supports.
- **Severity**: Low to medium.
- **Recommendation**: "has, on that definition, a want that no longer counts as adaptive" (about +3 words).

## Counterarguments to Address

### The void may be narrower than the page says
- **Current content says**: the one decisive test needs an option the adapted subject lacks, and the substitutes only shift suspicion.
- **A critic would argue**: Elster's test certifies every want whose object one can take or leave, and his "alternative proof" certifies every want for the unattainable. Most everyday wants fall in one class or the other. What remains is the class of wants for the only available thing.
- **Suggested response**: state the scope in one sentence in §The Test Changes What It Tests. Issue 1's fix supplies half of it.

### Report shift versus felt shift
- **Current content says**: the adjustment "arrives as an ordinary preference".
- **A critic would argue**: every study cited measures ratings. Scheler supplies the only claim that feeling itself shifts, and the page says his claim is untested.
- **Suggested response**: Issue 2's fix at the [P-VS1](/positions/value-in-selection/#p-vs1) paragraph. The body sections already say "judgments" where they quote.

### Absence of a mark is inferred, then asserted
- **Current content says**: the lead is careful ("no source surveyed here reports a felt cue"; "Unawareness is inferred from its absence in reports"). Later sentences are flat: "once the rationalisation has run, nothing marks it"; "The insider cannot see the adaptation"; "noticing requires the access the void denies".
- **A critic would argue**: the later sentences state as a fact about minds what the lead states as a gap in a literature search.
- **Suggested response**: soften the first of the three (see Language Improvements). The other two can stand once the first is scoped.
