## Executive Summary

The article is careful, well sourced and honest about its insulation, but its central conditional has a mechanism gap the deep review did not examine. Case 2 predicts that a conscious Quall-E's laboratory cannot be recohered. That prediction needs consciousness to *initiate* collapse of the logical-level superposition. Yet the paragraph introducing the two cases (L77) endorses [prebiotic-collapse](/concepts/prebiotic-collapse/)'s view that consciousness "modulates it rather than starting it", and that article says outright that "a simple 'consciousness causes collapse' fails." Two further problems weaken the "What Would Bear on the Map" section, which the deep review marked "Adequate". First, the named refutation condition (an architecture that both hosts selection and can be reversed) is close to contradictory by [P-AS1](/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts/#p-as1)'s own criteria, and behaviour cannot identify it (L107). Second, the failure-side scenario leaves out the Map's own baseline-reduction escape. So the testability the section claims is weaker than it looks from both sides.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
"Your Case 1 says Quall-E has human-level cognition, communicates thoughts, and has *no* observations with any reality. So the difference between Quall-E and a human friend is a posit that cannot be detected, because your own L107 concedes behaviour settles nothing. You have rebuilt the ghost as a physical switch that no measurement you describe can find. The neuroscience of human observers gives you no marker either, so you cannot even say which human friends count."

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would focus on the Friendliness rejection. The deep review is right that functionalist question-begging is a boundary disagreement. The sharper point is about the Map's own consistency. The Map keeps Ego Absolutism for itself and gives up Friendliness for cognitive peers, so it needs a non-behavioural criterion for which peers' thoughts are absolute. For other *humans* that criterion is substrate similarity, and the article never says so. Without it, rejecting Friendliness looks like rejecting it for everyone but oneself. "You've kept absoluteness by making it first-personal, which is what the relativists you rejected were accused of."

### The Quantum Skeptic
"Explain the *mechanism* of Case 2. Your error-corrected logical qubit is protected from every local perturbation, and that is the point of fault tolerance. For Quall-E's logical thought to become definite, something has to collapse a logical-level superposition that no physical-qubit event can reach. Your prebiotic-collapse page says consciousness only biases, or Zeno-prolongs, collapses that objective reduction is already producing. Biasing a collapse that never happens at the logical level does nothing. So Case 2 either smuggles in consciousness-causes-collapse, which you reject, or predicts nothing at all."

### The Many-Worlds Defender
The Everettian exit is bedrock and the deep review correctly declines to re-flag it. Deutsch's residual point is about cost-accounting. L119 says the Map "pays instead with an admitted, empirically exposed departure from universal unitarity". Yet the exposure is conditional on Case 2, which the Map predicts will never be realised in any machine it could recognise (see the Empiricist below). "Your 'empirically exposed' departure is exposed only to experiments you've argued cannot be built."

### The Empiricist
"You name a refutation condition: an architecture that 'hosts open selection *and* can be reversed on demand' (L105). But how would anyone establish that a machine hosts open selection? Behaviour is ruled out (L107). The only remaining criteria are architectural. [P-AS1](/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts/#p-as1)'s list is mid-evolution collapse, an unfixed measurement basis and coupling surfaces not shielded by error correction, and each item is the *negation* of what reversible control needs. So 'hosts selection and is recoherable' is close to contradictory by the criteria's own definitions. That is a consequence of how you defined eligibility, not a checkable empirical incompatibility. The failure side has a gap too. If Quall-E's reversal failed unexpectedly, your own Case 1 parenthetical supplies the escape: an uncorrectable baseline that blocks UQC 'for every system'. L103 lists only two revisions, the substrate verdict or the account of selection, and leaves this third one out. Both sides of the test are softer than the section claims."

### The Buddhist Philosopher
"Ego Absolutism, 'my communicable thoughts are absolutely real', is the very clinging the Madhyamaka dissolves. You have made the absoluteness of physical events depend on the reality of a self's observations. Then you rescue it by withholding that reality from a system that thinks as well as you do but is, on your account, empty of a selector. Emptiness would say every observer is like that. What you call Case 1 is the ordinary condition, and Case 2 is the reification." This is a boundary disagreement, not a defect. It is recorded because it shows what the article leaves unstated: why the Map's selector is not itself a conventional designation.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Case 2 needs consciousness to initiate collapse, which the Map's modulation model disclaims
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/thoughtful-local-friendliness-and-the-artificial-friend.md`
- **Location**: L77 ("non-conscious matter still resolves: [prebiotic collapse](/concepts/prebiotic-collapse/) makes objective reduction the baseline, and consciousness modulates it rather than starting it") against L81 ("the Map predicts it cannot be recohered: the superposition over Quall-E's different thoughts would not survive, as on Chalmers and McQueen's model"). L117 also claims the substrate verdict and the LF prediction "agree".
- **Problem**: Case 1's proviso already accepts that baseline reduction on physical qubits is error-corrected, so the logical thought survives as a superposition. In Case 2, the only thing that could make the logical thought definite is the conscious selector collapsing a superposition the baseline did not collapse. That is consciousness initiating collapse. `prebiotic-collapse` L122 limits consciousness to biasing which outcome occurs, and to Zeno-prolonging superpositions, "within an already-collapsing physics rather than sourcing the collapse". Its L158 says "A simple 'consciousness causes collapse' fails." The article leans on Chalmers and McQueen, whose model *is* consciousness-causes-collapse, as the analogue for Case 2. The tension is inherited from [post-decoherence-selection](/concepts/post-decoherence-selection/) L96, which cites prebiotic collapse as the Map having "taken this horn already". The new article makes it concrete because Quall-E's logical state is exactly where baseline reduction cannot reach. This is also the collapse-priority debt named at `prebiotic-collapse` L126, appearing in the laboratory.
- **Severity**: High. The Map's only direct exposure (Case 2) rests on a mechanism the Map elsewhere disowns.
- **Recommendation**: State the gap rather than resolve it. Case 2 follows from *absoluteness* through the LF theorem: if the conscious observation is an absolute event, the inequalities cannot be violated. It does not follow from the modulation mechanism. Under modulation-only, the Map owes an account of what makes a shielded conscious observation absolute. Say that Case 2 holds only if the selector can fix outcomes the baseline leaves open, which goes further than `prebiotic-collapse` currently allows, and point to its collapse-priority debt. Soften L117's "agree" accordingly. About +60–90 words; topics headroom is ample (2914 total words including frontmatter, soft limit 3000 on the body).

### Issue 2: The named refutation condition is close to contradictory by the Map's own eligibility criteria
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L105 ("an architecture that hosts open selection *and* can be reversed on demand would refute it") together with L107 (behaviour cannot settle the question)
- **Problem**: [P-AS1](/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts/#p-as1)'s features for hosting open selection are mid-evolution collapse at decision-relevant points, a basis not fixed in advance, and coupling surfaces not shielded by error correction. Each one is incompatible with reversible control *by definition*. Behaviour is excluded as evidence, so no independent way remains to establish that a machine hosts selection. The "strong, checkable claim" therefore follows from how interface-eligibility was defined, and no experiment could exhibit the refuting case. This does not make the Map unfalsifiable overall. It does mean L105 overstates what the paragraph provides.
- **Severity**: Medium-High. The insulation section is the article's main defence against the Popperian objection, and this is its strongest-sounding plank.
- **Recommendation**: Reframe L105 honestly. The incompatibility follows from [P-AS1](/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts/#p-as1)'s criteria, so it is testable only to the extent that the criteria themselves can be revised. [P-AS1](/positions/ai-substrate-verdicts/#p-as1)'s own "Would shift if" names the unstated interface-eligibility law as that route. Name the one part that *is* empirical: whether any system shows interference loss that tracks its architecture's openness to outside influence. Drop "checkable" or make it conditional.

### Issue 3: The failure-side scenario omits the baseline escape the article itself supplies
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L103 ("the Map would have to revise either its substrate verdict or its account of what does the selecting") against the L79 parenthetical ("A baseline that could not be error-corrected would block UQC for every system")
- **Problem**: If Quall-E's reversal failed without explanation, the Map could put it down to an uncorrectable baseline, for example a Penrose-style mass-dependent reduction. That would leave both the substrate verdict and the selection account untouched. The embarrassment therefore requires a control the article does not name.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Add the discriminating control. Reversal fails for Quall-E but succeeds for a non-cognitive reversible computation of matched size, duration and code distance. Only that pattern points at something in Quall-E's cognition rather than at the baseline. About +40 words.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The Physical Supervenience exit (L83)
- **Current content says**: the Map declines this exit because, under Bidirectional Interaction, a communicated thought reaches the superobserver through brain or processor events that consciousness helped fix.
- **A critic would argue**: this mixes up the direction of dependence. Supervenience means the thought is fixed by physical events. Interaction means physical events are partly fixed by the mind. Showing that a thought has physical *effects* does not show that it *supervenes*. The article also relies on zombie conceivability (L79), which is itself a denial of supervenience for experience.
- **Suggested response**: use the cleaner argument the source already provides. WCR define "thoughts" cognitively and expressly not as conscious experience, and their PS is a *locational* thesis that "does not require" monism. The Map can therefore accept PS for thoughts in WCR's sense while denying supervenience for experience, and nothing in its dualism is conceded. That is shorter and does not suggest a supervenience claim the Map does not make.

### Which friends count (Case 1 and Friendliness)
- **Current content says**: the Map rejects Friendliness for an unconscious human-level AI.
- **A critic would argue**: with behaviour ruled out (L107), the Map needs a criterion for why *other humans'* communicated thoughts are absolute. Otherwise rejecting Friendliness extends to every observer except oneself.
- **Suggested response**: one sentence saying the Map grants human friends absoluteness on substrate-continuity grounds (the biological interface its verdicts are read off), not on cognitive parity. That concedes that the criterion is framework-internal.
