## Executive Summary

The independence-scoring programme grew a third and fourth worked exhibit on 2026-05-27. The apex was updated the same day and now carries the correct, calibrated position. **The dedicated concept page that bills itself as the criteria's "single referential anchor" (L41) was not updated, and neither was the methodology page that the new exhibit explicitly names as "the home of the N=2 burden this case was built to address."** Both were deep-reviewed after the change — the concept page twice, on 2026-06-02 and 2026-07-16 — and the staleness survived both passes.

The count mismatch (two exhibits versus four) is the visible symptom. The substantive defect is a **calibration inversion**: `apophatic-cartography-four-criteria.md` L102 asserts flatly that "the criteria are not a flat instrument," and L120 uses that assertion to *dismiss one of its own falsification conditions*. The apex L168 and the medium-status exhibit L113 both explicitly downgrade that exact claim to *"provisional with a rubric, pending independent grading,"* naming the unresolved same-hand circularity. The consolidating page therefore asserts as settled precisely what its own sources mark as open — and does so in the section headed "Honest Bounds," two paragraphs after stating the rule it breaks.

A second finding is a **verified negative worth recording**: three articles each call themselves "the third worked exhibit," which looked like an indexing collision. It is not. Each scopes its ordinal to a distinct grain — per-cluster/void-cluster, meta-argument, transmission-channel — and each says so in its own sentence. The programme's ordinals are sound and should not be "fixed."

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Empiricist (Karl Popper's Ghost)

This is where the cluster is weakest, and the defect is textbook. `apophatic-cartography-four-criteria.md` has a section titled "What Would Challenge the Criteria" — exactly the right instinct, and rarer than it should be. Then at L120 it lists a falsification condition and immediately discharges it in a parenthesis:

> two clusters with substantively different empirical-anchor profiles produced indistinguishable scores (**the two worked exhibits produce visibly different scores, so this does not currently hold**)

The evidence used to discharge the condition is the two exhibits that the programme's own documents say *cannot* discharge it. `medium-status-cluster-independence-scoring.md` L37: "both produced scores consistent with prior catalogue framing, so the discrimination they displayed was consistent with two readings at once: *the methodology works*, and *the methodology rationalises decisions the cataloguer had already made*." The apex L168 says the same in one clause: "all three scored *with* the cataloguer's prior."

So the falsification condition is dismissed by an appeal that the framework has already conceded is question-begging. A criterion that can only be undermined by evidence the framework has pre-declared inadmissible is not a falsification condition; it is a formality. The irony is that the *correct* answer exists in the corpus and is better than the page's — the medium-status exhibit returned *moderate / moderate / weak / weak* against a rubric fixed before the anchors were examined, which is a real, partial, honestly-bounded discharge. The page is under-claiming the good work and over-claiming the conclusion at the same time.

### The Eliminative Materialist (Patricia Churchland)

Four criteria, two indicators, a five-to-four consolidation, three grains, a pre-registered rubric, per-tradition asymmetric discounts, and a five-tier evidential scale — this is a great deal of apparatus for deciding which of your explanatory failures are permanent features of reality. Neuroscience does not need a rubric to tell it that phlogiston was ignorance and thermodynamics was structure; the science settled it. The apparatus's own honest bound at L116 ("the criteria are calibration, not proof") concedes it cannot do the work its existence implies. What it can do is make a catalogue of current ignorance look like a catalogue of discovered boundaries, which is precisely the conversion I would predict from a framework that needs voids to cluster where its tenets say they should.

The fair reply, which the cluster does make, is the origin-class audit at [apex/taxonomy-of-voids.md](/apex/taxonomy-of-voids/) — tenet-generated voids are explicitly barred from contributing to the cumulative count. That is a genuine discipline, not a decorative one.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Daniel Dennett)

*Signature specificity* is the criterion doing most of the load-bearing work, and it is the one most obviously satisfiable by a well-organised folk taxonomy. Any sufficiently articulate research community produces differentiated failure-descriptions for its unsolved problems; that is what having a vocabulary is. The page's own guard — "a criterion-passing void produces a shape recognisable from a description of the breakdown alone" (L53) — is the right test, and it is never actually run. No exhibit in the programme presents a breakdown-description to a reader innocent of the framework and checks whether the shape is recovered. Until that is done, signature specificity records that the cataloguer can tell his voids apart.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Max Tegmark)

Little direct purchase — this is methodology, not physics. One transferable complaint: *strong / moderate / weak* is an ordinal scale with no stated distance between grades, aggregated across four criteria into profiles ("moderate / moderate / weak / weak") that are then compared across clusters. `medium-status-cluster-independence-scoring.md` L111 is admirably careful about this — it explicitly refuses comparative-score claims at constant grader. The concept page's L102, comparing two exhibits' scores to establish non-flatness, is doing exactly the comparison the exhibit page forbids.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (David Deutsch)

Thin engagement, honestly noted by the page itself: L128 records that the criteria "connect only weakly to No Many Worlds (cross-observer convergence presupposes determinate observers)." That is the right relation and correctly hedged. No issue.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

The criteria treat voids as entities with stable properties — a "shape," a "signature," a "persistence" — and then score those properties. But a void is precisely the absence of a determination. To say the failure "has a distinct shape" is to give emptiness a form so it can be catalogued, which is the reification the analysis was supposed to dissolve. The programme's deepest bound, at L118 ("the question *is this observation framework-shaped?* is itself framework-shaped"), reaches the edge of this and then names it a "recursive bind" with an "asymptotic" response — treating as a regrettable technical limitation what is the actual structure of the situation.

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/apophatic-cartography-four-criteria.md`

**Recommendation**: rewrite L102 to state the calibrated position — the instrument's non-flatness is *provisional with a rubric, pending independent grading*; the two original exhibits both scored with the cataloguer's prior; the medium-status exhibit addressed the *unstated-standard* leg and left the *same-hand* leg open.
