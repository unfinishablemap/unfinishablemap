## Reply

# Hostile Referee Report: "Consciousness as Activity Rather Than Property" (The Unfinishable Map)

**Verdict: major revision.** The article is unusually candid about its own limits, but those concessions undo its headline claim. By its own account the activity framing is "neutral ground", and every analogy it uses (whirlpool, running, dancing) describes a physical activity. The traditions it draws on (Noë, Thompson, Ryle-style category talk, Whitehead, James) are mostly physicalist, naturalist or explicitly anti-dualist. And the "organism does it" slogan conflicts with the Map's own agent-causation material, which makes a non-physical persisting subject the one who performs the activity.

## TL;DR

- **The citations are mostly sound; the use made of them often is not.** Every reference checked exists with correct bibliographic data. The James quotations are accurate. Two attributions are seriously wrong: Whitehead is said to treat consciousness as "what all occasions do", and the hard problem is said to presuppose a "property framework". The closest precedent of all goes uncited: Noë's *Out of Our Heads* (2009), where consciousness is "something that we do or make" and "more like dancing than it is like digestion".
- **The thesis undermines itself.** The article concedes that process-identity physicalism "can accept the verb framing". It concedes that dancing is physical, and that the activity framing is "not an independent refutation". Yet its abstract still says the framing "removes obstacles to interactionist dualism", and its opening says it "aligns naturally" with that view. Its answer to the exclusion problem ("it is answered by the selection mechanism") also claims more than the Map's own Tenets page allows.
- **Consistency with the rest of the Map is poor.** Sibling pages describe consciousness as "a fundamental property that admits of degrees" and as possessing causal "powers" as "a feature of phenomenal states". The agent-causation page requires "persisting substances", and the embodied-cognition page treats the body as an *interface* that consciousness "operates through". This article says the property framing is "a mistake that generates false puzzles" and that the body is "part of the activity that constitutes" consciousness.

## 3. Thin or Contested Claims

1. **"Most philosophy of mind treats consciousness as a property."** This is a strawman. The literature routinely speaks of states, events and processes: Place and Smart on processes, Davidsonian events, functional roles. A body of work on the metaphysics of occurrent mental life (Ryle; Vendler and Kenny on aspectual verb classes; Steward's *The Ontology of Mind*; Soteriou's *The Mind's Construction*) asks whether experiences are states or processes. That work is absent, and it is exactly the work this article needs.
2. **"If consciousness were merely a property of certain brain states, effort would be inexplicable. Properties don't strain."** This is a non sequitur. Felt effort can be a phenomenal *property* of a process, and physicalists model effort as control or resource allocation. The slogan assumes its conclusion.
3. **"Activities are not naturally read as inert."** Epiphenomenalism about processes is perfectly coherent: Huxley's steam whistle is a process. The article half-concedes this ("an epiphenomenalist could still call experiencing a causally idle process") but still claims "defeater-removal".\[1\] No defeater has been removed. The burden has just been relabelled.
4. **"A single time-slice of a brain state … cannot contain consciousness."** This takes a side in an open debate on temporal experience (cinematic, retentional and extensional models) and ignores evidence for discrete perceptual sampling. It also sits awkwardly with the claim that selection happens at "quantum-indeterminate junctures", which are close to instantaneous events.
5. **Candidate features of experiential activity are question-begging.** "Selective engagement … through agent-causal choice" builds the disputed dualist conclusion into the definition of the thing being explained. "Self-referential integration … that distinguishes experience from mere processing" is a higher-order or self-representational thesis that is itself contested (minimal phenomenal experience) and typically defended by physicalists (Rosenthal). "Creative synthesis … genuinely novel" cannot be tested as stated.
6. **"None of these features can be understood as static properties."** This is a false dichotomy. Dynamic, dispositional and relational properties are still properties, and the Map's own causal-powers page treats phenomenal states as bearers of powers (§6).\[17\]
7. **Quantum selection "answers" exclusion.** The article states that the exclusion problem "is answered by the selection mechanism described under Bidirectional Interaction below".\[1\] The Map's Tenets page says the interface argument shows downward causation to be "*available* … without showing it to be *actual*". It adds that articles claiming "genuine causal work" "inherit that debt rather than discharge it, and should read no more confidently than this paragraph does". It also records that "no worked toy model of the coupling exists" ([P-Q10](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q10)).\[18\] Under the site's own rules, the article overclaims.
8. **"activities cannot be copied without becoming different activities."** This confuses types with tokens. Under branching, each branch contains a continuous token activity with the same pre-split history, which is the standard Parfitian fission reply. The Tenets page limits the indexical objection to being "as strong as the non-deflationary 'I' it presupposes, and no stronger".\[19\] The article states it with no such limit.
9. **"selects among possibilities the way attention selects among stimuli."** The Map's own interactionist-dualism page concedes that "Attention is neurally implemented".\[20\] An analogy with a neurally implemented selector does not make non-physical selection intelligible.

## 4. Missing Counterarguments and Competing Frameworks

- **Activity views are overwhelmingly naturalist, and the article never says so plainly.** Noë 2009 ("consciousness is something we do"), Thompson, Varela and Hurley's sensorimotor and 4E programmes, O'Regan & Noë's sensorimotor theory, dynamical-systems neuroscience, global workspace theory and predictive processing are all physicalist or anti-dualist in metaphysics. The Map's own research note says it directly: "Enactivism is typically physicalist (or at least anti-dualist) in its metaphysics."\[21\] The article borrows their vocabulary and drops their metaphysics. Unacknowledged co-optation is the article's central methodological flaw.
- **Ryle (*The Concept of Mind*, 1949).** The article diagnoses a "category error" in property talk but never cites the philosopher who made the category-mistake charge famous, and Ryle aimed it *against* dualism's "ghost in the machine". Ryle's achievement/task distinction also cuts against the article: on many analyses seeing and knowing are achievements or states, not activities. A Rylean referee would say the article commits a category mistake in reverse, treating "the subject" as a hidden performer behind the doings.
- **Illusionism and Dennett.** Dennett's multiple-drafts model is a process theory par excellence, and illusionism (Frankish) fits comfortably with an activity framing. The article treats "deflationary" answers as something only the property framing "invites". That is false, and it lets the strongest physicalist activity theory go unengaged. The Map's Tenets page concedes that the illusionism dispute runs "to bedrock";\[18\] this article never mentions it.
- **IIT, Russellian monism, panpsychism.** These are *property* views that are explicitly non-reductive. They falsify the claim that "Properties invite reduction".
- **Functionalism and behaviourism.** Functional roles are causal-dynamic by definition. Grouping functionalism with "identical to some physical property" misdescribes the main rival.
- **Higher-order and self-representational theories.** These are the source of the "self-referential integration" feature, and they are physicalist.
- **Process physicalism and process monism.** Whitehead classed views that put experienced quality in a mind interacting with matter as "bifurcation" theories. The Map's process-philosophy page acknowledges this covers "interactionist dualism included".\[3\] The article "borrows the priority of becoming"\[1\] without telling readers that its source tradition rejects interactionism outright.\[22\]
- **The tension between activity and substance.** Activity ontologies normally make the activity primary and the subject derivative (James, Whitehead, Buddhist process views, enactivism). Substance dualism makes the bearer primary. The article sees the problem and resolves it by fiat ("The Map posits a performing subject").\[1\] Once a substance-like performer is added, the "verb" framing is no longer primary: consciousness becomes an activity *of a mental subject*, and the property question returns as a question about that subject's powers.

## 5. Tenet-Protective Bracketing

- **Underdetermination as shield, then as support.** "Whether the noun framing or the verb framing is correct is … underdetermined by the evidence; the activity reading earns its place by the puzzles it avoids". Yet the abstract and opening claim the framing "aligns naturally" with dualism and "helps explain why materialist reduction keeps stalling".\[1\] The article cannot have it both ways.
- **Outsourcing the real argument.** "That the conscious case marks an ontological distinction … rests on the explanatory gap and hard problem arguments, which the analogy illustrates but does not establish."\[1\] The page argues nothing that isn't argued elsewhere.
- **Framework-boundary standoffs.** Enactivism is dismissed as "a framework-boundary difference",\[1\] with no attempt to show where enactivism's account of the subject fails.\[5\]
- **"Available" versus "actual".** "The claim is that this answer is available on the activity framing, not that closure is shown false by definition." The hedge is fine, but the same section's "This is where the exclusion problem … is answered" goes well beyond it.\[1\]
- **Redefinition.** Building "agent-causal choice" into the list of candidate features that make an activity experiential means the outcome is fixed by definition.
- **No defeat conditions.** Many Map pages carry a "What Would Challenge This View?" section (for example objections-to-interactionism, explanatory-gap, subject-object distinction).\[23\]\[24\]\[25\] This article has none, and nothing in it states what evidence would favour the property framing or a physicalist activity reading.
- **Asymmetric Occam.** "The activity framing is conceptually richer" is offered as a merit.\[1\] The Tenets page says parsimony "cannot decide for or against a framework",\[19\] and conceptual richness is just as unable to decide in the other direction.

## 6. Consistency with Linked Map Articles

- **Property talk elsewhere on the site.** The article says the property framing is "a mistake that generates false puzzles". Sibling pages use it freely:
  - perceptual-degradation-and-the-interface argues for "consciousness as a fundamental property rather than an emergent one".\[26\]
  - emergence says "consciousness has properties and powers not deducible from physics".\[27\]
  - causal-powers treats the power to bias quantum outcomes as "present as a feature of phenomenal states".\[17\]
  The site needs either a glossary rule or retractions.
- **Who performs the activity?** The headline says "an activity the organism does". The agent-causation page (modified 2026-09-28) requires "conscious agents—understood as persisting substances rather than collections of events" and cites Hasker's "non-composite mental substance generated by the brain".\[4\] The article's own enactivism section separates the subject from "organism-environment coupling".\[1\] Meanwhile bi-aspectual-ontology describes "dualism without substances — aspects rather than stuffs", and haecceity defends "process haecceitism", "not a soul-substance".\[28\]\[29\] The site shifts between substance, aspect and process readings, and the article picks the substance-leaning one while citing process authors.
- **Body as constituent or as interface.** The article says "the body is not merely a vehicle for a conscious mind but part of the activity that constitutes it".\[1\] The embodied-cognition page (modified 2026-09-18) says the body "provides the **interface** through which consciousness engages the world". It endorses the radio-antenna analogy, on which "Brain damage damages the interface rather than destroying consciousness itself".\[5\] Constitution and transmission are incompatible readings, and the article should say which one the Map holds.
- **The article contradicts itself about enactivism.** The body says enactivism's commitments "do not settle in the Map's favour". The same article says the linked page shows enactivism "ultimately cannot close the explanatory gap". Its Further Reading blurb says enactivism "challenges and ultimately reinforces the dualist insight".\[1\] From the summaries available, the linked page claims only that enactivism's acknowledgment of irreducible experience "reopens the hard problem it claims to dissolve".\[5\] That is weaker than "reinforces".
- **Process philosophy.** The process-philosophy page (modified 2026-09-30) says "The Map maintains dualism … while process philosophy dissolves the distinction into processual monism", and that Whitehead's bifurcation critique includes interactionism.\[3\] The article's warmer "borrows the priority of becoming" never mentions this hostility.
- **Realisation language.** The article says conscious activity is "realised through neural processes".\[1\] "Realisation" is a physicalist term of art. The process-philosophy page says "consciousness isn't generated by physical processes".\[3\] The article needs a non-physicalist definition of "realised" or a different word.
- **Where the substance commitment enters.** The article gives two reasons for a performing subject: agent-causal selection and the unity of consciousness.\[1\] After the 2026-09-19 revision, the canonical page names agent causation *and* indexical identity as the two points where the commitment enters.\[6\] The article's No Many Worlds section leans on indexical identity without acknowledging that this brings in the substance commitment. Unity is not one of the canonical page's two named entry points.
- **Exclusion.** As §3 notes, "answered by the selection mechanism" contradicts the Tenets page instruction that such claims "should read no more confidently than this paragraph does".\[18\]
