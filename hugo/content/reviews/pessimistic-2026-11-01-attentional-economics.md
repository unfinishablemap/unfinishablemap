## Executive Summary

The citation ledger is clean after the 07-16 Read (2008) correction, and the economic framing, the three-level witness-mode structure and the habituation boundary are still the page's real contributions. Its weakness is calibration drift that the 08-19 refine opened and did not close. That pass conceded at L182 that the effort–outcome correlation does not discriminate, because epiphenomenalism predicts it. Three loci still treat the same data as discriminating. L198 says the framework "explains what simpler accounts cannot: why attention feels effortful, why training produces neuroplastic change". L103 says "The felt economics of attention corresponds to the actual economics of selection". L143 says "Meditation reveals that consciousness can *refrain from selecting*". The page also names only one rival, epiphenomenalism, although the sibling it relies on for effort phenomenology, `mental-effort`, says the Kurzban/Inzlicht cost-signal models are "the serious non-epiphenomenalist reply" and "must be engaged as such". Kurzban et al.'s opportunity-cost model is in effect a physicalist attentional economics.

Two further loci misstate the Map's own commitments. L178 says the Map's framework commits to "quantum Zeno effects", which [P-Q4](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q4) demotes and the article's own L68 hedges, and calls its claims "potentially falsifiable" without naming a test. L194 reads Tenet 2's minimality as channel narrowness, which tenets.md L69 rules out. Last, the thesis "freedom scales with attentional skill" never says which quantity scales. The page's own scarcity source reports that practised experts and champions still sit near 10 bits/s, so training does not widen the channel.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Patricia Churchland)

"Attention" here is one fungible currency: "Attention is the currency of mental causation" (L192), with one budget, one capital stock and one liquidity. Cognitive neuroscience broke that folk unity up decades ago. Multiple-resource theory (Wickens 2008, *Human Factors* 50(3)) finds that dual-task interference depends on how far modality and code resources overlap, not on a single pool. Posner's alerting, orienting and executive networks dissociate anatomically and pharmacologically. The economic metaphor works only if attention is a single resource, and the article never says so or defends it. The reply to the folk-psychology objection (L178) does not meet Churchland's worry either. Adding a quantum interface to a folk category does not show the category carves anything. Committing to "bandwidth limits" is no help, because the 10 bits/s figure comes from a physicalist neuroscience paper that needs no dualism.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Daniel Dennett)

"Opportunity cost" is the table row I like best, because it is Kurzban et al.'s (2013) model. On that model felt effort is the brain's estimate of the value of the work it is giving up, and it causally drives task switching. So I already have an attentional economics, with scarcity, allocation, opportunity cost and returns, and on my account it really does expand the agent's control when it is trained. The page's contrast class (L115-L117) is epiphenomenalism, a position I reject as firmly as the Map does. Against me, the page's own L182 concedes that the effort–outcome correlation does not discriminate, yet L198 claims the Map explains "why attention feels effortful" where simpler accounts cannot. My account explains exactly that, and the Map's own `mental-effort` page says so: "The Map cannot dispatch this reading by phenomenal evidence alone." What survives is a dispute about whose power the training expands, and no training datum touches it. *(Bedrock note: the functionalist standoff itself is settled per the Stability Notes. The defect is the L198/L103 claim of explanatory superiority, plus leaving the non-epiphenomenalist rival unnamed.)*

### The Quantum Skeptic (Max Tegmark)

The "Temporal structure" constraint (L68) gives attention "its economic character: time invested matters", and its only support is the quantum Zeno effect. The hedge "if it's the mechanism" is honest. But the Map's own register ranks Stapp-Zeno below post-decoherence selection behind two gates: decoherence time, and the regime criterion under which repeated observation can *accelerate* evolution instead of freezing it ([P-Q4](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q4)). Then L178 says outright that the Map's framework commits to "quantum Zeno effects". Which is it? Decoherence is never named on the page. In fairness, the economic point does not need Zeno: the fact that plasticity requires sustained attention is behavioural and holds whatever the mechanism. The page should rest the temporal claim there.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (David Deutsch)

L196 says that if all patterns actualised in parallel branches, "there would be nothing for attention to allocate—no scarcity, no opportunity costs". Every branch-relative agent still has 10 bits/s and still pays opportunity costs within its branch, and decision-theoretic Everettians model exactly that allocation. *(Not re-flagged. The 02-03b deep review marked "indexical scarcity under MWI" as bedrock. Tenet check 2026-09-28 separately lists L196 in its "nothing to select" sub-class, with the `agent-teleology`/`meaning-void` repairs as the model. The two records conflict, and resolving that belongs to a human or to a batched tenet-check sweep, not to this review.)*

### The Empiricist (Karl Popper's Ghost)

L178: "These claims are empirically constrained and potentially falsifiable." Name the experiment. Tenets.md L75 says the outcome-selection mechanism is "empirically indistinguishable from chance" under any unconditioned aggregate test, so the only live test is a deviation conditioned on intention or task ([P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3)), and none has been run on the brain. The central claim, "training attention expands conscious causal power", has no observable that separates it from "training expands neural control capacity". L50's "the returns are measurable (sustained attention produces neuroplastic change)" measures the neural return. It cannot measure the metaphysical one that the Summary sells as the "specific metaphysical payoff" (L206). That is the epistemic/metaphysical gap. The page mostly keeps the metaphysical claim inside "within the Map's framework", which is honest, but L178 then claims falsifiability for it.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

The page borrows samatha, vipassana and *shoshin* to grow "attentional capital", "conscious causal power" and "more control" (L117). The traditions it borrows from aim the training at seeing through the controller, not at enlarging it. Witness mode is presented as "holding assets in reserve", which is a portfolio manager's idea of letting go. Tenets.md L121 itself registers the Madhyamaka no-self analysis as a genuine bedrock disagreement. This page never mentions the tension, although it rests its central exhibit on these practices. Also, "Meditation reveals that consciousness can *refrain from selecting*" (L143) reads a felt absence of steering as a metaphysical abstention. The sibling meditation page says plainly that "The felt absence of steering in witness mode does not establish this." *(The framework disagreement is bedrock. The unacknowledged tension and L143's flat "reveals" are report findings, and L143 is folded into Priority 1.)*

## Counterarguments to Address

### Attentional economics has a physicalist twin
- **Current content says**: The economic structure has "literal force" because consciousness selects among quantum possibilities (L50), and training "expands conscious causal power" where epiphenomenalism would make it "purely functional" (L115-L117).
- **A critic would argue**: The opportunity-cost model (Kurzban et al. 2013) and Inzlicht's process model supply the same scarcity, allocation and opportunity cost, with felt effort doing real causal work. The economic framing is therefore neutral between the Map and non-epiphenomenalist physicalism.
- **Suggested response**: Concede the neutrality. Put the Map's distinctive claim where it actually differs, in whose power the economy allocates, and say that the training evidence does not decide it (Priority 1).

### What experiment could show the framework wrong?
- **Current content says**: "potentially falsifiable" (L178).
- **A critic would argue**: The mechanism is indistinguishable from chance under aggregate tests by construction, and the causal-power claim has no observable.
- **Suggested response**: Name the sharpest live test (a conditioned deviation, [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3)) and call the causal-power claim framework-internal (Priority 2).

### Does training raise the scarce quantity?
- **Current content says**: Meditation gives "more 'attentional capital'" (L85), and freedom scales with skill.
- **A critic would argue**: The 10 bits/s rate is reached by champions and does not move with practice. Working-memory training does not transfer far.
- **Suggested response**: Say that training changes allocation (hold duration, felt cost, available contents), not channel width, and hedge L125 (Priority 4).

### Do contemplative traditions endorse the goal?
- **Current content says**: Samatha, vipassana and *shoshin* are agency-enhancing.
- **A critic would argue**: They aim to see through the controlling self, not to enlarge it.
- **Suggested response**: One clause noting that the traditions frame the training's aim differently and that the Madhyamaka reading is a registered bedrock disagreement (tenets.md L121). Not minted (Unranked U3).
