## Executive Summary

The article's central finding — that granting a momentary locus makes 8.102's premise "no sufferings belong to anyone" false, so the Map's "close to terminological" verdict "fails normatively" — depends on reading "anyone" as ranging over *any bearer whatever*. The verse and its setup (8.97–101) are about *persons* and the partiality they would ground, and on that reading a momentary locus leaves the premise true and the inference intact. The article already concedes the conclusion survives by another route, so what remains of the finding is a claim about one premise under one scope reading, and the lede and the sibling article both describe it more strongly than that. Two further structural issues: the Relation section says **Dualism** (Tenet 1) supplies the momentary subject-pole, which the tenet's own text does not; and §Ownerless, or Merely Unranked? counts Goodman's conventional-level "welfare" talk as a partial concession, although the article's own two-truths exposition (§asymmetry) explains that talk away.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
"You have staged a dispute between two ontologies of pain-moments, the abhidharma one with bare suffering-events and yours with suffering-events plus a subject-pole, and neither side has a single neural datum. Everything the article calls a 'finding' comes down to the scope of a quantifier in an eighth-century verse. The phrase 'genuine subject-pole not reducible to the mechanism realising it' in §implications carries all the Map's weight and is simply stipulated." Fair as a description; the article does label that claim "only on the Map's reading", so this is bedrock and no defect. The article's weak point here is elsewhere: it says the subject-pole comes from Dualism (see Issue 2), which lets the stipulation look as if a tenet had licensed it.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
"'For-me-ness' is exactly the kind of introspective seeming that a self-model produces. Your own [ownership void](/concepts/mine-ness/#the-ownership-void) concedes that the mechanism of ownership is not introspectable. So the sole evidence for a momentary *bearer* over a bare suffering-event is a seeming you admit you cannot inspect. Śāntideva, Metzinger and I agree on this and you are the outlier." The article flags Metzinger's reading of 101 as something that "would move this article" but treats it as a future contingency. It is in fact the direct rival to the article's central premise. If 101's "the one for whom there is suffering does not exist" denies the momentary locus as well, the finding has no content. The sibling calls this "the objection's strongest form" and an open question, and this article relies on its answer without flagging that dependence at the point where the finding is stated.

### The Quantum Skeptic
No purchase. The article touches no physics and draws on neither quantum tenet. Nothing to press.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
"You say you keep Tenet 4 out of this. But your tenets page says No Many Worlds 'rests on a non-deflationary persisting I', and your momentary locus is the same subject-side, just sliced thinner. If the thin locus is real in the way the Map needs here, why is its diachronic continuation not also real in a way that restores the partiality 8.97–99 attacks?" The cascade isolation (§Relation, No Many Worlds paragraph) is deliberate and stated. The Deutschian point is that the isolation leaves a question open: what makes a momentary locus a *bearer* without also making it a stage of a persisting one. The article does not need to answer this, but "the same isolation holds here" presents the isolation as free, and it is not.

### The Empiricist
"Take your Occam paragraph. The ownerless reading is more parsimonious, and you 'decline to let that count' because your tenet says simplicity is unreliable. Then, one sentence later, you let the Parity Reply's parsimony-pressed-consistently point count *for* you. What would show you wrong? You list readings that 'would move this article', and every one is a text to be obtained. None is a consideration that would lead you to drop the bearer." The article's symmetry line ("Neither side may stop deflating where it suits them") is good. But the first half of the Occam paragraph still has Tenet 5 switching off a consideration that cuts against the Map, and "as the tenet requires" makes that sound like an obligation when it is a choice. Low severity, since the 2026-09-18 tenet check counted this line among the corpus's legitimate declines, but the wording of "declines to let that count in its own favour" is ambiguous about whose favour is meant (see Language).

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nāgārjuna)
"Your momentary locus is one more conventional designation, a subject-pole imputed onto a feeling-moment. On my analysis it is as empty as the person, and exactly as useful conventionally. You have not granted a bearer that the abhidharmika lacks. You have reified a *prajñapti* and then found that the reification falsifies a premise. Śāntideva's question 'why is any limitation put on this?' does not care whether your moment is owned. It asks what grounds a *limitation*, and a moment with no future grounds none." This is the strongest critique, developed as Issue 1. The Parity Reply section shows the article knows the Madhyamaka move. What it has not done is apply that move to its own locus.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The finding depends on an uncharitable scope reading of "anyone" in 8.102
- **File**: [concepts/the-ownerless-suffering-argument.md](/concepts/the-ownerless-suffering-argument/)
- **Location**: lede paragraph 2 ("That verdict survives metaphysically and fails normatively… Śāntideva's inference *needs* the sufferings to be ownerless at exactly that grain"); §map-meets paragraphs 2–3 ("'no sufferings belong to anyone' becomes false, because a momentary locus is a someone"; "Śāntideva's premise quantifies over bearers rather than over badness").
- **Problem**: The dialectical context the article itself reconstructs (8.94–99) is about *partiality*: the objector's reply rests on a special relation to "my own future self". Verse 101's first clause denies *the person*, and its question "to whom will that suffering belong?" asks for the partiality-grounding owner the objector needs. Read with that scope, "no sufferings belong to anyone" means "to any person, any bearer of the kind partiality could attach to". A momentary locus is not such a bearer, and the article concedes this: "a momentary locus has no future toward which to be partial". On the charitable reading, then, the Map's locus leaves 102's premise true and the inference untouched. The finding survives only on a wide-scope reading ("any bearer whatever"), and the article asserts that reading without arguing for it. That makes this a scope equivocation in the claim the whole article depends on. The same move recurs in the sibling, which now reads the verdict as "substantive in the normative register, where the ownerless premise quantifies over bearers". Nāgārjuna's critique above sharpens it: the conclusion's rhetorical question asks what grounds a *limitation*, and the Map's reply supplies nothing that could ground one.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: The claim needs to be either argued or downgraded. (a) *Argue*: give a textual or philosophical reason to read "anyone" wide. Candidates are the abhidharma commentarial reading of *pudgala* against *dharma*, or the point that Garfield, Jenkins and Priest's "the one for whom there is suffering" is naturally read as any experiencer. (b) *Downgrade*: say that on the wide reading the Map's locus falsifies the premise, and on the narrow (person-scoped) reading it does not. In that case the finding is that the argument's wording is hostage to the metaphysics of bearers, which is already the article's own first general conclusion in §implications. Option (b) is length-neutral if the lede's "fails normatively" sentence is rewritten rather than extended.

### Issue 2: The Relation section attributes the momentary subject-pole to Tenet 1, which does not supply it
- **File**: [concepts/the-ownerless-suffering-argument.md](/concepts/the-ownerless-suffering-argument/)
- **Location**: §Relation to Site Perspective, Dualism paragraph ("**Dualism** supplies the momentary locus the finding turns on… The locus is the subject-side of experience"); §implications ("because the locus is a genuine subject-pole not reducible to the mechanism realising it").
- **Problem**: Tenet 1 reads "There is more than just matter involved in reality", and the tenets page stresses that it is neutral about substance versus property and records a *persisting subject* as an unstated background posit, not a tenet entailment. Irreducible momentary experiential events are fully compatible with Tenet 1 *without any bearer*. The abhidharma inventory the article describes, with ultimately real momentary suffering-dharmas and no *svalakṣaṇa* for persons, is itself a dualism of momentary mental and material events in the relevant sense. Tenet 1 therefore cannot tell the Map's position apart from the ownerless one on the question that matters here. The subject-pole comes from a separate commitment, the Zahavi/Strawson structure the sibling borrows, which the sibling's own Dualism paragraph credits only for the locus's *non-physicality*, not its existence. The article's own disclosure ("on a commitment rather than a finding") is honest, but it names the wrong commitment, and a reader will take it that the tenet decides the dispute. This is the tenet-inheritance-matrix failure pattern: the article draws on a posit through a tenet's alignment line.
- **Severity**: Medium-High
- **Recommendation**: Rewrite the Dualism paragraph so it says Tenet 1 supplies the locus's *irreducibility* if there is one, while its *existence* as a bearer rests on the minimal-self structure borrowed in [no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value](/concepts/no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value/#maps-reply), which Tenet 1 neither entails nor excludes. Add one clause noting that abhidharma dharma-realism is also compatible with Tenet 1, so the tenet is neutral on 8.101. This is roughly length-neutral if "along with its price" and the ownership-void sentence are tightened.

### Issue 3: §Ownerless, or Merely Unranked? counts conventional-level speech as a concession
- **File**: [concepts/the-ownerless-suffering-argument.md](/concepts/the-ownerless-suffering-argument/)
- **Location**: §unranked ("A *welfare* is something a subject has… a partial concession available to the Map").
- **Problem**: §asymmetry explains, through Garfield, Jenkins and Priest, that on the abhidharma reading persons are "conventionally real", and the Parity Reply says conventional distinctions are action-relevant. A reductionist in the Parfit/Siderits line can speak of "welfare" and "sentient beings" freely at the conventional level without any ultimate commitment to bearers. That is the purpose of the two-truths framework. Goodman's "whose welfare is to be promoted" is therefore just what his position predicts he would say, and it counts neither as a concession nor as evidence that he "reaches for beneficiaries". The article hedges this ("not a vindication"), but the section's premise, that a *welfare* word imports a subject, is the naive reading its own earlier section rules out.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Either cut the section to a sentence or two (which frees about 150–200 words, useful with 137 words of headroom), or reframe it: the ranking/ownership distinction is the Map's, and Goodman's gloss is *compatible* with it, not a concession to it. The unranked-versus-ownerless distinction is worth keeping. Evidence drawn from word choice is not.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The inference survives the momentary locus
- **Current content says**: granting a momentary locus removes "the tradition's stated bridge from ownerlessness to impartiality".
- **A critic would argue**: a momentary locus has no future and no past, so it can ground no *limitation*, and 102's closing question asks only what grounds a limitation. Swap "belongs to no one" for "belongs to no one who could be favoured" and the bridge is rebuilt in the premise's own words, with no appeal to the similarity argument.
- **Suggested response**: concede it, and state the finding as scope-relative (Issue 1b). The honest residue is that the *literal* ownerless premise is false on the Map's ontology while a slightly reworded premise does the same work. That difference is small, and the text should say so.

### The locus is itself a *prajñapti*
- **Current content says**: the Map asserts no *ātman*, and the locus "survive[s] the analysis of the person".
- **A critic would argue** (Madhyamaka): the analysis that dissolves the person dissolves any imputed subject-pole too. The Parity Reply's second half, which the article turns against the reductionist, applies with the same force to the Map's locus.
- **Suggested response**: one sentence in §rivals acknowledging that the Parity Reply's symmetry cuts against the Map's locus as well as against the reductionist's dharmas. The article already says "Neither side may stop deflating where it suits them" in its Relation section. That principle should be applied to the Map inside §rivals too.

### Chadha's eliminativism does not obviously remove streams
- **Current content says**: "If persons are eliminated, conventionally real streams are not available as value-bearers either."
- **A critic would argue**: eliminating *persons* is compatible with keeping causal series (*santāna*) of dharmas as non-personal streams. Eliminativism about the person-concept is not eliminativism about the causal series the person-concept was imputed onto.
- **Suggested response**: either cite the passage of Chadha (2021) that extends the elimination to streams, or weaken the sentence to "narrows" (the section's own title) and not "not available".
