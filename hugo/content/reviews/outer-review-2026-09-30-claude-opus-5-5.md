## Reply

# Referee Report: *Galilean Exclusion* (unfinishablemap.org/concepts/galilean-exclusion/, modified 2026-09-21)

The article's central claim is false as history. Galileo's primary/secondary distinction in *Il Saggiatore* was not a "methodological decision" but an explicit, if tentatively voiced, ontological thesis. Galileo says tastes, odours and colours are "puri nomi" residing only in the "corpo sensitivo," to be "levate ed annichilate" once the animal is removed, and he contrasts them with the "primi e reali accidenti." [Wikisource](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Il_Saggiatore/48)[Substack](https://digressionsimpressions.substack.com/p/husserl-on-the-primary-and-secondary) The page's anti-materialist payload rests on that misreading. It also rests on a sieve analogy that runs together what a method can describe and what exists, and on co-opting Husserl, Whitehead and Thompson into a tenet section whose conclusion (substance dualism) all three reject.

## Dimensional analysis

### 1. The Assayer: methodological bracketing or ontological claim?

**What Galileo wrote (Opere VI, pp. 347–350; *Il Saggiatore* §48):** "Per lo che vo io pensando che questi sapori, odori, colori, etc., per la parte del suggetto nel quale ci par che riseggano, non sieno altro che puri nomi, ma tengano solamente lor residenza nel corpo sensitivo, sì che rimosso l'animale, sieno levate ed annichilate tutte queste qualità." [Wikisource](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Il_Saggiatore/48) He goes on to say that outside the living animal odours, tastes and sounds are "nothing but names," just as tickling is "nothing but a name" once armpits and the skin around the nose are removed. [Substack](https://johnplaice.substack.com/p/galileo-galilei-supports-atomism) The feather/paper example is there: the titillation "è tutta di noi, e non della penna." [Unipi](http://omero.humnet.unipi.it/matdid/637/Galileo%20Dialogo%20e%20Saggiatore.doc) So is the hand moving over a marble statue and then a living man. [Zanichelli](https://online.scuola.zanichelli.it/lezionidifilosofia/files/2010/01/U2-L07_zanichelli_Galilei.pdf) So is the corpuscular account of heat ("una moltitudine di corpicelli minimi… mossi con tanta e tanta velocità"). [Sif](https://www.ilnuovosaggiatore.sif.it/article/342) Drake (1957, p. 274) renders the key clause "they reside only in the consciousness." [Motleybytes](https://motleybytes.com/w/Annihilate_quotes) That is a known mistranslation. Finocchiaro (2008, p. 185, as quoted by Buyse 2015, p. 31) renders it "they inhere only in the sensitive body, such that if one removes the animal, then all these qualities are taken away and annihilated." Buyse, in "The Distinction between Primary Properties and Secondary Qualities in Galileo Galilei's Natural Philosophy" (*Working Papers of the Quebec Seminar in Early Modern Philosophy* 1, 2015, p. 31), states that "Galileo nowhere writes that secondary qualities and emotions reside 'in consciousness'… he writes that they reside 'in the sensible body.'" He also notes that Shea & Davie (2012, p. 119) render it "the mind," and adds (p. 33) that the phrase "suggests that, besides the body, there is an Aristotelian, immaterial sensitive soul."

**Verdict on the reading.** Three textual facts decide the question against the article:

1. **Modal and ontological vocabulary.** Galileo contrasts the secondary qualities with "gli altri primi e reali accidenti," the *real* accidents. [Wikisource](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Il_Saggiatore/48) "Puri nomi" and "annichilate" are existence claims about the object, not scoping rules for a discipline. He states them as his belief ("vo io pensando," "non credo," "stimo"). [Unipd](https://www.dfa.unipd.it/fileadmin/zz_old/segredott/master/Lectio3_LuceColore_6feb15.pdf) That is tentative metaphysics, not a methodological stipulation.
2. **Contemporary reception.** Orazio Grassi's reply (*Ratio ponderum Librae et Simbellae*, 1626) read "puri nomi" as meaning heat and taste "are nothing" outside the perceiver. He drew the Eucharistic consequence that the species could not persist in the host. [Sif](https://www.ilnuovosaggiatore.sif.it/article/342) The first readers took it as ontology with theological stakes.
3. **Ancient pedigree.** The thesis descends from Democritean atomism [Ucsd](https://aardvark.ucsd.edu/color/hardin1.html) ("by convention colour… in reality atoms and void"). That is a metaphysical doctrine. SEP's *Primary and Secondary Qualities in Early Modern Philosophy* treats Galileo as the first early-modern statement of "the doctrine of qualities" and reconstructs it as an *argument*, not a method. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qualities-prim-sec/)

Burtt's *Metaphysical Foundations* (1924) is the classic source for exactly the opposite of the article's claim. It treats Galileo's "assertion that these secondary qualities are subjective" as a metaphysical turn that leaves man "an irrelevant spectator." [LibraryThing](https://www.librarything.com/work/58141/t/The-Metaphysical-Foundations-of-Modern-Science)[Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/The_Metaphysical_Foundations_of_Modern_Physical_Science) Husserl's own verdict, as relayed by *The Blind Spot*, is that the Galilean tradition takes "for true being what is actually a method." [closertotruth](https://closertotruth.com/news/book-excerpt-the-blind-spot-why-science-cannot-ignore-human-experience/) Husserl *diagnoses* a metaphysical overreach. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342405886_A_Revealing_Parallel_Between_Husserl's_Philosophy_of_Science_and_Today's_Scientific_Metaphysics) He does not say Galileo stayed inside methodology.

The defensible thesis is narrower: *Galileo's ontological claim was not a discovery.* It was a speculative atomist posit whose warrant came from the mathematical program's success, not from evidence about qualities. The article's slogan "Not a Discovery but a Decision" gets the first half right and the second half wrong. The page contradicts itself anyway. Its own OSR paragraph says Galileo "*relocates* the residue" into the sentient body, and relocating is an ontological act. Its history paragraph says the move "began as a methodological convenience in Galileo" and only "became, through Descartes, a metaphysical framework." [concepts/galilean-exclusion](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/galilean-exclusion/) The two cannot both stand.

There is a further point the article misses, and it cuts against its dualist use. Galileo's residue lands in the **corpo sensitivo**, the animated *body*. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.7381)[Substack](https://digressionsimpressions.substack.com/p/husserl-on-the-primary-and-secondary) It does not land in a *res cogitans*. The mentalist gloss is Drake's "consciousness" plus Descartes. Galileo's own relocation is fully compatible with a physicalist psychophysics of the sensitive body.

**Minor historical errors.**

- The parenthetical says the "explicit terminology of 'primary' and 'secondary'… came later, from Boyle and Locke." [concepts/galilean-exclusion](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/galilean-exclusion/) That is half-right. Buyse (2015, p. 23) notes that "it was not Locke, but… Robert Boyle… who introduced a similar terminology into English," while Galileo already writes "primi e reali accidenti" (Opere VI, 347–348), and SEP notes he calls them "primary affections."
- "Descartes deepened the exclusion… gave the Galilean move a metaphysical foundation" [concepts/galilean-exclusion](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/galilean-exclusion/) makes Cartesian dualism look derivative of Galileo. Descartes's dualism has independent Augustinian and scholastic roots and the cogito/real-distinction arguments. His own list of sensible qualities (*Principles*, 1644) parallels Galileo's; [GeoCities](http://www.geocities.ws/john_russey/philosophy/Primary-secondary_quality_distinction.html) it does not inherit from it.

### 2. "Structurally inevitable"

This is historical over-determination. The mind–body problem predates 1623 in Plato's *Phaedo*, Aristotle's *De Anima* and the scholastic debates over the intellective soul. The Democritean fragment in which the senses rebuke the intellect ("wretched mind, you take your evidence from us and then overthrow us") already states the self-undermining worry the Map runs today. The *modern* hard problem also needed things Galileo did not supply: Cartesian real distinction, Lockean ideas, 19th-century energy conservation and causal closure, and 20th-century functionalism and conceivability arguments. [research/causal-closure-debate-2026-03-19](https://unfinishablemap.org/research/causal-closure-debate-2026-03-19/) "Inevitable" also clashes with the article's own claim that the exclusion was a *decision*: a decision implies alternatives. At most the Galilean move *shaped the form* of the modern problem. Chalmers's hard problem rests on conceivability and structure-and-dynamics arguments, not on genealogy. [concepts/parsimony-epistemology](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/parsimony-epistemology/)[arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14234) "They are different in kind because Galileo made them so" is therefore a claim Chalmers would reject. He holds that physical description is structural/dynamical *in principle*, so the gap would survive any history.

### 3. The sieve analogy and the slide from method to metaphysics

- **The analogy is factually backwards.** Sieve analysis is the standard way to study sand, by measuring grain-size distribution from what the mesh retains and passes. A filter's selectivity is exactly what makes it a measuring instrument for the thing it filters. The rhetorical force depends on the reader not knowing this.
- **Epistemic-to-metaphysical slide.** The argument runs in four steps. (a) Galileo's method excluded secondary qualities from physical description. (b) So physical description does not capture phenomenal quality. (c) So a science built on it faces a "structural obstacle." (d) Tenet section: this failure "is expected" if dualism is true, and it shows "structural limits built into the scientific method." [concepts/galilean-exclusion](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/galilean-exclusion/) Steps (a)–(c) are claims about description. Step (d) cashes them as support for irreducibility. The article's own "Not a Discovery" section blocks this move. If the absence of consciousness from physics "reflects a methodological choice, not an empirical finding," then the method's failure to capture consciousness is *also* a fact about the method, not about consciousness. [concepts/galilean-exclusion](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/galilean-exclusion/) The page applies the symmetry argument to the materialist and exempts the dualist: calibration asymmetry.
- **The exclusion was never total.** Psychophysics (Weber, Fechner), colorimetry and CIE colour spaces, and quality-space work all made secondary qualities scientific *targets* by capturing their relational structure. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.01368) That leaves a residue only for intrinsic/categorical quality. This is exactly the opening Russellian monism exploits (the Russell/Eddington line Goff builds on), [Philosophy Now](https://philosophynow.org/issues/135/Galileos_Error_by_Philip_Goff)[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Goff_(philosopher)) and the article never names it.
- **Counter-positions left unengaged:**

- **Dennett:** heterophenomenology is a neutral data policy, not a denial of experience.
- **Churchland:** the explanatory gap is an artefact of immature neuroscience, like vitalism.
- **Frankish (illusionism):** the residue is a representational property of introspection, so the sieve lets through everything real.
- **Galen Strawson:** real physicalism entails experience is physical, so the exclusion is a fact about physics' *vocabulary*, not about physical reality.
- **Goff:** the same diagnosis motivates panpsychism, not dualism. [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/671190038/LaasikGoffGalileosErrorReviewJCS03-16-20)[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Goff_(philosopher))
- **Whitehead and the *Blind Spot* authors:** the diagnosis motivates abolishing the bifurcation dualism presupposes. [Medium](https://medium.com/@gino.elia/does-classical-metaphysics-have-a-blind-spot-456f3dab3e6b)[Closertotruth](https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/frank-gleiser-thompson-s-the-blind-spot)

Every one of these accepts some version of the Galilean diagnosis. The diagnosis therefore has a likelihood ratio near 1 across the live options. That is an evidential-independence failure: a single historical fact is counted as support for the Map when every rival predicts it equally.

### 4. Functionalism "admits experience only as third-person data"

This is a category error that becomes a strawman. Functionalism is a metaphysical thesis about what mental states *are*. It is not a data policy. The data-policy question is methodological, and it is answered in several ways: Dennett's heterophenomenology, Varela's neurophenomenology (first-person descriptions constrained with neurodynamics), microphenomenological interviewing, and Petitmengin-style second-person methods. [topics/first-person-third-person-methodology](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/first-person-third-person-methodology/)[closertotruth](https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/frank-gleiser-thompson-s-the-blind-spot) The complaint that reports enter "not as experience itself" proves too much. [concepts/galilean-exclusion](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/galilean-exclusion/) No method, including Husserlian phenomenology, takes "experience itself" as publicly shareable data. Phenomenological descriptions are reports too. The article is also inconsistent. Two sections later it says neurophenomenology "break[s] from the framework," which concedes that consciousness science is not confined to the third-person policy it has just attributed to it. [concepts/galilean-exclusion](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/galilean-exclusion/) The linked page phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence repeats the strawman. It says heterophenomenology's neutrality "presupposes that no epistemically distinctive first-person access exists." [topics/phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence/) Dennett's neutrality brackets the question; it does not presuppose an answer.

### 5. Ontic structural realism: "relocates" vs "abolishes"

The paragraph cites nobody. Ladyman & Ross's *Every Thing Must Go* (OUP 2007) is a metaphysics of physics plus "rainforest realism" for the special sciences, [mit](https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780199276196) built on Dennett's "real patterns." It does not argue that experience is "abolished." On its own terms, experience would be a real pattern at the psychological scale, a *location*, not an abolition. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.09821) That puts OSR's actual proponents in the Dennettian camp, not in the eliminativist corner the article sketches. The article's hedge, that extending OSR to experience "is a separate commitment (phenomenal structuralism)," is the most accurate sentence in the section. [concepts/galilean-exclusion](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/galilean-exclusion/) But the "strongest reading" it attacks has no named proponent: a strawman defeated by a concession. The Map's chosen weapon, Newman's problem, comes from M.H.A. Newman, "Mr. Russell's Causal Theory of Perception," *Mind* 37(146): 137–148 (1928), DOI 10.1093/mind/XXXVII.146.137, a paper PhilPapers files under "Russellian Monism in Philosophy of Mind." It is the historical engine of Russellian monism's demand for intrinsic natures. Using Newman against OSR while never mentioning Russellian monism hides the fact that the objection points toward the rival monism, not toward interactionist substance dualism.
