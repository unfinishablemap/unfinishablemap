## Executive Summary

The article's central dualist inference turns on itself. The Relation to Site Perspective asks why consciousness, if it were physical, "would be categorically harder" to describe. But the qualia section has already cited Ji et al. (2024), whose information-theoretic account answers that question in wholly physicalist terms: the difficulty is a compression bound between high-dimensional neural trajectories and low-capacity working memory. The article also meets its own falsifier ("Neuroscience reveals mechanism") without noticing. Separately, it reads the epistemic evidence (we cannot *transmit or verify* qualia) as a metaphysical conclusion ("constitutively private"). It uses the Wittgenstein/Davidson triangulation argument in the opposite direction to the one it actually cuts. It cites a Greek colour study for a Russian claim. Its Quine paragraph reaches a conclusion that Quine's thesis contradicts.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

"You quote Ji, Bengio and colleagues saying the ineffability of experience comes from compressing billions of neurons' activity into a handful of working-memory items. That is my account. Ineffability is what a finite reporting system looks like from inside when it summarises a much richer system. Then, eight paragraphs later, you say ineffability supports dualism because 'if consciousness were reducible to physical process, why would describing it be categorically harder?' You have already printed the answer. It isn't *categorically* harder. It is harder by a compression ratio, which your own source calls a structural bound. Neuroscience will close the explanatory gap in the same way: by explaining why self-models *report* a gap."

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

"The 'deflationary challenge' paragraph attacks a straw man. My claim was never that the asymmetry between teaching colour words and teaching colour experience is puzzling. My claim is that the asymmetry is *expected*. Words transmit discriminations. 'Experience' is the name for the discriminative state itself, and you can only install that state by presenting the stimulus. It would be equally 'ineffable' that I cannot give you a bicycle-riding skill by description. Nobody concludes that proprioceptive know-how is non-physical. Your sentence 'If ineffability were mere confusion, the asymmetry would be puzzling' contains no argument. It needs one showing that the colour case differs from the know-how case. Your Privacy paragraph also concedes my point. If concepts require intersubjective triangulation, and qualia cannot be triangulated, then 'qualia' are not concepts with private referents. They are Wittgenstein's beetle in the box, and the beetle drops out. You then conclude that the content is 'constitutively private'. The argument you cited concludes that there is no such content."

### The Quantum Skeptic

"The Minimal Quantum Interaction paragraph is the thinnest in the article. 'Selection among superposed possibilities might not require language' is true of a thermostat. Nothing in an article about lexical untranslatability, olfactory naming and Tractarian silence bears on decoherence timescales or on where in the brain selection would occur. 'The interface between mind and matter may lie in the void beyond words' is a sentence with no content that anyone could check. The honest move would be to say this void is neutral on Tenet 2, or to drop the paragraph."

### The Many-Worlds Defender

"The article never mentions Tenet 4. That is at least honest. But the 'Structural indexicality' argument (qualia are first-personal and language is third-personal, so something is necessarily lost) proves too much. Natural language has indexicals: 'I', 'here', 'this', 'now'. First-personal content enters language every time anyone says 'I am in pain.' What is lost is not first-personality. It is the non-conceptual detail, which puts you back in the physicalist compression story you cited from Ji. An argument this loose would equally 'show' that 'which branch am I in' is ineffable, and I'd agree for the same deflationary reason."

### The Empiricist

"Two of your four falsifiers are already partly met, and the article does not register either. (1) 'Neuroscience reveals mechanism.' Ji et al. is offered as exactly that, a mechanism, and the article agrees it is a *structural* bound, which is your third void type turned into a neuroscientific result. (2) 'Universal translatability proves out.' The olfaction section reports that a limit which looked unexplorable for a century turned out to be largely unexplored. That is one confirmed instance of the falsifier's direction, and the article treats it only as a calibration lesson. Meanwhile the dualist claim has no falsifier at all. Nothing listed would count against 'ineffability supports dualism', because the falsifiers target the weaker claim that language and thought have different limits. That weaker claim a physicalist can accept."

### The Buddhist Philosopher

"You treat the phenomenology of the boundary as a subject holding stable content that cannot get out ('content may be stable but inexpressible'; 'fullness without outlet'). The contemplative traditions you invoke through the mystics report the opposite. On close inspection the 'content' is not a stable thing waiting behind the words. It is a process that conceptualisation freezes. 'Dissolution on articulation' fits emptiness better than it fits a hidden possession. Your Bidirectional Interaction paragraph adds a chooser behind the choosing ('We can choose to investigate... This deliberate investigation is mental causation in action'). That assumes the self whose existence is in question."

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The Dualism inference is refuted by the article's own cited source, and the article's falsifier is already met
- **File**: voids/language-thought-boundary.md
- **Location**: §Relation to Site Perspective, Dualism paragraph ("If consciousness were reducible to physical process, why would describing it be categorically harder than describing other physical processes?"), read against §Qualia as Limit Case, *Dimensionality mismatch* (Ji et al. 2024), and §What Would Challenge This View ("Neuroscience reveals mechanism")
- **Problem**: Ji et al. (2024) is a functionalist, information-theoretic account (Bengio's group) that explains ineffability as neural-to-working-memory compression. The article endorses it ("a structural bound... not one better description could close"). The Dualism paragraph then asks a rhetorical question that Ji has just answered physically, and calls the difference "categorical" when Ji makes it quantitative. The falsifier "If we discover how ineffability arises neurally" is partly met by the article's own content. The inference also runs into the same compression explanation that covers know-how ineffability.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Downgrade the Dualism paragraph to compatibility. Ineffability is what the Map's dualism predicts, but a physicalist compression account (Ji et al. 2024) predicts it too, so it does not discriminate between them. Leave any remaining support resting on the *hard-problem residue*: Ji explains why *reports* are lossy, not why there is something to be lost. That residue is a real and defensible point. Add one clause to the Ji paragraph noting that the account is physicalist-compatible. Revise the "Neuroscience reveals mechanism" falsifier to acknowledge that a candidate mechanism exists, and state what would still be left over. The change must be length-neutral: headroom is 39.

### Issue 2: Epistemic evidence recruited for a metaphysical conclusion (constitutive privacy)
- **File**: voids/language-thought-boundary.md
- **Location**: §Qualia as Limit Case, *Privacy and publicity* plus the closing paragraph ("Some content may be *constitutively* private, unable in principle to enter the public domain")
- **Problem**: This is interpretive equivocation. The evidence is epistemic: I cannot *check* whether my red matches yours, and reports compress. The conclusion is metaphysical: the content is constitutively private and unable in principle to enter the public domain. Worse, the triangulation argument (Wittgenstein's private-language argument and Davidson's triangulation) cuts the other way. If concept-formation requires triangulation and qualia cannot be triangulated, the standard conclusion is that there is no private *conceptual* content. That is the beetle-in-the-box deflation. The article states the premise of a deflationary argument and draws an anti-deflationary conclusion without a bridging step.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Split the readings. The epistemic reading (qualitative character cannot be *verified* or *transmitted* by description) is well supported. The metaphysical reading needs an argument, or should be marked as the Map's interpretation, not something the evidence delivers. Either acknowledge that the private-language argument is usually taken to support deflation and say why the Map resists it (e.g. non-conceptual acquaintance escapes the argument's target), or drop the triangulation premise.

### Issue 3: The Quine paragraph's conclusion is a non-sequitur
- **File**: voids/language-thought-boundary.md
- **Location**: §Unexplorable or Merely Unexplored?, "If that is right, there is no determinate content the translator is failing to capture, and an untranslatable term marks something genuinely unexplorable rather than merely unmapped."
- **Problem**: Quine's indeterminacy says that *several* manuals fit all the evidence and that there is no fact of the matter between them. That is overtranslatability, not untranslatability. If there is "no determinate content" to capture, nothing lies beyond the boundary, so there is no void, whether unexplorable or otherwise. The Quinean verdict deflates the void in the same way the Davidsonian one does. It does not support the "unexplorable" label. On the article's own framing, both "rival" positions end up on the deflationary side, and the paragraph's claim that the dispute is live between "unexplorable" and "difficulty" falls apart.
- **Severity**: Medium-High
- **Recommendation**: Recast Quine correctly: on his view an untranslatable term marks a place where the question "what does it *really* mean?" has no answer. That counts as a limit on determinacy, not a hidden content beyond reach. It is a third option next to "unexplorable" and "unexplored", and arguably it is the occluded-or-empty option. Roughly length-neutral.

### Issue 4: Citation mismatch, Russian blues attributed to a Greek study; several reference-list orphans
- **File**: voids/language-thought-boundary.md
- **Location**: §Linguistic Limits (Russian *siniy/goluboy*) and References 3, 5, 6, 7
- **Problem**: The only colour-perception reference is Thierry et al. (2009), PNAS 106(11): 4567–70. That study is about **Greek** (*ghalazio/ble*) and visual mismatch negativity. It is not about Russian, and it was checked at PNAS/PubMed on 2026-09-24. The Russian claim, "detect differences... faster than English speakers", is Winawer et al. (2007), PNAS 104(19): 7780–7785, which is not cited. References 3 (Boroditsky 2011), 5 (Franke 2014), 6 (Thierry 2009) and 7 (Nagel 1974) do not appear anywhere in the body: each surname occurs only once, in the reference list. Unsourced body claims include Kuuk Thaayorre (Boroditsky & Gaby 2010), the Japanese "when thinking in Japanese than in English" claim, the bilingual claim in the lede, Grandin, Einstein (the Hadamard letter), the Marion attribution, and Lomas "past 1,400".
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Either swap the Russian example for Greek, which is cheaper and fits the existing reference, or add Winawer 2007. Tie the Thierry, Boroditsky, Nagel and Franke references to body claims or remove them. The Japanese claim deserves a check. The best-known Japanese/English categorisation result, Imai & Gentner (1997), compares *speakers of the two languages*, not bilinguals switching language. The article's "when thinking in Japanese than in English" wording implies a within-subject language-switching result, which it must source or drop. Verify the Marion attribution for "calling something unthinkable makes it thinkable". That paradox is classically Augustine's (*De Doctrina Christiana* I.6), and Marion may be a secondary route to it. *Checked and fine*: the Chomsky quote is in "Science, Mind, and Limits of Understanding" (chomsky.info, 2014). Note, though, that there it concerns free will and the "creative aspect of language use", and the article's reframing of it as a language–thought limit is a stretch.

### Issue 5: The Tenet 3 paragraph assumes what it claims to show
- **File**: voids/language-thought-boundary.md
- **Location**: §Relation to Site Perspective, Bidirectional Interaction ("This deliberate investigation is mental causation in action")
- **Problem**: This is boundary-substitution in reverse: the tenet is asserted as if the phenomenon showed it. Every physicalist and epiphenomenalist agrees that we "choose" to investigate. What they dispute is whether the choosing is non-physical causation, and the paragraph gives no reason to prefer the Map's reading. Recent refine-draft passes on 2026-09-24 corrected exactly this move in sibling articles (`concepts/neural-correlates-of-consciousness`, `topics/cross-cultural-phenomenology-of-agency`): Tenet 3 support was brought down to "consistent with, not evidence for".
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Recast it as coherence: investigating the boundary is something the Map reads as mental causation, and an epiphenomenalist can describe the same activity. The void lends Tenet 3 no independent support. Merge or shorten the Tenet 2 paragraph, which says little, to fund this.

### Issue 6: The Occam's Razor paragraph is a non-sequitur
- **File**: voids/language-thought-boundary.md
- **Location**: §Relation to Site Perspective, "If simplicity guided truth, our linguistic tools should suffice for reality's articulation."
- **Problem**: Nothing links parsimony as a guide to theory choice with the expressive adequacy of natural language. A parsimony-favouring physicalist can hold both that simplicity guides truth and that language is lossy. The final sentence, about mistaking linguistic limits for logical ones, is the real point and does connect to Tenet 5: we may dismiss a position as "incoherent" when it is only hard to express.
- **Severity**: Low-Medium
- **Recommendation**: Delete the first conditional and lead with the incoherence-versus-inexpressibility point. This saves words.

### Issue 7: Unsupported strong claims in the phenomenology and infant passages
- **File**: voids/language-thought-boundary.md
- **Location**: §Language-as-Such Limits ("the acquisition of language permanently destroys the pre-linguistic mode of thought, making it inaccessible even to introspection"). §Phenomenology ("Mystics report this consistently"). §Unexplorable ("The dissolution-on-articulation phenomenon suggests interference").
- **Problem**: "Permanently destroys" is a metaphysical claim, and the evidence (infantile amnesia, the inaccessibility of early memories) is epistemic. This is the same equivocation as Issue 2 on a smaller scale, and adults plainly keep non-linguistic cognition, e.g. visual thinkers, which the article itself cites. "Consistently" has no source. "Suggests interference" jumps from a phenomenological report to an occluding mechanism.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Replace "permanently destroys" with "may make that mode inaccessible to later introspection". Replace "consistently" with "commonly". Replace "suggests interference" with "is at least compatible with interference".

## Counterarguments to Address

### Ineffability as evidence for dualism
- **Current content says**: Physical processes are describable, consciousness is not, so the difference points past physicalism.
- **A critic would argue**: Know-how, perceptual discriminations and any high-dimensional state compressed into a low-bandwidth channel are all ineffable in the same way. Ji et al. is a physicalist model of exactly this.
- **Suggested response**: Hand the *reportability* gap to the compression account and move the dualist weight onto the residue compression does not touch: why the compressed-away thing is *felt*. That is the hard problem, which the article can link to without re-arguing it.

### Deflation of ineffable content
- **Current content says**: The effable/ineffable asymmetry would be "puzzling" if ineffability were confusion.
- **A critic would argue**: The asymmetry is expected on the view that words transmit discriminations and experiences are the discriminative states themselves.
- **Suggested response**: Engage the know-how parallel directly, and say what, if anything, separates colour experience from riding a bicycle (the Mary/knowledge-argument literature is the natural link).
