## Overall verdict

The article is a lucid introduction to the inverted-spectrum intuition, but it is not yet a defensible argument for the conclusion it repeatedly draws from that intuition. Its sound conditional is:

1. If functionalism is true, complete functional duplicates cannot differ phenomenally.
2. If complete functional duplicates can differ phenomenally, functionalism is false.

Almost all the philosophical work lies in establishing the second premise. The article instead builds phenomenal difference into its scenario, labels the subjects functionally identical, and then treats the resulting description as evidence that the combination is possible. Stipulation is doing the work that argument should do.

The problem becomes acute when the article reaches the Map’s Bidirectional Interaction and Minimal Quantum Interaction tenets. It then concedes that an actual phenomenal difference should affect preferences, emotional valence, reaction times, introspective reports, or other behaviour, and concludes that strict behavioural identity is physically unrealised. That concession abandons the empirical and nomological versions of the argument. What remains is only the disputed claim that functionally identical but phenomenally inverted subjects are coherently conceivable or metaphysically possible. # 1. Does the article support phenomenal differences between functional duplicates?

## 1.1 It does not distinguish the required kinds of duplication

The article initially specifies that Invert and Nonvert pass the same tests, make the same discriminations, use identical neural pathways, and have identical behavioural effects. This is more than ordinary behavioural matching, but it is still not a complete account of functional duplication.

A complete functional role could include:

- every actual and counterfactual discrimination;
- attentional capture and perceptual salience;
- affective and aesthetic response;
- memory encoding and retrieval;
- learning and adaptation;
- confidence and metacognitive accessibility;
- reaction-time distributions;
- imagery and cross-modal associations;
- introspective classifications;
- causal and historical relations to the environment;
- every downstream quantum-level effect admitted by MQI.

The Stanford survey makes the relevant dialectical distinction explicit: behaviourally undetectable inversion is sufficient against behaviourism; **functionally** undetectable inversion is needed against functionalism; and **physically** undetectable inversion is needed against physicalism. It also warns that perfect internal duplicates may differ in extrinsic functional or representational respects. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+2Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qualia-inverted/)

The article repeatedly collapses those standards. Matching familiar colour tests does not establish complete functional identity. Matching a finite experimental battery does not establish identity across all counterfactual dispositions. And refuting functionalism would not by itself refute physicalism.

## 1.2 The Map’s own causal tenets destroy the stipulated duplication

The most important internal contradiction occurs in the MQI section. The article says an actual inversion should generate fine-grained differences in aesthetics, emotion, reaction time, or introspective report. But those are functional differences. A theory cannot simultaneously claim:

- phenomenal character is causally efficacious;
- phenomenal character differs between the subjects;
- every relevant functional consequence is held fixed.

At most, the Map can say that coarse tests may fail to detect subtle functional differences. That would be an empirically interesting hypothesis about **shifted or altered colour processing**, but it would no longer be a counterexample involving functional duplicates.

The philosophical-zombies article already handles the parallel problem more honestly. It says an interactionist must reject the full stipulation of a physical duplicate with identical reports, acknowledges that this weakens the zombie argument, and calls the remaining argument conditional. “Inverted Qualia” should import exactly that concession into its opening thesis and conclusion rather than leaving it buried near the end. [philosophical-zombies](/concepts/philosophical-zombies/)

## 1.3 The article slides among five distinct claims

The article moves without sufficient warning between:

1. **Describability:** one can write down the words “same function, different qualia.”
2. **Prima facie conceivability:** the description produces no immediately apparent contradiction.
3. **Ideal or positive conceivability:** sufficiently complete reflection still reveals no contradiction.
4. **Metaphysical possibility:** the scenario obtains in some possible world.
5. **Nomological or biological possibility:** beings governed by relevant laws could instantiate it.

The first is easy. The second is plausible. The third is contested. Neither the fourth nor fifth follows without a modal bridge.

The article acknowledges the Kripkean objection but answers that phenomenal concepts’ distinctiveness “itself requires explanation” and that no hidden essence has been exhibited. That does not establish possibility. Failure to know the identity-maker is not evidence that there is none. Nor does an opponent’s explanatory debt establish the Map’s modal premise.

The article’s own final position is especially revealing:

- actual strict inversion is said to be physically unrealised;
- its causal theory predicts detectable functional consequences;
- the remaining force is described as “conceptual.”

That is a retreat from an empirical or biologically grounded possibility argument to a modal intuition. The conclusion should be recalibrated accordingly.

## 1.4 Anti-functionalism would still not entail dualism

The statement that phenomenal difference under functional identity would show that “something beyond physics” determines qualia is invalid.

Even a successful inverted-qualia argument against functionalism would leave open:

- type-identity or neural-identity physicalism;
- biological or substrate-sensitive physicalism;
- nonfunctionalist representational physicalism;
- physical views on which microphysical, dynamical, or historical properties omitted by the functional description determine experience.

The Stanford taxonomy correctly requires physical duplication, not merely functional duplication, for an argument against physicalism. The Map’s own first-order-representationalism article also notes that Block is a physicalist: his objections to strong representationalism and functionalism do not thereby support dualism. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+2The Unfinishable Map+2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qualia-inverted/)

Thus the article’s proper conclusion is, at most:

> If metaphysically possible beings can share every relevant functional property while differing phenomenally, functionalism about phenomenal character is false.

It cannot move directly from that conditional to nonphysical properties.

## 1.5 Inverted Earth is overstated

The Inverted Earth section says that after 50 years the traveller’s functional role has “completely shifted” while the qualitative character remains unchanged. Neither half is established.

A wide functionalist may include causal history, environmental embedding, memory, adaptation and representational content in the relevant role. Whether the traveller has become fully functionally identical to a native is therefore exactly what is disputed. Similarly, the claim that the original qualitative character remains unchanged through decades of adaptation is not an independent datum. It is Block’s controversial verdict about the case.

The Stanford treatment presents Inverted Earth as an apparent counterexample built partly from content externalism, not as a settled demonstration that complete function changes while phenomenology stays fixed. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qualia-inverted/)

The article converts an argumentative scenario into an observed trajectory.

## 1.6 It omits the strongest direct counterargument—despite citing its author

Chalmers’s organizational-invariance argument is the most conspicuous omission. Chalmers, despite being a naturalistic property dualist, argues that systems with the same sufficiently fine-grained functional organization have the same phenomenal states as a matter of natural necessity. His Fading Qualia and Dancing Qualia arguments contend that phenomenal fading or switching under preserved organization would produce implausible radical errors or unnoticed experiential changes. [Consciousness.net+2Consciousness.net+2](https://consc.net/papers/qualia.html)

This matters for three reasons.

First, it demonstrates that dualism does not entail acceptance of inverted qualia under complete functional identity.

Second, it directly attacks the article’s central premise while preserving irreducible consciousness.

Third, it exposes the relevant dispute for the Map: what counts as the appropriate **grain of functional organization**? If the consciousness–quantum interface changes between Invert and Nonvert, the two systems differ causally and were never fine-grained functional duplicates. If the interface does not change anything, the Map has recreated the epiphenomenalism it rejects.

The neighbouring Functionalism page now links to organizational invariance and calls it the sharpest pro-functionalist case, but “Inverted Qualia” neither links to nor engages it. [functionalism](/concepts/functionalism/)

# 2. Missing or inadequate counterarguments

## 2.1 Representationalism

The article’s answer to representationalism is effectively:

> They represent the same redness through different qualitative modes.

*[unverified quote — the reviewer flags this as "effectively", and it is a compression, not the article's words. Source (concepts/inverted-qualia.md L104): "both may accurately represent redness through different qualitative modes." The blockquote drops the "may" hedge. The objection itself is fair.]*

This is not yet an argument. “Different qualitative modes” either means:

- different representational properties, in which case representationalism may accommodate them; or
- intrinsic nonrepresentational “mental paint,” in which case the anti-representational conclusion has been assumed.

The literature offers several distinctions the article omits:

- narrow versus wide representational content;
- appearance properties represented by experience;
- Fregean perceptual modes of presentation;
- relational or observer-relative colours;
- content externalism;
- the possibility that an alleged inverter misperceives despite using the community’s colour vocabulary correctly.

The Stanford survey treats inverted-spectrum cases as capable of supporting several different and partly incompatible conclusions, including appearance properties, modes of presentation and colour relativism. It also shows that the intrinsicness of qualia is a separate premise that representationalists may reject. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qualia-inverted/)

The Map’s first-order-representationalism article is better calibrated than the target article: it describes the dispute as unresolved and explicitly notes that Block’s “mental paint” position is pressure internal to physicalism rather than an argument for dualism. The target page should not reduce that live dispute to a two-sentence objection and reply. [first-order-representationalism](/concepts/first-order-representationalism/)

## 2.2 Quality-space asymmetry

The article correctly notes some human colour-space asymmetries, but then weakens the objection too easily.

A full quality space includes more than an abstract hue circle. Relevant structure includes lightness, saturation, discriminability, unique hues, category boundaries, similarity relations, warm–cool classifications and relations to achromatic experience. The Stanford survey explains why a simple 180-degree rotation fails: the three-dimensional colour solid and the number of discriminable steps are not symmetric under that transformation. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qualia-inverted/)

Palmer is also overstated. The article says Palmer showed certain alternatives are “plausible.” Palmer’s published abstract says that literal rainbow reversal would not work and that three other transformations **might** work, depending on the relevance of particular colour categories. That is a conditional opening, not a result establishing an undetectable inversion. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/color-consciousness-and-the-isomorphism-constraint/1EAB7ED27EF19CAE825EFE6B71C92EF6)

The article entirely omits Hilbert and Kalderon’s stronger response: they argue not merely that actual human colour space is asymmetric, but that every possible quality space must be asymmetric. That argument may be resisted, but it directly attacks the article’s move from actual asymmetry to invented symmetric creatures and therefore belongs in the central dialectic. [Hilbert+1](https://www.hilbert.people.uic.edu/papers/invertedspectrum.pdf)

Retreating to aliens with a stipulated symmetric quality space does not automatically rescue the argument. It sacrifices the claim of biological proximity and still leaves the disputed move from a describable formal permutation to a genuinely possible phenomenal permutation.

## 2.3 The phenomenal-concepts strategy

The article’s PCS treatment is rhetorically dismissive but philosophically incomplete.

“Phenomenal concepts themselves need explanation” *[unverified quote — the article's actual wording (L106) is "If phenomenal concepts work this differently, this itself requires explanation." The substance is the same; the quoted string is the reviewer's.]* is not a rebuttal; explaining their distinctive cognitive role is precisely what PCS proposals attempt. Likewise, the observation that we cannot exhibit a hidden physical essence of colour experience establishes our present epistemic position, not the absence of such an essence.

Recent work shows that the strategy remains technically active rather than resting on a generic water–H₂O analogy. Zhong’s 2024 discussion distinguishes competing direct-reference approaches and presses a double-aspect problem; Zhou’s 2025 account develops an “indicating without revealing” physicalism; and the Map’s own PCS page concedes that the exchange remains open. [The Unfinishable Map+3OUP Academic+3Springer+3](https://academic.oup.com/pq/article/74/3/978/7325259)

A serious treatment should specify:

1. which account of phenomenal concepts is under discussion;
2. whether the objection concerns reference, acquisition, cognitive role, conceptual isolation or explanatory adequacy;
3. why that particular account fails;
4. why its failure positively supports dualism rather than merely leaving the metaphysics unsettled.

The current paragraph treats the physicalist’s inability to provide a universally accepted explanation as evidence for the Map. That is the asymmetric standard identified in the changelog.

## 2.4 Contemporary work on colour experience and individual variation

The article’s empirical colour bibliography stops at Palmer in 1999. That is inadequate for a page invoking colour science and actual individual differences in 2026.

Recent results complicate both crude uniformity and crude inversion:

- A 2023 PNAS study found that interobserver variation in hue scaling is structured by multiple relatively local factors rather than one privileged global opponent-axis transformation. This supports real individual variation but works against a simple whole-space inversion model. [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.2202262120)
- A 2024 study found that repeated colour-discrimination training shifted discrimination thresholds, some unique hues, category boundaries and reported colour appearance. That indicates plasticity and coupling among experience, learning and discrimination—precisely the sort of functional manifestation a strict hidden-inversion case must exclude. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60283-4)
- Recent work has developed observer-specific image corrections based on measured hue scaling. Again, differences in appearance are inferred through systematic judgements and can be used to alter stimuli; they are not phenomenally radical but functionally invisible swaps. [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12124830/)
- A 2025 fMRI dataset operationalises colour-experience structure through extensive pairwise similarity judgements, including no-report conditions. This does not solve the metaphysics of qualia, but it shows that contemporary research studies relational phenomenal structure rather than treating colour experience as an inaccessible monadic label. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04511-0)
- Other 2025 neuroscience found area-specific colour biases shared across brains, providing evidence of substantial common neural structure alongside individual variation. [Journal of Neuroscience](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/45/42/e2717202025)

None of these studies proves phenomenal identity across people. But none supports a complete global inversion between functional duplicates either. The fair conclusion is that normal observers exhibit measurable, structured and partly plastic variation constrained by common perceptual and neural organisation. Actual variation is not evidence for strict inverted qualia because it generally appears in the very judgements, learning effects or neural–behavioural relations that make the observers functionally different.

## 2.5 Illusionism

The article’s illusionism section is better than its representationalism section because it concedes that the bare regress is disputed. Nevertheless, it then loads the evidential scales by requiring the illusionist to explain why phenomenal self-ascriptions are “systematic, universal, and resistant to correction” without citing evidence for universality or engaging recent positive models.

The neighbouring Illusionism article is notably more current. It explicitly says that the bare regress proves nothing, describes functional seemings, quasi-phenomenal properties and the distortion thesis, and cites work through 2025. It characterises the 2020s illusionist programme as active rather than stalled. [illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/)

The target article’s external references stop at Frankish 2016 and Strawson 2006. Its prose should be synchronised with the more careful neighbouring treatment. In particular, it must distinguish:

- the invalid bare-regress objection;
- the stronger self-representation objection;
- the demand to explain specific quasi-phenomenal content;
- the meta-problem of why subjects make robust phenomenal judgements.

# 3. The Map’s commitments are repeatedly treated as evidence

## 3.1 Intrinsicness is assumed in the description of the phenomenon

The article says qualia are intrinsic and private and that the redness of red is not structure or relation. Those are not neutral descriptions accepted by all sides. They are substantive conclusions rejected by functionalists, representationalists, relationalists and illusionists.

Once qualia are defined as intrinsic, nonrelational properties, functional and representational analyses have already been excluded by terminology. The scenario then appears to establish what its vocabulary presupposes.

The neighbouring Qualia article compounds the problem by first giving the neutral “what-it-is-like” sense, then treating intrinsicness, privacy, ineffability and direct apprehensibility as constitutive facts. It even claims that aesthetic quality space lacks the asymmetries affecting colour inversion, without supplying an argument or evidence. [qualia](/concepts/qualia/)

The site needs to distinguish:

- **minimal phenomenal character:** what an experience is like;
- **intrinsicness thesis;**
- **privacy thesis;**
- **ineffability thesis;**
- **direct-acquaintance thesis.**

Only the first should be definitional.

## 3.2 Dualism supplies the crucial possibility premise

The article says the thought experiment supports dualism, but its confidence in the experiment depends on already treating phenomenal properties as independent of functional and physical structure.

A neutral referee cannot accept:

> The phenomenal facts remain open after the functional facts because phenomenal facts are nonstructural.

*[unverified quote — this is the reviewer's own reconstruction of a premise, introduced as something "a neutral referee cannot accept" rather than as a citation; "nonstructural" appears nowhere in the article. The nearest actual text is L154: "physics describes structure and relations, but the redness of red is not a structure or relation—it's a quality… The specification leaves the qualitative facts open." The circularity charge is fairly aimed at that passage.]*

That is simply the Map’s dualist thesis expressed as a premise. The article must offer an independent reason to think the functional specification is complete while the phenomenal fact remains undetermined.

## 3.3 MQI is used as both repair and confirmation

MQI repairs the causal problem by predicting subtle behavioural differences. But the article then treats that prediction as though it aligned the thought experiment with colour science and strengthened the overall case.

It does the opposite. Once MQI creates downstream differences, the subjects are not functional duplicates. MQI may provide a framework in which phenomenal variation is causally expressed, but it cannot simultaneously preserve the anti-functionalist counterexample.

Nor is MQI independently evidenced by the inversion scenario. The reasoning would otherwise be circular:

1. assume nonphysical consciousness influences quantum outcomes;
2. infer that phenomenal differences create subtle functional differences;
3. use those differences to support the framework that predicted them;
4. continue describing the case as one of functional identity.

## 3.4 No Many Worlds is attached without a discriminating argument

The claim that first-person indexicality supports real collapse does not follow. An Everettian can grant that each branch-relative subject has a definite, indexical experience. The existence of a fact about what *this* subject experiences does not by itself establish that alternative branches are nonactual.

The latest changelog now recognises this problem elsewhere in the corpus. On 28 July it revised another article to concede that branch-local phenomenology does not discriminate between singular actualisation and branching, and it relabelled global nonactuality as a Map posit rather than a conclusion delivered by experience. “Inverted Qualia” retains the older overclaim and is now out of step with the site’s developing standard. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

## 3.5 The Occam section mistakes persistence for evidence

The continued philosophical popularity of a thought experiment does not show that its intended metaphysical interpretation is true. Persistence may reflect:

- a durable introspective intuition;
- limitations of human concepts;
- underdetermination;
- pedagogical vividness;
- disagreement over the standard of conceivability;
- the absence of an agreed theory of consciousness.

Rejecting simplistic appeals to parsimony does not supply positive evidence for dualism. At most it blocks one defeasible argument against adding ontology. The article instead moves from “Occam is unreliable under incomplete knowledge” to “the persistent intuition probably tracks something deep.” That is not a valid inference. *[unverified quotes — neither string is in the article. The first is a gloss of the tenet's title; the second paraphrases L172: "This persistence suggests the explanatory gap is not merely apparent but reflects something deep about consciousness's nature." Note the reviewer's "probably tracks" is stronger than the article's "suggests… reflects". The inference criticised is real, but it is hedged in the source.]*

## 3.6 Whitehead and meditation provide framework-relative illustrations, not confirmation

The Whitehead section may show that inversion can be described within a Whiteheadian ontology. It does not independently establish that Whitehead’s ontology is true, that phenomenal qualities are eternal objects, or that two distinct concrescences could produce completely identical causal profiles. The section also makes detailed claims about Whitehead without including a Whitehead primary source in the external bibliography.

The meditation section is weaker. It moves from alleged differences between jhāna and Madhyamaka reports to the conclusion that qualia may have both constructed and irreducible aspects. Divergent contemplative reports are compatible with differences in training, doctrine, attention, reporting vocabulary, interpretation and task. Moreover, divergent reports are themselves functional differences; they cannot support hidden inversion under functional identity.

# Final assessment

The article successfully explains why inverted qualia are intuitively troublesome for functionalism. It does not establish that complete functional duplicates can differ phenomenally. Its empirical material supports, at most, structured individual variation—not strict inversion. Its modal case relies on contested intuitions, and its response to the conceivability–possibility challenge is inadequate. Its interactionist commitments then remove the strict functional identity needed by the argument.

Most seriously, the article repeatedly turns the Map’s preferred conclusions—intrinsic qualia, dualism, quantum causal influence, single-world actualisation and suspicion of parsimony—into premises and then reports the argument as convergent evidence for those same conclusions.

The defensible position is narrower:

> Inverted qualia provide an intuitive, conditional challenge to functionalist theories of phenomenal character. Whether the relevant scenario is metaphysically possible remains disputed; actual individual colour variation does not instantiate it; and the Map’s interactionism predicts that genuine phenomenal differences would prevent complete functional duplication.
