## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would single out **testimony's §Testimony and Scientific Methodology**. It separates the *epistemic* reading ("consciousness is accessed first-personally", which "a physicalist grants … without strain") from the *metaphysical* reading. It then says plainly that the step from one to the other "needs a further premise the methodology does not supply: that the first-person mode of *access* tracks a first-person mode of *being*". This is the explanatory gap stated at the right strength. The article never pretends that report-dependence proves irreducibility. Its Dualism bullet later picks up that premise in the open: "This is the dualist premise doing the work, not a result the epistemology established on its own." The hard problem appears here as a structural feature of the evidence. The article does not use it as a rhetorical trump.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

**Collective phenomena** is the one wing article where the Minimal Quantum Interaction tenet actually carries weight. The article is honest about how much. The interface objection to group minds ("Communication between people … is classical, macroscopic, and far too coarse-grained to serve as a quantum consciousness interface") is labelled "a model-relative consequence, not an independent impossibility proof." §The Instructive Asymmetry concedes that "the null hypothesis that groups simply are not conscious predicts the same observation." Stapp would praise the move of placing *where* consciousness acts at the process-1 locus inside individual brains. He would praise more the refusal to count the absence of group minds as confirmation. Testimony's closing MQI line, "tangential here", is a good example of a tenet declared inactive instead of being forced into service.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The wing's best phenomenology is:

- **Intersubjectivity's Husserl section**: the other "is accessible precisely *insofar as* they are constituted as inaccessible. If I could directly enter your experience, you would not be another subject but an extension of me."
- **Social understanding's three features** of understanding a person: resonance, texture and *limits awareness* ("It feels like reaching and not quite touching").
- **Collective phenomena's Zahavi subsection**, where shared grief "is not a summation of separate griefs but a mode of experiencing grief available only in the plural."

Nagel would add **testimony's §Testimonial chains**. A meditator's report passes to a commentator, then a philosopher, then a textbook, and "each transit re-crosses an intersubjective void, preserving propositional structure while losing the original phenomenal ground." That explains why consciousness discourse gets worn smooth by retelling, and I have not seen the point made this way elsewhere.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Intersubjectivity's **co-emergence** option (Merleau-Ponty's intercorporeality) and its closing "ontologically individual, phenomenologically enriched" position avoid crude substance dualism. Individual minds stay real, but they are not self-sufficient atoms. Collective phenomena's claim that "I-mode and we-mode are different configurations of the same consciousness, not different consciousnesses" is the same instinct at the social scale. None of this praise is used to raise the evidential status of any creature. The wing makes no tier claims on process-philosophical grounds.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

**Trust's §Trust as Conscious Risk** is the wing's agency article. It draws on Holton's point that "you can decide to trust someone in a way you cannot decide to believe something" and makes trust a self-forming act: "You cannot genuinely trust someone without experiencing the choice to do so." Social understanding's use of Strawson's reactive attitudes, and collective phenomena's treatment of obligation as something a violator "understood and chose to breach", make the same point for accountability. The wing consistently treats persons as sources of commitments, not only as channels for them.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

Intersubjectivity's claim that understanding another person "appears to deepen indefinitely without reaching a terminus" is humility built into the subject matter. So is social understanding's "Some regions of another person's experience may be permanently beyond reach." Social construction's handling of Metzinger is the model of honest non-victory: the Map's disjunction "engages the self-model view on the Map's terms; it does not defeat it on Metzinger's." The same article treats the regress against eliminativists and Buddhist *anatta* the same way: "decisive *given* the Map's commitment … not … an in-framework refutation."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This persona decides the verdict for the wing, and the verdict comes in two parts.

**Praise, where restraint was chosen:**

- **Social construction, §Developmental priority.** The article had every structural opportunity to read Rochat's neonate self-touch data as evidence for a phenomenal subject, and it declined. The data are "compatible with a purely *biological* self-model"; Rochat's levels emerge "precisely *through* social mirroring"; "the phenomenal-subject claim rests on the transcendental argument … not on the neonate data alone." It applies the same discipline to the exile and depersonalisation cases: "Dissolution-in-adults is therefore neutral between the two views." This is the "tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating" pattern applied to developmental evidence, and it is worth naming as the wing's standard.
- **Social understanding, §The Developmental Arc.** "What the data do is remove a defeater … the arc is what the synchronic argument predicts, not an independent confirmation of it." This is the defeater-removal vs. evidence-upgrade distinction stated almost exactly.
- **Testimony's LLM paragraph** is the wing's best treatment of the AI question. The claim "is therefore best stated as a *framework commitment* rather than an established result", and the honest register is "*if* testimony is constituted by its felt dimension, *then* a non-conscious system transmits data without testifying in the full sense — not a flat denial that machines testify."
- **Intersubjectivity** concedes that the developmental record "underdetermines the constitutive question". It also carries the Oostenbroek et al. (2016) non-replication of neonatal imitation, instead of leaning on Meltzoff & Moore (1977) alone.

**Calibration concerns (flagged, not praised).** In each case below, the calibrated version already exists in a sibling article:

1. **"Would be coincidental" without the common-cause reply.** See intersubjectivity L119: "some of the strongest everyday evidence for bidirectional interaction … would be coincidental". See also social understanding L159: "the systematic correlation between expanded consciousness and expanded social complexity across species would be coincidental. The Map finds this implausible". Testimony's Bidirectional bullet names the physicalist's "ready third option", that both report and experience are "fixed by the same physical state", and answers it. Collective phenomena's bullet does the same for epiphenomenalism and ends "falls short of proof". The two laggards claim more than the article that does the work. Social understanding's phrase "expanded consciousness … across species" also assumes a cross-species *scale of degree of consciousness* that the Map does not measure anywhere. That is a tier assertion hidden inside a tenet bullet.
2. **No-Many-Worlds bullets that misdescribe MWI.** Intersubjectivity L121 says the other must be "not a superposition of all possible versions of them across branching worlds." On Everett, within a branch the other person is not a superposition. Testimony's bullet already concedes exactly this: "Testimony does not fail branch-internally … the Map's objection rests on the singular ownership … that its rejection of branching (a tenet, not a derived result) secures". Collective phenomena's bullet frames its version as a conditional "boundary disagreement, not a demonstration." Social understanding L161 has the same unconceded wording as intersubjectivity.
3. **An unfalsifiable prediction labelled falsifiable.** See social understanding L145: "AI systems will continue to improve at social tasks indefinitely without achieving genuine social understanding … The prediction is falsifiable in principle". "Genuine" is defined phenomenally in the same article, so no behavioural result can falsify the prediction. The article's own "What Would Challenge This View?" list gives better falsifiers than this sentence does. Testimony's conditional register is the fix.
4. **Trust's closures.** Three spots in trust close questions too quickly:
   - L123 ("This is not mere optimism bias; it reflects the causal efficacy of conscious states") uses the style guide's banned construction, and it states Tenet 3 as the explanation of self-fulfilling trust. A physicalist predicts the same self-fulfilling dynamic.
   - L115 ("The Map maintains that none of these challenges currently succeeds") is flat where testimony and collective phenomena say "left standing".
   - L79 turns habitual trust without phenomenology into "trust degrading into mere reliance". That definitional move protects the necessity claim from its obvious counterexample. The move may be defensible, but it should be named as a stipulation.
5. **A downward tier slip.** Collective phenomena L74: "Ant colonies demonstrate that intelligence without consciousness is possible." The Map's own `invertebrate-consciousness-as-interface-test` treats insect consciousness at "realistic possibility" (New York Declaration). The *colony-level* claim is fine, because no colony mind is posited. Stated about "ant colonies" without that scope, the sentence asserts an absence the Map does not hold. Birch's discipline cuts both ways: asserting that something is absent is as much a tier error as asserting that it is present. The fix is scope ("colony-level intelligence without a colony-level subject"). It needs no new argument.
6. **Lead only, not verified this pass.** Social understanding L117: "Emotional contagion requires no consciousness — mirror neurons … can produce it mechanically, as Frans de Waal's research … indicates (de Waal, 2008)." The Russian-doll model in de Waal 2008 places contagion at the core of empathy. I have not confirmed that it claims contagion *requires no consciousness*. Check this against the paper before editing. It is a possible citation-framing-accuracy-lens case, not a finding.

The Process Philosopher and Hardline Empiricist personas do not conflict anywhere in this wing. No article uses process-philosophical resonance to raise anyone's evidential status. The calibration concerns above are all *overclaims of necessity or evidence*, and they get `refine-draft` tasks, not expansion tasks.

## Content Strengths

### consciousness-and-testimony
- **Strongest point**: the epistemic/metaphysical split in §Testimony and Scientific Methodology, and the eliminativist section's honest scoring: "the debt is real, not that it has been paid."
- **Notable quote**: "The circle is not vicious because the alternative — a consciousness-independent check on consciousness — asks for something incoherent, not merely unavailable."
- **Why it works**: it answers the unfalsifiability charge by showing that every basic epistemic source (perception included) has the same structure. It does not simply declare the circle "productive".

### consciousness-and-collective-phenomena
- **Strongest point**: the two-claim opening ("Groups do not think; but what groups achieve requires thinkers") and the Gilbert / List–Pettit subsection. That subsection admits that Gilbert "is therefore an ally on the Map's first claim … while pressing on the second" and that "making that out properly is work this article does not do."
- **Why it works**: the 2026-09-20 deep review left an article whose every tenet bullet states its own limits.

### social-construction-of-self-vs-phenomenal-self
- **Strongest point**: the structure/content distinction. Social construction shapes *what* you experience yourself as and cannot generate *that* there is a subject. The Winawer Russian-blues and Barrett material is given its full strength first, as "the constructionist's strongest point".
- **Notable quote**: "The labelling is social; the arousal is not."
- **Why it works**: it lets the rival win the territory the rival actually holds.

### consciousness-and-intersubjectivity
- **Strongest point**: the three-position map of the priority question (consciousness first, intersubjectivity first, co-emergence), and the asymmetry section's third point, that the gap "gives moral significance to the attempt."

### consciousness-and-social-understanding
- **Strongest point**: the two-therapists case and its careful comparison with Mary: "The parallel is not exact: Mary gains a first-person quale, while the therapist uses first-person experience for second-person understanding."

### phenomenology-of-trust
- **Strongest point**: the bridge/friend contrast, and the betrayal section's argument that asymmetric Bayesian updating explains the *magnitude* but not the *kind* of betrayal's damage ("What is damaged is not an estimate but an orientation").
