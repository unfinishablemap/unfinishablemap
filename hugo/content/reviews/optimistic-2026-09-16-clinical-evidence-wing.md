## Executive Summary

The wing's distinctive strength is that the "constrain, don't establish" discipline is done *inside* the articles rather than delegated to reviewers: conversion-disorder partitions its own evidence into tiers and forbids the robust tier from lending weight to the interpretive one; phantom-limb runs a common-cause-null audit on its own three pillars; clinical-neuroplasticity down-counts three streams to one preferential signature and names the exact move ("Removing a defeater is not the same as upgrading the evidence"); disconnection-neuroscience publishes a three-rule citation discipline for everyone who cites it. The article that states the rulebook all of this follows — the five standards in `clinical-evidence-quality-standards` — is a near-orphan that no wing article links, although five of them invoke `evidential-status-discipline` at the very points where the clinical standards apply. One article, `consciousness-and-neurodegenerative-disease`, is the calibration outlier: its body is careful ("an interpretation, not a straightforward observation") but its tenet section says the evidence "supports" non-identity and that "the observations favour" the filter reading — the Process-Philosopher-friendly framing doing tier-upgrade work the Hardline Empiricist must flag. Two expansion opportunities are genuinely absent from the corpus and would strengthen the wing's central claim rather than decorate it: the anosognosia *reversal* literature (a lesioned-substrate case with an experimentally controlled, reversible trigger) and sham-controlled neurofeedback (the closest design the literature has to the "consciousness placebo" the quality-standards article says cannot exist).

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Three articles locate the gap exactly where Chalmers puts it — between a fully specified production story and the felt character it is supposed to produce — and each declines the easy version. `phantom-limb-phenomena` is the cleanest: it concedes that neuromatrix and predictive-processing theory "were constructed to specify exactly this production-conditions story," then holds that phantoms "exhibit the standard hard problem in a setting where the failure to bridge is particularly stark, without differentially refuting materialism." The article even supplies an operational anchor for its own framework commitment (a non-content-vocabulary derivation of the phenomenal-aboutness of impossible-posture pain "would warrant retracting the commitment"). `clinical-phenomenology-and-altered-experience` L103 makes the in-principle point about melancholic time and explicitly refuses the argument from present ignorance: "The point is not that neuroscience has not *yet* found the measure — that would be an argument from present ignorance, the eliminativist's vitalism analogy in reverse." And `synaesthesia` states the residue after predictive processing has taken its share with unusual precision: the model "does not say why deploying one is accompanied by a colour rather than producing a system that discriminates and reports as though it were." Chalmers would recognise each as the hard problem stated at clinical resolution, not restated as a slogan.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp's own proposal appears twice in the wing and is handled the way he would want a sympathetic site to handle it — as a serious mechanism, kept at model level. `clinical-neuroplasticity-evidence-for-bidirectional-causation` presents the Schwartz–Stapp–Beauregard quantum Zeno account as "one possibility," quotes the 2005 paper directly, and adds "This proposal is speculative and faces challenges regarding decoherence timescales in warm biological tissue." `neuroplasticity` is more careful still: the conservation-law point is stated "As a property *of the model*… That is a feature of the theoretical proposal, not an established fact about brains." Stapp would also note that `clinical-evidence-quality-standards` turns its five standards on the Map's own quantum claims and grades them honestly ("the appropriate language is 'compatible with' or 'suggestive of'"), which is the kind of self-application that makes the quantum-mind position credible to outsiders.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`clinical-phenomenology-and-altered-experience` is the wing's Nagelian centre. Its form/content distinction ("A depressed person does not merely feel sad about particular things — the entire temporal structure of experience shifts"), its "double grounding" of first-person report by third-person physiology, and above all its "involuntary variation" point — clinical disruption "eliminates the worry that first-person reports merely reflect the practitioner's expectations or training" — together answer the standard objection to contemplative evidence from an unexpected direction. `consciousness-and-neurodegenerative-disease` keeps what-it-is-like at the centre of the identity question ("She has lost the ability to narrate her identity, not the identity itself"). And `synaesthesia` insists on the point Nagel would insist on: within-species divergence is "not exotic, not cross-species, not hypothetical… The two minds whose phenomenology diverges share the same neural architecture."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

`disconnection-neuroscience` is where the wing is most Whitehead-friendly: the transmitter-not-producer reading, in which "the channel is broken, the broadcast is intact, the receiver simply does not get this part of it," treats the mind–brain relation as a coupling of processes rather than a substance sitting in a substrate. `clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence` reads DID as "different organisation of access within [one brain]" rather than different tissue, and the wing consistently prefers relational and connectivity-level descriptions to localisation. Whitehead would praise all of this as avoiding crude substance dualism.

**Constraint applied**: this wing contains no minimal-organism content, so the Process Philosopher's praise cannot be tested against nematodes or *Hydra*. It can be tested against terminal lucidity and dementia, and there the praise must stop short. The process-friendly framing in `consciousness-and-neurodegenerative-disease` — "Disease destroys the tools without destroying the user" — is tenet-coherent. It is *not* evidence-elevating, and the article's tenet section treats it as if it were (see the Hardline Empiricist below). Process-philosophical compatibility removes a defeater for the filter reading of neurodegeneration; it supplies no positive evidence that consciousness persists undiminished behind the behavioural markers.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane finds his datum in `conversion-disorder-as-consciousness-side-fault`, where the deficit is indexed to *where the agent's volition is directed*: Hoover's sign shows "the strength is present, but only when volition is not directed *at* the affected limb," and the article states the structure carefully — "the access fails as a function of where conscious volition is directed." That directed volition is a variable the clinic can manipulate, and that its redirection restores capacity, is the closest thing the wing has to an experimental handle on agency. `neuroplasticity` adds the willed/instructed attention distinction ("the *quality* of consciousness — whether one is genuinely deciding or merely implementing — may affect plasticity outcomes"), and `clinical-neuroplasticity` keeps the Map's commitment to "the reality of mental causation… not to any specific mechanism." Kane would want the wing to keep the agency datum and the interface interpretation apart, and it does: conversion's Bidirectional Interaction section grants that "an epiphenomenalist or functionalist redescribes the failure as altered connectivity within the physical domain."

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

McGinn's praise goes to three explicit refusals to close. `clinical-evidence-quality-standards` L120: "A finding can clear every methodological bar and still leave the question of consciousness's nature exactly where it was." `clinical-dissociation` closes its trauma section with "The selectivity is real; which framework earns it the clinical data do not settle." And `consciousness-and-neurodegenerative-disease` L75 makes the admission a less honest article would bury: the filter theory's flexibility "means it can accommodate almost any observation pattern. This is the hallmark of an unfalsifiable framework, and honesty requires acknowledging it" — followed by a real "What Would Challenge This View?" list. McGinn would call this the correct epistemic posture for a domain where the evidence constrains but does not decide.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This persona's verdict is load-bearing for the wing because every article rests on the same inference pattern — clinical selectivity or reversibility read as interface architecture — and the whole question is whether the tier is held.

**Praise, by article, for tier discipline done in-body:**

- `conversion-disorder-as-consciousness-side-fault` is the wing's exemplar. It grades three layers separately: the architectural finding "sits at the *strongly-supported* tier," the sedation recovery "is a *realistic possibility* on its own and must not be quoted as though it were the robust part," and the interface reading "sits at the *live-hypothesis* tier." Then the rule: "The robustness of the architecture must not be allowed to lend its weight to the interface reading." That is the named pattern — tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating — written as a sentence the article obeys.
- `disconnection-neuroscience` publishes the discipline as citation rules: "*Do not let layer 1 lend its grade to layer 2*… A passage that has used the channel reading to organise the data must not then cite the data as if the data had thereby earned the channel reading the empirical force of the anatomy." Birch would want every clinical article in the corpus to carry this paragraph.
- `clinical-neuroplasticity-evidence-for-bidirectional-causation` L104 names the move exactly: "Clinical neuroplasticity therefore removes a defeater — the epiphenomenalist claim that consciousness can make no measurable difference — without thereby establishing dualist causation over its physicalist rival. Removing a defeater is not the same as upgrading the evidence for one's preferred reading."
- `phantom-limb-phenomena` runs the common-cause-null audit on itself and reports the result against interest: "The base is single; the three pillars are not three independent confirmations."
- `clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence` discounts its own four-fold convergence when predictive processing offers a shared upstream cause ("The routes' residual independence is weaker than four-fold") and concludes "the signature earns the *architecture* while leaving its dualist *significance* to be argued separately."
- `synaesthesia` states its tier in so many words: "The interface reading is speculative-integration tier rather than empirically forced."
- `clinical-evidence-quality-standards` supplies the meta-rule: "Raising evidence quality removes defeaters and narrows the space of tenable readings; it does not by itself convert a metaphysically neutral result into support for one framework over another."

**Calibration concern 1 — `consciousness-and-neurodegenerative-disease` (flag, not praise).** The body is calibrated: L85 "One way to read this hierarchy — though it is an interpretation, not a straightforward observation"; L89 "The Map reads it as evidence for persisting consciousness; a sceptic could read the same behaviour as intact affective circuitry operating without a phenomenal subject"; L107 concedes the behavioural markers "may not track phenomenal experience reliably." The Relation to Site Perspective then outruns it. L129: "The persistence of phenomenal consciousness through massive neural destruction supports the view that consciousness is not identical to brain processes" — the body has just said the persistence is inferred from markers that may not track it. L137: "in this domain the observations favour a more complex relationship between brain and consciousness than production models provide" — the body's own account is that production theorists "can argue that consciousness requires only a minimal neural substrate," which it calls "a reasonable response." L135's No-Many-Worlds paragraph claims MWI "would treat the identity question as indeterminate: in some branches the patient retains identity, in others she doesn't," which is not what any Everettian holds about a single patient's psychological continuity within a branch. The article uses none of the five-tier vocabulary and has zero links to `evidential-status-discipline`; it is the only wing member besides `neuroplasticity` with neither. This is the Process Philosopher's praise cashing out as a tier-upgrade, and per the review contract it generates a `refine-draft` task (Medium Priority 1), not an expansion. The fix is small — the body already contains the calibrated sentences; the tenet section needs to be brought down to them.

**Calibration concern 2 — `neuroplasticity` L127 (minor).** Under "Neuroplasticity demonstrates:" the article lists "Directed mental practice produces measurable structural and functional changes," while its own meditation section (L105) reports that Kral et al. 2022 "found no evidence of structural brain changes from MBSR" and that "Structural claims from short-term meditation programmes should be treated with caution." The list item is not false in general (skill-learning structural plasticity is a separate literature) but the article cites no such literature, so as written the "demonstrates" list asserts what the body withdrew. A one-clause hedge suffices; folded into Medium Priority 1's sibling task rather than minted separately, since the same task already touches integration in this file.

**Where the two personas converge**: on `conversion-disorder`, `disconnection-neuroscience` and `phantom-limb`, the Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist praise the same passages for different reasons — the transmitter reading is developed *and* held at live-hypothesis. Those three have resolved the tension honestly. `consciousness-and-neurodegenerative-disease` has not.

#### 2. Sham-controlled neurofeedback as the nearest thing to a consciousness comparator
- **Builds on**: [clinical-evidence-quality-standards-consciousness-research](/topics/clinical-evidence-quality-standards-consciousness-research/) L50 ("there is no 'consciousness placebo' — no way to run a clinical intervention with all the same physical components minus the conscious experience") and L106 (control adequacy), [neuroplasticity](/concepts/neuroplasticity/) L117 ("attention… selects which neural patterns receive reinforcement") and L140 (the "fair test"), [brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary](/topics/brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary/) L63 (neurofeedback BCIs defined; 3818w), [the-observer-witness-in-meditation](/topics/the-observer-witness-in-meditation/) L52 (Garrison 2013 real-time fMRI neurofeedback).
- **Would address**: **Measured 2026-09-16**: "neurofeedback" occurs in 7 live body files, none pairing it with "sham" (grep of each file: 0); Thibault 0 hits, Schabus 0 hits corpus-wide; no article, research note, or todo line. Sham neurofeedback is the design that gets closest to the missing comparator: the same conscious effort, the same belief that one is training one's brain, with the feedback decoupled from the subject's own signal. The headline result — Schabus et al. 2017, *Brain* 140(4), 1041–1052, DOI 10.1093/brain/awx011, double-blind placebo-controlled, "Better than sham?", finding real feedback no better than sham in primary insomnia — and the methodological synthesis in Thibault, Lifshitz & Raz, *Brain* 141(2), e11, DOI 10.1093/brain/awx330 (both verified at Crossref this run) are a test the Map's Tenet-3 reading must price rather than avoid. Two readings and the article must state both: (a) sham-equivalence deflates "attention selects patterns" to expectancy, which the quality-standards article's own unblinding section would then have to absorb; (b) the sham arm *is* a mental-effort condition — the subject's intention is real even when the feedback is fake — so sham-equivalence may show that intention, not contingent feedback, does the work, which is what the interactionist would predict. Which reading the data support is decidable only by designs that vary intention while holding feedback fixed, and the article should say what those would look like. Host check: quality-standards is 48 words from hard and cannot host; neuroplasticity has 896 headroom but three articles need the page, so a dedicated topic is the right unit.

- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 3 (the fair test named in `neuroplasticity` L140); Tenet 5 (the deflationary reading is simpler and may still be wrong about which arm is the control).
