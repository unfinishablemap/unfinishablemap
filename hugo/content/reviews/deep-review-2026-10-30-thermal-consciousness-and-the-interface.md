- **Single-route thermal architecture asserted in Map voice (L71)**. Old: "one receptor sheet, one lamina I pathway, one insular destination serve both reports". This is Craig's routing, and L51 correctly attributes it ("On Craig's reading"). But at L71 it was stated as fact, and it is contested. Nakamura & Morrison 2008 report that the cutaneous thermoregulatory afferent pathway runs from spinal neurons through the lateral parabrachial nucleus to the preoptic area, and that it "exists in parallel with the spinothalamocortical somatosensory pathway that mediates temperature perception". Yahiro et al. 2017 (*Sci Rep* 7:5031, doi 10.1038/s41598-017-05327-8): in thalamic-lesioned rats the cortical response to skin temperature was compromised, yet heat- and cold-avoidance behaviour stayed intact, and LPB inactivation abolished both. The interoceptive (thermoregulatory) role therefore does not share Craig's insular route, at least in the rat. **Fixed** (+20 body, +18 reference): "one receptor sheet serves both reports, though in the rat the thermoregulatory signal then ascends via the lateral parabrachial nucleus, parallel to the spinothalamocortical pathway that mediates temperature perception (Nakamura & Morrison, 2008)". The claim is scoped to the rat because Craig argues the primate lamina I–VMpo–insula projection differs. The gradient argument keeps its premise, since one receptor sheet still serves both roles. The downstream fork fits Crucianelli & Ehrsson's separability, which the paragraph already engages ("distinguishable, not which side the modality lives on"). Nakamura and Morrison were added to the L89 naturalist roster (+2), so neither is enlisted for dualism.
