## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found (all fixed)

1. **Lockwood's response misdescribed (attribution error).** L77 said "Lockwood argued the problem dissolves under contemporary physics, where fields rather than discrete particles are fundamental." The primary text (Lockwood 1993, consc.net/event/reef/lockwoodgrain.pdf) says otherwise on both counts:
   - It explicitly **rejects** the emergent-field route: such fields would be "in stark contrast to any actual field known to science, and in clear violation of the theoretical demands of quantum mechanics. Well I, for one, simply don't believe it".
   - His own proposal rests on the quantum-mechanical observable. Quantum mechanics shows "it is a classical prejudice to suppose that the world is not intrinsically structured at anything but the level of elementary particles". There are "no observables, or sets thereof, which are a priori privileged". The contents of consciousness "correspond to eigenvalues of a set of observables which ... are distinct from anything that the physiologist is likely to settle on".
   - Lockwood presents this as "the bare bones of the theory I favour", which he says can "render it intelligible". He does not say the problem dissolves.

   **Fix**: "Lockwood traced the problem to "a classical prejudice": quantum mechanics privileges no set of observables, so awareness may track a set of brain observables different from any a physiologist would choose." The quoted phrase was grep-verified in the raw PDF text.

2. **Grain-problem origin date contradicts the corpus's own settled attribution.** The 09-27 refine wrote "originating with Wilfrid Sellars (1965)", following Lockwood's n.9 ("a difficulty attributed to Wilfrid Sellars", citing *Review of Metaphysics* 18, 1965). The corpus had already settled on 2026-08-06 that the pink-ice-cube argument first appears in PSIM (1962), and `concepts/grain-mismatch` and `voids/resolution-void` date it 1962.
   - Re-checking the 1965 text (ditext.com/sellars/ia.html) shows the two attributions are compatible. §VI ([204]–[206]) restates the argument in abstract form: raw-feel universals would not become "internally more complex" when identified with brain-state universals, unlike chemical universals identified with micro-physical ones. That is the passage Lockwood cites.
   - **Fix**: "(1962, 1965)", with a PSIM References entry in the corpus's canonical form.
   - "named and developed by Michael Lockwood (1993)" became "developed by". Lockwood 1993 makes no naming claim; its n.1 says his views were first set out in ch. 10 of *Mind, Brain and the Quantum* (1989).

3. **Seager's view narrowed, and an unverifiable quoted coinage.** L89 said "the resulting brain becomes a "big simple" without experiential parts."
   - Seager's own 2010 term is "large simples": "something which is metaphysically simple: utterly lacking parts". The SEP *Panpsychism* entry, which Seager co-authors, says the post-fusion brain "entirely lacks parts".
   - "Without experiential parts" therefore narrowed the view (dropped qualifier). The quoted "big simple" could not be grep-verified in the cited 2016/2017 chapter.
   - **Fix**: "the post-fusion conscious brain is a simple, entirely lacking parts". The quotation marks were dropped.

4. **Map-internal cross-link overclaim (calibration).** L121 said the [mathematical-structure-of-the-consciousness-physics-interface](/topics/mathematical-structure-of-the-consciousness-physics-interface/) page "reaches a parallel primitiveness conclusion", "leaving an unanalysable coupling".
   - That page (L140–L144) finds only that the selection operator "has no agreed mathematical form". It states: "Whether this gap reflects scientific immaturity or a fundamental limit of mathematical description is itself an open question."
   - The article had turned an open question into a conclusion.
   - **Fix**: the article now says the page "leaves open whether that reflects scientific immaturity or a fundamental limit; on the latter reading" the residue reappears as the irreducible seam. Word count is unchanged.

5. **Map-internal cross-link overclaim.** The L133 parenthetical said [four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy](/topics/four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy/) shows "that the combination problem is what pulls the Map away from thick-mind panpsychist quadrants even when the monist temptation is strong".

   - **Fix**: the parenthetical now states only those two placements. This saves 15 words.

6. **Open P3 task discharged.** The L125 mysterian paragraph had the flat verdict "a framework that faces an *empirically locatable* mystery (interaction) is preferable". This clashed with the paragraph's own closing hedge and with the open-individualism sibling's "locatable in principle".
