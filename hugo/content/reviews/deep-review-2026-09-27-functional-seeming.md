## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found
- **Misattribution: qualia taxonomy cited to Frankish (2023).** The classic/diet/zero taxonomy is set out in Frankish (2012) "Quining Diet Qualia". The 2023 *Klesis* paper mentions "zero qualia" only in footnote 7 ("I have also used the term 'zero qualia' (Frankish, 2012)"), which I confirmed by grepping the raw eprint text. The glosses were also unfaithful. Diet qualia were glossed as "retaining some mystery", but Frankish defines them as "the phenomenal characters (subjective feels, what-it-is-likenesses, etc.) of experience" and argues they have *no distinctive content*. Zero qualia were glossed as "minimal functional states...", where his definition is "the properties of experiences that dispose us to judge that experiences have introspectable qualitative properties that are intrinsic, ineffable, and subjective". The article's classic-qualia list (ineffable/intrinsic/private/directly apprehensible) was Dennett's 1988 list, not Frankish's definition. **Fixed:** the section is rewritten to cite Frankish 2012 with his definitions and his actual argument (the diet notion collapses), plus a note that the 2023 paper keeps "zero qualia" as an alias for quasi-phenomenal properties.
- **Orphan reference: Frankish (2016)** was listed in References but never cited inline. **Fixed:** it is now cited for quasi-phenomenal properties (the 2023 paper says "I have dubbed ... quasi-phenomenal properties (Frankish, 2016a)") and for the zombie section (§1.7 "Zombies and what it is like").
- **Named idea cited to the wrong work: Pereboom's "qualitative inaccuracy hypothesis"** was cited only to 2016. The hypothesis is from Pereboom (2011) *Consciousness and the Prospects of Physicalism*, which Frankish 2016 itself cites (Pereboom 2011, pp. 34–5). **Fixed:** both works are now cited and Pereboom 2011 is added to References.
