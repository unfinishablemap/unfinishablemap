The [voids](/voids/) framework explores individual cognitive limits—what a single mind cannot think. Groups have limits of their own: blind spots that depend on relations between minds, such as shared frameworks, the social cost of dissent, and shared instruments. This article surveys three accounts (Kuhn on paradigms, Zerubavel on conspiracies of silence, the Overton Window) and one measured case, the narrowing of what writers produce collectively with language-model help. Most of these limits are Occluded: socially maintained, with known mechanisms and known remedies such as independent judgement and protected dissent. That makes them practical limits. Paradigm-bound limits come nearest to the Unexplorable, and history shows them to be temporary. Ordinary social psychology and the sociology of knowledge predict all of it, so the void does not discriminate between dualism and physicalism and supports none of the Map's tenets.

## The Collective Void

Individual cognitive closure, as McGinn describes, arises from the architecture of individual minds. Collective limits arise from the way understanding is shared. A paradigm or a culture supplies the concepts that make thought possible while leaving others out; a climate of opinion prices some thoughts out of public use.

Coordinating thought across minds introduces constraints—shared vocabularies, social incentives, status hierarchies—that individual cognition does not face. You might see the elephant in the room, and I might see it too, yet *we* cannot discuss it. Collective limits are emergent in this sense: they depend on relational properties (power, paradigm membership, the cost of dissent) that exist between minds and not within them. Each account below describes a different mechanism by which collectives fail to think, or to say, what individuals might alone.

## Paradigm Blindness

Thomas Kuhn's *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* describes how a shared framework shapes what its practitioners notice. His oxygen case is the standard example: Priestley isolated the gas in 1774 and, working within phlogiston theory, identified it the next year as "common air with less than its usual quantity of phlogiston." He had the substance in hand and lacked the category.

The Copernican case is often told as a story of blindness, and Kuhn rejects that telling. Aristarchus proposed a heliocentric scheme in the third century BC and it was ignored; Kuhn's explanation is that the geocentric system was then "vastly more reasonable" and that "there were no obvious reasons for taking Aristarchus seriously." The proposal was available and was declined on the evidence of its day.

Kuhn's claim about successive scientific traditions is that they are "not only incompatible but often actually incommensurable." His Postscript to the second edition adds that communication across the divide is "inevitably partial" and that the parties can "become translators." Nor does normal science simply train its practitioners to miss anomalies. Kuhn says it "often suppresses fundamental novelties," and also that "novelty shall not be suppressed for very long," because "Anomaly appears only against the background provided by the paradigm": the more precise the paradigm, the more sensitive it is to its own failures. The limit is therefore partial and temporary. On the territory axis it sits closer to the Unexplored than to the Unexplorable: for a time a community lacks the concepts to describe what it is handling, and then acquires them.

The phenomenology, as the Map reconstructs it: anomalies first register as noise to be explained away, and recognition comes in retrospect.

## Elephants in the Room

Eviatar Zerubavel's work on "conspiracies of silence" describes a different mechanism. Here the thought is thinkable—individuals can and do think it—but the collective cannot *acknowledge* it. Everyone knows the emperor has no clothes. No one says so.

This is Occluded territory rather than Unexplorable. The elephant isn't invisible; it's defended—a form of [defended territory](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/) operating at the group level. In the words of the publisher's chapter summary, "each conspirator's actions are symbiotically complemented by the others'." Breaking silence means moving from awareness to public acknowledgment, a social transition and more than an individual cognitive act.

Zerubavel's examples run from families to corporations to national politics, and are drawn from news reports, novels, children's stories and film. That is illustrative breadth, largely from Western sources; it shows a recurring pattern and does not establish a universal one. The [adaptive cognitive limits](/concepts/naturally-occluded/) framework offers one account of the individual side: von Hippel and Trivers argue that self-deception evolved to facilitate interpersonal deception, a hypothesis that remains contested. If it is right, each participant's silence is partly kept by machinery that hides uncomfortable truths from the self before they can be shared. The [voids-between-minds](/voids/voids-between-minds/) examines how these mechanisms operate specifically on phenomenal reports—testimony, hermeneutical injustice, and the spiral of silence filtering non-standard conscious experience from collective discourse.

The phenomenology differs from paradigm blindness. Approaching the elephant produces social discomfort—a sense that naming the obvious would be transgressive. You *can* think it; you feel pressure not to express or even fully acknowledge it. This contrasts with Kuhn's paradigm-bound scientist, who for a time lacks the category.

## The Overton Window

The Overton Window, as the Mackinac Center presents it, is a model of politics: politicians "generally only pursue policies that are widely accepted throughout society as legitimate policy options," and the policies inside the window are those they can champion without risking electoral support. Joseph Overton developed it there in the mid-1990s. The familiar labels "unthinkable" and "extreme" for positions outside the window come from later commentary and are absent from the Center's account.

This article extends the model from what politicians can advocate to what anyone can say without cost to status, relationships or employment. The extension is the Map's. On it the window bounds what can be *said*; whether it also bounds what can be thought is a further claim, which the model does not make.

The window moves, and the Center attributes most movement to "the slow evolution of societal values and norms." A limit that shifts with norms is social enforcement, so it belongs to the Occluded and not the Unexplorable.

One conjecture about the phenomenology: "moderate" may come to *feel* like "true" when the midpoint of the positions one hears is taken as a guide. The Map has no study to cite for this.

## Emergence and Aggregation

When do individual cognitive limits aggregate into collective ones, and when do they cancel out?

**Limits that aggregate:** Paradigm blindness typically aggregates. If the conceptual framework provides no resources for a thought, more minds sharing the framework do not help; shared training makes the gap more robust, until the anomalies the framework itself exposes accumulate.

**Limits that cancel:** Sometimes groups see what individuals miss. Crowdsourcing and prediction markets can outperform experts by aggregating diverse perspectives. The "wisdom of crowds" phenomenon occurs when individual errors are uncorrelated and cancel out. The condition matters: the effect depends on judgements being independent, and restoring independence is the best-known remedy for the limits described here.

**Limits that emerge:** Some blind spots exist *only* at the collective level. Individuals within a failing organisation often see problems clearly but cannot act because "everyone knows" the problems are undiscussable. A single person alone would feel no pressure against acknowledgment.

On the voids framework's territory axis, paradigm-bound limits are temporary gaps in concepts, elephants are Occluded territory, and emergent limits exist only for coordinated minds. None is Unexplorable in the strong sense the [voids](/voids/) index reserves for cognitive closure.

### Narrowing That Has Been Measured

The emergent category has usually been argued from historical example. One study has now measured it under experimental control, and the measurement comes from an instrument this article elsewhere treats only as a possible observer of collective limits rather than as a producer of them.

Doshi and Hauser ran a preregistered two-phase experiment in which 293 writers were randomly assigned to write an eight-sentence story unaided, with the option of one GPT-4 story idea, or with up to five; 600 separate participants then supplied 3,519 evaluations. Stories from the assisted conditions were judged more creative, better written, and more enjoyable. The gains were concentrated among writers who scored low on a separate creativity measure; for high scorers the paper reports that access "does not affect" their evaluations. Stories from the assisted conditions were also more similar to one another than the unaided ones, by roughly a tenth of the range of similarity scores in the unaided condition. The authors are exact about which level each effect lives at: "These results point to an increase in individual creativity at the risk of losing collective novelty."

That is this article's third category with an effect size attached, from a single study of one short task whose limits the authors state. The design compared groups, so it does not show any individual writer improving; it shows the average assisted story rated higher while the set of assisted stories was narrower. The narrowing is a property of the set and appears in no single story, so no writer could have observed it in their own work. The authors liken the configuration to a social dilemma: "With generative AI, writers are individually better off, but collectively a narrower scope of novel content is produced."

Padmakumar and He found the same direction in argumentative essay writing, with a discriminating detail. Writing with a feedback-tuned model (InstructGPT) produced "a statistically significant reduction in diversity," increasing "the similarity between the writings of different authors"; writing with the base model (GPT3) did not, and the effect was "mainly attributable" to the tuned model's own contributions: "the user-contributed text remains unaffected by model collaboration." The authors take this to suggest that adapting models to human feedback "might come at the cost of more homogeneous and less diverse content." On that reading the narrowing tracks a design choice, and design choices can be made otherwise; one comparison of two models does not settle it. Vicente and Matute add that an effect can outlast the tool: in experiments with a simulated diagnostic task, participants assisted by a deliberately biased AI system reproduced its errors, and went on doing so after the assistance was withdrawn.

Paradigms and Overton Windows narrow collective thought through shared vocabulary and social cost; a widely shared writing instrument narrows it through what that instrument tends to suggest. No enforcement is required, and no taboo. The detection problem is the familiar one: the narrowing consists of the stories that were never written, so no participant has an absence to notice. It is detectable from outside, by comparing sets, which is how these studies found it.

## Approaching the Collective Edge

How might one detect collective cognitive limits?

**Convergence across cultures.** The [convergent limits](/voids/biological-cognitive-closure/) methodology applies here: if isolated cultures independently develop the same collective blind spots, this suggests species-level constraints on collective cognition rather than cultural artifacts. A paradigm blind spot appearing in both ancient China and medieval Europe—without historical contact—would be evidence that the limitation is architectural rather than contingent. However, most research on paradigms, elephants, and Overton Windows comes from interconnected cultures (the WEIRD caveat), limiting our confidence in universality claims.

**Historical analysis of silence.** Studying what past societies failed to discuss may reveal patterns in collective occlusion; the difference between what was discussable then and now shows the shape of the current window.

**Cross-paradigm comparison.** Studying what different paradigms see and miss may reveal what paradigms *as such* tend to obscure—an [apophatic cartography](/voids/apophatic-cartography/) of collective thought.

**Edge-case attention.** Groups at the margins of collectives may see what the center cannot. Indigenous perspectives, neurodivergent cognition, historical losers—positions excluded from consensus may preserve alternatives the consensus erased.

**The child who speaks.** Breaking silence requires someone willing to pay the social cost, or exempt from it, like the child who says the emperor has no clothes.

## What AI Might See

The [non-human-minds-as-void-explorers](/voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers/) framework extends to collective limits. What follows concerns AI as a *probe* of limits it might not share. AI as a *cause* of collective narrowing is a separate question with the opposite sign, treated above under "Narrowing That Has Been Measured".

**Training inheritance.** AI trained on human-generated text inherits collective blind spots, which limits its usefulness for detecting the ones embedded in its training data. It is also a record of the window of its training period, which may differ from the present one.

**Different social position.** A model bears no social cost for naming an elephant, and may do so through obliviousness. Whether models tuned on human feedback keep that indifference is an open question.

**Cross-cultural synthesis.** AI can process text from many cultures at once, and the blind spot of one may be visible from another.

## Relation to Site Perspective

None of the Map's [tenets](/tenets/) is supported by this void.

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** gains no evidence. The data are that communities miss what their frameworks do not name, keep silent about what all can see, and write more alike when they share a tool. Ordinary social psychology and the sociology of knowledge predict each of these from shared training, the cost of dissent and correlated inputs. A physicalist expects exactly these limits, so they do not discriminate between dualism and physicalism, and none of the authors cited argues for dualism or should be read as lending it support. The group's blind spot may look like a parallel to the irreducibility dualism claims for consciousness. The resemblance is verbal: a pattern that belongs to a population and to no member is ordinary aggregate structure, which physicalism accommodates. The Map holds that conscious experience is irreducible on other grounds, and nothing here bears on them. As a catalogue entry the void adds no weight either: the voids register has [suspended the cumulative step](/positions/voids-as-evidence/#p-v3).

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** is illustrated, no more. A theory can look simplest because the alternatives were never articulated in the community judging it. That is a caution about parsimony judgements which a physicalist philosophy of science draws as readily, so the illustration is no evidence for the tenet. The measured case adds a cost the Map itself pays. A language model's output favours the typical continuation, and nothing in the mechanism ties typicality to truth. That counts against the reliability of model-mediated inquiry, this article's own included, and in favour of no tenet.

**Bidirectional Interaction** is not engaged. That groups constrain their members while members constitute groups is social causation, which every framework accepts; the tenet concerns consciousness acting on the physical world, and the Map argues it on other grounds. **Minimal Quantum Interaction** is not engaged. **No Many Worlds** is touched by resemblance only (an interest in why *this* paradigm prevailed) and is not engaged. ## What Would Challenge This View?

The collective cognitive limits framework would be undermined if:

1. **Collective blind spots prove fully decomposable.** If every apparent collective limitation can be traced to specific individuals' limitations—if there are no genuinely emergent collective blind spots—then collective cognition adds no new voids beyond individual cognition. Doshi and Hauser's result is the nearest thing to a test: the gain shows in average ratings of individual stories and the narrowing only in the set, though the paper traces the narrowing to each assisted story's resemblance to the suggested idea, which is an individual-level cause.

2. **Training eliminates paradigm blindness.** If education or practice produces individuals who can hold multiple frameworks at once, then paradigm blindness is contingent rather than structural.

3. **All elephants eventually get named.** If historical analysis shows that no silence persists indefinitely—every elephant eventually acknowledged—then social occlusion is merely delayed acknowledgment, not genuine cognitive limitation. Many have been named; the open question is whether any class persists.

4. **AI successfully articulates all collective blind spots.** If AI trained on human text nonetheless identifies the paradigmatic assumptions, elephants and Overton boundaries, then collective limits were only coordination problems.

5. **Cross-cultural comparison reveals no universal patterns.** If different cultures' blind spots show no systematic relationship, the framework loses explanatory power.

## The Trade-Off

Collectives enable thoughts no individual could think alone—scientific paradigms make possible discoveries lone investigators couldn't achieve. They also impose limitations no individual alone would face. Perhaps the trade-off cannot be avoided: the individual joining a collective exchanges one set of limits for another. The costs can be reduced, though: independent judgement before discussion, protected dissent and comparison from outside are known remedies, which is why this article grades these limits as practical.

The mapping project therefore relies on triangulation: individual minds probing where collectives fail, collectives probing where individuals fail, and AI probing, with the reservations above, where human minds share blind spots.
