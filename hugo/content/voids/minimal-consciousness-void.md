Consciousness cannot reach its own lower bound by looking. The simplest possible conscious experience—if it exists—lies beyond *concurrent first-person examination*, provided what introspection, attention, and memory add becomes part of the experience examined: a premise the Map holds without proof. The instrument is then made of what it measures, and always reads too high. Indirect routes—no-report neuroscience, third-person cessation-EEG, retrospective sampling—constrain the minimum without reaching it, which is why the void is best classified as partially probe-able and contested rather than flatly unexplorable.

In the Map's [voids](/voids/) taxonomy this makes the void **partially probe-able / contested**: the direct concurrent limit is real (the [Direct vs Indirect Access](#direct-vs-indirect-access) section makes it precise), while the indirect routes constrain the floor without establishing it. An **occluded** reading is also available—the absence of a discoverable lower bound may reflect what consciousness is rather than a limit of method—but it is the *least*-earned label, resting on the conditional "if consciousness is fundamental," a [tenet](/tenets/#dualism) the Map holds rather than a finding the evidence delivers.

## The Enrichment Problem

The argument rests on one premise: that what concurrent introspection adds—attention, metacognition, memory encoding—becomes part of the experience examined, not machinery running alongside it or a later, richer record of it. The Map holds this as its own reading of experience; nothing cited here establishes it. Attention may instead select or sharpen without adding phenomenal content, and a report may be richer than its episode. On the premise, the limit has three dimensions:

**Introspective enrichment.** Attending to an experience then complicates it. David Hume registered the general worry in the *Treatise* Introduction: reflection "would so disturb the operation of my natural principles, as must render it impossible to form any just conclusion from the phenomenon." His remark concerns introspection's self-disturbance broadly, not minimal consciousness, which he nowhere discusses. The [observation-and-measurement-void](/voids/observation-and-measurement-void/) documents such transformation; here it would bite hardest, since any addition moves the experience away from minimality.

**Regressive enrichment.** Examining the simplest experience would create a more complex one: experience-being-examined. Seeking the minimal version of *that* adds another layer—a regress receding from its target with each step.

**Report enrichment.** Any experience reported, described, or silently noted has passed through cognitive machinery that adds structure. Schooler and Schreiber (2004) discuss factors—detection, transformation, substitution—that may lead meta-consciousness to misrepresent experience. A truly minimal experience—if it occurs—might leave no trace accessible to report.

If the premise holds, the three converge: consciousness's basement level lies one step below what concurrent introspection can observe.

## The Search That Finds Too Much

Thomas Metzinger's Minimal Phenomenal Experience (MPE) project represents the most systematic attempt to characterise the simplest form of consciousness. Working from "sixteen phenomenological case-studies" and "six semantic constraints" from the existing literature (Metzinger 2020), the project sought to identify "pure awareness"—consciousness stripped of all content.

The result is instructive. What advanced meditators call "contentless" awareness they also describe, under careful questioning, as phenomenologically rich. The subsequent MPE-92M questionnaire, 92 items long, yielded a tentative [12-factor model](/topics/measuring-the-witness-attitude/#factor-12) of "pure awareness" phenomenology (Gamma & Metzinger 2021). The search for the minimum kept finding more than nothing.

This does not mean the meditators are wrong. They may be reporting the minimal state accessible to introspection—the lowest floor the observing mind can reach. But the enrichment problem guarantees this floor lies above the actual minimum, the two separated by the cost of observation. The companion question—whether such contentless awareness persists into genuine unconsciousness or merely seems contentless to a reporting mind—is taken up in [contentless awareness and the evidence question](/topics/contentless-awareness-evidence/).

Two caveats keep this evidence honest. First, the convergence is partly non-independent: the survey's respondents were new, but its 92 items came from the literature and practitioner pilot studies, so the phenomenological "floor" and the questionnaire "profile" share one report-tradition's framing and are not independent lines. Second, Metzinger (2020) supplies a deflationary reading of why the state *feels* empty: the MPE is the content of a predictive model of tonic alertness, contentless-feeling because of phenomenal *transparency*—the brain has no introspective access to its own arousal-regulation algorithm, so only bare "wakefulness" content appears. The felt emptiness is then what a physicalist would predict, and the void's "occluded/irreducible" gloss is one interpretation of the transparency datum—consistent with it, not licensed by it.

## Direct vs Indirect Access

The enrichment problem blocks one route to the minimum, not all of them. It blocks *concurrent first-person introspection*: a mind cannot attend to its own minimal state in real time without enriching it. But several indirect routes sidestep concurrent introspection, and taking them seriously is what downgrades the void from "unexplorable" to "partially probe-able / contested."

No-report paradigms decouple the neural correlates of consciousness from the act of reporting. Tsuchiya, Wilke, Frässle and Lamme (2015) argue that requiring a behavioural report both overestimates those correlates (by including the machinery of report) and underestimates them (by excluding experience too faint to report). Inferring a conscious state from physiological markers rather than introspective access probes states below the report threshold—the region the void calls sealed—without the observer enriching the target.

Retrospective and third-person routes add further oblique purchase. [Descriptive Experience Sampling](/concepts/descriptive-experience-sampling/) (Hurlburt and Akhter 2006) interrogates experience shortly *after* a sampling beep, trading concurrent contamination for memory's reconstructive cost—a different limit, not the same one. Its reach into the faint end is bounded in a way that matters here: the beep recruits report, and report plausibly reaches only attended experience, a limit the sampling tradition raised against itself. Windt, Nielsen and Thompson (2016) argue that "dreamless" [sleep](/voids/sleep-consciousness-void/) hosts a taxonomy of minimal experiences rather than a clean blank, direct pressure on the assumption that the faint end is unreachable. Third-person cessation-EEG (discussed below) seeks neural signatures of the edge from outside the experiencing system entirely.

These routes share a structure: each constrains the minimal state rather than establishing it. The void's genuine epistemic limit survives—the *direct concurrent* minimum stays out of reach. What does not survive is the claim that the minimum is wholly beyond inquiry. It is contested terrain probed obliquely from several directions, not a sealed region.

## The Missing Threshold

Integrated Information Theory (IIT) approaches the lower bound mathematically. On IIT, a system forming a maximum of integrated information (Φ) is conscious to that degree, however simple. In Tononi (2004) a bare photodiode discriminates but presumably does not see; Tononi and Koch (2015) hold that one "made up of a sensor and a memory element can have a modicum of experience."

The criterion (Φ > 0) is itself a boundary—zero Φ, no experience—so IIT denies only that size or complexity sets the floor; above zero, experience grades continuously. IIT is one graded model, contested (a 2023 open letter from 124 scholars called it pseudoscience) and not the Map's own. The void does not depend on it: on any graded view, a determinate boundary can lie below degrees too faint to examine.

A further complication: "minimal" may not be one dimension. On the multidimensional reading of global states (Bayne et al. 2016; Birch et al. 2020)—a live, contested framing, not a settled result—the void does not dissolve but multiplies: not one lower edge but several, each resisting a clean threshold, which sharpens the basement problem. [minimal-consciousness](/concepts/minimal-consciousness/) develops the plural framing across its independent axes.

On the Map's [dualist](/tenets/#dualism) tenet, no neuron count or Φ value generates consciousness; yet it couples with matter where [interface conditions](/concepts/minimal-consciousness/) permit—a physical eligibility condition. What stays intrinsic is the faintest degree's character, which concurrent introspection cannot examine.

## Deflationary Alternatives

The void's second, existence premise—that a sub-introspective conscious minimum exists but lies permanently below examination—is not the only available reading of the introspective failure. Three leading frameworks explain the same failure without positing any consciousness beneath what introspection reaches. The evidence underdetermines which reading is correct; the void is a contested interpretation of the introspective limit, not a forced consequence of it. Naming where each alternative's disagreement bites keeps the dualist commitment from silently closing the question.

[Higher-order thought theory](/concepts/higher-order-theories/) (Rosenthal; Lau and Rosenthal 2011) holds that a first-order state is conscious in virtue of being targeted by a suitable higher-order representation. On the Map's account of the theory, that representation is typically unconscious; introspection is a further, conscious, third-level act. So the constitutive layer lies beneath anything introspection adds. That makes the rival stronger: it can grant that examining a state changes it and still hold that nothing is experienced below higher-order representation, leaving the void no fainter experience to be about. The disagreement falls on the void's existence premise, not its enrichment premise, and introspective evidence cannot settle it.

[Global workspace theory](/concepts/global-workspace-theory/) (Dehaene and Naccache 2001; Mashour 2024) locates the lower bound at the threshold of global broadcast. A signal too weak to ignite the workspace is processed but never [consciously accessed](/concepts/access-consciousness/); the failure to introspect below the minimum is, on this account, sub-threshold signals failing to broadcast, not a fainter experience being inaccessible. The theory predicts a determinate ignition threshold: below it, processing without consciousness, not consciousness without report. Its ignition predictions met [an adversarial test in 2025](/concepts/global-workspace-theory/#the-cogitate-experiment), with disputed results. The rivalry is genuine without the void denying a floor: at issue is whether any experience lies below ignition, [outrunning access](/concepts/phenomenal-overflow/) and unexaminable from inside.

[Illusionism](/concepts/illusionism/) (Frankish 2016) makes the strongest move: on the Map's reconstruction, the apparent void is the resolution limit of the brain's self-monitoring—a computational blind spot that introspection reifies into a metaphysical depth. There is no minimal phenomenal experience permanently out of reach because there is no phenomenal experience of the relevant kind to begin with; what looks like an inaccessible floor is the edge of a self-model's representational grain. The Map's disagreement here is a framework boundary, not a refutation, and is named as such: illusionism denies the very explanandum—the what-it-is-likeness of experience—that the Map takes as given under its [dualist](/tenets/#dualism) commitment. The two frameworks share too little common ground for either to defeat the other on the other's terms; the clash is bedrock, and honesty requires marking it that way rather than presenting the void's premise as having answered illusionism.

The Map reads the introspective limit as the void does—as the signature of a sub-introspective phenomenal floor the instrument of inspection cannot reach—because its dualist commitment makes phenomenal presence prior to and independent of the access machinery that broadcasts or monitors it. That reading is the Map's, not a neutral deduction the three frameworks would force.

This sharpens the headline claim. "Permanently beyond examination" carries an epistemic sense—introspection cannot reach the minimum—and a metaphysical one—a determinate minimum exists, fixed and unreachable. On the enrichment premise the evidence supports the first: looking enriches, so the looked-at floor always lies above the actual one. It does not by itself establish the second; the higher-order and illusionist readings deny that a determinate sub-introspective minimum exists at all. The void earns the epistemic claim on its enrichment premise and holds the metaphysical claim as its dualist reading of an underdetermined situation—not a result the inaccessibility argument delivers on its own.

## Approaching from the Edge

Buddhist contemplative traditions offer the closest experiential approach. Practitioners report experience simplifying—loss of body sense, temporal orientation, spatial location—until something remains that resists further reduction. Laukkonen et al. (2023) distinguish two cessations: momentary nirodha, "a short 'gap' or 'cut' in the stream of consciousness in the realm of milliseconds or seconds," and nirodha samāpatti, an extended absence entered intentionally, traditionally for up to seven days. The EEG evidence they survey is a few case studies of one or two adepts, mostly of brief nirodha (Chowdhury et al. 2023: 37 self-identified events, one meditator), plus one then-unpublished 90-minute recording of extended cessation. A momentary gap cannot separate absence from experience too faint to recall; a longer one adds outside observation. Either way the retrospective report of "nothing" is heterophenomenology—an account of an absence, not third-person confirmation of zero consciousness. It indicates an edge; it cannot certify what lies on the far side.

Simplified awareness—the [edge-experience](/voids/edge-states-and-void-probes/) Metzinger calls the "zero-person perspective" (Metzinger 2024)—and cessation bracket the void, but the bracket cannot be closed from within. From above, the meditator cannot observe the transition without enriching it; from below, cessation offers nothing to report. This makes cessation an *ambiguous probe*, not near-confirmation of an inaccessible lower edge. As [edge-states-and-void-probes](/voids/edge-states-and-void-probes/) frames it, a report admits a *transcendence* reading (the practice stepped off the scale) and an *edge-mapping* reading (it approached the lowest rung), undecidable from the inside, though third-person EEG offers oblique purchase.

## What This Void Implies

The minimal consciousness void differs from other voids in a specific way: the direct-concurrent limit, on the enrichment premise, is structural, not contingent. Better instruments cannot help, because the instrument *is* consciousness, and using it creates the enrichment—unlike voids where future methodological innovation might make progress.

Several implications follow:

**The hard problem persists all the way down.** The [boundary-and-projection](/voids/boundary-and-projection/) void asks about the *spatial and functional extent* of consciousness; the minimal consciousness void asks about its *intensity*—two orthogonal edges resisting investigation. Whatever the minimum is, it still has the property of being "something it is like," and that property resists third-person explanation at every level of complexity. Simplification does not dissolve the mystery; it isolates it.

**Consciousness may have no minimum we can find.** If consciousness extends broadly across the animal kingdom (Nagel) and some graded view is right that vanishingly small degrees suffice, ever-fainter experiences may lie below any we can examine—an asymptote on the Map's reading, even above a determinate zero.

**Investigation of consciousness is self-limiting.** On the enrichment premise the *direct concurrent* enrichment problem is structural—the instrument modifies the measurement. This places the void alongside the [observation-and-measurement-void](/voids/observation-and-measurement-void/) and [self-opacity](/voids/self-opacity/) as a sign that consciousness may have a constitutive relationship with opacity: it functions in part because certain aspects of its operation remain invisible to itself. The indirect routes above soften the verdict without overturning it—they constrain the floor, but none reads it off.

## Relation to Site Perspective

None of the Map's [tenets](/tenets/) is supported by this void; the voids register has [suspended the cumulative step](/positions/voids-as-evidence/#p-v3).

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** *favours* the void's metaphysical reading rather than establishing it. If consciousness is fundamental, no physical threshold generates its lower bound, though interface conditions gate coupling. On the dualist reading the void marks features resisting third-person analysis; on a physicalist reading the same introspective limit is an observer-effect over a determinate floor. The enrichment problem fixes the epistemic limit either way; which metaphysics it signifies is the Map's tenet-guided interpretation, not a deduction the evidence compels.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** is illustrated, no more: the search for the simplest conscious experience has not delivered a clean "atom of experience," a difficulty a physicalist can grant equally.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** is not engaged: attention altering an experience is an observer-effect physicalism predicts equally, and the Map's case that consciousness acts on the physical world rests on other grounds.
