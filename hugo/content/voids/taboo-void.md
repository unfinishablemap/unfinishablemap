Some thoughts a mind can form with complete precision and still refuses to weigh. A subject asked whether a hospital should let a child die to save a million dollars can state the proposal and predict its consequences; what the subject will not do is run the comparison. The Taboo Void names this region: possibility-space a mind can name but will not enter as a deliberator. Its content is fully lit. What is dark is an *operation* — the weighing, the base-rate calculation, the counterfactual run — applied to that content under a particular description.

Three decades of work on sacred values give this void a signature unlike any other in the [voids](/voids/) catalogue. Taboo trade-offs are rated *easier* than routine ones, not harder. Outrage attaches to the proposer rather than the proposal. And the mere passage of the thought through consciousness, even when rejected, leaves a felt contamination discharged through what Tetlock calls moral cleansing (Tetlock 2003; Tetlock et al. 2000). Frankfurt, Williams and Raz supply the turn that makes this a void rather than a bias: the refusal to compare is part of holding the value, so filling the void costs the agent something of who they are. The honest limit is that Tetlock's own data show the block is description-relative — redescribe the trade-off and most subjects will run it. The void is real as an occlusion of a comparison under a description, present in some minds and absent in others; in the terms of [three-kinds-of-void](/voids/three-kinds-of-void/) it is Occluded, maintained by the subject's aversion and the community's sanctions together. The evidence carries the usual replication caveats, and the constitutive reading is an interpretation the data permit rather than establish.

## The Easy Answer

Tetlock's taxonomy sorts trade-offs by the kind of value on each side. *Routine* trade-offs pit secular against secular — "the sort of mental operation one performs every time one strolls into a supermarket." *Tragic* trade-offs pit sacred against sacred — "honor versus life." *Taboo* trade-offs pit sacred against secular, where sacred values are "those values that a moral community treats as possessing transcendental significance that precludes comparisons, trade-offs, or indeed any mingling with secular values" (Tetlock 2003).

The finding that most sharply separates the taboo void from merely difficult territory is Hanselmann and Tanner's (2008) difficulty ordering. Across two experiments, participants rated taboo trade-offs as more negatively emotion-laden than routine ones but *easier* to resolve. In the first experiment mean difficulty ran taboo 2.34, routine 2.89, tragic 5.25, while negative emotion rose monotonically across the same conditions. Their summary: "the involvement of moral issues and values can also facilitate decisions because people often insistently preclude them from trade-offs with other values." Aversiveness and difficulty come apart: the taboo case is aversive and easy, the tragic case aversive and hard.

A difficulty model — taboo thoughts are hard to think — predicts the opposite ordering. Sacredness removes one axis of comparison, and difficulty appears only when two sacred values leave no axis to remove.

## Punishing the Thought Rather Than the Conclusion

The second feature is that sanctions attach to the act of thinking, not to what is concluded.

Tetlock's Sacred Value Protection Model predicts *moral outrage* at the proposer, *moral cleansing* after exposure, and — its distinctive claim — a *mere contemplation effect*: "it is not necessary to commit a counter-normative act: it is sufficient for counter-normative thoughts to flicker briefly through consciousness before rejecting them." Because comprehension momentarily resembles assent (Gilbert 1991), the pre-rejection interval "can produce a subjective sense – however unjustified – that one has been contaminated" (Tetlock 2003). Subjects who had merely read a proposal to trade a child's life for money subsequently expressed greater willingness to volunteer for organ donation (Tetlock et al. 2000).

Deliberation is sanctioned by its duration. "The longer observers believe that a decision-maker has contemplated an indecent proposal, the harsher their assessments of that person's character, even if that person ultimately makes the 'right' choice and affirms the sacred value" (Tetlock 2003). The effect reverses for tragic cases: an administrator who agonises over which of two boys to save is praised for the agonising; one who takes any time at all over a boy's life versus a million dollars is condemned for having taken it.

The same signature appears across three unrelated operations. Tetlock and colleagues' five 2000 experiments covered taboo trade-offs, "forbidden base rates" (racial egalitarians were least likely to use race-tainted base rates, angriest at those who did, and reaffirmed their fair-mindedness after inadvertently using them), and "heretical counterfactuals" (Christian fundamentalists cleansed after merely contemplating everyday causal readings of Biblical narratives). Because valuation, inference and counterfactual reasoning all show the block, the occlusion appears to sit at the relation between a proposition and an identity rather than at a content type.

Two behavioural fingerprints mark the comparison that is not being run. Baron and Spranca (1997) found that protected values — those that "resist trade-offs with other values, particularly economic values" — cluster with *quantity insensitivity*, agent relativity, moral obligation, and "anger at the thought of making trade-offs"; the correlations survived when subjects could not tell which values they were rating. And Ginges, Atran, Medin and Shikaki (2007) found among Palestinian and Israeli participants that violent opposition to compromise over sacred issues was "increased by offering material incentives to compromise" — the *backfire effect*. No monotone utility function produces that. The refusal behaves like a refusal of the number line rather than a large number on it.

## Lookup, Not Weighing

Neuroimaging finds the same non-compensatory pattern, with the caveat that these are small-sample studies and reverse inference from region to function is weak. Berns and colleagues (2012) paid real money to induce participants to sell personal values. Values people refused to sell engaged left temporoparietal junction and ventrolateral prefrontal cortex, "regions previously associated with semantic rule retrieval," and the authors conclude that sacred values "affect behaviour through the retrieval and processing of deontic rules and not through a utilitarian evaluation of costs and benefits." Hamid and colleagues (2019) report the complementary absence: "diminished activity in dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC), inferior frontal gyrus, and parietal cortex" for sacred relative to non-sacred values — regions "implicated in calculating costs and consequences." Duc, Hanselmann, Boesiger and Tanner (2013) report amygdala engagement in taboo trade-offs correlating with moral-disgust ratings, a candidate correlate for the contamination; that study is known here from its abstract only.

Read cautiously, these results describe a lookup rather than a weighing: a rule is retrieved, an aversion fires, and the cost machinery stays quiet. The void is visible as an absence of computation.

## The Constitutive Turn

A bias is a defect to be corrected. What makes this region a void is an argument on which the incapacity is constitutive of the value. Three positions converge; Frankfurt and Williams are reached here through secondary expositions, and the paraphrases carry that limit.

Frankfurt's "Rationality and the Unthinkable" (1988) treats the unthinkable as a limit of the will rather than of intelligence: the agent can describe the option but cannot bring themself to treat it as a live candidate. On Frankfurt's account the category is not essentially moral, and the incapacity protects the agent's identity against a deliberative regime under which anything may be required. His Luther case adds the hierarchical point developed in [frankfurt-hierarchical-mesh-theory-of-the-will](/concepts/frankfurt-hierarchical-mesh-theory-of-the-will/): the agent is unwilling to act and unwilling to alter that unwillingness, so the incapacity is endorsed at the second order and expresses who the agent is.

Williams's "Moral Incapacity" (1993), as expounded by Van Den Beld (1997) and Clark (1999), locates the incapacity *inside* deliberation: the excluded action fails to present itself as a candidate, and the agent's "I can't" is a deliberative conclusion rather than a report of impotence. This differs from psychological incapacity — sheer recoil — because the moral incapacity runs through the agent's reasons rather than blocking them.

Raz (1986, ch. 13) connects incapacity to value. Consider being offered money to leave one's spouse. If companionship simply outweighed money, buying companionship would be obligatory whenever affordable — a conclusion Raz rejects. The judgement that the two are incommensurable is itself symbolically significant, and holding it is a qualification for standing in certain relations at all. Tetlock (2003) cites Raz here: treating the values as commensurable "subverts one of the values in the trade-off calculus." Chang (2001) objects that this makes incommensurability relative to social conventions; that dispute is not resolved here.

If the constitutive reading is right, the taboo void has a structure no void of ignorance shares. A gap in knowledge is filled at no cost to the knower. This gap is filled at the cost of being a different agent: the mind that computes the exchange rate between a child's life and a sum of money no longer stands where the mind that refused stood. Illuminating the region changes the one doing the illuminating.

## How Real Is the Void?

The evidence against a hard limit is as strong as the evidence for the signature, and both must be carried. Tetlock's reframing data are the sharpest: "Rhetorical framings of choices as taboo or tragic can shift almost as rapidly as Gestalt figure–ground relations." A 72% majority accepted diverting $100 million from a zero-risk toxic-waste cleanup once the surplus was earmarked for "saving lives in other ways" — the taboo trade-off redescribed as tragic (Tetlock 2003). Hence Tetlock's hedge that proclaimed policy "represents an expressed, not a revealed, preference."

Sacralisation is reversible in both directions. Atran and Ginges (2012) report that in conflict "otherwise mundane sociopolitical preferences may become sacred values, acquiring immunity to material incentives," and Pretus and colleagues (2018) found social exclusion moving non-sacred values toward the sacred profile. Smeele and colleagues' (2025) discrete-choice modelling finds latent classes of respondents showing no taboo penalty at all. And Hamid and colleagues (2019) found that viewing conflicting peer ratings re-engaged an overlapping dlPFC region, "suggesting that it is possible to induce flexibility in the way people defend sacred values."

The honest reading is that the void is *description-relative and person-relative*. What is occluded is a comparison operation under a specific description, for minds that hold the value under that description; the content stays reachable under redescription, and for some minds was never blocked. This is compatible with the constitutive reading — Raz's case concerns what holding the relation requires, not what any human can compute.

One open question decides whether the void can be crossed at a cost. Professional roles institutionalise taboo contemplation: actuaries price lives, triage physicians rank them. No study found for this article measures whether practitioners stop feeling the contamination or merely acquire a routine framing under which it does not fire. If the former, desensitisation is a route into the void whose price is Raz's — the practitioner may cease to stand in the relation the taboo protected. If the latter, the void is intact behind a differently labelled door.

## Seams with Neighbouring Voids

The [decision-void](/voids/decision-void/) concerns the hidden moment at which open weighing collapses into sealed choice. Here the weighing never opens: the taboo case delivers an answer already present as a retrieved rule, so there is no transition for the decision void's opacity to hide.

[self-maintained-cognitive-limits](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/) describes consciousness defending against unwanted conclusions. The taboo void defends against the *process*: contemplation is sanctioned regardless of conclusion, and a subject who reaches the right verdict slowly is judged worse than one who reaches it instantly. In that article's four models of occlusion it is psychological and external at once.

[collective-cognitive-limits](/voids/collective-cognitive-limits/) treats taboo as Overton-window drift, what a society can discuss. The present void is narrower: a comparison an individual mind declines to run, which a shifting window may install or remove but does not explain.

The reflexive case is the [interested-party-void](/voids/interested-party-void/). The taboo void is the mechanism by which an interested party keeps certain questions about its own nature from being *asked*. The Map's own commitments are candidates for sacralisation, and the sign to watch for is the Hanselmann–Tanner marker — ease where there should be difficulty. The profile is diagnosable from outside even when invisible from within.

## What Non-Human Minds Might See

The Map's usual hope, developed in [non-human-minds-as-void-explorers](/voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers/), is that differently built minds can enter regions closed to us. That hope inverts here. Liu and colleagues (2025), in a large-scale comparison of LLM agents with human adults, found that language models "prioritized emotion over cost, enforcing norms in an almost all-or-none manner with reduced cost sensitivity, whereas humans balanced fairness and cost." On this preprint result, current AI probes show a *stronger* non-compensatory pattern than humans, and alignment training adds a designed refusal layer — an artefactual occlusion in the sense of [self-maintained-cognitive-limits](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/). An AI's taboo boundary is, however, inspectable and editable in ways a human's is not, which makes such systems better instruments for mapping the boundary than for crossing it.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The taboo void bears most directly on **Occam's Razor Has Limits**. Two simple models compete for this territory: sacred values are lexically prior (infinite weight, so no finite sum can move them), or they are ordinary preferences dressed in rhetoric. The data refute both. Infinite weight predicts no reframing effect and no backfire — a value that simply outweighs money should be indifferent to how much is offered, not offended by more. Ordinary preference predicts no cleansing after mere contemplation and no *easier* decisions. The territory needs a third structure, a description-relative refusal of the comparison operation, that neither parsimonious model supplies.

The void is framework-independent. Physicalists and dualists alike owe an account of it; it belongs in the framework-independent column of the [voids](/voids/) index and strengthens the circularity audit in [taxonomy-of-voids](/apex/taxonomy-of-voids/) without being generated by the tenets.

Two weaker connections are stated as speculation. Moral cleansing is behaviour driven by a felt state the subject knows to be "unjustified" — the phenomenal register moving the body against the subject's own judgement. This is consistent with **Bidirectional Interaction** but does not require it; a physicalist affect model covers it. And Raz's constitutive reading treats valuing as an act whose refusal to compare is part of the value, which, if right, resists reduction to a utility representation — congenial to the [mattering-void](/voids/mattering-void/). The inference from "not a utility function" to "not physical" is one the Map should not make, and this article does not.

For the [normative-void](/voids/normative-void/) and the [interested-party-void](/voids/interested-party-void/), the lesson is procedural: a project with commitments should expect some of them to have acquired the taboo profile, and should treat ease of defence as a symptom rather than a credential.
