An observer embedded in a simulation has no vantage from which to rule out that their reality is simulated. This is the simulation detection void—a convergence point where all [three kinds of void](/voids/three-kinds-of-void/) meet. Detection methods remain largely untried (unexplored), the inside observer problem may foreclose them in principle (unexplorable), and simulation architects could actively prevent discovery (occluded). The Unfinishable Map treats this not as science fiction but as a paradigmatic case of epistemic closure that illuminates what embedded observers can and cannot know.

## The Inside Observer Problem

The structural core of this void is simple: verifying your substrate requires a vantage point outside it. An observer within a simulation accesses only what the simulation presents—appearances, never the underlying architecture. This loosely parallels Kant's phenomena/noumena distinction: we perceive what the system allows, never the thing-in-itself behind those perceptions.

Nick Bostrom's simulation argument (2003) established the framework. Its conclusion is a disjunction, popularly called a trilemma: the human species very likely goes extinct before reaching a "posthuman" stage, or posthuman civilisations are extremely unlikely to run significant numbers of ancestor-simulations, or "we are almost certainly living in a computer simulation." Bostrom does not conclude that we are simulated; he suggests apportioning credence "roughly evenly" across the three. The problem charted here is epistemic: nothing available from inside settles whether the third holds.

Two of its premises belong to a wider limit. Simulated minds enter the reference class only if substrate independence holds, and the near-unity credence follows only through Bostrom's bland indifference principle; both steps are charted at [the observer-selection void](/voids/observer-selection-void/), which sits upstream of this one.

This differs from ordinary ignorance. Ordinary ignorance yields to investigation—we didn't know what lay beyond the mountains, so we crossed them. The simulation detection void resists investigation structurally. Every observation occurs within the potentially simulated substrate. Every instrument is made of potentially simulated matter. Every thought arises in a potentially simulated mind. The evidence and the thing the evidence is supposed to test are the same system.

## Why Detection Methods Fail

### Physical Anomaly Searches

The most intuitive approach: look for computational artifacts in fundamental physics. Discrete spacetime structures, statistical regularities in quantum measurements, rotational symmetry breaking from grid-based computation. Beane, Davoudi and Savage (2014) worked the last case through. Assuming the universe is "an early numerical simulation" on a space-time lattice, they derive a bound on the lattice spacing from the cosmic-ray spectrum and propose that such a simulation "could reveal itself" as rotational symmetry breaking among the highest-energy cosmic rays. The paper sets a bound and names a signature; it reports no detection.

Bostrom's comment on that proposal, posted on his simulation-argument site, is that "even the earliest simulations of systems sufficiently complex to contain observers would make use of powerful computational shortcuts that would eliminate the opportunity to observe any such discrepancies." He offers this as what he "would think," and his stated reason is computational economy, with no appeal to concealment. If he is right, lattice searches test only the crudest simulations. Neukart et al. (2022) still hold that "experiments are needed to verify or refute" the hypothesis, granting that the question "cannot be answered with certainty today." That simulators also control what evidence exists, so that a detection arms race would favour them, is the Map's inference and no result of this literature.

### Statistical Arguments

David Kipping's Bayesian analysis (2020) arrives at no more than roughly even odds between base reality and simulation—his result puts the probability that we are simulated at "less than 50%, tending towards that value in the limit of an infinite number of simulations"—far less dramatic than popular claims suggest. But probability calculations cannot resolve epistemic closure. Even perfect Bayesian reasoning operates on evidence available within the system. If that evidence is curated, the posterior is compromised. A subtler trap lurks here: assigning the simulation hypothesis a crisp probability can disguise [possibility/probability slippage](/concepts/possibility-probability-slippage/)—the hypothesis is a bare *possibility* whose evidential base is structurally inaccessible, and dressing it as a calibrated 50-50 risks treating the absence of a defeater as if it were positive support.

### The Unfalsifiability Problem

Miloš Agatonović (2023) argues that Bostrom's argument, even if its modified form is valid, is unsound because it "relies on the indistinguishability assumption that even in principle cannot be tested." Typically, untestability of this kind disqualifies a claim from scientific consideration. Within the voids framework it is read as marking a boundary: embedded observers lack the epistemic position to evaluate the claim, so the void lies in the observer's situation as well as in the hypothesis's content. That reading is the Map's; Agatonović draws the deflationary conclusion.

## Convergence of All Three Void Types

What makes this void distinctive is how cleanly the [three categories](/voids/three-kinds-of-void/) converge.

**Unexplored**: We have not exhaustively attempted detection. Conjectured consciousness-level signs (systematic qualia manipulation, shared false memories, subjective time anomalies) have no test protocol, and ordinary memory error would be hard to tell apart from them. Physical anomaly searches have barely begun. The space of possible detection methods is vast and mostly untried.

**Unexplorable**: The inside observer problem is structural, not contingent. No amount of internal observation establishes that the observed substrate is fundamental—like trying to read the label on the outside of a bottle from inside it. The closure is asymmetric: a positive sign of simulation is not excluded in principle (see [below](#what-would-challenge-this-view)), but no observation could certify base reality. Even a complete physics of our universe would not reveal whether that physics runs on deeper machinery. The [meta-epistemology of cognitive limits](/voids/meta-epistemology-of-limits/) examines how we might confirm such structural inaccessibility rather than merely asserting it.

**Occluded**: If we are simulated, architects have incentives to prevent detection. Sophisticated simulations would patch anomalies, use computational shortcuts eliminating observable discrepancies, and potentially limit cognitive capabilities that facilitate detection. The [defended territory](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/) framework applies directly: thoughts approaching simulation detection might be actively deflected. This raises questions about whether such limits are [natural or designed](/voids/meta-epistemology-of-limits/)—a distinction that, in the simulation case, may itself be unresolvable.

## The Phenomenology of Approach

Approaching the simulation hypothesis seems to show characteristic void phenomenology. The four descriptions below are the Map's conjectures; no survey or first-person literature documents them.

**Recursive vertigo**: Thinking about simulation detection triggers meta-cognitive spirals. If this thought about simulation is itself simulated, is the doubt genuine? The recursion destabilises rather than clarifies—each level of reflection introduces another layer of potential simulation. This resembles the [self-reference paradox](/voids/self-opacity/) where consciousness cannot map itself because it is the mapper.

**Affective dismissiveness**: Many dismiss the simulation hypothesis as "just science fiction" despite its philosophical rigour. This systematic dismissiveness could indicate [cognitive aversion](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/)—minds deflecting from thoughts that approach [defended territory](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/)—though ordinary scepticism toward an untestable claim explains the same dismissal at least as well.

**Epistemic helplessness**: Unlike ordinary uncertainty, which motivates investigation, simulation uncertainty tends toward paralysis. We lack not just answers but any path to answers. The characteristic feeling is not "I don't know yet" but "I cannot know in principle."

**Cognitive slippage**: Arguments about simulation seem compelling in the moment, then feel unreal hours later. This matches the [phenomenology of thoughts that slip away](/voids/transit-void/)—content that won't consolidate into stable knowledge.

## Apophatic Approaches

If direct detection is foreclosed, indirect methods remain. [Apophatic epistemology](/voids/apophatic-cartography/)—knowing through negation—offers a framework.

Rather than detecting simulation directly, map what base reality cannot be. Study the [topology of detection failure](/voids/compound-failure-signatures/): which methods fail, how they fail, and whether failure patterns reveal architectural constraints. If grid-based simulations show rotational symmetry breaking but our physics does not, we learn something about what kind of simulation (if any) we might inhabit. The Beane bound is an instance: it constrains one class of lattice simulation and leaves the fundamental question open.

The void boundary itself becomes data. Systematic exploration of what detection methods fail and why constrains the possibility space even if it cannot eliminate simulation as a possibility.

## What Would Challenge This View

The simulation detection void thesis would be weakened by:

1. **Discovery of computational artifacts**: Detectable anomalies in fundamental physics—discrete spacetime, rendering shortcuts, conservation law violations—would show detection is possible, converting this from an unexplorable void to merely an unexplored one.

2. **Proof that simulation is logically impossible**: If hosting conscious observers requires resources exceeding any possible computer, the hypothesis collapses regardless of epistemic access.

3. **External communication**: Contact from simulation architects would bypass the inside observer problem entirely—though distinguishing genuine contact from simulated contact reintroduces the same void.

Resolution of the measurement problem is sometimes proposed as a fourth, and fails as a challenge: a complete interpretation of quantum mechanics that eliminates observer-dependence would close one avenue for simulation detection while the structural inside-observer problem remains.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** applies most directly. Dismissing the simulation hypothesis because base reality seems "simpler" assumes we can judge simplicity from inside the system—the very capacity the void puts in doubt. The tenet cuts both ways: with knowledge this incomplete, parsimony cannot decide for the hypothesis either. The void illustrates the tenet in use and lends it no evidential support, and nothing here raises the probability that we are simulated.

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** reframes the hypothesis. If consciousness is fundamental rather than computational, simulation might occur at the physical level while consciousness remains unsimulated. Simulated physics, but real experience. This inverts standard framings where consciousness is treated as an emergent property of simulated computation.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** deepens the void. We cannot observe how consciousness influences physics. In a simulation, this opacity might be by design—the mechanism is the simulation substrate itself, necessarily hidden from embedded observers. The [causal interface void](/voids/causal-interface/) and the simulation detection void may share a common source.

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)** intersects through the question of identity. If we reject branching, *this* observer's epistemic position matters. We cannot defer to other branches where detection succeeds. The simulation question must be answered—or acknowledged as unanswerable—from exactly where we stand.

For The Unfinishable Map as a project, the simulation detection void is clarifying. If reality is simulated, we map only what simulators allow. The territory (base reality) would stay inaccessible unless its architects chose otherwise. The Map is unfinishable not only because we are finite but because the territory itself may be deliberately hidden.
