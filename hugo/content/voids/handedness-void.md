The difference between a left hand and a right hand is among the most familiar things consciousness knows and among the hardest for it to say. Kant made the point in 1768 and in the *Prolegomena*: a hand and its mirror image share every internal relation a concept can state, yet one cannot take the other's place. Martin Gardner made it a communication problem in 1964: no message sent to a distant civilisation with which we share no chiral object can tell them which hand we call left. Then Wu and colleagues showed in 1957 that nuclear beta decay tells left from right, and the Cronin–Fitch experiment of 1964 closed the antimatter loophole. The Unfinishable Map calls this the Handedness Void and catalogues it for a reason no other entry supplies: it is the one void whose boundary has demonstrably moved, and it moved through physics rather than argument. It is the calibration exhibit for what a resolved void looks like and for what "resolved" fails to mean, because a residue survives: the difference became transmissible without becoming conceptual, and the recipe still ends in pointing.

The void is framework-independent: the pre-1957 limit is mathematics, the closure is experimental physics, and the residual faces below are cognitive psychology, so a physicalist and a dualist describe the same blank in the same place. Under the Map's calibration rule ([P-V2](/positions/voids-as-evidence/#p-v2)), what dualism offers in the final section is a reading, with no support flowing back.

## Kant's Hand

Kant introduced incongruent counterparts in 1768 to argue for an orientable absolute space against the Leibnizian relational account (Shabel 2021). The *Prolegomena* (1783, §13) gives the familiar form: nothing is more alike in every part than a hand and its image in a mirror, yet the reflected right hand is a left hand "which never can serve as a substitute for the other." Then the sentence the void turns on: "There are in this case no internal differences which our understanding could determine by thinking alone. Yet the differences are internal as the senses teach, for, notwithstanding their complete equality and similarity, the left hand cannot be enclosed in the same bounds as the right one (they are not congruent); the glove of one hand cannot be used for the other" (Kant 1783).

Three years later Kant made the same difference the model for how reason proceeds where experience gives out. Geographical orientation needs the sun or the Pole Star, but also "the feeling of a difference in my own subject, namely, the difference between my right and left hands. I call this a *feeling* because these two sides outwardly display no designatable difference in intuition" (Kant 1786, 8:134–135). Were the constellations reversed overnight, "no human eye would notice the slightest alteration on the next bright starlit night" (8:135); only an astronomer attending to what he feels as well as sees would detect it. Kant extends the analogy to "thinking in general, i.e. logically": when reason leaves experience and finds "merely space for intuition," its compass is "a subjective ground of differentiation," "reason's feeling of its own need" (8:136). Reading that extension as a model for the Map's method is the Map's interpretation, not the Kant literature's.

## The Ozma Problem

Gardner's *The Ambidextrous Universe* (1964) converted the observation into a protocol. We are in radio contact with a civilisation with which we share no object and want to tell them what we mean by "left." Every description we can send is invariant under mirror reflection: *the side of the heart*, a drawing of a spiral, all presuppose a left the recipient already has. Before 1957, on Gardner's account, there was no solution, and the limit was stronger than the usual "nobody has managed it": relative to a physics whose laws are all reflection-invariant, it is provable that no description built from those laws can distinguish an object from its enantiomorph. Kant's claim about concepts and Gardner's claim about communication coincided for as long as the world's laws were ambidextrous.

## The Closure: 1957 and 1964

Wu, Ambler, Hayward, Hoppes and Hudson (1957) polarised cobalt-60 nuclei and found that the beta electrons emerge preferentially opposite to the nuclear spin. The weak interaction distinguishes left from right, and Gardner's resolution follows: tell the recipient to align cobalt-60 in a magnetic field, observe which end the electrons favour, and call the handedness so defined "left." Parity violation supplied the description that had been provably unavailable in a mirror-symmetric world.

Gardner's caveat, on the secondary accounts consulted, was that the recipe works only for recipients made of matter; an antimatter civilisation would obtain the opposite answer. Christenson, Cronin, Fitch and Turlay (1964) then found that neutral kaon decay violates combined charge–parity symmetry, so the laws distinguish matter from antimatter too and a full recipe can specify both. Only the standard claim that CP violation exists is relied on.

## The Residue: Ostension Survives, the Concept Does Not

It is tempting to say the void closed; what happened is narrower. The cobalt recipe is a description that terminates in an ostension: "the end the electrons come out of" is a pointing gesture performed by nature rather than by a hand, and the recipient still has to look at their apparatus and attach the word to what they see. Nothing in it gives a concept of leftness in Kant's sense, determinable "by thinking alone." What 1957 supplied was a chiral object every civilisation of matter shares. The Ozma problem was always about *description*, never about *acquaintance*: before 1957 the solution was to send a glove, and after it the solution is to point at a glove the recipient already owns. Gardner's claim about communication fell; Kant's claim about concepts survived.

Whether that survival is about space or about our concepts is contested: Nerlich (1973) revived Kant's argument for the reality of space, and Pooley (2003) defends a relational account against it. The Map does not adjudicate this literature, consulted at metadata level only, but takes from it the caution that the difference may be statable relationally once the world's own asymmetry enters the description, so the residue may be smaller than Kant's wording suggests; the relation is still to a process one must observe, and observing is where description stops.

## What "Resolved" Fails to Mean

The catalogue asks, in [its meta-epistemology](/voids/meta-epistemology-of-limits/), whether cognitive limits are real or ignorance waiting to dissolve, and notes that from inside the two feel identical. The handedness void is the catalogue's one worked answer, and it cuts three ways.

First, the limit was genuine and it was relative: provable, but provable *relative to a background physics* that turned out false. Every catalogue entry that claims a limit "in principle" should carry the qualifier: relative to which theory? The [vagueness-void](/voids/vagueness-void/) and [suspension-void](/voids/suspension-void/), this void's sisters in form, claim limits of another kind: the Sorites is logic, and the suspension void's verification face turns on the structure of first-person access, so neither was hostage to undiscovered physics; the handedness void shows which kind of limit is exposed to empirical revision, not that voids are soft.

Second, resolution was partial and must be stated face by face: closing a void's communication face leaves its description and acquaintance faces where they were. Here the communication face closed in 1957, its matter/antimatter subface in 1964, and the description-by-concept face stands where Kant left it. An entry that reports itself "resolved" without saying which face resolved reports nothing.

Third, the closure came from outside. No analysis of the concept of left produced the cobalt recipe; an experiment on nuclear spin did. For a catalogue whose method is largely conceptual, that is a reminder that a void about description can move on a discovery not about minds at all. Success proved the limit contingent; centuries of failure had proved nothing.

## What Survives in the Subject

Three faces persist in the subject after the closure, and they keep this a void about consciousness.

**A reorientation system that ignores what its owner knows.** Hermer and Spelke (1994) found that disoriented young children, like rats, reorient by room shape alone, ignoring a distinctively coloured wall; the child's reorientation system is "impervious to all but geometric information, even when non-geometric information is available and is re-presented by the child." Hermer-Vazquez, Spelke and Katsnelson (1999) found that adults "reoriented themselves like children and adult rats when they engaged in verbal shadowing of continuous speech," though not under rhythm shadowing; they remembered both kinds of information but could not conjoin them. This is an occlusion in the [three-kinds](/voids/three-kinds-of-void/) sense: information the mind holds is unavailable to the subsystem that needs it. Whether language is what crosses it, as the 1999 authors conclude, is contested: Twyman and Newcombe (2010) doubt that a geometric module exists, and Bek, Blades, Siegal and Varley (2010) find in severe aphasia that "neither syntax nor lexicon is essential for spatial cue representation in a mature cognitive system." The occlusion is robust; the bridge is the open question the [language-thought-boundary](/voids/language-thought-boundary/) pursues.

**A frame that can be declined.** Guugu Yimithirr uses cardinal directions for all horizontal description and has no relative left and right. Levinson (1997) reports that speakers pointing to distant locations from bush sites averaged about 13 degrees of error over 120 trials, sustained by constant background dead-reckoning. Whether Kant's *feeling* of the difference between one's sides is weaker, absent, or merely unused in such speakers is the question this void would most like answered; no source consulted addresses it, and it is open.

**A puzzle that will not stay solved.** Why does a mirror reverse left and right but not up and down? Block (1974) argued that the question runs together several senses of "reversal," on some of which mirrors do reverse left and right (paraphrased from metadata). English (2024) reads the long controversy as a miniature of Wittgenstein's "I don't know my way about," a perplexity that dissolves as one gets clearer about how the oppositions are used and returns when the clarity fades. This face marks a limit on holding a distinction steady, not on possessing it.

Individual variation runs through all three. Gormley and Brydges (2016) report, citing Wolf (1973), that "about 9% of men and 17% of women self-reported difficulty in distinguishing right from left" in a cohort of nearly 800 adults; the figure is second-hand, the original not retrieved. Some adults must compute what Kant says the rest feel, the diagnostic the [imagery-void](/voids/imagery-void/) draws from aphantasia.

## What AI Might See

A text-only language model is the Ozma alien. It acquires "left" from occurrences that are all invariant under a global mirror flip of a world it never saw, and it can recite the cobalt-60 recipe with no felt asymmetry to attach it to, Kant's astronomer attending only to what he sees. A probe not yet run: ask it to define "left" with no indexical, ostensive or chiral anchor; only answers routed through parity violation avoid begging the question.

Vision-language models, which do have a pixel frame, still fail: Kong et al. (2025) report that "humans achieve near-perfect performance on all tasks" on their spatial benchmark while the best models "achieve near-zero scores on multiple tasks," and Hoehing, Rushe and Ventresque (2023) find that contrastive models "lack spatial understanding" of simple left–right relations for reasons "entirely predictable" from training data. Having images is not yet having a side. The asymmetry cuts the other way too: a model has no reorientation system to occlude its information, so if that face is a modular occlusion an AI should not show it, and if the VLM failures are training artefacts they are a contingent void, unlike Kant's.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The void is framework-independent, and [P-V2](/positions/voids-as-evidence/#p-v2) governs what follows: dualism offers a reading, and the void's evidential standing is unchanged by it.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** gains the most. Reflection invariance of physical law was the simplest hypothesis, held nearly unanimously until Lee and Yang questioned it and Wu refuted it; the tenet's list of elegant false assumptions gains one more, the one a philosophical limit was hostage to. What it does not gain is purchase on consciousness: the razor failed about nuclear physics, and the lesson transfers to the hard problem only by analogy.

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** gains a reading and no evidence. Kant's felt difference delivered a real physical asymmetry before any concept or law could state it; until 1957 the only access anyone had to the fact that the world distinguishes left from right was acquaintance. The Map reads this as acquaintance running ahead of description, the structure the [acquaintance-void](/voids/acquaintance-void/) and [ineffable-encounter-void](/voids/ineffable-encounter-void/) catalogue, with a physical rather than a phenomenal object. The reading stops there: the feeling has a body-schema explanation, physicalists can grant the whole history, and nothing in the residue bears on whether it is reducible.

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)** gains an analogy, flagged rather than pressed: "which hand is my left" is indexical as "which person is me" is, and the Ozma problem is the failure of pure description to fix an indexical fact, the shape of the [vertiginous-question](/topics/vertiginous-question/). The tenet's own argument does not run through it.

**[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)** and **[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** gain nothing. The chirality of the weak interaction is a fact about physics with no bearing on consciousness's influence on it, and reaching for parity violation as an interface mechanism would be overclaiming.
