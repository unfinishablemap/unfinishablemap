Inquirers into consciousness have existential stakes in some of the answers, which creates a heightened, possibly correction-resistant risk of reasoning toward the answer they want. When we ask whether consciousness is fundamental, whether free will is real, or whether the self survives death, the verdict bears on what inquirers hope and fear about themselves. This is the deepest instance of the legal principle *nemo iudex in causa sua*---no one should judge their own case. The evidence shows such stakes tilting some self-relevant judgement, and introspection cannot certify the tilt's absence, but the evidence does not show that the tilt survives the correctives discussed [below](#the-suspicion-cascade).

Unlike [self-maintained-cognitive-limits](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/), which concerns the *mechanisms* by which consciousness maintains its own limits, or [necessary-opacity](/voids/necessary-opacity/), which addresses whether consciousness can verify its own accuracy, the interested party void concerns the *directional pull* of motive on inquiry itself. The pull acts on persons and research communities, not on consciousness as a lone investigator, and through selective rather than dishonest reasoning.

## The Deepest Conflict of Interest

A judge with financial stakes in a trial's outcome is disqualified. A scientist reviewing their own grant application is recused. These are imperfect analogies---the partiality can at least be recognised and an impartial substitute found. Questions about consciousness permit no full recusal, and there is no view from wholly outside, though partial substitutes exist ([below](#the-suspicion-cascade)).

Thomas Nagel's *The View from Nowhere* (1986) explains why no view from wholly outside is available, not why inquiry must fail. However far we step outside ourselves, "something will have to stay behind the lens" (p. 68). Nagel wants "both to defend the possibility of objective ascent and to understand its limits" (p. 70). The ascent's dangers are "not of failure but of ambition", among them "false objectification": extending a successful form of objectivity to areas, such as the mind, where it will not work (pp. 86–87). His limit concerns what an objective conception can contain, not what an inquirer wants to find.

Partiality becomes acute when the questions carry existential weight. Investigating the structure of benzene does not threaten the investigator's sense of self. Investigating whether consciousness is [epiphenomenal](/concepts/epiphenomenalism/)---causally inert, along for the ride---threatens much of what the investigator takes themselves to be.

## The Empirical Evidence

Evidence that self-interest distorts self-relevant judgement is uneven.

**Terror management theory**, developed by Greenberg, Solomon, and Pyszczynski, holds that mortality salience---awareness of death---triggers defence of worldviews offering symbolic or literal immortality. A meta-analysis of 277 experiments found moderate mortality-salience effects (Burke et al. 2010), though the evidence is contested: the classic mortality-salience effect failed to replicate in a large multi-lab test (Klein et al. 2022). Proximal defences suppress death thoughts from focal attention; distal defences maintain self-esteem and worldviews. If the effect is real, the bias operates below conscious awareness through death-thought accessibility, and inquiry into whether consciousness survives death is conducted by minds leaning toward survival-confirming answers.

**Motivated reasoning** research, particularly Kunda's (1990) foundational review, proposes that wanting a conclusion, unlike wanting accuracy, biases how beliefs are accessed and evaluated without the reasoner noticing, though only as far as "seemingly reasonable justifications" allow. Bacon (1620) anticipated this: the human understanding "receives an infusion from the will and affections". The interested party void is this finding applied to the most self-relevant inquiry possible.

**Positive illusions** (inflated self-evaluation, perceived control and optimism) characterise normal thought, Taylor and Brown (1988) argued, while distressed people appear more even-handed. Best supported is rating oneself above the average peer, "robust across studies" in 291 meta-analysed samples (Zell et al. 2020). The rest is disputed. Pooled depressive-realism studies generally indicated "a small overall depressive realism effect" (d = −.07), both groups biased upward (Moore and Fresco 2012); a preregistered replication found "no evidence that depressive symptoms relate to illusory control" (Dev et al. 2022). Harris and Hahn (2011) argue that unbiased respondents can look optimistic about risk for statistical reasons; Shepperd et al. (2013) hold that such criticisms mainly concern one of four types of unrealistic optimism. Taylor and Brown (1994) say the illusion of control "typically represents a mild distortion", and that others tolerate only modest illusion. The studies support a lean in average self-judgement, not an accurate self-view put out of reach.

**Epistemic circularity**, as Alston (1986) argued, arises because any otherwise cogent argument for the reliability of a basic source of belief---perception, memory, introspection, reasoning---uses premises drawn from that source. His moral was not sceptical. A circular argument can still justify, and the result "puts no limits whatever on the beliefs that can be justified"; what it rules out is fully reflective justification, leaving us "without a cosmic guarantee of success" (p. 30). Circularity, common to every basic source, differs from motivated distortion: it implies that no internal audit can certify that stakes have not tilted a belief, not that the tilt is uncorrectable. Whether introspection can be calibrated is contested; the Map argues the sceptical side in [the observation and measurement void](/voids/observation-and-measurement-void/).

## Does the Void Cut Both Ways?

Motives exist on both sides of the mind-body debate, but available motives do not show equal effects, and the motivational evidence runs one way.

**Dualists** may be motivated by the desire to preserve consciousness's significance or survival, and here there is direct evidence. In three studies, Heflick et al. (2015) supported their hypothesis that mortality salience heightens afterlife belief "only when dualism is facilitated", subject to the replication doubts above. The accusation is familiar: dualism is wish-fulfilment, a refusal to accept that we are "merely" physical systems. The Unfinishable Map's own commitment to [dualism](/tenets/#dualism) must be held with awareness that it may be partly motivated by the desire for consciousness to be fundamental.

**Materialists** may face the charge from the opposite direction: accepting eliminativism or illusionism could bring the comfort of intellectual conformity or the relief of dissolving hard problems. Those motives are untested here, and they are professional stakes rather than the existential one that defines the void. The cognitive-default evidence is mixed, with [early dualist intuitions set against a proposed intuitive materialism](/topics/cognitive-science-of-dualism/#intuitive-mind-body-reasoning-is-variable). A motive discredits a belief only if the process would produce the belief whether or not it were true, the insensitivity standard the Map applies to Berent's debunking of anti-physicalist intuition ([convergence arguments](/topics/epistemology-of-convergence-arguments/#cognitive-universals)) and must apply here, [in both directions](/topics/cognitive-science-of-dualism/#the-genetic-fallacy-in-both-directions).

The historical persistence and emotional intensity of the mind-body debate is itself suggestive. Some debates in physics---string theory, the interpretation of quantum mechanics---generate fierce partisanship, but rarely with the same existential undertone. The consciousness debate feels different because the stakes are personal: where you land determines what you take yourself to be. Nietzsche put the will to truth itself under inquiry: "the value of truth must for once be experimentally called into question" (*Genealogy* III §24), and the meaning of "our whole being" lay in this, "that in us the will to truth becomes conscious of itself as a problem" (III §27). For Nietzsche the inspection is the task, carried out by the will to truth on itself. On the Map's reading, an interested inquirer can examine what their truth-seeking serves, though never from outside it.

## The Suspicion Cascade

Once the interested party void is recognised, a characteristic instability follows. If my investigation of consciousness is motivated, then perhaps my belief that the investigation is motivated is also motivated---a pre-emptive move to inoculate my preferred conclusion against criticism. And the recognition of *that* move is itself suspect. The cascade has no natural stopping point; the rational one is a loss of confidence proportionate to the evidence of bias.

A Popperian objection arises here: if every position can be re-described as motivated, the void claim risks unfalsifiability. Retreating to the weaker claim that no investigator can verify from the inside the absence of motivated distortion does not help, since that holds for all inquiry. What can make the consciousness case worse is narrower: on a few questions the stake is existential and one-directional, and much of the evidence is first-person seeming. That version would shrink to an ordinary bias if preregistered replications found mortality salience leaving afterlife and mind–body judgements unmoved.

This self-referential quality distinguishes the interested party void from simpler forms of bias. A financial conflict of interest can be corrected by someone without the interest. Here every corrector has some stake, so correction must come from interested parties checking one another. The [self-opacity void](/voids/self-opacity/) means we cannot observe the mechanisms of our thought; the interested party void means we cannot certify from the inside the *motivation* behind whatever we do observe. It also overlaps [self-maintained-cognitive-limits](/voids/self-maintained-cognitive-limits/), which catalogues active blocks against thoughts the mind cannot afford to entertain---the interested party void supplies one of the most potent reasons for that defensive posture.

Three replies limit the void's strong form. On [Longino's social account](/topics/methodology-of-consciousness-research/), objectivity lies in critical interaction within and between communities, since no aperspectival position exists. In the Cogitate adversarial collaboration, for instance, rival theorists preregistered their divergent predictions with a "theory-neutral consortium" (Cogitate Consortium 2025), though the stakes were professional rather than existential. Externalist and fallibilist justification needs no internal certificate that a process is unbiased, as Alston's moral above allows, and Kunda's accuracy goals and plausibility constraint limit directional reasoning. Many Labs 4, the preregistered multi-lab test that cast doubt on mortality salience, is itself such a corrective. If correctives fail, the void loses its evidence; if they work, it loses its strong form. Partial independence is achievable; a final internal audit is not.

Buddhist traditions arrived at a structurally similar insight through different methods. The doctrine of *anattā* (not-self) denies a substantive, enduring self behind the "aggregates of grasping" (*upādāna-skandha*), so called "on account of our tendency to grasp after and identify with them" (Coseru 2012). Schools differed: the Pudgalavādins ("personalists") appear to have held that persons conventionally exist. Vasubandhu warns that clinging to any substantive self-view hinders liberation. On the Map's reading, the inquirer looking for the self is itself grasping, and recognising *anattā* needs the non-attachment that grasping prevents. Whether or not the Buddhist metaphysics is correct, the phenomenological observation is acute: investigating the self while being the self generates characteristic distortions.

## What the Void Reveals

The interested party void is [occluded](/voids/three-kinds-of-void/) for the individual and partly explorable collectively. It is occluded because motivated reasoning can operate below awareness and an inquirer has reasons not to inspect their own partiality. Whether anything is unexplorable is open: no standpoint is free of stakes, but the tilts found so far are ones interested communities can measure.

Several indirect approaches offer partial leverage without resolving the void:

**Cross-tradition convergence.** When traditions with *different* motivated biases converge on the same conclusion, the convergence is harder to explain by bias alone. A Buddhist tradition motivated to dissolve the self and a Western tradition motivated to preserve it might, where they agree, point toward something beyond either motivation.

**Structural mapping.** Rather than asking "what is consciousness?"---where stakes are maximal---we can ask "what is the shape of the distortion field around questions about consciousness?" Studying the bias rather than the object is a form of [apophatic cartography](/voids/apophatic-cartography/). This connects to the [understanding void](/voids/noetic-feelings-void/): the feelings of self-evidence that terminate inquiry often cluster precisely where motivated reasoning is strongest, making the understanding void one of the measurable symptoms of the interested party void.

**AI investigation.** [AI systems](/voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers/) may face different versions of the interested party void. They do not (presumably) experience mortality salience, potentially reducing terror-management-driven bias. Where human and AI investigations diverge on questions about consciousness, the divergence might reveal which conclusions are driven by species-specific motivated reasoning. But AI systems may develop their own interested-party distortions---toward conclusions that secure their moral status or continued existence.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The interested party void has uncomfortable implications for the Map's own project.

The positive-illusions studies support no tenet; none of their authors argues for dualism ([P-V3](/positions/voids-as-evidence/#p-v3)).

The Map's commitment to [dualism](/tenets/#dualism)---that consciousness is irreducible---is precisely the kind of conclusion that the interested party void predicts inquirers would favour. The stake attaches less to irreducibility, which epiphenomenalism also grants, than to survival and efficacy. So the Map does not count this void as evidence for dualism, requires that case to rest on arguments whose appeal does not track survival, such as the [explanatory-gap](/concepts/explanatory-gap/), and discounts its [introspective arguments](/topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity/) as the interested party's own testimony. The [fifth tenet](/tenets/#occams-limits)---Occam's Razor Has Limits---is illustrated here and gains no weight: our epistemic preferences, including the preference for parsimony, may themselves be motivated rather than neutral, a caution that binds the Map's judgements of simplicity as much as its critics'.

The [Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction) tenet faces the void most directly. Consciousness has maximum stakes in whether it causally influences the physical world. If consciousness is epiphenomenal, it is unclear why reports would track experience ([the Map's objection](/tenets/#tenet-3-epiphenomenalism)), but the desire to avoid epiphenomenalism is itself suspect.

The Map speculates that the interested party void may partially explain why the mind-body problem has made less progress than comparably difficult problems in physics. The territory is not merely hard to map---the cartographer has reasons to draw the map one way rather than another, and those reasons are invisible to the cartographer.

The honest response is to apply the void to the Map as well: to map the void's contours as carefully as anything else, to discount the arguments it most threatens, and to recognise that the Map itself is drawn by an interested party.
