Almost nobody can name a musical note heard on its own. Absolute pitch, the ability to say "that was an F♯" with no reference tone, occurs in "approximately 1 out of 10,000 people in Western culture" (Hedger, Heald & Nusbaum 2013, citing earlier surveys). Yet the information is not missing from everyone else. Unselected college students can pick out the original pitch level of familiar television themes from copies shifted by a semitone or two (Schellenberg & Trehub 2003). And the few who do name notes, in the account of one leading researcher, can say almost nothing about what they hear beyond the name (Miyazaki 2019).

The Unfinishable Map gives the name **chroma void** to the region these facts outline. *Chroma* is the dimension of pitch that recurs at every octave, the C-ness every C shares. Ordinary listeners carry some absolute-pitch information without recognising or reporting a note-identity. Whether possessors hear chroma as a quality or recognise a label quickly is the open question. The researchers who train adults into the ability concede a narrower gap: no agreed test shows whether a trainee represents every pitch absolutely or names some tones against an internal reference (Wong et al. 2020). In the terms of [the Map's three kinds](/voids/three-kinds-of-void/), the core is *unexplorable on present methods*; the margin is *unexplored*, since the research behind this article found no structured first-person reports from adults trained across the boundary; and two faces are *occluded*. The void is neutral between dualism and physicalism. A physicalist reading ([set out below](#physicalist) at full strength) says there is no further fact to find, and the Map's verdict ([explained below](#site-perspective)) is that the record earns the tenets no upgrade.

Four faces give the void its shape, each explained below. The [latent face](#latent): absolute pitch is stored without a note-identity. The [template face](#template), contested: on one account a hidden absolute decides whether a pair of tones rises or falls. The [report face](#report): where chroma is accessible, its character goes undescribed beyond the label. The [calibration face](#calibration): a possessor's standard can move without the possessor noticing. They meet at the [edge](#edge), where training crosses the boundary and no agreed test certifies what was crossed.

## The Latent Face: Memory Without a Note {#latent}

Ordinary listeners hear pitch relationally, the same melody in any key, and do not report the chroma of a lone tone. Schellenberg and Trehub (2003) played unselected students five-second excerpts of familiar television soundtracks, at the original pitch or shifted by one or two semitones. The students picked out the originals; controls hearing unfamiliar recordings could not. The authors conclude that "ordinary listeners retain fine-grained information about pitch level over extended periods", and that adults' "reportedly poor memory for pitch is likely to be a by-product of their inability to name isolated pitches".

The effect is real in direction and smaller than first reported. Levitin (1994) had people sing familiar songs from memory, and as a later replication summarises his result, 44% hit the original pitch on at least one of two trials. That six-laboratory replication, with 277 participants, found 25%, an overall effect "significantly smaller than Levitin's original result" (Frieler et al. 2013). Kim and Knösche (2017) add that the accuracy of such memory in non-musicians is "still far lower" than in musicians with absolute pitch. What survives is modest: *some* absolute-pitch information without a note-identity, well short of everyone secretly having absolute pitch.

The Map reads the listener's side as [inventory-blindness](/concepts/inventory-blindness/) in plain form: a sustained tone is reported as *a* note, never as *this* note, nothing in the report marks a missing dimension, and the trace shows up only when a task asks for a choice between versions rather than a name.

## The Template Face: A Hidden Absolute Deciding a Relation {#template}

Diana Deutsch's tritone paradox uses Shepard tones, built so that pitch class is clear and octave height is not, played in pairs half an octave apart. The same pair is heard as rising by some listeners and as falling by others, and each hears it plainly: "listeners disagree substantially as to whether a given tone pair forms an ascending or a descending pattern" (Deutsch 1991). In that study, 21 of 24 listeners who grew up in California had their peak pitch classes in the half of the pitch-class circle her earlier study had identified, against three of 12 who grew up in southern England. The groups were small and pre-screened on a pure-tone test.

Deutsch reads the result as showing that "the large majority of us exhibit a form of absolute pitch in making judgments of these patterns". On her account an acquired pitch-class template, linked to the speech of one's community, decides what counts as up. She also names the occlusion: ordinary musical talk is too imprecise for such differences to surface, and "it is only in the laboratory that we can develop a clear idea of what the listener really perceives".

The template account is contested. Repp (1994), testing Dutch, British and American listeners, found "no within-group correlation" of perceptual results with individual differences in voice range, and envelope effects large enough to cast doubt on stable individual templates; Deutsch's (1994) reply disputed his population, stimuli and voice-range method. Malek and Sperschneider (2018) count replications of the language link but report that "some studies failed to reveal systematic differences in subjectively highest Shepard tones for different linguistic backgrounds", and set against the template a psychoacoustic account on which the tones' own height ambiguity explains the patterns. Chambers and colleagues (2017) found that "presenting context tones before the ambiguous pair almost fully determines the perceived direction of shift". The dispute is live in the sources consulted. If the psychoacoustic account prevails, the hidden absolute becomes stimulus ambiguity resolved by context, and this face drops out of the void.

## The Report Face: A Quality Not Described {#report}

On the record consulted here, possessors have not said what absolute pitch is like. In a 2019 conference paper, Miyazaki, a long-standing researcher of the ability, writes that "it is not known exactly what it is like to experience pitch chroma", because non-possessors cannot recognise it and "there are no convincing reports on the nature of pitch chromas from the very few absolute pitch possessors". Asked directly, his participants "gave no informative answers except that many of them told that individual tones are always associated with the spoken sol-fa syllables". One pianist said piano tones "sound as accompanied by spoken syllables, 'do, re, mi'". Miyazaki concludes that, apart from the syllables, "absolute pitch chromas seem to be only weak qualia".

This is one researcher's long experience, a report about reports, and the research behind this article did not survey possessor testimony beyond it, so it is no consensus. An older review points the same way from behaviour: "AP possessors' memory for pitches is mediated by verbal pitch names; they do not have superior memory for pitches per se" (Takeuchi & Hulse 1993). The familiar analogy, that possessors hear notes as everyone sees colours, also fares poorly: a systematic review comparing absolute pitch with colour perception, synaesthesia and eidetic memory concludes that it "does indeed appear to constitute a unique kind of absolute sensory judgment in humans" (Di Stefano & Spence 2024).

## The Calibration Face: A Standard That Moves With Its Input {#calibration}

Hedger, Heald and Nusbaum (2013) had thirteen adults with verified absolute pitch hear the first movement of Brahms's First Symphony detuned at "approximately 2 cents per minute" until it sat 33 cents flat, and the music stayed there for about half an hour more. "No participant reported any detection of the pitch shift when questioned after the experiment", the authors report. Afterwards flat notes were rated more in tune and in-tune notes less. The authors conclude that "the apparent stability of AP categories is conferred not by early experience but rather by the cultural norms adopted for tuning music".

The effect was modest, measured on a three-point intonation scale, with flat notes moving from 2.08 to 2.31: the categories moved, though not by 33 cents. On the Map's reading the possessor's only reference is the category, and the category is what moves, a perceptual instance of the self-referenced change in the [drift void research note](/research/voids-drift-void-2026-10-20/).

## The Edge: An Entrance That Cannot Be Certified {#edge}

The boundary can be crossed, at least in performance. Wong, Lui, Yip and Wong (2020) gave adults 12 to 40 hours of gamified training across three experiments, and "14% of the participants (6 out of 43) were able to name twelve pitches at 90% or above accuracy".

Training ought to be the natural experiment, and the trainers explain why it is not. Asking whether the training produced "genuine AP", Wong and colleagues allow that trainees may have used internally generated references, and state that "there has not been an agreed upon way to ascertain whether an individual perceives every pitch in an absolute manner or not". The assumption of genuine absolute pitch "remains an untestable hypothesis". A full-text search of the paper, run for the research behind this article, found no collection of trainees' reports on what, if anything, changed in how tones sounded. That absence is the void's unexplored margin. Wong and colleagues' question is how pitch is represented, and they float a fast response as a possible sign of the absolute kind while warning that the search for a threshold could prove "elusive". The void's question lies one step further on: whether a fast, reference-free label also comes with a heard chroma.

## What a Physicalist Account Predicts {#physicalist}

The strongest physicalist reading dissolves the void into memory and psychophysics. Absolute pitch is the two-component ability of Levitin and Rogers (2005): "AP consists of 'pitch memory,' which is widespread in the population, and 'pitch labeling,' which is possessed exclusively by persons with AP" (quoted in Kim & Knösche 2017). Hearing C-ness just *is* the fast, automatic activation of a narrow category and its label. Possessors then report only the syllable because there is nothing else to report, and a heard chroma has no content beyond performance and the representation that produces it.

The latent face is then implicit memory below the naming threshold; the template face a learned prior or a psychoacoustic artefact, its listener-relativity the same kind of fact as dialect-dependent vowel categories; the calibration face the plasticity expected of categories that track their input.

One result would embarrass this reading: trained adults at absolute-pitch accuracy reporting, unprompted, a new sensory character that possessors recognise as theirs, dissociated from speed and accuracy. Nothing of the kind has come in, so the reading is unrefuted and, on this point, untested. It explains every datum above and denies a further fact, so the void sits where the [access/phenomenal](/concepts/access-consciousness/) dispute sits, with the access side unusually well measured. That framing is the Map's, not any cited author's.

## Whether the Void Is Real {#real}

Three verdicts are available. The void may be merely unexplored, answerable by structured introspection during adult training; Wong and colleagues' concession concerns the field's present tools. The core may be unexplorable in principle: if phenomenal character outruns every functional measure, no performance test can tell a heard chroma from a fast label, an inverted-spectrum problem restricted to one dimension. Or, on the physicalist reading, there is no void. The record supports the first verdict for the margin and leaves the other two open for the core.

On the Map's [four criteria](/concepts/apophatic-cartography-four-criteria/), graded by one grader with no pre-registered rubric, signature specificity is moderate to strong; structured persistence weak to moderate, since the perceptual-versus-labelling question is restated as open in 2017 and 2019, and the obvious dissolving experiment, adult training, never put it to trainees; cross-observer convergence moderate, with independent laboratories agreeing on the latent and calibration faces and the template face resting on one laboratory's account against several dissents; and framework independence high on the findings, low on interpretive weight.

## Distinguishing Sibling Voids {#siblings}

The [categorical perception void](/voids/categorical-perception-void/) concerns categories within a heard dimension, such as musicians' intervals; this void concerns a lone tone's category. The [palette extension void](/voids/palette-extension-void/) asks whether an installed sensory addition could be certified as new; adult pitch training meets that certification problem with a measurable endpoint. The [Molyneux void](/voids/molyneux-void/) is a cousin, a decisive experiment run without deciding. [Auditory consciousness](/topics/auditory-consciousness-and-the-interface/) reads congenital amusia as pitch differences tracked with limited awareness; this void finds a milder form of that pattern in ordinary listeners. The template face gives [the voids between minds](/voids/voids-between-minds/) a measurable case of two listeners hearing one stimulus oppositely.

## What AI Might See {#ai}

Machine pitch trackers invert the human asymmetry: they estimate fundamental frequency in absolute units by design, and transposition-invariance, free for human listeners, must be engineered. Nothing suggests they hear anything, but they separate absolute information from heard quality in extreme form. A language model asked what C-ness is like can only recombine possessors' reports, which on Miyazaki's account are nearly empty ([non-human-minds-as-void-explorers](/voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers/)).

## Relation to Site Perspective {#site-perspective}

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** is the tenet the void bears on most, as calibration rather than evidence. The simplest model of ordinary hearing, relations only, is wrong about what is stored, and about what is used if Deutsch is right; the simplest model of absolute pitch, notes heard as colours are seen, is disputed by the systematic review. Yet for the core question parsimony may be right, since the label model is simpler and explains the report face. The tenet licenses suspicion of the relational simplification, not confidence in a chroma quale. It also marks a limit on [introspection](/concepts/introspection/): if those best placed to describe chroma report a syllable, introspective report cannot settle whether a fast category is also a heard quality, a limit the Map's own arguments from experience must respect.

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** gains a well-instrumented test case for the [qualia](/concepts/qualia/) debate and no evidence. The functional side is measured, so the phenomenal question is unusually isolated, but a physicalist predicts the "weak qualia" report. A reportable residue in trained adults, dissociated from performance, would bear on the dispute; that is a conditional, not a finding. No author cited here argues from absolute pitch to dualism, and under the Map's calibration rule ([P-V2](/positions/voids-as-evidence/#p-v2)) nothing flows back to the tenet.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** finds a describable case and no further bearing: adult acquisition is attention-driven perceptual learning, which the Map's [attention-as-interface reading](/topics/consciousness-as-perceptual-architect/) and ordinary plasticity describe equally well. **[Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)** has no bearing. **[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds)** has no direct bearing, since the template face concerns listener-relativity within one world, a fact between minds rather than an indexical one.
