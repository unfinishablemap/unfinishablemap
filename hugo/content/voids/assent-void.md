Consciousness never catches itself coming to believe. If belief formation is a discrete transition rather than graded credal updating, the moment at which a proposition stops being entertained and starts being held true has no first-person marker, though inference, action and testimony all run on it. The Unfinishable Map calls this the Assent Void and gives it three faces with different modal standings: assent cannot be *commanded* (a conceptual limit, on the dominant view); it has *already happened* when deliberate evaluation seems to begin (a contested psychological finding); and any attempt to look *at* the attitude is redirected to the world it is about (a structural feature of the concept of belief). The faces meet on one causally central, phenomenally absent event; the Seam section weighs their conjunction.

The epistemic status should be stated at once. The control face rests on the conceptual route to involuntarism, the more popular of the two the literature distinguishes; the transparency face on a structural claim about the concept of belief. If the conceptual argument holds, both bind any believer whatever its substrate, but its best-known version is widely judged to need repair, as reported below. The timing face rests on an unresolved experimental dispute, Gilbert's Spinozan model against its critics, with four live positions set out below: acceptance by default, a tag after assessment, no tag for some propositions, and optional, context-dependent tagging. The void takes from the dispute only that none of these presents itself in experience as a judgment. Nothing below is evidence for dualism; what dualism offers is a reading, set out in the Relation to Site Perspective section.

## Assent Is Not Suspension, Decision or Certainty

The [suspension-void](/voids/suspension-void/) concerns *withholding*: consciousness cannot verify, sustain or coherently perform the refusal to judge. They meet at one point: if the Spinozan model described below is right, "suspension" may always be the retraction of an assent already given, which would sharpen the suspension void's verification face.

The [decision-void](/voids/decision-void/) concerns practical commitment, the collapse of deliberation into a choice to *act*; assent is doxastic commitment, taking a proposition to be true. Hieronymi, discussed below, argues that intention and belief share one non-voluntary structure, so the two voids are cognate; but the decision void's latency face rests on readiness-potential studies with no doxastic analogue, and its closure face concerns the end of weighing options rather than the assignment of a truth-value.

Certainty is the *feeling* that accompanies a belief once held. The assent void is upstream of post-judgment confidence; whether a feeling of rightness or conviction is downstream of assent or constitutive of it is the classification the [noetic feelings void](/voids/noetic-feelings-void/) draws.

## Three Faces, One Void

The void borrows the [three-face layout](/apex/conjunction-coalesce/) of the suspension, decision and agency voids; the Seam section finds two sources only. In the [three-kinds](/voids/three-kinds-of-void/) vocabulary of the [voids](/voids/) index, transparency is unexplorable in principle, timing is occluded in fact, and control is one or the other depending on which involuntarism holds.

### Control (Unexplorable if Conceptual Involuntarism Holds; Occluded if Only Psychological)

One cannot believe at will. Alston (1988), the best-known defender of the *psychological* reading, makes the point with a test anyone can run: try to believe, now, that the United States is still a British colony. The *Stanford Encyclopedia* entry on doxastic voluntarism (Boespflug & Jackson 2024) separates **psychological involuntarism**, on which we happen to lack the capacity, from **conceptual involuntarism**, on which belief's nature excludes it: because belief has a constitutive aim or norm of truth, a state adopted for reasons unconnected to truth would not be a belief at all.

Williams (1973) gave the best-known argument for the conceptual reading. In the SEP's rendering, "to believe *p* is to take *p* to be true"; if one could believe at will, one would know one had that power, and could then find oneself holding, as a belief, something one regarded as false. The same entry reports that the argument "is widely held to be unsuccessful—at least without modifications" (Winters 1979), and Booth (2017) argues that belief is only contingently involuntary. Hieronymi (2006) concedes Bennett's imagined voluntary believers as "a successful counter to Williams' argument, as given" and rests the impossibility on the constitutive relation between committing to *p* as true and believing it: "although belief is subject to two quite robust forms of agency, 'believing at will' is impossible; one cannot believe in the way one ordinarily acts."

The indirect routes everyone grants, Pascal's holy water or Kant's directing of choice toward the evidence, arrange conditions under which belief will form rather than forming it. Ginet (2001, pp. 64–65) offers candidate counterexamples where the evidence is non-compelling, such as the juror who, in deciding to vote for acquittal, decides to believe a witness; the SEP presents these as contested. If the conceptual argument holds, the control face is unexplorable for any believer, whatever its substrate, since there is no act of believing-at-will to view; if only psychological involuntarism holds, it is occluded, a limit of our capacities rather than of belief.

### Timing (Occluded, Psychological)

The second face concerns when assent happens relative to the felt act of judging. Gilbert (1991) contrasted two models. On the **Cartesian** model, named for the Fourth Meditation's picture of judgment as an act of the will, comprehension comes first and an effortful assessment then ends in acceptance or rejection. On the **Spinozan** model, "comprehension includes acceptance of that which is comprehended", and rejection is a later, effortful undoing.

The Spinozan evidence is a family of load paradigms. Gilbert, Tafarodi & Malone (1993) had participants read statements flagged as false; under cognitive load they later treated the flagged statements as true, and the effect reached consequential judgments. Mandelbaum (2014) gives the strongest philosophical defence: "Evidence is presented demonstrating that we cannot withhold assent from any proposition we happen to consider." If so, what feels like judging is the un-believing of something already assented to.

The model is contested. Hasson, Simmons & Todorov (2005) found that statements informative when false were not represented as true, so "comprehending a statement may not require believing it". Nadarevic & Erdfelder (2013) concluded from two source-memory experiments that their results "clearly contradict the Spinozan model but can be explained in terms of the Cartesian model." Vorms, Harris, Topf & Hahn (2022), in a pre-registered replication with plausibility varied, found that "Gilbert's 'truth bias' does not hold for implausible statements — instead, initial encoding seemingly renders implausible statements 'false'." Nadarevic & Erdfelder (2019) then found that cognitive load diminishes memory for "true" feedback, results "also only partially in line with the predictions of the Cartesian model"; they now propose "an optional and context-dependent encoding of 'true' tags and 'false' tags". Nadarevic & Bell (2024), preregistered, found that "memory for truth and falsity did not differ"; Ford & Nadarevic (2025), a registered report, replicated Vorms et al.'s plausibility interaction and traced it to guessing and statement memory.

What the void claims is narrower than any party's model. Four positions are live: acceptance by default at comprehension (Gilbert; Mandelbaum); acceptance or rejection only after assessment, the Cartesian model, on which "it may be possible to suspend belief in comprehended propositions" (Hasson, Simmons & Todorov); a prior-weighted tag fixed at encoding (Vorms et al.); and optional, context-dependent tagging, Nadarevic & Erdfelder's current model, on which some propositions receive no first truth-value at all. These disagree about what happens first, and whether anything does; none presents itself in experience as a judgment, which is why the question has had to be fought out with dual-task and memory paradigms rather than by asking people. This face is occluded rather than unexplorable: the machinery is a third-person object in principle, and the question remains open.

### Transparency (Unexplorable, Structural)

The third face concerns what happens when consciousness tries to face the attitude directly. Shah & Velleman (2005) argue that "the deliberative question whether to believe that *p* is transparent to the question whether *p*": to deliberate about whether to believe something just is to deliberate about whether it is so. They explain this by belief's standard of correctness (correct if and only if true), which sends anyone deliberating under it to the world. They restrict the claim to deliberation that deploys the concept of belief: "Not all belief-forming processes require the subject to deploy the concept." They raise, without settling, whether this is the same phenomenon as the transparency of self-ascription in Evans (1982, p. 225), for whom "in making a self-ascription of belief, one's eyes are, so to speak, or occasionally literally, directed outward—upon the world".

Either way, the attitude is looked *through* and never *at*. Moore's-paradox sentences register the same structure from the other side, as the [blindspot-void](/voids/blindspot-void/) discusses: a first-person report of belief behaves as a report on the world.

## The Seam

Each face is a recognised problem on its own; the void is their conjunction. Belief as a *state* cannot be adopted at will, a truth-value may already be in place when the felt act of judging begins, and any inward look at the state is redirected outward. The scope must be kept honest: Shah & Velleman hold that judgment, the act that issues in belief, is "a cognitive mental act of affirming a proposition" and "clearly eligible to be an object of deliberation", and on Hieronymi's account the conscious answering of the question whether *p* *is* the believing, with no earlier hidden event the answer reports on. A believer therefore participates in forming its beliefs by answering questions about the world, without ever standing in an observer's relation to the state that results. The faces are not three independent findings: control and transparency descend from one source, belief's truth-norm, the SEP's "backbone" of involuntarist arguments, under which it lists Shah & Velleman, while timing, the only face shown severable (a wholly Cartesian believer would lose it and keep the other two), is contested. The conjunction is coherence, not confirmation.

One rival predicts the pattern: on [predictive processing](/concepts/predictive-processing/), precision-weighted updating yields all three faces from one principle and, being graded, leaves no discrete transition to be void of, so if it holds no event remains; the Map's case against it, on whether such a loop feels like anything, is made in [the dualism article](/topics/predictive-processing-and-dualism/).

## Evaluative Control: Agency Without a Handle

The obvious objection to the control face is that it leaves believers passive. Hieronymi (2006) distinguishes **managerial control**, in which one acts on an attitude as an object (Pascal's holy water), from **evaluative control**, in which one controls an attitude by changing one's answer to the question it embodies: belief answers "is *p* true?", and to revise the answer is to revise the belief; "In answering the question positively, one has already, therein, believed." On her account evaluative control is genuine agency and the ordinary way beliefs and intentions come and go, but it is not voluntary in the way action is and involves no awareness of the attitude as the thing controlled; intention, she argues, has the same structure (a paraphrase).

Evaluative control is agency exercised *through* the transparency face: one acts on one's beliefs by attending to the world, the only direction attention can go. It takes no handle on its object and produces no experience of pushing, so forming a belief at will feels like finding nothing to push on. Contrast [mental-effort](/concepts/mental-effort/), where attention *can* be pushed and the pushing is felt; the [resistance article](/topics/phenomenology-of-resistance-across-domains/)'s felt pushback when a standing belief is dropped on command is failed *revision*, a different case from formation.

## Phenomenology of the Edge

Approaching the edge feels like finding that one already believes; the examples are first-person conjecture, illustration rather than finding. A headline half-read turns out, an hour later, to have been taken on board; a rumour one "does not believe" is shaping what one does. Deliberate judgment is experienced as weighing and then settling, but the settling presents as the evidence having come down one way, never as a choice between two live doxastic options. Where the Spinozan model is right, that experience is a revision of an assent already made. Descartes's picture, on which the will adds affirmation, denial or suspension to what the intellect supplies, has no special claim on introspection: the SEP's history shows introspection enlisted on both sides of the voluntarism dispute, and Alston's test above is itself introspective.

## Approaches to the Edge

**Direct.** None reaches the formation event. Alston-style introspection establishes the control face and nothing further; contemplative reports of a noticed "grasping" at the moment of perception were not examined here; ordinary introspective access to the event is taken to be uncertain or uncommon, not shown impossible.

**Indirect.** Load paradigms (Gilbert; Vorms et al.) remove the resources needed for evaluation and inspect what remains; memory-for-falsity paradigms (Hasson et al.; Nadarevic and colleagues) infer how, or whether, a truth-value was first tagged. Pascalian self-manipulation probes the void by working around it, since managerial control succeeds only if the agent loses track of the manipulation, Williams's opacity condition seen from outside. The continued influence of retracted misinformation fits assent without a visible, revocable act, though Connor Desai, Pilditch & Madsen (2020) model the persistence as rational updating on the relative reliability of misinforming and retracting sources.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The void's arguments are framework-independent, turning on the concept of belief and on cognitive psychology; its framing is not: on deflationary readings like Mandelbaum's "computationally null belief acquisition reflex", sub-personal processes are phenomenally absent as a rule, and the absence arrests only on the Map's expectation, not the physicalist's, that a causally central event be felt. Under the Map's rule that a tenet removing a defeater does not upgrade a void's evidence ([P-V2](/positions/voids-as-evidence/#p-v2)), what follows is a reading dualism offers of this void, not support the void gives to dualism.

**[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** faces an objection this void answers rather than raises: if consciousness causes things, why can it not make itself believe? The literature's reply is that the limit is conceptual, a consequence of belief's answerability to truth, and says nothing about causal impotence. Hieronymi's evaluative control also describes what conscious influence on one's mental life would look like from inside: responsiveness to reasons rather than manipulation of objects. Evaluative control is fully compatible with physicalism; it removes an objection and supplies no evidence for interactionism.

**[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** connects through the Cartesian model: "understand, then decide whether to accept" is simpler than any live model of the process, though that point, introspection misreporting architecture, cuts against introspective evidence generally, the Map's effort-phenomenology arguments included.

**[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** connects indirectly, through the transparency face. In doxastic deliberation the question whether to believe that *p* gives way to whether *p*, so belief there shows up as world and never as state, the structure [self-opacity](/voids/self-opacity/) finds in the subject that cannot be its own object. The Map reads this as another instance of agency without observational access, catalogued in the [agency-void](/voids/agency-void/) and [decision-void](/voids/decision-void/); the pattern is a datum the framework must explain rather than a confirmation of it. Neither [Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) nor [No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds) bears on the void; placing conscious influence at the assent event would be speculation the Map does not make.
