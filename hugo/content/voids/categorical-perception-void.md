Where the world offers a continuum, report arrives already carved; whether experience does is untested. Voice-onset time runs smoothly from 0 to 60 milliseconds, but a listener reports /b/ and then, abruptly, /p/; hue, facial expression and musical interval behave the same way. The perceiver discriminates two stimuli that straddle a boundary more easily than two equally different stimuli inside one, and reports the verdict rather than the gradient. This is categorical perception, and the Map's categorical perception void is the region it hides: the interior of each category, reported at reduced resolution by an operation the perceiver did not choose and cannot watch.

The strong version of this void has been retired by the field that discovered it. The textbook claim, traceable to Liberman and colleagues in 1957, was that a listener can hear no differences among speech sounds beyond those the phoneme labels reveal. McMurray's 2022 survey concludes that within speech perception this strongest form is widely regarded as an artefact of task demands, and that the underlying representation of the continuum appears linear, a verdict qualified below. What survives is narrower: a limit on *default report* and a limit on *provenance*. "Default" means one thing here: what the perceiver reports when asked to categorise, which is the category; ordinary behaviour uses the gradient (explained below). And nothing in the experience of a boundary says whether it tracks a joint in the world, a threshold of the auditory system, or one installed by language, training or the first year of life. An installed boundary feels exactly like a discovered one. In the terms of [three-kinds-of-void](/voids/three-kinds-of-void/) it is Occluded, the compression being the perceiver's own operation.

What this article adds to the neighbouring [resolution-void](/voids/resolution-void/), [language-thought-boundary](/voids/language-thought-boundary/) and [expertise void](/voids/expertise-and-its-occlusion/) is the origin face as a perceptual worked example of the [source-attribution void](/voids/source-attribution-void/), and a contrast the catalogue had not drawn: the same mind that is vague where its concepts should be sharp is sharp where the world is continuous.

## The Warp and Its Deflation

The robust core is a behavioural signature, called the warp here for brevity: discrimination is better across a category boundary than within one, controlling for physical difference, the definition in Goldstone and Hendrickson (2010), and it has been reported in five domains, speech being the original. In colour, Winawer and colleagues (2007) found Russian speakers, whose language obligatorily distinguishes lighter *goluboy* from darker *siniy*, faster to discriminate two blues straddling that border than two from the same side, with the advantage removed by a concurrent verbal task but not a spatial one. In faces, Etcoff and Magee (1992) found expressions morphed in constant physical steps discriminated more poorly within an expression category than across one. In music, Burns and Ward (1978) found trained musicians perceiving melodic intervals categorically under standard procedures. Pérez-Gay Juárez, Sicotte, Thériault and Harnad (2019) taught subjects to sort textures into two categories by feedback alone and found between-category separation and, to a lesser extent, within-category compression in dissimilarity judgements, with a correlated decrease in the occipital N1.

The deflation is equally well replicated. Gerrits and Schouten (2004) ran the same vowel continuum through two discrimination tasks: a four-interval task showed no categorical perception, while a two-interval forced-choice task on identical stimuli produced the classic pattern; Schouten, Gerrits and van Hessen (2003) attribute the variation to task factors. Burns and Ward's musicians lost most of their categorical signature under an adaptive procedure, and continuous ratings of how /b/-like a token is track the acoustics. The colour face has deflated in stages. Witzel and Gegenfurtner (2011), with 230 observers, found category effects that were real but not lateralised as Gilbert, Regier, Kay and Ivry (2006) had reported, leaving lateralisation unsettled. Martinovic, Paramei and MacInnes (2020) found the Russian advantage at blue/green but not at *siniy/goluboy*, whose boundary drifted toward lighter shades with a lighter stimulus set. Later work with Witzel reads such effects as post-perceptual: with same- and different-category pairs equally discriminable, He, Witzel, Forder, Clifford and Franklin (2014) found category effects only from 200 milliseconds; and Martinovic and colleagues (2026), re-running the Greek-blue paradigm, found its early mismatch response driven by contrast adaptation and unreliable for contrast-equated category changes. The Whorfian colour effect should be carried as task-dependent, its locus unsettled and often post-perceptual.

So the between face splits, as the [palette-extension-void](/voids/palette-extension-void/) split, into two questions. Can the perceiver reach the within-category continuum? Yes, by task, continuous rating or training. Is it reported under a categorisation instruction? No. An occlusion that vanishes when the question is asked differently is an occlusion of explicit report, not of capacity.

## Discovered or Installed

Perceived boundaries have three candidate origins. Some may track discontinuities in the world. Some are built into the senses: chinchillas trained on /d/ and /t/ endpoints place the boundary on the continuum where humans do (Kuhl and Miller 1975), evidence that linguistic knowledge is not necessary for boundary-like responding there. Others are demonstrably installed. Pérez-Gay Juárez et al. (2019) manufactured the texture boundary by feedback in one session, in the roughly half of participants who learned the categories. The *siniy/goluboy* border is one English speakers do not carry. The interval scale belongs to musicians. And the phonemic inventory is pruned early: Werker and Tees (1984) found English-learning infants discriminating Hindi and Salish consonant contrasts at six to eight months and largely losing the ability by ten to twelve, while infants learning those languages kept it, which Werker and Hensch (2015) place inside a critical-period model.

The origin face is the phenomenological point these results make together. An adult English speaker reports /r/ and /l/ as two sounds; an adult Japanese speaker reports one; each reports a joint, and neither can inspect its provenance. The [infant consciousness void](/voids/infant-consciousness/) carries the narrowing data as a developmental limit; here the point is what the adult inherits: clean joints left by a pruning the adult cannot remember or undo by attention.

Burnston (2021) closes the other exit, against proponents of [cognitive-penetration](/concepts/cognitive-penetration/) who hold that beliefs permeate perception as categories are learned: to guide learning, a belief would have to describe contents the subject can already perceive, so it cannot specify the ability actually acquired, and the learning process must operate outside cognitive influence. If that is right, the Map reads the installed boundary as opaque from both directions: the perceiver cannot see whether it was installed, and the perceiver's own intentions during learning could not have specified what was.

The provenance limit has no first-person method, and real boundaries have mixed causal origins rather than one discoverable provenance. Whether a felt joint is world-given or installed is inferred only by comparing the perceiver to a differently carved perceiver. Boundary drift, as at the *siniy/goluboy* border, is only a weak probe: range effects shift every judged boundary, so drift shows context-dependence, not installation. None of this is available inside a single perceiver's experience: the source-attribution void's general finding, content without an origin tag, met at a perceptual boundary.

## The Gradient That Is Kept and Used

The residue face is fixed by two results. Toscano, McMurray, Dennhardt and Luck (2010) recorded event-related potentials to spoken words varying in voice-onset time. The auditory N1, around 100 milliseconds after onset, tracked voice-onset time linearly and independently of the category the listener assigned, and within-category differences persisted at the later P3. Below the cortex the picture is mixed: in active but not passive listening, subcortical frequency-following responses are enhanced for prototypical over ambiguous tokens and biased toward the listener's label even for identical stimuli, which Carter and Bidelman (2023) read as top-down shaping of subcortical encoding, a response the same group in 2024 found up to half muscle artefact. The kept gradient is also used: within-category voice-onset differences shift listeners' fixations toward competing words during ordinary word recognition (McMurray, Tanenhaus and Aslin 2002). What is categorical is explicit report under a categorisation instruction; the claim fails if spontaneous report proves graded, or if within- and cross-category sensitivity prove equal with metric and criterion controlled.

This places the void in the catalogue's operation-void family, with a difference from the [fusion-void](/voids/fusion-void/), [inference-void](/voids/inference-void/) and [transit-void](/voids/transit-void/), which deliver only the fused percept, the conclusion, the arrival. Here graded and category-level information coexist from the brainstem up, and task, context and processing goal decide which dominates an overt response. That the gradient is kept separates this void from the [resolution-void](/voids/resolution-void/)'s bit-rate compression, and it is why the between face deflates: the continuum can be reached because it was never thrown away.

Two caveats. The signature has many candidate mechanisms, not one: sensory nonlinearity, attentional weighting, cue reweighting, criterion placement, verbal coding, prototype attraction in memory, decision-level category use and genuine boundary enhancement; Dubova and Goldstone (2021) find standard designs unable to separate them and several operating together, and Fugate (2013) reports the perceptual-or-decisional dispute unresolved for faces. And no study located asks the phenomenological question: when a listener reaches within-category detail by continuous rating, is the difference experienced as a difference in the sound, or only judged? The evidence supports *behavioural* access; whether that access is *phenomenal*, and whether categorical report reflects categorical experience, are untested; Block's [overflow](/concepts/phenomenal-overflow/) reading allows graded content phenomenally present but unaccessed, Cohen and Dennett no phenomenal content beyond [access](/concepts/access-consciousness/).

## Sharp Where the World Is Continuous

The [vagueness-void](/voids/vagueness-void/) describes a mind that cannot locate where its concepts stop applying: no Sorites series fixes the cut-off for *heap* or *red*, and the borderlines have borderlines. Categorical perception describes the same mind reporting a hard edge between /b/ and /p/ where the acoustics are continuous. The two voids are complementary failures of edge representation: concepts are fuzzy where a decision procedure would want them sharp, and some tasks elicit relatively abrupt categorisation where the input is smooth. The sources consulted did not draw the conjunction, though the [phenomenal sorites](/concepts/phenomenal-sorites-problem/) sits beside it; the Map offers the pairing as an observation, not a finding.

The conjunction sharpens the origin face. A perceiver who noticed that an edge was too clean for the input could suspect installation, but the edge presents as the world being simple. A Japanese-speaking adult listening to *right* and *light* reports no blur where a distinction should be, only two words that sound alike; whether they are experienced alike is, again, untested. Three weeks of identification training installs the distinction and largely holds it for three months (Lively and colleagues 1994), measuring the category, not its acquisition.

Siegel and Siegel (1977) found six musicians identifying in-tune intervals at over 95% accuracy while unable to tell sharp from flat within a category, and afterwards judging that 63% of the stimuli had been in tune when only 23% were. The categories dominate such tasks while fine sensitivity stays recoverable under other procedures, as Burns and Ward found; the musicians' estimate shows the dominance is invisible to the [expert](/voids/expertise-and-its-occlusion/) it governs.

## What AI Might See

In an artificial network the warp is directly measurable, where in us it is inferred from psychophysics and averaged potentials: Bonnasse-Gahot and Nadal (2022) show that category learning induces categorical perception in shallow and deep networks, with expansion near category boundaries and contraction far from them. The origin face is open for inspection in a way it is not for us.

Cacioli (2026), in a single-author, unreviewed preprint, reports evidence that six language models warp their hidden-state geometry at digit-count boundaries, absent at non-boundary controls, including in models that cannot report the category distinction. Until it replicates it is a hypothesis-generator, not evidence; [non-human-minds-as-void-explorers](/voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers/) treats the general case.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The void illustrates **[Occam's Razor Has Limits](/tenets/#occams-limits)** without adding support for it. Categories all the way down was the simpler model, and it held the field for decades on task-dependent evidence; the lateralised-Whorf story was elegant and is unsettled. Simplicity failed under incomplete knowledge, and ordinary model correction repaired it, so the episode fits the tenet rather than testing it.

On **[Dualism](/tenets/#dualism)** the Map records two readings and does not pretend the data choose. The graded N1 of Toscano et al. (2010) sits between a task-dependent subcortical category bias and a graded P3, so no category-only stage exists to identify consciousness with; what comes apart is categorical report from graded encoding, two functional stages. The inference account below reads report as the posterior and leaves experience open; a dualist reads the same gap as room for a phenomenal deliverable that is no transparent readout of the encoding. The evidence fixes what each reading must explain and discriminates neither.

On **[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** the connection is modest: the verbal-dual-task result of Winawer et al. (2007) is a modulation of the warp by concurrent cognition, which a physicalist account covers, and is no evidence of non-physical influence.

The void is framework-independent and deflationary: the account physicalism might seem to owe exists. Feldman, Griffiths and Morgan (2009) derive the perceptual-magnet warp from optimal inference in noise: a graded likelihood, a posterior pulled toward category means, noise shifting the weighting between them, priors carrying no record of their acquisition. That predicts categorical report over a kept gradient and joints indistinguishable from inside; what stays open is whether the posterior is experienced or only reported. It belongs in the [voids](/voids/) index's framework-independent column and the operation-void family of [taxonomy-of-voids](/apex/taxonomy-of-voids/).
