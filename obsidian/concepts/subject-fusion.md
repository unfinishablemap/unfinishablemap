**Subject fusion** is the case in which two subjects of experience are succeeded by one. Two people's brains or bodies are joined, gradually or all at once, until a single stream of experience carries forward from both. It is the converse of fission, and every camp accepts that description; the dispute is over who, if anyone, survives, and how many subjects exist along the way. It is unrelated to [[fusion-void|the fusion void]], which concerns perceptual cue fusion: the hidden combining of sensory estimates into one percept, with one subject throughout.

The Unfinishable Map's closed individualism fixes the two ends of the case and leaves the middle owed. Its preferred reading of *partial coupling*, where two channels are linked and each continues, is that two subjects remain; this mirrors its reading of split-brain disconnection, in which the channel changes and the subject count does not show. *Complete union* of two substrates ends both subjects and begins a third. That verdict mirrors the Map's fission verdict and rests on the same unproved premise about how subjects pair with substrates. Between the ends the Map predicts a step rather than a slope, never 1.34 subjects, at a point no physical description marks. The sharpest result is that no first-person evidence marks the step either, since the fused subject seems to remember both lives. All of this is framework-internal, and no evidence discriminates it from its rivals. The far end of the gradient also has no human datum at all: what is said about it is said about a thought experiment.

## The Case and Its Gradient {#gradient}

Derek Parfit set out the case in 1971: "Physically, fusion is easy to describe. Two people come together. While they are unconscious, their two bodies grow into one. One person then wakes up." The psychology is harder. "The one person who results from a fusion can, similarly, q-remember living the lives of the two original people. None of their q-memories need be lost. But some things must be lost" (Parfit 1971). A *q-memory* is an apparent memory caused in the right way by a past experience, without presupposing that the rememberer is the one who had it ([[quasi-memory-and-the-circularity-objection|quasi-memory]]).

Parfit's case is complete and instantaneous. Run gradually, it becomes a gradient:

- **Communication.** Brain-to-brain interfaces link two or three people and carry one binary decision at a time. Rao et al. (2014) decoded motor imagery from one person's EEG and delivered it by transcranial magnetic stimulation to a second person's motor cortex, producing a touchpad press: in their words, "a rudimentary form of direct information transmission." BrainNet (Jiang et al. 2019) extended this to three people collaborating on a Tetris-like game. Both are experiments, and both transmit decisions, not experiences.
- **Partial sharing.** Krista and Tatiana Hogan are craniopagus twins, joined at the head with a reported thalamic connection. A peer-reviewed philosophical treatment, Cochrane (2021), records the evidence base frankly: "There has yet to be a thorough first-hand scientific report of their case published in the academic literature." Working from documentaries and journalism, he argues from the locality of content processing for shared consciousness, defined as "a case where neural connection falls short of bringing about either a singular subject of experience or a wholly unified consciousness," such that "there are some single token experiences that both simultaneously possess." The twins were joined from birth, so theirs is a synchronic case of coupling, not a diachronic merger.
- **Complete union.** Only thought experiments reach this end. Luke Roelofs (2019, ch. 8) takes up the gradual version: two people whose brain-interfacing "gradually changed them into a single, integrated, person combining the traits of both," and the question whether that is "the destruction or the survival of the original subjects of experience" (chapter abstract).

No human case approaches the far end. No callosotomy has been reversed to provide the empirical inverse of split-brain surgery, the Hogans are congenital, and the interfaces carry bits.

## Rival Readings of the Outcome {#rivals}

Call the two original subjects A and B, and the subject that wakes up C. The readings below differ on what C is.

**Survival comes in degrees.** Parfit's verdict is that "fusion, while not clearly survival, is not clearly failure to survive, and hence that what matters in survival can have degrees." On his [[parfit-reductionism|reductionism]] there is no further fact to find, and his attitude follows: "If we were about to undergo a fusion of this kind, would we regard it as death? Some of us might. This is less absurd than regarding division as death." The bioethicists Hazem Zohny and Julian Savulescu (2024) work in the same Parfitian idiom. They place cases "along a spectrum from loosely connected pairs to fully merged minds" (abstract) and treat speed as one factor among several. A rapid merger "would represent a kind of death of an existing psychological identity, with replacement by a new merged one". If the merger is gradual, "psychologically the identities composing the merger may persist". At the fully merged end, the pair "are for all intents and purposes an individual patient."

**The count is indeterminate.** Eric Schwitzgebel and Sophie R. Nelson (2026), arguing from naturalistic views of consciousness, hold that "Whole-number-countability might be an accident of typical vertebrate biology" (abstract). They apply this to fusion directly. Two alien brains share a sliver of tissue; "Tiny Overlap gradually becomes Massive Overlap as the aliens' brains slowly fuse," and "In the middle of this process, there would be two partially overlapping fields of experience that would plausibly count as neither determinately one nor two subjects." They put the same question to the Hogans: must every possible pair of craniopagus twins house "either determinately one conscious subject or two?"

**Both persist.** A and B continue, sharing C's experience. The four-dimensionalist device David Lewis (1976) built for fission, two persons sharing stages, can be run forward so that two persons share their later stages. Combinationist views reach a similar picture through overlapping subjects that share token states (see [[manyism]]).

**Replacement.** Neither original persists, and C is new. At the micro level this is panpsychist fusionism, on which micro-subjects cease when they fuse into a macro-subject; the Map reads that as emergence relabelled ([[panpsychisms-combination-problem]]). At the level of persons it is the Map's own verdict at the complete end.

Sam Coleman's (2014) dilemma, as [[apex/mereology-of-mind|the mereology apex]] states it, constrains this list. If A and B combine into C, either A and B cease, in which case nothing combined, or all three coexist, in which case C was added rather than composed. Read across time, the dilemma, if sound, rules out a fifth option: C built from A and B as parts.

## The Map's Reading: Two Fixed Ends and an Unlocated Step {#map-reading}

What follows is the Map's proposal, derived from its [[positions/individuation-and-subjecthood|individuation register]]. None of the authors above holds it.

**The partial end.** The Map reads split-brain surgery as severing a channel "without disclosing a subject-count", since the channel is not the subject (P-CS4). Parity runs the other way too: linking channels does not disclose a count either. The Map's preferred reading of the brain-to-brain pairs and the Hogans is therefore two subjects with coupled channels and some shared content. Like its split-brain reading, this is compatible with the data and not forced by it, and no measure settles it (P-I3). The Map needs only shared *content*, so it can stay neutral on Cochrane's stronger claim of shared *tokens*.

**The complete end.** The Map holds that a perspective is a particular that does not divide, and that in fission no successor is the original (P-I4). Fusion runs the mirror. Identity is transitive, so C cannot be both A and B: that would make A identical to B. At most one original survives. Getting from *at most one* to *none* takes a further premise, namely that a perspective does not survive the union of its substrate with another. This mirrors the fission premise that a perspective does not survive substrate division. Both are claims about subject–substrate pairing, a law the Map owes and does not have ([[positions/subject-census|P-SC2]]). On the Map's reading, then, complete fusion is two endings and one beginning, on the same conditional footing as fission. A pairing law under which one original's pairing extends to the united substrate would narrow the verdict to at most one, just as its fission analogue would.

**The step between.** The [[coupling-engagement-condition#what-could-make-engagement-non-graded|existential reading]] of coupling holds that a subject either is or is not coupled to a system, and "existence does not come in degrees even where everything enabling it is graded." Applied over time, the count changes by a step at some point on the coupling gradient, and it is a whole number at every moment. The Map's commitments fix no particular sequence. The count might go from two to one, or from two to three to one if C begins before A and B end; that second path makes Coleman's coexistence horn a passing episode. On this reading a gradual merger lengthens the approach without smearing the step, so gradualness does not let the original subjects persist, whatever it preserves of the psychological identities Zohny and Savulescu track. Here the Map's prediction and Schwitzgebel and Nelson's diverge on the shape of the count: a step on one side, a stretch of neither-one-nor-two on the other. Because the boundary is unreadable (P-I3), the difference shows up in no observation. It marks a framework boundary rather than a test. The cost is the diachronic form of the one [[phenomenal-sorites-problem#epistemicism|epistemicism about the phenomenal sorites]] accepts: a sharp change at a point nothing physical marks.

**Unreadable from inside as well.** Third-person unreadability is already registered (P-I3). Fusion adds the first person. C q-remembers living both lives. Taken as memories of its own past, that testimony points two ways at once: it says C is A and that C is B, which transitivity forbids. The Map already concedes that memory is only de facto immune to misidentification, so it cannot certify persistence ([[immunity-to-error-through-misidentification]]). A fission product's memories at least testify coherently, though on the Map's verdict mistakenly. A fusion product's memories cannot be read consistently at all. The step itself leaves no trace: A does not experience its ending, and C's experience begins already furnished with two pasts. Nothing available to C, or to anyone observing it, shows whether it is A continued, B continued or new; the Map's verdict comes from theory, conditionally, and not from anything C can find. In the middle of the gradient, no one can tell whether a second subject is still on the other side of the link.

**What remains owed.** Where on the coupling gradient one pairing ends and another begins is a question for the pairing law (P-SC2). This is the same debt fission draws on, now with a named case.

## What Follows, and From What {#implications}

Some conclusions follow from taking coupling cases seriously at all. Counting by bodies fails, since no view surveyed here settles the craniopagus case by counting bodies. The *weighting* question is also separate from the *census* question. Roelofs and Sebo (2024) observe that "If two subjects share very few mental states, then it seems that we should count the value of those states twice, but if they share very many mental states, then it seems that we should count the value of those states once," and propose that welfare-relevant states are holistic. That asks how much a shared state counts. It does not ask how many subjects there are, the distinction [[moral-census-opacity]] draws.

Other conclusions follow only on the Map's reading. Complete fusion is two endings and a beginning: the additive counting of copies ([[positions/ai-consciousness-scope|P-AC3]]) run backwards. That is harsher than Parfit's "less absurd than regarding division as death", and harsher than a gradual merger in which psychological identities persist. Whether a merger ends its originals depends only on whether it reaches the step, and no one can see the step. Zohny and Savulescu's practical verdict that a fully merged pair is "for all intents and purposes an individual patient" is, on this reading, safe only once the step has been passed, which nothing shows. Consent to complete fusion is consent to ending at an unknown point. The precaution over ranges of counts that census opacity counsels extends to any coupling technology designed to approach union.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]].** The Map's indexical objection presupposes a determinate, non-deflationary "I" (P-I2). Parfit's view lets identity branch or fuse, as [[haecceity]] puts it, because it treats identity as pattern, the move that branch-relative indexicality makes for [[many-worlds]]. Fusion is where the Map's determinate subject is most exposed: the "I" cannot find out from inside which subject it is. The Map's answer is that the fact remains determinate while going unread. That answer no more refutes the reductionist than the Map's indexical case refutes the Everettian ([[mad-dog-everettianism]]); both disagreements sit at a framework boundary. The asymmetry with branching holds here too. In branching the Map declines the postulated division. In fusion the union is real, so the Map grants it and both perspectives end.

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]].** On the Map's reading, consciousness is not built from parts, so C cannot be composed of A and B; Coleman's dilemma argues the point for subjects. Fusion of persons is therefore not combination, and the pairing premise is what makes it replacement.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]].** Once conscious states condition physical outcomes, the subject count is part of the state description ([[positions/subject-census|P-SC1]]). At the step, [[census-change-points|the selection law's arity]], its subject count, changes at a point the law cannot locate.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]].** The graded picture is simpler: no jump, no unmarked threshold. The tenet only bars simplicity from deciding; the step rests on the Map's commitment to determinate subjects, not on evidence, and the Map books its cost: a sharp boundary that nothing reads.
