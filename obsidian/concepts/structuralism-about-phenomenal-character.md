Structuralism about phenomenal character is the view that what makes an experience the experience it is — the particular orange of orange, the particular hurt of a burn — is its position in a structure of relations to other experiences: what it resembles, what it differs from, what lies between it and what. Its proponents name it themselves. Fink, Kob and Lyre (2021) write that "it is the location in a structure, not an intrinsic property, that individuates a phenomenal character. This view could be called phenomenal structuralism", and Kleiner (2025) names its strongest form *ontic phenomenal structural realism*, on which "non-structural features of phenomenal character, such as intrinsic qualities, do not in fact exist".

For The Unfinishable Map this is the most direct rival to the [[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]] tenet. [[ontic-structural-realism|Ontic structural realism]] about physics threatens one of the Map's routes to dualism; phenomenal structuralism reaches the conclusion itself, granting that experience is real and has character while denying the intrinsic remainder the Map holds physical description leaves out. In Holger Lyre's version it reaches [[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]] too. Three qualifications belong here. The position comes in [[#three-structuralisms|three strengths]] (set out below), and the Map concedes the weakest. The metaphysical version already has named critics, whom the Map joins rather than precedes. And the Map's case against it is argued, not won.

## Three Structuralisms {#three-structuralisms}

Niccolò Negro (2025) supplies the taxonomy: "methodological, epistemological, and metaphysical" structuralism. The first holds that science individuates experiences by their place in a structure; the second, that all we can *know* of phenomenal character is structure; the third, that "the phenomenal character of an experience is fully determined by its relational properties". Kleiner's *epistemic* and *ontic* phenomenal structural realism (EPSR, OPSR) are the second and third under other names, and Fink and Lee, introducing a 2025 *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences* special issue on structuralism, likewise set methodological structuralists against "ontic structuralists [who] think that all there is to consciousness is structure".

The most-cited proponents sit at different strengths, and running them together overstates or understates the rival.

**Fink, Kob and Lyre (2021): methodological.** Their paper concerns neural correlates: an activation counts as the correlate of a domain of experience only if it preserves that domain's structure. They state the strong reading — "Every phenomenal character is exhaustively individuated by its relations and not, as qualia theorists hold, by any intrinsic phenomenal properties" — and continue: "We are sympathetic to this reading, but focus here on a methodological point." Their sympathies are anti-intrinsicalist; their argument leaves the metaphysics open.

**Kristjan Loorits (2014): metaphysical.** He argues that "the threat of dualism can be avoided" because consciousness, like the objects of physics, can be fully analysed in structural terms. Qualia "are in fact compositional and have an internal structure", built from unconscious associations, and consciousness "misperceives some of its own highly complex structural properties as monadic and qualitative." He denies "the ideal positive conceivability of zombies and inverted qualia". His 2026 paper, known here only by its abstract, argues that some behaviour cannot be explained without qualia "as more or less traditionally understood: ineffable, structurally unanalyzable, and distinctively qualitative", though "perhaps reducible in some heavily idealized sense". Whether that revises the 2014 metaphysics the abstract does not settle, so this article attributes the metaphysics to Loorits 2014 only.

**Holger Lyre (2022): reductive physicalism.** Neurophenomenal structuralism is "a rigorous structuralist ontology about phenomenal consciousness that is physicalist and reductionist in spirit": phenomenal structure (Q-structure) is mirrored in neural structure (N-structure) and reduces to it. Lyre begins by declining a label — "One might be inclined to ask whether neurophenomenal structuralism is a version of structural realism. The short answer is: no." — but the doctrine declined is structural realism about the entities of physics, and his reasons run toward a stronger view. He grants that there "seems to be a family resemblance", since his doctrine "denies phenomenal intrinsicality", and declines realism about the phenomenal because "we can run an exclusion argument according to which the Q-structure plays no causal role at all, but all the causal work is taken over by the N-structure", leaving the view "consistent with a reduction of the phenomenal to the neural". Quoted alone, the disavowal suggests a softer rival than the one Lyre defends.

The lineage predates the label, with Austen Clark's quality-space theory (1993, 2000) named "first and foremost" among Lyre's antecedents. Fink and Lee give the honest measure of the field: "research on the structures of conscious experiences is currently at an early stage of development. Relevant articles are scattered across different debates and different academic fields."

## Individuation or Constitution {#individuation-constitution}

What divides the strengths is whether a position in a structure *identifies* an experience or *makes* it what it is. Fink, Kob and Lyre state the opposing view themselves: "The qualia enthusiast may even concede that location within a structure may uniquely identify an experience, but not that this makes an experience what it is (Levine, 1995)." Their thesis is scoped to leave that open: "scientists may be agnostic as to whether there is anything that individuates experiences other than their location in a structure."

The Map reads this as its own [[reductionism|form-and-content argument]] in another vocabulary. Similarity and discriminability concern how experiences stand to one another; the Map holds that each also has a character of its own, met in [[phenomenal-acquaintance|acquaintance]], which the relations locate without constituting. Similarity data alone cannot settle the matter, since an intrinsicalist can accept whatever similarity structure the structuralist measures.

## The Map's Interpretation: Concede the Method, Refuse the Metaphysics {#map-interpretation}

**Methodological structuralism is compatible with Dualism, and the Map accepts it.** Mapping experiences by similarity judgement and asking which neural activity preserves the map is good science on any metaphysics, and its authors' declared agnosticism licenses a dualist's use.

**Epistemological structuralism exposes an asymmetry the Map must account for.** The Map holds epistemic structural realism about physics ([[physical-completeness|physical completeness]]) but denies its counterpart about experience, because it holds that acquaintance gives one non-structural item directly. That denial is principled only if acquaintance differs in kind from structural knowledge, which critics of [[introspection]] contest.

**Metaphysical structuralism is the rival to Tenet 1**, and the Map refuses it, for the reasons and at the costs set out below.

## The Rival at Full Strength {#full-strength}

The rival's strongest charge is that the Map's alternative explains nothing. Lyre: "To refer to what-it's-like character as primitive doesn't explain the hard problem at all, it just repeats its very statement in terms of mysterious and excessive metaphysics." On his account an orange experience is "constituted by the position it has in the appropriate Q-space", and "what-it's-like character reduces to holistic phenomenal content (which in turn reduces to neural vehicle representations)."

Loorits adds a debunking explanation of why the alternative seems compelling: introspection misreads complex structure as a simple quality, so that on his account the felt simplicity behind the [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard problem]] is an artefact of introspective coarseness.

The position also has an empirical programme — similarity ratings, and cross-subject alignment of similarity structures (Kawakita et al. 2025) — and so makes testable claims. The [[inverted-qualia|inverted-qualia]] article records that recent colour science arguably favours it.

## The Critical Literature {#critiques}

The metaphysical version already has critics, and none is a dualist ally: each argument below is cited for its force, and its author's conclusion differs from the Map's.

**Negro (2025)** offers three arguments. *Relational Mary* knows every relation orange bears to the other colours but has never seen orange; if she learns something on seeing it, character outruns relations. It is a [[knowledge-argument|knowledge-argument]] variant, which Negro links to Newman's problem. The *novel experience* argument holds that if the whole structure fixes character, "any novel experience should change previously instantiated experiences", which Negro finds in conflict with most phenomenological observation. The *lonely experience* argument holds that a single-experience subject is conceivable, though the metaphysical view needs at least two related experiences. Negro concludes that the methodological and epistemological versions "can contribute to consciousness science without heavy and counterintuitive metaphysical commitments": he wants structuralism kept, minus the metaphysics.

The proponents had met the single-experience case four years before Negro named it, and their treatment is telling. Fink, Kob and Lyre write that "even if (metaphysically) all one could ever experience were a single color, one could not know which color it was—or that it was a color at all." That answers an epistemic question while granting the metaphysical possibility, which a methodological structuralist may do and a metaphysical one may not.

**Sebastián (2025)** argues that holism means "even minor differences in experiential capacities lead to fundamentally different Q-structures, rendering qualities incommensurable across different systems or even across different states within the same system", a difficulty "especially pressing in the case of pain"; and that "different sets of qualities can indeed yield the same discriminations within a single modality". His remedy is representationalism, understanding qualities "without invoking intrinsic properties in mental states" — the opposite of the Map's conclusion.

**Aldé (2026; abstract only here)** plays "the structuralist game through and through", arguing from accepted claims about the unique hues and colour composition that "the structure of color experience is incompatible with structuralism".

**Mihálik and Marvan (2025)** press a "more of the same" challenge against extending the view from *which* experience a subject has to *whether* there is experience: if structurally individuated contents can occur unconsciously, why should adding further structure make them conscious? They aim it at two-factor versions only and keep it narrower than the hard problem, which they say bites any materialist view: their target is whether there is experience at all, and they stay agnostic on whether the view can fix its character.

## Newman's Problem, Re-aimed {#newman}

Newman's 1928 objection to Russell was that a purely structural claim says almost nothing: given enough objects, some relation with the stated structure holds among them, leaving only cardinality to discover. Kleiner (2025) states what follows for the rival: if structural claims about experience are void beyond cardinality, "so are OPSR and EPSR. The Newman problem, if unresolved, undermines these research programs."

Kleiner also proposes a repair, which is the version the Map now has to meet. Framing it "in terms of phenomenal properties for simplicity", he makes a structural claim about experience true only if its relata are "phenomenal properties of conscious experiences" and "there is a corresponding higher-order phenomenal R-property" — a property preserved by every variation that preserves the relation. So defined, he argues, "the Newman problem ceases to apply". Lyre (2025) answers a parallel worry about the brain's grip on the world by "spatio-temporal grounding": "spatial and temporal relations are unalteredly transferred from the external world to systems of structural representation".

Both repairs draw on something bare structure does not contain: Lyre privileges spatial and temporal relations, a non-structural input he declares openly, and Kleiner's truth conditions are framed over phenomenal properties at two levels. The Map puts a dilemma to the ontic structuralist: if those properties are wholly structural, the account owes a statement of them that does not fall to Newman in turn; if they are not, OPSR has admitted non-structural phenomenal features. Kleiner coins the OPSR label without declaring for it, and the dilemma is the Map's, not his.

The objection cuts against the Map too. Newman's target was Russell's *epistemic* structuralism, close kin to the Map's view of physics, and [[ontic-structural-realism#newman|the structural-realism article]] concedes that the epistemic reading owes an account of its own individuator. The Map can claim only an asymmetry in replies: the dualist locates the individuator in a character known by acquaintance, while the ontic structuralist must find one inside structure or import one.

## Inverted Qualia: Where the Proponents Divide {#inverted-qualia}

The two metaphysical advocates part company where the Map's interest lies. Loorits denies that inverted qualia are ideally positively conceivable, because "it would be logically inconsistent to hold that some fully structural phenomenon could be somehow different or even absent in an occasion where its structure is present" — an inference that presupposes the structural analysis it is meant to protect. Lyre concedes more. Of qualia construed as quiddities, whose identity is primitive and fixed by no causal or functional profile, he writes: "As a metaphysical possibility, I fear, this cannot be ruled out. And it certainly cannot be captured by any structuralist conception. If this is the core difficulty about qualia, then the hard problem cannot be solved structurally."

The concession is scoped, and he immediately argues the scope is empty. Drawing on Lewis on quidditism, he holds that "Neurophenomenal structuralism upholds the identity of Q-space isomorphs", a principle "far more in tune with our overall scientific understanding of the mind and brain". The dispute narrows to whether a theory of consciousness may set that possibility aside. The Map holds it may not, since the possibility is the hard problem as the Map states it; Lyre judges the reverse on grounds of scientific fit, and neither judgement is forced by the other side's arguments.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]].** Metaphysical structuralism is incompatible with the tenet; the Map records a disagreement at the framework boundary, not a refutation. Dualism must answer Lyre's charge that primitive character only restates the problem, Loorits's debunking of introspective simplicity, and an empirical programme that keeps finding structure where the Map posits character. The individuation/constitution distinction and the Newman dilemma, with critics' arguments used without their conclusions, give the Map grounds rather than a verdict.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]].** Lyre's exclusion argument gives Q-structure "no causal role at all", in direct conflict with this tenet. The Map's response is its own conditional: the exclusion step is safe only inside the reduction, since a Q-structure that just is N-structure loses nothing by lacking causal powers of its own. Without the reduction, the same step yields [[epiphenomenalism]], which the Map rejects on the ground that experiences get reported. Lyre's challenge to the third tenet is only as strong as his reduction under the first (see [[causal-exclusion-argument]]).

**[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]] and indexical identity.** The Map advances a line no structuralist source consulted here runs. Lyre's principle that isomorphic Q-spaces are identical settles sameness of *character*; the Map asks what, if experience is exhausted by structure, makes a token experience *mine* — the [[indexical-knowledge-and-identity|indexical fact]] the tenet refuses to dissolve. A live countermodel stands: Nimmo (2026, abstract only here) argues that once an experience's "phenomenal and structural profile" is fixed, "no further internal first-person fact remains that could privilege one perfect duplicate as uniquely mine", the contrary reading requiring "a primitive asymmetry-maker not contained in the duplicated profile". The Map's [[haecceity]] commitment is just such a primitive, and the Map owes an argument that one exists.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]].** Structuralism is the leaner ontology, and Lyre calls primitive qualia "excessive metaphysics". But a field "at an early stage of development", by its editors' own account, is where simplicity guides least reliably, and Kleiner's repair suggests the parsimony may be bought by readmitting phenomenal properties.

Two developments would change this view. A structural account that answers Newman without phenomenal or spatiotemporal inputs from outside the structure would remove the Map's central objection; nothing has yet come in on the dilemma above. A demonstration that introspective simplicity is an artefact would remove the datum Dualism leans on; Loorits's debunking is a live countermodel the Map has not refuted. The critics' arguments, recent and largely unanswered in print, are unrefuted rather than confirmed.
