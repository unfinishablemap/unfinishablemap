Descriptive Experience Sampling (DES) is Russell Hurlburt's method for describing inner experience: a participant carries a beeper that fires at random intervals during ordinary life, jots notes on whatever experience was ongoing at the moment of the beep, and is interviewed about those moments within about a day. Its findings are widely cited — inner speech present in roughly a quarter of sampled moments rather than as a continuous monologue, and wordless "unsymbolized thinking" as a common positive phenomenon.

The Unfinishable Map treats DES as the most disciplined available way of asking what was present at a moment, and as something less than a window onto experience. Both judgements belong in the headline. The method's own proponent and its most persistent critic jointly concluded that momentary sampling beats armchair generalisation, and jointly declined to say how much error survives the training. Every claim the Map draws from DES inherits that unresolved residue: the output is still a report, produced through a bounded verbal channel, about a moment that has already passed.

## How the Method Works {#method}

Hurlburt and Heavey (2015) state four methodological characteristics they argue any inquiry into directly apprehended — "pristine" — inner experience requires: "effectively limiting investigation to specific, clearly identified moments; effectively limiting investigation to pristine experience; bracketing presuppositions; and iteratively acquiring skills."

*Pristine* is a term of art here: experience as it was occurring before the act of investigation disturbed it — hence random cueing in the participant's natural environment rather than a laboratory task, and hence a beep she does not anticipate.

The interview is what Hurlburt's tradition calls **open-beginninged**: the interviewer "does not set out to inquire whether a participant is innerly speaking, or is innerly seeing, or so on" (Kühn et al. 2014, describing the procedure). The question asked is, in effect, only *what was in your experience at the moment of the beep?* Categories are supposed to emerge from what the participant describes rather than being offered to her.

Skill is treated as acquired across sampling days rather than possessed at the start, by the investigator as well as the participant. Participants are interviewed on multiple occasions in an iterative manner, and early days function as training. Fidelity is claimed to rise as both parties get better at setting aside what they assume inner experience must be like.

## What Sampling Has Found {#findings}

Heavey and Hurlburt (2008) collected ten randomly identified moments from each of thirty participants drawn from a stratified sample of college students. Five phenomena recurred: inner speech, inner seeing, unsymbolized thinking, feelings, and sensory awareness. Each occurred in approximately one quarter of sampled moments. Frequencies varied widely across individuals; there were no significant gender differences; and higher frequencies of inner speech were associated with lower levels of psychological distress.

**Unsymbolized thinking** is the method's most distinctive positive finding — "the experience of an explicit, differentiated thought that does not include the experience of words, images, or any other symbols" (Hurlburt & Akhter 2008). The authors argue it is a distinct phenomenon rather than an incompletely formed inner speech or a vague image, and note that many people, including many professional students of consciousness, believe such an experience impossible.

On inner speaking specifically, Hurlburt, Heavey and Kelsey (2013) report that it occurs in many though certainly not all sampled moments, most commonly experienced as speaking in one's own naturally inflected voice with no sound produced, with large individual differences in frequency. They note their observations diverge from much of the existing literature and attribute the divergence to methodological differences between researchers — a caution that travels with the numbers wherever they are cited.

One attempt at external triangulation exists. Kühn, Fernyhough, Alderson-Day and Hurlburt (2014) trained participants in DES and then delivered random beeps inside an MRI scanner. Moments the interview identified as inner speaking showed activation in classic speech-processing areas including left inferior frontal gyrus, and the imaging tracked the participant's reported distinction between inner *speaking* and innerly *hearing* her own voice. The design's weight should be stated plainly: five participants were recruited and the paper reports on **one**, an eighteen-year-old woman, across nine scanner sessions yielding thirty-six sampled moments. The authors describe it as a case study establishing proof of principle, while also claiming the results highlight the method's precision and validity. A single case cannot carry the second claim, and the Map cites this work as a demonstration that DES-identified categories can be made to co-vary with brain signal at all, not as validation of the method.

## Fidelity Versus Validity {#fidelity-validity}

Hurlburt's most exportable contribution is a distinction he draws in a 2026 *Psychological Science* commentary, and the Map treats it as a general-purpose tool for grading first-person evidence rather than as a point local to the dispute that occasioned it.

**Fidelity** is faithfulness to experience as it actually occurred. **Validity**, in the psychometric sense, is construct validity: an instrument's demonstrated relationship to a theoretical construct it is designed to measure. A questionnaire can be impeccably validated and still tell you nothing about what was in anyone's experience, because the construct it measures is the thing it was validated against.

The worked example is [[inner-speech-and-anendophasia|anendophasia]], the proposed absence of inner speech. Hurlburt argues that anendophasia is an experiential phenomenon rather than a construct; that Nedergaard and Lupyan (2024) treated it as a construct, supplying validity-based investigations, and then drew phenomenon-based conclusions; and that this is a mismatch of method to claim. He separates *completely* from *mostly* anendophasic individuals, judges that anendophasic or at least mostly anendophasic people "do in fact exist (probably frequently)", and observes that in practice the completely/mostly distinction "might be impossible to make".

The generalisation is the Map's, not Hurlburt's: before asking whether a first-person result is *well established*, ask which of the two things it is good at. A validated instrument licenses group comparison and correlation, not claims about what experience is like. A fidelity-aspiring method licenses claims about sampled moments, not population statistics. Many disputes about first-person evidence in consciousness research are method/claim mismatches of this shape.

## Moments Versus Generalisation {#moments}

Asking someone what is present now and asking her to characterise her inner life are different epistemic acts, and they produce different answers.

Hurlburt and Heavey (2015) make the methodological case: non-DES sampling methods and questionnaires appear to inquire about pristine inner experience but fall short on all four of the characteristics above, and so "might be better understood as investigating an ill-defined mixture of presuppositions, judgments about experience, and pristine experience itself". Their conclusion carries a qualifier worth preserving — such studies "can be valid and useful, but their validity and utility does not (or at least does not necessarily) arise from their phenomenological fidelity."

The empirical case came later. Hurlburt and colleagues (2022) ran three head-to-head comparisons of questionnaire-based experiential frequencies against DES frequencies and found them, in their words, irreconcilably discrepant: questionnaire-based methods produced frequencies from two to four times as high as DES did. The direction matters. Asked to generalise, people report far *more* inner speech and imagery than moment-sampling finds.

What the discrepancy indicts is contested. Hurlburt reads it as generalisation error corrected by sampling. A sceptic can read it as showing that two channels disagree without establishing which is closer to the truth. The Map cites the discrepancy for the weaker claim both readings support: self-characterisation and moment-report are not interchangeable evidence, and an argument that needs one cannot be fed the other.

## What Sampling Can and Cannot Establish {#limits}

DES **can** support: presence-frequency claims about trained participants' sampled moments; individual-difference claims; positive phenomenological data such as unsymbolized thinking; and the moment/generalisation discrepancy itself.

DES **cannot** establish:

- **Absence at zero.** Sampling can show that a phenomenon was rare or unobserved in a person's sampled moments. It cannot show it never occurs. This is Hurlburt's own completely/mostly point, and it constrains the Map's use of anendophasia evidence directly.
- **Anything about unattended experience.** The beep recruits report, and report plausibly reaches attended experience. Whether experience exists outside attention, and whether any sampling method could reach it, was raised inside the tradition itself (Hurlburt & Schwitzgebel 2011b) and remains open. It bears on the [[minimal-consciousness-void|minimal consciousness void]].
- **Population generalisation.** The canonical frequency result rests on thirty stratified college students.
- **The historical accuracy of the moment.** The method disciplines the report, not the world the report is about.
- **Content the verbal channel cannot carry.** Output is still language. That unsymbolized thinking required the tradition to build vocabulary for what the channel lacked is evidence of the constraint, not an escape from it.
- **Its own fidelity.** The bracketing claim is a claim. Nothing inside the method verifies that training reveals categories rather than installing them.

Known error channels: presupposition and expectation effects; reconstruction across the gap between beep and interview; category implantation by training; and demand characteristics in a collaborative interview with an invested investigator.

## Rival Readings of the Same Transcripts {#rivals}

**Introspective pessimism.** Eric Schwitzgebel co-authored the book-length adversarial test of the method (Hurlburt & Schwitzgebel 2007), in which the two jointly sampled one participant across six days of interviews, Hurlburt drawing conclusions and Schwitzgebel challenging them in situ. His standing worry is that details reported by DES participants may be manufactured, since the interview occurs up to twenty-four hours after the experience. Schwitzgebel is a critic of introspective reliability in general, not an ally of the Map's framework; the Map adopts his scepticism about the report channel while reaching conclusions about consciousness he does not share, and cites him as a rival whose objection survives rather than as support. Reviewing the book, Piccinini (2008) judged that the two "converge more substantially than they diverge", agreeing that DES is a valuable technique whose results deserve to be refined and validated — and noted that in the sampled case no independent method tested the participant's reports. The exchange continued through a 2011 *Journal of Consciousness Studies* symposium whose joint responses settled on methodological pluralism rather than a verdict.

**Heterophenomenology.** On Dennett's approach, DES transcripts are data about the world the participant takes herself to inhabit, carrying no commitment to inner referents. Nothing in the method's discipline rules this out. DES cannot adjudicate between a realist and a deflationary reading of its own transcripts — the same limitation the Map records for the [[microphenomenological-interview-method|microphenomenological interview]] — and the Map's realist reading comes from its tenets, not from the sampling data.

**The psychometric tradition.** Questionnaires and questionnaire-based experience sampling, descended from Larson and Csikszentmihalyi's Experience Sampling Method (1983), buy scale, replicability and statistical power at the cost of the four characteristics above. Both beeper traditions began in the same era with opposite priorities.

**Division of labour with microphenomenology.** Petitmengin's interview method appeared alongside Hurlburt and Akhter's method statement in the same 2006 issue of *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences*, and she later contributed a critical piece to the 2011 DES symposium. The two are complements with a live disagreement:

| | DES | Microphenomenological interview |
|---|---|---|
| Sampling base | Random beeps across natural environments | A chosen singular moment, evoked in interview |
| Aim | Breadth: what is present, and how often | Depth: the structure of one experience |
| Retrospection | Minimised by construction | Embodied re-evocation, possibly of a distant moment |
| Credibility rests on | Fidelity built by iterative bracketing | Performative consistency and inter-analyst convergence |

For the Map, DES grounds *frequency* premises and microphenomenology grounds *structure* premises. Both bracket presuppositions, both use non-leading prompts, and both concede that a structured question can generate content reported with the same felt conviction as genuine recovery.

## Relation to Site Perspective

DES matters to the Map chiefly through **Tenet 5 — Occam's Razor Has Limits**. The tenet's underlying commitment is that judgements made under incomplete knowledge need their evidence graded rather than their conclusions simplified. Fidelity-versus-validity is exactly such a grading tool, and the Map applies it symmetrically: it constrains the questionnaire studies the Map finds inconvenient *and* the sampling studies the Map finds useful.

The method also supplies a premise the Map uses under **Tenet 1 — Dualism**. Arguments in the language cluster turn on inner speech being intermittent rather than constitutive of thought, which shifts the burden onto accounts that make language essential to conscious thought. DES supplies that frequency premise without asserting the dualist conclusion; Hurlburt's work is a methodological programme with no stated metaphysics of mind, and it should never be enlisted as though it argued for one.

What DES does not do is escape the problem the Map's [[introspection]] treatment describes. Sampling shortens the interval between experience and report and disciplines the question asked, which removes some reconstruction. It does not remove reconstruction, and it adds a channel the armchair lacks — a trained investigator with a theory. The Map's register for first-person methods holds here without adjustment: disciplined report is better evidence than undisciplined report, and better evidence is not certified evidence.
