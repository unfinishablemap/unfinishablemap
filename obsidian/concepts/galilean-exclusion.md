The Galilean exclusion is the move, made systematic in one influential reconstruction of modern science, by which mathematical physics excluded sensible qualities from its explanatory primitives. Galileo's distinction between primary qualities (shape, size, motion, number — properties he could not conceive a body without, and which mathematics describes) and secondary qualities (colour, taste, warmth, sound-as-experienced) kept the first as the vocabulary of physics and relocated the second into the perceiving animal, where they were to be explained causally by the first. The passage was incidental to Galileo's own programme (Miller, 2025); Husserl and Whitehead later read it as founding. The reconstruction is interpretive, but the exclusion it names is real: one condition of modern science's success, it shaped the modern form of the [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard problem of consciousness]] without supplying the problem's force. The genealogy is compatible with the Map's dualism and does not independently establish it; the authors who built it reject that conclusion, and the [objections below](#what-would-challenge-this-view) say what it leaves open.

## The Distinction and Its Logic

In *The Assayer* (1623), Galileo argued that heat, colour and taste do not belong to external objects but arise from their interaction with perceiving subjects: a feather tickles, but ticklishness is not in the feather.

The vocabulary matters. In §48 of *Il Saggiatore* Galileo writes that he cannot conceive a corporeal substance without its being bounded, shaped, placed, moving or at rest, one or many — but feels no such necessity to conceive it as white or red, bitter or sweet, sonorous or mute. Hence, he says, "vo io pensando" — "I am inclined to think" — that tastes, odours and colours, on the side of the object in which they seem to reside, are "puri nomi" (mere names) and have their residence only in the "corpo sensitivo" (the sentient body), so that if the animal is removed they are "levate ed annichilate" (removed and annihilated); and we should not believe them truly and really distinct from the other "primi e reali accidenti" (first and real accidents) merely because we have given them different names (Galilei, 1623/1957, p. 274; *Opere* VI, 347–350). Drake's translation renders "corpo sensitivo" as "consciousness"; the Italian says *body*, and Finocchiaro's "sensitive body" is closer. Two features carry weight below: the passage is voiced tentatively, and it is ontological — the qualities are annihilated when the animal goes, and are contrasted with accidents that are real. Galileo is saying where the qualities are, not merely which variables physics should model.

What remains when the relocated qualities are set aside is the mathematical skeleton — particles in motion, describable by geometry and number — and Galileo's insight was that this skeleton is sufficient for physics: you do not need to know what red looks like to predict a wavelength. The relocation was also a research programme rather than a dismissal: the qualities now placed in the sentient body were to be explained by the motions and contacts of the primary ones, a programme Piccolino and Wade (2008) read as anticipating Helmholtz and present-day sensory neuroscience.

Descartes gave a related division a metaphysical architecture: *res extensa* (extended, measurable substance) against *res cogitans* (thinking substance), with experience outside physical description as a matter of ontology. Cartesian dualism had its own roots and is not derivative of Galileo, but it supplied the two-substance framework within which the relocated qualities became a domain of their own. (The terms "primary" and "secondary" qualities are Boyle's, made canonical by Locke.) Galileo's own claim was thus already ontological rather than a methodological convenience — he *relocates* the qualities rather than merely omitting them — but it was a speculative posit, made in passing within an atomist account of heat, and it was Descartes who turned the relocation into a system. The [[primary-secondary-quality-boundary|boundary the exclusion presupposes]] is itself unstable — it admits no clean, non-circular criterion — which, on the Map's synthesis rather than any external finding, is the deeper reason the relocation never amounted to a discovery about qualities.

## Why the Exclusion Worked

The exclusion was spectacularly productive. By restricting attention to observer-independent, quantifiable properties, physics achieved a universality no prior knowledge system had managed, and chemistry, biology and eventually neuroscience adopted the template: describe the world in third-person terms, eliminate subjective variation, seek laws that hold regardless of who observes. Setting aside the variation between observers bought precision, repeatability and cumulative progress, and each triumph confirmed the approach, until what Galileo had advanced as a tentative posit hardened into a default metaphysics, assumed rather than argued: the mathematically describable properties are real, the relocated qualities derivative, reducible or illusory.

## The Problem When Consciousness Is the Target

The exclusion generates a distinctive difficulty when science turns to consciousness itself. Two things need separating. Galileo's secondary qualities were attributes ascribed to objects — the warmth of the fire — which he denied were in the objects at all. [[qualia|Qualia]] — the redness of red, the painfulness of pain — are properties of the experience itself, the character of seeing or hurting rather than a feature of the thing seen. The relocation connects them: qualia are what the exclusion deposits in the perceiver, the residue the primary-quality programme was to explain, and what consciousness *consists of*. Asking a method whose primitives were chosen to leave that residue out to explain it creates a mismatch between method and target.

A natural objection: cognitive science has moved beyond Galileo, since neuroscientists incorporate subjective reports as data and [[concepts/functionalism]] maps mental states to functional roles rather than ignoring them. Two things are run together there. Functionalism is a metaphysical thesis about what mental states *are* — occupants of roles in a causal economy — and the genealogy bears on it, if at all, only through the conditional stated next; the case against it belongs to [[concepts/functionalism|its own article]], not to history. The evidential policy is separate: consciousness science mostly admits experience through reports — verbal behaviour, button presses, ratings — and a functionalist, an identity theorist and a dualist could each adopt that policy or depart from it, as neurophenomenology's first-person channel does. What survives is narrower: where the tools of description are observer-independent and the phenomenon is not, the distance between report and reported is where the dispute lives.

The claim is best put as a conditional rather than an image. Galilean explanation is structural-functional: it accounts for a phenomenon by specifying the arrangement, dynamics and functional roles of its parts. If phenomenal character is not exhausted by structure and function, then an explanatory form confined to them is incomplete for consciousness. The first premise is history. The second is the central disputed proposition of the philosophy of mind — the Map defends it in [[arguments-against-materialism|the arguments against materialism]] and [[philosophical-zombies|the zombie literature]]; physicalists of most kinds deny it — and the history does not establish it. What the genealogy establishes is narrower and still worth having: it blocks the inference from the method's success to the completeness of its ontology, since a method whose primitives were chosen to exclude quality cannot count its silence about quality as evidence that there is none. It does not establish that no expanded science, with different primitives or a disciplined first-person channel, could explain experience; whether one can is what [[neurophenomenology-and-contemplative-neuroscience|neurophenomenology]] and the Map's interface programme are testing. Whether the obstacle is peculiar to consciousness is a further question: the Map's [[emergence-as-universal-hard-problem|universalist reading of the hard problem]] argues that a remainder of the same shape survives every level transition, so that consciousness is where the residue becomes impossible to overlook rather than the only place it arises.

The [[explanatory-gap]] between neural description and felt experience gains sharper diagnosis in this light: more than an empirical problem awaiting research, it reflects a framework of explanation assembled from primitives chosen to leave out the phenomenon now under investigation. Husserl made this argument in *The Crisis of European Sciences* (1936, §9), naming Galileo's "mathematization of nature" — mathematical idealisations substituted for the concrete world of lived experience — and arguing (§9h–i) that the life-world from which the idealisations were built becomes their forgotten meaning-fundament. Husserl's own conclusion runs the other way from the Map's: his remedy is transcendental phenomenology, his subject is the life-world as ground of meaning rather than any neural gap, and a psychophysical dualism of mind set over against mathematised nature is for him a product of the Galilean paradigm, not its correction. Thompson builds on Husserl's critique of objectivism in *Mind in Life* (2007), and with Frank and Gleiser (2024) argues that modern science has come to treat mathematical-physical description as if it exhausted what is objectively real (a paraphrase of a theme running through both books, not a quotation; the Galileo-specific framing is chiefly the later book's). Everything excluded from that description — including experience — then becomes either reducible to physics or scientifically invisible. The *Blind Spot* authors reject the Map's conclusion too: they place naturalistic dualism and panpsychism — "injecting a dualist or panpsychist 'extra ingredient' into an otherwise unchanged and preserved blind-spot worldview" — and illusionism alike within the ambit of the Blind Spot (Frank, Gleiser & Thompson, 2024, p. 196). Their target is the metaphysics reified onto scientific abstraction, not scientific method, which their constructive programme presupposes can be reformed to keep experience in view; their non-dualism is a rival to the Map's reading, not a witness for it.

Whitehead identified the same structural problem from a different angle, naming it the "bifurcation of nature" in *The Concept of Nature* (1920, ch. II, "Theories of the Bifurcation of Nature") — the splitting of reality into the scientifically respectable (mathematical structure) and the dismissed (experienced quality), which he took for an artefact of the Galilean method rather than a discovery, and which [[process-philosophy]] was designed to overcome. His remedy is the opposite of a dualism: he refuses "any theory of psychic additions to the object known in perception" — greenness furnished by the perceiving mind — because "all our sense-perceptions are in the same boat, and must be treated on the same principle" (Whitehead, 1920, ch. II). A view that relocates quality into a mind is, in his terms, a bifurcation theory; the Map cites him for the diagnosis and counts his process monism among the rivals to its cure.

## Not a Discovery but a Posit

Galileo did not *discover* that secondary qualities are unreal, and he did not merely *decide* to leave them out of physics either. He asserted — tentatively, in the vocabulary of accidents — that they are mere names on the side of the object and real only in the sentient body. What warranted the assertion was not evidence about qualities but the success of a mathematical programme that had no use for them, and that success is what the posit has traded on ever since. The programme's productivity shows that mathematical physics can describe physical structure while setting consciousness aside, not that consciousness is nothing beyond physical structure.

The slide from the method's silence to metaphysical absence underlies much of the consciousness debate. When materialists argue that everything real is physical, they often rely implicitly on the exclusion: if physics has never needed subjective qualities, they must not be fundamental. The reasoning is circular. Physics never needed them because its primitives were chosen to do without them; their absence from physical description reflects that choice, not an empirical finding.

This point cuts more than one way. It tells the materialist that the absence of consciousness from physics does not prove consciousness is physical; the dualist, symmetrically, that the method's failure to capture qualities is no evidence *for* their non-physicality, since a method built to leave quality out would fail whether or not quality is physical; and the mysterian that the difficulty may reflect the method's structure rather than any intrinsic ineffability.

A third position accepts the exclusion and denies there was ever a residue to exclude: [[ontic-structural-realism|ontic structural realism]] about physics is largely silent on consciousness, and it is the separate thesis of phenomenal structuralism that extends it to experience, so that Galileo's *relocated* residue is *abolished*. The circularity charge above does not reach a position that denies the remainder was ever a datum; the Map's pressure on it is Newman's problem, which is pressure rather than refutation and, as [[ontic-structural-realism#newman|the structural-realism article]] concedes, cuts both ways.

## The Exclusion in Contemporary Debates

The [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard problem]] takes its modern form from the exclusion, though not its force. David Chalmers's formulation — why is physical processing accompanied by subjective experience? — rests on a conceptual and modal claim: physical description is structural and dynamical in principle, and no description of structure and dynamics entails phenomenal character. That claim would survive any history; Chalmers's premise gives the genealogy its bite, not the reverse. Chalmers himself is a naturalistic property dualist whose position in *The Conscious Mind* leans toward epiphenomenalism and treats interactionism warily; he is cited for the modal premise, not for the Map's causal picture. What the exclusion supplies is the shape of the modern problem — a structural physics facing a residue it had placed in the perceiver — and the hard problem is less puzzling, though no less real, once one sees why the vocabulary of physics is structural: it was chosen that way, and the choice was rewarded.

Theories of consciousness divide along a line traceable to the exclusion. Approaches within the Galilean framework (functionalism, computationalism, Global Workspace Theory) describe the structural and functional correlates of consciousness with increasing precision and struggle to bridge structure and quality; approaches that break from it (panpsychism, [[neurophenomenology-and-contemplative-neuroscience|neurophenomenology]], radical enactivism) gain resources for addressing experience directly. Whether they pay for those resources in rigour is deferred to [[methodology-of-consciousness-research]], whose verdict is that neurophenomenology is a real methodological advance whose power to close the metaphysical gap remains disputed.

The [[consciousness-and-the-authority-of-formal-systems|authority of formal systems]] debate reflects the same legacy: mathematics excels at the qualities Galileo kept, and its success with structure does not establish sovereignty over those he relocated.

## What Would Challenge This View? {#what-would-challenge-this-view}

The most direct challenge grants the history and denies the inference. On the [[type-identity-theory|identity theory]] the relocated quality is something physics describes under another concept, not something it left out: one property under two concepts, with the [[phenomenal-concepts-strategy|phenomenal-concept strategy]] explaining why the two feel unbridgeable. The relocation then produced an epistemic gap without an ontological one, and the genealogy, which shows only that the primitives were chosen structurally, cannot tell the two apart; the Map's reply lives in [[arguments-against-materialism|the arguments against materialism]], not in the history.

[[colour-ontology-and-the-secondary-quality-residue|Representational and relational theories of colour]] press from the other side. Colour as a relation between surface reflectance and a visual system is a perfectly good scientific target, measured by psychophysics and colorimetry for a century: Galileo's own secondary qualities became scientific objects because relational structure is tractable. The residue the argument can still point to is intrinsic quality — what standing in the relation is like — and the claim here is restricted to it.

[[heterophenomenology|Heterophenomenology]] is a data policy rather than a denial: Dennett treats reports as data about what subjects believe about their experience while suspending judgement on whether the experiences are as believed, a policy neutral between the metaphysical options that answers the genealogy by declining its terms. The eliminative and illusionist replies (Churchland; Frankish's [[illusionism]]) go further: the relocated residue is a misrepresentation. The genealogy does not refute them — a method built to leave quality out would do so whether or not there was quality to leave — so that disagreement stands at the framework boundary and is noted rather than settled here.

The diagnosis does not deliver dualism even where it is accepted whole. Goff's *Galileo's Error* (2019) runs the same history and concludes to [[panpsychism]]; Strawson reaches a "real physicalism" on which the physical is intrinsically experiential. Both reject dualism: the genealogy is shared property, and what each builds on it comes from premises the history does not supply.

[[russellian-monism|Russellian monism]] draws a related lesson: physics describes structure and is silent about what bears it. Newman's problem — structure alone fixes only cardinality — is more often read as pressure toward that view than toward dualism, and [[ontic-structural-realism#newman|the structural-realism article]] concedes that it cuts both ways and that the composite it attacks, structuralism about physics and mind together, is the Map's own join.

[[predictive-processing|Predictive processing]] is the live attempt to make phenomenal structure the *target* of a Galilean-descended formal science rather than its residue: Laukkonen, Friston and Chandaria (2025) propose an active-inference theory on which consciousness is a specific recursive structure of inference. If such a formalism captured phenomenal structure, the claim that a structural method leaves quality out would retreat to intrinsic quality alone; whether it retreats that far is not settled here, and the authors' physicalism is engaged as a rival.

Finally, the revisability objection: a sieve has a fixed mesh and science does not. Secondary qualities were outside physics in 1623 and are measured now; a science's primitives are not fixed by its founding. The reply is the conditional above, which covers only what structure and function do not exhaust — and revisability leaves open whether that set is empty.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The Unfinishable Map's [[tenets]] engage with the Galilean exclusion at multiple points.

**Dualism**: The genealogy is compatible with the Map's claim that consciousness is not reducible to physical processes, but does not independently establish it. A method whose primitives leave quality out would fail to capture it on every live view: type-B physicalism and the [[phenomenal-concepts-strategy|phenomenal-concept strategy]] expect the gap as a conceptual artefact, [[russellian-monism|Russellian monism]] as physics' silence about intrinsic natures, [[illusionism]] as the trace of a misrepresentation, and *Blind Spot* non-dualism as a reified abstraction. The datum discriminates among none of these, so under the [[evidential-status-discipline|evidential-status discipline]] it sits at the compatible register: it removes a defeater, the inference from silence to absence, and supplies no support of its own.

**Bidirectional Interaction**: If consciousness causally influences the physical world, a physics whose primitives exclude it describes causation incompletely wherever it acts. That follows from Tenet 3; the Galilean history supplies no independent evidence for downward causation. Nor is the exclusion why physics misses the contribution: on the [[born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface|Born-preserving reading]] the influence is invisible to aggregate statistical tests by design, because it is posited to average to the Born weights. The exclusion explains why physics lacks a vocabulary for the contribution; it does not predict that the contribution exists.

**Occam's Razor Has Limits**: The genealogy blocks a particular parsimony argument — the one that counts consciousness's absence from physics as evidence that there is nothing to count, then credits materialism with simplicity for omitting it. That argument assumes what it needs, and it is not the live one: identity theorists [[type-identity-theory|identify]] experience with what physics describes rather than omitting it, and their [[parsimony-epistemology|parsimony]] case rests on causal completeness. Whether a materialist account actually omits phenomenal facts remains the substantive dispute; the tenet adds only that simplicity cannot settle it while that dispute is open.
