Quantum divine action (QDA) is the theological program that locates God's special action at the point of quantum measurement: God fixes which physically-permitted outcome becomes actual, injecting no energy and breaking no law. It is the single closest live rival to the position this site defends, because it agrees with the Map's physics *exactly*, leaving two things to disagree about: the agent, and its scope. The Map holds that a creaturely mind biases quantum outcomes ([[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Tenet 3]]); QDA assigns that same biasing to God. Where the two are co-located in *scope* as well as in physics—a divine selector acting at the same few mind-linked events the Map's channel acts at—no experiment can distinguish them, and the Map takes its side on non-empirical, framework-relative grounds rather than on evidence or parsimony. That is the honest predicament this article confronts.

The indistinguishability is narrower than it first looks. Murphy's God acts at *every* measurement and Plantinga's at *all* collapses, whether or not a mind is present, whereas the Map's channel acts only where minds are. Tests conditioned on the presence of a mind therefore bear on the difference in principle: [[selection-only-mind-influence]] is explicit that the Map's empirical predictions have to be sought in conditional structure rather than in outcome frequencies, since Born-rule preservation constrains only the long-run marginal and leaves the mind-conditioned distributions open. That names a research programme, not a result, and leaves [[ensemble-level-epiphenomenalism|the epiphenomenalism charge]] live rather than settled.

The core contribution below is an **objection ledger** (introduced in [Inherited versus scope-peculiar objections](#inherited-versus-scope-peculiar-objections)): a sorting of the standard objections to QDA into those the Map *inherits*, because they arise from the shared quantum joint, and those that fall on *universal scope*—on any agent that fixes outcomes everywhere—which a selective channel escapes whether its agent is divine or creaturely. The sorting criterion is scope, not agent, and that fact does most of the work in what follows.

This page treats QDA as a named rival doctrine, standing beside [[panpsychism]], [[illusionism]], and [[occasionalism]]. Occasionalism enters in three separable roles. It is QDA's *historical ancestor*, the classical position of which QDA is the specifically quantum descendant and which [[occasionalism]] reconstructs in depth. It is a *contrast class*: it relocates all causation to God as a general metaphysics, where QDA claims only the collapse outcomes. And it is a *failure mode* universal-scope QDA has to steer away from, objection 7 in the ledger below. Distinct from all three is the Map's own [[selection-only-channel|selection-only channel]], which shares QDA's joint but places a finite mind at it.

## NIODA: Russell's Non-Interventionist Objective Divine Action

The flagship form of QDA is Robert John Russell's **Non-Interventionist Objective Divine Action** (NIODA), developed across the five-volume CTNS/Vatican Observatory "Scientific Perspectives on Divine Action" series (1990s–2002). Russell holds that God acts *objectively* (a real event in the world, not merely in a believer's perception) and *specially* (a particular providential act beyond creation and conservation), yet *non-interventionistically* (breaking no law), by determining which outcome is realized among the ontologically-open possibilities the wave function leaves undetermined. Russell's own formulation is that God takes the role of a nonphysical "hidden variable" that determines the precise outcome.

Russell insists on two conditions. First, a *collapse* interpretation of quantum mechanics on which the indeterminism is ontological rather than merely epistemic, so there is a genuine gap in which to act. Second, that divine action stay *within* the Born-rule probability distribution over long runs, so no statistical signature betrays it. Both conditions are exactly the ones the Map's own mechanism must meet.

## Plantinga's Divine Collapse-Causation

Alvin Plantinga makes the same structural move from the analytic-philosophy side. In *Where the Conflict Really Lies* (2011) he proposes **divine collapse-causation**: when a quantum measurement collapses the wave function to a definite result, it is God who causes that particular outcome. Because collapse is not fixed by prior physical law, God supplies the determination "without in any way abrogating the laws of physics." Plantinga finds the view attractive partly because it dovetails with libertarian free will.

Plantinga's version tends toward God causing *all* collapses—constant engagement everywhere—which sharpens the omni-determining worry the ledger below isolates. In a companion methodological essay, "What is 'Intervention'?" (2008), he argues that on contemporary quantum mechanics there is no principled way even to *define* intervention, so the alleged conflict between science and special divine action cannot be stated in the first place.

## The Scope Split: Murphy, Tracy, Ellis

Within bottom-up QDA a scope disagreement runs directly parallel to a choice the Map must also make. **Nancey Murphy** holds that God acts in *every* quantum measurement event, "activating or actualizing one or other of the quantum entity's innate powers," with macroscopic effects arising by amplification. **Thomas Tracy** holds that God acts in *only some* events—the "particular providence" version—which he argues sits better with treating the laws of nature as reflecting nature's genuine structure. Tracy also raises the "God of the gaps" worry against his own position: selecting only some events can look like exploiting an epistemic gap.

**George Ellis** combines quantum indeterminism with top-down causation, exploring quantum-triggered genetic mutation as a concrete amplification pathway from micro-indeterminacy to macroscopic biological consequence.

The all-events / some-events / creaturely axis is the very axis on which the Map plants its flag. The Map's channel is Tracy-like in *scope* (selective) but Murphy-unlike in *agent* (creaturely, not divine).

## Inherited versus scope-peculiar objections

The distinction that organises the rest of this page is between objections the Map shares with QDA and objections that fall on universal-scope QDA alone. Sorting by scope rather than by agent matters: most of the second column is derived, in the critics' own statements, from an agent who acts *everywhere*, so Tracy-style selective divine action escapes those objections on exactly the terms the Map does.

**Inherited (they arise from the shared quantum joint):**

1. **Measurement-problem dependence.** Both NIODA and the Map need a collapse interpretation with genuine ontological indeterminism. If a deterministic hidden-variable theory (Bohmian mechanics) is correct, the open gap vanishes. Many-Worlds closes it too, and both parties reject that horn on their own grounds: the Map under [[tenets#^no-many-worlds|Tenet 4]], Russell by making a collapse interpretation a stated condition of NIODA. Neither side gains ground there. The Bohmian horn and the general unsettledness of the measurement problem remain shared liabilities.
2. **Decoherence and the amplification problem.** Environmental decoherence at warm, wet, macroscopic scales appears to leave little exploitable indeterminacy, and single determinations rarely aggregate predictably into macroscopic effects. Saunders presses this against the bottom-up programme generally; Qureshi-Hurst and Bennett find the evidence for the specific quantum-to-mutation pathway ambiguous even as they grant that quantum biology looks promising. Murphy's accumulation model is the version both critiques find wanting. The Map faces the same problem, but not to the same degree. A divine selector carries no spatial commitment and may act wherever indeterminacy is most exploitable. The Map's channel has to act in a warm wet brain, the regime where the objection bites hardest. Shared in kind, asymmetric in magnitude, and the asymmetry runs against the Map.
3. **Episodicity.** Collapse events are sporadic and discrete; continuous, coherent agency has to be reconstructed from a stutter of isolated determinations. This presses on any quantum-interface proposal, divine or creaturely.
4. **Mind of the gaps.** Tracy raises the "God of the gaps" worry against his own selective position: acting at only some events can look like exploiting an epistemic gap that a maturing physics will close. The worry attaches to *selectivity*, not to divinity, and the Map's channel is selective. A creaturely mind biasing a few quantum events—and only the ones the unsettled measurement problem leaves open—is a mind of the gaps in precisely Tracy's sense. This is the one row that records a liability the Map carries and universal-scope QDA escapes: Murphy's God, acting at every event, exploits no gap in anyone's knowledge. It is also the only non-physics-facing item the Map inherits here.
5. **The sufficiency critique.** Saunders' survey of attempts to reconcile special divine action with quantum mechanics and chaos theory concludes that we remain far from a satisfactory account—the proposals identify a joint without specifying a mechanism that does the work claimed of it. The critique is addressed to divine action, but nothing in it depends on the agent being divine. Any proposal locating non-physical determination at the measurement joint owes the same specification, and the Map's [[selection-only-channel|selection-only channel]] is a partial answer rather than a discharged one.

**Peculiar to universal scope (they arise from an agent that fixes outcomes everywhere; a selective channel escapes or transforms them, whether its agent is divine or creaturely):**

6. **Theodicy.** If God determines quantum outcomes—including the mutations behind cancers and the events behind natural disasters—God bears direct responsibility for natural evil. Qureshi-Hurst and Bennett press exactly this against NIODA's extensive agency over evolution and genetics. A creaturely selective channel does not universalize responsibility this way.
7. **The deism-versus-occasionalism dilemma.** Too little divine action collapses toward deism (an absentee God); too much—Plantinga's "all collapses," Murphy's "every event"—collapses toward [[occasionalism]], God as the sole true cause, erasing creaturely causation. QDA at universal scope must thread a needle a selective channel sidesteps.
8. **The scope / Born-statistics explosion.** This is Arnauld's classical *explains-too-much* charge in modern dress. If God sets *every* outcome, the observed Born statistics become brute divine habit rather than something explained, and the specificity of nature dissolves into one universal will. A selective, local biasing of a few events does not incur this explosion.
9. **The "non-interventionist" label dispute.** Critics (Saunders; Qureshi-Hurst) argue that determining countless outcomes throughout the universe is *maximally* interventionist in substance, so the "non-interventionist" branding misrepresents the view. This objection is specific to the universal-scope version.

### What the ledger does not settle

Objections 6 through 9 do not separate the Map from *Tracy-style* selective divine action. Each is derived, in the wording given above, from universal scope: God answerable for the mutations behind cancers, God as sole true cause, God setting *every* outcome, God determining countless outcomes throughout the universe. Tracy's God acts at only some events and escapes all four on the same terms the Map does—and bears objection 4, the gaps worry, on the same terms too, since Tracy raised it against himself. What separates the Map from Tracy is the agent alone, and no row in the ledger discriminates on agent. That is the co-location the opening paragraph concedes, at its sharpest: against Murphy and Plantinga the ledger does real work; against the Map's nearest rival it does none, and Tenet 3 carries the disagreement unaided.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The Map's stand against *selective* QDA is **non-empirical and framework-relative**, and saying so plainly is a requirement of intellectual honesty rather than a weakness to be hidden. Where scope matches, the physics matches exactly, no experiment can adjudicate, and the disagreement is entirely over the agent. Against the universal-scope versions it is not purely non-empirical: because their God acts whether or not a mind is present, mind-conditioned tests bear on the contrast in principle, as [[selection-only-mind-influence]] sets out. That is a research programme with nothing in hand, and the Map does not bank a win it has not collected.

[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Tenet 2 (Minimal Quantum Interaction)]] names the shared joint: quantum indeterminacy as a real, law-respecting opening for non-physical determination, with no energy injected and the Born distribution preserved over long runs. QDA and the Map agree here almost word for word. The parting is at [[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction)]]: the Map commits to *finite* minds having genuine causal reach, whereas QDA routes all such reach through God. The Map takes the creaturely-agent side as an avowed commitment—the framework exists precisely to secure creaturely mental causation—and not because it has evidence QDA lacks.

Two refusals keep this honest. First, the Map refuses any parsimony win. [[tenets#^occams-limits|Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits)]] binds symmetrically: the Map cannot claim that minimal creaturely selection at a few events is *simpler than*, and therefore truer than, an omni-determining agent at every collapse. Simplicity does not track truth when knowledge is incomplete. The case against QDA is not that it is less parsimonious but that it discards the thing the Map's framework exists to secure. Second, the Map refuses any pretense of empirical adjudication against a rival whose scope matches its own; that co-location is admitted in full.

On [[tenets#^dualism|Tenet 1 (Dualism)]], the two views agree that mind and agency are not reducible to matter—a non-physical agent acts at the joint in both. They part only on whether *finite* minds have any causal reach at all. What the ledger secures is narrower than a comparative win. The objections that would be decisive—theodicy, the drift to occasionalism, the Born-statistics explosion—fall on universal scope rather than on a selective channel, which distances the Map from Murphy and Plantinga but buys it nothing against Tracy, who is distanced from them on identical terms. The ledger also debits the Map. It inherits from selectivity a gaps worry that universal-scope QDA escapes, and the decoherence problem presses harder on a channel confined to neural tissue than on an agent free to act anywhere. The net: the Map is better separated from the versions of QDA it least resembles, and no better off against the one it most resembles.
