The Map's value-sensitive horn—that felt value guides which outcome consciousness selects ([[valence-and-conscious-selection]])—is under-specified until it names *which* valence selects. Selection runs on valence assessed *before* an outcome is actual, **anticipated** valence, which the affective-forecasting literature shows diverging from the outcome's **experienced** valence, though less than it first appears. Anticipation is itself felt: the dread or relish one has *now* while simulating an outcome is anticipated in its object and experienced in its vehicle, so the selector can consult a present valence that forecasts a later one. And selection needs a correct *ranking*, not magnitudes, so the gap bears on selection only where it reverses ranks. The Map's candidate position: the felt valence of anticipating *drives* selection while the outcome's experienced valence *constitutes* value, at the cost that the driver is a fallible forecast of what it serves.

This is the **affective-forecasting gap** as it bears on the [[selection-criterion-problem|selection-criterion problem]]. For salient events and for duration the gap is a directional bias, not noise; elsewhere the evidence is mixed. The article offers one candidate resolution of the "anticipated vs experienced valence" question that [[valence-and-conscious-selection]] holds open—alongside, not above, the parent's graduated position on valence acting through attention—and hands [[the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection|the value-blind steelman]] a fair exhibit before replying. A later section adds the retrospective twin: the channel recording an experience opens only after the state ends, and how and when it opens shapes what reaches the record.

## Where the Gap Is Structured

LLM readers already know the impact bias (Wilson & Gilbert 2003, 2005); what matters here is its consequence for the Map's selection law, arising where the gap is *directional and predictable* rather than scatter a selector could average away.

Three sources give it structure. **Immune neglect**: forecasters fail to anticipate the "psychological immune system"—rationalisation and reframing—that dampens negative affect, so they overestimate how *long* bad feelings will last (Gilbert et al. 1998). **Focalism**: forecasters fixate on the focal event and neglect everything else that will shape mood, inflating its predicted impact (Wilson et al. 2000). **Prospection** names the mechanism: forecasting simulates a future event, and the simulation is systematically unrepresentative—truncated, decontextualised, present-biased (Gilbert & Wilson 2007)—so selection on prospected valence inherits its distortions.

The contested edge is wider than one exchange. Levine et al. (2012) argue the *intensity* component of the impact bias is partly a procedural artifact; Wilson & Gilbert (2013) rebut. Patel & Urry (2024) open their review with "Evidence for affective forecasting errors is mixed" and trace part of it to whether affect is measured discretely or dimensionally. The bias is also conditional on who forecasts: in the 2024 US election, partisan losers overestimated the result's affective impact while winners "did not display systematic forecasting errors" (Stavrova et al. 2026). The Map therefore leans only on the *duration* result, which both sides concede, and claims structure for salient events, not forecasting in general.

## Utility and Its Layers

Kahneman, Wakker & Sarin (1997) supply the vocabulary, distinguishing two core meanings of utility: **decision utility**, the weight of an outcome in a decision, and **experienced utility**, its hedonic quality. Experienced utility is assessed in real time as **instant utility** or retrospectively as **remembered utility**, and the **total** experienced utility of an extended episode is, on their normative theory, the temporal integral of instant utility. Remembered utility is thus a retrospective assessment rather than a third co-equal utility, and departs from the total so reliably that its errors "can induce a preference for dominated options."

The utility the Map's argument needs comes from an earlier paper: **predicted utility**, a person's forecast of the experienced utility a future outcome will have (Kahneman & Snell 1992)—what the opening called anticipated valence. It is distinct from decision utility, a weight read off the choice made rather than consulted before it, so "selection runs on decision utility" would be near-circular. This article's claim is that selection consults *predicted* utility, a fallible forecast of experienced utility, trained on remembered utility.

Remembered utility is a selective summary. Redelmeier & Kahneman (1996) found that retrospective judgments of painful procedures track the **peak** and the **end** intensity, with **duration neglect**: episode length had almost no effect. A meta-analysis of 174 effect sizes found the peak-end effect large (r = 0.581, 95% CI 0.487–0.661) but "comparable to the effect of the overall average (mean) score", while the duration effect was "essentially nil" (Alaybek et al. 2022). Memory keeps the mean alongside the extremes and discards the length, so remembered utility partly departs from the total rather than replacing it. The distortion compounds across a pipeline: experience → (peak-end-weighted, duration-blind, mean-preserving) memory → (impact-biased) forecast → selection.

In these terms, *felt value guides choice* reads most naturally as *experienced* utility doing the work, whereas selection consults *predicted* utility trained on *remembered* utility; whether the forecast is itself felt is taken up below.

## Before Distortion: Whether the Episode Reaches the Record at All

The pipeline's first arrow does not always carry anything. Remembered utility can fail by being *absent* rather than distorted, and the two failures are not comparably tractable: a bias of known sign and size can be corrected, while an explicit-recall zero is indistinguishable from an empty interval. That is the prospective gap's retrospective twin, and anaesthesia is where it has been measured.

Anaesthesia's report channel has been probed at three points by three methods. The isolated forearm technique, leaving one arm unparalysed so the patient can respond to command *within* the state, found responsiveness in 4.6% (12 of 260) after tracheal intubation, five signalling pain (Sanders et al. 2017). The modified Brice questionnaire, within 24 hours of surgery, found reported awareness in about 1 in 800 (Walker et al. 2016; Tasbihgou et al. 2018 set the three side by side); spontaneous report, about 1 in 19,600 (Pandit et al. 2014, the NAP5 audit). A roughly 900-fold spread, produced jointly by ascertainment method, endpoint, population and regimen rather than by latency. Nor is the 4.6% constant: among 18–40-year-olds Lennertz et al. (2023) found 11% (37 of 338), female sex raising the adjusted odds (OR 2.7) and continuous anaesthesia before laryngoscopy lowering them (OR 0.43).

Two guards belong on that gradient. The figures come from different studies, populations, endpoints and case definitions, so it is not a controlled comparison; and responsiveness to command establishes connected *responsiveness*, the step to phenomenal experience being an inference the five pain signals make strong but not free—the 4.6% is not an incidence of experience. The Sanders cohort does supply a within-population contrast, none of the 253 patients questioned after surgery having explicit recall, but its two endpoints are different capacities, and anaesthetics disrupt encoding and consolidation independently of elapsed time: a dissociation between connectedness and explicit memory, not a controlled effect of latency, which [[anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface]] decomposes and this article defers to.

Nor does "no explicit recall" mean "no record". Linassi et al.'s (2021) meta-analysis found implicit-memory events under deep sedation or general anaesthesia in 43 of 119 cohorts (36.1%). So the distorted-or-absent binary needs a third route: an implicit trace no questionnaire reaches, which can shape a later forecast without the forecaster knowing why.

Generic scepticism about retrospective report would be the wrong lesson, though the calibration literature covers reconstruction of a retained episode, not anaesthetic amnesia. Where an episode *is* retained, remembered intensity is only mildly biased: a fifteen-study meta-analysis of paediatric pain memory found a small overestimation (SMD = 0.28; Cuenca-Martínez et al. 2024); no completed adult equivalent exists; the adult calibration point remains Erskine, Morley & Pearce's 1990 review, judging recall moderately accurate. Memory is roughly right about *magnitude*; the failure named here concerns *presence*, the worse of the two: peak-end distortion recomputes an episode that survived; an absent episode never reaches the record.

Report delay varies, and the Map's own felt-state evidence sits at several points along it. The revised Mystical Experience Questionnaire's validation paper discloses its timing: "MEQ30 ratings were provided seven hours after capsule administration", and the original normative sample rated experiences that "occurred on average eight years before completion of the questionnaire" (Barrett, Johnson & Griffiths 2015). The psychometrics are not in question; the timing shows even well-controlled felt-state evidence to be a delayed reconstruction, and the Map should inherit the disclosure.

Treating the reported frequency and content of a felt state as partly a property of how and when the channel was opened is the Map's own framing, and the sources establish it only within anaesthesia and in one direction: null reports under-count. They license no quantified discount for the Map's other felt-state evidence, and this article applies none. For the selection currency the consequence is narrow: where the recording channel cannot reach a state in real time, the explicit record the forecast is trained on can be empty—one information source removed from a forecaster that also draws on semantic knowledge, vicarious report and whatever implicit trace the episode left.

What would count against the claim: confidence that an unreported episode occurred should fall where a real-time probe returned nothing, as for 248 of 260 Sanders patients; where implicit-memory testing is negative; and under regimens that lower measured incidence, as in the Lennertz cohort.

## A Mechanistic Correlate, Read With Care

Decision neuroscience supplies a quantity that plausibly biases action selection—a *prediction* signal. Many midbrain dopamine neurons code a **reward-prediction error** (Schultz 2016)—"many", not "most", since that account "in its original form, [is] probably too simple" for phasic dopamine (Gershman et al. 2024). The error is a teaching signal; a predictor-driven chooser consults the learned value it updates, so what a felt forecast would supplement or rival is that value, not the update (see [[the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection]]). Whether the error reaches feeling is contested: momentary happiness, first modelled on reward-prediction errors, proved "not sensitive to learning-irrelevant variables (i.e. reward prediction error)" (Blain & Rutledge 2020).

Berridge & Robinson's (2016) wanting/liking dissociation makes a neighbouring cut: "wanting" (incentive salience) is the anticipatory pull that guides choice, "liking" (hedonic impact) the experienced good, and addiction is wanting what is no longer liked. Two caveats keep the parallel with anticipated and experienced valence honest: wanting is not a forecast of liking—"desire can decouple completely from memories and from learned predictions of outcome value" (Berridge 2023)—and "liking" is an objective hedonic reaction that Berridge's own taxonomy separates from conscious pleasure ([[wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork]]). The mapping is interpretive—these literatures concern behavioural selection, and their physicalist authors' constructs are operational—supplying the structure, predictive ≠ experienced, not a mechanism.

So flagged, the gap is an exhibit for the value-blind rival, which predicts the divergence—choice controlled by a learned value, feeling idle—where a value-sensitive selector consulting a miscalibrated forecast merely accommodates it; calling it a calibration problem does not cancel that likelihood-bearing force.

## The Disambiguation the Map Must Make

This article first posed the choice as exclusive: either the outcome's *experienced* valence selects, which the evidence refuses—selection consults a predicted, remembered proxy—or *anticipated* valence selects through a mechanism-like predictor. The dichotomy is mis-posed, as the parent's [[valence-and-conscious-selection#limits-and-open-questions|anticipation problem]] had gestured. Anticipation has a felt character: the dread of the appointment and the relish of the meal are occurrent valences at choice, anticipated in their *object* and experienced in their *vehicle*—an instant utility of their own, in Kahneman, Wakker & Sarin's terms. The third option is the most congenial to interactionism: selection consults a *presently felt* valence, the experienced quality of simulating each outcome, without backward causation or non-phenomenal computation. The forecasting literature then documents an epistemic fact: felt anticipation at t₁ forecasts the outcome's felt valence at t₂ poorly.

That softens the dilemma without cancelling the cost, and must not trade on an ambiguity. "Currency" has two senses: what selection is causally *sensitive to*, and what selection is normatively *for*. In the first sense the candidate currency is the felt valence of anticipation; in the second it is the outcome's experienced valence, which [[consciousness-value-connection]] argues constitutes value. The "constitutes value" horn answers the second question where the first was posed, so the result is a relocation rather than a division of labour: value-sensitivity moves from the outcome to the present feeling that forecasts it. Whether that feeling selects *as felt*, or a sub-personal predictor selects and the feeling accompanies, is the fork this article cannot close and the value-blind steelman presses.

The gap's cost depends on what selection needs from its currency: a correct *ranking* of the live options, not their magnitudes, so a shared exaggeration of every option's predicted unpleasantness leaves the ordering intact. The bias bears on the selection law only where it reverses ranks—an option forecast as better experienced as worse—or where a differential bias swaps options forecast as close. The evidence above is almost all absolute; the one study measuring both found forecasters who "could predict when a day/event would make them feel better/worse than usual, showing relative accuracy, but sometimes made small errors in forecasting their absolute affect levels, showing absolute inaccuracy" (Moeck et al. 2026). A reversal needs a salient event, a differential rather than shared bias—focalism inflates the focal option specifically—and a margin smaller than the differential. Where those hold selection misfires; otherwise a miscalibrated currency still ranks correctly.

The distinction remains testable, though not by the test first proposed here—correct the forecast and watch whether selection changes: a mechanism-only model whose input *is* the forecast predicts changed choice too, and a randomised defocusing instruction (N = 670) "did not improve affective forecasting accuracy" (Sobel Misieczko & Barber 2026), so a debiasing arm needs a manipulation check first. What discriminates is dissociating the inputs, in [[positions/value-in-selection|P-VS2]]'s battery framing:

|Manipulation|Value-blind learned value|Present felt valence|Anticipated simulation|Valence→attention hybrid|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Debias forecast (checked)|changes|changes|changes|changes|
|Shift present affect; forecast, learned value fixed|none|changes|none|via attention|
|Shift learned value; affect, forecast fixed|changes|none|none|none|

Only the lower two rows separate the models; the parent's [[graduated-middle-path-valence-modulated-attention|attentional clamp]] separates the last two columns.

## Relation to Site Perspective

This concept offers one candidate resolution of a question the Map holds open, sharpening rather than dissolving the value-sensitive horn.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]**: If consciousness selects on the felt valence of anticipation, the interaction is real and phenomenal at the point of selection, but routed through a fallible forecast. The Map should not let this narrow the tenet into near-epiphenomenalism of the *outcome's* evaluative dimension—the parent fork's trap for its value-blind horn. The outcome's experienced valence keeps *evaluative* standing, constituting value and setting the calibration target; but a set-point does not switch the circuit, and the step from constituting value to causal work in selection is the open one.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]]**: A selection law reading off anticipatory valence is no less minimal than one reading off attention. But if that valence reduces to a learned-value or wanting computation, the value-blind steelman's parsimony advantage strengthens, and the Map's reply must be the prior-commitments reply—reject illusionism, hold [[phenomenal-value-realism|phenomenal value realism]]—not a parsimony contest it would lose. This tenet constrains the interaction empirically; parsimony is [[tenets#^occams-limits|Tenet 5]]'s question.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]**: This tenet licenses declining the more-parsimonious pure-predictive reading despite the forecasting evidence—but only if the decline is *argued*: the gap is a genuine cost the value-sensitive horn must pay, and simplicity is an unreliable guide where knowledge is incomplete, so the argument, not the razor, carries the decline. The same tenet governs the report material: the parsimonious reading of a null report is that there was nothing to report, which within anaesthesia the isolated-forearm and implicit-memory findings show under-counts; extending that to other felt states is the Map's own inference.

**[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]**: Under many-worlds the gap is moot—every outcome occurs, so "which valence selects" has no referent. Only if one outcome is really chosen does it matter which valence chooses.
