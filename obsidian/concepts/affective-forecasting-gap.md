The Map's value-sensitive horn—that felt value guides which outcome consciousness selects ([[valence-and-conscious-selection]])—is under-specified until it names *which* valence does the selecting. Selection runs on valence assessed *before* an outcome is actual, so on **anticipated** valence, which the affective-forecasting literature shows diverging from the **experienced** valence of the outcome—though the divergence is narrower than it first appears. Anticipation is itself felt: the dread or relish one has *now* while simulating an outcome is anticipated in its object and experienced in its vehicle, so the selector can consult a present experienced valence that merely forecasts a later one. And selection needs a correct *ranking*, not correct magnitudes, so the gap bears on selection only where it reverses ranks. The Map's candidate position: the felt valence of anticipating *drives* selection while the outcome's experienced valence *constitutes* value, at the cost that the driver is a fallible forecast of what it serves.

This is the **affective-forecasting gap** as it bears on the [[selection-criterion-problem|selection-criterion problem]]. For salient events and for duration the gap is a directional bias rather than noise; elsewhere the evidence is mixed. The article offers one candidate resolution of the "anticipated vs experienced valence" question that [[valence-and-conscious-selection]] holds open—alongside, not above, the parent's graduated position on which valence acts through attention—and hands [[the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection|the value-blind steelman]] a fair exhibit before replying. A later section adds the retrospective twin: the channel that records an experience opens only after the state ends, and how and when it opens shapes what reaches the record.

## Where the Gap Is Structured

LLM readers already know the impact bias (reviewed in Wilson & Gilbert 2003, 2005); what matters here is its consequence for the Map's selection law, which arises where the gap is *directional and predictable* rather than scatter a selection mechanism could average away.

Three sources give it structure. **Immune neglect**: forecasters fail to anticipate the "psychological immune system"—rationalisation and reframing—that dampens negative affect, so they overestimate how *long* bad feelings will last (Gilbert et al. 1998). **Focalism**: forecasters fixate on the focal event and neglect everything else that will shape mood, inflating its predicted impact (Wilson et al. 2000). **Prospection** names the underlying mechanism: forecasting simulates a future event, and the simulation is systematically unrepresentative—truncated, decontextualised, present-biased (Gilbert & Wilson 2007)—so selection on prospected valence inherits its distortions.

The contested edge is wider than one exchange. Levine et al. (2012) argue the *intensity* component of the impact bias is partly a procedural artifact; Wilson & Gilbert (2013) rebut. Patel & Urry (2024) open their review with "Evidence for affective forecasting errors is mixed" and trace part of the inconsistency to whether affect is measured discretely or dimensionally. The bias is also conditional on who forecasts: around the 2024 US election, partisan losers overestimated the result's impact on their affect while partisan winners "did not display systematic forecasting errors" (Stavrova et al. 2026). The Map's argument therefore leans only on the *duration* result, which both sides of the artifact debate concede, and claims structure for salient events, not for forecasting in general.

## Utility and Its Layers

Kahneman, Wakker & Sarin (1997) supply the vocabulary. They distinguish two core meanings of utility: **decision utility**, the weight of an outcome in a decision, and **experienced utility**, its hedonic quality. Experienced utility is assessed either in real time as **instant utility** or retrospectively as **remembered utility**, and the **total** experienced utility of an extended episode is, on their normative theory, the temporal integral of instant utility. Remembered utility is thus a retrospective assessment of experience rather than a third co-equal utility, and it departs from the total so reliably that its errors "can induce a preference for dominated options."

The utility the Map's argument needs comes from an earlier paper: **predicted utility**, a person's forecast of the experienced utility a future outcome will have (Kahneman & Snell 1992)—what the opening called anticipated valence. It is distinct from decision utility, a weight read off the choice actually made rather than consulted before it, so "selection runs on decision utility" would be near-circular. This article's claim is that selection consults *predicted* utility, a fallible forecast of experienced utility, trained on remembered utility.

Remembered utility is a selective summary. Redelmeier & Kahneman (1996) found that retrospective judgments of painful procedures track the **peak** and the **end** intensity, with **duration neglect**: episode length had almost no effect. A meta-analysis of 174 effect sizes found the peak-end effect large (r = 0.581, 95% CI 0.487–0.661) but "comparable to the effect of the overall average (mean) score", while the duration effect was "essentially nil" (Alaybek et al. 2022). Memory keeps the mean alongside the extremes and discards the length, so remembered utility partly departs from the total rather than replacing it. The distortion compounds across a pipeline: experience → (peak-end-weighted, duration-blind, mean-preserving) memory → (impact-biased) forecast → selection.

In these terms, *felt value guides choice* reads most naturally as the claim that *experienced* utility does the work, whereas selection consults *predicted* utility trained on *remembered* utility. Whether the forecast is itself felt is taken up below.

## Before Distortion: Whether the Episode Reaches the Record at All

The pipeline's first arrow does not always carry anything. Remembered utility can fail by being *absent* rather than distorted, and the two failures are not comparably tractable: a bias of known sign and size can be corrected, while an explicit-recall zero is indistinguishable, from the record's side, from an empty interval. That is the retrospective twin of the prospective gap, and anaesthesia is where it has been measured.

Anaesthesia's report channel has been probed at three points by three methods. The isolated forearm technique, which leaves one arm unparalysed so the patient can respond to command *within* the state, found responsiveness in 4.6% of patients (12 of 260) after tracheal intubation, five of the twelve signalling pain (Sanders et al. 2017). The modified Brice questionnaire, administered within 24 hours of surgery, found reported awareness in about 1 in 800 (Walker et al. 2016; Tasbihgou et al. 2018 set the three side by side). Spontaneous patient report found about 1 in 19,600 (Pandit et al. 2014, the NAP5 audit). A roughly 900-fold spread, produced jointly by ascertainment method, endpoint, population and regimen rather than by latency. Even the 4.6% is not a constant: among 18–40-year-olds Lennertz et al. (2023) found 11% (37 of 338) responding after intubation, female sex raising the adjusted odds (OR 2.7) and continuous anaesthesia before laryngoscopy lowering them (OR 0.43).

Two guards belong on that gradient. The figures come from different studies, populations, endpoints and case definitions, so it is a gradient rather than a controlled comparison; and responsiveness to command establishes connected *responsiveness*, with the step to phenomenal experience an inference the five pain signals make strong but not free—the 4.6% is not an incidence of experience. The Sanders cohort does supply a within-population contrast, none of the 253 patients questioned after surgery having explicit recall, but its two endpoints are different capacities, and anaesthetics disrupt encoding and consolidation independently of elapsed time: a dissociation between connectedness and explicit memory, not a controlled effect of latency, which [[anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface]] decomposes component by component and this article defers to.

Nor does "no explicit recall" mean "no record". Linassi et al.'s (2021) meta-analysis found implicit-memory events under deep sedation or general anaesthesia in 43 of 119 cohorts (36.1%). So the distorted-or-absent binary needs a third route: an implicit trace no questionnaire reaches, which can shape a later forecast without the forecaster being able to say why.

Generic scepticism about retrospective report would be the wrong lesson, though the calibration literature covers ordinary reconstruction of a retained episode rather than anaesthetic amnesia. Where an episode *is* retained, remembered intensity is only mildly biased: a meta-analysis of paediatric pain memory across fifteen studies found a small overestimation (SMD = 0.28; Cuenca-Martínez et al. 2024); no completed adult equivalent exists, and the adult calibration point remains Erskine, Morley & Pearce's 1990 review, which judged recall moderately accurate. Memory is roughly right about *magnitude*; the failure named here concerns *presence*, the worse of the two, since peak-end distortion recomputes an episode that survived whereas an episode can fail to reach explicit memory at all.

Report delay varies, and the Map's own felt-state evidence sits at several points along it. The revised Mystical Experience Questionnaire's validation paper discloses its timing: "MEQ30 ratings were provided seven hours after capsule administration", and the original normative sample rated experiences that "occurred on average eight years before completion of the questionnaire" (Barrett, Johnson & Griffiths 2015). The psychometrics are not in question; the timing shows that even well-controlled felt-state evidence is a delayed reconstruction, and the Map should inherit the disclosure.

Treating the reported frequency and content of a felt state as partly a property of how and when the channel was opened is the Map's own framing, and the sources establish it only within anaesthesia and in one direction: null reports under-count. They license no quantified discount for the Map's other felt-state evidence, and this article applies none. For the selection currency the consequence is narrow: where the recording channel cannot reach a state in real time, the explicit record the forecast is trained on can be empty—one information source removed, without blinding a forecaster that also draws on semantic knowledge, vicarious report and whatever implicit trace the episode left.

What would count against the claim: confidence that an unreported episode occurred should fall where a real-time probe returned nothing, as for 248 of 260 Sanders patients; where implicit-memory testing is negative; and under regimens that lower measured incidence, as continuous pre-laryngoscopy anaesthesia roughly halved the odds in the Lennertz cohort.

## A Mechanistic Correlate, Read With Care

Decision neuroscience supplies a quantity that plausibly biases action selection—and it is a *prediction* signal. Most midbrain dopamine neurons code a **reward-prediction error**: activation for better-than-predicted outcomes, baseline for fully-predicted, depression for worse-than-predicted (Schultz 2016). The signal codes the predictive, economic-value-like component, not the hedonic "pleasure" aspect. At the motivational level, Berridge & Robinson's (2016) wanting/liking dissociation makes the same cut: "wanting" (incentive salience, mesolimbic dopamine) is the anticipatory pull that guides choice; "liking" (hedonic impact, opioid hotspots) is the experienced good. They come apart—addiction is intense wanting of substances no longer liked.

This mapping is **interpretive, and the article flags it as such rather than overclaiming a mechanism.** The reward-prediction-error and wanting literatures concern *behavioural and motivational* selection; the Map's selection is over *quantum-underdetermined neural outcomes*. There is no direct neuroscience of "anticipated phenomenal valence as a selection input." What the dopamine and wanting findings supply is the *structure*—predictive ≠ experienced—not a ready-made neural mechanism for the Map's selection law. Reading off a worked mechanism here would repeat the over-reach the steelman article warns against in the opposite direction; Schultz and Berridge are physicalists whose constructs are operational, and the wanting/liking dissociation is empirical while its metaphysics is not theirs to settle.

So flagged, the gap is an exhibit for the value-blind rival. A mechanism-only theory predicts exactly this divergence: choice controlled by a learned predictor—a teaching signal, an incentive-salience pull—with feeling a downstream fact doing no selective work. The divergence is equally compatible with a value-sensitive selector consulting a miscalibrated felt forecast, so it decides nothing; but it is not neutral, carrying some likelihood-bearing force for the rival, which predicts it, over a reading that must accommodate it.

## The Disambiguation the Map Must Make

This article first posed the choice as exclusive: either the outcome's *experienced* valence selects, which the evidence refuses—selection consults a predicted, remembered proxy—or *anticipated* valence selects and selection is steered by a mechanism-like predictor. The dichotomy is mis-posed, as the parent's [[valence-and-conscious-selection#limits-and-open-questions|anticipation problem]] had gestured. Anticipation has a felt character: the dread of the appointment and the relish of the meal are occurrent valences at the moment of choice, anticipated with respect to their *object* and experienced with respect to their *vehicle*; in Kahneman, Wakker & Sarin's terms the anticipating state has an instant utility of its own. A third option was available, and it is the most congenial to interactionism: selection consults a *presently felt* valence—the experienced quality of simulating each outcome—needing no backward causation from the eventual experience and no non-phenomenal computation. What the forecasting literature documents is then an epistemic fact: the felt valence of anticipating at t₁ forecasts the felt valence of the outcome at t₂ poorly.

That softens the dilemma without cancelling the cost. The Map's candidate position becomes: the felt valence of anticipation *drives* selection, the outcome's experienced valence *constitutes* value ([[consciousness-value-connection]]), and their divergence is a calibration problem for a selector value-sensitive throughout. The currency selection spends is still a forecast of the value it serves, and the fork this article cannot close is whether the anticipatory feeling selects *as felt* or a sub-personal predictor selects and the feeling accompanies—the question the value-blind steelman presses, untouched by the present-affect point.

How much the gap costs depends on what selection needs from its currency: a correct *ranking* of the live options, not their magnitudes, so a shared exaggeration of every option's predicted unpleasantness leaves the ordering intact. The bias bears on the selection law only where it produces rank reversals—an option forecast as better experienced as worse—or errors near a choice boundary, where a differential bias can swap options forecast as close. The evidence cited above is almost all absolute; the one study measuring both found everyday forecasters who "could predict when a day/event would make them feel better/worse than usual, showing relative accuracy, but sometimes made small errors in forecasting their absolute affect levels, showing absolute inaccuracy" (Moeck et al. 2026). A reversal needs a salient event, a bias differential rather than shared across the options—focalism inflates the focal option specifically—and a margin between options smaller than the differential. Where those hold selection misfires; where they do not, a miscalibrated currency still ranks correctly.

The distinction remains testable. If selection is value-sensitive on felt valence, interventions that *correct* forecasts—defocusing or de-biasing manipulations—should change which outcome is selected wherever they change the ranking; if selection runs on a predictive channel, debiasing the felt forecast should leave a selector driven by the reward-prediction-error or wanting signal unmoved. The readings come apart in the lab, where the [[valence-and-conscious-selection|parent fork]] locates its discriminating battery.

## Relation to Site Perspective

This concept offers one candidate resolution of a question the Map holds open, and sharpens rather than dissolves the value-sensitive horn.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]**: If consciousness selects on the felt valence of anticipation, the interaction is real and phenomenal at the point of selection, but routed through a fallible forecast. The Map should not let this narrow the tenet into near-epiphenomenalism of the *outcome's* evaluative dimension—the trap the parent fork tracks for its value-blind horn. The outcome's experienced valence remains what constitutes value and the calibration target; a present feeling of it does the selecting.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]]**: A selection law reading off anticipatory valence is no less minimal than one reading off attention. But if that valence reduces to a reward-prediction-error or wanting computation, the value-blind steelman's parsimony advantage strengthens, and the Map's reply must be the prior-commitments reply—reject illusionism, hold [[phenomenal-value-realism|phenomenal value realism]]—not a parsimony contest it would lose. This tenet's minimality constrains the interaction empirically rather than adjudicating between readings; parsimony is [[tenets#^occams-limits|Tenet 5]]'s question.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]**: This tenet licenses declining the more-parsimonious pure-predictive reading despite the forecasting evidence—but only if the decline is *argued*. The gap is a genuine cost the value-sensitive horn must pay, and Tenet 5 permits paying it: simplicity is an unreliable guide where knowledge is incomplete, so the argument, not the razor, carries the decline. The same tenet governs the report material: the parsimonious reading of a null report is that there was nothing to report, which within anaesthesia the isolated-forearm and implicit-memory findings show under-counting; extending the under-count to other felt states is the Map's own inference.

**[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]**: Under many-worlds the gap is moot—every outcome occurs, so "which valence selects" has no referent. Only if one outcome is really chosen does it matter which valence does the choosing.
