**Diverging-worlds Everettianism** reads the Everett interpretation as a plurality of worlds that never share parts. Each world has its own complete history, including a pre-measurement stretch that is qualitatively identical to its neighbours' pre-measurement stretches but not numerically the same. The rival *overlapping* reading has one world that literally splits. Simon Saunders argued that nothing in the mathematics forces overlap, and Alastair Wilson built the diverging reading into a full metaphysics of modality, **quantum modal realism**, on which Everett worlds *are* the metaphysically possible worlds. This is the strongest rival to the Map's indexical objection to many-worlds. That objection asks *which successor will I be?*, and the question presupposes fission: one subject becoming many. On divergence there is no fission. Each subject has exactly one future, and the question does not arise. The Map concedes this. Its fission-shaped argument does not reach the diverging view. What survives is narrower: a haecceitistic residue (why am I *this* one of many qualitatively identical subjects?), the posit that unchosen alternatives occur nowhere, and the absence of any work for conscious outcome-selection. Those are framework-boundary disagreements. None of them refutes the view on its own terms.

## Overlap Versus Divergence

The distinction comes from David Lewis's metaphysics of possible worlds. Worlds *overlap* (in the philosophers' sense Lewis called "branching") when they share parts, so that the very same object or person exists in several of them. Worlds *diverge* when they share no parts: two diverging worlds can match exactly up to some time and differ afterwards, but their early segments are duplicates, not one shared segment.

In the Everettian setting, Saunders and Wallace (2008) argued that the choice matters less than it seems for ordinary talk. They offered a semantics on which, "whether branching or diverging, speakers cannot say in advance which branch or world is theirs". So an Everettian's statements of uncertainty come out true, and they took this to solve the "incoherence problem", the charge that Everett leaves no room for genuine uncertainty about outcomes. Tappenden (2008) replied to their use of Lewis in the same journal issue.

Saunders (2010) then pressed the metaphysical question directly. He observed that "he who believes he is contained in each of a number of worlds cannot also wonder which of them he's in". He proposed a mereology for branch vectors on which orthogonal branches have no parts in common, so Everett worlds diverge. He concluded that "there is no good reason to think EQM is really a theory of overlapping worlds". Saunders is careful about how strong this is. He suggests that overlap versus divergence may be underdetermined by the mathematics, with each picture suited to different purposes. (Quotations are from the arXiv preprint of the chapter.)

## Quantum Modal Realism

Wilson's *The Nature of Contingency* (2020) takes divergence as the correct reading and builds on it. The book's framework, which Wilson calls quantum modal realism, identifies metaphysical possibility with Everettian possibility: to be a possible world is to be an Everett world, and contingency is variation across Everett worlds. The multiverse is a complex of possible worlds, not a single possible world. Actuality is indexical: each world is actual for its own inhabitants, as in Lewis's modal realism. Wilson gives a "Future Argument for Divergence" for the diverging reading. Chance becomes self-locating: in the book's Everettian Principal Principle, as reviewers summarise it, rational credence at a time is set by the proportion of Everett worlds that match the agent's world up to that time.

Because diverging worlds share no parts, a person does not exist in more than one of them. Modal claims about a person, such as "I might have seen spin-down", therefore run through qualitatively similar *counterparts* in other worlds, as in Lewis's framework, and not through transworld identity. The Map's [[quantum-immortality-and-the-quantum-suicide-survival-argument|quantum immortality article]] draws the consequence. The pre-measurement observer does not become all of its survivors, so no single *I* stretches across the branches, and the runaway survival expectation never forms.

## What the Diverging View Dissolves

Three of the Map's anti-MWI moves are built around fission, and divergence disarms each of them.

**The "which successor is me?" question.** The [[many-worlds|many-worlds]] article's central argument begins: before measurement there is one of you, and afterwards there are two equally real continuations. On divergence, that first sentence is false. Before the measurement there were already many qualitatively identical observers, each in its own world with exactly one future. No subject is shared among successors, so there is nothing to divide. The question "which successor will I be?" has a determinate answer in every world: the one in this world.

**The "no fact to be uncertain about" charge.** The Map argues that self-locating uncertainty normally concerns a single world with facts one does not know, and that on MWI "you" end up in both outcomes, so there is nothing left to be ignorant of. Divergence restores the missing fact. I am in exactly one world, I do not yet know which, and my uncertainty is ordinary ignorance of where I am located. This is precisely the move Saunders and Wallace built their semantics to secure.

**The double-duty tension.** The Map's [[many-worlds#MWI's Branch-Relative Indexicality Reply|canonical reply]] to Saunders and Wallace holds that branch-relative indexicality must be local within each branch, while decision-theoretic Born-rule derivations need a unified cross-branch relation from the pre-branching agent to all of her successors. On divergence the agent has no successors in other worlds, only counterparts. Her credences are those of one subject uncertain about her location, so the unified cross-branch perspective is not needed. The tension largely loses its target. Whether the world-proportion measure *earns* Born weights, as opposed to presupposing them, is a separate question that remains open inside the Everett literature (see [[probability-problem-in-many-worlds]]).

The felt singularity and felt openness of experience also give no leverage here. Each diverging observer has exactly one future and anticipates one outcome. Felt openness is consistent with the Map's single-world reading, and it does not defeat the diverging one.

## What Survives

**The haecceitistic residue.** Divergence removes fission, but it does not remove multiplicity. Before the measurement there are many qualitatively identical subjects, and I am one of them. On the Map's [[haecceity|haecceitism]], my being *this* subject rather than a duplicate is a non-qualitative fact. Quantum modal realism handles de re modality and chance through qualitative matching: counterparts, and the proportion of worlds that match up to a time. On that approach the fact the Map insists on either reduces to self-location or does not exist. The [[vertiginous-question|vertiginous question]] therefore survives, in a changed form. The question "which successor will I become?" gives way to "why am I in this world rather than one of its duplicates?" Oddly, divergence *agrees* with the Map that identity does not branch. The disagreement is over whether a non-qualitative fact grounds that non-branching.

**Global exclusion.** Wilson's alternatives are non-actual *for us*, because actuality is indexical. As [[background-commitments#Posit Three: Global Exclusion of Unchosen Alternatives|the Map's background commitments]] note, that satisfies the word "nonactual" without satisfying the Map's posit, which requires that no incompatible alternative be realised *anywhere*, whether indexed to another world or not. On quantum modal realism every outcome is realised in some world. The Map adopts global exclusion as a posit and does not derive it. The diverging view denies that posit, and cannot be convicted of any internal error for doing so.

**No work for conscious selection.** The dynamics of diverging Everettianism are still unitary. Which world I am in is fixed; what I lack is knowledge of it. So there is no indeterminacy for consciousness to bias, and the Map's [[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|minimal quantum interaction]] has nothing to act on. This point needs to be stated in its narrow form. [[quantum-measurement-and-consciousness#Deny the Gap: Many-Worlds|The broader mental-causation complaint]] says that if every choice branches into all alternatives, preferring one cannot bring it about. Divergence weakens that version: choices do not branch, and within a world the agent's reasons cause her action in the ordinary way. What remains is that the Map's *particular* mechanism, selecting among real alternatives, has no alternative to exclude. A diverging-worlds dualist would have to locate mental causation elsewhere.

**Consciousness unexplained, extravagance, basis.** Divergence leaves these as the [[many-worlds]] article states them. It does not connect consciousness to the physics. Its ontology is no smaller: a continuum of worlds present from the start. It still depends on decoherence to fix which histories count as worlds. The Map treats extravagance and the preferred-basis worry as non-decisive in any case.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The Map's [[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]] tenet is usually defended with a fission picture, and against diverging-worlds Everettianism that picture is aimed at the wrong target. The honest accounting is:

- **Conceded.** The "which successor" argument, the "no fact to be uncertain about" charge, and most of the double-duty tension do not reach the diverging view. Divergence also dissolves quantum immortality without collapse. Saunders's point that the mathematics may not settle overlap versus divergence means the Everettian can decline the representation the Map's objection needs.
- **Retained, as framework boundaries.** (1) *Haecceity versus qualitative matching*: the Map holds that being this subject is a non-qualitative fact, while quantum modal realism grounds de re modality in counterpart similarity. (2) *Global exclusion*: the Map posits that unchosen alternatives occur nowhere, while Wilson makes them real but non-actual from here. (3) *Selection*: the Map's [[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|bidirectional interaction]], in its outcome-selecting form, needs genuine indeterminacy, and diverging Everettianism has none.

None of the three is a refutation earned inside Wilson's system. Each is a place where the Map's starting commitments differ from his, and [[tenets#^occams-limits|Tenet 5]] rules out settling the difference by counting worlds. What the diverging view clarifies is where the Map's anti-Everett case really rests. It does not rest on a puzzle about fission. It rests on the claims that there is exactly one actual history and that being *this* subject is a further fact. This is the same relocation the [[many-worlds-argument|formal argument]] records for branch-relative identity, and the [[one-world-wager|one-world wager]] counts both claims as costs the Map chooses to carry.
