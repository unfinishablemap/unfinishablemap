On the Map's dualist framework, control-theoretic sophistication is **orthogonal to phenomenality** in a deliberately narrow sense: the control mathematics is substrate-indifferent, and possessing it is not sufficient for feeling. Integral feedback, robust (near-perfect) adaptation, and robust homeostasis are domain-general control competencies, realised in systems no one credits with experience—an industrial PID loop end to end, an operational-amplifier integrator for the integral operation itself—and installable, by synthetic-biology protocol, in a living cell. Four claims need separating. Integral feedback is not *sufficient* for experience; it is not *identical* to experience; whether it *contributes to* an experience-supporting organisation is open; and whether its presence is *evidence* of experience depends on background theory. The op-amp floor refutes the first two; the rivals engaged below contest the last two. This concept isolates the narrow orthogonality as a **reusable primitive**: a coherence commitment the Map carries across cases, so each new instance of exquisite regulation is not re-litigated from scratch.

The primitive matters because "it regulates robustly" is one of the most persuasive-sounding routes from mechanism to mind. A cell that tracks a setpoint, remembers where it has been, and corrects its own errors looks, described in the right vocabulary, like an agent with interests. The [[#the-primitive|control-theoretic account]] (given below) drains that appearance of its inferential force without denying any of the behaviour: every competency is real, and none of it licenses the step from regulation to feeling. What the competency is evidence *of* in a living system is a further, theory-laden question—a defeasible functional marker at best, decisive nowhere. The strongest opposing view—the [[#the-rival|homeostasis-and-feeling family]] (Man & Damasio, Seth, allostasis theorists)—ties a specific kind of regulation constitutively to affect, and is engaged fairly below rather than caricatured.

## Integral Feedback and Robust Perfect Adaptation: The Control Primitive {#the-primitive}

Robust perfect adaptation is a control system's capacity to return a specified output asymptotically to its setpoint after a persistent disturbance of a defined class, and despite variation in its own parameters, given closed-loop stability and operation within its dynamic range. The engineering strategy that delivers it is **integral feedback control**: the controller integrates the error (the running difference between output and setpoint) over time and drives that accumulated error to zero.

Yi, Huang, Simon and Doyle (2000) established the primitive's biological reach by showing that integral control is structurally inherent in the Barkai–Leibler model of chemotactic adaptation, under assumptions they identify—a model-structural result, not an organism-level theorem. In their framing, "Integral feedback control is a basic engineering strategy for ensuring that the output of a system robustly tracks its desired value independent of noise or variations in system parameters." They argue "that integral control in some form is necessary for a robust implementation of perfect adaptation," and generalise: "integral control may underlie the robustness of many homeostatic mechanisms." The structural precondition was supplied by Barkai and Leibler (1997), who showed that robustness of adaptation follows from network *connectivity* rather than fine-tuned parameter values—confirmed experimentally by Alon, Surette, Barkai and Leibler (1999). A robustness nuance the primitive must preserve: what is robust is the *precision* of adaptation (the return-to-baseline setpoint), not the *kinetics* (how fast it returns). "Near-perfect" names the precision claim, carefully—and a finite range: *E. coli* adaptation grows imprecise at high attractant concentrations as receptor methylation sites saturate (Neumann et al. 2014), and in noisy networks the population-mean precision survives, traded against variance and transient performance (Briat, Gupta & Khammash 2016; Olsman et al. 2019).

The philosophically decisive point—decisive against one inference, not against every rival—is that integral control is substrate-indifferent. The same mathematical relation is realised by an operational-amplifier circuit performing literal integration, by the integral channel of an industrial PID loop holding a process variable on setpoint, and by a biochemical network methylating a receptor. It names a dynamical property, not a kind of stuff and not a kind of inner life. [[functionalism|Organisational functionalism]] (Putnam, Block) is untouched, and should be said to be: it individuates mental states by total functional organisation, not by one operation, so it *predicts* that the op-amp feels nothing, as do structural theories such as [[integrated-information-theory|integrated information theory]] for a three-component circuit. The floor refutes the inference from the local motif to feeling, not those views.

## The Domain-General Ladder

The primitive recurs across an unbroken ladder of systems, and the ladder is what makes the orthogonality vivid. It begins with devices no one credits with experience and climbs, without a break in the control mathematics, to organisms whose phenomenal status is actively contested:

- **On-off and PID controllers.** The simplest regulators sit *below* the primitive: a household thermostat is a bang-bang device—if temperature is under setpoint the heat is on, otherwise off—and has no integral term at all. An industrial PID controller adds one, accumulating error over time to hold a process variable on setpoint. Both are textbook regulators, wholly transparent, with no candidate for felt experience anywhere in them.
- **Op-amp integrators.** An analog circuit can implement the integral term *as such*—the very operation Yi et al. identify in the bacterium—from an amplifier, a resistor and a capacitor.
- **Synthetic gene circuits.** Aoki, Lillacci, Gupta and colleagues in the Khammash group (2019) engineered an *antithetic integral feedback* controller into living *E. coli*, conferring robust perfect adaptation by design. The competency is portable, on their qualifiers: the theorem covers "arbitrary intracellular networks with noisy dynamics" for the population average and the time average of single cells; the experiment demonstrated one construct, growth-rate control in *E. coli*. This is also the rung where the ladder leaves uncontested ground: the host is a living cell, and cellular-basis-of-consciousness theorists (Reber and Baluška) hold that living cells already feel. What survives that disagreement is the engineerability point, which concerns the *installation* of the competency rather than the host's phenomenal status.
- **Bacterial chemotaxis.** [[bacterial-chemotaxis-and-minimal-biogenic-cognition|The single-cell instance]]—sensing, short-term memory, and adaptive decision compressed into one neuron-free cell—is one worked example on this ladder, not its subject. The Map treats it in depth elsewhere; here it is a rung.
- **Physiological allostasis.** Whole-organism regulation of internal states sits at the top of the ladder and is where the rival theories of feeling locate their claim.

The ladder has no joint at which the control mathematics changes character—at one grain. Rungs can share a *local transfer function* (the op-amp and the bacterium do), an *end-to-end regulatory architecture* (the PID process and the engineered cell come closer), or a *total causal organisation* (no two rungs do, and that is the grain at which [[phenomenology-vs-function-axis|the phenomenology-function axis]] locates the real dispute with functionalism). The floor comparison holds at the first grain and says nothing at the third. A property that can be soldered together from op-amps, or installed into a cell by a synthetic-biology protocol, is a structural and dynamical fact. Its presence in an organism is therefore a defeasible functional marker at best—and decisive nowhere—for whether that organism feels.

## The Orthogonality Thesis as a Reusable Primitive

Three considerations make the orthogonality claim a primitive the Map can reuse rather than re-derive case by case.

**The integrator floor.** Integral control is implemented in devices that plainly feel nothing: a three-component analog circuit performs the very operation Yi et al. find in the bacterium. If the *same* control property carried phenomenality, that circuit would qualify—so the property itself cannot be what carries phenomenality. Any argument of the form "this system regulates robustly, therefore it experiences" over-generates: it licenses attributing experience to the op-amp. The floor holds even against the most inflationary rival in view: cellular-basis-of-consciousness theorists tie sentience to *life*, so they decline the op-amp too—which is exactly why the reductio bites at the circuit and not at the engineered cell. The enactivist sharpens that concession: what the cell has and the circuit lacks is *endogenous normativity*—an autonomous, precarious organisation whose setpoints matter to it because failure ends it (Di Paolo 2005; Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde 2009). The Map grants that this changes the comparison class above the local grain. It does not grant that precariousness derives phenomenality, and here the disagreement is closer to bedrock than a refutation would be: the reply below treats a self-maintaining organisation as a more elaborate controller, and [[enactivism-challenge-to-interactionist-dualism#Autopoietic Enactivism|autopoietic enactivism]] denies exactly that step, holding that self-production already constitutes a point of view. What survives is a standing reductio against the upward inference. The mirror argument over-generates just as badly, and the Map is bound by that one. "The mechanism is complete, therefore nothing is felt" would license denying experience in humans, whose behaviour is—on the physicalist assumptions such an argument borrows—likewise explicable without functional residue. Mechanistic completeness no more settles the phenomenal question downward than robust regulation settles it upward, and the second reductio constrains the Map's own floor verdicts exactly as the first constrains its opponents' inflationary ones.

**Engineerability.** Robust perfect adaptation can be wired into a molecular circuit deliberately (Aoki et al. 2019). A capacity you can install by design is a fact about dynamics and connectivity, not evidence of an inner point of view that the wiring somehow summons.

**Explanatory closure.** In each case on the ladder, the classical control loop leaves no functional residue that an experiencer is required to fill. Every term in the description—"senses," "tracks a setpoint," "adapts," "corrects error"—names a dynamical feature; none names anything felt. This defeats the picture of experience as a homuncular gap-filler in the loop, not views on which experience is identical with, realised by, or lawfully associated with a richer organisation; those are met above at the grain distinction and below at the rival. The gap between the complete control story and the question of experience is exactly the explanatory gap, untouched by the sophistication of the regulation.

The primitive's job is economy. Once the narrow orthogonality is fixed at the level of the control principle, the Map need not re-fight the inference at every rung. New examples of remarkable regulation—in immune signalling, in plant tropisms, in engineered agents—inherit a commitment rather than a finding: the regulation is granted in full, and the phenomenal inference is marked as unlicensed by the mechanism. On the Map's own evidential ladder the primitive is *interface-compatible, not interface-discriminating*: evidence supports a reading over rivals only when it names a feature the rivals do not equally predict ([[born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface#Compatibility vs. Support|compatibility versus support]]), and unfelt regulation is predicted by the Map and by every rival below. The same rule binds the Map's own markers—the [[cross-modal-capability-division|cross-modal capability division]] is held as consonant-not-probative, and P-M1 forbids tenet-grounded coherence from upgrading itself into evidence.

## The Homeostasis-and-Feeling Rival {#the-rival}

The strongest opposing view holds that regulation, or a specific kind of it, is constitutively tied to feeling or is the route by which feeling arises. It deserves statement at full strength.

Kingson Man and Antonio Damasio (2019) argue that an intelligent agent should hold self-preservation as a meta-goal grounded in homeostasis, and propose that a machine implementing a homeostasis-resembling process—especially under genuine bodily vulnerability, via soft-robotic embodiment—might thereby acquire a source of motivation and a means of evaluating its behaviour akin to feelings in living organisms. Anil Seth and Manos Tsakiris (2018) ground selfhood in *control-oriented interoceptive inference*: experience of being an embodied self is the form that predictive regulation of the body's internal states takes from the inside. Allostasis theorists (Sterling; Barrett & Simmons) distinguish *predictive* regulation—the brain anticipating needs before errors occur—from reactive homeostatic feedback, and route valence and arousal through that predictive-control architecture. Its strongest current form is active inference: Laukkonen, Friston and Chandaria (2025) locate minimal experience in a recursive, precision-weighted self-model and extend it downward—"our model seems to suggest that consciousness clearly precedes introspection or complex metacognition … Even a tiny agent can incorporate ongoing feedback from within its own inferential machinery." That claim targets the very rung this article describes as able to run dark, and on the rival's home ground: the top of the ladder is where these theories locate feeling, not neutral territory.

Crucially, the rival does not claim that *all* control implies feeling. The claim is restricted to embodied, self-jeopardising, predictive regulation of an organism's own continued existence. A thermostat is explicitly excluded: it has no stake, no body that can die, no self-model. The disanalogy with the op-amp is, for the rival, principled rather than ad hoc. The Map develops the interoceptive side of this debate in [[interoceptive-consciousness-and-the-interface|interoceptive consciousness and the interface]] and its own regulatory account in [[the-psychophysical-control-law|the psychophysical control law]], and cites them here rather than re-deriving the position. These authors build *physicalist* models; the Map may engage their mechanics but must not enlist them as allies for a dualist conclusion they reject.

## The Honest Reply: The Restriction Relocates the Gap

The Map's reply is not that control can never accompany feeling—that would beg the question against the restricted claim. The honest reply is that the rival's restrictions *relocate* the explanatory gap without closing it.

"Genuine vulnerability," "self-model," "embodiment," and "prediction" are themselves functional and organisational notions. A system that models its own viability, predicts threats to it, and is materially destructible under load is a system with a more elaborate control architecture: richer than a thermostat's, but the same *kind* of thing—the step the autopoietic enactivist denies, as noted above. Adding these conditions specifies *which* regulators the rival cares about; it does not explain why any regulator, however self-referential and predictive, is *accompanied by* experience rather than running dark. The restricted claim supplies a correlation-rich **bridge principle**—wherever this architecture, there feeling—not a derivation from the architecture to the feeling. The question the hard problem asks is left standing: why is it *like* anything to be this predictive homeostat, when the predictive homeostasis is fully specified without remainder? Against active inference the reply is the same relocation, graded honestly as a framework-boundary stalemate rather than a refutation: the Map says the recursive self-model is specified without remainder, Laukkonen and colleagues say the precision-weighted loop at sufficient depth *is* the feeling, and each claim is internal to its own theory. The control mathematics both sides accept does not decide between them.

This is the same wedge the Map drives across its competency cluster, and it is the mirror image of a use the Map makes of control theory in the other direction. [[control-theoretic-will|Control-theoretic will]] runs the analogy *toward* consciousness—modelling the will as a low-bandwidth controller steering neural dynamics—while treating that vocabulary as a modelling tool that never settles the ontology by itself. This concept runs the analogy the other way, using control theory to show what regulation *fails to entail*. Both draw on the same primitive: the controller/plant description is metaphysically neutral, so no amount of it constitutes, or rules out, an inner life. The neutrality that keeps control-theoretic will from *proving* dualism is the same neutrality that keeps robust adaptation from *proving* sentience.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^dualism|Tenet 1 (Dualism)]]** grounds the narrow orthogonality and nothing wider. Dualism grounds non-constitution: if phenomenality is not reducible to functional or physical organisation, no quantity of control-theoretic organisation can *constitute* it. It does not ground the absence of psychophysical regularities: Chalmers' organisational invariance is itself a property-dualist thesis, and a dualist who holds that psychophysical laws exist must allow functional organisation to be evidence about where experience occurs. Irreducibility licenses the claim that the organisation is not the experience, not evidential independence—even inside the Map's framework. Integral feedback is organisation of exactly the kind the hard problem shows to be insufficient: fully specifiable, substrate-portable, and silent on the first-person question. The Map's neural-interface commitment *licenses* unfelt regulation wherever the interface substrate is absent—a license the framework grants, not a proof it exhibits.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits)]]** disciplines the conclusion in both directions, and the symmetry it enforces is the article's conclusion. The elegance of an integral-control explanation does not license inferring the *absence* of experience any more than the presence of regulation licenses inferring its *presence*: simplicity is not truth-tracking here. The primitive therefore claims something narrow: robust adaptation underdetermines the phenomenal question symmetrically—not sufficient for feeling, its mechanistic completeness not sufficient for feeling's absence, its evidential weight in a given organism set by background theory. That is smaller than evidential independence, and it is what the arguments here earn.

The concept also bears on **[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction)]]**, which locates consciousness's causal role at a specific interface. The explanatory closure claimed above is closure of the *classical control description*. The Map's bidirectional influence enters elsewhere—at post-decoherence selection (positions register P-Q1), below the grain at which a controller is specified—and [[the-psychophysical-control-law|the psychophysical control law]] records that commitment alone does not constitute specification. The classical loop is closed, and the posited selection concerns which classically permitted trajectory is realised rather than a term missing from the loop; there is no oscillation between the two. Robust regulation is precisely the kind of competence that can run without that interface, so its presence cannot mark where the interface bottoms out. What control theory contributes to the Map's positive programme is a vocabulary for what a genuine controller *does*; what it cannot contribute is a discriminating test of whether the controller is felt.
