The *intrinsic nature* of the physical is what matter is in itself, as opposed to what it does. Physics describes matter relationally and structurally—mass by its gravitational and inertial behaviour, charge by its electromagnetic interactions, a particle by its dispositions to affect other particles. None of this says what *bears* these dispositions. The intrinsic nature is the categorical base that instantiates the relational structure physics maps. In its modern form the distinction is Bertrand Russell's (1927) and Arthur Eddington's (1928). The term for the intrinsic properties themselves—*quiddities*, the "whatness" of fundamental entities, the qualities in virtue of which they enter into the relations physics describes—is a later import into the debate: Armstrong (1989) seems to have coined *quidditism*, Black (2000) argued against that view by name, and Lewis (2009) is a standard source.

The distinction matters because, on the structuralist reading of physics the Map adopts, physics cannot reach it: structural description is its method, and structure all that method expresses. That reading generates three downstream problems that recur across the Map: the **inscrutability problem** (intrinsic natures cannot be read off physical theory, however complete), the **Russellian opening** (if consciousness is the one intrinsic nature we know directly, perhaps intrinsic nature and consciousness coincide), and the **placement question** (where, in an ontology, does the non-physical aspect of reality live). The Map's answer to the third is that the non-physical aspect belongs at the level of the intrinsic base—though, as the [[#relation-to-site-perspective|Relation to Site Perspective]] section explains, the Map places consciousness there as a *distinct interacting aspect* rather than identifying it with quiddities as Russellian monism does.

## The Structural/Intrinsic Distinction

Russell drew the distinction sharply in *The Analysis of Matter*: physics tells us about the structure of the physical world—the pattern of relations among its parts—but is silent about the intrinsic character of whatever stands in those relations. In his words: "Physics, in itself, is exceedingly abstract, and reveals only certain mathematical characteristics of the material with which it deals. It does not tell us anything as to the intrinsic character of this material" (p. 10). Eddington put it as a contrast between the "world of shadows" (p. xvi) that physics symbolises and the unknown reality the symbols stand for.

The core observation is robust across physics as practised. As physics has matured it has become *more* abstract and relational, not less, though the trend does not favour the quidditist over the structural realist, who reads it as evidence that there is nothing but structure. Quantum field theory and general relativity specify the relations among quantities with extraordinary precision while saying nothing about the intrinsic nature of what is so related. On the structuralist reading, a complete future physics would be a complete account of structure and still silent on intrinsic nature, the silence belonging to the method rather than to current theory. That permanence is a claim about the *method* of physics rather than a theorem—[[physical-completeness|physical completeness]] calls it a bet on the method-claim, not a proof—and the [[ignorance-hypothesis|ignorance hypothesis]] is the physicalist view that bets the other way.

Many Map articles build on this premise without re-deriving it. On the structuralist reading, physics describes:

- **Relational properties** — how an entity stands to other entities (distance, force, field interaction)
- **Dispositional properties** — how an entity is disposed to behave under conditions (mass as resistance to acceleration)
- **Structural properties** — the higher-order pattern these relations and dispositions form

Physics does *not* describe:

- **Quiddities** — the absolutely intrinsic, categorical natures that bear the dispositions and occupy the structural roles

The Stanford Encyclopedia cautions that the structural/non-structural distinction is not the relational/intrinsic one: a ball's sphericality is non-relational, yet physics characterises it (Alter and Pereboom 2023). The lists use Pereboom's *absolutely* intrinsic (2015), meaning not grounded in extrinsic properties of the thing or its parts; sphericality, and mass as physics specifies it, are only relatively intrinsic.

The *ontic* structural realist holds that structure is all there is—there are no quiddities, only relations "all the way down." The quidditist holds that something must instantiate the structure: relations require relata, dispositions require bearers, a role requires an occupant. The Map sides with the quidditist, and should be exact about how far the siding goes. The relations-without-relata objection is tempting but not decisive: moderate ontic structural realism (Esfeld and Lam 2008) keeps relata while denying them intrinsic identity, which absorbs the objection while retaining the part that matters here—no quiddities. So this is a considered preference against a live rival rather than the rejection of an incoherence, and [[ontic-structural-realism|ontic structural realism]] sets out the rival, the narrower asymmetry the Map can actually claim, and why Chalmers is more generous to these views than the Map has been. The positive reading is developed in [[bi-aspectual-ontology|bi-aspectual ontology]] and reflected in the [[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]] tenet.

The bearer premise has a second rival, which rejects it outright. Pure-powers theorists give every fundamental property a dispositional essence (Bird 2007), so a disposition needs no categorical base: it is its own bearer. Powerful-qualities theorists, the Map among them ([[causal-powers]]), deny the occupant/role split rather than supply an occupant: quality and power are one property. The Map's siding therefore rests on phenomenal character, which it takes to be exhausted by no causal role ([[consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions#The Pandispositionalist Challenge|the pandispositionalist challenge]]), and not on "dispositions require bearers," which pure-powers theorists decline without incoherence. That tells against powers all the way down. Crediting mass and charge with a non-structural nature too is a preference, which a powerful quality satisfies as well as a categorical quiddity does.

## Hempel's Dilemma and What "Physical" Means

The distinction bears on a problem about the word *physical* itself. [[hempels-dilemma|Carl Hempel's dilemma]] (Hempel 1980) asks what "physical" means in claims like "everything is physical." If it means *what current physics describes*, the claim is probably false, since current physics is incomplete and acknowledged to be so. If it means *what a completed future physics will describe*, the claim is, in Montero's phrase, "impossibly vague" (Montero 2003): the charge is not that it cannot be tested but that its content waits on a physics no one can yet specify, which might include fundamentally mental properties.

The dilemma is a problem for defining "physical" by reference to physics, and it gives no independent evidence that physics omits quiddities; Montero judges it not fatal on its own even to such definitions. Russellian physicalists add that quiddities may themselves be physical (Pereboom 2015), and on readings of "physical" built on a no-fundamental-mentality condition (Montero 2003; Wilson 2006), non-mental quiddities can count. On readings that are not Russellian-physicalist, and granting the structuralist reading of physics, neither horn reaches quiddities: "everything is physical" is then either a claim about structure (true but partial) or a claim about a complete ontology that structural physics is in no position to underwrite. Montero and Papineau (2005) put the same dilemma to the [[causal-closure|causal-closure principle]]: over current physics it is "likely to be false", over a future physics "irredeemably vague". Neither horn tells against the closure that matters to the mind–body debate, closure of the non-mental, which is a determinate thesis ([[hempels-dilemma#limits|what the dilemma cannot do]]).

## The Inscrutability of Quiddities

If intrinsic natures exist but physics cannot reach them, can anything? The *inscrutability problem* (sometimes the quiddity-inscrutability or humility thesis, following Lewis 2009 and Langton 1998 on Kantian humility) says no, at least from the outside. Two distinct quiddities could occupy the same structural role—ground the same dispositions, enter the same relations—and be indistinguishable to any physical measurement, because measurement detects only relational effects. This role-swap is the quidditist's permutation premise, and on Hildebrand's distinction (2016) swapped worlds are indistinguishable even to their inhabitants under bare quidditism but can differ qualitatively under qualitative quidditism; the exception below needs the second. Granting the premise and the structuralist reading of physics, this *quiddistic indeterminacy* hides the categorical base behind the structure it instantiates.

The inscrutability has a notable exception, and the exception is the engine of the Russellian programme. There is one region of reality whose intrinsic nature we may know not by structural inference but by being it: our own conscious experience. We know what red looks like by seeing it, what pain is like by feeling it—non-relationally, from the inside. If consciousness is intrinsic nature directly acquainted, it is the one quiddity not hidden behind structure. This asymmetry—structure-only knowledge of everything external, intrinsic acquaintance with consciousness—is the hinge on which Russellian monism turns. The hinge is also the programme's [[revelation-thesis|most contested premise]]: that introspection delivers a *quiddity* rather than itself a structural or representational state is precisely what eliminativists and representationalists deny, and the [[phenomenal-acquaintance|phenomenal-acquaintance]] relation it presupposes is doing heavy lifting it may not be able to bear. The asymmetry is the Russellian's strongest move, not a settled result.

The [[intrinsic-nature-void|intrinsic-nature void]] develops the negative side: the inscrutability may be a permanent boundary, where matter's unknown intrinsic nature and consciousness's unknown substrate converge in territory neither physics nor introspection can map.

## From Distinction to Theory: The Russellian Move

The structural/intrinsic distinction is a premise, not yet a theory of mind. Russellian monism is the theory that builds on it: physics describes structure, consciousness is the one intrinsic nature we know, so perhaps intrinsic natures *are* phenomenal (panpsychist Russellian monism) or *ground* the phenomenal (panprotopsychist Russellian monism). The intrinsic base, on this view, just is where consciousness lives—not a separate substance but the categorical inside of the physical.

This article deliberately stops at the distinction and the placement question; the theory and its difficulties—the combination problem, the instability between physicalism and dualism (Cutter 2019), the parity argument (Pautz 2017), the epiphenomenalism worry (Howell 2015), and the fragmentation into incompatible variants—are treated in [[russellian-monism]]. The distinction is a building block, and [[disguised-property-dualism]] argues that several "monisms" built on it are dualisms in disguise once their two irreducible property-types (structural and quidditative) are made explicit.

## Relation to Site Perspective {#relation-to-site-perspective}

The intrinsic-nature distinction is one of the Map's most important borrowed tools, and the Map's reading diverges from Russellian monism's at a precise point.

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]].** The Map accepts the distinction's core: physics is structurally complete but ontologically partial (see [[physical-completeness]]). The Map locates the non-physical aspect of reality at the intrinsic base—but as a *distinct, interacting* aspect rather than as the quiddities of physical particles. [[bi-aspectual-ontology|Bi-aspectual ontology]] frames this: structure (what physics describes) and actuality (supplied by consciousness at neural interfaces) are two irreducible aspects. Physics is missing an ontology, on the Map's reading, not merely the inside of the ontology it already describes.

The divergence is drawn from inside the Map's model, and turns on separating two identities. Identity *within* a phenomenal powerful quality, a felt quality that is also a power, is what the Map relies on for [[consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions|causal efficacy]]. Identity of consciousness *with physical quiddities* is the Russellian claim it declines: consciousness would then be wherever structure is, grounding every physical disposition, and could not be the further, local factor that Tenets 2–4 have selecting among outcomes at [[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|quantum indeterminacies]] in neural systems while physics alone fixes them elsewhere. That makes Russellian identity incompatible with the Map's interface model; it does not show identity theories causally idle, and Russellian monists answer that charge (Alter and Pereboom 2023). "At the intrinsic base" therefore means at the level of fundamental ontology that structural description leaves out: the intrinsic base is everywhere, while the actuality consciousness supplies is [[bi-aspectual-ontology|local to neural interfaces]].

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]].** The distinction is a standing illustration of this tenet. The simplest hypothesis—that structural physics exhausts reality—is cheaper than the Map's, and the question is what the economy costs rather than whether the hypothesis collapses on inspection. The Map treats this as a case where parsimony is unreliable under incomplete knowledge: adding intrinsic nature looks like an ontological cost, while denying it risks buying the tidier ontology at the price of determinacy—the pressure point [[ontic-structural-realism|ontic structural realism]] locates in Newman's problem, that structure alone fixes little more than cardinality and cannot say which relations are real. Newman raised that objection against Russell's *epistemic* structuralism, which is the Map's own position, so what it yields is an asymmetry in available replies rather than a refutation of the rival. The epistemic reading can place the individuator in the nature beneath the structure, where the ontic reading must find one inside it, but the Map then owes an account of that individuator and has none.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]] and [[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]].** Whatever occupies the intrinsic base must, for the Map, be the kind of thing that can support causal power and influence quantum outcomes. An intrinsic nature that was merely categorical and inert would re-import epiphenomenalism, so the Map reads the base in powerful-qualities terms, as causally contributing rather than as a placeholder a law refers to.

**A discipline note on what the distinction earns.** The intrinsic-nature gap is a point about the *limits of physical description*, not positive evidence that consciousness occupies the gap. Following the [[evidential-status-discipline|evidential-status discipline]], the distinction *removes a defeater*—it shows that "physics describes everything" does not entail "physics describes everything that exists"—but it does not by itself *establish* that the non-physical aspect is real or that it lives at the intrinsic base. The Map's dualism rests on the combination of this gap with the hard problem and the anti-physicalist arguments, not on the gap alone. The gap creates room; it does not furnish the room.

## What Would Challenge This View

The Map's use of the intrinsic-nature distinction would be undermined if: (1) a structuralism accounted for individuation, modality and causation without independently individuated categorical natures, leaving quiddities no work to do; (2) a pure-powers metaphysics met the pandispositionalist challenge, removing the phenomenal case on which the Map's siding with the quidditist rests; or (3) the Russellian identification of consciousness *with* quiddities proved more economical than the Map's distinct-aspect reading once both ledgers are counted, the Russellian's combination problem against the subject-pairing law the Map owes and does not have (P-SC2).

The second and third have not been achieved, on the Map's assessment: it knows of no pure-powers account that meets the pandispositionalist challenge, and the combination problem, pressed since James (1890), has [[combination-problem|constructive programmes]] that remain contested, while the Map's own pairing debt remains open. The first is live, and [[ontic-structural-realism|ontic structural realism]] gives it the treatment a list of falsifiers cannot. The cost is specific: eliminative ontic structural realism would remove this article's ontological-remainder route—and the parallel argument in [[physical-completeness|physical completeness]]—while leaving [[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]] standing, since the tenet rests on the [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard problem]] and the anti-physicalist arguments rather than on matter having quiddities. And the position does not reach consciousness by itself: carrying "no intrinsic natures" across from matter to experience is the work of [[structuralism-about-phenomenal-character|structuralism about phenomenal character]], a separate opponent with its own literature. So the first falsifier stands unrefuted, though what it would take from the Map is narrower than the list implies.
