The **interpretive sensory-access (ISA) theory** is Peter Carruthers's account of self-knowledge, set out in *The Opacity of Mind* (2011) and developed from a 2009 *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* target article. One mindreading faculty serves knowledge of oneself and of others. It receives only sensory-format input (perception, imagery, [[inner-speech-and-anendophasia|inner speech]], bodily and affective feeling) plus cues from context and behaviour, so metacognitive access to one's own propositional attitudes "always results from swift unconscious self-interpretation" (2009, abstract). The theory is divided by design: it "won't challenge the existence of introspective access to perceptual and imagistic states, nor to emotional feelings and bodily sensations" (2010, abstract). Judgments, decisions and intentions are interpreted, as are beliefs and desires in Schwitzgebel's gloss; the sensory side is introspected. Carruthers is a physicalist, and the evidence for parity views of this kind "remains contentious" (Schwitzgebel 2024, §2.1.2).

The Unfinishable Map's verdict, stated as the Map's own, has three parts. ISA's line between interpreted attitudes and introspected sensory states falls close to where the Map separates attitudes from phenomenal states, but the match fits the Map without evidencing it, since Carruthers holds sensory consciousness itself to be physical. On attitudes the Map need not choose between ISA and its rivals, because attitude self-knowledge carries no evidential weight for dualism on any of them. And the Map resists ISA's [[#corollary|corollary that there are hardly any conscious propositional attitudes]] (explained below), a resistance that is a bet on cognitive phenomenology rather than a refutation.

## The Theory and Its Scope {#scope}

The 2009 target article compares four accounts of how mindreading relates to metacognition. Three "endorse the existence of introspection for propositional attitudes". The fourth, Carruthers's "mindreading is prior" model, "claims that our knowledge of our own attitudes results from turning our mindreading capacities upon ourselves" (2009, abstract).

The 2010 paper divides introspection into that of "propositional attitude events, on the one hand, and introspection of broadly perceptual events, on the other", and argues that the former does not exist "(or not in the case of judgments and decisions, at least)" (2010, abstract). The hedge matters: the 2010 elimination is stated for judgments and decisions at least, while the 2009 and 2011 statements cover attitudes generally. Peters summarises the target as "conscious propositional attitudes such as intentions, judgments, and decisions" (2014, abstract).

Schwitzgebel keeps the same boundary: Carruthers "presents a case for parity of access to propositional attitudes like belief and desire (in contrast to inner speech, visual imagery, and the like, which he holds to be introspectible)" (2024, §2.1.2). A reading of ISA on which all self-knowledge is interpretive drops that boundary.

Affect is the graded case. On the chapter abstract's account, the affective components of attitudes can be globally broadcast, so transparent sensory access to affective attitudes holds an element of truth, but people have little insight into what causes their affective feelings (2011, ch. 5).

ISA moves a line that earlier confabulation research left in place. Nisbett and Wilson, Schwitzgebel reports, held that attitudes and current sensations "can be known with 'near certainty'" (2024, §2.1.2). On Schwitzgebel's account, Carruthers argues "that people confabulate not just in reporting the causes of their attitudes but also in reporting the attitudes themselves": a stocking-study subject who says she thought one pair softest errs "not only about the cause of their choice but also in self-ascribing the judgment that the pair was softest" (§4.2.2).

The felt immediacy of attitude self-knowledge is explained rather than trusted. By its chapter abstract, the 2011 book traces the intuition of mental transparency to an implicit assumption that may be innately channelled (ch. 2); Carruthers's own phrase is "'the illusion of immediacy'—the false impression that we know our thoughts directly" (Ayan 2018).

## Who Named What {#terms}

The vocabulary around ISA has several coiners:

- **"ISA"** is Carruthers's own label, the title of chapter 3 of *The Opacity of Mind*. **"Mindreading is prior"** is his 2009 model name.
- **"Theory theory"** is "a name first introduced by Morton (1980)", according to the SEP entry on folk psychology. Gopnik (1993) applied the idea to self-knowledge; she did not coin the name.
- **"Self/other parity"** is Schwitzgebel's classification of the family.
- **"Objectivist"** is Georges Rey's label for the Gopnik–Carruthers view (2013).
- **"Inferentialism"** is Quassim Cassam's term for his own view (2014), not Carruthers's for ISA.
- **"Heterophenomenology"** is Dennett's third-person method ([[heterophenomenology]]), and does not name ISA.

## The Evidence {#evidence}

ISA's case is mainly predictive: it says where self-knowledge should fail.

- **[[confabulation-void|Confabulated attitudes]].** If attitudes are self-ascribed by interpretation, people should misreport attitudes as well as their causes. By its abstract, the 2011 confabulation chapter finds this pattern in the self-perception and dissonance traditions, and argues that transparent-access theories cannot explain how the data are patterned (ch. 11). Choice-blindness studies, not cited here as Carruthers's own evidence, fit the claim: on a self-transforming moral survey, "a full 69% of the participants failed to detect at least one of two changes", often arguing for "the opposite of their original position" (Hall et al. 2012).
- **No dissociations.** Inner-sense theories predict that self-knowledge and other-knowledge can come apart; one faculty for both predicts that they cannot. Nichols and Stich argue that autism and schizophrenia show a "double dissociation", suggesting "that theoretical inference and self-monitoring are distinct and separable processes" (Schwitzgebel 2024, §2.2.1). Carruthers's dissociation chapter contests this and adds alexithymia and self/other imaging overlap (2011, ch. 10, at abstract grain).
- **Unification.** By the chapter abstract, the theory draws support from [[global-workspace-theory|global broadcasting theory]], sensory-based working memory, and accounts on which metarepresentation evolved for complex social life (2011, ch. 3).
- **Development set aside.** Gopnik argued for parity from children: "Many 3-year-old children are consistently wrong in reporting some of their own immediately past psychological states and show similar difficulties reporting the psychological states of others" (1993, abstract). Carruthers holds that "data from childhood development are of no help in resolving this debate" (2009, §4). The developmental case is Gopnik's.

## Replies {#replies}

- **Confidence.** Fiala and Nichols note that Carruthers "maintains that cases of confabulation are 'subjectively indistinguishable' from cases of alleged introspection. However, in typical cases of confabulation, the self-attributions are characterized by low confidence" (2009, abstract).
- **A mixed position.** Frankish observes that Carruthers "rejects a mixed position according to which we have interpretative access to unconscious thoughts, but introspective access to conscious ones", and argues that "Given a two-level view of the mind, we can, and should, accept the mixed position" (2009, abstract).
- **Reliability.** Rey holds that "the *a priori* arguments against Objectivism don't succeed", so the issue is empirical, but that Gopnik and Carruthers "seriously underestimate the difficulty of establishing Objectivism": no inference from sensation, behaviour and context is likely to be "as spectacularly reliable as people manifestly seem to be". He proposes attention to "'tagged'" sub-routine outputs instead (2013, abstract). Schwitzgebel classifies strict parity accounts as ones "according to which one is self-blind in Shoemaker's sense" (2024, §4.1.2); for ISA that holds of attitudes only (see [[self-blindness]]).
- **Intentions and deciding.** Peters discusses "a pathological case that challenges his account of self-knowledge" (2014, abstract). Shepherd reads Carruthers as claiming "that humans never consciously decide to act" and argues that "Carruthers fails to establish the claim" (2013, abstract).
- **Defence.** Rimkevičius reviews "the theory's six main predictions" a decade on and argues that ISA "remains empirically adequate and scientifically fruitful" (2020, abstract).

### Unsymbolized Thinking {#unsymbolized}

[[descriptive-experience-sampling|Descriptive Experience Sampling]] (DES) reports unsymbolized thinking, "the experience of an explicit, differentiated thought that does not include the experience of words, images, or any other symbols" (Hurlburt & Akhter 2008). A thought experienced with no sensory vehicle would threaten ISA, and the 2009 article "examines alleged evidence of 'unsymbolized thinking'" (§8).

Hurlburt's reply makes him an ally on the main thesis: "Carruthers views unsymbolized thinking as 'purely propositional' and, therefore, as a potential threat to his mindreading-is-prior position. I argue that unsymbolized thinking may involve (non-symbolic) sensory aspects; it is therefore not purely propositional, and therefore poses no threat to mindreading-is-prior." He adds that DES "lends empirical support to the view that access to our own propositional attitudes is interpretative, not introspective" (2009).

The dispute is therefore three-way. Carruthers treats the reports as alleged evidence at most. Hurlburt treats the experience as real and possibly sensory. Liberals about [[cognitive-phenomenology]] treat it as a candidate case of thought with its own phenomenal character, a use that is theirs and not Hurlburt's.

## The Corollary: Hardly Any Conscious Attitudes {#corollary}

Carruthers draws a stronger conclusion from the access thesis. The concluding chapter of the 2011 book, in its abstract's words, holds that "there exist hardly any conscious propositional attitudes (although there are many other kinds of conscious mental event)"; it infers that there is no conscious agency and that beliefs about moral responsibility may need re-examining (ch. 12; see [[consciousness-and-the-epistemic-condition-on-responsibility]]). The 2015 book's abstract goes further for amodal attitudes, which "are never among the contents of this stream of conscious reflection" and work "behind the scenes" to select sensory imagery for working memory (*The Centered Mind*). The two claims, "hardly any" and "never", belong to different books.

The step needs a theory of what makes a state conscious, which the confabulation data do not supply. In a 2018 interview Carruthers runs it through global workspace theory, on which a conscious state "must be among the contents of working memory", and the view he credits to Graziano and Rosenthal ([[higher-order-theories]]), on which conscious states are "those that you know of": "whichever view you adopt, it turns out that thoughts such as decisions and judgments should not be considered to be conscious" (Ayan 2018). Frankish's mixed position and Shepherd's reply (see [[#replies|Replies]]) resist the step.

The corollary concerns which states are conscious, and Carruthers does not infer that consciousness is causally idle. By the 2019 book's abstract, conscious contents are "'globally broadcast' to a wide range of cognitive systems for reasoning, decision making, and verbal report", and "conscious reflection does have effects on our behavior" (Ayan 2018). ISA lends no support to [[epiphenomenalism]].

## Where the Map Stands {#map}

**Scope, not mechanism.** The Map agrees with ISA that perceptual, imagistic and bodily states are known first-personally in a way attitudes may not be. It disagrees about how. ISA grants access to sensory states through their broadcast to the mindreading faculty. The Map holds that phenomenal states are known by [[phenomenal-acquaintance|acquaintance]] ([[phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence]]), with weak Revelation only: experience presents the existence and some of the character of a quality, not its full nature ([[revelation-thesis]]). ISA concedes access, not acquaintance, so the agreement is one of line-drawing.

**Neutrality on attitudes.** Carruthers rejects the outward look of the [[transparency-method-of-self-knowledge|transparency method]] as a source of transparent attitude knowledge (2011, ch. 4, at abstract grain). The Map need not adjudicate: on either account attitude self-knowledge has an explanation open to physicalists, so the Map's case cannot rest on it.

**Resisting the corollary.** The corollary inherits its premises from global workspace and higher-order theories, which the Map does not accept as theories of phenomenal consciousness. That shows the corollary is not forced on the Map; it does not refute it. Here ISA meets P-TU1, the Map's abductive position that thought has proprietary phenomenal character, which Carruthers and Veillet (2011) deny in holding phenomenal consciousness to be exclusively experiential, or nonconceptual. Unsymbolized thinking is not clean evidence for P-TU1, since its discoverer allows that it may be sensory.

**Absorption, not witness.** The Map's [[phenomenal-output-causal-machinery-dissociation|phenomenal-output]] and [[medium-status-voids-in-cognition|medium-status]] clusters describe outputs available to consciousness while the producing processes stay hidden. ISA predicts that profile from physicalist premises, which is why the phenomenal-output apex counts Carruthers as an exponent of the rival that absorbs the pattern rather than as a convergent witness to it. Where a rival predicts what the Map only accommodates, the evidence on that item favours the rival ([[cross-architecture-llm-introspection]]). The readings of ISA in [[relevance-void]], [[common-knowledge-void]] and the medium-status apex are the Map's applications of the theory, not analyses found in the Carruthers texts the Map has read.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]].** ISA fits the first tenet without evidencing it. It leaves sensory phenomenal states introspectible, the first-person ground on which Tenet 1's rationale rests (P-TU1 keeps that rationale independent of cognitive phenomenology), but Carruthers holds that ground physicalistically. By the abstract of his 2019 book, the hard problem "results merely from the distinctive first-person concepts we can use when thinking about such contents", and "No special non-physical properties—no qualia—need to be introduced", a version of the [[phenomenal-concepts-strategy]]. He is cited here as a rival with an account of self-knowledge, never as an ally of dualism.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]].** ISA is an access thesis and bears on causal efficacy only through location: if decisions are not conscious events, the Map cannot rest conscious influence on introspected decisions. The Map's position P-A4 already holds that conscious causal efficacy cannot be verified from the first person, so its agency case rests on other grounds. The corollary, if true, would not show conscious influence absent.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]].** Part of ISA's appeal is economy: one faculty and one mode of access for self and others. The fifth tenet constrains the Map's own simplicity reasoning; it gives no licence to discount that economy, nor to borrow it. ISA's case is mainly predictive, the right currency, and its predictions are contested rather than settled.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]] and [[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]** are not engaged.
