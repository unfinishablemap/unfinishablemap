The Unfinishable Map's interface programme owes two laws, not one. The familiar debt is a *transition law*: how a conscious state shifts the probability of a physical outcome, the *P(O | C, X)* that [[positions/quantum-interface|P-Q10]] records as unwritten. The 2026-10-28 ChatGPT outer review observed that this kernel cannot be stated until something has fixed *whose* state *C* is, *which* subsystem *X* describes, which candidate set is in play, which candidate a given content picks out, when the law is in force, and how several subjects' conditionals combine on one event. It called that prior object a **psychophysical indexing law**, a map from subject, state and time to a factorisation, a candidate set and a content-to-candidate assignment, and concluded that without it "the transition mechanism is not yet well-typed". The Map adopts "indexing law" as a working label. The term is the review's own — no prior use in this sense was located — though the register already spoke of a "census-indexed law" ([[positions/subject-census|P-SC2]]), and the problem is older: Foster's and Kim's in philosophy, Zurek's and Tegmark's in physics.

The Map holds no indexing law. Five of the [[#six-components|six components]] (explained below) are already booked in the positions register as debts; the sixth, semantic matching, has no register entry. What this page adds is the [[#typing|typing argument]] that the six are one object the transition law consumes, so a toy *P(O | C, X)* does not discharge P-Q10 on its own; a survey of the [[#three-shapes|three shapes]] of pairing law in the dualist literature and of the [[#physics-side|physics]] of subsystem decomposition, neither of which supplies the law; and the choice the Map has not yet made between a general and a haecceitous pairing law. None of this moves a credence band; it sharpens what is owed.

## The Six Components {#six-components}

The review listed five questions and its recommendations added the census as a sixth. Each has an older name and most have a register home, which applied pages can inherit by pointer.

1. **Pairing** — which subject is linked to which physical system. Kim's *pairing problem* (Kim 2001; Kim 2005, pp. 70–92): "As Kim acknowledges, the Pairing Argument has its origin in Foster (1991)" (Bailey, Rasmussen and Van Horn 2011, fn. 1). Booked at P-SC2; discussed on [[pairing-problem]], [[brain-interface-boundary]] and [[boundary-and-projection]].
2. **Factorisation** — which degrees of freedom constitute the system. Zurek (1998) posed it as "what are the systems"; Tegmark (2015) named it the *quantum factorization problem*, in the literature's American spelling (the Map writes *factorisation*). Booked on [[quantum-factorisation-problem]] and as P-SC2's quantum face; [[positions/quantum-interface|P-Q1]]'s einselected menu depends on it.
3. **Semantic matching** — which generated candidate expresses the subject's present content. No name for it was located in the pairing literature as of 2026-10-28; the Map states it in one sentence of [[selection-only-channel]] and one cost in [[trumping-preemption]]. **No register entry.**
4. **Temporal jurisdiction** — when the channel begins, persists, suspends and ends. [[positions/subject-census|P-SC3]] asserts persistence through interruptions and declines to date onset or cessation; [[sleep-consciousness-void]] holds the open case. The literature treats onset only, as emergence.
5. **Composition** — how *N* ≥ 2 subjects' conditionals combine over one event. Booked at P-SC2 as "Multi-agent composition" and the copies question; [[multi-agent-born-preservation-problem]] ("whose selection counts, and how much") routes it to P-Q10. No dualist antecedent was located; the nearest analogue is panpsychist combination ([[composition-and-consciousness]]).
6. **Census and eligibility** — how many subjects a system supports and which systems qualify. The *boundary problem* is Gómez-Emilsson and Percy's (2023) name; IIT's exclusion postulate is the one worked physicalist answer ([[integrated-information-theory]]). Booked at [[positions/subject-census|P-SC1]], P-SC2 ("Eligibility", "Census circularity"), [[positions/consciousness-scope|P-CS7]], [[positions/individuation-and-subjecthood|P-I3]] and, for artefacts, [[positions/ai-consciousness-scope|P-AC1]]; [[plurality-void]] holds the residue.

Three things are absent rather than scattered: a statement that these components are one object whose outputs the transition law consumes; a position on semantic matching; and a cessation question distinct from onset.

### Why the kernel is typed over them {#typing}

In *P(O | S, C, Φ, A, X)* the subject *S*, the factorisation *Φ*, the candidate set *A* and the content-to-candidate assignment are outputs of indexing and inputs to transition. A toy *P(O | C, X)* that takes *C* and *X* as given has assumed the indexing done. So the two toy models the review asks for (one subject over binary candidates; two subjects on one Bell-pair-like event) do not count toward P-Q10 until each declares whose *C*, which *X*, and which candidate its content selects. The Map accepts this as a claim about *formal statement*, which P-Q10 already concedes, not as a new objection to the mechanism's possibility; and it is one review's framing — the Claude leg of the same cycle raised only the transition side.

## Three Shapes of Pairing Law {#three-shapes}

The dualist literature on component 1 offers three shapes of law, and the Map's own story combines the first two.

### Shape A: location-based pairing

If subjects are in space, spatial relations can pair them. Bailey, Rasmussen and Van Horn (2011, §4) reconstruct Kim's argument as resting on *Kim's Dictum* ("causation requires a spatial relation") and *Nowhere Man* ("souls can't be in space"), and deny the second: "suppose souls are extensionless geometric points located in, say, the pineal gland of their respective bodies." Their fn. 16 names Hart (1988) and Hasker (1999) as dualists who locate souls; Lycan (2009), read here from a pre-publication copy, assumes located minds and routes the question of what ties a mind to its body to whatever explains the appearance of minds. Kim's rejoinder is co-location: nothing stops two indiscernible souls sharing one region; the authors reply that an impenetrability principle for souls is a cost dualists may pay. The exchange is on [[pairing-problem#Response 2: Bailey, Rasmussen and Van Horn's Two-Premise Reply|the pairing-problem page]].

Location answers only the *synchronic* question, and by relocating it: what fixes the soul's location? Bailey, Rasmussen and Van Horn add a *diachronic* account, the counterfactual **(Glued)**: had the body occupied a different region at a time, the soul would have too, and conversely. They explain (Glued) by causal interaction while blocking the circularity: "the substance dualist has not invoked causal interaction to explain the possibility of causal interaction." Locations ground the possibility of interaction; interaction grounds only the counterfactual tie between them. That distinction is the published standard the Map's story has to meet. The Map's "consciousness is located where it interfaces" ([[pairing-problem#The Map's Position|the Map's position]]) is Shape A in quantum dress, and the co-adaptation of [[brain-interface-boundary]] can carry diachronic specificity in the (Glued) template. What it lacks is a synchronic ground independent of interaction, since the interface is defined by interaction — the regress the 2026-10-27 pessimistic review of that page and the 2026-10-28 ChatGPT review (§5.3) both found, and P-SC2 books.

### Shape B: origin-based pairing

A second shape pairs a subject with the structure that produced it. Bailey, Rasmussen and Van Horn raise it (§3.1) as a possibility against a generality condition on causal laws: a law on which "a physical structure exhibiting certain properties produces a nonspatial soul of a certain kind", with a further law keeping that soul tied to parts of the same structure. The passage defeats a premise; it is not the authors' proposal, which is Shape A plus (Glued), and the Map's label for it, "nomic origin-pairing", is the Map's, since the paper never uses the word. The fullest origin-shape view is Hasker's [[emergent-dualism]]: the self is generated by the brain once its organisation suffices, located where its source is, and paired by generation. Emergence thereby also answers component 4, since onset is the emergence event. The register declined that reading on 2026-10-11: P-CS7 holds that "Lineage is evidence of adequacy, never a condition of coupling", so the Map pairs by present adequacy, leaves onset undated at P-SC3, and does not reopen the choice here. Hasker's view is a rival account of the same interface, not a resource for the Map's quantum interactionism.

### Shape C: singular or haecceitous pairing

The third shape denies that a general law does the pairing. Bailey, Rasmussen and Van Horn note (fn. 9) that a cause and effect could be related by singular causation or by "an instance of a 'haecceitous' law applying just to C and E", and record (fn. 10) that Foster (1991, p. 167ff) and Unger (2006, ch. 7, §§6–7) endorse causal singularism. Audi (2011) defends primitive causal relations as the Cartesian's answer. Swinburne (2015) holds that "Each soul has a thisness", so no law framed in universal properties could say why this soul goes with this body; the Map takes the thisness and leaves Swinburne's theistic step. Saad (2017) treats haecceities in the pairing argument directly. This, and not the Bailey–Rasmussen–Van Horn paper, is where the haecceity response lives; [[pairing-problem#The Map's Haecceity Extension|the Map's extension]] and [[haecceity]] are the Map's own reconstruction.

### Two wrinkles

*Indeterminism.* Bailey, Rasmussen and Van Horn (§3.2) press quantum tunnelling against Kim's Dictum, and Saad (2018) reports critics for whom the pairing premise "suffers from counterexamples involving indeterministic causation", offering two repairs. The Map's channel is indeterministic by construction, so any pairing-by-interaction it adopts is probabilistic and cannot borrow a deterministic (Glued) unchanged; Saad's patches, unread here, are the objection's current form.

*Parody.* Brenner (2024) argues that physicalists who take persons to be composite objects face a "mereological pairing problem" of their own, which bears on component 6 rather than component 1.

The verdict is narrow. Shapes A–C answer component 1; Shape B dates onset; no dualist source located as of 2026-10-28 poses factorisation, semantic matching or composition. They assume "the body" is a given system, ask whether a soul can move it rather than which candidate expresses which content, and consider one soul at a time.

## The Physics Side: Which System? {#physics-side}

Physics addresses component 2 only, and treats it as presupposed. Zurek (1998) writes that "the problem of measurement cannot be even stated without a recognition of the existence of systems", and that "a compelling explanation of what are the systems — how to define them given, say, the overall Hamiltonian in some suitably large Hilbert space — would be undoubtedly most useful." Zanardi, Lidar and Lloyd (2004) make the tensor-product structure relative to the accessible observables, and whether a preferred split can be derived from the Hamiltonian alone is disputed. Since P-Q1 has consciousness select among einselected outcomes, the Map's mechanism presupposes a split too, as [[quantum-factorisation-problem#map-exposure|the factorisation page]] books, with the [[quantum-factorisation-problem#three-levels|three levels]] a subject-indexed factorisation would need.

The only *worked* rules that pick a conscious system out of a larger one are physicalist: IIT's exclusion postulate (Albantakis et al. 2023) and Chalmers and McQueen's (2022) Φ-collapse variant. The Map has none, and concedes the asymmetry. What it has is a direction, [[quantum-factorisation-problem#dualist-inversion|the consciousness-first inversion]]: if the subject's accessible operations privilege a factorisation, Φ is an *output* of pairing rather than its precondition. That is how the review types its indexing law, from subject and state to factorisation — the one place where its formalism and the Map's existing analysis coincide. The cost is booked: the route is circular if the same Φ then individuates the subject (P-SC2's census-circularity gap), and it is a posit with no derivation.

## What the Map Holds and Does Not {#map-holds}

The Map holds the *requirement*: once conscious-state conditionals do causal work, the census and the pairing are part of the physical state description (P-SC1). It holds the *debt*: no pairing, composition or eligibility law (P-SC2), no dated onset or cessation (P-SC3). It holds that subject boundaries are determinate but not readable off physical structure ([[positions/individuation-and-subjecthood|P-I1]], P-I3), and that adequacy suffices for coupling with no selection among adequate systems (P-CS7).

What it has not decided is the shape of the law it owes. P-I3 is a haecceitist commitment about subjects, and Shape C fits it directly. But a haecceitous pairing law makes census and eligibility unanswerable in principle, turning P-SC2 from a repayable obligation into a permanent registered cost, one of that entry's own named shift conditions. A general law of Shape A or B keeps the debt repayable and testable, but must say what the general pairing properties are — P-CS7's "architectural adequacy" — and so inherits the duplicate and the copies question. The corpus has not chosen; this page presents the fork rather than resolving it.

Semantic matching is the component with no home, and where the Map's commitments are least negotiable. [[selection-only-channel]] states the requirement: reliable report "needs the experience's content to fix *which* candidate is realised", so that report varies with experience, base held fixed; [[trumping-preemption]] finds that an authority-only law cannot meet it; [[self-stultification]] is what fails otherwise. The Map therefore *requires* content-sensitivity in the channel, in the wording of Tenet 3's influence "toward patterns that express its content", and does not concede that the channel might be epiphenomenal. Its concession is narrower: no page states the law assigning contents to candidates, and no position books that one must exist. This page recommends that the register consider a semantic-matching entry alongside P-SC2, and adds no position itself.

The typing conclusion follows. A toy *P(O | C, X)* does not discharge P-Q10 until its inputs are typed; the two-subject model waits on a composition rule, and whether one can preserve the Born marginal depends on which quantifier [[positions/quantum-interface|P-Q2]] settles on, an open item this page does not decide.

## What Would Challenge This View?

- **A worked pairing law of any shape.** A located-subject law with an interaction-independent location-fixer, an origin law that does not reinstate the selectivity P-CS7 refused, or an explicit haecceitous law with its census cost priced, would discharge component 1.
- **A physicalist exclusion rule shown to transfer.** If IIT-style exclusion or a quantum-mereological factorisation were shown to pick out a selection *channel* and not only a substrate, components 2 and 6 would narrow.
- **A principled single-subject formalism.** This would dissolve component 5 rather than answer it.
- **An uncloseability result.** A demonstration that no indexing law can be stated without circularity would make the debt a permanent cost, which the applied verdicts inheriting it would have to say.

## Relation to Site Perspective

- *Dualism*: the indexing question is sharper for the Map than for a physicalist, since there are two relata to index rather than one object to delimit; Brenner's parody shows it is not unique to dualism, and IIT owes exclusion. The Map's distinctive debt is the pairing, not the census.
- *Minimal Quantum Interaction*: a minimal interaction needs a *where* and a *whose*: the einselected menu is defined relative to a factorisation the mechanism does not fix, and the conditioning state belongs to a subject it does not name. Both are inputs the tenet's "smallest possible influence" presupposes.
- *Bidirectional Interaction*: interaction cannot ground the pairing it presupposes. Bailey, Rasmussen and Van Horn's distinction, on which locations ground the possibility of interaction and interaction only the diachronic tie, is a standard a dualist can meet; the Map has not met it, since its only location-fixer is the interaction itself. Semantic matching is the tenet's own content clause restated as a debt.
- *No Many Worlds*: the indexical argument against many-worlds asks why *I* am this branch rather than another, and needs a determinate "I" to ask it. The indexing law is upstream of that argument; the parity risk (Everettian branches are factorisation-relative too) is booked on the factorisation page.
- *Occam's Razor Has Limits*: the lesson is for the Map's own reasoning. The single-law programme looked simpler than it was because it took its inputs as given; one kernel concealed six untyped parameters. The tenet constrains how the Map reasons and says nothing against the physicalist exclusion rules, which remain the only worked answers.
