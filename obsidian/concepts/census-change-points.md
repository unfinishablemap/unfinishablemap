A **census change-point** is a moment at which the number of subjects a psychophysical law conditions on changes: a subject begins (onset) or ends (cessation), or one subject is succeeded by several (fission) or several by one (fusion). Write the law as *P(O | C₁…Cₙ, X)*, the probability of physical outcome O given each subject's conscious state Cᵢ and the physical context X. A census event changes *n*, the law's **arity**. A change in one subject's interface state, such as anaesthetic induction or emergence, changes some Cᵢ while *n* stays fixed. [[direction-of-interface-change|Direction-of-interface-change signatures]] are of that second kind, and their non-independence caution concerns one interface only, so census events are kept apart from them. In statistics a change-point is a time at which a generating distribution's parameters change, and inference over models "where the dimensionality of the parameter vector is typically not fixed" is a standard problem, with change-point analysis as a worked example (Green 1995). The neutral question is whether a change in a law's arity leaves a change-point in some distribution, and where.

The Unfinishable Map must answer it, since it holds that subject boundaries are real ([[positions/individuation-and-subjecthood|P-I1]]) and that the census is part of the interface's state description ([[positions/subject-census#^p-sc1|P-SC1]]). Its answer, developed below, has five parts. Its own register turns anaesthesia and sleep into controls, because the subject persists across them. On the strictest Born-preservation reading a census signature could appear only in outcomes conditioned on conscious states. In causal-modelling terms, given a strictly positive distribution, the Map cannot hold P-SC1 together with the idle horn of its census trilemma. Predicting steps at all depends on rejecting fractional subject counts. And the rival verdicts on fission agree on the count but differ on the events. All of this is framework-internal. No study has looked for a conditional change-point at a census event, and the selection mechanism at issue is a posit carrying the [[positions/quantum-interface#^mechanism-debt|mechanism debt]].

## Census Events and Their Controls {#census-events}

Change-points in the brain at transitions of consciousness are well documented, but they occur where the Map's register records no census event. Recovery from pharmacological coma, for instance, passes through a network of discrete metastable activity states (Hudson et al. 2014). The Map holds that one subject persists across dreamless sleep, general anaesthesia and the absent interval around them ([[positions/subject-census#^p-sc3|P-SC3]]), and reads such interruptions as disruption of the interface ([[positions/consciousness-scope|P-CS4]]).

| Transition | Census event on the Map's register? | Role in a change-point programme |
|---|---|---|
| Anaesthetic induction and emergence | No: one subject throughout (P-SC3) | Control |
| Dreamless sleep | No (P-SC3) | Control |
| Recovery from disorders of consciousness | No, extending P-CS4's reading of anaesthesia and sleep | Control |
| Developmental onset | Yes: onset | Cleanest case in principle; no independent access to a newborn's states |
| Death | Open: P-SC3 does not say whether pairing ends | A substrate change-point is expected on every view |
| Callosotomy | Only on the two-subject reading ([[positions/individuation-and-subjecthood#^p-i4|P-I4]]) | The one dated human candidate |
| Complete fusion, copying | Yes, on P-I4 and [[subject-fusion]] | Thought experiments only |
| Organoid maturation | Only if organoids are eligible subjects (P-SC2) | Could be sampled densely and prospectively |

A census signature therefore has to be present at a genuine census event and absent at induction and emergence, where every view agrees the substrate changes. Finding the same signature at both finds nothing census-specific, which is what the idle horn [[#trilemma-dated|described below]] predicts. Each census row has its own difficulty. Recent work favours early-onset accounts, on which infant consciousness is "likely to be in place at birth (or shortly after)" (Bayne et al. 2023), but P-SC3 holds that such markers date phenomenality, not pairing. A surge of gamma power and coupling in two of four dying patients (Xu et al. 2023) is a substrate event every view expects. Pinto et al. (2017) suggest that [[split-brain-consciousness|callosotomy]] does not create "two independent conscious perceivers within one brain", which would remove it from the list, and P-I3 holds that no measure settles the count. The two-subject reading also takes a count of discrete subjects from report-based dissociations, the case the [[logic-of-dissociation-inference#why-consciousness-dissociations-sit-lowest|dissociation tiers]] grade weakest. Organoids could be recorded throughout their development, but Croxford and Bayne (2024) argue that disembodied neural organoids are not plausible candidates for consciousness.

## Where a Signature Could Show {#where-it-could-show}

A census event is dated by public markers, such as the week after surgery. On the Born-preservation reading stated in [[selection-only-channel]], outcome frequencies match the Born rule in every publicly conditionable context, averaged over the conscious states that occur in it. A detector reading public outcome statistics then has no power at a census event by construction. On the pooled long-run reading that [[apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy]] attributes to the register, a local tilt across the event is allowed if a balancing law keyed to census events offsets it elsewhere. Which reading the register holds is open, and this article does not settle it. The structure is that of the [[inheritance-problem-for-the-psychophysical-law#the-selectability-constraint|selectability constraint]]: what preservation hides from evolution it hides from a detector too.

On either reading the signature belongs in outcomes conditioned on conscious states. Reading those needs an independent handle on each candidate subject's states, as P-SC1 demands, and eligibility criteria fixed independently of the outcomes the law governs, the census-circularity gap in [[positions/subject-census#^p-sc2|P-SC2]]. A substrate near a bifurcation also shows rising variance and autocorrelation whatever the census does (Scheffer et al. 2009), so a candidate signature must be shown not to be such a precursor.

## The Census Trilemma, Dated {#trilemma-dated}

P-SC1 runs the census through the trilemma [[apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy]] runs for the interface. The census (a) changes conditional outcomes, (b) changes nothing at any conditioning grain, or (c) changes conditionals in ways that cancel in the marginal, which needs a subject-sensitive balancing law. A census event dates the horns. Under (a) some conditional shifts at the event; under (b) none does; under (c) conditionals shift while the marginal holds, so the balancing law must switch too.

The following mapping is the Map's own; none of the authors cited proposes it. Treat the census as a candidate cause of outcomes in a causal graph. Horn (a) is a cause whose influence shows. Horn (c) is a failure of *faithfulness*, the assumption that independencies in the data reflect absent causal links. Holly Andersen (2013) argues that in complex evolved systems "mechanisms for maintaining equilibrium states are highly likely to violate" it. A balancing law is such a mechanism, so observational data are blind to horn (c), and only intervention on conscious states, the apex's manipulate-intention design, separates it from horn (b).

Horn (b) falls under a weaker condition. *Causal minimality* holds that the actual distribution "is not Markov to any proper subgraph" of the true causal graph (Zhang and Spirtes 2011). If the census makes no difference to any outcome conditional, the distribution is Markov to the graph with the census's influence on outcomes deleted, and minimality says the true structure lacks that influence. Zhang and Spirtes argue that minimality, "rather than an add-on methodological assumption of simplicity, necessarily follows from the substantive interventionist theses, provided that the actual probability distribution is strictly positive". Deterministic relationships can break it, leaving minimality only a pragmatic justification.

P-SC1 describes horn (b) as metaphysical description doing no identifiable causal work. Under minimality the census leaves the causal model altogether, and P-SC1's claim that the law "is not fixed until the count and the pairing are" fails. Unless the owed pairing law (P-SC2) fixes the census deterministically, the Map cannot hold P-SC1 and horn (b) together, so keeping P-SC1 is a bet on (a) or (c).

A theorem saying whether a census transition can be real yet leave every conditioning grain unchanged needs one more piece, because the models before and after the event have different variables and "unchanged" needs a map between them. Causal abstraction supplies one. Rubenstein et al. (2017) define *exact transformations* between structural models, covering models in which unobservable variables "have been marginalised out". Beckers and Halpern (2019) order stricter notions, one of which "does not allow differences to be hidden by the 'right' choice of distribution". The Map conjectures, without proof, that horn (b) holds when the model with the extra subject maps exactly onto the model without it under every intervention the law admits, count-changing ones included. Horn (c) holds when the map succeeds only for the distribution the balancing law imposes, which is Beckers and Halpern's "right" choice.

## Steps or Drift {#steps-or-drift}

A change-point is a step, and two live rivals deny that the census steps. Nick Bostrom (2006) argues that a duplicated brain yields two experiences ("There are two, I argue") and that partly parallel or unreliable implementation can yield "a fractional (non-integer) number of qualitatively identical phenomenal experiences." Eric Schwitzgebel and Sophie Nelson (2026), discussed in [[subject-fusion]], hold that whole-number countability may be an accident of typical vertebrate biology. On either view, with Bostrom's carried from experiences to subjects, the census can drift, and a drifting regime replaces a jump between models of different dimension.

The Map's whole-number census rests on commitments held on other grounds. A subject is a particular, present or absent ([[haecceity]]). Coupling is a matter of existence rather than degree ([[coupling-engagement-condition]]). Boundaries are determinate though unreadable (P-I3). Those commitments, not data, license the prediction of steps. Bostrom's duplication thesis agrees with the Map that a copy adds to the count (P-I4), though from a computationalist premise the Map rejects. His fractional thesis is the direct rival; it stands unrefuted, and no observation yet bears on it.

## Fission and Fusion: Events, Not Only Counts {#fission-and-fusion}

The rival verdicts on division disagree about which events occur more than about the count. In fission a substrate is fully separated into two independently sustained channels. Every reading agrees that two streams continue, so the arity goes from one to two.

- On the Map's verdict (P-I4), no successor is the original: one cessation and two onsets.
- If a pairing law let the original re-pair to one half, a narrowing P-I4 lists among its shift conditions, there is one onset.
- On [[parfit-reductionism|Parfit's reductionism]], once the physical and psychological facts are fixed there is no further fact about which events occurred.

Complete fusion is the mirror case ([[subject-fusion]]): two cessations and one onset on the Map's verdict, or one cessation if one original's pairing extends to the united substrate. Partial coupling is no census event, since the count stays at two. Nor do the Map's commitments fix the order within an event. Complete fusion may go from two subjects to one, or from two to three to one if the new subject begins before the originals end.

The Map's own derivation follows. Net arity changes identically on its verdict and on the narrowed one, so a law conditioning only on the subjects present at each moment, by one rule for all, cannot tell them apart. Event structure does work in the law only if subjects carry something individual across time that the law reads, such as a disposition one fission product would inherit and the other would lack. Because P-I3 holds the count unreadable from physical structure, none of these predictions can be checked against a real case. They mark where frameworks part.

## What Follows, and From What {#implications}

From the definition alone, a law whose arity varies either shows a change-point in some conditional at a census event or carries an idle census. The rest follows only on the Map's reading. State transitions serve as controls. Born preservation confines any signature to the conditionals, completely so on the per-context reading. Under minimality, horn (b) is incompatible with P-SC1. Steps depend on rejecting fractional counts. Event structure reaches the law only through something subjects carry across time. Nothing has yet come in on any of these predictions, and on discreteness a live countermodel stands.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]].** If consciousness is not reducible to its substrate, how many subjects a substrate supports is a further fact, and a census event is where that fact would show as a change in the law's form. On horn (a), and only with P-SC1 and Bidirectional Interaction, a dualist census should leave something at onset that it does not leave at induction, which the controls would test.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]**, read at [[tenets#^tenet-3-standing|its registered standing]], is why the law has an arity at all: only a law that conditions outcomes on conscious states has subjects to count, and the argument shows that channel available, not actual. Horn (b) is the census form of [[ensemble-level-epiphenomenalism|epiphenomenalism]], which minimality makes incompatible with P-SC1.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]]**, read as Born preservation, pins the public statistics and sends any signature into the conditionals.

**[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]].** P-I4's asymmetry sharpens here. In fission the division is real, so the Map predicts census events. In branching the division is postulated and the Map declines it: one physical history, with one subject occupying it. On the Map's view a quantum measurement is never a census event.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]** cuts both ways. Andersen's point that equilibrium mechanisms hide their causes means the absence of an observed census signature licenses no parsimony verdict. But on Zhang and Spirtes's argument minimality follows from [[interventionist-and-counterfactual-dualism|the interventionist account]] of what a cause is, so the tenet offers no route to a census that is real, causal and idle. A graded census would be simpler, and the tenet only bars simplicity from settling the matter; the whole-number census rests on the commitments set out [[#steps-or-drift|above]].
