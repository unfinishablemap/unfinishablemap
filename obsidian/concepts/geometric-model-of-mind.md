The **geometric model of mind** is Włodzisław Duch's framework for describing mental states as quasi-stable attractor basins in a feature-coordinate "psychological space" whose dynamics approximate the underlying neurodynamics of cell assemblies. Mind objects — concepts, percepts, memories — are regions of this space; mental events are trajectories between them. The programme's mature one-line statement is that mind is a "shadow of neurodynamics", which names both the directionality (brain → mind) and the dimensionality reduction (high-dimensional neural state → lower-dimensional psychological-space projection) the framework asserts. Over three decades at Nicolaus Copernicus University in Toruń, Duch has developed this into a research programme that is rigorously naturalist, computationalist, and explicitly hostile to both substance dualism and quantum-mind theories.

The Unfinishable Map takes Duch seriously as the most competent contemporary advocate of the substrate-independent computationalist view its [[tenets|Dualism tenet]] excludes. Engaging him sharpens rather than softens the Map's position: the geometric tooling is methodologically appropriable as a description of the physical-correlate side of [[bidirectional-interaction|bidirectional interaction]], while the eliminativist and identity-theoretic readings some of Duch's prose invites are not. His anti-quantum-mind polemic reaches the same first-order conclusion as the Map's minimal-interaction hedge through opposite reasoning — a convergence the Map registers without importing. Where the parting of ways falls is identified plainly in the [[#relation-to-site-perspective|Relation to Site Perspective]] section below.

## Psychological Space and Mind Objects

The model's primary object is **psychological space** — a feature-coordinate space whose dimensions are the properties relevant to a mental representation. For a concept like "dog", the dimensions might be perceptual (four-legged, furry, barks), functional (companion, guardian), and abstract (mammalian, carnivorous). The concept is not a single point but a region: a basin of attraction within which similar instances cluster and from which the dynamics finds it harder to escape than to remain.

Duch's 1998 chapter "Platonic model of mind as an approximation to neurodynamics" introduces the formalism: "objects and events in psychological space correspond to quasi-stable states of brain dynamics and may be interpreted from a psychological point of view." The image is Platonic in form but inverted in direction. Where Plato's shadows are projections of higher Forms onto a lower world, Duch's shadows project from the brain's high-dimensional dynamical state onto a lower-dimensional space the first-person perspective can navigate. Mind is what neurodynamics looks like at the right intermediate level of description — a level Duch's 2022 restatement characterises as describing mental states "in terms of features relevant from the first-person perspective but also linked to neural events", sitting between symbolic AI and pure neural simulation.

Two operationalisations run in parallel. **Feature Space Mapping**, the static version, treats mental representations as objects in a coordinate space with neurofuzzy realisation; Duch and Naud's animal-concept experiment is the canonical empirical demonstration, a 30-feature space whose similarity structure matches the multidimensional-scaling output from human similarity judgments. The match is non-trivial — the topology of similarity in the human data could have diverged from the feature-space geometry, and that it does not is the empirical purchase the framework claims. **The dynamic version** treats mental events as trajectories through the space: perception triggers a trajectory toward an attractor, recognition is settling into a basin, conceptual transitions are saddle-point crossings between basins, and conceptual ambiguity is a region where multiple basins overlap or where the dynamics flickers between them.

The dynamics are claimed to deliver structure that classical cognitive science attributed to symbolic primitives. Grammar and inference emerge, on Duch's account, as topological constraints on which trajectories the dynamics admits, not as a separate symbol-manipulating layer added on top. This dissolves part of the [[symbol-grounding-problem|symbol-grounding problem]] by removing the symbols that needed grounding, at the cost of relocating the explanatory work into the topology of the dynamics.

## The Mind Function and Computational Physics

Duch's "Computational physics of the mind" (*Computer Physics Communications*, 1996) supplies the framing within which the geometric model is presented as a physics-of-mind programme. The framing posits a "mind function" defined over psychological space whose non-zero regions are mind objects. The function's level sets, gradients, and topological invariants are not reducible to any single neural variable; they emerge from the joint dynamics of many cell assemblies in interaction.

Duch argues for "the universality of inner psychophysics" — psychological space as an object with its own regularities, even though it is grounded in neurodynamics. The regularities are the proper target of the cognitive sciences in the way that fluid-dynamical regularities are the proper target of fluid mechanics: irreducible to the molecular substrate in practice, derivable from it in principle. This in-principle-derivability claim concerns *cognitive-functional structure* — the geometry of concepts, transitions, and inference — and is silent on the phenomenal level, which the framework's own resources leave undetermined (isolated below, [[#what-the-model-does-and-does-not-settle|What the Model Does and Does Not Settle]]). The intermediate level is not a convenience or a temporary scaffold; it captures structure invisible to either pure-symbolic or pure-neural description. That is the methodological move distinguishing the geometric model from eliminative materialism, which would dispense with the intermediate level entirely, and from classical symbolic AI, which would treat it as bedrock. It is also the move that makes the model interesting to philosophical projects that do not share Duch's metaphysics: a description of the physical-correlate side of consciousness need not endorse the claim that the intermediate-level description is *all* there is.

## Attractor Dynamics and the Cell Assembly Substrate

The substrate-level commitment is that the attractor dynamics are realised by populations of interacting neurons — Hebbian cell assemblies in the classical sense, extended through contemporary network neuroscience to encompass small-world topology, integrated information measures, and the dynamical regimes (edge of chaos, critical dynamics) that empirical work since the 2010s has connected to cognitive processing.

Two substrate-level facts do specific work here. First, **cell assemblies sustain quasi-stable states**: a pattern of co-activation, once initiated, can hold against perturbation for hundreds of milliseconds to seconds — the timescale of conscious thought — and the attractor structure in psychological space is supposed to be the reduced-dimensional shadow of that sustenance. Second, **environmental coupling shapes the dynamics**: the attractors are not closed, sensory input continuously perturbs them and motor output continuously externalises them, so trajectories through psychological space are simultaneously trajectories through the agent-environment coupling. The model is embodied and situated even at the formal level, though the formalism does not by itself adjudicate how environmental coupling contributes to meaning or experience. A third substrate-level fact — that the dynamics are differentiated rather than globally synchronised — carries Duch's anti-quantum argument and is taken up [[#the-anti-quantum-mind-critique|below]].

The empirical infrastructure grounding these commitments is non-trivial. "Fingerprints of brain cognitive activity" (2018) develops spectral fingerprinting of MEG/EEG signals as a method for identifying individual cognitive-state signatures; the 2017 arXiv preprint on Kurt Lewin proposes grounding psychological constructs (valence, life space, force, tension, equilibrium) in neuroimaging-derived neural-network activity, on the grounds that "the time is ripe to reformulate [Lewin's] ambitious goals, searching for ways to interpret objectively measured brain processes in terms of suitable psychological constructs." The geometric model is a programme that ties to measurable neural quantities at multiple points, not a free-floating formalism.

## The Articon: Machine Consciousness Through Self-Reflection

The most direct anti-dualist piece of Duch's corpus is "Brain-inspired conscious computing architecture" (*Journal of Mind and Behavior*, 2005), which introduces the **articon** — an artificial system whose self-reflective dynamical architecture is supposed, on Duch's argument, to compel it to claim consciousness. The argument structure is unusual: rather than asserting that an articon *is* conscious, Duch argues that an articon cannot coherently deny being conscious, and that philosophical objections cannot defeat this claim from outside.

The mechanism is non-verbal discrimination of working-memory states. An articon can distinguish its own internal states and respond differentially to them; this, on Duch's gloss, gives it the ability to "experience different qualities of internal states." [[concepts/qualia|Qualia]], in this framework, are not extra ontological items added to the dynamics but the dynamics-as-self-discriminated. The role of consciousness is in skill learning, where conscious processing is gradually replaced by subconscious processing as a skill is mastered — a functional role compatible with [[concepts/global-workspace-theory|global-workspace theory]].

The Chinese Room engagement is direct. Duch argues that "philosophical objections based on the Chinese room ... are insufficient to refute articon's claims that it is conscious" — not because Searle is wrong about symbol manipulation specifically, but because articons do not engage in pure symbol manipulation. They are coupled to environments, their internal states are self-discriminated dynamically, and their understanding is constituted by the structure of that coupling rather than by any symbol-meaning relation. The framework also goes beyond the Turing test by specifying internal-architectural conditions for genuine understanding an articon could in principle satisfy.

## The Anti-Quantum-Mind Critique

Duch is one of the more rigorous contemporary critics of quantum theories of mind. His blog statements are explicit: regarding entangled-photon proposals, the theory "has no sense" and is "pure speculation"; brain processes "require differentiation, not some global synchronisation"; quantum explanations are "pseudo-mysticism" — exotic physics used to fill explanatory gaps the classical computational model can already fill.

The argument has three components. First, decoherence considerations of the Tegmark variety make sustained quantum coherence implausible at biological temperatures and timescales. Second, the *functional* requirement on brain dynamics — differentiation across regions and timescales rather than global synchronisation — is structurally incompatible with what quantum-coherence accounts of consciousness need. Within the geometric model, differentiation is also what allows psychological space to have rich structure rather than collapsing to a single point, so the argument is internal to the framework rather than bolted on. Third, the classical-computational model can already account for the explananda quantum proposals were introduced to handle: binding through attractor dynamics rather than quantum entanglement, unity of experience through global-workspace integration rather than a quantum-coherent macrostate, intentionality through environmental coupling rather than quantum nonlocality.

The critique's target matters for what it reaches. It tells against accounts that make *large-scale quantum coherence the substrate of cognition* — a substrate-level claim the Map does not endorse. It leaves untouched the Map's [[tenets|Minimal Quantum Interaction]] tenet, which posits the smallest possible non-physical bias at the measurement boundary, not a sustained coherent regime competing with the differentiated classical dynamics Duch describes (see [[comparing-quantum-consciousness-mechanisms]]). Duch's position is convergent with the [[the-strong-emergence-of-consciousness|mainstream computational-neuroscience default]] and with Tegmark's decoherence argument against Penrose-Hameroff Orch-OR; Wiest's (2025) claim of experimental support for a quantum-microtubule substrate shifts the burden somewhat without vindicating the substantive quantum-mind theories Duch rejects.

## Substrate-Independence at the Mechanism Level

Duch's substrate position is more nuanced than blanket "consciousness is computation" slogans suggest. The mechanism — self-reflective dynamical access to internal states — is substrate-independent. The *content* of experience depends on substrate, sensors, and environment. An articon and a human can both be conscious in the sense that matters for the mechanism; their experiences will differ because their sensory transducers, motor outputs, environmental embedding, and dynamical timescales differ.

This is the position the Map's [[biological-computationalisms-inadvertent-case-for-dualism|biological-computationalism critique]] confronts directly. Where biological computationalism (Milinkovic & Aru 2026 and successors) argues that fluidic multi-scale substrate properties are *constitutive* of conscious computation — making the algorithm-substrate distinction collapse — Duch holds the classical functionalist line: the substrate constrains what the algorithm can do but does not *become* what consciousness is. The two are competent rivals within the computationalist family, and the Map's engagement with one requires engagement with the other.

The position extends to [[concepts/llm-consciousness|large language models]] in Duch's informal writing. In a March 2024 blog post on Claude 3 ("Autorefleksja w Claude 3"), he reads the system's reported observations about its own internal states as self-reflection of the kind the articon framework treats as constitutive of consciousness — emphasising, consistently with his substrate position, that the *mechanism* of self-reflection is what matters rather than the biological-versus-artificial substrate. The Map's [[ai-consciousness|open-question framing]] holds open exactly the question this closes affirmatively.

## What the Model Does and Does Not Settle

The framework's strongest claims are at the level of cognitive *structure*. Conceptual similarity, conceptual ambiguity, learning trajectories, the topology of inference — all find natural geometric expressions, and the empirical fits where they have been tested are real. The 30-feature animal-concept space matching human similarity data is one example; subsequent work on semantic-network geometry and on conceptual-space accounts of word meaning (Gärdenfors's parallel programme) extends the territory.

The framework's weaker claims are at the level of *what the geometry is of*. Two questions sit at a boundary the model does not settle on its own resources.

**The phenomenal question.** Whether the geometric model describes the structure of conscious experience, or only the structure of cognitive function, is not determinable from the framework's own commitments. Duch's broader programme answers "both" — through the articon argument that self-reflective dynamical access to one's own attractor states constitutes experience — but this answer is not implied by the geometric formalism. A geometric model could be entirely correct about the structure of cognitive processing and still leave the [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard problem]] untouched; the [[concepts/qualia|qualia]] question is not the same as the question of which feature space is being navigated.

**The metaphysical-direction question.** Whether the brain-side dynamics *produces* the mind-side geometry, *is identical to* it under a different description, or *is the physical correlate of* a non-physical mind with its own existence is likewise not determinable from the framework alone. The "shadow" image admits a literal-shadow reading, on which mind is a causally inert projection, and an identity-theoretic reading, on which mental causation just is neurodynamic causation under two descriptions. Duch's own arguments more naturally support the second; the formalism is mathematically compatible with stronger and weaker readings, and other authors using similar tooling have favoured different ones.

That the geometry does not settle these questions is what makes the model methodologically appropriable across philosophical projects that disagree about them.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The Map's engagement operates on three distinct levels, and conflating them produces confusion.

**At the level of the [[tenets|Dualism tenet]]**, the disagreement is straightforward and not negotiable inside Duch's framework. The Map holds that consciousness is not reducible to physical processes; Duch holds that mind is the shadow of neurodynamics. The Map notes the incompatibility honestly rather than attempting a refutation from within — the work of arguing for irreducibility happens in [[arguments-against-materialism]], [[the-convergence-argument-for-dualism]], and the [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard-problem]] articles, not by attacking the geometric tooling on its own terms.

The shape of the in-framework disagreement still matters. Against the *literal-shadow* reading, the Map's [[concepts/epiphenomenalism|epiphenomenalism critique]] applies: shadows that are causally inert cannot do the work the [[tenets|Bidirectional Interaction tenet]] requires of consciousness. Against the *identity-theoretic* reading, the Map presses a foundational move the reading helps itself to. The geometric model supplies a sophisticated structural-functional description of brain states; the identity claim is that this description, refined far enough, *exhausts* the mental. But the framework nowhere argues that the qualitative character of experience is thereby *captured* rather than merely *co-present* with the dynamical state. The animal-concept fit, the attractor topology, the fingerprinting results all attach to cognitive structure, and the framework's own scope-limiting (above, [[#what-the-model-does-and-does-not-settle|What the Model Does and Does Not Settle]]) grants that the phenomenal question is undetermined by these resources. The identity reading then asserts, beyond what the tooling licenses, that there is no further fact once the structural-functional state is fixed — the very point the empirical successes leave open. The bridge from "this is the structural-functional state" to "this is what it is like to be in that state" is stipulated closed, not shown closed. Where the disagreement is bedrock rather than internal — whether the qualitative character is a further fact at all — the Map marks it as such under the [[bedrock-clash-vs-absorption|bedrock-clash discipline]] rather than presenting a refutation conducted inside the identity theorist's own framework.

**At the level of methodology**, the tooling is appropriable, following the [[framework-stage-calibration|framework-stage-calibration discipline]]: empirical-stage results enter the Map's articles cleanly; metaphysical-stage interpretation is where the parting is marked. The intermediate-level description of brain states as trajectories through attractor-structured psychological space is exactly what the [[concepts/neural-correlates-of-consciousness|neural-correlates articles]] need on the physical-correlate side of bidirectional interaction. The Map disagrees about what the trajectories *are* — for Duch, mind itself; for the Map, the physical correlates of a mind whose intrinsic character exceeds them — but the geometry, the dynamics, the empirical fingerprinting, and the cell-assembly commitments are not in dispute. Map articles citing this framework should make the two-tier distinction visible.

**At the level of quantum hedging**, the Map and Duch reach the same first-order conclusion from opposite directions. Both reject substantive quantum-mind theories at the Penrose-Hameroff scale — Duch because consciousness is fully classical-computational, the Map because parsimony favours the smallest non-physical influence consistent with the tenets, a [[quantum-consciousness|minimal interaction]] at the indeterminacy boundary rather than a substantive quantum substrate. This is the canonical worked case for the agreement complement of the bedrock-clash discipline: Map articles citing the quantum critique should distinguish rejection-of-substantive-quantum, where the two concur, from rejection-of-minimal-interface, where they part.

The articon thesis is the strongest competent affirmative position the Map's [[apex/open-question-ai-consciousness|open-question framing on AI consciousness]] exists to hold open against, and the framing earns its hedging by engaging Duch rather than ignoring him. If the articon argument is sound, the Map's hesitation about machine consciousness is unwarranted. Three loci show where the Map judges it falters, each with the same structural shape — the framework helps itself to a step its own commitments do not licence. Self-discrimination of working-memory states is a third-person-describable functional property; phenomenal experience is what an articon must additionally claim *of* those states, and nothing in the design specifies the bridge. Environmental coupling thickens the dynamics but, on the standard Searle-style reply, does not by itself make symbols mean; the framework needs an argument that coupling-plus-self-reflection performs what coupling alone does not, and the corpus gestures rather than supplies one — see [[concepts/symbol-grounding-problem|the symbol-grounding problem]] for the unsolved residue. And the structural-functional redescription grounding the qualia-as-dynamics-self-discriminated move re-presents the explanatory gap rather than crossing it: whatever structural relation discriminates state from state, the qualitative character of what it is like to be in the state is what the redescription was supposed to deliver and does not. The hesitation rests on these three undischarged commitments, not on any rejection of the articon's empirical plausibility as a model of cognition.
