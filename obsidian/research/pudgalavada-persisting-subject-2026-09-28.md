## Executive Summary

The reviewers are right that the Pudgalavāda is the closest *Buddhist* precedent for the Map's persisting subject. It is the one Buddhist school that held the person to be real, "true and ultimate", irreducible to the five aggregates and not separate from them, and it held that the person continues through rebirth. Their assumption that the precedent is *close*, however, holds only for structure. It fails for the two features the Map most needs. (1) **Non-physicality/separability.** The pudgala "cannot be found apart from" the aggregates and "cannot exist without" them (Priestley, IEP). Carpenter's reconstruction states outright that nothing in it "implies the existence of an immaterial substantial substratum". (2) **Agency.** Carpenter's pudgala is "nor an active agent of unity distinct from the unique unity it is". Her Pudgalavādin even says: "This is no further fact, venerable Sir, no extra entity." The Map's subject is an agent-causal, numerically determinate "further fact" ([[haecceity]]; [[where-the-substance-commitment-enters]]), and on those features its nearest Indian precedent is the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika ātman. That is the view Vasubandhu refutes in the *second* half of AKBh ch. 9.

The refutations therefore split. The *Kathāvatthu* opening and Vasubandhu's entity/designation dilemma reach the Map only partly, and in one case the Map can take a horn the Pudgalavādin refused. Vasubandhu's anti-Vaiśeṣika **agent argument** ("what activity do the Vaiśeṣikas attribute to a 'soul'?") and his **ownership-as-mastery argument** ("Memory belongs to whatever causes memory") reach the Map directly. Both are verified in Pruden's translation. The ownership argument, however, targets ownership understood as *control/mastery*. It does not touch ownership as phenomenal for-me-ness. That bears on the claim in [[buddhist-perspectives-on-meaning]] that the ownership horn is "near bedrock" (see Key Debates below).

## Key Sources

### Priestley, "Pudgalavada Buddhist Philosophy", *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
- **URL**: https://iep.utm.edu/pudgalavada-buddhist-philosophy/
- **Type**: Encyclopedia (author: Leonard Priestley, Toronto; entry undated on page)
- **Key points** (all [V] against downloaded HTML):
  - Five schools: Vātsīputrīya (original), Dharmottarīya, Bhadrayānīya, Sāmmitīya, Shannagarika; only Vātsīputrīya and Sāmmitīya large. Per Xuanzang (7th c.) the Sāmmitīya was "by far the largest of the Shrāvakayāna schools", about a quarter of Indian monastics.
  - Core claim: "The Pudgalavādins described the person or self as 'inexpressible,' that is, as indeterminate in its relation to the five aggregates, since it cannot be identified with the aggregates and cannot be found apart from them: the self and the aggregates are neither the same nor different." Unlike other schools, they took this indeterminacy to "characterize a real self, a self that is 'true and ultimate.'"
  - Fifth category of knowables: past, present, future phenomena, plus Nirvana (fourth); "The self or person, not to be described either as the same as the dependent phenomena of the temporal world or as distinct from them, is the fifth."
  - Three designations of the person: by the aggregates appropriated in a life; by transition between lives; by final passing away (parinirvāṇa).
  - **Ontological status unstable in the sources**: "they seem to have regarded the self, at lest [sic] initially, as conceptual, though 'true and ultimate.' A later source represents them as maintaining that it is neither conceptual nor substantial, and still later sources ascribe the view to them that the self is indeed substantial."
  - Fire/fuel: "fire is described in terms of its fuel ... but the fire is not the same as the fuel, nor can it continue to burn without the fuel. Similarly, the person ... cannot exist without a body, feelings and the other aggregates."
  - Arguments: (a) scriptural, especially the burden-bearer sutta, where the orthodox readings "show signs of strain"; (b) memory of past lives: "if there were no person distinguishable from the aggregates, there would be no real basis for identifying oneself ... with the person that one was in a previous life"; (c) loving-kindness needs real objects; (d) karma: "the operation of karma is incomprehensible if the person is nothing more than an assemblage of phenomena" (the clay-ox vs living-ox example).
  - Priestley's speculative reconstruction: the self as "a local manifestation" of something, possibly Nirvana ("the self and Nirvana are neither the same nor different"). He flags it as reconstruction: the evidence is "quite limited, much of it surviving only in translation, and some of it from hostile sources."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral-to-supportive on Dualism's *negative* claim only (anti-reduction of the person). No interactionism, no agent posit.
- **Quote**: "the self in their view remains something mysterious and only partially amenable to the principles of rational thought."

### Kathāvatthu I.1, *Puggalakathā*, in Aung and Rhys Davids trans., *Points of Controversy* (PTS 1915)
- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.135916 (djvu OCR; noisy)
- **Type**: Primary text (Theravāda Abhidhamma), English translation.
- **Key points** ([V] against OCR, lines cleanly legible):
  - Opening question: "Is 'the person' known in the sense of a real and ultimate fact?" The Pudgalavādin says yes. Then: "Is the person known in the same way as a real and ultimate fact is known?" "Nay, that cannot truly be said." The Theravādin concludes: "If the latter statement (2) cannot be admitted, then indeed the former statement (1) should not be admitted."
  - The commentary glosses "in the same way": "either as the factors of mind and body are known, by immediate consciousness, or under one of the twenty-four relation-categories."
  - The commentary identifies the Puggalavādins as "the Vajjiputtakas and Sammitiyas", and glosses "person" as "soul, being, vital principle".
  - The comparison is then run against each aggregate, sense base and element in turn ("that they are not mutually distinct things, is false").
  - The rebirth trilemma (§158ff.): "Does (a person or) soul run on (or transmigrate) from this world to another ...?" Yes. Is it the identical soul? No. A different soul? No. "both the identical and also a different soul"? No.
  - The Pudgalavādin's scriptural appeal ([V]): "Did the Exalted One say 'There is the person who works for his own good'?"
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with any real subject; the argument's force depends on a closed list of ultimates (dhammas).

### Vasubandhu, *Abhidharmakośabhāṣya* ch. 9, trans. Pruden from La Vallée Poussin, vol. 4
- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/abhidharmakosabhasyamvol4vasubandhupoussinpruden1991
- **Type**: Primary text, English via French. Berkeley: Asian Humanities Press, 1988–90 (this volume ©1991). Page numbers below are the volume's running pagination.
- **Key passages** (all [V] against OCR):
  - p. 1314, opening framework: "There is neither direct perception nor inference of a soul independent of the skandhas. We know then that a real soul does not exist."
  - p. 1314, the entity/designation dilemma: "The Vatsiputriyas admit a pudgala which is neither identical to the skandhas nor different from them. ... If it has a separate existence, like physical matter, then the pudgala exists as an entity. If it is only a collection, like milk, then it exists as a designation." Then: "If the pudgala is an entity, it will be different from the skandhas ... (or it will be produced by causes and then it will not be eternal as you say), and you will have to name its causes; or it will be unconditioned: and this is a non-Buddhist opinion; and if it is unconditioned, the pudgala will be useless."
  - p. 1315, the Vātsīputrīya reply via fire/fuel, in their own words: "if it were different from the skandhas, it would be eternal and thus unconditioned; but if it were identical to the skandhas, it would then be susceptible of annihilation."
  - pp. 1316–17: Vasubandhu presses "in relation to". If fire depends on or coexists with fuel, it differs from fuel, and "as fire does not exist when fuel is absent, likewise the pudgala does not exist without skandhas."
  - p. 1319, perception: the Vātsīputrīya holds that the pudgala "is perceived by all six consciousnesses", discerned "indirectly", with a relation that is "inexpressible". Vasubandhu: "It follows from this explanation too that the pudgala exists solely as a designation exactly like milk."
  - pp. 1336–37, the undeclared questions: if the Buddha sees the pudgala in nirvāṇa but will not declare it, "we then conclude that [the Vatsiputriyas] admit that the pudgala is permanent."
  - pp. 1340–41, **ownership/memory**: "[The Vatsiputriyas:] In the absence of a soul, whose is the memory? ... This genitive designates its master." "As Caitra is the master of the cow ... he directs and employs the cow as he pleases." Vasubandhu: "What you call 'master' is then simply the cause, and what you call 'subject' is simply the result. ... Since the cause suffices as master, why require a self to which you could attribute memory? Memory belongs to whatever causes memory."
  - pp. 1351–52, **agency** (against the Vaiśeṣikas, not the Personalists): "Nowhere does anyone maintain the existence of an agent conforming to your definition, 'He who possesses independent power is termed an agent.' That which one terms the agent of a certain action, is, amongst all its causes, that which is the principal cause of this action." And: "Memory causes a wish or a desire for action to surge up; from desire there proceeds imagination; from imagination there proceeds effort which gives rise to a vapor which sets in motion bodily action. In this process, what activity do the Vaisesikas attribute to a 'soul'?"
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with Dualism (substance reading) and with the agent-causal reading of Bidirectional Interaction. Vasubandhu's criterion that the real is the causally efficient is one the Map *shares* (see Key Debates).

### Gold, "Vasubandhu", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (rev. 2021)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vasubandhu/
- **Type**: Encyclopedia (Jonathan C. Gold, Princeton; first pub. 2011, substantive revision 7 Jan 2021)
- **Key points** ([V]):
  - "For Vasubandhu, everything that is real or substantial (dravya) is causally efficient".
  - Vasubandhu poses "a dilemma for the Personalists, between saying that the 'person' is uncaused—which would be to adopt a non-Buddhist view—and specifying the cause". A caused person "must change as they change".
  - Against ineffability: "ineffability in regard to an entity that is ostensibly perceived undermines the functionality of perception as a source of knowledge."
  - **Personalist freedom argument**: "The Personalist challenges Vasubandhu to explain how it can be ... that things do not arise in predictable patterns." Gold: "something like an incredulous denial that we could be 'mere automatons'". Vasubandhu's answer is "the possibility of change in a self-regulating system".
  - Against the non-Buddhist controller self: "Just what kind of a controller is the 'self'? ... If the 'self' is an uncaused agent, why can't I, for instance, focus my concentration indefinitely?" Gold: "The intuitive sense of control is a mistake." Vasubandhu's "interest is only to indicate the Buddhist position's comparative simplicity and causal parsimony."
- **Tenet alignment**: The parsimony framing bears directly on Tenet 5.

### Coseru, "Mind in Indian Buddhist Philosophy", *SEP* (rev. 2012)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mind-indian-buddhism/
- **Type**: Encyclopedia (Christian Coseru)
- **Key points** ([V]): Pudgalavādins held that persons "can be said to conventionally exist, insofar as they are conceived in dependence upon the five aggregates and the six types of cognitive awareness (cf. Priestley 1999)" and "regarded them as neither identical nor different from the aggregates". **[R]**: "Siderits (2003: 89–96) makes a good case for taking the Pudgalavādins as proposing something analogous to the notion that persons non-reductively supervene on the aggregates." Coseru's own gloss (§6): personalists "hold that persons non-reductively supervene upon the psychophysical aggregates."
- **Note**: this is the source [[buddhist-perspectives-on-meaning]] cites as "(Coseru 2012)". The citation is correct for the "neither identical nor different" wording.

### Carpenter, "Persons Keeping Their Karma Together: The Reasons for the Pudgalavāda in Early Buddhism" (2015)
- **URL**: https://philpapers.org/rec/CARPKT; author-hosted draft https://projectintegrity.files.wordpress.com/2016/03/amber-carpenter_-persons-keeping-their-karma-together-the-reasons-for-the-pudgalavada-in-early-buddhism1.pdf
- **Type**: Book chapter in Tanaka, Deguchi, Garfield and Priest (eds.), *The Moon Points Back*, Oxford UP, 2015 (bibliographic details [V] at PhilPapers and Carpenter's Integrity Project page).
- **Caveat**: the quotes below are [V] against the **author-hosted draft dated 18 July 2010**, not the published OUP text. Wording and pagination may differ. Cite the chapter, and do not give draft page numbers.
- **Key points**:
  - Thesis: Abhidharma "ultraminimalism" is too minimal to account for personhood. The Personalist insists that the individuation of person-constituting continua is ultimately real: "individuation of causal continua is presupposed, not projected. That some elements belong to each other, and not to any others, is really, ultimately true."
  - Opening pastiche (explicitly *not* in the Milindapañha): "This is no further fact, venerable Sir, no extra entity ... some causal streams are really individuated."
  - "None of this, note, implies the existence of an immaterial substantial substratum for the aggregates." The pudgala "is thus no substance, no 'underlying subject' of properties, nor an active agent of unity distinct from the unique unity it is".
  - Footnote 67: unsure "whether this view should be considered reductionist on Parfit's terms, or as a variant of the 'further fact view'". Parfit's dichotomy may not be exhaustive.
  - The Pudgalavādin "would acknowledge himself defeated" if pudgalavāda could not be distinguished "from commitment to an eternal soul".
- **Tenet alignment**: Supports anti-reductionism about individuation (close to [[haecceity]]'s particularity claim). **Explicitly disclaims** the non-physical substrate and the agent posit.

### Carpenter, "The Sāṃmitīyas and the Case of the Missing Who: A Buddhist Whodunit?" (2017)
- **URL**: https://projectintegrity.files.wordpress.com/2016/03/the-return-of-consciousness_carpenter2.pdf
- **Type**: Chapter in Anders Haag (ed.), *The Return of Consciousness: A New Science on Ancient Questions*, Ax:son Johnson Foundation, 2017, pp. 211–236 (pagination from the PDF's running heads; volume details [V] at integrityproject.org).
- **Key points** ([V]): It poses the forensic "who" question: "Only something persisting through changes in all those particulars ... at once an agent and a subject, as well as the synthesiser ... could be the sort of thing that could be responsible". Standard Abhidharma "rejects the ultimate reality of any such thing as an agent–subject-unifier". Free will is called "the red herring": the Buddhist "does not just reject substance: she replaces it with process, or continuity."
- **Tenet alignment**: Frames exactly the Map's agent-subject need, then argues that Buddhists can meet responsibility without it.

### SN 22.22 *Bhārasutta* (The Burden)
- **URL**: https://suttacentral.net/sn22.22/en/sujato
- **Type**: Primary text (Pāli root + Sujato trans.), [V] via SuttaCentral API.
- **Key lines**: "Katamo ca, bhikkhave, bhārahāro? Puggalo tissa vacanīyaṁ. Yvāyaṁ āyasmā evaṁnāmo evaṅgotto" ("And who is the bearer of the burden? The individual, it should be said; the venerable of such name and such clan"). Verse: "Bhārā have pañcakkhandhā, bhārahāro ca puggalo" ("The five aggregates are indeed burdens, and the individual is the bearer of the burden").
- **Note**: the "such name and such clan" gloss is what the orthodox reading uses. The bearer is specified by a *conventional* designation.

### SN 44.10 *Ānandasutta* (Vacchagotta's question)
- **URL**: https://suttacentral.net/sn44.10/en/sujato
- **Key lines** ([V]): "kiṁ nu kho, bho gotama, atthattā" (Sujato: "does the self survive?"; more literally "is there a self?"). The Buddha is silent; to Ānanda he explains that affirming would side with eternalists and denying with annihilationists, and that the confused Vacchagotta would think "It seems that the self that I once had no longer survives." Both sides used this text (AKBh pp. 1336–37).
- **Translation caution**: Sujato's "survive" is interpretive. Quote the Pāli or flag the rendering.

## Major Positions

### Pudgalavāda (Personalism)
- **Proponents**: Vātsīputrīya, Sāmmitīya and three minor offshoots; no surviving named philosophers. Modern reconstructors: Priestley, Thích Thiện Châu (1999), Carpenter, Siderits [R].
- **Core claim**: the person is real ("true and ultimate") and inexpressible, neither the same as nor different from the aggregates, and a fifth category of knowables.
- **Key arguments**: burden-bearer sutta; the Buddha's refusal to deny the self; memory of past lives; object of mettā; karma (clay ox); unpredictability of the mind (Gold's report).
- **Relation to site tenets**: shares the Map's refusal to reduce the person and its demand for real diachronic individuation. It does not share non-physicality (inseparable from rūpa), agency, or the substance leaning. In the Vātsīputrīyas' own words, difference from the aggregates would make the person "eternal and thus unconditioned", which is why they refused difference.

### Abhidharma reductionism (Theravāda Kathāvatthu; Sarvāstivāda/Vasubandhu)
- **Core claim**: the person is a designation (prajñapti) over a causal series of momentary dharmas.
- **Key arguments**: closed list of ultimates; entity/designation dilemma; ownership as causation; action as a complete causal chain; parsimony.
- **Relation to site tenets**: conflicts with the persisting subject (background commitment 1) and with the agent-causal reading of Tenet 3. It is compatible with Dualism's negative claim, since dharmas include irreducible mental ones.

### Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika ātman
- **Core claim**: a permanent, non-physical self-substance in which cognition, desire and effort inhere. Treated in [[indian-philosophy-of-mind]].
- **Relation to site tenets**: this, not Pudgalavāda, is the closest Indian precedent for the Map's agent-subject. The Map differs by rejecting an *unchanging* core ([[self-and-self-consciousness]] "Diachronic Identity"). The Map's position therefore sits *between* the Pudgalavāda and the Nyāya view: real and particular like the pudgala, non-physical and agentive like the ātman, but not unchanging.

## Key Debates

### Which refutations reach the Map's subject

| Refutation | Target | Reaches the Map? |
|---|---|---|
| *Kathāvatthu* I.1: "known in the same way as a real and ultimate fact?" | Pudgala | **Partly.** It presupposes a closed list of ultimates, each known by immediate consciousness or under the 24 relations. The Map's subject is also not an object among objects, so the Map faces the same charge: an ultimate known in no standard way. Its reply (given as for-me-ness, not observed) is a framework difference, not a refutation. |
| *Kathāvatthu* rebirth trilemma (same / different / both) | Pudgala | **Weakly.** The Pudgalavādin had to answer "none of these". The Map answers "same", with numerical identity through qualitative change, so the form does not trap it. The cost is shifted onto the eternalism charge below. |
| AKBh entity/designation dilemma (p. 1314) | Pudgala | **Yes, but the Map takes a horn.** The Map says *entity*, and accepts Vasubandhu's own criterion that the real is causally efficient (Tenet 3). Owed: (a) "name its causes": the Map is silent on the subject's origin; (b) Gold's gloss that a caused person "must change as they change". The Map's reply, numerical identity through qualitative change, is the organism analogy in [[self-and-self-consciousness]]. Vasubandhu would say the organism is itself a designation, so the reply begs the question. This is the true bedrock point. |
| AKBh fire/fuel analysis (pp. 1315–17) | Pudgala | **Mostly no.** It targets the *neither-same-nor-different* relation, which the Map does not assert of subject and brain. |
| AKBh ineffable perception (p. 1319) | Pudgala | **Partly.** It reaches any claim to introspective access to the subject. The Map rests on givenness of for-me-ness, not perception of a subject, but the pages should not say the subject is "found" in experience. |
| AKBh ownership as mastery (pp. 1340–41): "Memory belongs to whatever causes memory" | Pudgala | **Splits.** It reaches ownership-as-control, which the Map already concedes (constrained influence). It does **not** reach ownership as phenomenal mineness. The Buddhist rejoinder there is different: reflexive awareness (svasaṃvedana) without an owner, which is the live contemporary debate ([[mine-ness]], [[witness-consciousness]]). |
| AKBh agent argument (pp. 1351–52): memory → desire → imagination → effort → action; "what activity do the Vaisesikas attribute to a 'soul'?" | Vaiśeṣika ātman | **Yes, directly.** This is a mental-causal-closure argument against an agent-self. The Map's reply must be that the chain is not sufficient at the selection point (the minimal quantum interaction story). Vasubandhu's own definition of agent, "amongst all its causes, that which is the principal cause", is one the Map can accept. |
| "What kind of controller?" plus parsimony (Gold) | Non-Buddhist self | **Answered** on control ([[buddhist-perspectives-on-meaning]] already says the subject claims "constrained influence, not sovereignty"). The **parsimony** leg is live: it is Tenet 5's target, and the Map's reply is Tenet 5 itself. |
| Personalist unpredictability argument vs "self-regulating system" (Gold) | Reverse direction | An ancient ancestor of the Map's own argument from agency. Vasubandhu's reply is the ancestor of the compatibilist/predictive-processing reply. Useful as a *historical symmetry*, not as evidence. |

### Is the ownership horn "near bedrock"?
- [[buddhist-perspectives-on-meaning]] currently says the ownership horn is harder and "near bedrock". Vasubandhu's actual ownership argument (verified above) converts "whose?" into "what caused it?". That dissolves ownership-as-mastery, and the Map does not need mastery. What remains is the for-me-ness sense, where the Buddhist opponent is Dignāga-style reflexive awareness (ownerless mineness), not Vasubandhu's causal reduction. **Suggestion**: the page could say *which* ownership sense is at bedrock. This is a refinement lead, not a defect, and it should be checked against [[mine-ness]] before briefing.

### Does the pudgala posit a "further fact"?
- [[haecceity]] L125 says: "That further fact's nearest precedent is the Pudgalavāda 'person'". Carpenter's reconstruction has the Pudgalavādin say "This is no further fact ... no extra entity", while her fn. 67 leaves open that it may be "a variant of the 'further fact view'". Priestley reports that the later sources made it substantial. **The claim is defensible but contested.** A qualifier ("on some reconstructions") would make it accurate. Refinement lead only.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **`concepts/pudgalavada` (recommended)**: "Pudgalavāda: the Buddhist Personalist Precedent", about 1,800–2,200 words (concepts soft band). The lead should say it is the nearest *Buddhist* precedent for the persisting subject and should state its limit in the first paragraph (inseparable from the aggregates; no agent). ## Gaps in Research

- **No surviving Pudgalavādin text was read.** The *Sāṃmitīyanikāya Śāstra* (Venkataramanan 1953 trans.) and the *Tridharmakhandaka* are known only through Priestley's reports. Every Pudgalavādin argument in this note comes via Priestley (sympathetic reconstruction) or via hostile sources (*Kathāvatthu*, Vasubandhu).
- **Siderits 2003 pp. 89–96** (non-reductive supervenience reading) is [R] via Coseru only.
- **Duerlinger 2003** and his 1982 *Philosophy East and West* paper on fire and fuel were not read. The AKBh passages were verified in Pruden instead.
- **Carpenter 2015** was verified against a 2010 author draft, not the OUP text.
- **Kathāvatthu OCR** is noisy. Only lines legible without reconstruction are quoted. The Pāli (e.g. *saccikaṭṭhaparamaṭṭhena*) was not checked against the PTS edition.
- **The *Kathāvatthu* date** is traditional (Third Council). Modern scholarship dates its growth over centuries; this was not researched.
- **Reflexive awareness (svasaṃvedana) as the real rejoinder on ownership** is flagged, not researched. Before any article leans on the "ownership splits" claim, check Dignāga/Dharmakīrti sources and Siderits–Thompson–Zahavi (eds.), *Self, No Self?* (OUP 2011).
- **Personalist freedom argument**: [V] only as Gold's report of AKBh. The corresponding Pruden passage was not located in the OCR.
