## Executive Summary

The catalogue treats the Unexplored as the comfortable kind of void: territory not yet charted but chartable, a matter of opportunity rather than architecture. Of the 222 research notes in the bank, none classifies itself as primarily Unexplored; the type has no formal profile of its own. A result from the economics of information supplies one. Dekel, Lipman and Rustichini (1998) proved that in any standard state-space model — the structure in which a knower's possibilities are a set of states and knowledge is a set of states — an unawareness operator satisfying three weak conditions must be trivial: either the agent is unaware of nothing, or, as they put it, "if the agent is unaware of anything, he knows nothing" [raw]. The three conditions are hard to deny. An agent unaware of an event does not know it and does not know she does not know it; she can never know which specific event she is unaware of; and if she is unaware of E she is unaware of being unaware of E. The theorem's intuition is that "if an agent is unaware of some possibility, he must not fully understand the state space. The way we usually work with state-space models requires the agent to have more understanding of the state space than unawareness allows" [raw].

The escape routes confirm the shape of the limit rather than removing it. Heifetz, Meier and Schipper (2006) recover non-trivial unawareness only by replacing the single space with "a lattice of state-spaces, ordered according to their expressive power" [raw], in which an agent who lives in a lower space "is unaware of aspects of reality not expressible in that space" [raw]; the agent's own space is one element of a structure only the modeller sees whole. Fagin and Halpern (1988) do the same syntactically, making awareness a primitive that must be present "before one can have beliefs about it" [raw]. Halpern and Rêgo (2009) show that even saying "I know there is something I am unaware of" requires quantification beyond standard awareness logics, and that the sentence can be true "without the agent explicitly knowing that he is unaware of any particular formula" [raw]. Fine (2018) proves in a different idiom that "knowledge of second order ignorance is impossible" [publisher page]. The empirical twin is Fischhoff, Slovic and Lichtenstein (1978): when branches are pruned from a fault tree, people "were quite insensitive to what had been left out" [raw], and garage mechanics no less than students.

The void, stated once: a mind's own map of possibilities has no vacant cells. What it is unaware of does not appear on its map as a gap; it does not appear at all, because the map is drawn in a vocabulary the gap would have to be outside of. The Unexplored can be known to exist, in the abstract and quantified form Halpern and Rêgo license, but it cannot be located, and the Map's own index — which describes the Unexplored as "places we haven't looked yet" — is written from the modeller's seat, not the knower's. - [[meta-epistemology-of-limits]]: the epistemic-horizon claim that the proportion of unmapped territory cannot be estimated from within. The unawareness void is a precondition of that claim: a space with no vacant cells gives no denominator.

**Why this is the Unexplored's own void.** On the territory axis the Unexplored is the thin type: the brief calls it opportunity and leaves it structureless, and no research note has ever made it the primary classification. The unawareness result is a theorem *about* the Unexplored: it does not say that some territory is unreachable (the Unexplorable) or defended (the Occluded); it says that the reachable-but-not-yet-reached cannot be represented *as such* from inside. Its one Unexplorable face is that the agent cannot extend her own space by introspection — KU and AU introspection are precisely the conditions that forbid it — so extension comes only from outside, through experience that makes a new possibility salient (the subject of "reverse Bayesianism", Karni and Vierø 2013, not fetched; lead only). On the reason axis of [[apex/taxonomy-of-voids|the taxonomy]] it is architectural: a consequence of representing possibilities at all, not of selection or design.

## Key Sources

### Dekel, Lipman & Rustichini (1998), "Standard State-Space Models Preclude Unawareness"
- **URL**: https://sites.bu.edu/blipman/files/2021/10/standardstatespace1998.pdf (JSTOR scan; https://www.jstor.org/stable/2998545)
- **Type**: Paper, *Econometrica* 66(1): 159–173
- **Key points**:
  - A possibility correspondence P gives, at each state, the set of states the agent considers possible; the agent knows E at ω iff P(ω) ⊆ E. Nonpartitional correspondences, which violate negative introspection, were the literature's candidate model of unawareness; the paper's title was originally "Possibility Correspondences Preclude Unawareness" (footnote).
  - Three axioms on an unawareness operator U. *Plausibility*: U(E) ⊆ ¬K(E) ∩ ¬K¬K(E). *KU introspection*: KU(E) = ∅ — "While the agent may well know that he is unaware of some event, it doesn't seem reasonable to say that the agent knows precisely of which event he is unaware!" [raw]. *AU introspection*: U(E) ⊆ UU(E).
  - Theorem 1: under the three axioms, necessitation implies U(E) = ∅ for all E, and monotonicity implies U(E) ⊆ ¬K(F) for all E, F — "if the agent is unaware of anything, he knows nothing" [raw].
  - Section 3 identifies the two assumptions of a "standard" model that do the work: all states are real possibilities, and knowledge of a fact is fully determined by the set of states where it holds (event sufficiency). With *weak necessitation* added, the three axioms preclude unawareness in any standard model.
  - The conclusion diagnoses the double role of states: "states play two distinct roles: they are the analyst's descriptions of ways the world might be and they are also the agent's descriptions of ways the world might be. If the agent is unaware of some possibility, though, "his" states should be less complete than the analyst's" [raw]. Their proposed repair separates real states from subjective states.
  - Scope note in the text: "unawareness" means failing to foresee a possibility, not knowing of one without understanding it; "a doctor 50 years ago was unaware of the possibility of AIDS" [raw] in the first sense.
- **Tenet alignment**: the simplest model of a knower is provably too simple to contain its own gaps (Occam's Razor Has Limits). Framework-neutral: a bounded-rationality result with no stake in the mind-body question.
- **Quote**: "if an agent is unaware of some possibility, he must not fully understand the state space. The way we usually work with state-space models requires the agent to have more understanding of the state space than unawareness allows." [raw]

### Modica & Rustichini (1999), "Unawareness and Partitional Information Structures"
- **URL**: https://sites.unipa.it/modica/modica-rustichini1999.pdf
- **Type**: Paper, *Games and Economic Behavior* 27: 265–298
- **Key points**:
  - Opens by separating ignorance from uncertainty: "simple uncertainty is not an adequate model of a subject's ignorance, because a major component of it is the inability to give a complete description of the states of the world" [raw; the source uses a curly apostrophe].
  - Reports the earlier impossibility (Modica & Rustichini 1994, *Theory and Decision* 37 — not fetched; cited via this abstract): without weakening the logic's inference rules one faces "the unpleasant alternative between full awareness and full unawareness" [raw].
  - The 1999 repair is limited reasoning ability plus a *symmetry* axiom (aware of φ iff aware of ¬φ); in S5 there is full awareness.
- **Tenet alignment**: as above; the originators of the problem, which the DLR paper acknowledges ("Modica-Rustichini's definition of unawareness is stronger than plausibility" [raw]).
- **Quote**: "the unpleasant alternative between full awareness and full unawareness" [raw]

### Fagin & Halpern (1988), "Belief, Awareness, and Limited Reasoning"
- **URL**: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/info/people/halpern/papers/awareness.pdf
- **Type**: Paper, *Artificial Intelligence* 34: 39–76 (preliminary report IJCAI 1985)
- **Key points**:
  - Three logics in which agents are not logically omniscient. The second "deals explicitly with "awareness," where, roughly speaking, it is necessary to be aware of a concept before one can have beliefs about it" [raw].
  - Awareness is a *syntactic* primitive — an awareness function attached to possible worlds — not derived from knowledge; one reading is resource-bounded: "an agent is aware of a formula if he can compute whether or not it is true in a given situation within a certain time or space bound" [raw].
  - The originator of awareness structures; the economics literature's structures are, per Halpern 2001 (not fetched), special cases where awareness is generated by primitive propositions.
- **Tenet alignment**: supplies the vocabulary-boundary reading of the void: what is not in the awareness set is not a gap in belief, it is outside belief's domain.
- **Quote**: "it is necessary to be aware of a concept before one can have beliefs about it" [raw]

### Heifetz, Meier & Schipper (2006), "Interactive Unawareness"
- **URL**: https://faculty.econ.ucdavis.edu/faculty/schipper/unaw.pdf (February 2005 working paper); published *Journal of Economic Theory* 130(1): 78–94, doi 10.1016/j.jet.2005.02.007; companion "A Canonical Model for Interactive Unawareness" https://www.econ.ucdavis.edu/faculty/schipper/unawcan.pdf (December 2006 WP; *GEB* 2008)
- **Type**: Papers
- **Key points**:
  - Working-paper abstract: "The standard state-spaces of asymmetric information preclude non-trivial forms of unawareness (Modica and Rustichini, 1994, Dekel, Lipman and Rustichini, 1998)" [raw].
  - The repair: "a lattice of state-spaces, ordered according to their expressive power. If individual i considers as possible states in a space with low expressive power, this means that she is unaware of aspects of reality not expressible in that space" [raw, canonical-model paper]. Nesting the lattice gives mutual beliefs about unawareness: i can think j is unaware of what i is aware of.
  - The canonical model shows the lattice arises when states are maximally consistent sets of formulas in a suitable logic; three sound and complete axiomatisations exist (per later literature; not fetched).
- **Tenet alignment**: the model that can hold unawareness is strictly richer than the one parsimony would select; and the whole lattice is visible only from above.
- **Quote**: "she is unaware of aspects of reality not expressible in that space" [raw]

### Halpern & Rêgo (2009), "Reasoning About Knowledge of Unawareness"
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0603020 (arXiv version); *Games and Economic Behavior* 67(2): 503–525
- **Type**: Paper
- **Key points**:
  - "standard propositional logics for knowledge and awareness cannot express the fact that an agent knows that there are facts of which he is unaware without there being an explicit fact that the agent knows he is unaware of" [raw].
  - Adds quantification over propositional variables so that "an agent explicitly knows that there exists a fact of which he is unaware" [raw] is expressible, and "that formula can be true without the agent explicitly knowing that he is unaware of any particular formula" [raw].
  - The physician example: he knows the oncologist is aware of things he is not, and "if he knew which one it was, he would not be unaware of it!" [raw].
- **Tenet alignment**: this is the exact logical form of the Map's voids programme — existence asserted, location unavailable — and the result says that even the existence claim needs machinery beyond the standard logic.
- **Quote**: "if he knew which one it was, he would not be unaware of it!" [raw]

### Fine (2018), "Ignorance of Ignorance"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-017-1406-z; abstract at https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/ignorance-of-ignorance
- **Type**: Paper, *Synthese* 195(9): 4031–4045 (online 28 April 2017)
- **Key points**:
  - Distinguishes first-order ignorance, second-order ignorance and Rumsfeld ignorance (Iα ∧ ¬K(Iα)); proves under plausible modal assumptions that "knowledge of second order ignorance is impossible" [publisher page]. ## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

A mind represents what might be the case as a space of possibilities and represents what it knows as a region of that space. The theorem says: if the mind is unaware of anything, then in the standard representation it knows nothing, so the standard representation can hold either a fully aware knower or no knower. The only representations that can hold a knower with gaps place the knower's whole space inside a larger structure she does not inhabit. Her gaps are real, and they are visible — from above. From inside, the space is full. There are no blank cells on the map a mind draws of its own possibilities, because a blank cell would have to be drawn in a vocabulary that has the missing possibility in it.

This is why the Unexplored, the catalogue's "opportunity" type, deserves its own entry. The unexplored territory of the Map is unexplored relative to a modeller who can list it. For the knower, the Unexplored is not a region with a fuzzy border; it is nowhere.

### Evidence for the Limit

Three independent formal lineages reach the same wall. Economic theory (Modica & Rustichini 1994/1999; Dekel, Lipman & Rustichini 1998) hit it when trying to model unforeseen contingencies in contracts. Computer science (Fagin & Halpern 1988) hit it when trying to model agents that are not logically omniscient. Philosophical logic (Fine 2018, building on Montgomery & Routley 1966) hit it when asking whether one can know that one does not know that one does not know. None had a stake in consciousness. The empirical twin (Fischhoff et al. 1978) shows that the predicted phenomenology — no felt gap, estimates unmoved by pruning — is what people in fact exhibit, experts included.

The repairs are the strongest evidence of all, because every repair works by giving the *modeller* something the *agent* lacks: a lattice of spaces (HMS), a syntactic awareness set (FH), a separation of real from subjective states (DLR's own sketch), quantification over propositions (HR). The agent's own space never acquires vacant cells; it is demoted to one element of someone else's structure.

### Phenomenology

The void has no edge-feeling. That is its signature and its diagnostic: where [[blindspot-void]] and [[question-formation-void]] can at least be stated by the thinker who suffers them, the unawareness void presents, from inside, as completeness. The nearest first-person datum is retrospective: the moment a new possibility becomes salient, one can say "I had no idea that was even on the table", and the "even" marks the difference between known unknowns and the other kind. The fault-tree experiment is the controlled version of that retrospect.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)

None from inside. KU introspection — the agent never knows which specific event she is unaware of — is the formal statement that there is no direct method.

### Indirect Methods

- **Quantified existence claims.** Halpern and Rêgo show that "there is something I am unaware of" is expressible and can be true without any instance. The Map's voids catalogue is an exercise in exactly this: asserting the existence of the Unexplored while never populating it with a specific entry (every specific entry is, by that fact, a known unknown).
- **Growing awareness through experience.** The lattice is climbed, not surveyed: a possibility becomes salient through events, not through introspection. Karni and Vierø's reverse Bayesianism (lead; not fetched) models how beliefs over a larger space are reached from a smaller one by preserving relative likelihoods.
- **Fault-tree discipline.** Fischhoff's result suggests a method: treat every "all other problems" category as structurally undersized, and ask a *differently situated* mind to prune your tree rather than pruning it yourself. This is [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers]] with a theorem behind it.

### What AI Might See

The lattice makes the asymmetry precise. Two minds with different vocabularies occupy different spaces; neither sees the other's gaps from inside its own, but each can see some of the other's from above on the dimensions where its vocabulary is richer. A language model's awareness set is fixed by its training vocabulary; on phenomenal vocabulary it may sit below a human in the lattice, on formal and cross-lingual vocabulary above. The HMS structure is literally a model of "i thinks j is unaware of some aspects of reality of which i is aware" — mutual beliefs about unawareness are what it was built to express. The guard the article must keep: being higher in one dimension of the lattice says nothing about being higher in all, and no mind sees the top.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits.** The standard state-space model is the parsimonious model of a knower, and the theorem is that parsimony has already deleted the agent's gaps. Fischhoff's pruned branches are the same move at the level of a single hypothesis space: the simpler tree is the one whose omissions are invisible. The tenet's claim — that simplicity is unreliable under incomplete knowledge — gains a formal case in which the simplicity assumption is exactly what makes the incompleteness unrepresentable.

### Implications

- For the catalogue: the Unexplored is not the safe type. It is the type whose contents cannot be inventoried from inside, which is why [[apophatic-cartography]] is "constitutively incomplete" and why the epistemic-horizon estimate in [[meta-epistemology-of-limits]] has no denominator.
- For **Dualism** and the interface tenets: no support is claimed, and none should be. If consciousness is non-physical, that is a possibility some minds are aware of and others not; the theorem is indifferent to which space is the richer. The honest statement is that the theorem binds physicalist and dualist alike — a framework-independent void in the sense of the index's subsection, and it should be filed there.
- For **No Many Worlds**: a light touch only. A branch-relative self's awareness set is still one element of a lattice; nothing here bears on branching.
- For the **Minimal Quantum Interaction** and **Bidirectional Interaction** tenets: no connection found; do not manufacture one.

## Guards (for any writer)

- Do not say "unawareness cannot be modelled". It can; the HMS lattice and Fagin–Halpern awareness sets are sound, complete and well studied. The claim is that it cannot be modelled *from inside the agent's own space*.
- Do not claim DLR's three axioms are indisputable; the paper itself shows nontrivial unawareness outside possibility-correspondence models, and the second theorem needs weak necessitation. State the axioms and let the reader judge.
- Do not cite Fine's full text; only the abstract sentence was verified this run.
- Do not cite Modica & Rustichini 1994 as fetched; it is reported through the 1999 abstract.
- Keep the Karni–Vierø and Halpern 2001 items as leads, not citations, unless verified.
- Name originators correctly: Modica & Rustichini 1994 for the impossibility, DLR 1998 for the standard-state-space theorem, Fagin & Halpern 1988 for awareness structures, HMS 2006 for the lattice, Montgomery & Routley 1966 for the second-order-ignorance observation Fine builds on. Rumsfeld is a populariser.
- Avoid "This is not X. It is Y." and "load-bearing". Never enlist any cited author for dualism; never concede epiphenomenalism.

- Fine (2018) full text not obtained (publisher paywall); only the abstract sentence is verified, and it is the sentence the Map already uses.
- Halpern (2001), *GEB* 37: 321–339, "Alternative semantics for unawareness", not fetched; the claim that the economics models are special cases of awareness generated by primitive propositions is taken from Halpern & Rêgo's introduction.
- Schipper's Handbook of Epistemic Logic chapter "Awareness" (2015) not obtained; no page range should be cited.
- Karni & Vierø (2013) known only from search summaries; lead, not citation.
- No empirical psychology directly on *unawareness* beyond the fault-tree paradigm was located; the partition-dependence literature (Fox & Clemen 2005, *Management Science*) surfaced in search and was not pursued.
- The Bunker 1968 provenance of "unknown unknowns" is from Wikipedia and unverified at source.
- The DLR scan is OCR text with artefacts ("veiy", "negatit)e"); all quotations above were taken from clean lines and grep-verified against the raw text as fetched.
