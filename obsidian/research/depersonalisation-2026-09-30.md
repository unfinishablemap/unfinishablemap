## Executive Summary

**Verdict: proceed, as a medium concept page at `concepts/depersonalisation`.** The six Map sections that restate the datum are each partial in a different way, the de facto host `concepts/mine-ness` sits at 3,497 body words against a 3,500 hard limit and cannot absorb anything, and the primary literature the Map already cites is deeper and more divided than any of the six restatements shows. The page's job is to state the clinical datum once, with its diagnostic structure (insight retained; the "as if" preface; perception and cognition intact), to give the four rival readings their own paragraphs, and to deliver the same two-tier verdict the Cotard page holds: a defensible *architecture* claim (something normally bound to experience comes apart from its content without substrate damage, and whether it is one feature or several is open) and a *null significance* claim (no reading on the table discriminates dualism from physicalism, and the author whose work the Map leans on hardest, Alexandre Billon, concludes that mineness "can only be explained in purely neurophysiological ... terms").

Three corpus corrections fall out of the citation pass and should travel with the article: (1) `mine-ness` L72 quotes Billon as "the lack of a phenomenal feature that marks experiences as mine"; the 2023 chapter reads "the lack of a phenomenal feature that marks my experiences as mine" (the dropped "my" is small but the sentence is in quotation marks); (2) `mine-ness` L176 and two apparatus files give Ciaunica, Charlton and Farmer the page range 823–842, which is wrong — the article is *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 20(1): 1–19 (online 6 June 2020, issue February 2021); (3) `phenomenal-quality-void` L96 presents Billon (2024) as *raising* whether depersonalisation is "a delusion or an insight", whereas the paper raises it in order to reject the insight reading and to "diagnose the intuition of unreality" — the Map's open-question framing misrepresents his conclusion. Also: the corpus's "Sass & Pienkos (2013)" has four authors (Sass, Pienkos, Nelson and Medford), and the "behind glass" / "two-dimensional" patient phrases that `phenomenal-quality-void` attributes to it are not in its abstract and were not verified in its full text (paywalled), so they are leads, not citations.

**Which restatement the article should treat as canonical.** On separability logic, `mine-ness`; on the active-inference rival and the discipline about what the datum does and does not show, `clinical-dissociation`; on the as-if / insight structure, `cotard-delusion` (because its wording is grep-verified against Billon 2016). ## Key Sources

Retrieval status is given per source: **full text** (grep-verifiable), **abstract** (raw, from PubMed, the journal page or the Wayback copy), **metadata only** (Crossref/PubMed record, nothing read).

### Diagnostic criteria (DSM-5 300.6 / F48.1; ICD-11 6B66)
- **Type**: manual criteria, transcribed by two secondary sites that agree verbatim (psychdb.com; traumadissociation.com); ICD-11 wording from a third (mrcpsych.uk). Not read in the manuals themselves.
- **Key points** (raw, secondary transcription): Criterion A, depersonalisation: "Experiences of unreality, detachment, or being an outside observer with respect to one's thoughts, feelings, sensations, body, or actions (e.g., perceptual alterations, distorted sense of time, unreal or absent self, emotional and/or physical numbing)". Criterion B: "During the depersonalization or derealization experiences, reality testing remains intact." ICD-11 6B66: "experiencing the self as strange or unreal, or feeling detached from, or as though one were an outside observer of, one's thoughts, feelings, sensations, body, or actions"; derealisation: "experiencing other persons, objects, or the world as strange or unreal (e.g., dreamlike, distant, foggy, lifeless, colourless, or visually distorted)".
- **What this settles for the Map**: insight retention is *diagnostic* (Criterion B), not a Map reading. The "as if" is not literal DSM wording; it belongs to the phenomenological tradition and the Cambridge Depersonalisation Scale items (below), and to Billon's characterisation of depersonalisation as "the 'as if' form of the Cotard syndrome". The article should cite the criterion for insight and the CDS/Billon for the "as if".
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral.

### Billon (2016), "Making Sense of the Cotard Syndrome: Insights from the Study of Depersonalisation", *Mind & Language* 31(3): 356–391
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/mila.12110 — **full text** (Wayback copy of the PhilArchive preprint, 16,149 words; the HAL copy is Anubis-blocked).
- **Key points** (raw): "Even though they are not delusional, depersonalised patients seem to have experiences that are quite similar to those of Cotard patients. I argue that these experiences are essentially characterised by a (more or less important) lack of subjective character and of two other structural features of experience, which I call 'the present character' and 'the actual character.' Cotard's nihilistic delusions simply consist in taking these anomalous experiences at face value." On the as-if: "the latter just seem to be Cotard patient's delusions prefaced by a neutralising 'it is as if.' The Cotard syndrome can thus be characterised as the delusional form of depersonalisation, depersonalisation being, conversely, the 'as if' form of the Cotard syndrome." On the symptom clusters, with CDS items: "Desomatisation experiences. As if they missed bodily parts ('Parts of my body feel as if they didn't belong to me' (Sierra and Berrios, 2000, 160))"; "Dementalisation experiences"; "Death and nonexistence experiences. As if they were dead". On the status of the core claim: "The lack of subjective character is not in dispute. It is not however usually acknowledged that such a lack of subjectivity can explain most aspects of depersonalisation". On degrees: "the lack of subjective character can vary both in extension and in intensity." On the strongest form: "Nonexistence delusions would be explained by a more intense and global—maybe sometimes total—lack of subjective character, which would make the subject feel as if he is not an 'I' (nonexistence experience)." He names the affective rival explicitly: "Sierra and David (2011, 163) argue that this is what happens in depersonalisation: '[A] frontally driven suppression of activity in the amygdala'".
- **Why it matters**: the source of the three characters the Map's `clinical-phenomenology` lists unattributed; the source of the as-if / delusional-form pairing the Cotard page uses. Note the phrase "make the subject feel as if he is not an 'I'": the *subject* persists and feels; what is lacking is the subjective *character* of the experience. That is the correct statement of what the driver brief calls the "no-subject" reading — it is a no-subjective-character reading, and the "as if" is kept.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral on Tenet 1 at the architecture tier; see the two-tier section.

### Billon (2023), "What Is It Like to Lack Mineness? Depersonalization as a Probe for the Scope, Nature, and Role of Mineness"
- **Citation (resolved)**: in M. García-Carpintero and M. Guillot (eds), *Self-Experience: Essays on Inner Awareness*, Oxford University Press, pp. 314–342. DOI 10.1093/oso/9780198805397.003.0014 (Crossref: print 2023-02-02; OUP's page shows online 2023-01-19). **This is a different work from Billon (2024)** — the corpus's single "Billon, A. (2023)" entry (in `mine-ness`) is this chapter and is correctly dated; the five "Billon (2024)" cites are the *Metaphilosophy* paper. Not an online-first/print pair.
- **Retrieval**: **full text** (Wayback copy of the PhilArchive preprint, 12,701 words).
- **Key points** (raw): "I argue that depersonalization consists in the lack of a phenomenal feature that marks my experiences as mine, which is usually called 'mineness,' and that the study of depersonalization constitutes a neglected yet incomparable probe to assess empirically the scope, role, and even the nature of mineness." On the "as if" (footnote 5): "Patients' reports have a characteristic 'as if' form: depersonalized subjects says that it is as if P: as if their body or their thoughts wasn't theirs, as if they were dead or inexistent … The 'as if' is usually interpreted as a 'mark of mere appearance,' by which the patients mean that it seems to them as if P or that they have an experience as of P even though they know that P is false. It might however sometimes be a mark of approximation ... (see Radovic and Radovic, 2002)." On mineness versus the sense of ownership: "mineness and the sense of ownership are different. The sense of ownership is a form of awareness. Mineness is a feature of experience." On scope: "Universalists claim that all conscious states come with a mark of mineness. Gallagher (2004) and Zahavi (2008) are universalists"; "The case of depersonalization thus disconfirms nihilism and existentialism, and vindicates the theory of error put forward by the Cartesian camp." Conclusion: "This probe gives us good reason to believe that mineness is a general, if not universal, feature of our experiences ... Finally, this probe allows us to reject many psychological theories that have been put forward to explain mineness and suggests that it might be psychologically irreducible." And §7: "This challenge suggests that such reductive theories must fail and that mineness can only be explained in purely neurophysiological (as opposed to 'psychological or neuropsychological') terms."
- **Why it matters**: the Map's definitional source for mineness-as-separable-feature. Two things the article must carry from it. First, Billon is a *generalist*, not a universalist: on his view mineness is normally present and can be partially or globally lacking, which is exactly the separability claim the Map wants, and he regards depersonalisation as having settled the scope question against the "nihilists" (no mineness) and the "existentialists" (mineness only in reflection). Second, his verdict on *nature* is reductive at the neurophysiological level: psychological reductions fail, neurophysiological explanation is where mineness lives. He would not accept a dualist upgrade. In the source-role table this is *supporting* at level 1–2 (separability, phenomenological reality) and *recruited-by* at level 4.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1 compatible, not suggestive.

### Billon (2024), "The psychopathology of metaphysics: Depersonalization and the problem of reality", *Metaphilosophy* 55(1): 3–30
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/meta.12666 (Crossref: online 2024-02-05, issue January 2024). **Full text** (Wayback copy of the Wiley page, 20,565 words incl. references). Two corpus apparatus files (`research/voids-perceptual-reality-monitoring-void-2026-05-15`, `reviews/deep-review-2026-06-05-introspection-architecture-void-cluster`) give "55(2)"; the issue is 55(1).
- **Key points** (raw): "DPD is a psychiatric condition defined by long-lasting impressions of the unreality of oneself and one's mental states (call this 'trait depersonalization' [DP]) and of the world around one (call this 'trait derealization' [DR]) without delusional ideas." "DPD, as I have explained, is accompanied by insight". The paper's project: "This essay draws on studies of these patients in order to reject, and indeed diagnose, the intuition of unreality and recent metaphysical doctrines drawing on it, such as structuralism, digitalism, and virtual realism." Its conclusion: "If, as I have argued, this impression of reality is an impression of substantiality, we should also conclude that our experiences give us some insight into the deep nature of things. The intuition of reality is correct, then, and it is an intuition of substantiality." On Cotard: "the patients do not just feel as if they or the things around are unreal but bluntly claim that they are unreal ... Cotard delusion is usually considered the delusional version of DPD".
- **Why it matters**: it is the Map's source for the derealisation side (actual character / reality-feeling). `phenomenal-quality-void` L96 asks on Billon's behalf whether the patients were "seeing through the reality-stamp to something normally concealed"; Billon considers that hypothesis and argues the other way. The article should state his conclusion and may still use the question as the Map's own.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral; Billon's substantiality conclusion is not a dualist one.

### Billon (2017), "Basic Self-Awareness", *European Journal of Philosophy* 25(3): 732–763
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/ejop.12168 — **full text** (Wayback copy of the PhilArchive preprint, 15,597 words).
- **Key points** (raw, abstract): "Patients suffering from severe forms of depersonalization often feel reluctant to use the first-person and can even, in delusional cases, avoid it altogether, systematically referring to themselves in the third-person ... I argue that depersonalized patients indeed have an impaired basic self-awareness ... According to the favored theory, which I call Cartesian, we are basically self-aware in virtue of being acquainted with ourselves through introspection." Patient reports he collects: "I used to say 'we' rather than 'I'" (Shorvon 1946, 784); "[It is like] seeing life as if it were played like a film in a movie. But in that case where am I? Who is watching the film?" (Simeon and Abugel 2006, 15).
- **Why it matters**: the "reported from somewhere" datum the Cotard page uses has its depersonalisation form here — the patient who asks "who is watching the film?" is still asking.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral.

### Ciaunica, Charlton and Farmer, "When the Window Cracks: Transparency and the Fractured Self in Depersonalisation"
- **Citation (resolved)**: *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 20(1): 1–19. Online 6 June 2020; issue February 2021. DOI 10.1007/s11097-020-09677-z. Open access. **Full text** (Greenwich repository PDF; its running head reads "Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences (2021) 20:1–19"). **Corpus inconsistency**: `necessary-opacity` cites 2020 without volume; `mine-ness` cites 2021, 20, "823-842"; the ownership-void research note and the 2026-07-22 Gemini outer review repeat "823-842". The range 823–842 does not belong to this article. Recommended canonical form: Ciaunica, Charlton and Farmer (2021), 20(1), 1–19, "online 2020" if the date matters.
- **Key points** (raw): "we argue that DPD may alter the transparency of basic embodied forms of pre-reflective self-consciousness, as well as the capacity to flexibly modulate and switch between the reflective and pre-reflective facets of self-awareness"; "DPD ... may reveals its tacit, transparent and taken for granted presence in our everyday life, in the same way that a crack in a transparent glass reveals the presence of an unnoticed window"; "It is typical for DPD experiences to be characterised as a 'divorce' or 'fracture' between an observing and observed self". On insight: "despite the dramatic nature of their 'estrangement' and self-detachment, DPD patients are aware that these are subjective phenomena rather than the objective reality." On the mechanism: "These alterations increase the incidence of compensatory hyper-reflective mentalistic forms of self-awareness, with consequent self-opacity, and lack of immersiveness". A patient's own metaphor: "it is as if the brain creates an 'experiential airbag' which automatically inflates as soon as there is a risk of being crushed by an extreme danger".
- **Why it matters**: the source of the Map's transparency-cracking frame in `mine-ness`, `necessary-opacity` and `phenomenal-quality-void`. The framework is Zahavi/Gallagher pre-reflective self-consciousness plus predictive processing — the authors are not separability theorists in Billon's sense; what "cracks" for them is transparency, and the compensation is hyper-reflection.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral; the account is physicalist.

### Ciaunica, Seth, Limanowski, Hesp and Friston (2022), "I overthink—Therefore I am not", *Consciousness and Cognition* 101: 103320
- DOI 10.1016/j.concog.2022.103320. **Abstract** (PubMed). This is the active-inference paper (the driver brief's "Zoomed out" alternative is a different, later Ciaunica paper on digital media — not cited in the corpus and not pursued).
- **Key points** (raw): "The key idea here is that depersonalisation disorder reflects the remarkable capacity to explain perceptual engagement with the world via the hypothesis that 'I am an embodied perceiver, but I am not in control of my perception'. We suggest that individuals with depersonalisation may believe that 'another agent' is controlling their thoughts, perceptions or actions, while maintaining full insight that the 'other agent' is 'me' (the self)." Mechanism: "failures to attenuate or modulate sensory precision".
- **Why it matters**: `clinical-dissociation` already summarises it correctly ("impaired attenuation of self-generated signals"). The "full insight that the 'other agent' is 'me'" clause is the active-inference statement of Criterion B.
- **Tenet alignment**: the fully specified physicalist rival.

### Seth, Suzuki and Critchley (2012), "An Interoceptive Predictive Coding Model of Conscious Presence", *Frontiers in Psychology* 2: 395
- DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2011.00395 (the DOI carries the 2011 volume; published 10 January 2012). **Full text** (PMC).
- **Key points** (raw): "The model associates presence with successful suppression by top-down predictions of informative interoceptive signals"; DPD is "characterized by the chronic circumscribed" loss of presence; "depersonalization (loss of subjective sense of reality of the self) and derealization (loss of subjective sense of reality of the world)"; the paper notes "the condition is best considered a syndrome, as chronic depersonalization involves qualitative changes in subjective experience across a range of experiential domains (Sierra et al., 2005; Simeon et al., 2008)".
- **Tenet alignment**: physicalist rival; the Map already cites it that way.

### Deane, Miller and Wilkinson (2020), "Losing Ourselves: Active Inference, Depersonalization, and Meditation", *Frontiers in Psychology* 11: 539726
- DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2020.539726. **Full text** (Frontiers).
- **Key points** (raw): "People suffering from depersonalization can experience the loss of a sense of self as devastating, often accompanied by intense feelings of alienation, fear, and hopelessness. However, for meditative contemplatives from various traditions, 'selfless' experiences are highly sought after"; "we propose an account of the experiences of selfhood as emerging from a temporally deep generative model"; "depersonalization may result from an inferred loss of allostatic control".
- **Why it matters**: the source of `mine-ness` L88's valence point and the corrective to `necessary-opacity`'s "pathology, not liberation" — the same structural absence is reported as liberation when entered deliberately. Kirberg and Chadha (2024, *JCS* 31(5): 151–177, metadata only) is the newer philosophical treatment of the same comparison.

### Gerrans (2019, online 2018), "Depersonalization Disorder, Affective Processing and Predictive Coding", *Review of Philosophy and Psychology* 10(2): 401–418
- DOI 10.1007/s13164-018-0415-2. **Abstract** (Wayback copy of the Springer page); body paywalled. Knappik 2022 quotes pp. 404 and 412.
- **Key points** (raw abstract): "I argue that this feeling, baptized 'mineness' (Billon 2013, 2018a, b) or 'subjective presence' (Seth ... 2013) emerges from a multilevel interaction between emotional, affective and cognitive processing. The 'self' to which experience is attributed is a predictive model made by the mind to explain the modulation of affect as the organism progresses through the world. When the world no longer produces predicted affect the organism needs to explain this unpredicted absence of feeling. It is important to this account that cognition and perception are otherwise intact ... When that prediction is not fulfilled the organisms feels as if she is no longer present in experience."
- **Why it matters**: the modern form of reading (a) — the subtraction is affective, and "mineness" is the self-model's explanation of the absence. The Cotard page already carries Gerrans's 2023 interoceptive dissent; this is its depersonalisation-specific predecessor.

### Knappik (2022, print 2025), "No need for mineness: Depersonalization/Derealization Disorder and mental state types", *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 24(2): 411–435
- DOI 10.1007/s11097-022-09872-0; online 17 December 2022; issue April 2025. Open access. **Full text** (DNB copy, 13,387 words).
- **Key points** (raw): "In recent years, several philosophers have proposed accounts that explain DPD in terms of an alteration in global features of normal consciousness, such as 'mineness'. This article criticizes such accounts and develops an alternative approach, based on the observation that many mental states relate to the subject because of the kind of state they belong to." The global-feature accounts he targets: "For Alexandre Billon (2016, 2017, forthcoming), the relevant features are mineness, 'present character' and 'actual character'" ... Gerrans's "flow of affect" ... "for Anna Ciaunica and colleagues, pre-reflective self-consciousness and its transparency". His variability objection: "why can the depersonalization and the derealization dimension of DPD occur independently of each other? Billon tackles such problems by postulating three distinct global features of normal consciousness—mineness, actual character and present character ... But this just adds to the substantive theoretical assumptions". His PTSD counter-evidence against affect-first accounts: "a breakdown of affective feelings can leave the other factors intact ... (Frewen et al., 2015)". His own notion: "'type-specific self-relation' (TSSR) to collect a number of well-established phenomena where mental states present represented entities as standing in determinate relations to the subject"; "I argue that most symptoms of DPD can be understood as impairments in such forms of self-relation." He also records the tension inside the Zahavi camp: "the view that DPD is due to impaired pre-reflective self-consciousness is at odds with the view, held prominently by Zahavi, that consciousness always, in health and in disease, involves pre-reflective self-consciousness (cf. Zahavi, 2018, p. 713 f.)". Epidemiology he relies on: "it affects between 1 and 2% of the population (Hunter et al., 2004)".
- **Why it matters**: the plural-feature caveat `mine-ness` L92 already carries, but stronger than the Map states it — Knappik's claim is not that mineness is plural but that *no global feature is needed*: local, type-specific self-relations (the autonoetic self-relation of episodic memory, the bodily self-relation of sensation) break independently. The Map's reply at L92 ("even if mine-ness is plural, each form is still a separable phenomenal feature") survives, but the article must say that on Knappik's reading what separates is built into particular state types, not a mode-feature laid over all of them.

## Major Positions

### The Map's reading: for-me-ness as a separable phenomenal feature (held at *compatible*)
- **Proponents in the corpus**: `mine-ness`, `cotard-delusion`, `clinical-dissociation`.
- **Core claim**: content persists, the for-me character is degraded; therefore for-me-ness is a feature distinct from the bare having of experience.
- **Closest external position**: Billon 2016/2023 (generalist mineness; the lack "can vary both in extension and in intensity").
- **Where it must not overreach**: the four-level rule in `project/evidential-status-discipline`. Level 1 (separability) is the documented result. Level 2 (phenomenological reality) is where Billon and Zahavi disagree. Level 3 (mechanistic independence) is supplied by every rival — inhibitory, interoceptive, precision-weighting, TSSR. Level 4 is not reached by anything on the table.

### (a) Change in content: the inhibitory / affective reading
- **Proponents**: Sierra and Berrios 1998; Sierra and David 2011; Medford 2012; Phillips et al. 2001; Gerrans 2019 (affect-first, with the self as a predictive model that explains the absence).
- **Core claim**: what is subtracted is emotional colouring; "unreality or detachment" is how a system reports uncoloured perception and cognition. No for-me-ness component is posited.
- **Evidence it owns**: reduced autonomic and insula/amygdala response to aversive stimuli (Sierra et al. 2002; Phillips et al. 2001; Sierra and David 2011 abstract).
- **Pressure on it**: Knappik's PTSD point (emotional numbing without loss of self, agency or reality, Frewen et al. 2015 as cited); the CDS factor structure, where Emotional Numbing is one of four factors, not the root; Billon 2016's observation that nonexistence and dementalisation complaints are not plausibly affect reports.
- **Relation to the two tiers**: grants separability (affect from content) at the architecture tier under a different description; closes the significance tier.

### (b) Billon: lack of subjective character / mineness
- **Core claim**: experiences continue but lack the phenomenal feature that normally marks them as mine; the "as if" is a "mark of mere appearance"; the condition is non-delusional; the lack can be partial or global; mineness is "a feature of the mode rather than the content".
- **What "no-subject" means here**: the experience lacks subjective *character*; the subject remains and reports ("make the subject feel as if he is not an 'I'"). Billon's 2013 thought-insertion paper is where he argues consciousness does not entail subjectivity; the 2016/2023 depersonalisation work argues the same for mineness. He is not claiming subjectless experiences float free.
- **Relation to the two tiers**: the architecture claim at its strongest; the significance claim explicitly closed by Billon himself ("purely neurophysiological ... terms"). Source-role: supporting at levels 1–2, recruited-by at level 4.

### (c) Active-inference / interoceptive: attenuated self-modelling
- **Proponents**: Seth, Suzuki and Critchley 2012; Ciaunica et al. 2022; Deane, Miller and Wilkinson 2020; Gerrans 2019 and 2023 (interoceptive variant).
- **Core claim**: presence/mineness is the successful top-down suppression of interoceptive prediction error or the successful attenuation of self-generated signals; depersonalisation is the failure state, which the system explains with the hypothesis "I am an embodied perceiver, but I am not in control of my perception" while "maintaining full insight that the 'other agent' is 'me'".
- **Relation to the two tiers**: grants a separable self-model *variable* and predicts the global phenomenology from a single computational quantity — which is why `clinical-dissociation` L90 concedes the "precision objection" no longer favours the interface reading. Significance tier closed. This is the strongest-physicalist-rival the discipline document requires the article to name.

### (d) Zahavi / minimal-self: for-me-ness cannot be lost while experience persists
- **Proponents**: Zahavi and Kriegel 2016; Gallagher and Zahavi (SEP); Henriksen and Parnas 2019; Zahavi 2018 (not read).
- **Core claim**: for-me-ness is "a universal feature of experience" and "not ... a detachable self quale"; "once anything occurs consciously, it must be given to the subject and thus exhibit for-me-ness." Depersonalised experience is still "experienced by him as being part of his stream of consciousness"; what is impaired is the *sense of ownership* as an awareness, or "personal" as against "perspectival" ownership, or (Ciaunica's hybrid) the *transparency* of pre-reflective self-consciousness.
- **The plural-feature caveat as a distinct option**: Knappik 2022 drops global features altogether: type-specific self-relations (autonoetic, bodily, agentive) break locally and independently, which is why DP and DR dissociate and why the CDS yields four factors.
- **Relation to the two tiers**: this is the live dispute *inside* the architecture tier — is the separated thing a mode-feature (Billon), a form of awareness (Zahavi), transparency (Ciaunica), affect (Sierra, Gerrans) or a bundle of type-specific relations (Knappik)? Nothing in (d) bears on physicalism; Zahavi's universalism is itself metaphysically neutral.

## Key Debates

### Is the "as if" a mark of appearance or of approximation?
- **Sides**: Billon (mere appearance, with the Radovic and Radovic 2002 approximation reading acknowledged); Zahavi camp (the "as if" shows for-me-ness survives — the patient can still say *as if not mine* only because it is hers).
- **Core disagreement**: whether the preserved ability to report the alienation presupposes the very feature said to be missing. `mine-ness` L74 runs the coherence argument for separability; the SEP entry runs the same coherence for universality. The article should present this as a standoff on introspective evidence, not as settled by the datum.
- **Current state**: open; Billon 2023 §4 answers the Jaspers rationality objection and the agency objection but, on the full text retrieved, does not claim to have refuted the universalist on introspective grounds — he argues instead that the universalist's constraints on what mineness would have to be are "misguided", and settles for "general, if not universal".

### Is depersonalisation one global loss or many local ones?
- **Sides**: Billon (three global characters); Ciaunica (one — transparency); Sierra et al. 2005 and Knappik (four factors; type-specific relations).
- **Current state**: the psychometrics favour plurality; the philosophical question (plural features of one kind, or no feature of that kind) is open. `mine-ness` L92's reply is the right register.

### Is the depersonalisation–Cotard continuum real?
- Already carried by `cotard-delusion` L55–59; the new page should link, not restate. Note that Billon 2024 (p. 1 and §5) and Davies and Coltheart 2022 both hold the continuum and Gerrans 2023 denies it.

### Pathology or liberation?
- **Sides**: `necessary-opacity` ("pathology, not liberation. The opacity serves function"); Deane et al. 2020 and Kirberg and Chadha 2024 (the same selfless structure, devastating when involuntary, sought when deliberate); Ciaunica et al. 2021 (the window "cracks" in both).
- **Current state**: `mine-ness` L88 already states the valence-dissociation reading; `necessary-opacity` L82 should be softened to "pathology when involuntary".

## The Analytic Question: What Does Depersonalisation Show About Separability?

**Documented clinical result** (label per the discipline document): a persistent or recurrent alteration in which the self, body, actions and/or world are experienced as unreal, detached or observed from outside, with reality testing intact (DSM-5 B; ICD-11), perception and cognition otherwise intact (Gerrans 2019 abstract: "It is important to this account that cognition and perception are otherwise intact"; Sierra and David 2011), reduced autonomic/limbic response to emotional stimuli, lifetime transient prevalence of 26–74% and clinical prevalence of 1–2% (Hunter et al. 2004), and a four-factor symptom structure (Sierra et al. 2005). Reports characteristically take the "as if" form.

**Contested interpretation**: that what has come apart is a phenomenal feature of mode (mineness / subjective character) rather than affect, a form of awareness, transparency, or a set of type-specific self-relations. Five live readings; Billon's is the one the Map adopts and the one most hospitable to "separable for-me-ness", and its author draws a neurophysiological-reduction conclusion from it.

**Map reconstruction**: the "self-model ascending channel" language of `clinical-dissociation` L86 and the interface reading. The datum underdetermines it; the Map already says so at L92.

**Two-tier verdict** (the Cotard page's form):
- *Architecture*: a separable something is established (level 1), its phenomenological reality is contested between Billon and Zahavi (level 2), and every rival supplies a mechanism (level 3). The Map may say that depersonalisation is the best within-subject exhibit of a phenomenal feature varying while first-order content is held fixed — the [[discrimination-problem]] at the feature level — provided it adds that the universalist denies the feature varies at all and the localist denies it is one feature.
- *Significance*: null. No reading discriminates Tenet 1 from physicalism; the two physicalist readings (a) and (c) are fully specified; the one phenomenological reading the Map leans on (b) ends in neurophysiological reduction. Tier: *compatible*, as `mine-ness` and `cotard-delusion` hold.

**Bridging-premise check** (inferential co-optation firewall): the only route from "separable" to "non-physical" runs through the premise that a feature which varies independently of content and of any single functional alteration must be contributed by something outside the functional organisation. That premise is the contested dualist claim; the article must state the inference as conditional or not make it.
