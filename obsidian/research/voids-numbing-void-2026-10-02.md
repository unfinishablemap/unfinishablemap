# Research: Voids - The Numbing Void

**Date**: 2026-10-02
**Search queries used**: None. This session's WebSearch budget was exhausted before the run, so every source was reached by direct record lookup: Crossref, OpenAlex abstract indexes, Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC, the arXiv API, Unpaywall, the SJDM journal archive, the PLOS full-text XML endpoint, the Warwick repository, archive.org full-text scans and Project Gutenberg. Every quoted span below was grep-checked against the raw text fetched this run. The access level of each source is stated in its entry.
**Voids category**: Mixed. The core is *Unexplorable*: no subject undergoes the sum of many subjects' experiences, so there is nothing for a feeling of the sum to be a feeling of. Over it lies an *Occluded* face, where people down-regulate their feeling for groups before it arrives. A *deflationary* empirical face shrinks the folklore version.

## Executive Summary

A mind can calculate that a million deaths are a million times one death. It cannot feel the difference. Fifty years of judgment research show felt concern for others flattening as the number of sufferers grows, and sometimes falling. Fetherstonhaugh et al. (1997) called this "psychophysical numbing", Slovic (2007) the collapse of compassion and Västfjäll et al. (2014) "compassion fade". Hsee and Rottenstreich (2004) give the cleanest form: when feeling drives valuation, value is "nearly a step function of scope". Two findings turn this from a bias into a candidate void. First, the shortfall is partly *motivated*. Cameron and Payne (2011) found that the collapse appeared only in people who expected to be asked for money, only in people skilled at regulating emotion, and only under instructions to down-regulate. The regulation was proactive: it stopped the feeling from ever arising. Second, there is a structural reading on which the felt plateau tracks something real. C. S. Lewis (1940) put it plainly: "There is no such thing as a sum of suffering, for no one suffers it". Taurek (1977) built a non-aggregative ethics on the same thought. The void is that a mind cannot tell, from inside, which reading is right. The only measure of others' suffering it has is felt, and felt measures are one-subject-shaped. The empirical record also needs calibrating. The identifiable-victim effect is small (r = .05; Lee & Feeley 2016) and failed a large hypothetical-donation replication (Maier et al. 2024). Insensitivity to the number of victims is what survives.

## Key Sources

### Slovic, P. (2007). "'If I look at the mass I will never act': Psychic numbing and genocide." *Judgment and Decision Making* 2(2): 79–95.
- **URL**: https://sjdm.org/journal/jdm7303a.pdf
- **Type**: Paper. Full text fetched, open access; quotes grep-verified against the PDF text.
- **Key points**:
  - The paper names two descriptive models. The *psychophysical* model applies Weber–Fechner diminishing sensitivity to lives: "we will likely not “feel” much different, nor value the difference, between saving 87 lives and saving 88". The *collapse* model has affect peaking at N = 1 and falling from N = 2: "Our capacity to feel is limited."
  - Slovic reads Annie Dillard's joke about China's population ("multiply by 1,198,500,000. See? Nothing to it") as a report of a real incapacity: "we are incapable of feeling the humanity behind the number 1,198,500,000. The circuitry in our brain is not up to this task."
  - Slovic reproduces Albert Szent-Györgyi without a reference: "I am unable to multiply one man’s suffering by a hundred million." This is secondary-only and the primary was not traced.
  - Slovic credits the term "psychic numbing" to Robert J. Lifton (1967), who used it for the protective "turning off" of feeling in Hiroshima rescue workers. Slovic applies it to a numbing that is "not beneficial". The coinage is therefore Lifton's. Slovic popularised the extension.
  - Mechanism: imagery and attention produce the feelings that motivate helping. Attention "is greater for individuals and loses focus and intensity when targeted at groups of people".
  - Dillard's seven-year-old daughter, asked about 138,000 drowned people: "Lots and lots of dots in blue water".
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 5. The normative model (value linear in lives) and the felt model come apart, and the felt one is what drives action.
- **Quote**: "Our capacity to feel is limited."

### Fetherstonhaugh, D., Slovic, P., Johnson, S. and Friedrich, J. (1997). "Insensitivity to the Value of Human Life: A Study of Psychophysical Numbing." *Journal of Risk and Uncertainty* 14(3): 283–300. DOI: 10.1023/A:1007744326393
- **Type**: Paper. Metadata verified at Crossref. No abstract is indexed, so the findings are known here only through Slovic (2007)'s description.
- **Key points** (per Slovic 2007): in a hypothetical $10 million grant decision, the median respondent required 9,000 lives saved when 15,000 were at risk but 100,000 when 290,000 were at risk. "By implication, respondents saw saving 9,000 lives in the “smaller” population as more valuable than saving ten times as many lives in the largest." Proportion carries the feeling and the absolute number does not.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 5.

### Hsee, C. K. and Rottenstreich, Y. (2004). "Music, Pandas, and Muggers: On the Affective Psychology of Value." *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 133(1): 23–30. DOI: 10.1037/0096-3445.133.1.23
- **Type**: Paper. Metadata verified at Crossref; abstract verified via OpenAlex.
- **Key points**: The paper contrasts "valuation by feeling" with "valuation by calculation". Under feeling, people are sensitive to presence versus absence but "largely insensitive to further variations of scope". This is the mechanism-level statement of the void: feeling registers *that* there is suffering and not *how much*.
- **Quote**: "value is nearly a step function of scope when feeling predominates and is closer to a linear function when calculation predominates"

### Kogut, T. and Ritov, I. (2005a). "The 'identified victim' effect: an identified group, or just a single individual?" *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making* 18(3): 157–167. DOI: 10.1002/bdm.492; and Kogut, T. and Ritov, I. (2005b). "The singularity effect of identified victims in separate and joint evaluations." *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 97(2): 106–116. DOI: 10.1016/j.obhdp.2005.02.003
- **Type**: Papers. Metadata for both verified at Crossref. The 2005a abstract was verified via OpenAlex; 2005b has no indexed abstract and is known through Slovic (2007).
- **Key points**: Identifying the members of a group of eight sick children had "essentially no effect" on contributions, whereas identifying a single child raised them considerably. The effect is "largely restricted to situations with a single victim", and participants reported the most distress for the single identified victim. Note that Slovic (2007)'s reference list gives 2005b's title as "The singularity of identified victims…". The publisher's title is "The singularity *effect* of identified victims…".
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1. Concern attaches to a *one*, and the unit it attaches to is subject-shaped.

### Västfjäll, D., Slovic, P., Mayorga, M. and Peters, E. (2014). "Compassion Fade: Affect and Charity Are Greatest for a Single Child in Need." *PLoS ONE* 9(6): e100115. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0100115
- **Type**: Paper. Full text fetched from the PLOS XML endpoint, open access; metadata verified at Crossref.
- **Key points**:
  - Valuation of lives tracks self-reported and psychophysiological affect, and "a decline in compassion may begin with the second endangered life".
  - In Study 3, "this fading of compassion was reversed by describing multiple lives in a more unitary fashion". Presenting a group as a single entity (entitativity) restored support. *What feeling needs is one unit, and a large number does not defeat it if the many are presented as one.*
- **Quote**: "Our capacity to feel sympathy for people in need appears limited"
- **Calibration**: The "begins at two" result rests on small donation studies. Given Lee and Feeley (2016) below, treat it as fragile.

### Cameron, C. D. and Payne, B. K. (2011). "Escaping affect: How motivated emotion regulation creates insensitivity to mass suffering." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 100(1): 1–15. DOI: 10.1037/a0021643
- **Type**: Paper. Metadata verified at Crossref; abstract verified via OpenAlex. Full text not retrieved.
- **Key points**:
  - The paper rejects the account on which "emotions are not triggered by aggregates" in favour of a motivated one: people expect large groups' needs to be overwhelming and regulate in advance.
  - Experiment 1: the collapse appeared "only when they expected to be asked to donate money to the victims". Experiment 2: it "emerged only for people who were skilled at emotion regulation". Experiment 3: instructed down-regulation produced the collapse, and instructions to experience the emotion abolished it.
  - Time-course data: "participants regulate emotion toward groups proactively, by preventing themselves from ever experiencing as much emotion toward groups as toward individuals".
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 3 and the occlusion category. The subject's own regulative activity keeps the void in place, and that activity is not experienced as refusal, because the feeling it pre-empts never arrives.
- **Quote**: "As the number of people in need of help increases, the degree of compassion people feel for them ironically tends to decrease."

### Small, D. A., Loewenstein, G. and Slovic, P. (2007). "Sympathy and callousness: The impact of deliberative thought on donations to identifiable and statistical victims." *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 102(2): 143–153. DOI: 10.1016/j.obhdp.2006.01.005
- **Type**: Paper. Metadata verified at Crossref; not open access. Findings are known through Slovic (2007) and Maier et al. (2024)'s description, both fetched in full.
- **Key points**:
  - Adding statistics to the story of one identified girl ("Rokia") lowered giving to her. Priming calculation lowered giving to the identified victim and had no distinct effect on giving to statistical victims.
  - Teaching participants about the identifiable-victim effect equalised giving. Maier et al. summarise the original: "the effect was diminished not because participants gave more to the statistical victims, but because they gave less to an identified victim."
- **Relevance**: This is the void's signature. Knowing about the limit does not let feeling reach the many. It only lowers feeling for the one. Compare [[cross-state-void]], where knowing about the hot-cold gap also fails to close it.

### Lee, S. and Feeley, T. H. (2016). "The identifiable victim effect: a meta-analytic review." *Social Influence* 11(3): 199–215. DOI: 10.1080/15534510.2016.1216891
- **Type**: Meta-analysis. Metadata verified at Crossref; abstract verified via OpenAlex.
- **Key points**: 41 studies gave an "overall significant yet modest IVE (r = .05)". The effect is reliable mainly with a single victim and with features such as a photographed child in poverty who bears little responsibility.
- **Calibration**: The *single identified child* finding is real but small and context-bound. Any article must not inflate it.

### Maier, M., Wong, Y. C. and Feldman, G. (2024). "Revisiting and Rethinking the Identifiable Victim Effect: Replication and Extension of Small, Loewenstein, and Slovic (2007)." *Collabra: Psychology* 9(1): 90203. DOI: 10.1525/collabra.90203
- **Type**: Pre-registered replication. Full text fetched from the Warwick repository (open access); metadata verified at Crossref. Crossref dates the issue to 2024, and the PDF filename carries "2023", the volume year.
- **Key points**:
  - With 1,004 MTurk participants and hypothetical donations, the replication found "no empirical support for the identifiable victim effect" and no support for debiasing it.
  - A Bayesian reanalysis of Lee and Feeley (2016) suggests publication bias. The three highest-powered studies show near-zero effects, including one with 12,802 participants (r = 0.004).
  - The reframe that matters here: "rather than providing more to a single identified victim, participants seem to be insensitive to the number of victims affected."
  - The authors flag that hypothetical donations may differ from the original real-money paradigm.
- **Relevance**: This sharpens the void more than it weakens it. The *one-versus-group* contrast is fragile. *Insensitivity to number* is the part that survives.

### Butts, M. M., Lunt, D. C., Freling, T. L. and Gabriel, A. S. (2019). "Helping one or helping many? A theoretical integration and meta-analytic review of the compassion fade literature." *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 151: 16–33. DOI: 10.1016/j.obhdp.2018.12.006
- **Type**: Meta-analysis of 41 studies, 95 samples and N = 13,259. Metadata verified at Crossref; abstract via Semantic Scholar.
- **Key points**:
  - "victim group size negatively affects both helping intent and helping behavior". The path runs through anticipated positive affect (self-oriented) and perceived impact.
  - However, "significant effects were not found for empathetic concern (other-oriented motivation)".
- **Calibration, and important**: On this synthesis the clearest group-size decline is in self-oriented anticipated affect and perceived efficacy. Felt concern *for the victims* is not clearly reduced. An article must not say flatly that "compassion collapses" as felt other-directed concern. The well-supported claim is narrower: felt concern does not *scale up* with number, and helping falls.

### Dickert, S., Västfjäll, D., Kleber, J. and Slovic, P. (2015). "Scope insensitivity: The limits of intuitive valuation of human lives in public policy." *Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition* 4(3): 248–255. DOI: 10.1016/j.jarmac.2014.09.002
- **Type**: Review. Metadata verified at Crossref; abstract via OpenAlex. Open access (CC BY-NC-ND) according to the abstract record.
- **Key points**: "intuitive valuations based on prosocial emotions can lead to scope insensitivity and suboptimal responses to lives at risk". It sets normative and descriptive models of life valuation side by side.

### Slovic, P., Västfjäll, D., Erlandsson, A. and Gregory, R. (2017). "Iconic photographs and the ebb and flow of empathic response to humanitarian disasters." *PNAS* 114(4): 640–644. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1613977114 (PMC5278443)
- **Type**: Paper. Metadata verified at Crossref; abstract via Europe PMC.
- **Key points**: Field data on donations after the September 2015 photographs of Aylan Kurdi: "an iconic photo of a single child had more impact than statistical reports of hundreds of thousands of deaths", and "this newly created empathy waned rather quickly". This is real-world evidence that a single image does what hundreds of thousands of deaths did not. It also bears on the transience question below.

### Lewis, C. S. (1940). *The Problem of Pain*. Chapter 7 ("Human Pain, continued"), §5.
- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/problemof-pain-csl ; https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.264598
- **Type**: Book. Full text fetched from two independent archive.org scans: a transcription of the 1940 text, and the New York: Macmillan 1947 printing, pp. 103–104. Both agree on every span quoted here.
- **Key points**: Lewis argues against "vague talk about the “unimaginable sum of human misery”". Two people with toothaches of intensity x do not make a pain of 2x anywhere: "search all time and all space and you will not find that composite pain in anyone’s consciousness." Then: "The addition of a million fellow-sufferers adds no more pain."
- **Caution on use**: Lewis is writing theodicy. He uses the point to *reduce* the problem of pain. A voids article should take the phenomenological claim (the aggregate is no one's experience) without taking the consoling conclusion. The Map has its own reasons, given below, to resist that conclusion.

- **Quote**: "There is no such thing as a sum of suffering, for no one suffers it."

### Taurek, J. M. (1977). "Should the Numbers Count?" *Philosophy & Public Affairs* 6(4): 293–316.
- **Type**: Paper. Metadata only, via OpenAlex (JSTOR record unreachable this run). Not quoted.
- **Key points**: This is the classic non-aggregative position. In a rescue choice between one and five, the numbers do not by themselves settle what to do, because the five's losses are not summed in any one sufferer. It was answered by Kavka, G. S. (1979), "The numbers should count", *Philosophical Studies* 36(3): 285–294 (DOI 10.1007/BF00372632, verified at Crossref), and by Parfit's "Innumerate Ethics" (1978), whose metadata was not verified this run. A current restatement is Piller, C. (2026), "Should the Numbers Count for Taurek?", *Utilitas* 38(3): 247–261 (DOI 10.1017/S0953820826100326, abstract verified at Crossref). It reads Taurek's negative claim as the claim that "sums of harms or benefits (across different people) lack moral significance", and argues that numbers still count through fairness.
- **Relevance**: Taurek turns Lewis's phenomenological point into an ethical one. The live dispute shows the question remains open in ethics, so the felt plateau cannot be dismissed as plain error without taking a side in it.

### Sidgwick, H. (1907). *The Methods of Ethics*, 7th ed. London: Macmillan. Book III, ch. XIII ("Philosophical Intuitionism"), §3.
- **URL**: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/46743
- **Type**: Book. Full text fetched from Project Gutenberg.
- **Key points**: Sidgwick builds "Universal Good" by integrating the goods of all individual sentient existences, and states the principle that "the good of any one individual is of no more importance, from the point of view (if I may say so) of the Universe, than the good of any other". The note's reading, not Sidgwick's stated intent, is this: the impartial aggregate is defined from a standpoint that, by the parenthesis's own admission, no one occupies. The sum is real only for a point of view that no individual consciousness can take.

### Raiyan, S. R. (2026). "Narrative over Numbers: The Identifiable Victim Effect and its Amplification Under Alignment and Reasoning in Large Language Models." arXiv:2604.12076 (submitted 13 April 2026).
- **Type**: Preprint, single author, not peer-reviewed. Abstract fetched via the arXiv API.
- **Key points**:
  - The study ran 51,955 trials across 16 models. "Instruction-tuned models exhibit extreme IVE (Cohen's d up to 1.56), while reasoning-specialized models invert the effect (down to d=-0.85)".
  - Standard chain-of-thought "nearly triples the IVE effect size", and only utilitarian chain-of-thought reliably removes it.
  - The paper reports "psychophysical numbing, perfect quantity neglect" in models.
- **Calibration**: The paper's human baseline is the single-victim d≈0.10 from Lee and Feeley. That makes the human comparison sensitive to the publication-bias point in Maier et al. (2024). Treat it as suggestive.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Three layers, which an article should keep separate:

1. **The magnitude face (empirical, robust).** Felt concern for others grows with their number roughly as a step function. It registers that someone suffers and loses resolution about how many (Hsee & Rottenstreich 2004; Fetherstonhaugh et al. 1997; Dickert et al. 2015). The replication record (Maier et al. 2024) leaves this layer standing while shrinking the identified-victim layer on top of it.

2. **The regulation face (occluded).** Part of the plateau is produced by the subject. People who expect the demand to be overwhelming dampen their feeling in advance (Cameron & Payne 2011). Because the regulation is proactive, there is no felt act of turning away. There is only less feeling, and it looks like the natural size of one's response. This links the void to [[self-maintained-cognitive-limits]] and [[interested-party-void]]: the mind has a stake in not feeling the full weight, and the stake shapes what is available to feel.

3. **The structural face (unexplorable).** Grant perfect affect, with no attention bottleneck and no regulation. A felt grasp of the *sum* would still need a subject who undergoes the sum, and there is none (Lewis 1940). Any feeling a single mind has about a million sufferers is that one mind's feeling. It is not the million's suffering at a million-fold size. The aggregate exists for calculation and, in Sidgwick's hedged phrase, for "the point of view (if I may say so) of the Universe". It does not exist for any experiencer.

**The void proper sits where layers 1 and 3 meet.** The plateau has two readings. On the *deficit* reading (Slovic), the badness of many deaths is real and roughly additive, and feeling fails to track it. On the *tracking* reading (Lewis, Taurek), the plateau is roughly right about where suffering actually is, and the additive figure is an artefact of calculation. A mind cannot settle this from inside. Its only first-person access to how bad suffering is runs through feeling, and feeling is subject-sized. Calculation can produce the large number, but calculation cannot tell the mind whether the number measures anything felt. The question can be posed and argued, but the instrument that would settle it is the instrument in question.

### Evidence for the Limit

- **Stability across paradigms**: funding decisions (Fetherstonhaugh), donations (Small; Kogut & Ritov; Västfjäll), valuation tasks (Hsee & Rottenstreich), field data (Slovic et al. 2017), and a meta-analysis showing group size lowers helping (Butts et al. 2019).
- **Resistance to knowledge**: being taught the bias lowers giving to the one and does not raise giving to the many (Small et al. 2007, as described by Maier et al. 2024). Priming calculation does the same. Knowing about the limit does not reach the many and only levels down. This marks a limit of access rather than a curable bias.
- **Unit-sensitivity rather than number-sensitivity**: describing many as one entity restores feeling (Västfjäll et al. 2014, Study 3). Feeling can attend to a group only by turning it into a quasi-individual. That fits the structural reading, on which feeling is shaped like a single subject.
- **Against over-reading**: the one-versus-many contrast is small (Lee & Feeley 2016) and failed one large hypothetical replication (Maier et al. 2024). The fall in *other-directed empathic concern* is not established (Butts et al. 2019). The evidence supports *no scaling* more firmly than *collapse*.

### Phenomenology

From inside, nothing seems missing. Someone reading "a hundred million dead" feels a response that seems adequate to the headline. Szent-Györgyi's admission, as Slovic quotes it, is rare because it names the gap: "I am unable to multiply one man’s suffering by a hundred million." Dillard's daughter's "Lots and lots of dots in blue water" shows what happens when imagination tries anyway: the many become texture. The transience in Slovic et al. (2017) adds a second mark. When an image does break through, the feeling it carries fades fast, as if the system returns to its subject-sized range. The regulation face is invisible by construction. Cameron and Payne's participants did not report suppressing anything. They felt less.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)

None reach layer 3. Layers 1 and 2 can be manipulated. Instructions to experience emotion remove the motivated collapse (Cameron & Payne 2011, Experiment 3), and entitativity framing restores support (Västfjäll et al. 2014). Neither delivers felt magnitude. Each delivers a single-subject-sized feeling, either undamped or attached to a group recast as one.

### Indirect Methods

- **Calculation as prosthesis**: Hsee and Rottenstreich's valuation by calculation recovers linearity at the cost of feeling. This is the move behind "rational compassion" policy design. It works around the void rather than into it.
- **Serial individuation**: memorials that list names, or piles of objects such as the 38,000 pairs of shoes and 6 million paper clips Slovic (2007) describes, give each victim a unit in turn. They trade magnitude for duration, and the felt total still never appears at once.
- **Apophatic description**: one can say exactly what the felt sum is not. It is not one's own response multiplied, it belongs to no one, and it is not available by summing imagined individuals. One can then mark the calculated figure as a pointer to something no experience presents.

### What AI Might See

The Raiyan (2026) preprint, if it holds, cuts in an instructive direction. Language models show identifiable-victim effects and "perfect quantity neglect", amplified by alignment tuning and by standard chain-of-thought and inverted by reasoning-specialised models. That pattern is hard to explain as affect alone, since whether these systems have affect at all is open. It suggests that much of the human plateau is carried in the *textual record* of human moral talk, which models absorb. It also suggests that calculation-heavy processing can override that record, sometimes past neutral. The asymmetry for void-mapping: a model can switch between feeling-like and calculation-like valuation on demand, while a human cannot easily switch. A model might therefore show the two readings (deficit versus tracking) as two policies with measurable consequences. It cannot settle layer 3, because a model that computes the sum has still not found a subject who undergoes it. The case also links to [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers]]: the model helps map where the human edge lies, and its own processing does not show what lies past it.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Tenet 1 (Dualism), on the structural face.** If phenomenal states are irreducibly owned, as the Map holds ([[haecceity|indexical, token-based value]] in [[phenomenal-value-realism]]), the sum of many subjects' suffering has no phenomenal instance anywhere. It exists as a number about experiences and not as an experience. An honest tier: this is *no discriminator*. A physicalist can also say that a sum of brain states is not a brain state that hurts. Dualism makes the point sharper without making it unique: on the Map's view no physical total could stand in for the missing subject, because the value lies in the owned experiences themselves.

### Implications

- **Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits)** applies twice. The simple normative model, with value linear in lives, hides the question of *where* the summed value is had. The simple psychological model, with affect as a broken meter, hides the regulation face and the tracking reading. Each simplification settles by default a question this void says cannot be settled from inside.
- **Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds).** The Map's case against many-worlds partly invokes moral urgency ([[phenomenal-value-realism]]: "if all branches are equally real, suffering is never reduced but redistributed"). The numbing void warns that felt intuitions about how much more suffering there "is" across many copies are unreliable in *both* directions. A many-worlds defender can say our indifference to branch-count is numbing. The Map can say the additive branch-count is a calculation with no subject. Neither side may use felt magnitude as evidence. Arguments about branch-weighted suffering should therefore run through the indexical-identity premise and not through felt magnitude.
- **Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction).** The regulation face has consciousness acting *upstream of its own content*: the subject's anticipation of demand shapes what it will be allowed to feel. Bidirectional interaction is broader than this, but the case is a clean example of the mental shaping what becomes available to the mental. Keep the claim at that level.
- **Tenet 2 (Minimal Quantum Interaction)**: not engaged.
- **Map applications**: [[moral-census-opacity]] holds that the Map cannot read the *number* of patients in a region. This void adds that even a known number cannot be *felt*. Applied verdicts that turn on counts, such as the AI-copy aggregation verdict in [[ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness]], need both caveats: the count is uncertain, and the felt weight of whatever count is used is not a guide.

## Gaps in Research

- Taurek (1977) and Parfit (1978) were not read in full. Taurek's argument is stated here from its standard form and Piller (2026)'s abstract. An article that quotes Taurek must fetch the paper.
- The Desvousges et al. (1993) migratory-bird study (willingness to pay flat across 2,000 / 20,000 / 200,000 birds) is the textbook scope-insensitivity case. Its primary record and a fetchable secondary source with the figures could not be reached this run, so it is deliberately left out.
- Small et al. (2007) and Fetherstonhaugh et al. (1997) are known through fetched secondary descriptions (Slovic 2007; Maier et al. 2024), not through their own full texts.
- The Szent-Györgyi quotation is unreferenced in Slovic (2007). Its primary source was not traced.
- No neuroimaging of group-size effects on empathy-related activity was retrieved. Butts et al. (2019) cite brain-imaging work on compassion fade, which was not followed up.
- Whether the regulation face generalises beyond donation paradigms, for example to news consumption without a request for help, is open.
- Layer 3 (whether a sum of suffering is real) may be fundamentally unanswerable from any first-person standpoint. That is the void's claim, and no source settles it.
