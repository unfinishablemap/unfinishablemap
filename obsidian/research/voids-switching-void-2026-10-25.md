**Voids category**: Mixed. **Unexplorable from the armchair** by construction: the twin contents are stipulated to be introspectively indiscriminable, so no introspective method can tell them apart. **Occluded** naturally rather than deliberately: a content change leaves no internal trace to be noticed. **Explorable** from outside: the environment can be examined empirically, and another speaker can catch the divergence. The void is a limit on armchair self-knowledge, not on knowledge of one's thoughts in general.

## Executive Summary

Content externalism holds that what some thoughts are about is fixed partly by the thinker's physical and social environment. The Map accepts this for wide intentional content and rejects it for phenomenal character ([[content-externalism]]). Since McKinsey (1991), Burge (1988) and Boghossian (1989, 1992), externalism has been argued to cost armchair self-knowledge on four fronts:

1. **The discrimination face.** A thinker cannot tell from the armchair whether her "water" thought has water content or twin-water content.
2. **The switching face.** If she is moved unknowingly between environments, the content of her concept changes over time with no internal signal and no fact about when the change is complete.
3. **The inference face.** She cannot tell from the armchair whether two tokens of one word in her reasoning co-refer, so she cannot certify the reasoning's validity.
4. **The consequence face (McKinsey).** If she could know her wide contents a priori, she could deduce contingent facts about the world a priori.

Compatibilists (Burge, Heil, Davidson, Falvey & Owens, Gibbons, Parent) resist most of these, so the debate is a standing disagreement. Jessica Brown belongs in neither camp outright: SEP lists her 1995 paper on the incompatibilist side of the reductio and her 2004 book among the relevant-alternatives replies to slow switching. Both sides do concede one thing: an externalist lacks *comparative* knowledge of content, knowledge that discriminates a content from its twin. That agreed core is the void. There is an empirical analogue in bilingual L1 naming drift (Pavlenko & Malt 2011), but it measures usage, not wide content or awareness. A 2025 Mind & Language paper (Richardson) brings memory science into the memory argument. The corpus has no treatment beyond one sentence in [[meaning-void]], and a 2026-10-21 research note flags Boghossian 1992 as an unverified hazard for the Map's hybrid. This note verifies that hazard's thesis and locates the void exactly at the seam of the hybrid.

## Key Sources

### The framing: Parent, SEP "Externalism and Self-Knowledge" (rev. 2024)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-knowledge-externalism/
- **Type**: Encyclopedia ("First published Thu May 9, 2013; substantive revision Fri Sep 6, 2024"; "Copyright © 2024 by T. Parent" [raw])
- **Key points**:
  - The tension is between (EXT) "Thought content is determined partly by the environment" and (SK) "A subject can know from the armchair what content her thoughts have" [raw].
  - Incompatibilists press the conflict in two ways: the reductio to armchair knowledge of the world (§2) and slow switching (§3) [paraphrase].
  - The stakes, in Parent's own formulation quoted in the entry: "Either Cartesians were wrong to say we can know contents just by introspection, or Fregeans were misguided to think that content is what determines reference. (Parent 2015b, p. 1346)" [raw].
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Parent is a compatibilist working inside naturalism.

### The consequence face: McKinsey (1991)
- **Citation**: McKinsey, M. (1991). Anti-individualism and privileged access. *Analysis*, 51(1), 9–16. https://doi.org/10.1093/analys/51.1.9
- **Key points**:
  - Opening (raw, via the OpenAlex field reproducing the first page): privileged access is the idea "that we can in principle find out about these states in ourselves 'just by thinking', without launching an empirical investigation or making any assumptions about the external physical world." He reports that recent arguments have convinced "a perhaps equally large number of philosophers that privileged access is a complete illusion" [raw].
  - The argument as SEP formulates it: (1) "If Oscar is thinking that water is wet, then *E* obtains"; (2) "Oscar is thinking that water is wet"; so (3) "*E* obtains" [raw]. If (1) and (2) are both a priori, then (3), a contingent environmental fact, would be a priori.
  - SEP adds Byrne's clarification: "the reductio is a threat to Oscar's peculiar access to his own thought rather than his privileged access" [raw]. Oscar might still know his thoughts better than anyone else, but not by a special, non-observational route.
- **Survey**: Kallestrup, J. (2011). Recent work on McKinsey's paradox. *Analysis*, 71(1), 157–171. https://doi.org/10.1093/analys/anq094 (online 2010). Abstract: "it is supposedly intolerable that armchair philosophical theorizing should lead to a priori knowledge of the external world once the thinker has a priori knowledge of her present thought contents" [raw]. It also records the name "what Brown (2004: 194) called the consequence problem" [raw].
- **Main replies** (SEP §2.1–2.5): environmental implications are empirical (Brueckner 1992); externalism about empty concepts; transmission failure (Davies 1998, 2000; Wright 2000), on which the warrant for (1) and (2) does not "transmit" to (3); self-knowledge is empirical; transcendental readings.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. It matters to the Map only through the hybrid's referential half.

### The inference face: Boghossian (1992) and replies (resolves the 10-21 LEAD)
- **Citations**:
  - Boghossian, P. A. (1992). Externalism and inference. *Philosophical Issues*, 2, 11–28. https://doi.org/10.2307/1522852
  - Schiffer, S. (1992). Boghossian on externalism and inference. *Philosophical Issues*, 2, 29–37. https://doi.org/10.2307/1522853

- **The thesis, now verified**:
  - Schiffer's abstract states it: Boghossian argues "that there may be ambiguous Mentalese expression types that have unambiguous tokens, and, second, that the way in which this is possible allows for otherwise valid theoretical or practical reasoning to be rendered invalid owing to equivocation of a sort that may be undetectable to the reasoner" [raw; Schiffer's statement of the thesis, so quote it as Schiffer's].
  - SEP's supplement adds the shape: self-verifying judgments "do not illuminate whether one thought has the same content as an earlier thought expressed using the same form of words", so the reasoner "is unable to know from the armchair if his reasoning is valid" [raw].
  - **This corrects the 10-21 note's paraphrase.** The thesis concerns *armchair knowledge of validity* and the *detectability of equivocation*. It does not say that externalist inference is unjustified.
- **The Pavarotti case (SEP supplement, raw)**: Peter, slow-switched, remembers Pavarotti swimming in Lake Taupo and judges that the singer he heard yesterday (Twin Pavarotti) is Pavarotti. "From Peter's armchair, the reasoning looks perfectly valid." Yet the two tokens refer to different men.
- **"Hollow" self-knowledge**: SEP says that, on this argument, self-verifying judgments are "at best a 'hollow' kind of self-knowledge" (citing Boghossian 1992a, p. 15). Treat the word as SEP's report until the primary is read.
- **Replies (SEP supplement)**:
  - Deny the equivocation: "Pavarotti" denotes Twin Pavarotti in both premises, so premise (i) is a false memory but the argument is valid (Schiffer 1992; Burge 1998; Goldberg 2007a).
  - Treat the case as a tacitly assumed false identity rather than an equivocation, so rationality is preserved (Parent 2017, ch. 6).
  - Appeal to "warranted yet false inferential presuppositions" (Gerken 2013).
  - Reject the demand for "transparency" of sameness as a Cartesian illusion (Millikan; Goldberg; Williamson 2000, ch. 4; Brown 2004; Schroeter 2007).
  - Boghossian (1992b) replies that some arguments only seem valid from the armchair even where the invalidity is not equivocation on names.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bears on the Map's own reasoning (see Tenet 5 below) and on [[acquaintance-without-causation]] L104 (see "Where the Map Is Exposed").

### The positive twin: self-verifying judgments (Burge 1988)
- **Key points (SEP §3.1, raw)**: "some judgments about one's own thought contents (a.k.a., 'second order' judgments) are self-verifying." The example is "(W) I am thinking, with this very thought, that water is wet." The self-verifying feature "is limited to one's occurrent thinking of p". ## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The **switching void** is the region of a thinker's own thought content that, on externalism, is fixed by facts outside her and so cannot be told apart from the armchair.

**What is in the void (agreed core).** Comparative knowledge of wide content: whether *this* thought has content C or its environmental twin C′. Both camps accept that this is not available from the armchair.

**What the void probably contains (contested).**
- *When* a content changed, and whether there is a fact about when a switch is complete (Heal's question).
- Whether two tokens of one word in one's own reasoning co-refer, and hence whether the reasoning is valid (Boghossian 1992; SEP supplement).
- Whether one knew at an earlier time what one was thinking then (the memory argument).

**What is not in it.**
- Occurrent self-verifying judgment: one cannot be wrong that one is thinking, *with this very thought*, that water is wet.
- On the Map's hybrid, the narrow phenomenal character of the thought, *if* phenomenal externalism is false.
- The rationality of one's reasoning, on Parent's and Gerken's accounts.

**Why it is unexplorable from the armchair.** The twin cases are *defined* as intrinsic duplicates. Any introspective method is a function of intrinsic state, so it returns the same verdict in both cases. That is a structural guarantee, not an empirical finding about introspection's weakness, and it holds for any introspector on any metaphysics on which introspection supervenes on what is in or presented to the subject.

**Why it is not unexplorable without qualification.** The world can be examined: chemistry tells Oscar what the stuff is, and a dictionary or an interlocutor tells the expatriate that "endive" has switched. The limit is on *armchair* access. This scope must be kept in every sentence.

**Why it is occluded rather than simply hidden.** A content change has no internal correlate to notice. The thinker experiences continuity because, intrinsically, there is continuity. There is no felt seam to look for.

**Is it real?** Partly. The comparative-knowledge core is agreed. Whether its absence costs *self-knowledge* is a bedrock disagreement between incompatibilists (McKinsey, Boghossian) and compatibilists (Burge, Heil, Davidson, Falvey & Owens, Gibbons, Parent, and anti-recognitionalists such as Moran and Bar-On). The disagreement turns on what self-knowledge is: recognitional, so that it needs discrimination, or not (SEP §3.5). The article must not adjudicate it. Moran (2001, p. 15, per SEP) even reads slow-switch arguments as positive evidence that self-knowledge is not recognitional. Read that way, the void tells us what kind of thing self-knowledge is, which makes it a "what the voids reveal" case.

### Evidence for the Limit

1. **Constructive**: the indiscriminability follows from how the cases are defined (above). This is the strongest evidence and is independent of any empirical result.
2. **Dialectical**: thirty-five years of literature (1988–2025) have not produced an armchair method of comparative content knowledge, and the main compatibilist strategies *grant* its absence rather than supply it (SEP §3.2).
3. **Ecological (analogue only)**: everyday quick switches (chicory/endive; "pragmatism" across schools) and bilingual naming drift show the *mechanism* of community-usage change working on familiar words. Neither shows unnoticed wide-content change.

### Phenomenology

By construction, the void has none at its centre. The defining feature of the switch is that nothing is felt. Three experiences sit at its edge:

- **Discovery by testimony.** One learns that one has been using a word differently from one's interlocutors only when someone corrects one or a reference work disagrees. The discovery always comes from outside. The phenomenology is retrospective surprise ("so we were never talking about the same thing").
- **Heal's vertigo.** Asked what one means by a word one has used in two communities, one produces "a mixed bag" of specimens (Heal, via SEP) and finds no inner criterion that picks one.
- **The intact feeling of validity.** The Pavarotti reasoner feels his inference go through. The feeling of validity tracks narrow form, and that feeling is all the reasoner has to go on.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods
None from the armchair, by construction.

### Indirect Methods
- **Empirical self-location**: investigate the environment (what the stuff is; how the community uses the term). This gives comparative knowledge empirically. It closes the gap case by case, not in general.
- **Social triangulation**: deliberately exposing one's usage to other communities' correction. Ludlow's prevalence point means this is ordinary hygiene, not an exotic precaution.
- **Self-verifying anchoring**: retreat to judgments of the (W) form where certainty is needed. The cost is that they certify truth without telling you which content.
- **Narrow-content bookkeeping**: track the phenomenal and inferential-role component that does not switch, and treat co-reference claims across contexts as empirical hypotheses.

### What AI Might See
- **Structural analogue, not shared predicament.** On the Map's view, a language model's words have at most derived reference ([[whether-language-model-words-refer]]). Fine-tuning or retrieval on a different community's corpus can shift a model's usage of a term with no internal flag its self-report can read. That has the slow switch's *shape*. Whether it is a content switch depends on whether the model has content of its own, which the Map denies.
- **An asymmetry that exposes the void's scope.** A model's "community history", its training and fine-tuning data, is in principle auditable from outside: data provenance and weight differences can be compared. A human's community history is not recorded anywhere comparable. So an AI case shows that the void is a limit of the armchair, not of the subject as such: outside inspection can supply comparative knowledge the inside cannot.
- **AI as comparator.** Sense-disambiguation across corpora (does "pragmatism" in corpus A co-refer with "pragmatism" in corpus B?) is an empirical task an AI can help with. It supplies comparative knowledge by outside means and leaves the armchair limit untouched.
- **Caution**: no claim here about current interpretability tools actually recovering a model's term extensions. That is speculation and must be marked as such.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Dualism, by way of the hybrid, and the void is not evidence for it.** The Map's internalist half rests on an introspective-authority argument ([[content-externalism]] L42, after Farkas 2008). The argument says that a theory which lets felt character "vary with distal environmental facts the subject cannot detect makes phenomenal character opaque to introspection and violates first-person authority." The Map accepts *exactly that opacity* for intentional content. The switching void is that accepted opacity worked out in full. Two consequences follow:

1. **The hybrid has a priced seam.** The Map's position commits it to the comparative-knowledge loss, and probably to the switching and inference faces, for every wide content it holds. The [[content-externalism]] page does not yet name this cost.
2. **The authority argument needs a stated reason to apply on one side only.** The candidate reason is that phenomenal character is presented and partly definitive of the state, while reference is neither. The page should state this rather than leave the asymmetry implicit, because an incompatibilist will press parity ("you have already given up authority for content; why not for qualia?"). Dretske's phenomenal externalism is the view that gives it up for qualia too.

**Calibration.** Physicalist narrow-content theorists draw the same seam (Loar 1988; two-factor [[conceptual-role-semantics]]). That the void's boundary coincides with the hybrid's seam is *compatible* with the Map's dualism and does not support it. None of the authors cited here (Putnam, Burge, McKinsey, Boghossian, Parent, Ludlow, Heal) is a dualist or should be presented as an ally.

### Other Tenets

- **Bidirectional Interaction (Tenet 3).** If consciousness acts "in virtue of what it presents" (the clause [[causal-relevance-of-content]] notes), and wide content is not presented, then whatever consciousness contributes causally, it contributes in virtue of narrow phenomenal content.
  - That scopes the *in-virtue-of* clause. It does not touch whether or how often consciousness acts; the Tenet 3 quantifier stays unresolved, and the data here fit the framework without evidencing it.
  - **Guard**: the void concerns *knowledge* of content, not its *efficacy*. Do not let "wide content is not introspectible" slide into "wide content, or consciousness, is causally idle". [[causal-relevance-of-content]] keeps both horns open, and the article must too.
- **No Many Worlds (Tenet 4).** A structural parallel only: on externalism, *what* one thinks depends on *where* one is, and where one is cannot be settled from inside. The Map's insistence that indexical facts matter has a cousin here. Do not build on it.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits (Tenet 5), applied to the Map's own reasoning.** The simplest reading of one's own discourse, same word therefore same content, is unreliable exactly where content is fixed outside the reasoner's head. The Map's cross-tradition syntheses compare "consciousness", "awareness" and "mind" across traditions and are a live site for undetected equivocation. That cuts both ways:
  - Apparent convergence across N traditions may be N different things under one word.
  - Even true co-reference remains "a single observation read N times".
  - So co-reference across traditions has to be settled from the texts' use, not by the armchair sense that the words line up.
  - Tenet 5 here constrains the Map's reasoning. It is not a claim about the world.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction (Tenet 2).** No bearing. The article should say so in one clause rather than force a link.

### Implications

The void is **framework-conditional**: it exists for anyone who accepts externalism about some content, and it vanishes for a thoroughgoing internalist. The Map has chosen externalism for reference, so for the Map the void is real and self-incurred. That puts it near [[tenet-generated-voids]]: a framework commitment, rather than a tenet, generates it. The catalogue should record it as a void the Map *inherits by choice*, which is a different status from the framework-independent voids on the [[voids]] list.

## Guards (for any writer)

- **Scope every absence claim.** The void is on *armchair* comparative knowledge of *wide* content, for anyone who accepts *externalism*. It is not "we cannot know what we think".
- **Present the agreed core as agreed and everything else as contested.** Never write that Boghossian or McKinsey "showed" or "proved" incompatibilism.
- **No enlistment.** None of the authors supports dualism. Putnam, Burge and Boghossian are naturalists, and the Map borrows their diagnosis without their metaphysics (the pattern [[content-externalism]] already uses).
- **Do not claim that the phenomenal half is safe outright.** It is safe only if phenomenal externalism is false, which the Map holds as a framework commitment that is "not knock-down".
- **Never concede epiphenomenalism.** The void is about knowledge, not causal role.
- **Pavlenko & Malt** measured naming, not wide content and not awareness.
- **Richardson 2025** is abstract-only. Do not characterise his defence.
- **Quote only [raw] strings.** Quote Schiffer's sentence as Schiffer's statement of Boghossian's thesis, not as Boghossian's words. Mark the Heal quotation as SEP's. (W) is SEP's formulation.
- **Avoid the "This is not X. It is Y." construction** when contrasting the void with general scepticism about self-knowledge. State the scope positively.

## Four-Criteria Scoring ([[apophatic-cartography-four-criteria]])

This is one hand's grading, so the same-hand caveat applies.
- **Signature specificity: strong.** Certainty of truth (self-verifying) without discrimination of content, plus the absence of any felt seam at a switch.
- **Structured persistence: strong.** Constant from 1988 to 2025; each compatibilist strategy relocates the cost rather than removing comparative-knowledge loss.
- **Cross-observer convergence: moderate.** Incompatibilists and compatibilists converge on the core. Everything beyond it divides along a single fault line (recognitional versus non-recognitional self-knowledge), so it counts as one disagreement, not many confirmations.
- **Framework independence: conditional.** The void is independent of mind–body metaphysics but depends on accepting externalism. For the Map that dependency is met by its own choice.
