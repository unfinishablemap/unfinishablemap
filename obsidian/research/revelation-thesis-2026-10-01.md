## Executive Summary

"Revelation" names a family of theses with one shape — *to have an experience is to be in a position to know the nature of what it presents* — and two subject matters. The **colour version** (Johnston 1992, as quoted by Byrne & Hilbert 2007 and SEP) says a standard visual experience fully reveals the intrinsic nature of the colour seen; Byrne & Hilbert decompose it into Self-Intimation plus Infallibility and show it to be "tantamount to Minimal Primitivism", then argue that realist primitivism is "unacceptable". The **phenomenal version** (Lewis's 1995 "Identification Thesis"; Stoljar 2006/2009; Damnjanovic 2012; Trogdon 2017; Goff 2011/2015/2017 as "Phenomenal Transparency"; Liu 2019/2021) says that having an experience with quale Q puts one in a position to know Q's essence. Lewis granted it folk-theoretical status and said "A materialist cannot accept the Identification Thesis"; Goff turned it into a premise against physicalism; Damnjanovic and Trogdon reply that a physicalist can accept a robust or partial reading; Stoljar denies it is even intuitive; Liu argues it is intuitive and explains the intuition of dualism. The 2017–2025 turn (Majeed, Goff 2018, Botin 2023, Lin 2025, O'Conaill 2025) is that strong Revelation cuts *both* ways: it threatens panpsychism and Russellian monism as much as physicalism, and pressures any realist toward epiphenomenalism.

For the Map the result is clean: it needs **weak Revelation** (existence plus partial nature — Goff's "translucent", Trogdon's "partial essential characterization", Lewis's de re "harmless look-alike") and must **deny strong Revelation** (full essence revealed — Lewis's Identification, Goff's transparency, Byrne & Hilbert's Self-Intimation). Weak Revelation is compatible with physicalism, so it earns the Map nothing above *compatible* on the over-reach ladder; strong Revelation would contradict the Map's own self-opacity commitments and threaten Tenets 2–3. The argument from revelation is therefore **not available to the Map** as an argument for Tenet 1 — a result worth stating because the Russellian rival the Map engages relies on exactly that argument.

### Byrne & Hilbert, "Color primitivism" (2007)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10670-006-9028-8 — *Erkenntnis* 66(1–2): 73–105 (Crossref-verified).
- **Retrieval status**: **full text** (preprint PDF at `sfu.ca/~kathleea/colour/docs/colorprimitivism.pdf`, 13,413 words; all quotes grep-verified in the extracted text).
- **Key points**:
  - "A thesis that often turns up in discussion of primitivism is one Johnston has dubbed: REVELATION" — a third source attributing the *name* to Johnston. **[documented textual claim]**
  - "Revelation admits of two readings": is what is revealed the similarity fact itself, or that the similarity is *in the nature* of the colours?
  - "for present purposes we can treat Revelation as equivalent to the conjunction of two theses": **SELF-INTIMATION** — "If it is in the nature of the colors that p, then after careful reflection on color experience it seems to be in the nature of the colors that p" — and **INFALLIBILITY** — the converse. "Self-Intimation, in particular, is a very strong thesis. It implies that colors are not reflectance types". **[documented textual claim]**
  - "Self-Intimation—and so Revelation—entails Minimal Primitivism"; and given Infallibility as a background primitivist assumption plus Completeness, "Revelation is not an optional extra for primitivists: it is tantamount to Minimal Primitivism." **[documented textual claim]**
  - **The two theses, in their own words** (§3, potted history): "there is the Cartesian idea that careful introspection reveals the essence of the mental—in other words, Revelation-about-the-mind is true. Second, there is the Cartesian idea that color is in the mind, not in bodies ... Putting those two together yields Revelation and Eliminativism, and hence E-primitivism." Contemporary eliminativists who "at least flirt with Revelation" are listed as Hardin, Maund, Boghossian & Velleman, Averill and Chalmers. **[documented textual claim]**
  - The confusion to avoid: "Revelation will appear to be obviously true if one confuses it with the claim that ordinary visual experience is sufficient to know what colors are. Once that confusion is laid to rest, the status of Revelation is not so clear." (Their Yablo discussion: one can know *what* salt is while ignorant of its nature.)
  - Verdict: the standard objections to realist primitivism "are of little force", but "on closer examination, R-primitivism is unacceptable. Theorists of color should return to the usual suspects." Their decisive argument is the "Permuted Earth" argument against non-causal R-primitivism.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral on consciousness; their target is object colour. Their "Revelation-about-the-mind" is the thesis the rest of this note concerns.

### Lewis, "Should a materialist believe in qualia?" (1995)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1080/00048409512346451 — *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 73(1): 140–144 (Crossref-verified; also *Faith and Philosophy* 12(4): 467–471, 1995, and *Papers in Metaphysics and Epistemology*, CUP 1999, 325–331).
- **Retrieval status**: **full text** (PDF at andrewmbailey.com/dkl/, 2,885 words; quotes grep-verified).
- **Key points**:
  - Opening: "Should a materialist believe in qualia? Yes and no." Qualia are "the occupants of a certain functional role that is spelled out in our tacitly known folk psychology".
  - The thesis (p. 141–142): "Folk psychology says, I think, that we identify the qualia of our experiences. We know exactly what they are - and that in an uncommonly demanding and literal sense of 'knowing what'." ... "Call this the Identification Thesis." Spelled out: "the knowledge I thereby gain reveals the essence of Q: a property of Q such that, necessarily, Q has it and nothing else does." **[documented textual claim]**
  - "A materialist cannot accept the Identification Thesis. If qualia are physical properties of experiences, and experiences in turn are physical events, then it is certain that we seldom, if ever, identify the qualia of our experiences. Making discoveries in neurophysiology is not so easy!" (p. 142) **[documented textual claim]**
  - The **no-hidden-grain corollary** (note 4): "If we know exactly what the qualia of our experiences are, they can have no essential hidden structure - no 'grain' - of which we remain ignorant ... it is a consequence of the Identification Thesis that if we fail to notice structure, there is no structure there to notice." **[documented textual claim]** — This is the span that matters for the Map (see Analytic Question).
  - Two "harmless look-alikes" a materialist "can and should accept": (i) "the broad, de re content of my knowledge does, in the strongest sense, identify the qualia"; (ii) knowing what qualia are "in a not-so-demanding, not-so-literal, everyday sense" (the potassium example). **[documented textual claim]**
  - Lewis never uses the word "Revelation"; the identification of his Identification Thesis with Revelation is the literature's (Liu 2019: "With the phrase 'identify the quale in the appropriate "demanding and literal" sense', Lewis refers to the thesis of revelation"; Majeed 2017 abstract: "the identification thesis (revelation)"). **[contested interpretation — but uncontested in practice]**
- **Tenet alignment**: Lewis is a committed materialist; the Map must cite him *as rival* (co-optation firewall). His concession that the thesis is folk psychology is the datum both sides use.

### Goff — the Russellian use
- **Goff (2011)** "A posteriori physicalists get our phenomenal concepts wrong", *AJP* 89(2): 191–209, DOI 10.1080/00048401003649617 (Crossref-verified). **Abstract-only** (OpenAlex reconstruction, imperfect): a posteriori physicalists (Papineau, Loar) "departs from common sense in holding that our phenomenal concept of pain is opaque: thinking of pain in terms of how it feels reveals nothing of what it is for something to feel pain." - **Goff (2015)** "Real acquaintance and physicalism", in Coates & Coleman (eds.), *Phenomenal Qualities*, OUP, 121–143, DOI 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198712718.003.0005 (Crossref-verified). **Metadata only.**

## Major Positions

### Colour-Revelation (Johnston's constraint)
- **Proponents of the constraint as datum**: Johnston (who abandons it), Strawson 1989, Campbell 2005 (moderate), Gert 2008, Allen 2011/2016 (who argues naive realism entails its *falsity*).
- **Core claim**: a standard visual experience reveals the intrinsic nature of the colour of the object.
- **Key arguments**: Byrne & Hilbert's decomposition (Self-Intimation + Infallibility) and the "tantamount to Minimal Primitivism" result; their Permuted Earth argument against realist primitivism.
- **Relation to tenets**: Neutral. It is a thesis about object properties. The colour page uses it correctly as the hinge of the primitivism debate and nowhere imports it as a thesis about experience.

### Phenomenal Revelation, strong (Lewis's Identification; Goff's Transparency; Byrne & Hilbert's "Revelation-about-the-mind")
- **Proponents**: as a *truth* — Goff 2011/2015/2017, Nida-Rümelin 2007, Strawson, Horgan & Tienson 2001 (per Liu), Cortesi 2023 (A-Rev); as a *folk commitment to be rejected* — Lewis 1995; as a *folk commitment that explains the intuition of dualism* — Liu 2021.
- **Core claim**: having an experience with Q puts one in a position to know Q's full essence (Liu's (D); Lewis's "demanding and literal sense").
- **Key arguments**: the Damnjanovic/Goff reconstruction — if Q were physical and fully revealed, its physicality would be revealed; it is not; so Q is not physical.
- **Relation to tenets**: Superficially friendly to Tenet 1, actually hostile to Tenets 2–3 and to the Map's self-opacity commitments (see Analytic Question). **The Map should deny it.**

### Phenomenal Revelation, weak (partial essential characterization; Goff's "translucent"; Lewis's look-alikes; Zhou's "essence-indicating")
- **Proponents**: Damnjanovic 2012 ("a robust reading ... a posteriori physicalists can, and should, accept"), Trogdon 2017, Diaz-León 2014, Zhou 2025; Lewis's two harmless look-alikes.
- **Core claim**: experience reveals that there is a quality, some of its character (similarity structure, felt valence), and de re which property it is — without revealing its full essence.
- **Relation to tenets**: What Tenet 1's explanatory-gap rationale and the colour page's "a qualitative character is *given* in experience" actually require. Compatible with physicalism, hence earns the Map **compatible** and no more.

### Revelation-denial (Stoljar; type-B PCS on Goff's reading)
- **Proponents**: Stoljar 2006/2009 (not even intuitive); Papineau and Loar as read by Goff 2011 (phenomenal concepts opaque).
- **Relation to tenets**: The Map's PCS page L141 already argues against the opaque/"indicating only" retreat; `tenets.md` L103 holds that the first-person mode of presentation "requires that mode to *be* something beyond what it refers to". Both commit the Map to weak Revelation about the *mode*; neither commits it to strong Revelation about the *referent*.

### The both-ways turn (Majeed, Goff 2018, Botin, Lin, O'Conaill)
- **Core claim**: strong Revelation threatens panpsychism and Russellian monism exactly as it threatens physicalism (a quiddity's role in grounding mass is not revealed in experience either), and pressures every realist toward epiphenomenalism; the only escape is an essence/grounds distinction (Chalmers; Goff p. 198) that Lin argues is equally available to physicalists.
- **Relation to tenets**: This is where the Map's interest lies. It converts Revelation from a weapon the Map might borrow into a constraint the Map must respect.

## Key Debates

### Is Revelation intuitive, and does that matter?
- **Sides**: Lewis, Braddon-Mitchell, Hill, Papineau concede it is folk theory; Liu argues it linguistically; Stoljar denies it. **Current state**: open. Liu's claim that revelation *explains* the intuition of dualism is the meta-problem lens the Map lacks (`concepts/meta-problem-of-consciousness` has 0 hits for "Revelation" in this sense; its single "revelation" is a colloquial use at L41).

### Is Revelation compatible with physicalism?
- **Sides**: incompatible — Lewis, Goff, Nida-Rümelin, Liu (on the de dicto reading); compatible — Damnjanovic (robust reading), Trogdon (partial reading), Diaz-León, Zhou (essence-indicating, non-satisfactional reference). **Current state**: the dispute has resolved into a dispute about *which* Revelation; nobody now holds that *partial* Revelation refutes physicalism.

### Does Revelation favour panpsychism / Russellian monism?
- **Sides**: Goff 2017 yes; Majeed 2017 (exposition and assessment), Botin 2023, Lin 2025 no or not without a distinction strategy equally available to physicalists; Liu 2021 records it as unclear. **Current state**: live, 2017–2025, and it is the debate the Map's Russellian-monism pages should cite.

### Does Revelation push toward epiphenomenalism?
- **Sides**: Goff 2018 raises it and answers with "consciousness+"; Mørch answers with phenomenal powers (the Map already treats Mørch at `topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions` L120 as "the Map's closest competitor" and at `topics/metaproblem-of-consciousness-under-dualism` L78). **Current state**: open, and it is the Map's own exposure.

### One thesis or two?
- **The answer the literature gives**: two, formally parallel. Byrne & Hilbert name "Revelation-about-the-mind" separately from colour-Revelation; Liu 2019 sets the colour thesis aside for its namesake; Cortesi records the name travelling from colour (Johnston on Strawson) to mind. The bridge is explicit in Byrne & Hilbert: Cartesian Revelation-about-the-mind plus colour-in-the-mind *yields* colour-Revelation for the eliminativist. **[Map reconstruction]** The Map's colour page describes exactly that bridge from the other side: when four of five colour ontologies relocate the quality into experience, the Revelation that survives is phenomenal-Revelation about the relocated quality, and the colour page's closing sentence — the criticism "doubts whether the given character licenses a conclusion about objects, not whether there is a given character" — is weak phenomenal-Revelation stated without the name.

## The Analytic Question: What does the Map need from Revelation?

**[Map reconstruction throughout, unless marked.]**

**1. What the Map's existing claims presuppose.** Tenet 1's rationale is the explanatory gap: "no amount of neurological detail has yet told us *why* there is something it is like to be conscious" (`tenets.md` Dualism section). The colour page holds that "What no party disputes is that a qualitative character is *given* in experience; they disagree about whether the giving is veridical" (L66). `intrinsic-nature` L67 states the Russellian hinge — "We know what red looks like by seeing it, what pain is like by feeling it—non-relationally, from the inside" — and immediately marks it "the programme's most contested premise". `tenets.md` L103 holds that the first-person mode of presentation "requires that mode to *be* something beyond what it refers to". Each of these needs **weak Revelation**: that experience reveals the *existence* of a phenomenal quality and *some of its character* (that it is like this rather than that; that it is not exhausted by causal role). None needs the full essence revealed.

**2. Why the Map must not assume strong Revelation.** Three independent reasons:
- *Self-opacity.* Lewis's corollary — "if we fail to notice structure, there is no structure there to notice" — is a consequence of the strong thesis. The Map holds the opposite at `voids/self-opacity` ("Consciousness cannot fully know itself"), `voids/phenomenal-quality-void` (the mechanism "hides itself either way"), and `concepts/introspection` (eight occurrences of "unreliab", Schwitzgebel engaged). Strong Revelation and self-opacity cannot both be held.
- *The interface.* Goff 2018: "introspection does not seem to reveal our conscious states as being essentially causal." The Map's Tenets 2–3 posit a selection power at the quantum interface, and `positions/quantum-interface` L145 says the interface "is structurally selected to leave only indirect traces". If the full essence of a phenomenal state were revealed in having it, and no selection power is revealed, then the power is not essential — which is the epiphenomenalist's conclusion by another route. The Map's only escapes are Goff's: an essence/grounds distinction (and Lin 2025 argues that strategy rescues physicalism just as well) or a phenomenal-powers view (Mørch's, already the Map's "closest competitor"). Cheaper to deny strong Revelation.
- *The rival.* Strong Revelation is the engine of Goff's argument *for* Russellian monism and *against* physicalism. The Map engages Russellian monism as a rival on the grounds (`russellian-monism` L55–71; `russellian-monism-versus-bi-aspectual-dualism` L68–82) that it is unstable and fragmenting. A Map that borrowed strong Revelation would be borrowing the rival's premise while rejecting its conclusion. (Corrected 2026-10-02: an earlier clause here said Majeed 2017 "shows" the premise makes panpsychism the natural terminus; his abstract offers "an exposition, as well as an assessment" of Lewis's revelation-plus-panphenomenalism exception to humility, and makes no such claim.)

**3. The tier result.** On the compatible / suggestive / discriminating ladder (`project/evidential-status-discipline` §"Compatibility vs. Support at the Rival-Model Interface"): weak Revelation is **compatible** with physicalism (Damnjanovic, Trogdon, Diaz-León, Zhou all accept a form of it) and so with everything; it discriminates nothing. Strong Revelation *would* be discriminating against physicalism — that is its whole appeal — but the Map cannot hold it. **Net: the argument from revelation is not available to the Map as support for Tenet 1.** Tenet 1 must continue to rest on the explanatory gap and conceivability, and any future tenet-section sentence of the form "experience reveals that the quality is non-physical" is an over-reach of exactly the kind the 2026-09-30 synthesis cluster 6 flagged in `galilean-exclusion`.

**4. Which Revelation does PCS deny, and does the Map's PCS engagement commit it?** Type-B PCS, on Goff's reading, denies strong Revelation (phenomenal concepts opaque or at best translucent); Trogdon's "indirect challenge" is that if experience reveals *anything* of nature, the PCS account of phenomenal concepts as mere recognitional/indexical devices is under pressure. The Map's PCS page (L141) argues that the "indicating without revealing" retreat "simply restates the explanatory gap" — a weak-Revelation premise (concepts reveal *something* of why the state is experienced). `tenets.md` L103 likewise claims only that the mode of presentation is "something beyond what it refers to". Both are consistent with weak Revelation and silent on strong. **The one sentence a Revelation page should add to the PCS engagement**: the Map's objection to opaque phenomenal concepts does not require, and must not drift into, the claim that phenomenal concepts are *transparent* in Goff's sense.

**5. Where a page sits.** Between `phenomenal-acquaintance` (the relation) and `intrinsic-nature` (the Russellian hinge the relation is supposed to deliver), as the *epistemic strength* parameter of the relation: acquaintance reveals *how much*? It should cross-link `phenomenal-transparency-opacity-spectrum` with an explicit disambiguation (Metzinger's transparency: experience hides itself as representation; Goff's transparency: concepts reveal their referents' natures — opposite polarities, same word), `phenomenal-concepts-strategy` (which Revelation PCS denies), `russellian-monism` and `russellian-monism-versus-bi-aspectual-dualism` (Goff's use; Majeed/Lin/Botin), `colour-ontology-and-the-secondary-quality-residue` (the colour version and the bridge), `meta-problem-of-consciousness` (Liu 2021), `self-opacity` and `introspection` (why the strong thesis is denied), and `epiphenomenalism` (Goff 2018).
