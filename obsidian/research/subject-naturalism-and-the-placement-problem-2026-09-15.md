## Executive Summary

Huw Price's programme is exactly the "framing-undercut" objection the outer reviewers said the Map never fields, and the corpus really does lack it: "placement problem", "subject naturalism" and "global expressivism" return zero across live articles, and Price appears only in the time-symmetry cluster. The structure is clean. *Object naturalism* says all there is is the world studied by science; combined with *representationalism* (the "proto-theory" that statements represent aspects of the world), it generates *placement problems*: where in the natural world are the truthmakers for moral, modal, semantic and mental talk? Price's *subject naturalism* starts instead from science's account of us as natural creatures, argues that it is theoretically prior (the *Priority Thesis*), and holds that representationalism is a bad proto-theory by subject-naturalist lights — so the semantic ladder from "we use the term 'X'" to "what is X, naturalistically?" never gets climbed, and the placement problems "dissolve" into questions about the functions of vocabularies. Three things matter for the Map. First, Price himself files Chalmers-style property dualism inside the placement game as the "expansionist" strategy — so on his taxonomy the Map is one more contestant in a game he thinks should not be played, and the Map has to say why the question is not malformed. Second, Price never develops the mental case: consciousness is listed among the "classic misfits" and then dropped; the M-worlds paper, the NWM introduction and EPR contain no treatment of phenomenal experience, and neither the deVries nor the MacFarlane review finds one. Third, the 2020s replies keep the contest live from inside naturalism: Beasley (subject naturalism cannot target the subjects it is about — "a subject naturalist analogue of a placement problem"), Legg & Giladi (global expressivism smuggles in a word–world dualism and nominalism), Shapiro (it collapses into global subjectivism; Price's strategy needs no scientific vocabulary), Knowles (anti-representationalism does not escape placement metaphysics), Macarthur (liberal naturalism dissolves placement by a different route — persons are not posits). The Map's best reply is not to defend "matching" but to point out that its Tenets 2–3 are first-order causal claims in the very vocabulary Price grants e-representational status, so they are not dissolved but adjudicated on evidence — and that a functional story about consciousness-*talk* is a meta-problem strategy that Price has not actually supplied.

## Key Sources

### Price, "Naturalism without Representationalism" (2004)
- **URL**: https://prce.hu/w/preprints/naturalism-final.pdf (author preprint; published in De Caro & Macarthur, eds., *Naturalism in Question*, Harvard UP 2004, pp. 71–88; reprinted in *Naturalism Without Mirrors* 2011 and as Lecture 1 of *Expressivism, Pragmatism and Representationalism* 2013, pp. 3–21)
- **Type**: Paper / book chapter (the founding statement)
- **Key points**:
  - Object naturalism defined as an ontological and epistemological doctrine: "in some important sense, all there is is the world studied by science ... all genuine knowledge is scientific knowledge." Its cost: "in so far as philosophy is concerned with the nature of objects and properties of various kinds, its concern is with something in the natural world, or with nothing at all."
  - Placement problems named: "The difficulties stem from the fact that in many interesting cases it is hard to see what natural facts we could be talking about."
  - Subject naturalism: "philosophy needs to begin with what science tells us about ourselves. Science tells us that we humans are natural creatures, and if the claims and ambitions of philosophy conflict with this view, then philosophy needs to give way. This is naturalism in the sense of Hume, then, and arguably Nietzsche."
  - **Priority Thesis** (verbatim): "Subject naturalism is theoretically prior to object naturalism, because the latter depends on validation from a subject naturalist perspective." Gloss: "If the presuppositions of object naturalism turn out to be suspect, from this self-reflective scientific standpoint, then subject naturalism gives us reason to reject object naturalism."
  - The *linguistic conception* of placement problems: "If the linguistic conception is correct, then placement problems are initially problems about human linguistic behaviour (or perhaps about human thought)." The step to metaphysics is the *semantic ladder*, which "relies on what we may call the representationalist" assumption. Two arguments that object naturalism cannot then be validated: semantic deflationism (if "truth" and "refers" are not substantive relations, the ladder has no rungs) and Stich's indeterminacy (even a substantive theory of reference will not be determinate enough to settle "What is belief?" any more than "Are there any beliefs?").
  - The closing challenge, verbatim: "The difficult opponent is the naturalist who takes advantage of a non-representationalist theoretical perspective to avoid the material mode altogether. If such an opponent can explain why natural creatures in a natural environment come to talk in these plural ways — of 'truth', 'value', 'meaning', 'causation', and all the rest — what puzzle remains? What debt does philosophy now owe to science?"
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with Tenet 1 at the level of *well-formedness*: the dualism-vs-materialism question is, for Price, a placement question that lapses. Note the conflict is not a rival answer; it is a denial that there is a question.
- **Note on the target**: the 2004 paper argues in general; "mind" appears four times and consciousness, phenomenal and qualia zero times. Its worked cases are truth, value, meaning and causation.

### Price, *Naturalism Without Mirrors* (OUP New York, 2011) — draft introduction
- **URL**: https://prce.hu/w/preprints/IntroductionFinal.pdf (author draft, "Introduction: moving the mirror aside"); OUP page https://global.oup.com/academic/product/naturalism-without-mirrors-9780195084337
- **Type**: Book (collected essays 1991–2009)
- **Key points**:
  - The "matching game" metaphor: statements as stickers to be placed on truthmakers; the placement problem is the mismatch between "the rich world of ordinary discourse and the sparse world apparently described by science."
  - Consciousness appears once, as a misfit: "the problem cases are not just the classic misfits, such as the (apparent?) truths of aesthetics, morality, and other normative matters, or those of consciousness."
  - **Price's taxonomy of placement strategies**, with dualism inside it: (1) reductionism, "a noteworthy recent version" being Jackson's Canberra Plan; (2) expanding the right-hand side — either more natural facts than we thought, or "the constraint itself is at fault, and ... we need to recognise that there are non-natural facts"; Price calls these "super-additive" or "expansionist" approaches, and his footnoted example is: **"David Chalmers' (1996) view of consciousness is a familiar example."** (Moore on moral facts is the other.) (3) reducing the left-hand side — eliminativism, fictionalism, expressivism. Price's own view is the Wittgensteinian alternative that "attributes the plurality in question to diversity in the things we do with language, rather than to some pre-existing metaphysical or natural diversity in the world," which he calls "a more economical way of doing justice to similarly pluralistic intuitions."
  - Global expressivism is not local expressivism made global: "I am not proposing merely that genuine representation turns out to be a linguistic function that is not in play in our own language, but that representation (in this sense ...) is a theoretical category we should discard."
  - The two nodes of representation (later e-/i-representation): covariation with the environment vs. "internal cognitive role"; and the concession that on the internal notion "there is [an] interesting sense in which mental and linguistic representation are to be characterised and identified in terms of their roles in networks of various kinds" — causal-functional, normative-inferential, or computational.
  - The dissolution stated: "if representation is viewed as relation to our natural environment, univocity leads to the placement problem in an acute form. The problem is solved by abandoning the external notion of representation in favour of an internal notion; ... and by insisting that we theorise about our relations to our natural environment in a different, non-semantic vocabulary. So long as we practice our naturalism in another key ... we retain univocity where it matters, while avoiding the placement problems altogether."
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with Tenet 1 (dualism is the "expansionist" placement move); conflicts with Tenet 5 by motivation (the whole programme is sold as "more economical"); *neutral-to-congenial* on the network concession — mental representation characterised by causal-functional role is compatible with the Map's interface framework.

### Price, "Two expressivist programmes, two bifurcations" (EPR 2013, ch. 2, pp. 22–44)
- **URL**: https://sites.pitt.edu/~rbrandom/Courses/Antirepresentationalism%20(2020)/Texts/Price%20Two%20Expressivist%20Programmes%20Two%20Bifurcations.pdf (course copy); book DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511842498
- **Type**: Lecture (René Descartes Lectures, Tilburg 2008)
- **Key points**:
  - The mantra/proto-theory diagnosis restated: "The puzzle stems from combining the mantra with Representationalism ... Object naturalists combine this proto-theory with the mantra's restriction on the available truth-makers, and it is the combination that leads to the puzzles."
  - Footnote 9 repeats the classification: "David Chalmers's (1996) view of consciousness is a familiar example" of the non-naturalist expansionist option.
  - **e-Representation** defined: "the environment-tracking paradigm of representation, dependent on such notions as covariation and 'indication relations' (Field 1994) — think of examples such as the position of the needle in the fuel gauge ... it puts the system–world link on the front foot." **i-Representation**: "something counts as a representation in virtue of its position or role in some cognitive or inferential architecture."
  - The *new bifurcation thesis*: no first-class/economy-class distinction is available at the i-representational level ("the answer to this question must be 'no'"), but vocabularies may differ in "their e-representational functions" — which is where science's tracking of the environment lives.
- **Tenet alignment**: This is the hinge for the Map's reply. Price keeps a level (e-representation, "a different, non-semantic vocabulary") at which claims about what covaries with what in the natural environment are assessed as ordinary science. The Map's Tenets 2–3 are claims of that kind.

### Beasley, "Naturalism without a subject: Huw Price's pragmatism" (2020/2023)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1080/0020174X.2020.1820903 — *Inquiry* 66(10): 1793–1820 (online 16 Sep 2020); full text at https://sites.pitt.edu/~rbrandom/Courses/Antirepresentationalism%20(2020)/Texts/Beasley%20Naturalism%20without%20a%20subject_%20Huw%20Price's%20pragmatism[6158].pdf
- **Type**: Paper (read in full)
- **Key points**:
  - Abstract, verbatim: "Price's view doesn't work because no subject naturalist vocabulary is sufficient to describe any assertional practice; there is no way to connect his expressive-functionalist explanations to the practices and concepts which are their subject – nor, even, to the human subjects who are the focus of a philosophical anthropology."
  - The core move (§3): "the restrictive constraints of subject naturalism mean that no vocabulary so constrained is expressively powerful enough to do that – no subject naturalist expressivist story is able to actually target the conceptual activity it is supposed to explain. **This is a subject naturalist analogue of a placement problem**, and its consequences for Price's pragmatism, and its proposed philosophical anthropology, are grim."

### Shapiro, "Price's Subject Naturalism and Liberal Naturalism" (2022)
- **URL**: https://shapiro-philosophy.media.uconn.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/541/2020/12/SubjectNaturalism.pdf (final preprint); in De Caro & Macarthur, eds., *The Routledge Handbook of Liberal Naturalism*, Routledge 2022
- **Type**: Handbook chapter (read in full)
- **Key points**:
  - Two Pricean strategies distinguished: undermining the *representationalist route* to object naturalism, and undermining the *functional uniformity* presupposition. Price has since conceded that an object naturalism which rejects representationalism is available (citing Knowles 2018, 297–300; Price 2011c: 5; 2013: 25n4) — so the first strategy only bites a representationalist object naturalist.
  - Neither strategy needs natural-scientific vocabulary: "I see no reason why deflationary explanations of semantic talk must be presented from the 'scientific perspective of a linguistic anthropologist'"; quoting Knowles (2011: 79): "if ultimately there is no necessity about using scientific conceptions in the logic of expressivist explanation, then it becomes very unclear what the naturalism really amounts to here."
  - Both strategies involve semantic ascent and so "must employ semantic locutions tied to propositional-attitude ascriptions" — which "by Price's own lights" cannot be reconstructed "from within the austere perspective of the natural sciences."
  - Recommends to the subject naturalist a liberal naturalism "about inquiry into discourse and thought, not a metaphysical view about the extent of the natural realm."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral; useful as the honest statement that Price's *naturalism* is doing less work than his *anti-representationalism*. Also lists Shapiro 2014 (*PQ* 64: 497–506) and Shapiro 2021 "Global expressivism as global subjectivism" (*Phil Studies* 179(3): 777–799, DOI 10.1007/s11098-021-01694-1; abstract unobtainable).

## Major Positions

### Subject naturalism / global expressivism (Price)
- **Proponents**: Huw Price; allies with qualifications — Blackburn (quasi-realism, but local), Brandom (inferentialism; "global anti-representationalism?"), Macarthur (co-author 2007; later critic on scientism), Horwich, Williams.
- **Core claim**: begin with science's account of us as natural creatures; representationalism is a bad proto-theory; placement problems for the "M-worlds" lapse once representation is replaced by i-representation (inferential role) plus e-representation (environment-tracking, theorised in non-semantic vocabulary); vocabularies are explained by their functions in the lives of natural creatures.
- **Key arguments**: Priority Thesis; deflationism blocks the semantic ladder; Stich-style indeterminacy; the "what puzzle remains?" challenge; the Carnapian internal/external diagnosis.
- **Relation to site tenets**: denies that Tenet 1 states a well-formed metaphysical question; treats Chalmers-style dualism as the "expansionist" placement move; motivated by economy (Tenet 5 territory). Price never applies the machinery to phenomenal consciousness.

### Object naturalism with the location problem (Jackson; the Canberra Plan)
- **Proponents**: Jackson, Pettit, Braddon-Mitchell.
- **Core claim**: serious metaphysics must locate every genuine property in the physical story via a priori conceptual analysis ("entry by entailment").
- **Relation to site tenets**: the physicalist pole of the contest Price dissolves; the Map already opposes it on first-order grounds.

### Liberal naturalism (De Caro, Macarthur, McDowell; Beasley and Shapiro recommend it to the subject naturalist)
- **Core claim**: nature outruns the theoretical scientific image; persons, reasons and values are natural but not scientific posits; placement is dissolved by refusing scientism, not by dropping representation.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral on Tenet 1 (McDowell is no dualist), but denies that the scientific image is the whole of nature — congenial to Tenets 1 and 5 in method, hostile to the Map's insistence on a *causal* interface (Tenets 2–3) only if it treats causal talk about persons as category-confused.

### Peircean realism (Legg & Giladi; Misak's Peircean line)
- **Core claim**: metaphysics as "maximally general inquiry into the nature and structure of reality" is indispensable; global expressivism hides a word–world dualism and nominalism; real generals exist.
- **Relation to site tenets**: keeps the contest live; aligns with the Map's reading of pragmatism as convergent inquiry (already in `concepts/pragmatism`), not with any particular answer.

### The subject-naturalist-analogue objection (Beasley)
- **Core claim**: a scientific-image vocabulary cannot serve as a pragmatic metavocabulary for normative practices; the anthropology cannot reach the anthropos.
- **Relation to site tenets**: structurally parallel to the Map's reflexive argument; strictly about normativity and content, not phenomenality.

## Key Debates

### Is the dualism–materialism question malformed or open?
- **Sides**: Price (malformed: a placement question generated by representationalism) vs. Jackson (open and answerable by location), Chalmers (open; Price's own example of the expansionist answer), Legg & Giladi (open; metaphysics indispensable).
- **Core disagreement**: whether "is consciousness physical?" is an internal question in a scientific framework, an external pseudo-question, or a question that survives the collapse of representationalism because it is *causal* rather than *semantic*.
- **Current state**: ongoing; Price's 2019 and 2023 pieces still defend the global programme; the 2016–2025 literature (Knowles, Legg & Giladi, Beasley, Shapiro, Zhu) is predominantly critical from *inside* pragmatism and naturalism.

### Can the placement problem be dissolved for the *mental* specifically?
- **Sides**: Price asserts the general dissolution and lists consciousness among the misfits; no text of his read here applies it. Liberal naturalists dissolve it differently (persons are not posits). Illusionists and meta-problem theorists (Frankish, Chalmers 2018) hold that consciousness-*talk* still needs a functional explanation on any view.
- **Core disagreement**: whether explaining why natural creatures talk of "experience" (Price's "what puzzle remains?") is *available* without either (a) a first-order theory of what experience is, or (b) the network/i-representation concession collapsing into functionalism, which the Map's `concepts/pragmatism` already answers in "the functionalist concession."
- **Current state**: unresolved; a genuine gap in Price's corpus, confirmed by two reviewers' silence on it.

### Does subject naturalism need science at all? (the scientism charge)
- **Sides**: Macarthur 2014, Shapiro 2022, Knowles 2011 vs. Price.
- **Core disagreement**: whether the "naturalism" is doing work or is a rhetorical badge on an anti-representationalism that runs on semantic ascent and attitude ascriptions.
- **Current state**: Shapiro's negative answers to his questions (1)–(3) are, so far as this search found, unanswered by Price directly.
