## Executive Summary

Ladyman and Ross's rainforest realism takes Dennett's (1991) test of reality, compression relative to the bit map. They add projectibility and make the test their criterion of existence: "what exist are just real patterns" (*Every Thing Must Go*, p. 121, as quoted by Hawley 2010). Ontology is then scale-relative and has no fundamental level. The sources read here answer the subject's question in three parts.

**1. Cognitive mind is located, as non-reductive functional real patterns.** Ross is a co-author of the book, and Spurrett contributed to it. In 2004 the two defended "the anti-reductionist, functionalist consensus" of cognitive science against Kim's exclusion argument, using the pattern ontology. They argued that "we need not choose between reducing minds or finding them causally impotent", rejected talk of "emergence", and denied that supervenience holds generally. Wallace (2003) extended "Dennett's criterion" to conscious observers on a condition he states himself, "if we are happy to extend" it, and he named the extension "functionalism". The answer to the brief's sub-question is therefore **yes**: where this programme gives mind a place, the place is non-reductive functionalism. Two qualifications make it unusual. It rejects general supervenience, and its authors call themselves "not physicalists" (2010, p. 181).

**2. Ladyman and Ross have stayed silent on phenomenal consciousness when pressed.** In the 2010 *Metascience* symposium, Paul Humphreys argued that a structuralist position "has to deny the scientific relevance of intrinsic properties", which in theories of mind include "qualia and the first person perspective" (pp. 171–172). The authors' reply runs from p. 179 to p. 185. It answers Humphreys at length but never mentions qualia, the first person, consciousness, intrinsic properties or subjectivity; the raw counts are zero for each, against 19 mentions of "Humphreys". In a 2015 interview Ladyman said of consciousness, "I don't have specific views about this". Of OSR he said, "I am not sure what implications this has for debates about consciousness". The book itself was not retrievable, so whether it discusses consciousness remains open. But the authors' own later statements are on record, and none supplies a view.

**3. The join was made from the phenomenal side.** Kristjan Loorits's 2019 Helsinki dissertation combines "a neurobiological account of qualia ... with a version of structural realism (Ladyman et al. 2007)" (p. 1). It holds that "phenomenal experiences are real patterns and not merely useful patterns in an instrumentalist sense" (p. 26). Asked why those patterns are conscious, it answers that "it would make no sense to ask" (p. 25). His 2014 paper, which the Map already cites, had sketched the same move through Ladyman and Ross's pattern-of-patterns framework. This **overtakes three claims** on `concepts/ontic-structural-realism`: L37 ("published only in outline"), L59 ("Loorits ... without resting on OSR about physics"; "this article cannot close"), and L105 ("if pressed"). See [Defects](#defects).

**The Map's question, answered as the Map's analysis.** The Galilean-exclusion argument on the OSR page bites, but at a different place. Rainforest realism does not abolish a residue, as eliminative OSR would. Instead it makes compression of a data set the criterion of existence. Run over third-person records, that criterion can certify the neural pattern that correlates with experience, but not that the pattern is felt. The sharper conflict with the Map is elsewhere, in the Primacy of Physics Constraint and its inductive support from "a lack of downward influence" (Humphreys's summary, p. 170). That conflict bears on [[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]], and [[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]] is built to meet it. Details in [The Map's Analysis](#map-analysis).

## Major Positions

### Rainforest realism (Ross 2000; Ladyman and Ross 2007)
- **Core claim**: realism about the ontologies of the special sciences. Reality is the reality of real patterns: projectible, compressive and non-redundant. Ontology is scale-relative, and no fundamental level is assumed. It is conjoined with OSR about physics.
- **Constraints**: the PPC (ETMG p. 44) and the PNC. Fundamental physics is "that part of physics about which measurements taken anywhere in the universe carry information" (p. 55).
- **On mind** (Ross and Spurrett 2004): cognitive and behavioural states are real causes; functionalism is "in no serious philosophical trouble"; general supervenience is denied; "emergence" talk is rejected.
- **On phenomenal consciousness**: no view in any source consulted. Ladyman (2015): "I don't have specific views about this."
- **Relation to tenets**: compatible with nothing the Map says about intrinsic phenomenal character, and incompatible with none of it explicitly. Its live conflicts with the Map are the PPC's inductive case against downward influence (Tenet 3) and its Occam framing (Tenet 5).

### Functionalist real-pattern observers (Wallace 2003)
- **Core claim**: Dennett's criterion extended "to conscious observers". An observer is "any pattern which behaves like an observer", which is "functionalism". Combined with decoherence, this multiplies observers across Everettian branches.
- **Relation to tenets**: this is the step Tenet 4 (and Dualism) refuse. Wallace himself presents it as conditional.

### Phenomenal real-pattern structuralism (Loorits 2014, 2019)
- **Core claim**: phenomenal experiences are real patterns in neural data by Ladyman and Ross's criteria. Consciousness is "a fully structural phenomenon". The question of why a given pattern is conscious "would make no sense".
- **Relation to tenets**: directly contrary to Dualism. It is the strongest available form of the "join" that the OSR page could not locate.

### The critic's position (Humphreys 2010)
- **Core claim**: structuralism's denial of the scientific relevance of intrinsic properties is the same issue that splits theories of mind over qualia and the first-person perspective. Mind is the one domain where there is "first person perspective to account for".
- **Relation to tenets**: aligned with Dualism. Humphreys is a philosopher of science raising it as an objection to ETMG, not a dualist.

## Key Debates

### Does the real-patterns criterion vindicate mental causation?
- **Sides**: Ross and Spurrett (2004) say yes: Kim's exclusion argument equivocates between a scientific and a metaphysical notion of causality. Wallace (2004) agrees and sharpens the point: on this view "the causal power of the mental stands and falls with the causal power of almost everything", which comes "close to a reductio of Kim's argument". Humphreys (2010) finds the real patterns "anemic" and the book "dismissive" of ontological emergence.
- **Current state**: open. Ladyman (2008, *Philosophy of Science* 75(5): 744–755) and Cordovil, Santos and Symons (2023) take up exclusion under OSR; both are unread here.
- **For the Map**: the vindication covers functional causes, the kind the scientific notion of causality tracks. It is silent on whether *felt* character makes a difference, which is the Map's question (see `topics/argument-from-reason` L85).

### Can real patterns carry phenomenal consciousness?
- **Sides**: Loorits (2019) says yes and dismisses the residual question. Wallace (2003) says yes for observers, if one accepts functionalism. Humphreys (2010) says the structuralist must deny intrinsic properties, and that this is where the qualia split lies. Ladyman (2015) has no specific view.
- **Current state**: no published reply to Humphreys by Ladyman or Ross was found. No critique of Loorits's real-pattern grounding specifically was found. Negro (2025), already on the structuralism page, argues against metaphysical phenomenal structuralism generally (abstract only).

## The Map's Analysis {#map-analysis}

Everything in this section is the Map's reading. Attribute to Ladyman and Ross only what the Key Sources record.

### Locate, eliminate, or leave out?

- **Cognitive and intentional mind: located.** It is located as real patterns, on a non-reductive functionalism with three features standard non-reductive physicalism lacks. There is no general supervenience. Causation is treated as a scale-relative scientific notion rather than a metaphysical one. And the authors refuse the label "physicalist", which they define as every useful special-science claim being explained by fundamental physics (2010, p. 182).
- **Phenomenal consciousness: left out by Ladyman and Ross, and located by others using their machinery.** In no source consulted do the authors eliminate consciousness. Ladyman's "I doubt consciousness exists on very short timescales" treats it as a scale-relative real thing (2015). They locate it nowhere either. Locating was done by Wallace, conditionally and as functionalism, and by Loorits, outright and as structure.
- **On the Map's taxonomy.** Under the "no fundamental mentality" constraint of `topics/hempels-dilemma`, the view counts as non-dualist despite its anti-physicalist label. Fundamental physics, "that part of physics about which measurements taken anywhere in the universe carry information", contains no mentality, and the PPC subordinates special-science hypotheses to it. This is the Map's inference, so do not write that Ladyman and Ross are physicalists.

### Does the Galilean-exclusion argument bite?

The OSR page contrasts two moves: "**Galileo relocates the residue into the sentient body; ontic structural realism abolishes it.**" Rainforest realism is a third. It **readmits by pattern**: tables, beliefs and selves return, as real patterns at their scales. The residue is neither relocated nor denied. It is simply not something the criterion can see. The bite therefore moves from ontology to the criterion of existence:

1. **The criterion is defined over a data set.** Dennett's test asks whether there is a description "more efficient than the bit map". Ladyman and Ross add projectibility and non-redundancy. What counts as existing is fixed by what compresses the data.
2. **If the data are third-person records** (neural firings, behaviour, reports), the criterion can certify the pattern that correlates with experience. It cannot certify that the pattern is felt. Loorits's own example shows this: a neural rhythm is a real pattern "in data consisting of individual neuronal firings" (p. 26). Which of the several real patterns in that data is the experience is fixed by its correlation with what the subject reports seeing. That correlation relies on the subject's report, which is first-person evidence.
3. **If the data include the felt given**, the criterion admits phenomenal character as a datum. Loorits grants that every theory treats some data elements "as a sort of nonstructural given", to be analysed by "some other theory" (p. 23). The Map's question is which theory analyses the felt given, and whether the regress ends in a structural account or in acquaintance. The OSR page's "acquaintance with one non-structural item" (L89) is where this lands.
4. **Loorits's answer to the residual question is a refusal.** "It would make no sense to ask why that particular structure is phenomenally conscious" (p. 25). That follows only if consciousness is "a fully structural phenomenon", which is the thesis at issue. It is the same circularity the structuralism page L104 finds in his inverted-qualia argument.

**Counter-pressure, stated fairly.** A real-patterns theorist can reply that the "felt given" is itself a heterophenomenological posit, a pattern in reports. On that view the dilemma's second horn is an unearned extra. That is the illusionist and Dennettian boundary (`concepts/heterophenomenology`), and the Map records it as a framework disagreement, not a refutation.

### The sharper conflict: PPC and Bidirectional Interaction

The PPC rejects any special-science hypothesis that conflicts "with fundamental physics, or such consensus as there is in fundamental physics". Tenet 2 is written to avoid that conflict. It requires "no detectable energy injection, no detectable Born-statistics deviation, no conservation-law violation", with mind acting where "physics leaves outcomes undetermined". So the principle is not where the Map and rainforest realism part. They part in two places.

- **The induction.** By Humphreys's account, ETMG backs the primacy of physics partly with "a lack of downward influence from special science phenomena to physical phenomena". Bidirectional Interaction is a claim of exactly such downward influence. The Map's standing reply is that the induction is drawn from domains where no consciousness-sensitive indeterminacy was probed. That is an argument about what the evidence covers, not a demonstration.
- **Closure's status.** Whether causal closure belongs to "such consensus as there is in fundamental physics" is unclear on their own terms. Ross and Spurrett separate "two distinct notions of causality (one primarily scientific, the other metaphysical)". Ladyman's SEP entry has ontology featuring "different emergent structures of causation and law" at different scales. ETMG ch. 5, "Causation in a Structural World" (pp. 258–297), presumably says more, but it is unread, so do not attribute a view of causation's level to the book. If closure is not a fundamental-physics claim, the PPC does not protect it. That conditional is the Map's.
- **The vitalism parallel.** Their one firm unifiability commitment is that vitalism's rejection is "plausibly based on reflection on fundamental structure" (2010, p. 184). Ladyman's interview analogy (gases and pressure, organisms and life) points the same way. The Map's [[vitalism]] page answers this analogy. Anyone folding this material should link there and not re-argue it.

### Tenet 4: real-pattern observers and real-pattern individuals

Wallace's Everettian observers come from the functionalist extension of Dennett's criterion, which Wallace marks as optional ("if we are happy to extend"). ETMG goes further about individuals in general: "All individuals, we will argue, are second-order real patterns" (243, via Hawley). Elsewhere they are "epistemological book-keeping devices" (240). On either reading, the subject is a pattern without haecceity. This feeds the indexical line on OSR L101 ("which structure-instance is mine") and Nimmo's countermodel already recorded there. The Map's refusal of the functionalist extension is what keeps its rejection of many-worlds from being merely a preference about branches.

### Tenet 5: whose Occam?

Ladyman and Ross present the criterion as Occam's razor: it "expresses the principle of Occam's razor, in restricting ontological commitment to what is required for a maximally empirically adequate science" (ETMG pp. 233–234, via Franklin and Robertson 2024, p. 22). Ladyman (2015) finds "positing extra physical properties solely to account for emergent phenomena" "not fecund". On the Map's reading, empirical adequacy is measured on an evidence base that the Galilean exclusion shaped. An Occam applied over that base cannot weigh what the base was built to leave out. This is Tenet 5's incomplete-knowledge clause, and it does not show that the razor is wrong.
