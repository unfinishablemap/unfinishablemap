## Executive Summary

Cognitive motor dissociation (CMD) is a positive response on a task-based fMRI or EEG command-following paradigm in a patient who shows no command-following at the bedside. Its prevalence depends on the denominator, method and site. The best single cohort, Bodien et al. 2024 (NEJM), found CMD in 60 of 241 patients without observable command-following (25%), but that denominator mixes 140 coma/vegetative-state patients with 101 patients in the minimally conscious state minus (MCS−), a state already classified as conscious; among the clinically unconscious (coma/VS) the derived rate is 28/140 = 20%. Earlier figures (Kondziella et al. 2016 meta-analysis: 14% of VS on active paradigms, "roughly 15%"; Claassen et al. 2019: 15% of acute ICU patients on EEG) are lower, and Bodien et al. report their own figure as "5-10 percent higher than previously reported." The ≈40% misdiagnosis rate (Schnakers et al. 2009: 41%; Andrews et al. 1996: 43%; Childs et al. 1993: 37%) is a different finding: patients given a consensus VS diagnosis who show MCS behaviours on a *standardized behavioural* examination. Behavioural assessment caught those patients; they are not covert cases. The detection paradigms have high false-negative rates (Bodien: test responses absent in more than 60% of patients who *do* follow commands at the bedside) and low inter-modality agreement (fMRI–EEG kappa 0.02–0.04). What the figures license is that bedside behaviour, even standardized and repeated, misses volitional cognition in a minority of patients, and that a negative test shows nothing. They do not license "consciousness persists where its neural basis has failed." CMD is detected *by* preserved, task-specific neural activity, and its predictors (traumatic aetiology, MCS− over VS, absence of midbrain lesions, preserved thalamocortical function) are neural-integrity predictors. On the Map's evidence ladder CMD is compatible with the interface reading and does not discriminate it from production.

### Bodien et al. 2024 — the multinational CMD cohort
- **Citation**: Bodien YG, Allanson J, Cardone P, Bonhomme A, Carmona J, Chatelle C, et al. (39 authors; last author Schiff ND). Cognitive Motor Dissociation in Disorders of Consciousness. *N Engl J Med* 2024;391(7):598–608. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2400645. PMID 39141852.
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39141852/ ; author manuscript PMC7617195
- **Status**: abstract (PubMed) + full text of the author manuscript (PMC BioC). The published NEJM wording was not read; where the manuscript and the reviewer's quotation differ, the manuscript is quoted.
- **Key points**:
  - Prospective cohort at six centres; "convenience sample of 353 adults."
  - "We detected cognitive motor dissociation in 60 of the 241 participants (25%) without an observable response to commands, of whom 11 had been assessed with the use of fMRI only, 13 with the use of EEG only, and 36 with the use of both techniques."
  - **Denominator.** The 241 are coma/VS (140, labelled "Unconscious (coma/vegetative state)" in Table 1) plus MCS− (101). The CMD group was "more likely to have a CRS-R diagnosis of minimally conscious state minus (53% versus 38%)", i.e. 32 MCS− and 28 coma/VS. **Derived rates: coma/VS 28/140 = 20%; MCS− 32/101 = 32%.**
  - **False negatives.** "responses on task-based fMRI or EEG occurred in 43 of 112 participants (38%) with an observable response to verbal commands"; manuscript: "Responses to fMRI and EEG command-following tests were absent in more than 60% of participants who demonstrated evidence of command-following via behavioral responses on bedside assessment." The analytic methods were "intentionally designed to minimize the potential for a false positive result, which may increase the likelihood of a false negative finding."
  - **Reliability.** Kappa "0.02-0.04 for agreement between fMRI and EEG"; CMD frequency across sites "ranging from 2% to 45%"; "our results may not be representative of global cognitive motor dissociation prevalence."
  - **History.** "cognitive motor dissociation was observed in 10-20% of persons with a disorder of consciousness" in prior studies; "5-10 percent higher than previously reported." (The Claude reviewer quoted "5 to 10 percentage points", possibly the NEJM wording, which was not read here.)
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Defeats behavioural sufficiency for absence; non-discriminating between production and interface.

### Claassen et al. 2019 — acute ICU EEG
- **Citation**: Claassen J, Doyle K, Matory A, Couch C, Burger KM, Velazquez A, et al. Detection of Brain Activation in Unresponsive Patients with Acute Brain Injury. *N Engl J Med* 2019;380(26):2497–2505. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1812757. PMID 31242361.
- **Status**: abstract.
- **Key points**: Consecutive ICU series, machine-learning EEG on hand-movement commands. "A total of 16 of 104 unresponsive patients (15%) had brain activation detected by EEG at a median of 4 days after injury." The cohort was unresponsive to commands, "including some patients with the ability to localize painful stimuli or to fixate on or track visual stimuli" (i.e. some MCS− profiles). - **Follow-on**: Egbebike et al. 2022 (*Lancet Neurol* 21(8):704–713; PMID 35841909; abstract): "Cognitive-motor dissociation was seen in 27 (14%) patients" of 193 acute patients, and predicted faster recovery.

### Aubinet et al. 2025 — "Covert consciousness: what's in a name?"
- **Citation**: Aubinet C, Claassen J, Edlow BL, Fischer D, Gosseries O, Koch C, Kondziella D, Massimini M, Young MJ. Covert consciousness: what's in a name? *Brain* 2025;148(12):4248–4252. doi:10.1093/brain/awaf349. PMID 40985747; PMC12677022.
- **Status**: full-text. **Exists; verified.** A short position paper (workshop proposal), not a systematic review.
- **Key points**:
  - The 25% is an **umbrella** figure: "up to 25% of behaviourally unresponsive patients with acute or chronic disorders of consciousness reveal high spatio-temporal complexity following a direct electrical or magnetic pulse to the brain, highly differentiated EEG responses or voluntary modulation of their brain activity on command, each of which has been interpreted, to varying degrees, as evidence of consciousness." It pools PCI, passive EEG and command-following, so it is a different quantity from Bodien's CMD rate.
  - Proposed label scope: "it would not apply to patients with MCS−, MSC+ or locked-in-syndrome" (source typo "MSC+"). Covert consciousness in their sense is a subset of coma and VS/UWS. Bodien's 25% includes MCS−.
  - Test asymmetry: "someone who passes an fMRI or EEG motor imagery test is likely conscious"; "only 75%–85% of overtly conscious individuals have positive motor imagery tests"; "TMS-EEG has a 5% false negative rate, compared to the 62% false negative rate of task-based fMRI and/or EEG"; "In unresponsive patients (VS/UWS), PCI detects high levels of complexity in about 20% of patients, consistent with the incidence of covert command-following."
  - Epistemic stance: "Today, this question cannot be answered with certainty and thus requires diagnostic humility."
  - **Competing interest** (declared): Koch holds an executive position in, and Koch and Massimini have a financial interest in, a company developing a consciousness-assessment device. Relevant to how the umbrella figure is framed.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral; congenial to Tenet 5 in its narrow epistemic form (behaviour is an unreliable index).

## Major Positions

### The covert-awareness (clinical-realist) reading
- **Proponents**: Owen; Monti; Bodien, Edlow, Claassen, Schiff; Aubinet et al.
- **Core claim**: A positive, specific, sustained response to a command on fMRI or EEG is strong evidence that the patient is conscious ("likely conscious", Aubinet et al.), whatever the bedside behaviour.
- **Key arguments**: The response requires comprehension, selection and maintenance over tens of seconds; the activation map matches healthy volunteers; CMD predicts recovery (Claassen 2019; Egbebike 2022); some responders have used the paradigm to answer questions (Monti 2010).
- **Relation to site tenets**: Congenial to the Map's anti-behaviourist stance. It does not reach dualism: the evidence *is* neural activity, and the inference runs through a neural-functional marker.

### The deflationary or sceptical reading
- **Proponents**: Nachev & Husain 2007; Goldfine et al. 2013 (methodological); partly Naccache 2018; Klein 2017 on agency.
- **Core claim**: Task-locked activation does not by itself establish consciousness (Nachev & Husain: possibly "wholly automatic and unconscious") or intentional agency (Klein). Positive EEG classifications can be statistical artefact (Goldfine). Behavioural MCS criteria establish only a "cortically mediated state" (Naccache).
- **Relation to site tenets**: Cuts against every Map page that says CMD patients "harbour awareness" or retain "full conscious function". The Map should present CMD as *evidence of probable consciousness*, which is how the clinicians themselves present it.

### The production (physicalist) reading, at full strength
- **Proponents**: the mainstream of the field by default; stated as an argument by ChatGPT 5.6 Sol Pro §4.4 and Claude Opus 5.5 §2.2 of the 2026-10-01 outer reviews.
- **Core claim**: CMD is consciousness produced by preserved cortical and thalamocortical machinery whose output pathway to the motor system is damaged. No interface is needed; the dissociation is a lesion-level fact.
- **Key arguments** (all from sources read):
  1. *Detection is neural.* CMD is defined as preserved task-related neural responses without motor output. Every positive case is a case in which specific cortical machinery is intact and active. Nothing in the data shows consciousness persisting where its neural basis has failed.
  2. *The predictors are neural-integrity predictors.* Traumatic over anoxic aetiology (Laigaard 2026; Bodien 2024), MCS− over VS (Laigaard: 38.7% vs 28.4%), younger age (Bodien), no midbrain lesions and preserved thalamocortical function (Franzova 2023).
  3. *The dissociation has its own mechanism.* Disrupted thalamus–M1 fibres (Fernández-Espejo 2015); failed motor-command integration in the anterior forebrain mesocircuit (Franzova 2023).
  4. *Independent capacity measures converge.* PCI identifies high complexity in ~20% of VS (Casarotto 2016: 9/43), "consistent with the incidence of covert command-following" (Aubinet 2025); a metabolic threshold near 42% of normal "accounted for the current level, or imminent return, of awareness in 94%" (Stender 2016). Production predicts exactly this triangulation: one underlying variable (preserved thalamocortical integrity) seen through three instruments.
  5. *The production argument never rested on behaviour.* The physicalist does not identify consciousness with motor output. The anaesthesia, deep-sleep and brain-death data are read on neural measures (PCI, metabolism), so a contaminated behavioural category leaves that evidence base untouched, and CMD sharpens it.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Conflicts with Dualism, but the conflict cannot be settled by CMD data. The host's own L97 grants the disconnection move ("the interface account grants it") and says the two readings "converge on the same anatomy and differ only on what the anatomy does." That concession is correct and should govern the whole section, L95 included.

### The interface / filter reading (the Map's)
- **Core claim**: The brain is the channel; CMD is damage to the output leg of the channel with the experiential side intact.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Compatible with Dualism and Bidirectional Interaction. On the evidence ladder it stays at **compatible / non-discriminating**, as [[consciousness-and-causal-powers]] L180 and [[phenomenology-of-consciousness-doing-work]] L141 already grade it ("interface-compatible and non-discriminating"). The host, [[qbism]], [[substrate-independence]] and [[identity-across-transformations]] grade it higher than those pages do. That is a cross-page inconsistency the new page would settle.

## Key Debates

### What does a positive test show?
- **Sides**: Owen/Monti/Bodien/Aubinet (probable consciousness) vs Nachev & Husain (activation underdetermines conscious intention) vs Klein (conscious, but not intentional agency).
- **Core disagreement**: Whether the step from task-specific activation to consciousness needs an independent validation that does not exist. Casarotto 2016 is the field's best attempt: PCI calibrated on people who could report.
- **Current state**: Clinical consensus treats positive command-following as strong evidence ("likely conscious"); philosophers keep the inference marked as an inference. Aubinet et al.'s "diagnostic humility" concedes the residual.

### What does a negative test show?
- **Sides**: All parties agree that it shows little. Bodien: >60% false negatives among behavioural command-followers. Aubinet: only 75–85% of overtly conscious people pass. Laigaard: "Absence of evidence for CMD is not evidence of its absence."
- **Current state**: Settled. This is the Map's strongest *legitimate* use of the literature: true covert-consciousness prevalence is unknown and bounded below, not above, by the test figures. It cuts both ways, though: the same unknown prevents the Map from asserting a prevalence.

### Prevalence: 15% vs 20% vs 25% vs 28–39%
- **Sides**: Kondziella 2016 (VS active paradigms 14%, "roughly 15%"); Claassen 2019 (acute EEG 15%); Egbebike 2022 (acute EEG 14%); Kazazian 2025 synthesis (~20% of non-command-followers); Bodien 2024 (25% of coma/VS/MCS−; 20% of coma/VS derived); Aubinet 2025 umbrella (up to 25%, includes PCI and passive EEG); Laigaard 2026 pooled published IPD (UWS 28.4%, MCS− 38.7%, coma 32.8%).
- **Core disagreement**: Mostly denominator, modality count, site and publication selection, not a substantive dispute. Bodien's multimodal assessment raises yield; IPD pools of published single-subject data skew upward.
- **Current state**: The defensible one-line statement is: *"Roughly 15–25% of patients without bedside command-following show command-following on fMRI or EEG, depending on cohort and method (Bodien et al. 2024: 60 of 241, 25%; 20% among those clinically in coma or the vegetative state); because the tests miss many conscious patients, the true figure is unknown."*

### The ≈40% misdiagnosis rate is not a covert-consciousness figure
- **Core point**: Schnakers 2009 (41%), Andrews 1996 (43%) and Childs 1993 (37%) measure behavioural misclassification against a behavioural reference (CRS-R, extended observation, eye-pointing). Wannez 2017 shows that a single CRS-R still errs 36% of the time against six. The patients concerned are not behaviourally unresponsive under good assessment.
- **Current state**: Settled in the clinical literature; conflated in the Map (host L131, L183; [[degrees-of-consciousness]] L82).

## Assessment of the Host's Falsifiers (L161–167)

The host lists five falsifiers; three bear on CMD.

- **F1 (L161) "Complete neural silence correlated with preserved consciousness."** *Unreachable* for CMD: every CMD detection method reads neural activity, so a covert-consciousness finding is by construction not a neural-silence finding. Edlow 2026 (behavioural coma) shows covert consciousness without *wakefulness*, not without neural activity. The host notes that the Map does not predict this either, so it would falsify both readings and discriminate neither. Recommendation: keep only as a statement of what the Map does *not* claim, or drop.
- **F2 (L163) "Precise Φ calculations reliably predicted CMD presence."** Φ is not computable for brains, so the falsifier as written is unreachable. Its reachable proxy, PCI, is already in play: high PCI in ~20% of VS (Casarotto 2016), "consistent with the incidence of covert command-following" (Aubinet 2025). Overlap studies are ongoing per Aubinet. If PCI predicts CMD tightly, the interface reading can say PCI measures interface integrity, so the result would not discriminate. It would only embarrass a Map claim that CMD escapes integration measures, a claim [[integrated-information-theory]] L111 comes close to making. Recommendation: restate as "PCI–CMD concordance", report that the current data point toward concordance, and grade it non-discriminating.
- **F4 (L167) "All CMD cases shared a specific preserved neural circuit."** *Partly reached already, in the production-friendly direction.* Franzova 2023: no CMD patient had midbrain lesions, thalamocortical function was preserved, and the failure lay at motor-command integration. Fernández-Espejo 2015: a thalamus–M1 lesion explains absent overt movement. Laigaard 2026 reads the aetiology gradient as preserved large-scale cortical connectivity. The host's own L97 concedes the interface reading predicts the same anatomy, so a positive result here does not falsify the interface either. Recommendation: report the existing data, then state the non-discrimination.

**What would discriminate?** Only a design with a consciousness criterion that is *independent of the neural capacity measures*: verified, content-specific recall after recovery of events that occurred while capacity measures (PCI, FDG-PET metabolism, multimodal CMD) were all far below threshold. Production predicts no such residual; a filter reading that lets experience outrun its neural support predicts some. Two caveats keep this honest. (i) The tests' high false-negative rate means recall after a *negative CMD task* is expected on both views; only recall below *capacity* thresholds (PCI, metabolism) carries weight. (ii) The Map's own minimal-interaction commitments forecast no detectable residual, so the design can embarrass production but cannot confirm the interface. The new page should say plainly that CMD prevalence data, at any value, are evidentially neutral between the two readings.

## What the Figures License

**Licensed:**
1. Bedside behaviour, even standardized and repeated, misses volitional cognitive capacity in a minority of patients without command-following: roughly 15–25% depending on cohort, method and site, and 20% of the clinically unconscious in the best cohort.
2. A negative test shows almost nothing (>60% false negatives among behavioural command-followers; 75–85% pass rates in conscious people). The true prevalence of covert consciousness is unknown and plausibly higher than any test figure.
3. Positive CMD is strong *evidence of probable* consciousness, by the field's own standards ("likely conscious"; "diagnostic humility").
4. The ≈40% figure licenses a separate claim: unstandardized clinical judgement and single examinations misclassify roughly two in five consensus-VS patients who show MCS behaviours on proper assessment.

**Not licensed:**
- "25% of unconscious patients retain awareness" (denominator; inference stated as fact).
- "The consciousness was there all along" (no temporal data).
- "Consciousness persists when its neural basis fails" (detected because the neural basis is active).
- "Full cognitive function" / "entirely severed interface" / "memory formation".
- "Undermines the materialist argument" or "contaminates the production argument's evidence base" (that evidence base is neural, and CMD sharpens it).
- Any discrimination between production and interface readings.

**Map-congenial point that survives intact**: Tenet 5 in its narrow epistemic form. The simplest bedside inference from absent behaviour to absent experience is unreliable, and the reliability gap is measured, not speculative. That differs from "parsimony fails against production", which the data do not show.

- **Philosophical literature** (Shea & Bayne 2010; Klein 2017; Naccache 2018; Bayne, Hohwy & Owen 2017): abstracts only. Klein's akinetic-mutism argument and Shea & Bayne's natural-kind method need full-text reading before the article characterises them beyond the abstract sentences quoted.
- **Recovered-patient testimony** (retrospective reports of the covert period) was not researched. It is the only route to phenomenal rather than functional evidence and is the input the discriminating design needs.
