## Executive Summary

**New dispositionalism** (the name is Randolph Clarke's, 2009) is the compatibilist thesis that an agent's ability to do otherwise is a *disposition* — Fara's canonical formulation: "An agent has the ability to A in circumstances C if and only if she has the disposition to A when, in circumstances C, she tries to A" (Fara 2008, p. 848). Its founders are Michael Smith (2003), Kadri Vihvelin (2004, 2013) and Michael Fara (2008). The view revives the classical conditional analysis (Moore, Ayer) while inheriting the post-1994 metaphysics of dispositions — finks (Martin 1994; Lewis 1997) and masks/antidotes (Johnston 1992; Bird 1998) — so that the counterexamples which sank the conditional analysis (the comatose man who *would* walk if he chose, but cannot choose) are handled as finked or masked dispositions rather than as refutations. Its two payoffs are, first, that unmanifested dispositions are uncontroversially compatible with determinism, so an unexercised ability to do otherwise is too; and second, that Frankfurt cases fail, because the counterfactual intervener merely *masks* the agent's ability without removing it — a compatibilist *defence* of the Principle of Alternate Possibilities.

The standard replies are Clarke's (2009) *subject-changing* objection — a disposition to A when one tries leaves untouched whether one's *trying* is up to one, so the view "leaves unresolved the main points of dispute concerning free will" — Whittle's (2010) *local/global* distinction (dispositionalism vindicates only global, circumstance-general abilities; the free-will-relevant ability is local, and determinism does remove it), and Franklin's (2011) *ability/opportunity* split (finks and masks show an agent keeps the ability but loses the *opportunity*, and freedom needs both). The second wave (Kittle 2015; Vetter 2019; Vetter & Jaster 2017; Jaster 2020, 2021; Cyr & Swenson 2019; Hofmann 2022; Kittle 2024; Turyn 2025) has kept the view live: Jaster (2021) argues that understanding abilities "gives us no reason to think that the ability to do otherwise relevant for free will is compatible with either Black's presence or determinism", while Hofmann (2022) builds a *leeway* account of free decision on new-dispositionalist rational abilities, and Turyn (2025) uses masking to answer Pereboom's four-case argument.

**For the Map** the view attacks the premise, stated in `concepts/compatibilism` and `concepts/quantum-indeterminacy-free-will`, that leeway must be *categorical*. The research shows that word to be doing two jobs at once. New dispositionalism grants that the ability to do otherwise is *intrinsic* to the agent (Vihvelin's narrow ability supervenes on intrinsic properties) and denies that it must be *modal* in van Inwagen's sense (same past, same laws, a different act). The Map's Tenet 3 supplies the modal kind. So the argument never denies that the Map has what it claims; it denies that anyone *needs* it — and by Clarke's own verdict that question is not one a theory of ability can settle. The Map's cleanest reply is therefore Clarke's: locate leeway at the *choosing* (the trying), where the dispositional analysis is silent, and where the quantum interface does its work.

### Vihvelin, *Causes, Laws, and Free Will: Why Determinism Doesn't Matter* (2013)
- **URL**: https://global.oup.com/academic/product/causes-laws-and-free-will-9780199795185 ; NDPR review https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/causes-laws-and-free-will-why-determinism-doesn-t-matter/
- **Type**: Monograph. Oxford University Press, 2013, 284 pp., ISBN 9780199795185. **Chapters (Crossref, OUP prefix `10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199795185`)**: 1 "The Problem Introduced" (1-22); 2 "The Problem Distinguished" (23-56); 3 "Abilities, Choices, and Agent Causation" (57-88); 4 "The Unavoidability of Metaphysics" (89-124); 5 "Arguments for Incompatibilism" (125-166); 6 "The Abilities and Dispositions of Our Freedom" (167-214); 7 "Laws, Counterfactuals, and Fixed Past Compatibilism" (215-238).
- **Key points** (from Franklin's NDPR review, 19 October 2013, and McKitrick's *Ethics* review as summarised at PhilPapers):
  - Four theses: free will is possible; Frankfurt-style cases fail to undermine the traditional compatibility debate; there are no good arguments for incompatibilism; we have free will in virtue of possessing a bundle of dispositions *and* being in environments with no obstacle to their manifestation. She names the position "commonsense metaphysical compatibilism".
  - **Narrow vs wide ability** (p. 11, quoted in NDPR): narrow — "S has *what it takes* to do X: she's got the necessary skills and the psychological and physical capacity"; wide — "S has what it takes to X . . . and, *moreover*, she's got the means and opportunity and nothing external stands in her way". Narrow abilities supervene on intrinsic properties (intrinsic duplicates under the same laws share them); wide abilities add extrinsic conditions. "To have a narrow ability to act is to have a disposition or bundle of dispositions" (p. 171, quoted in NDPR).
  - **LCA-PROP**: her analysis of dispositions is a hybrid of Lewis's (1997) reformed conditional analysis and Manley & Wasserman's (2008) proportionality account — a disposition is retained when its intrinsic causal basis is retained and would produce the manifestation in a *sufficient proportion* of test cases. This is the fink/mask-proofing: a masked or finked disposition still has its basis and still manifests in enough of the cases. *Verbatim wording of LCA-PROP-ABILITY was not retrievable from an open source this run; see Gaps.*
  - **Frankfurt cases** (per McKitrick/PhilPapers summary and NDPR): split into **Bodyguard** cases (intervener triggered by the agent's beginning to choose or act otherwise — so the agent retains some ability to do otherwise, and the case cannot refute PAP) and **Preemptor** cases (intervener triggered by an earlier non-actional sign — the agent keeps *both* narrow and wide ability, because a counterfactual intervener leaves intrinsic properties untouched and is no "obstacle", an obstacle being something that would frustrate an *attempt*).
  - **Consequence Argument**: treated in ch. 5 and ch. 7; she takes van Inwagen's Beta version and presses Lewis's equivocation charge (weak vs strong law-breaking ability). Franklin faults her for ignoring the McKay-Johnson counterexample literature and stronger versions.
- **Tenet alignment**: **Conflicts** with the Map's reading of Tenet 3 at the level of *what leeway requires*; **neutral** on whether indeterminism obtains (she argues determinism "doesn't matter", not that it is true).
- **Reception**: Elzein, *Mind* 124(495): 994-998 (2015), DOI 10.1093/mind/fzv041 — "a very compelling articulation and defence" of the bundle-of-dispositions view (page excerpt); McKitrick, *Ethics* 125(4): 1230-1236 (2015), DOI 10.1086/680902; Franklin, NDPR 2013; Sartorio, *Criminal Law and Philosophy* 10(4): 875-888 (2016, online 2014), DOI 10.1007/s11572-014-9355-9, defending the actual-sequence view against her Frankfurt treatment.

### Fara, "Masked Abilities and Compatibilism" (2008) — READ IN FULL
- **URL**: https://academic.oup.com/mind/article/117/468/843/1109792 ; open offprint https://andrewmbailey.com/papers/Fara%20Mind%202008.pdf
- **Type**: Journal article. *Mind* 117(468): 843-865, October 2008. DOI 10.1093/mind/fzn078. Crossref-verified.
- **Key points** (all quotations grep-verified in the NFKC-normalised offprint):
  - Masking defined (p. 848): "An agent's ability to A in circumstances C is masked iff (i) The agent tries to A; (ii) circumstances C obtain; (iii) the agent retains the ability while trying to A; yet (iv) the agent does not succeed in Aing."
  - **The Dispositional Analysis** (p. 848): "An agent has the ability to A in circumstances C if and only if she has the disposition to A when, in circumstances C, she tries to A." Footnote 8 requires wide-scope readings.
  - Finks distinguished (p. 851-852 region): an ability removed *before* the attempt completes (general anaesthesia before counting to ten) is not masked — "It is essential to an ability's being masked that its possession survive an attempt to exercise it."
  - Frankfurt (p. 854): "The crucial feature of this Frankfurt-style case … is that if Jones were to try to act otherwise he would fail. All by itself, however, this conditional fact does not establish that Jones lacks the ability to act otherwise." The evil scientist is a *masker*: "Compare the case of a fragile glass, disposed to break when struck, which is carefully protected by packing material. The packing material does not remove the glass's disposition … Similarly, the evil scientist does not remove Jones's disposition to act otherwise when he tries." (The packing-material glass is Johnston's example, Fara n. 16 — this is the glass `concepts/compatibilism` L59 alludes to.)
  - Consequence ("master") argument (pp. 862-863): the transfer principle has a false instance, because an agent can be disposed to act otherwise even where "no one has the ability to prevent the masking conditions from obtaining"; "The dispositional analysis of abilities has rescued compatibilism." He then anticipates the reply that his sense of "ability" "is not the sense that is p[ertinent]" — the subject-changing charge Clarke makes explicit a year later.
  - Footnote 17 records the difference from Vihvelin (2004): she construes abilities as "bundles of dispositions".
- **Tenet alignment**: conflicts with Tenet 3's *modal* leeway premise; the fragile-glass move is precisely the "no indeterministic gap needed" payoff.

### Clarke, "Dispositions, Abilities to Act, and Free Will: The New Dispositionalism" (2009)
- **URL**: https://academic.oup.com/mind/article-abstract/118/470/323/951120
- **Type**: Journal article. *Mind* 118(470): 323-351, April 2009. DOI 10.1093/mind/fzp034. Crossref-verified. **Coins the name.**
- **Abstract (verbatim, publisher page)**: "This paper examines recent attempts to revive a classic compatibilist position on free will, according to which having an ability to perform a certain action is having a certain disposition. Since having unmanifested dispositions is compatible with determinism, having unexercised abilities to act, it is held, is likewise compatible. Here it is argued that although there is a kind of capacity to act possession of which is a matter of having a disposition, the new dispositionalism leaves unresolved the main points of dispute concerning free will."
- **Key argument** (as quoted in SEP "Abilities" §3.4, Clarke 2009 p. 339): if whether "S tries to A" "is not up to me … then even if I have a capacity to A, it is not up to me whether I exercise that capacity." SEP's gloss: "while the new dispositionalist has perhaps offered a theory of something, it is not a theory of ability." Clarke revisits the dispute in "Free Will and Abilities to Act", in *Streit um die Freiheit* (Mentis/Brill, 2019), pp. 41-62, DOI 10.30965/9783657792160_004 (Crossref-verified; not read).
- **Tenet alignment**: **the Map's ally**. Clarke is himself no libertarian partisan here, which makes the objection more useful: it says the dispositional analysis is orthogonal to the compatibility question rather than false.

### Whittle, "Dispositional Abilities" (2010) — READ IN FULL
- **URL**: https://philpapers.org/rec/WHIDA ; PDF https://www.generativescience.org/papers/nature/Whittle-_2010.pdf
- **Type**: Journal article. *Philosophers' Imprint* 10(12), September 2010 (the harvest task's "no. 4" is wrong; the masthead reads "volume 10, no. 12 september 2010").
- **Key points** (verbatim from PDF): names Fara (2008), Smith (1997, 2003) and Vihvelin (2004) jointly as "dispositional compatibilists" who "have revived the traditional compatibilist's project". ### Franklin, "Masks, Abilities, and Opportunities: Why the New Dispositionalism Cannot Succeed" (2011)
- **URL**: https://pdcnet.org/schoolman/content/schoolman_2011_0088_40910_0089_0103 ; https://philpapers.org/rec/FRAMAA-4
- **Type**: Journal article. *The Modern Schoolman* 88(1/2): 89-103, 2011.
- **Key points** (abstract): grants the dispositional analyses of Vihvelin (2004) and Fara (2008) for the sake of argument and denies they reach either conclusion (Frankfurt failure; compatibilism), because "agents' freedom and responsibility depend not only on their abilities" but on their *opportunities*; a mask removes opportunity while leaving ability, so a masked agent is not free to do otherwise. ## Major Positions

### New dispositionalism (single-disposition form)
- **Proponents**: Fara 2008.
- **Core claim**: ability to A in C = disposition to A when, in C, one tries to A. Frankfurt interveners mask; determinism leaves unmanifested dispositions in place; the transfer principle of the Consequence Argument has a false instance.
- **Relation to site tenets**: grants the agent an intrinsic, non-conditional ability; denies that the ability must be modal (same past, same laws). Conflicts with the Map's Tenet 3 *at the level of need*, not of possession.

### New dispositionalism (bundle form, "commonsense metaphysical compatibilism")
- **Proponents**: Vihvelin 2004, 2013; Smith 2003 (rational capacities).
- **Core claim**: free will = a bundle of narrow abilities (dispositions to choose and act on reasons, grounded in intrinsic causal bases, analysed by LCA-PROP) plus wide ability (no obstacle). Determinism changes neither; Frankfurt interveners are not obstacles.
- **Relation to site tenets**: as above; additionally a Lewisian counterfactual apparatus (ch. 7 "Fixed Past Compatibilism") that the Map's Consequence-Argument article already engages under Lewis's weak/strong thesis. No Tenet 4 issue: possible-worlds semantics for counterfactuals carries no commitment to Everettian branches.

### The subject-changing reply
- **Proponents**: Clarke 2009; Jaster 2021; anticipated by Fara himself (p. 863).
- **Core claim**: a disposition to A *if one tries* says nothing about whether the trying is up to the agent; the free-will question relocates to the choosing and is untouched.
- **Relation to site tenets**: **this is the Map's position stated by outsiders.** The Map's leeway claim concerns the selection event itself (`concepts/quantum-indeterminacy-free-will`; `concepts/agent-causation`), which is exactly where Clarke says the analysis goes silent.

### The local/global and ability/opportunity replies
- **Proponents**: Whittle 2010 (local/global); Franklin 2011 (ability/opportunity); Cyr & Swenson 2019 (opportunity-only requirement).
- **Core claim**: dispositionalism secures general abilities; responsibility and freedom track local ability-in-these-circumstances or opportunity, and determinism (or Black) removes those.
- **Relation to site tenets**: allies. Note the internal tension the Map should not paper over: Cyr & Swenson use these distinctions to argue for *no* general-ability requirement, which cuts against PAP as much as against the dispositionalist.

### Disposition-metaphysics scepticism about the analysis
- **Proponents**: Vetter 2019; Vetter & Jaster 2017; Jaster 2020.
- **Core claim**: abilities are not dispositions in the conditional sense at all (potentialities; success-linked; principled disanalogies), so the analysis fails before the compatibility question arises.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral; a second, independent line of resistance the Map can cite without endorsing.

## Key Debates

### Does the analysis change the subject?
- **Sides**: Fara/Vihvelin (the incompatibilist's modal "can" is a philosophers' artefact born of "mistakes about the metaphysics of causation, mistakes about the nature of laws, and mistakes about the logic of counterfactuals" — OUP blurb) versus Clarke/Whittle/Jaster (the analysis is fine for one kind of capacity and silent on the one at issue).
- **Core disagreement**: which sense of "can" the ordinary practice of praise and blame invokes.
- **Current state**: unresolved; the 2021-2025 literature still divides on it. **Calibration for the Map**: the Map's evidence that the modal sense is the practice's sense is the phenomenology of deliberation plus tenet-coherence — precisely the discipline P-A5 already records. Do not let an article claim the subject-changing charge is *won*.

### Do Frankfurt cases fail?
- **Sides**: Vihvelin, Fara, Smith (interveners mask; PAP survives) versus Fischer 2008, Haji & McKenna 2004, Sartorio 2016, Janzen 2016 (pre-decisional/Preemptor and brain-malfunction variants defeat the masking reply; the actual-sequence view stands).
- **Current state**: live. **Structural point for the Map**: `topics/frankfurt-cases-and-the-principle-of-alternate-possibilities` books the Map's answer as a *retreat to wide source*. The new dispositionalists are an unbooked *ally* on the leeway wing — they say the cases fail — and the Map does not have to accept their compatibilism to use their masking diagnosis, since a quantum-selected choice is, on the Map's own account, an intrinsic capacity whose exercise Black could mask but not remove.

### Ability versus opportunity
- **Sides**: Franklin, Cyr & Swenson, Kittle versus Vihvelin's "obstacle" definition.
- **Current state**: the definition of obstacle is where Franklin predicts incompatibilists will attack, and where Sartorio and Janzen do.

## Relation to the Map's Tenets — the analysis an article or insert should carry

1. **Split "categorical".** The corpus uses one word for two claims: (a) the ability is *intrinsic and unconditional* (not a "would-if" fact about the agent), and (b) the ability is *modal* in van Inwagen's sense — holding the past and laws fixed, the agent can act otherwise. New dispositionalism *grants* (a): Vihvelin's narrow ability supervenes on intrinsic properties and is analysed by a fink/mask-proofed dispositional theory that is emphatically not a bare conditional. It *denies* that (b) is needed. Tenet 3 supplies (b) through the quantum interface. So the correct statement of the disagreement is: the Map holds that (b) is what deliberation reports and responsibility needs; new dispositionalism holds that (a) suffices. That is a sharper and more defensible framing than "the dispositionalist reinterprets the ability away", which is false of Vihvelin.
2. **The reply is Clarke's, and it is the Map's own structure.** Fara's analysis is conditional on trying; Vihvelin's bundle is a bundle of dispositions to choose *on reasons*. Both leave open whether the trying or choosing is up to the agent. The Map's leeway lives at exactly that point — the selection among quantum-open alternatives — so the analysis does not reach the Map's claim. This should be said without triumph: Clarke's conclusion is that the analysis "leaves unresolved" the dispute, not that the incompatibilist wins it.
3. **An unbooked ally on Frankfurt.** The Map's leeway wing and the new dispositionalists agree that Frankfurt cases fail. The masking diagnosis is available to the Map on its own terms (the quantum-selected choice is an intrinsic capacity Black could mask but not remove), and it strengthens the Map's *first* line before the wide-source retreat the Frankfurt article books as its fallback. The cost: the Map then inherits the Fischer/Sartorio/Janzen counter-cases (pre-decisional signs; internal malfunction), and the Janzen form — where the "intervener" is a change in the agent's intrinsic properties — is the one a quantum-interface account must answer, since on that account the intrinsic basis of the choosing is partly non-physical.
4. **Tenet 5 cuts both ways.** Vihvelin's "determinism doesn't matter" is partly a deflation of the modal sense on grounds of metaphysical economy; Tenet 5 licenses the Map to resist that. But the same tenet forbids the Map from treating the modal reading as *obvious*: it is the contested reading, and P-A5 already says the Map's warrant for it is coherence with tenets adopted on other grounds.
5. **Positions register.** P-A5's shift trigger — the "genuine alternatives" residue shown "compatibilist-recoverable" — has new dispositionalism as its live candidate, and Clarke 2009 as the reason it has not fired. A one-line `Updated` note in `positions/agency-and-will` naming both would make the register's dependency explicit. "### The Dispositionalist Defence

A third reply denies that Black removes anything. Kadri Vihvelin (2013) and Michael Fara (*Mind*, 2008) analyse the ability to do otherwise as a disposition; a counterfactual intervener is then a *mask*, like packing material around a fragile glass — it guarantees the disposition will not manifest without removing it. Vihvelin sorts the cases: a *Bodyguard* intervener, triggered by the agent's beginning to choose otherwise, leaves the agent some ability to do otherwise by construction; a *Preemptor*, triggered by an earlier involuntary sign, leaves the agent's intrinsic properties, and so her abilities, untouched. On this view PAP survives and the cases fail. The reply is contested — John Martin Fischer (*Canadian Journal of Philosophy*, 2008) and Carolina Sartorio (2016) defend the actual-sequence reading, and Greg Janzen (*Australasian Journal of Philosophy*, 2016) presses cases where the 'intervention' is an internal malfunction that does alter the agent's intrinsic makeup. It matters here because the defence is available to a leeway view that is not compatibilist: the Map can accept the masking diagnosis while rejecting the dispositionalists' claim that the retained ability needs no open future."

Add to References: Vihvelin 2013; Fara 2008; Fischer 2008 (CJP 38(3): 327–342); Sartorio 2016 (*Criminal Law and Philosophy* 10(4): 875–888); Janzen 2016 (AJP 94(4): 646–657).
