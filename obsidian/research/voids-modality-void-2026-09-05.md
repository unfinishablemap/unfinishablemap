# Research: Voids - The Modality Void

**Date**: 2026-09-05
**Search queries used**: individuating the senses Grice 1962 criteria Nudds 2004 Macpherson; "oral referral" retronasal olfaction mislocalization Spence 2016 Rozin 1982; Hurley Noë 2003 "Neural plasticity and consciousness" cortical dominance deference rewired ferrets; Keeley 2002 "Making sense of the senses" how many senses; thermal grill illusion Craig Bushnell 1994 modality; Nudds "The Significance of the Senses" first-personal significance; Liang 2022 "Mind the Gap" modality gap contrastive
**Voids category**: Mixed — the *counting* face is a candidate Unexplorable (a first-person indeterminacy that third-person science can partly resolve), the *attribution* face is naturally occluded by perceptual architecture, and the *boundary* face is largely Unexplored

## Executive Summary

Consciousness cannot read its own modality structure. It cannot determine from inside how many senses it has, where one sense ends and the next begins, or which sense is delivering a given content — and the classification it does receive arrives as *evident*, whether or not it is correct. Three findings anchor the claim. First, the philosophical criteria for individuating the senses (Grice's four — representation, phenomenal character, proximal stimulus, sense organ — and their successors) diverge, the phenomenal-character criterion collapses into the representational one, and the leading treatments conclude either that the count is an empirical matter beyond introspection (Macpherson 2011) or that it is conventional with no first-personal significance at all (Nudds 2004). Second, the mind systematically mis-tags the channel of its own contents: retronasal smell is experienced as taste located in the mouth (Spence 2016 calls oral referral "the only example of such modality confusion that has been reported in the literature to date", though he argues it is universal); two innocuous thermal inputs are delivered as burning pain (Craig & Bushnell 1994); and a McGurk syllable is experienced "as a case of hearing" with the same evidence as veridical hearing (Fréchette 2023). Third, where input is rerouted or substituted, whether experience follows the cortex or the source — Hurley & Noë's cortical dominance versus deference — is settled by nothing available to the subject; rewired ferrets behave as if seeing through auditory cortex (von Melchner, Pallas & Sur 2000), and Hurley & Noë explicitly say the comparative gap cannot be closed "by inward scrutiny". The three faces conjoin in one structural claim: the modality of an experience is delivered as its *format*, never as a separate, checkable datum. The void is first-personal — psychophysics and neuroanatomy can recover the tag the subject cannot — which places it in the same family as the [[fusion-void|fusion void]] (hidden weights) and the [[source-attribution-void|source-attribution void]] (missing origin tags): here what is missing is the *channel* tag. It matters to the Map because the whole sensory-modality wing, including [[apex/cross-modal-capability-division|the cross-modal capability division]], runs the capability division "across five senses one modality at a time", presupposing the very carving this void says consciousness cannot verify.

## Key Sources

### Macpherson, F. (2011). "Taxonomising the senses"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-010-9643-8 (open-access author version: http://eprints.gla.ac.uk/43551/1/43351.pdf)
- **Type**: Paper — *Philosophical Studies* 153(1): 123–142
- **Key points**:
  - Frames the question in exactly the void's terms: "But what makes the senses different? How many senses are there? How many could there be?"
  - Treats the four traditional criteria (representation, phenomenal character, proximal stimulus, sense organ) not as rivals but as axes: "We can think of these criteria as defining a multidimensional space within which we can locate each of the senses that we are familiar with and which also defines the space of possible senses there could be." And: "I hold that the four criteria are relatively independent dimensions along which different possible kinds of senses could take different values."
  - The phenomenal criterion is parasitic on representation: "It seems that when we specify the phenomenal character of an experience, we almost always say what it was an experience as of—that is, what the experience represented, whether or not that representation was accurate." Hence "the phenomenal character criterion could turn out to look very much like the representational criterion."
  - Against Nudds's five: "I believe that the folk concept is simply silent with regard to the question of how many senses there are." And the count is not first-personally available: "How many actually exist is a question that only progress in science, together with philosophical investigation into the conditions required for the existence of sensory modalities, will be able to shed light on."
  - On sensory substitution: "Are people who use TVSS devices seeing, feeling, or perceiving in some different way? That is, are we replacing their vision, or are we extending their existing sense of touch to allow the detection of objects and properties it usually cannot detect, or are we creating a brand new sense?" — the criteria give different answers.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — the five-sense count is the simplest carving and she documents that "the number of senses that they recognize has frequently been altered" by scientists; simplicity here does not track structure. Dualism — the multidimensional-space proposal is neutral on ontology but shows that the *phenomenal* axis alone cannot partition the senses, which is the void's counting face.
- **Quote**: "I believe that the folk concept is simply silent with regard to the question of how many senses there are."

### Nudds, M. (2004). "The Significance of the Senses"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0066-7373.2004.00080.x
- **Type**: Paper — *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 104(1): 31–51
- **Key points** (via Macpherson 2011 and Fréchette 2023 — full text not retrievable, see Gaps):

### Keeley, B. L. (2002). "Making Sense of the Senses: Individuating Modalities in Humans and Other Animals"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.2307/3655759
- **Type**: Paper — *Journal of Philosophy* 99(1): 5–28
- **Key points** (via Fréchette 2023 and O'Callaghan 2012 — full text not retrievable):
  - Proposes a scientific criterion: senses are individuated by dedicated organs and physical energies (O'Callaghan: "Keeley (2002) suggests we individuate the senses in terms of organs evolutionarily dedicated to picking up information of a certain kind"). Secondary summaries report four jointly sufficient criteria — physics, neurobiology, behaviour, dedication — not verified against the paper.
  - Dismisses phenomenology-first answers: descriptive answers are "simply nonstarters for a scientific understanding" (p. 6, as quoted by Fréchette).
- **Tenet alignment**: Keeley makes the void's shape explicit from the other side — if the count is a neurobiological matter, then first-person access to it is not merely unreliable but irrelevant. That is the mirror of Nudds: one says there is nothing to see, the other that seeing is the wrong instrument.

### Fréchette, G. (2023). "Why does it matter to individuate the senses: A Brentanian approach"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/ejop.12735 (PMC10946872)
- **Type**: Paper — *European Journal of Philosophy*
- **Key points**:
  - Argues, against Nudds, that modality classification is part of experience itself: "I do not merely experience the blue vase on my table. I have an experience of the blue vase, which appears to me, evidently, as a case of seeing."
  - His own McGurk example is the void's cleanest exhibit: "This illusion works, I suggest, because we classify, on an elementary basis, our experience of the syllables heard as 'da da' as a case of hearing and not as a case of seeing or a case of visual speech perception interfering with auditory perception."
  - And the decisive sentence: "In the McGurk effect, my experiencing 'da da' as a case of hearing appears to me as evident as my experiencing 'ba ba' as a case of hearing does in a normal situation."
  - Surveys the field: Macpherson's revisable folk view, Keeley's eliminativism about descriptive answers, Casati/Dokic/Le Corre's hybrid criteria — "there is the impression of a gap" between descriptive and causal answers.
- **Tenet alignment**: this is the source that turns a debate about *taxonomy* into a void about *access*. Fréchette wants inner perception to be evident; his example shows the evidence is identical for a correct and an incorrect modality tag. That is the signature of a genuine void — the error signal is absent — and it is the same shape as the [[noetic-feelings-void|noetic feelings void]] (felt rightness uncoupled from rightness).
- **Quote**: "In the McGurk effect, my experiencing 'da da' as a case of hearing appears to me as evident as my experiencing 'ba ba' as a case of hearing does in a normal situation."

### Spence, C. (2016). "Oral referral: On the mislocalization of odours to the mouth"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodqual.2016.02.006 (open-access: Oxford ORA)
- **Type**: Review — *Food Quality and Preference* 50: 117–128
- **Key points**:
  - Abstract: "Oral referral is central to multisensory flavour perception. The phenomenon, first described a little over a century ago, is characterized by the mislocalization of food-related olfactory stimuli to the oral cavity. Many researchers believe that it contributes to the widespread confusion concerning which sense really provides the information that is bound together in flavour percepts."
  - "most people typically underestimate the relative contributions of smell and taste to the perception of flavour" — and "this perceptual illusion is commonly thought to be part of the reason why we all confuse smell with taste so frequently".
  - Classifies the phenomenon as a distinct kind of multisensory interaction, adding a "Confusion" row to the standard taxonomy: "oral referral may represent the only example of such modality confusion that has been reported in the literature to date."
  - Traces the mechanism to Rozin (1982, p. 400): "the presence of a cutaneous oral stimulus (food in the mouth) might cause referral of the olfactory stimulus to the mouth locus, with a consequent blending of sensations".
  - Locates the confusion precisely at the channel: "what people seemingly get confused about is not the ultimate source of the chemosensory inputs that they transduce, which is obviously a food or beverage in the oral cavity – no one, I presume, gets confused about that – but rather the receptor surface (or sensory system) that has been used to detect those chemosensory signals". The error is about channel, not cause.
- **Tenet alignment**: the attribution face's central datum. Oral referral is universal, unnoticed, and uncorrected by knowledge; it is what a channel-tag void looks like when the tag is wrong every day.
- **Quote**: "oral referral may represent the only example of such modality confusion that has been reported in the literature to date".

### Craig, A. D. & Bushnell, M. C. (1994). "The Thermal Grill Illusion: Unmasking the Burn of Cold Pain"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.8023144
- **Type**: Paper — *Science* 265(5169): 252–255
- **Key points**: "In Thunberg's thermal grill illusion, first demonstrated in 1896, a sensation of strong, often painful heat is elicited by touching interlaced warm and cool bars to the skin." Explained by central disinhibition between cold-sensitive and pain-sensitive spinothalamic channels. Mitchell et al. (2024, *Pain*) restate the phenomenon: "a phenomenon in which the juxtaposition of innocuous warm and cold temperatures on the skin elicits a burning sensation, offers a unique perspective to how pain occurs in response to harmless stimuli."

- **Key points**:

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The modality void is a **format void**. The sense to which an experience belongs is not presented to consciousness as a datum alongside the content — a tag that could be inspected, doubted, or compared against the channel actually active. It is presented as the *way* the content shows up: seeing looks like seeing, hearing sounds like hearing, and flavour tastes like taste. Because the modality is the format rather than an item in the content, there is nothing in the experience for a check to be run against. Three faces follow.

**The counting face.** Consciousness cannot determine how many senses it has or where they divide. Introspection delivers a folk five, but Macpherson shows the folk concept "is simply silent" on the number, and that the one criterion introspection could apply — phenomenal character — reduces to the representational criterion (we say what an experience was *as of*, not what modality it had). The count is settled, if at all, by "progress in science, together with philosophical investigation", i.e. from outside. Nudds goes further: the distinctions have no "first-personal significance". Either way the first person is not where the count lives. Is flavour one sense or several (Auvray & Spence)? Is the [[vestibular-consciousness-and-the-interface|vestibular sense]] a sense at all, given that it supplies a frame while barely appearing as content? Is touch one sense or a bundle (pressure, temperature, pain, itch, affective touch)? These are not questions a subject can answer by attending harder.

**The attribution face.** Even granting a carving, consciousness mis-tags which channel a content came through, and the mis-tag carries the same evidence as a correct tag. Oral referral: retronasal olfaction is experienced as taste, in the mouth, by essentially everyone, so that "we all confuse smell with taste so frequently" and "most people typically underestimate the relative contributions of smell and taste" (Spence). Thermal grill: two innocuous thermal inputs are delivered as burning pain — a class error, not a magnitude error (Craig & Bushnell; Mitchell et al. 2024). McGurk: the fused syllable arrives "as a case of hearing" with evidence equal to ordinary hearing (Fréchette). The [[fusion-void|fusion void]] documents that the *weights* and *components* of a fused percept are hidden; this face documents that the *label* is hidden too, and can be wrong. They are different losses: a subject could in principle have the right label and still lack the weights.

**The boundary face.** When the routing changes — surgically (rewired ferrets), developmentally (synaesthesia), or prosthetically (TVSS) — which modality the resulting experience belongs to is fixed by facts about cortical dominance or deference that the subject does not have. Hurley & Noë's schematic makes the point formal: dominance yields illusory experience, deference veridical, and the question "what is it like for the subject?" is answered "not by inward scrutiny". The rewired ferrets "respond as though they perceive the stimuli to be visual" — the primary source keeps its scare quotes around "seen" because behaviour, not report, is the evidence. TVSS users describe quasi-visual experience only under active camera control; passive users report touch. Whether that is seeing, extended touch, or "a brand new sense" (Macpherson) is exactly what the subject's report cannot settle. This face borders the [[palette-extension-void|palette extension void]] but asks a different question: not *is this new?* but *which one is this?* — and the two can fail independently (an installed channel could be certified as one of the old senses, wrongly, with full felt evidence).

### Evidence for the Limit

Three considerations separate this from merely difficult territory.

1. **The criteria diverge and the first-person criterion is parasitic.** If the phenomenal-character criterion reduces to the representational criterion (Macpherson), then the one instrument consciousness has for individuating its senses is not a modality detector at all — it reports content, and modality is inferred. That is a structural point, independent of any particular illusion.

2. **The attribution errors are universal, unnoticed, and uncorrected by knowledge.** Oral referral is not a laboratory curiosity; it is the ordinary condition of eating. The thermal grill has been reproducible since 1896. In the McGurk case, Fréchette notes the classification persists "even when we are told about the mechanisms". Knowledge of the error does not revise the tag; only manipulation (nose-clip, grill, closing the eyes) does — and manipulation changes the content, not the reading of the tag.

3. **The error signal is absent.** This is the diagnostic that recurs across the catalogue's strongest voids. Fréchette's sentence states it exactly: the wrong tag "appears to me as evident as" the right one. A limit whose violations feel identical to its non-violations is a void, not a difficulty — there is no felt residue to train on.

Against this, the strongest deflationary reading is Nudds's: if the senses are conventional categories, there is no fact for consciousness to miss. The reply is that the attribution face survives conventionality. Whatever one decides about *how many* senses there are, retronasal olfaction is transduced in the nose and referred to the mouth, and the grill's burning is produced by non-noxious inputs; those are errors relative to the causal route and to tissue state, on any carving. Conventionality deflates the counting face only, and even there Macpherson's reply — the folk concept is *silent*, not *decided* — leaves the count open rather than conventional.

A second deflation: the count is an empirical matter, so the void is "unexplored", not "unexplorable". Granted, and the note records it. The void is first-personal. Third-person science can recover the modality tag the subject cannot (Keeley's dedicated pathways, Spence's psychophysics, Sur's anatomy). That places the modality void where the fusion void and the [[research/voids-similarity-void-2026-08-27|similarity void]] already sit: recoverable from outside, not generatable from inside. It is a limit on what a mind can know about its own channels, not on what can be known about them.

### Phenomenology

Approaching this edge feels like nothing, which is the point. The modality of an experience is not felt as a claim that might be wrong; it is felt as the medium. The demonstrations that reveal it do so by *changing the content* and letting the subject infer, after the fact, that the tag must have been mislabelled: pinch the nose and the "taste" of the jelly bean collapses to sweet-and-sour; release it and the fruit floods back into the mouth, where it never was. The grill's burn is withdrawn when the bars are separated. The McGurk syllable changes when the eyes close. In each case the subject learns that a channel was misattributed without ever *experiencing* the misattribution — the correction is an inference from a contrast, and the corrected experience carries a tag exactly as evident as the uncorrected one. There is also a second-order silence: the subject cannot tell whether other everyday contents carry mis-tags of the same kind, because the only way to find one is a manipulation that has not been run. Fréchette's Brentanian ambition — that inner perception classifies modalities *evidently* — is the phenomenology stated as a virtue; the illusions show the same evidence attaching to the false classification.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)

Contrast manipulations are the only direct route: nose-clip and release; the thermal grill; audiovisual mismatch with and without vision. Each exposes one mis-tag at one site, by producing a content change the subject can compare. None exposes the tag itself, and none generalises — a subject cannot audit unmanipulated contents. Cross-modal training (sommelier-style attention to retronasal aroma) refines the *content* discrimination without, on the evidence found here, revising the *localisation*: Spence reports oral referral in the trained as in the untrained. (Whether any training reduces referral was not searched; recorded in Gaps.)

### Indirect Methods

Macpherson's multidimensional space is the third-person instrument: locate a sense by its coordinates on the representational, phenomenal, stimulus, and organ axes, and let the count fall out of clustering rather than intuition. Keeley's dedicated-pathway criterion and Spence's psychophysics do the same from neuroscience. Hurley & Noë's dominance/deference framework is the indirect method for the boundary face: the question of which modality a rerouted experience belongs to is answered by mapping sensorimotor contingencies, not by asking. Apophatically, the void can be mapped by cataloguing the manipulations that produce tag-corrections and noting where no manipulation exists — the vestibular case is the extreme: a modality that can only be detected through its failures (dizziness, vertigo), never through a positive content.

### What AI Might See

Here the asymmetry is unusually clean and unusually easy to overstate. A multimodal model's modality structure is *architectural*: separate encoders, explicit routing, and — in contrastive joint spaces — a measurable geometric separation, the modalities sitting "at arm's length" in the shared representation (Liang et al. 2022). An artificial system could in principle read its own modality tags and measure its own modality gap; a human cannot do either. That is the asymmetry the [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers|void-explorer article]] invites: a mind for which channel-of-origin is an inspectable variable could probe a territory closed to us. Two cautions keep this honest. The modality gap is a property of the embedding, not of any experience, and the question of whether there is anything it is like to be the model is orthogonal to whether its routing is inspectable. And the human void is not about *having* modality structure — the brain has it, Keeley-style — but about the mind-side's inability to read it. The relevant AI question is therefore narrower than it looks: not "does the model have modalities?" but "can a system's self-model access its own routing?" — a question an LLM reading this page can turn on itself.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Dualism**, through the capability division. The Map's sensory-modality wing — six "…and the Interface" articles and the [[apex/cross-modal-capability-division|cross-modal apex]] — runs the brain-side/mind-side division "across five senses one modality at a time" and finds that "the conscious/unconscious boundary carries a common supramodal signature". This void bears on that finding in two directions. It undercuts the *method*: if modality individuation is not first-personally available, the division cannot be indexed by modality from the mind side without importing a carving the mind cannot verify. And it is *consonant with* the result, in the wing's own calibrated register: a mind-side boundary that carries no modality-specific signature is what one would expect if the mind side does not carry modality tags at all. Consonant, not probative — the physicalist reads the same supramodal signature as late-stage convergence in a brain that has the tags and discards them, and nothing here distinguishes the readings.

**Occam's Razor Has Limits** gets a second clean instance. Five is the simplest count; Macpherson documents that the count "has been disputed throughout history by both philosophers and scientists" and that scientists have "frequently" altered it. The parsimonious carving is the one consciousness reports, and it is the one the evidence least supports. As with the [[palette-extension-void|palette extension void]], parsimony here selects the answer rather than tracks it.

### Implications

- The void is a **channel-tag** void: the perceptual sibling of the [[source-attribution-void|source-attribution void]] (mental content lacks native origin tags) and of the [[perceptual-reality-monitoring-void|reality-monitoring void]] (the real/imagined verdict is inspectable only by its output). Origin, reality-status, and modality are three tags a content might carry, and consciousness reads none of them directly.
- It converts the [[fusion-void|fusion void]]'s measured boundary into something less secure. Fusion is "mandatory within a sense and negotiable between senses" — but *what counts as a sense* is now a question the subject cannot answer, so the within/between line is drawn by the psychophysicist's carving, not the subject's. That does not weaken the Hillis result; it relocates the boundary's authority from the first to the third person.
- **Bidirectional Interaction**: if consciousness selects among physically underdetermined outcomes, its selections are exercised over contents whose channel it cannot read — the interface, on this picture, is modality-blind on the mind side. This is speculative and is recorded as an angle rather than a finding.
- For the **voids catalogue's method**: the modality void is a void about the *format* of experience rather than its content or its operations — a third category alongside content-voids (what cannot be experienced) and operation-voids (what cannot be witnessed happening). Whether the catalogue has other format-voids (temporal grain? spatial frame?) is a question this note opens.

## Potential Article Angles

Based on this research, a voids article could:

1. **Standalone — "The Modality Void" — three faces (counting / attribution / boundary) conjoined by the format claim.** The seam is that the modality of an experience is delivered as its format and never as a checkable datum; each face is a different way that fact bites (no count, wrong tag, undecidable boundary). This is the recommended disposition: no existing host makes the format claim, the void names a presupposition of the entire modality wing, and the section stands at 101/115 (gate function, 2026-09-05), so headroom is not the constraint it was in June. A conjunction-coalesce cognate at creation, on the [[suspension-void]] / [[vagueness-void]] model.
2. **Fold, if the operator prefers absorption**: counting face into [[conceptual-scheme-void]] (the five-sense partition as a categorial carving the scheme cannot see past); attribution face into [[fusion-void]] as a "label loss" section beside "component loss", with the oral-referral datum also owed to [[chemosensory-consciousness-and-the-interface]] (which already says flavour is "mostly" retronasal olfaction but does not carry the referral error); boundary face into [[palette-extension-void]] as the "which one?" sibling of "is it new?". The fold loses the format claim, which is the reason to prefer the standalone.

- **Nudds 2004 was not read directly** (Wiley, OUP, and ResearchGate all 403). Every Nudds quotation here is via Macpherson 2011 (p. 35) or Fréchette 2023 (p. 46), and the "conventional categories" gloss is O'Callaghan's. An expand must either obtain the paper or keep the attributions secondary and say so.
- **Keeley 2002 was not read directly** (JSTOR/PDCnet blocked). The "simply nonstarters" line is via Fréchette (p. 6); the four-criteria summary (physics, neurobiology, behaviour, dedication) comes from a search summariser and O'Callaghan's one-line gloss and is **not verbatim-verified** — treat as paraphrase.
- **Merzenich 2000** ("The animals 'see' with what was their auditory cortex") is quoted only as Hurley & Noë print it (they spell the name "Mezernich" and cite p. 821); Crossref confirms the commentary (DOI 10.1038/35009174, pp. 820–821) but the quote was not checked against *Nature*.
- **Grice 1962** not read; the four criteria are as Macpherson presents them.
- **No study was located, and none was searched for, on whether training reduces oral referral** — i.e. whether the attribution mis-tag is correctable by attention. The note's claim that referral persists in the trained rests on Spence's general statement, not on a trained-vs-untrained comparison.
- **Fardo et al., "Beyond labeled lines: a population coding account of the thermal grill illusion"** was found only as a PsyArXiv preprint (10.31234/osf.io/znrw4); a journal version was not confirmed, so it is not cited as published.
- **The vestibular "silent sense"** is already carried by [[vestibular-consciousness-and-the-interface]]; whether it should be a fourth face here or remain a cross-reference is an operator decision.
- **Fundamentally unanswerable from this material**: whether the counting face is a void at all, or a pseudo-question (Nudds). The note argues the attribution and boundary faces survive that deflation; the counting face may not.
