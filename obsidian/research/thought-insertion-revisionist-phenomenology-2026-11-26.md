## Executive Summary

The "revisionist" label in the harvest task came from an outer review. It covers papers that reject the *binary* model of thought insertion, the model on which some features of consciousness go missing while others stay wholly intact. The page already leaves that binary behind: two of its five readings (added element; universalist disturbance) reject it. Three sources are new to the corpus.

- **Mathieson (2024)**: thought insertion as "a disturbing or disrupting of the subjectivity (for-me-ness) of experience", against Duncan's agency-deficit argument.
- **Feyaerts and Sass (2024) with Sass and Feyaerts (2024)**: a two-part revision of the IDM, the clinical framework that Henriksen and Parnas's reading of thought insertion belongs to. It adds *exaggerated* self-presence (quasi-solipsism, ontological paranoia), and it argues that the "diminished minimal self" formula is hard to make coherent if minimal self is a necessary structure.
- **Raballo et al. (2025)**: an IDM-side response. It argues that the revision's examples of heightened self-presence seem to concern experiential *contents* and not structure.

López-Silva's 2018–2025 work on thought insertion is abstract-only here. It is not proposed for the page.

## Key Sources

### Mathieson (2024), "Schizophrenic Thought Insertion and Self-Experience"
- **Citation**: *Review of Philosophy and Psychology* 15(2): 523–539. DOI 10.1007/s13164-023-00680-2. Crossref gives online 2023-02-15 and print June 2024. CC BY 4.0.
- **Retrieval**: **[F]** publisher PDF from ANU Open Research (hdl 1885/287169), 17 pages, 8,734 words. Springer and PhilArchive are bot-walled. The PDF is the online-first version and prints **no page numbers**. The page numbers below map PDF page *n* to 522 + *n* (17 pages onto 523–539), so they are inferred.
- **Thesis (abstract)**: against "the prevailing binary approach which holds that certain features of consciousness go missing while others remain wholly intact", "a better explanation involves construing thought insertion as a disturbing or disrupting of the subjectivity (for-me-ness) of experience" (p. 523).
- **Target**: Duncan (2019, *RPP* 10(2): 299–318) **[2nd via Mathieson]** offers what Mathieson calls "the first sustained argument" (p. 524) from thought insertion to self-experience: the missing sense of agency is missed only because it is normally present. Mathieson's objection runs: "appealing to a missing sense of agency to explain thought insertion fails to discriminate it from other cases where agency is lacking" (p. 525). He credits the formulation to Farrell and McClelland (2017) and to Billon (2013).
- **Positive account**: thought insertion "arises as an end phenomenon out of this altered experiential framework" (p. 530), the prodromal self-alienation documented with the EASE. The account follows HPZ. The key line is: "There may be a disruption of for-me-ness but never a dissolution" (p. 535). On the deficiency/absence distinction: "Of course, something being deficient is different from something being missing", "so the dispute here might be verbal if these authors are not using these terms synonymously" (p. 533). Here he means the added-element authors, Zahavi and Kriegel, and Billon and Kriegel.
- **Inner-location inference (fn 6, p. 533)**: "Subjects cannot lack subjectivity in the minimal sense I endorse, since the very basis for patients' complaints that thoughts are 'in them' without being 'theirs' relies on some (at least minimal) sense of subjectivity or ownership being retained". This is the INNER-LOC premise of Billon 2013 §4.1. Mathieson cites Billon 2013 in the same footnote but does not engage §4.1. He describes Billon's notion of subjectivity as "defined as thoughts being phenomenally conscious", which compresses Billon's two-part claim (no subjectivity *and* no phenomenality). He is "broadly sympathetic to Billon's criticisms of the agency-deficit explanation" (p. 533).
- **Concessions** (stance guard):
  - (i) He needs only the weaker thesis: "The weaker claim is that for-me-ness manifests as an experiential constituent in some of our phenomenally conscious states … For present purposes the weaker version will do" (p. 534). Fn 8 adds that Duncan-style self-experience "is consistent with there being some totally self-less states of consciousness" (p. 535, citing Millière 2020).
  - (ii) He allows added-element readings: "I do not deny that thought insertion might involve additions to experience" (p. 533).
  - (iii) He collapses Guillot's split by stipulation: "I will use 'for-me-ness' 'subjectivity', 'minimal selfhood', and 'ipseity' synonymously … Nothing differs except the label" (p. 531), citing Zahavi 2018 for "why Guillot's conceptual distinctions are unproblematic for our use of for-me-ness".
  - (iv) Against Fuchs: "One point of contention is Fuchs's claim that patients with thought insertion lack ipseity" (p. 535, fn 9). This is a lead: Fuchs 2015 is another ipseity-lost voice, unread here.
- **Other content**: he replies to Schear's (2009) "refrigerator light effect" (p. 534) with a driving-on-autopilot case, arguing that reliable report presupposes prior pre-reflective awareness. Sass and Feyaerts use the same long-distance driver the other way, as "mindless absorption". This is a minor side finding only.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1 *compatible*. Mathieson separates a "metaphysical (or deflationary)" from a phenomenological thesis of for-me-ness and argues only the phenomenological one, which is metaphysically neutral.

### Feyaerts & Sass (2024a), "Self-Disorder in Schizophrenia: A Revised View (1. Comprehensive Review–Dualities of Self- and World-Experience)"
- **Citation**: *Schizophrenia Bulletin* 50(2): 460–471. DOI 10.1093/schbul/sbad169. Online 2023-12-09; PMC10919772 (not OA subset). Authors in this order: Jasper Feyaerts, Louis Sass.
- **Retrieval**: **[F]** published version via the Wayback snapshot of the PMC article page (2026-05-09), 10,643 words with the reference list. No page numbers in that rendering, so quote without pages. The OSF preprint (submittedVersion, `osf.io/rfv9y`) was downloaded and not quoted.
- **Thesis**: the IDM's "exclusive emphasis on so-called 'diminished self-presence'" misses "inflation of the ordinary boundaries of subjectivity or for-me-ness": quasi-solipsism and derealisation produced "by an excessive for-me-ness", ontological paranoia, increased "grip", and paradoxical combinations of both poles. They propose that "altered self-presence" and "altered grip/hold" can each move toward diminishment or exaggeration: "Such recognition avoids the common error of conflating any deviation from normalcy with deficit, diminishment, or decline".
- **On thought insertion**: one mention. First-rank symptoms such as thought insertion "do indeed suggest apparent passivization or loss of 'ownership' over thought, experience, or action". So thought insertion stays the paradigm of the *diminished* pole. No reading of it is offered.
- **On EASE**: it "does demonstrate the prominence (but not the exclusive presence) of core-self abnormalities in schizophrenia. It does not, however, illustrate primarily diminishment or loss of minimal self". They list EASE items for exaggerated self-presence (#5.2, #5.3, #5.8). They record specificity doubts, citing affinities with depersonalisation disorder, panic disorder and radical introspection.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral. The paper is clinical-descriptive.

### Sass & Feyaerts (2024b), "Self-Disorder in Schizophrenia: A Revised View (2. Theoretical Revision—Hyperreflexivity)"
- **Citation**: *Schizophrenia Bulletin* 50(2): 472–483. DOI 10.1093/schbul/sbad170. Online 2023-12-09; PMC10919789. **Authors in this order: Louis Sass, Jasper Feyaerts.**
- **Retrieval**: **[F]** published version via the Wayback snapshot of the PMC page (2026-05-09), 9,855 words. No page numbers. OSF preprint `osf.io/zk7nr` downloaded, not quoted.
- **The objection the fold uses**: they report the universalist reply in their own words, "One response 7 consists of arguing that self-disorders do still involve a disturbance at the level of minimal self, but that the latter would somehow be merely 'eroded' or 'diminished' instead of being entirely absent". Ref 7 is Henriksen, Parnas and Zahavi 2019. Then: "This solution can seem rather ad hoc (and in fact was proposed only after problems with the original model were noted). It would seem, in any case, that such a dimensional or quantitative understanding of mineness/for-me-ness runs counter to how minimal self is actually defined and understood in the phenomenological literature". Mineness there is "argued for as a logical necessity; and it is difficult to see how it could allow of being conceived in terms of degrees". The puzzle as they frame it: "How, or in what sense, could self-disorders involve a disturbance of minimal self if the latter is also assumed to be necessarily preserved throughout subjective life".
- **Against the agency/ownership account** (Gallagher 2015 as their ref 6): unbidden and obsessional thoughts lack agency without alienation (they cite Rasmussen & Parnas 2022, not Billon). Loss of self also hits passive states such as emotions and sensations, where the change "would seem to pertain to possession rather than to agency".
- **Neutrality on universality** (stance guard): the assumption that a pre-reflective sense of self imbues ordinary experience "is itself not uncontested" (Alweiss 2022; McClelland 2023; Howell & Thompson 2017; Dreyfus 2006; Schear 2009), and on whether minimal self is a sine qua non of consciousness, "Here we take no position on these vexed questions."
- **The revision**: "The shared factor is better conceived as the presence of something abnormal, namely, hyperreflexivity"; "The self-disturbance model should be re-configured around the concept of hyperreflexivity." Hyperreflexivity is "a rendering-explicit of aspects of experience that are normally transparent or implicit". It has operative (passive) and hyper-reflective (volitional) forms on a continuum. On exaggerated self-presence: "Here the 'for-me-ness' seems to be exacerbated and extended, not diminished". On diminished minimal self: "Though clearly an important symptom, it is not a constant one nor can it serve as a defining feature of schizophrenia." The model's status in their own words: it "is, of course, only a hypothesis".
- **Bearing on thought insertion**: indirect. The Klosterkötter basic-symptom progression ends in "classic symptoms of truly not owning one's own thoughts" (citing Klosterkötter 1992, 2001). That is the same prodromal sequence HPZ cite (Klosterkötter 1988, per the 2026-10-01 note), redescribed as hyperreflexive. So the **clinical-context ground the page's kind-claim leans on (L92) survives the revision**. What the revision contests is HPZ's *description* of that context as disturbed for-me-ness.
- **Coiner**: "The term 'hyperreflexivity' was introduced through discussion of the myriad parallels" between schizophrenia and modernist art. That is Sass's *Madness and Modernism*, cited in the revised 2017 OUP edition. The 1992 first-edition date is from memory and was not checked this run. Credit Sass, not Feyaerts, and not Sass & Parnas 2003. The IDM itself traces to Sass & Parnas (2003), *Schizophrenia Bulletin* 29: 427–444, per both parts and Raballo et al.
- **Structural parallel (this note's inference, not the authors' claim)**: a disorder characterised by an abnormal presence and not a lack is the same shape as the page's added-element readings (Billon & Kriegel 2015; Zahavi & Kriegel 2016). Sass and Feyaerts cite Billon and Kriegel only in a list of "various proposals". They draw no such connection and offer no reading of thought insertion. Do not put it on the page as theirs.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1 *compatible*. They expect "a heterogeneity of neural correlates", with unity on the "psychological and indeed the phenomenological plane" and "distal causal factors, both genetic and environmental". That claim is methodological and carries no dualist weight.

### Raballo, Henriksen, Poletti & Parnas (2025), "'Schizophrenia, Consciousness, and the Self' Twenty Years Later"
- **Citation**: *Schizophrenia Bulletin* 51(5): 1187–1192. DOI 10.1093/schbul/sbaf071. Online 2025-05-25; PMC12414551 (OA).
- **Retrieval**: **[F]** Europe PMC `fullTextXML`, about 5,300 words.
- **Response to the revision**: "However, it is not clear that these examples exemplify heightened self-presence, or, at least, it depends on how one defines self-presence. … The examples above all seem to concern specific experiential contents, but it is not obvious that they concern a heightened experiential structure, ie, increased self-manifestation of consciousness." Hence "the revised IDM is operating with a broader or different notion of self-presence than the original IDM". On the pre-onset self: what happens is "not an intact Self that is later eroded" but a reflective self becoming aware of earlier minimal-self alterations. Specificity is defended by meta-analysis (quoted under the Fold Plan). Developmentally, agency attenuation comes first and erosion of ownership later, ending in first-rank symptoms; corollary discharge is proposed as a mechanism.
- **What it does not do**: it does not answer the ad hoc/degree charge ("ad hoc" 0 hits).
- **Tension this note records (inference)**: Raballo et al.'s split between structure and content would let the Map say that degree varies in *contents* while the *structure* holds. But HPZ (per the 2026-10-01 note's [F] reading of HPZ p. 7, not re-read this run) explicitly reject the untouched-core picture: their fruit-syrup analogy against the artichoke. The Map cannot take Raballo et al.'s structure/content answer and HPZ's "disturbed" for-me-ness together without saying which gives. That is the open item behind fold item B. ## Major Positions (as they bear on the page's fifth reading)

### Universalist disturbance, now with four texts
- **Proponents**: Zahavi & Kriegel 2016; Henriksen & Parnas 2019; Henriksen, Parnas & Zahavi 2019; **Mathieson 2024**. Raballo et al. 2025 defend the IDM it draws on.
- **Grounds**: (a) clinical context, with thought insertion as the end phenomenon of prodromal self-alienation; (b) the inner-location inference. Mathieson uses both. None of the four engages Billon §4.1 against (b).
- **Pressure from the revision**: Sass and Feyaerts contest the *formula* "disturbed but not absent" and not ground (a).

### The revised IDM (Feyaerts & Sass; Sass & Feyaerts)
- **Core claim**: schizophrenic self-disorder is better characterised by an abnormal presence (hyperreflexivity) than by an absence (diminished minimal self), and self-presence can be exaggerated as well as diminished.
- **Stance on universality**: none taken.
- **Relation to the tenets**: compatible.

### IDM as defended (Raballo et al.)
- **Core claim**: the revision's "heightened self-presence" changes contents, not the structure of self-manifestation. Self-disorder is developmental.
- **Relation to the tenets**: compatible.

## Key Debates

### Can a necessary structure be disturbed in degree?
- **Sides**: Sass and Feyaerts (it is hard to conceive) against HPZ (it is disturbed, not lacking) and the Map's kind-claim (kind holds, degree varies). Raballo et al.'s structure/content distinction is a candidate answer, but it seems to cost HPZ their rejection of an untouched core.
- **State**: open. The 2025 commentary and the 2026 reply are unread.

### Is the universalist/added-element difference verbal?
- **Sides**: Mathieson says it "might be verbal" if "deficient" and "missing" are not synonyms. The page keeps the two readings separate and quotes Zahavi and Kriegel in both.
- **State**: unargued in the literature read.

## Relation to the Tenets (graded: compatible / suggestive / discriminating)

- **Dualism: compatible.** Neither the revision nor Mathieson's argument bears on non-physicality. Mathieson explicitly argues only a phenomenological thesis, and Sass and Feyaerts expect heterogeneous neural correlates with "genetic and environmental" causes. The universalist reading gains a fourth proponent, but it remains one of the page's five readings and is not upgraded. The page's existing grade (compatible, with "the edge, such as it is, to the rival") stands.
- **The kind-claim** is a Map position, not a tenet. The degree objection counts *against* its current formulation. That is the reason for fold item B, which states the debt without conceding the claim.
- **Bidirectional Interaction: compatible at most.** Sass and Feyaerts hold that focal awareness "may actually alter the experiences in question" through reification. Reading this as consciousness causally reshaping its own states would be an upgrade: on their account it is attention modulating experience, which a physicalist reads as attention modulating processing. Do not cite it as support for Tenet 3.
- **No Many Worlds**: nothing here.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits: compatible, illustrative only.** Sass and Feyaerts reject the simpler single-deficit model on descriptive grounds. Simplicity lost to the clinical record, but that concerns model choice inside psychiatry and is not evidence for the tenet's metaphysical use. The dispute again turns on what the data are, contents or structure, as the page's Occam paragraph already says.
