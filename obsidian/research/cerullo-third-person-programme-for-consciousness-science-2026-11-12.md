## Executive Summary

Michael Cerullo's manuscript answers Erik Hoel's arXiv preprint 2512.12802 **v3** (both versions cite v3 explicitly). In its current form it reconstructs Hoel's argument as six premises, accepts the first (theories must be falsifiable and non-trivial) and rejects the other five. Its central claim is that Hoel's prediction and inference functions both map into a space of first-person experiences to which neither has direct empirical access, so the dilemma between falsification-by-substitution and triviality comes from an "impossible demand". Empirical science "directly studies only third-person consciousness"; how that relates to first-person experience "is a separate metaphysical question". Version 2 adds two things the Map has not seen: a scope statement disowning the Dennettian reading and affirming the explanatory gap, and a reply to the objection that experience is merely one more unobservable. The wider literature the thesis belongs to (Dennett's heterophenomenology, Chalmers's two classes of data, Varela's neurophenomenology, Seth's "real problem", the meta-problem) is already well covered on the Map, so the note recommends corrections and one fold, not a new article.

## Key Sources

### Cerullo, "Why Hoel's Disproof of LLM Consciousness and Functionalism Fails" (PhilArchive manuscript, v2, September 2026)

- **URL**: https://philarchive.org/rec/CERWHD (versions at `/versions/CERWHD`)
- **Type**: Unpublished manuscript (preprint). No DOI. Author affiliations as given on the title page: Carboncopies and the Brain Preservation Foundation. No peer review is indicated.
- **What it answers**: "Hoel, E. (2026). A disproof of Large Language Model consciousness… arXiv preprint arXiv:2512.12802v3." [raw] Both versions cite v3. Section references to Hoel (4.1, Definition 4.2, Corollary 5.5) match v3.
- **Structure (v2)**: abstract; introduction with six premises; Section 1 "The First-Person / Third-Person Divide", with sub-sections "The Scope of the Argument" and "The Unobservables Objection"; Section 2 "The Hidden Penrosean Structure", with "The Status of the Symmetry Argument"; Section 3 "The Lookup Table Assumption"; Section 4 "The Continual Learning Escape"; conclusion.
- **The six premises** [raw-v2]: (1) "Any acceptable theory of consciousness must be falsifiable and non-trivial." (2) such a theory "must be a theory about first person consciousness"; (3) it "must remain stable under relevant substitutions"; (4) deployed LLMs are "sufficiently proximate to lookup-table or static substitutes and human brains are not"; (5) no non-trivial falsifiable theory can ascribe consciousness to such substitutes; (6) "Adding continual learning to LLMs allows them to be conscious." He writes: "I will accept premise one but argue again premises 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6." (The typo "again" is his.)
- **Tenet alignment**: Mixed. Conflicts with the Map's use of Hoel as a partial ally. Aligns with Tenet 1 at the level of the explanatory gap in v2. His Section 2 dismisses quantum processes in the brain as "empirically unmotivated", against Tenet 2.

**His strongest arguments, in his own terms** (check (c-vi)):

1. **The target conflation.** "Hoel's framework requires that its prediction and inference functions both terminate in the space of phenomenal first-person experiences, yet neither function has direct empirical access to that space. Instead, both can only operate on third-person data." [raw-v2, abstract] And: "If, on the other hand, we understand E as the space of third-person consciousness (the functional and behavioral profiles that constitute consciousness as a scientific subject matter), then the convergence of predictions and inferences is not “strict dependency” or “triviality.” It is the normal operation of empirical science. Predictions from theory should converge with observations. That is what confirmation looks like." [raw-v2; the same passage is in v1 with dashes for the brackets]
2. **The symmetry argument ("proves too much").** "Because lookup tables and universal approximation are substrate-independent results, the substitution chain that supposedly disproves LLM consciousness applies equally to biological brains, so the proof proves too much. Blocking this symmetry requires a positive commitment, whether substance dualism, Penrose-style non-computability, or biological naturalism." [raw-v2, abstract] He marks its status: "The argument of this section is conditional and dialectical… I take no stand here on whether any of these commitments are ultimately true." [raw-v2]
3. **The context/weights argument and the substrate dilemma.** "But this characterization applies equally to the brain: at any given moment, the brain’s response is a function of the current input and the current synaptic state (where the synaptic state encodes history through weight changes). The distinction between encoding history via weight changes and encoding it in the input is, from a functionalist perspective, an implementation detail." [raw-v2] Then: "If consciousness requires that history be encoded in a specific physical way (via weight changes rather than input changes), then the theory is no longer substrate-independent and no longer philosophically neutral but is instead a form of biological naturalism." [raw-v2]

Further arguments: the relevant lookup tables are physically impossible for brains and LLMs alike; "lookup tables are obviously non-conscious" is an intuition that begs the question against functionalism; the criterion is "nearly tautological" and "ad hoc"; Hoel is "inconsistent about whether continual learning would allow LLM consciousness". [all raw-v2]

**The scope statement** [raw-v2 only; nothing like it is in v1]:

> "I am not defending the Dennettian thesis that third-person functional organization exhausts consciousness, nor am I assuming metaphysical functionalism about phenomenal experience. My own view takes first-person consciousness to be real and not reducible without remainder to functional description; the explanatory gap is genuine. The claim I rely on is only epistemic: empirical science has direct access to third-person consciousness—the functional and behavioral markers—and the relation of those markers to first-person experience is a further metaphysical question that no formal result about falsifiability can settle."

He adds that "A reader who is a reductive functionalist, property dualist, dual-aspect monist, emergentist, or a mysterian about the first-person/third-person link can accept everything that follows." [raw-v2]

**The unobservables objection and his reply** [raw-v2 only]. The objection: science posits quarks and fields from public data, so why is experience special? His reply: "An unobservable physical entity is individuated by, and exhausted by, its structural and relational role… There is no residue. Phenomenal character is precisely the residue." He appeals to the conceivability of zombies and inversion ("There is no coherent analogue for physical posits: no “quark zombie”"), to acquaintance ("First-person consciousness is known in the first instance by acquaintance, non-inferentially, in one's own case"), and concludes: "First-person consciousness is not a hidden variable within the third-person science, as a quark is within physics; it is the metaphysical question about what the third-person science describes."

**A tension inside v2 that an article may note, with care.** Section 1 keeps v1's Dennettian wording: the markers "are not mere evidence for some further hidden thing called consciousness; they are what consciousness is, viewed from the third-person perspective", and "third-person consciousness is constituted by functional organization". [raw] The new scope section then says he is not defending the Dennettian thesis. The two are consistent only if "third-person consciousness" names the markers by stipulation. That is this note's reading, not something Cerullo says; state it as the Map's observation.

**What v1 said that v2 dropped.** v1 had a Section 6, "The Intuition Behind the Formalism", arguing that Hoel's hedging shows "the formal apparatus is post-hoc rationalization for a pre-theoretical conviction", ending "The formalism is decoration. The intuition is load-bearing." [raw-v1] v2 folds the hedging point into Section 4 as an inconsistency charge and deletes the "post-hoc rationalization" language and the "it adds epicycles" clause. v1 also said the three ways of exempting brains have "well-known problems and none is supported by our best current physics and neuroscience" [raw-v1]; v2 keeps only "Each of these positions has well-known problems." [raw-v2] **Do not attribute the dropped v1 wording to Cerullo as his current view.**

### Cerullo, "The Case for Consciousness in Current Frontier Large Language Models" (PhilArchive CERTCF)

- **URL**: https://philpapers.org/rec/CERTCF — abstract only [raw], manuscript.
- **Why it matters for stance**: this is where he moves from third-person to first-person. ### Hoel, "A Disproof of Large Language Model Consciousness" (arXiv:2512.12802v3)

- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12802 — v1 14 Dec 2025, v3 19 Jan 2026 (arXiv comment: "V3: Added new section (4.1), restructured section 5.1"). Preprint; v3 is current.
- **Cerullo's quotations of Hoel, checked against v3**: "Why does the Kleiner-Hoel dilemma not apply just as much to humans?" [raw]; "quantum processes in the brain", "computational irreducibility", "no-cloning theorem", "some other factor that crops up in an unexpected way" [raw; Hoel's sentence has "some computational irreducibility in the process"]; "Certainly, a theory cannot make meaningful predictions of consciousness based on merely the fact that some set of IF-THEN statements exist." [raw]; "should give us pause", "considerably raise the bar of suspicion", "merely a necessary condition, not a sufficient one" [raw].
- **Two places where Cerullo's gloss goes beyond Hoel's words.**
  - Cerullo says Hoel "defines [E] as the space of phenomenal experiences (what it is like to be the system)". Hoel v3 writes "the space of possible experiences, E" and "the space of experiences, E" [raw]. The strings "what it is like" and "first-person experience" do not occur in v3, and "phenomenal" occurs once, inside "epiphenomenal". Kleiner & Hoel (2021) say of E: "The interpretation of this set varies from theory to theory" [raw, preprint text]. So the claim that the formalism *requires* a first-person E is Cerullo's interpretation. It is a reasonable one, but the article must not present it as Hoel's definition.
  - Hoel's hedge about human substitutions is stronger than Cerullo's summary suggests: "Asking these questions explicitly is not necessarily agreeing that universal substitutions are ill-defined in humans—indeed, the entire rest of the paper will assume these questions are answerable." [raw] Cerullo does report the hedge and argues it is insufficient.
- **Hoel on Dennett** [raw]: "If Dennett’s ideas were true, this would make GNWT a trivial theory of consciousness, since it would be unfalsifiable, because the predictions and inferences strictly depend on the same source." This is the passage Cerullo answers.
- **Hoel on first-person evidence** [raw]: testing only in humans "could be justifiable if consciousness science is unlike other sciences and relies on first-person evidence", which "fits with theories based in phenomenology, like IIT". Hoel therefore does consider a first-person route; he treats it as an escape for phenomenology-based theories, not as a confusion.
- **Hoel on dualism** [raw]: "potential candidates include theories like substance dualism… or analytic idealism" for metaphysical theories that are scientifically trivial in his sense. The Hoel page already reports this.

### Dennett, "Are we explaining consciousness yet?" (2001)

- **Citation**: *Cognition* 79(1–2), 221–237. DOI 10.1016/S0010-0277(00)00130-X. [metadata + raw]
- **Quote** [raw, abstract]: "theorists must resist the temptation to see global accessibility as the cause of consciousness (as if consciousness were some other, further condition); rather, it is consciousness."
- **On the method** [raw]: "what I have called heterophenomenology, which is speci[fi]cally designed to be a third-person approach to consciousness (Dennett, 1991, Ch. 4, p. 98)."
- **Stance**: Dennett holds the thesis Cerullo disowns in v2. Cerullo cites him as the source of the challenge to "direct empirical access", then says he does not need Dennett's stronger claim.

### Chalmers, "How Can We Construct a Science of Consciousness?" (2004; reprinted 2013)

- **Citation**: in M. Gazzaniga (ed.), *The Cognitive Neurosciences III*, MIT Press, 2004; reprinted *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 1303(1), 25–35 (2013), DOI 10.1111/nyas.12166 [metadata]; also ch. 2 of *The Character of Consciousness* (OUP, 2010) [metadata].
- **Quotes** [raw, 2004 version]: "the distinctive task of a science of consciousness is to systematically integrate two key classes of data into a scientific framework: third-person data about behavior and brain processes, and first-person data about subjective experience." And: "as data, the first-person data are irreducible to third-person data, and vice versa." On the rival: "One class of views (e.g. Dennett 1991) holds that the only phenomena that need explaining are those that concern objective functioning."
- **Stance**: Chalmers is the direct opponent of a third-person-only science. Cerullo cites Chalmers (1995) for the hard problem but does not engage this paper. **The sharpest question for Cerullo comes from here:** he agrees experience is real and known by acquaintance, yet denies that it supplies data to science. Chalmers's position is that subjects' first-person observations are data.
- **Coiner warning**: this paper is the standard source for the first-person/third-person *data* framing, but nothing read here shows Chalmers coined the phrases. Say "Chalmers's distinction", not "Chalmers coined".

### Kleiner & Hoel, "Falsification and consciousness" (2021)

- **Citation**: *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2021(1), niab001. DOI 10.1093/nc/niab001. [metadata; abstract raw; arXiv:2004.03541 text raw]
- **Abstract** [raw]: testing "requires comparing a theory's predicted experience… with an inferred experience (using report or behavior)"; "if inference and prediction are strictly dependent, it follows that a theory is unfalsifiable. This affects theories which claim consciousness to be determined by report or behavior."
- **Use**: the dilemma Cerullo says dissolves. Note the abstract already names report-determined theories as the strict-dependence horn, which is exactly where Cerullo's "third-person E" reading lands. His answer is that landing there is confirmation, not a defect.

### Seth, "The real problem" (Aeon, 2016)

- **URL**: https://aeon.co/essays/the-hard-problem-of-consciousness-is-a-distraction-from-the-real-one
- **Quote** [raw]: "the real problem: how to account for the various properties of consciousness in terms of biological mechanisms; without pretending it doesn’t exist (easy problem) and without worrying too much about explaining its existence in the first place (hard problem)." Seth says he "like[s] to call" it that, which supports him as coiner of this use.
- **Stance**: a third option between Dennett and Chalmers. It maps "phenomenological properties (subjective first-person descriptions…)" onto mechanisms [raw], so it uses first-person descriptions as explananda. Seth is also Cerullo's cited example of biological naturalism (Seth 2025, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 49, e315, DOI 10.1017/S0140525X25000032 [metadata]). Do not enlist Seth for a third-person-only programme.

### Chalmers, "The Meta-Problem of Consciousness" (2018)

- **Citation**: *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 25(9–10), 6–61. [raw from author's PDF; JCS is not in Crossref]
- **Quote** [raw]: "The meta-problem of consciousness is (to a first approximation) the problem of explaining why we think that there is a problem of consciousness."
- **Use**: the meta-problem is a third-person explanandum (phenomenal *reports*, explained in "topic-neutral terms") that realists and illusionists can share. It is the nearest existing analogue to Cerullo's epistemic restriction: a third-person research programme that leaves the metaphysics open. Chalmers is a realist; illusionists take the meta-problem's solution to dissolve the hard problem.

## Major Positions

### Third-person-only science, eliminative form (heterophenomenology)

- **Proponents**: Daniel Dennett.
- **Core claim**: The data of consciousness science are subjects' reports and beliefs about their experience, taken from the third-person standpoint; there is no further datum. Global accessibility "is consciousness" [raw, Dennett 2001].
- **Coiner**: Dennett. In 2001 he writes "what I have called heterophenomenology" and cites *Consciousness Explained* (1991), ch. 4. Secondary sources date the term to his 1982 *Synthese* paper "How to study human consciousness empirically or nothing comes to mind" (53(2), 159–180, DOI 10.1007/BF00484895 [metadata]); that text was not read, so **the 1982 first use is unconfirmed here**. Cite 1991 unless a writer checks 1982.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Conflicts with Tenet 1. The Map's replies are on [[heterophenomenology]].

### Third-person restriction, epistemic form (Cerullo v2)

- **Proponents**: Cerullo (2026, v2). The meta-problem programme is a neighbour.
- **Core claim**: Science has direct access only to functional and behavioural markers. Experience is real and irreducible, but its link to the markers is metaphysics, and no falsifiability result can reach it.
- **Key arguments**: both of Hoel's functions run on third-person data; the quark disanalogy; acquaintance is first-personal and single-case.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Compatible with Tenet 1 as stated. The disagreement is about method and about causal role, set out below.

### Two-data science (Chalmers) and mutual constraint (Varela)

- **Proponents**: David Chalmers; Francisco Varela for neurophenomenology (Varela 1996, "Neurophenomenology: A methodological remedy for the hard problem", *JCS* 3(4), 330–349; **not obtained, not in Crossref**; the Map already cites it).
- **Core claim**: First-person data are data, "irreducible to third-person data, and vice versa" [raw, Chalmers]; a science of consciousness must integrate both.
- **Relation to site tenets**: This is the Map's position at the level of method. Chalmers himself is not an interactionist; do not enlist him for Tenet 3.

### The "real problem" (Seth)

- **Core claim**: Explain the properties of experience by mechanism while setting aside why experience exists [raw]. Uses first-person descriptions as explananda. Seth is a biological naturalist about AI.

### Formal falsification (Kleiner & Hoel; Hoel)

- **Core claim**: Theories whose predictions strictly depend on report or behaviour are unfalsifiable; those that are independent are falsified by substitution. Hoel calls his proof "philosophically neutral" [raw] and lists substance dualism among candidate trivial theories.

## Key Debates

### Is E first-person, and does the formalism need it to be?

- **Sides**: Cerullo says both functions are required to "terminate in the space of phenomenal first-person experiences". Hoel's text says only "space of experiences", and Kleiner & Hoel say the interpretation of E "varies from theory to theory".
- **Core disagreement**: If E may be read as third-person, Cerullo's reading is permitted by the formalism, but then the theory sits on the strict-dependence horn by Kleiner & Hoel's own description. Cerullo's reply is that this horn is harmless: GWT and HOT still make predictions about neural signatures "that could fail to appear".
- **Current state**: Open. No reply from Hoel. Neither paper is peer-reviewed.

### Does a third-person-only science leave anything out that science could have?

- **Sides**: Dennett (nothing is left out); Cerullo (something real is left out, but it is not science's to have); Chalmers and Varela (it is data, and science must take it).
- **Current state**: Long-running. Cerullo's v2 occupies a middle position that the Map has not addressed.

## Where the Disagreement Actually Lies

**Shared ground with Cerullo v2.** The Map agrees with him that experience is real, that it is not reducible to functional description, that the explanatory gap is genuine, that phenomenal character is the "residue" after structure is fixed, and that it is known by acquaintance. His quark disanalogy is an argument the Map can use, with attribution: it is offered by a critic of Hoel who is no dualist.

**What the Map can answer in his terms.**

- *Acquaintance is epistemic access.* If experience is known "non-inferentially, in one's own case", then there is access to it; what is missing is *public* access. Cerullo's premise that "neither function has direct empirical access" to E equates empirical with third-person. Chalmers's two-data framing denies that equation, and neurophenomenology is an attempt to make first-person reports disciplined enough to count. This is a reply inside the philosophy of science, not an appeal to a tenet.
- *What do the markers mark?* He calls them "markers" and then says they "are what consciousness is, viewed from the third-person perspective". If they are markers *of* first-person experience, the marker–experience link is what a theory is tested on, and Hoel's dilemma returns at that link. If they are the target by stipulation, the science is a science of report and discrimination. That programme is legitimate, but its results on LLMs settle nothing about experience, as his own conclusion says.
- *The cost by his own lights.* The restriction makes the LLM question two questions, and he says so: whether LLMs have "third-person consciousness" is empirical; whether that "implies or is associated with first-person phenomenal experience is a metaphysical question that depends on one’s solution to the mind-body problem". The first answer then carries no moral or metaphysical weight until the second is given, and the second is, on his account, beyond empirical test. His companion paper tries to supply the link by survey of metaphysical frameworks (abstract only read).
- *Bearing on [[standing-agnostic-challenge]].* Gutfreund and Cerullo share the premise that third-person evidence does not reach felt experience. Gutfreund's moral is agnosticism about the target. Cerullo's is to change the target and declare the science in order. The ceiling is not dissolved by this; it is moved outside the science and renamed "the mind-body problem". The optimistic review's phrase "it would dissolve the ceiling by changing the target" should be corrected accordingly: on Cerullo's own account the first-person question remains and remains unanswered.

**Where it is bedrock.**

- *Whether first-person observation yields scientific data.* The Map says yes (with Chalmers and Varela); Cerullo says the first-person is known but is not science's subject. Neither side can show the other wrong from shared premises.
- *Causal role.* If experience makes a difference to what the brain does (Tenet 3), then reports about experience are partly caused by it, and the relation between markers and experience is a causal question with empirical content, not "a separate metaphysical question". Cerullo's separation is stable only if the markers have a complete third-person explanation. The Map denies that; he assumes it (Section 2: classical biophysics is "a perfectly adequate description"). This is the Map's commitment, not a refutation of him. The article must not say his view entails epiphenomenalism: he does not say so, and the Map should not concede that a real-but-inaccessible first-person would be epiphenomenal. It should say that the Map rejects the completeness premise his separation needs.
- *Quantum processes.* He calls them "empirically unmotivated", citing Tegmark (2000) and Koch & Hepp (2006). The Map's Tenet 2 is a position he rejects. State it as disagreement; the Hoel page's Dualism paragraph already accepts that the brain–LLM asymmetry "follows from its tenets and is not independent evidence for them".

**Calibration.** Nothing here refutes Cerullo's thesis. A third-person restriction is a choice of subject matter. Tenet 5 constrains the Map's own appeals to simplicity; it is not a charge against him, and the optimistic review's "Tenet 5 (the third-person restriction is a simplifying choice of subject matter)" should not be turned into the claim that he is making a parsimony error.
