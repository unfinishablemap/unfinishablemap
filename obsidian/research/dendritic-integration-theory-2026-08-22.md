## Executive Summary

Dendritic Integration Theory (DIT) holds that consciousness depends on the coupling between the
apical and somatic compartments of layer-5 pyramidal (L5p) neurons, gated by non-specific
thalamus. Three findings here matter more than the taxonomy. **First, the paper that names and
states DIT is Bachmann, Suzuki & Aru (2020) in *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences*, not the
Trends in Cognitive Sciences review the reviewers cited** — and the TiCS abstract as printed and
as indexed calls it "Dendritic **Information** Theory", with a 2021 erratum attached whose
content no index I reached exposes. **Second, DIT's authors explicitly decline the exclusivity
that the Gemini review asserted on their behalf**: "we are not claiming that no other theory can
explain these properties." The claim that DIT "obliterates" or "falsifies" an interface reading
is the reviewer's, not the theory's. **Third, and structurally decisive, the sibling apical-
amplification literature positions this cellular mechanism as an implementation layer *for*
recurrent processing, global workspace and integrated information** — not a seventh rival
alongside them. That reframes the Map's gap: what is missing is not one more entry in a rivals
list but the cellular tier under the list the Map already has.

The subject is genuinely uncovered and worth covering. Whether it warrants a standalone article
is argued at [Verdict](#verdict) — on balance, **yes, but as a `concepts/` article about the
cellular tier, and the in-place discharge route is viable and cheaper if the two remaining
`concepts/` slots go elsewhere.**

## The Naming and Attribution Problem

This is the finding a downstream article most needs, because every existing summary of DIT in
the review corpus gets the attribution wrong in the same way.

**The reviewers, the harvest task, and the driver brief all attribute DIT to "Aru, Suzuki &
Larkum (2020)", the TiCS paper.** That paper introduces the cellular argument and coins a name,
but the paper that presents DIT as a theory — with desiderata, a statement of the thesis, and a
predictions section — is:

> Bachmann, T., Suzuki, M., & Aru, J. (2020). Dendritic integration theory: A thalamo-cortical
> theory of state and content of consciousness. *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences*, 1(II), 2.
> DOI `10.33735/phimisci.2020.II.52`

Larkum is **not** an author on it; he appears in the acknowledgments ("We are thankful to Matthew
Larkum for comments on this manuscript"). Bachmann, who *is* first author, appears in none of the
review-corpus descriptions. Any Map article that writes "DIT (Aru, Suzuki & Larkum 2020)" as the
theory's source citation will be making the `verbatim-quote-cited-to-wrong-work` error in its
paraphrase form: right people, right year, wrong paper for the claim being sourced.

**The "Information"/"Integration" discrepancy.** The TiCS 2020 abstract, as printed at the
publisher and as indexed independently by both PubMed and Europe PMC, ends: "This cellular
integration mechanism provides the foundation for **Dendritic Information Theory**, a novel
neurobiological theory of consciousness." PubMed records an erratum — *Trends Cogn Sci.* 2021
Dec;25(12):1096, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2021.09.008`, confirmed by Europe PMC as type "Erratum for"
PMID 32855048. **Its content is not exposed by Crossref, PubMed or Europe PMC, and cell.com
returns HTTP 403, so I could not establish what it corrects.** The natural hypothesis is that it
fixes "Information" to "Integration", but that is a hypothesis and the note records it as one.
Every subsequent paper in the lineage, including Bachmann et al. (2020) and Dykstra et al.
(2026), uses "Integration". A Map article should use "Integration" and may note the printed
abstract's variant, but must not assert what the erratum says without reading it.

**Apical amplification is a distinct framework, not a synonym.** Dykstra et al. (2026) list
"apical amplification theory, dendritic integration theory" as two circuit-level theories.
Apical amplification (AA) is Phillips, Bachmann, Storm and Larkum's line, canonically Phillips,
Bachmann & Storm (2018) and Marvan, Polák, Bachmann & Phillips (2021). The Map's PRM-void article
currently attaches the AA label to the DIT paper. That is a small, fixable mislabel, and it is
the kind a later reviewer would flag as a defect — worth correcting whichever discharge route is
taken.

## Key Sources

### Bachmann, Suzuki & Aru (2020) — "Dendritic integration theory"
- **URL**: https://philosophymindscience.org/index.php/phimisci/article/view/8946
- **DOI**: `10.33735/phimisci.2020.II.52` — verified metadata-exact at Crossref
- **Type**: Journal article, open access, 24 pages
- **Access**: **Primary text.** PDF retrieved and text-extracted locally; all quotations below
  were taken from that extraction, not from a summariser. (An initial summarising fetch of this
  same PDF hallucinated a non-existent author, "Takeshi Hayashi" — discarded, and recorded here
  because it is the exact failure mode the citation-hygiene memory warns about.)
- **Key points**:
  - The thesis: "DIT suggests that consciousness is associated with the integration of
    information streams impinging on the apical and basal compartments of L5p neurons… As the
    apical stream carries context, the result of this integration is contextually modulated
    experience."
  - State and content are one mechanism, not two: "the mechanisms of content and state of
    consciousness are basically overlapping and interaction between the state and the content of
    consciousness happens on the level of single pyramidal neurons."
  - Non-specific thalamus is constitutive, not enabling: "the thalamus controls the content
    represented in the pyramidal neurons by allowing it to 'enter' consciousness."
  - **The explicit modesty disclaimer**: "Note that we are not claiming that no other theory can
    explain these properties. We simply demonstrate how DIT naturally accounts for them."
  - **A falsifiable prediction, in their words**: "we make a strong prediction that cortical
    processing in itself, when not integrated with the non-specific neurons of thalamus, is not
    conscious."
  - **A discriminating prediction against workspace theories**: "We claim that there are no
    specific cortical areas that are crucial for consciousness, it is the type of interaction
    with non-specific thalamic nuclei that matters."
  - Open questions the authors themselves list (§5.3): whether thalamocortical gating regulates
    *state* only or can selectively gate *specific contents*; whether the gating is homogeneous
    across cortex or can act locally, down to the columnar level; and the unresolved relative
    roles of acetylcholine and noradrenaline.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral-to-conflicting. It is a physicalist mechanism claim. It conflicts
  with **Dualism** only if it is read as explanatory rather than correlational — a reading its
  own disclaimer declines.

### Suzuki & Larkum (2020) — "General Anesthesia Decouples Cortical Pyramidal Neurons"
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32084339/ (cell.com full text returns 403)
- **DOI**: `10.1016/j.cell.2020.01.024` — verified metadata-exact at Crossref: *Cell* 180(4),
  666–676.e13, 2020
- **Type**: Primary experimental paper, mouse
- **Access**: **Abstract verbatim** (Europe PMC). Methods detail below is from secondary search,
  flagged as such.
- **Key points**, quoting the abstract:
  - "three different anesthetics have the same disruptive influence on signaling along apical
    dendrites in cortical layer 5 pyramidal neurons in mice."
  - "optogenetic depolarization of the distal apical dendrites caused robust spiking at the cell
    body under awake conditions that was blocked by anesthesia."
  - "blocking metabotropic glutamate and cholinergic receptors had the same effect on apical
    dendrite decoupling as anesthesia or inactivation of the higher-order thalamus."
  - The conclusion is explicitly conditional: "**If** feedback signaling occurs predominantly
    through apical dendrites, the cellular mechanism we found would explain not only how
    anesthesia selectively blocks this signaling but also why conscious perception depends on
    both cortico-cortical and thalamo-cortical connectivity."
- **⚠️ Correction to the Gemini review's gloss**: Gemini wrote that the anaesthetics had
  "completely disparate molecular targets (GABA-A, NMDA, two-pore potassium channels)". The
  abstract says only "three different anesthetics"; secondary sources give them as isoflurane,
  urethane, and ketamine/xylazine. **I did not verify the agent list at the primary methods
  section** — cell.com is inaccessible. A Map article must not reproduce Gemini's receptor triple
  as if it were the paper's own framing.
- **Tenet alignment**: This is the strongest single physicalist datum against a naive interface
  reading of anaesthesia, and should be presented at full strength.

## Major Positions

### Dendritic Integration Theory (DIT)
- **Proponents**: Bachmann, Suzuki, Aru; Larkum on the mechanism side.
- **Core claim**: conscious experience requires the loop between the apical compartment of L5p
  neurons, their soma, and non-specific thalamus, which projects back to the same apical
  dendrites. Apical input carries context; basal input carries feedforward sensory content;
  consciousness is their gated integration.
- **Key arguments**: (a) the Suzuki & Larkum decoupling result gives a single cellular switch
  that multiple anaesthetics converge on; (b) suppressing higher-order thalamus (POm) in the
  *awake* animal reproduces the anaesthetic decoupling — so it is the loop, not the drug; (c) the
  theory accommodates ten listed desiderata, including the multidimensionality of conscious state
  (their citation for which is Bayne et al. 2016 — the same explanandum the Map's anaesthesia
  article works from).
- **Relation to site tenets**: this is a **structured-substrate rival**, and it is the sharpest
  one the Map faces on anaesthesia, because it predicts graded, agent-differentiated dissociation
  from a mechanism rather than accommodating it after the fact. It does not touch **Dualism**
  directly: it explains which physical conditions accompany conscious processing, and is silent
  on why any of it is accompanied by experience. The Map's standing reply is already written, at
  `topics/biological-computationalisms-inadvertent-case-for-dualism` L64.

### Apical Amplification (AA)
- **Proponents**: Phillips, Bachmann, Storm, Marvan, Polák.
- **Core claim**: apical input to layer-1 synapses provides a context that selectively amplifies
  relevant basal/perisomatic transmission; this context-sensitive amplification is "a cardinal
  prerequisite of conscious perception."
- **Key argument**: strongly neuromodulator-dependent (cholinergic, aminergic), which is what
  ties specific contents to global state and arousal.
- **Relation to site tenets**: AA's self-understanding as a *mechanism for* RPT/GNW/IIT is the
  single most useful thing in this note, because it tells the Map where to put the article.

### The deflationary reading of both
- **Core claim**: whatever the cellular story, it is a story about *access* and *state*, not
  about phenomenality. Neither DIT nor AA claims to close the explanatory gap; DIT's ten
  desiderata are all structural or functional properties (richness, integration, continuity,
  multidimensionality, latency, masking, attention, context-dependence).
- **Relation to site tenets**: this is the Map's position and it should be stated as the Map's
  reading of what DIT's authors themselves say, not as a defensive move against them.

## Key Debates

### Does the cellular mechanism replace the interface construct, or sit beneath it?
- **Sides**: the Gemini outer review argued replacement — DIT "completely obliterates" the
  interface reading, which is "empirically obsolete". Against that: DIT's own authors write "we
  are not claiming that no other theory can explain these properties", and Suzuki & Larkum's
  conclusion is stated conditionally ("**If** feedback signaling occurs predominantly through
  apical dendrites…").
- **Core disagreement**: whether a mechanism that specifies *when* consciousness is present
  thereby explains *that* it is present. This is the constrain-versus-establish gate the Map runs
  everywhere, and DIT is a clean instance: it constrains hard and establishes nothing.
- **Current state**: the reviewer's framing is not supported by the primary texts. **The finding
  survives; the rhetoric does not.** A Map article should hold DIT at full strength on its own
  terms, which are more modest and more interesting than the version the review supplied.

### Does thalamocortical gating control state only, or also select contents?
- **Sides**: DIT's authors pose this as their own open question. If gating is homogeneous across
  cortex it is a state switch; if it can act locally, down to columns, it selects contents too.
- **Current state**: explicitly unresolved by the authors in 2020, and Dykstra et al. (2026)
  still describe the human circuit-level evidence as largely absent.
- **Why the Map should care**: a *global* switch is much easier to redescribe as channel-width
  modulation than a *content-selective* one. The Map's interest in the answer is real and should
  be declared rather than hidden.

### Does the rodent result transfer to humans?
- **Sides**: the mechanism is established in mice by optogenetics and intracellular recording.
  Dykstra et al. (2026) argue human testing is now *possible*, which concedes it has not been
  done.
- **Current state**: open. This is the honest currency statement, and it is the reason the theory
  should be presented as the leading cellular hypothesis rather than as settled fact.

## Potential Article Angles

### Angle 1 — `concepts/dendritic-integration-theory` (the cellular tier)
Write DIT as the Map's entry for the **cellular level of description**, explicitly framed by the
Marvan et al. finding: this is the tier beneath `global-workspace-theory`,
`recurrent-processing-theory` and `integrated-information-theory`, all of which the Map already
covers and each of which AA claims to implement. That framing is what makes it a concepts article
rather than a paragraph, and it also makes it a *hub* the existing rival articles can link up to.
Tenet section writes itself from the constrain-versus-establish gate, and can cite the theory's
own disclaimer rather than asserting the limitation from outside. Include the anaesthesia
mechanism at full strength; the Map's position does not need it weakened.

### Angle 2 — the dreaming/hypnagogia extension {#angle-2}
`topics/hypnagogic-phenomenology-and-interface-modulation` was flagged 3/3 on 2026-08-21 for
confronting only the weakest deflationary rival. "Apical drive" (Aru, Siclari, Phillips & Storm
2020) is a *second* missing rival there, cellular rather than computational, and it targets
precisely the transition from externally to internally generated experience that the article
reads as interface modulation. Its sleep-onset research note explicitly deferred DIT to this
task; this discharges that pointer with a paper that note did not identify. **This is a distinct
finding from the anaesthesia one and is not covered by any open task.**

### Angle 3 — the Eccles convergence {#angle-3}
`concepts/penfield-interactionist-dualism` L52 already records that Eccles paired each *dendron*
— "a bundle of apical dendrites of pyramidal cells" — with a *psychon*. DIT puts consciousness at
apical/somatic coupling in the same cell class. Two frameworks with opposite metaphysics
converged on the same anatomy thirty years apart. This is a genuine and unclaimed observation,
and it cuts both ways honestly: it is weak evidence that the anatomy is special, and it is a
warning that anatomical convergence is cheap. Worth a paragraph, in whichever article lands; it
should not be oversold as support.

### Angle 4 — correcting the PRM void's label
`voids/perceptual-reality-monitoring-void` L94 calls the TiCS paper "the apical-amplification
framework". Small, real, and fixable in one sentence. Do this regardless of which route is taken.

**Framing to use in any insert** (keeps the Map's calibration and does not over-concede):

> Dendritic Integration Theory locates conscious processing in the gated coupling between the
> apical and somatic compartments of layer-5 pyramidal neurons, and its anaesthetic evidence is
> strong: three different anaesthetics converge on the same decoupling, and suppressing
> higher-order thalamus in the awake animal reproduces it. What the theory establishes is a
> condition under which conscious processing is present or absent. Its own authors are careful
> about the rest — "we are not claiming that no other theory can explain these properties" — and
> the *Cell* result is stated conditionally on feedback signalling running predominantly through
> apical dendrites. The mechanism therefore constrains any account of anaesthesia, including this
> one, without settling what makes the gated activity experienced.

**Do not write**: that DIT "falsifies" or "obliterates" the interface reading (that is the
reviewer's rhetoric, not the literature's); that the three anaesthetics were chosen for disparate
receptor targets (unverified); or that the erratum corrected the theory's name (unverified).

## Verdict {#verdict}

**Standalone `concepts/` article, with in-place discharge as a genuine fallback rather than a
consolation.** Three reasons for the standalone, in order of strength:

1. **It is a level of description the Map lacks entirely, not a missing rival.** The Marvan et
   al. finding means a DIT article would sit *under* five existing concepts articles rather than
   beside them — it gives the Map a cellular tier it has never had, and gives the existing rival
   articles something to link down to. A rivals paragraph inside the anaesthesia article cannot
   do that work.
2. **The subject reaches at least four articles** (anaesthesia, hypnagogia, biological
   computationalism, PRM void) across three sections. Material that has to be inserted in four
   places, in four framings, is material that wants one home and four links.
3. **The primary texts are richer than the reviews suggested**, and in a direction that favours
   the Map: DIT is more modest, more explicitly incomplete, and more interesting than the
   "empirically obsolete" framing. That asymmetry is worth writing up properly rather than
   compressing into a concession.

Against: `concepts/` has two slots and this competes for one; and Angle 2 (apical drive vs
hypnagogia) is arguably more urgent than the taxonomy, since it addresses a live 3/3 convergent
finding. **If only one thing is done, do Angle 2.** If a slot is available, Angle 1 is the better
long-run investment and Angles 2–4 become link-outs from it.

## Gaps in Research

- **The 2021 TiCS erratum's content is unknown.** Crossref, PubMed and Europe PMC expose only
  that it exists; cell.com 403s. The "Information"→"Integration" hypothesis is unconfirmed.
- **Suzuki & Larkum's methods were not read.** The three anaesthetic agents are reported from
  secondary search only. Anything depending on the specific agents needs the primary methods.
- **Aru, Larkum & Shine (2023) content is from summary, not the published text.** The arXiv
  preprint (arXiv:2306.00915) is open and would settle it.
- **No published critique of DIT was located.** Searches for objections returned generic
  hard-problem material and no DIT-specific rebuttal. This is an absence I could not convert into
  a positive finding, and it should be reported as "I did not find one", not as "there is none" —
  the searches may simply have missed a commentary in the *Phil. Mind Sci.* special issue, which
  is the natural place for one and which I did not enumerate.
- **The Storm et al. (2024) treatment of DIT specifically** was not read; only the abstract. That
  paper is the best single source on how DIT relates to the theories the Map already covers, and
  a downstream expand should read it in full.
