## Executive Summary

**Verdict.** Every published agent-causalist reply ends in the same primitive. None supplies a token-level ground for why the agent settled this way rather than that. Where the literature does supply modal structure, it supplies it at the type level: a propensity profile over the agent's exercises of her power. O'Connor's probability, which "simply measures relative likelihood" (as quoted in Bernáth & Tőzsér 2020), is the clearest case. That structure is exactly what van Inwagen's replay needs in order to run, and its compatibility with the agent's control over each token is contested (Bernáth & Tőzsér 2020). The Map's Born-preserving default puts the replay's premise exactly where its own law is missing. P-Q2 fixes frequencies "in every publicly conditionable context" and leaves "conditionals on the conscious state itself" free. A replay holds the whole prior state fixed, conscious state included, so it converges on precisely those free conditionals. The Map then faces the literature's fork at that grain. If it posits an objective conditional propensity, it grants van Inwagen's premise and meets the division-of-labour objection. If it denies one, it blocks the replay, but the Born-exact public marginal must then hold without being a mixture of conditional chances, which is Pereboom's wild coincidence. Both tines end at the law its Born-preservation apex says exact preservation needs, "a companion law over the distribution of conscious reasons", which the Map has never written. The modal structure that `topics/free-will` L94 says is "owed rather than supplied" therefore has a definite shape. It is that companion law, a law over the agent's conditional propensities (or over the conformity of propensity-free settlings). The token settling under it remains primitive on every account found. That is bedrock, and the article should say so in those words.

**Findings.**
1. **Van Inwagen's 2000 replay explicitly includes agent causation (verified raw, pp. 1, 16–18).** He grants agent causation "for the sake of argument", reruns the Alice replay with Alice agent-causing her brain-events in every replay, and concludes that "whether or not Alice's acts are the results of agent-causation—it is a mere matter of chance whether she will tell the truth or lie" (p. 17). His premise is an objective, "ground-floor" probability over the outcomes (p. 15).
2. **"Mind argument" is van Inwagen's label** ("which I have called the Mind Argument because it has appeared so frequently in the pages of *Mind*", 2000: 10). The earlier naming is the 1983 *Essay*, whose "Mind Argument" Fischer's 1988 review discusses. Fischer calls it the argument "(associated with Hobart)". Hobart 1934 (*Mind* 43) is the conventional first *Mind* instance. Nozick neither named nor first ran it. "Rollback argument" is a later label, and van Inwagen's own word is "replays".
3. **The replies fall into three families.** Bernáth & Tőzsér's 2020 taxonomy is verified raw.
   - *Accept the chance and call it harmless*: event-causal views (Kane, Balaguer, Franklin 2018).
   - *Deny robust probabilities for free choices*: Ginet, Lowe, Pink, with Buchak 2013 urging this on all libertarians.
   - *Keep robust probabilities and deny they make the act chancy*, because the agent has "dual control": O'Connor 2000, Clarke 2003, Griffith 2005.

   Archer 2022's grounding reply [lead] and Griffith 2010's "not fundamentally a problem of explanation" are versions of the third family. Franklin 2011 is the event-causal case that both arguments fail (abstract).
4. **P-Q2 leaves the Map's family open at exactly the grain the replay tests** (Born-exact frequencies in public contexts; "only conditionals on the conscious state itself are free"). Read with objective conditionals, the Map is in the third family, whose internal problem, the "division of labor" between probabilities and dual control, has the same structure as Pereboom's wild-coincidences objection. Read without them, it is in the second family, and the exact public marginal becomes that coincidence outright. The Map's apex already says its Born-preserving design "sits on" that objection. So the luck debt, the wild-coincidence debt and the companion-law debt are one debt (this note's synthesis; see the key deliverable below).
5. **Recommendation**: a new `concepts/rollback-argument-against-agent-causation` page of about 2,400 words. The three hubs it would otherwise join have 2–7 words of headroom (hard − 1 − count). The fallback fold is `topics/event-causal-libertarianism` at L80, with a budget of at most 250 words (headroom 867). See "Placement and Verdict" below.

## Answers to the Six Questions

### Q1. Does van Inwagen's 2000 replay explicitly include agent causation? YES (primary text, read raw in full)

Source: van Inwagen, "Free Will Remains a Mystery" (*Philosophical Perspectives* 14, 2000: 1–19), PDF at andrewmbailey.com/pvi. Page numbers match the journal's.

- **The stated project (p. 1).** "I will, however, present an argument for the conclusion that free will and indeterminism are incompatible even if our acts or their causal antecedents are products of agent causation. I see no way to respond to this argument." He assumes "for the sake of argument" that agent causation is possible and exists, and he expressly declines to argue that it is incoherent or unscientific.
- **His target (p. 11).** The project is "an argument for the conclusion that the concept of agent causation is entirely irrelevant to the problem of free will".
- **The agent-causal replay (pp. 16–17).**
  - He re-runs the replay: "Let us suppose that when Alice told the truth, she agent-caused certain" brain events that issued in her speech. In a replay in which she lies, she "must—according to the friends of agent causation—have been the agent-cause" of its antecedents too.
  - He grants the agent-causalist the Chisholm regress (p. 13: Reid is the agent-cause of his agent-causing, "and so ad infinitum"). He then asks whether the simultaneous occurrence of the whole infinite series is "a mere matter of chance".
  - His conclusion (p. 17): "whether or not Alice's acts are the results of agent-causation—it is a mere matter of chance whether she will tell the truth or lie".
- **The premise the argument runs on (p. 15).** After the replays converge on a ratio, "each of the two possible outcomes of this replay has an objective, 'ground-floor' probability of 0.5—and there's nothing more to be said". His closing example (pp. 17–18) gives the "ground-floor" probabilities as 0.43 and 0.57 and adds a divine revelation that the agent will be "the agent-cause of events in your brain" whichever way he acts. He finds the revelation irrelevant: if the argument has a flaw, "its flaw is not that it overlooks the possibility that my actions have their root in agent-causation" (p. 18).
- **Implication for the Map.** The replay needs a *probability*, and not merely openness. Van Inwagen infers the ground-floor probability from the convergent frequencies (p. 15), and his God reverts the universe "to precisely its state" before the choice (p. 14). For the Map that means the conscious state is held fixed as well, so the replay tests the conditionals on the conscious state that P-Q2 leaves free. This point is developed in the key deliverable below.
- **SEP's "van Inwagen 1983: 145"** (cited with 2000 in §3.2) was not read. Whether the 1983 *Essay* already applies the point to agent causation is a [lead]. Cite 2000 for the agent-causal replay.

## Key Deliverable: Does Any Reply Supply the Modal Structure?

**Short answer.** No reply supplies a token-level ground. Some replies supply type-level structure, and the Map's design leaves that structure unwritten at exactly the grain the replay tests.

### Two senses of "modal structure"

1. **Token level.** This is why this exercise went to A rather than B, given everything before it. Every family ends here in a primitive:
   - the agent's exercise "at the time of the choice—in the very act of bringing it about" (SEP Free Will);
   - "a matter of how Leo exercises his free will" (SEP §3.2);
   - "a basic capacity of just that sort" (O'Connor 2011, as quoted in Bernáth & Tőzsér);
   - "not fundamentally a problem of explanation" (Griffith 2010);
   - free acts that "could be inexplicable" (Griffith 2005 per Babakhanian);
   - free-choice facts that ground which world is actual (Archer 2022, [lead]).
   Archer's move, if the summary is right, inverts the order of explanation. It does not add a ground.
2. **Type level.** This is a profile constraining how the power is exercised across like circumstances. The literature does supply this:
   - O'Connor's objective probability, which "simply measures relative likelihood and serves to predict a distribution of outcomes" (as quoted in Bernáth & Tőzsér 2020);
   - Clarke's integrated view, in which reason-events co-cause with propensities;
   - Mele's history-shaped current probabilities.
   This is what the ChatGPT reviewer asked for ("dispositional or probabilistic constraints connecting the agent's reasons, character and attention to the exercise of the power").

### Why supplying type-level structure does not dissolve the objection

- **It is the replay's own premise.** Van Inwagen infers "objective, 'ground-floor'" probabilities from convergent replay frequencies (p. 15). A theory that supplies such probabilities over the agent's exercises supplies the premise. The probability-denying family (Ginet, Lowe, Pink; Buchak 2013 says the replay's chance premise "is totally unjustified"; Vicens 2016 finds no reason to posit "particular objective probabilities of our free choices") blocks the replay at exactly this step. It does so by refusing type-level structure.
- **Dual control and robust probabilities may be incompatible.** Bernáth & Tőzsér 2020 (*teorema* 39(2): 43–61; raw) argue "that metaphysically robust probabilities are incompatible with a kind of control which can ensure that free actions are not a matter of chance". Their reason: if the agent's dual control explains each token, it explains the pattern and hence the distribution, so the probabilities explain nothing. "This kind of 'division of labor' between agents and ground-floor probabilities is not possible." Their recommendation: "the libertarian should deny metaphysically robust probabilities with regard to free actions altogether if she still sticks to the idea that a high degree of control which can ensure that free actions are not a matter of chance is a condition of free action."
- **The field calls this open.** SEP Free Will (raw): "Why objective probabilities of this kind might present special problems beyond those posed by the absence of determinism has been insufficiently explored to date." It points to Vicens 2016 and O'Connor 2016, whose reply was not read.

### Where the Map sits (this note's synthesis; not a source claim)

- **The grain.** P-Q2 holds that outcome frequencies stay "Born-exact in every publicly conditionable context; only conditionals on the conscious state itself are free". Van Inwagen's God reverts "precisely" the prior state, so a replay of a Map-style selection holds the conscious state fixed too. What the replays converge on, if anything, is the conditional on the conscious state: the quantity P-Q2 leaves free. Physics fixes only the public marginal, the average of those conditionals over the states that actually occur.
- **The fork at that grain.**
  - *Objective conditionals (third family).* If the conditional on the conscious state is an objective propensity, van Inwagen's ground-floor premise holds at that grain and Bernáth & Tőzsér's division-of-labour objection applies. This is the reading the born-preserving apex's horn (a) needs, since it lets the conditionals differ from the marginal.
  - *No objective conditionals (second family).* If there is no chance profile conditional on the conscious state, the replay is blocked, as Lowe, Buchak and Vicens would block it. The acting-for-reasons note records, from Griffith's NDPR review, that Lowe says "We cannot do so with free choices (190-195)". But the Born-exact public marginal must then hold without being a mixture of conditional chances. Across many agents and occasions, propensity-free settlings would have to conform to the Born weights with nothing chance-based explaining the conformity.
  - Neither tine is closed to the Map by P-Q2 as stated. The second tine also gives a further reason, beyond the Lowe-misattribution P2s, why "close to Lowe" framings need scoping: borrowing Lowe's denial of probabilities would oblige the Map to say what then explains the Born-exact marginal.
- **Both tines meet the wild-coincidence problem.** Bernáth & Tőzsér's objection asks what explains the distribution if the agent settles every token. Pereboom's wild-coincidences charge asks the same question with physical statistical laws in place of agent-internal ones. On the first tine the question arises inside the agent's propensities; on the second it arises at the public marginal. The apex already says "the Map's Born-preserving design sits on" the wild-coincidence horn (`apex/consciousness-and-agency`, exclusion section).
- **The owed structure has a name in the Map already.** [[born-preserving-causal-efficacy]] says exact preservation "needs a companion law over the distribution of conscious reasons, and the Map has never written one". Under the default reading, the per-intention conditionals are "formally unconstrained". A written companion law would be the Map's type-level modal structure. On the first tine it is an O'Connor-style propensity profile over conscious states, constrained so that the public marginal stays Born-exact. On the second it is a law of conformity that makes the marginal's exactness lawful rather than coincidental.
- **So there is one debt, not three.** The modal structure the luck reply owes, the wild-coincidence cost and the companion-law debt are the same unwritten law. Discharging it would answer the ChatGPT reviewer's type-level demand. It would *not* touch the token-level primitive. If Bernáth & Tőzsér are right, it would sharpen the tension, because the more the law explains the distribution, the less is left for the agent's settling to explain.
- **Parity, for the blocked apex P2 on primitive parity.** On the first tine, the agent's settling and a primitive stochastic transition support the *same* counterfactuals about replays: the same frequencies, and the same unpredictability of the next token. On the second tine they differ only in that the settling has no frequency profile at all, which supports fewer counterfactuals, not more.
  - *What the agent-causal primitive claims beyond that*: a categorical fact about which thing is the source. P-A2 already says the difference is "metaphysical (which thing is the causal source)".
  - *This note's answer to the ChatGPT reviewer's "Why is one control and the other chance rather than merely being labelled differently?"*: on the sources found, the difference is categorical and not counterfactual. No reply converts it into a counterfactual difference. That is bedrock, and the article should say so plainly.
  - *What the materialism comparison then needs*: whether the Map's demand that materialism "explain rather than redescribe" (apex L173) survives this. That belongs to the blocked apex task. The literature here gives that task its premise: the Map's own primitive does not explain the token contrast either.

### What would change the verdict

- A reply that grounds the token in something other than the bare exercise without restoring determinism. None was found.
- A demonstration that dual control and robust probabilities coexist. O'Connor 2016 and the "influence" strategy that Bernáth & Tőzsér reject (pp. 52–54) are the candidates.
- A Map-side companion law that is both Born-preserving and motivated independently of the luck problem (`apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy` lists this as an open item).

## Major Positions

### Agent causation is irrelevant to luck (van Inwagen 2000; Mele 2005, 2006 ch. 3; Haji; Levy 2011 ch. 3)
- **Core claim**: present luck reapplies with the agent-cause in place.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral on dualism. It presses Tenet 3's agent-causal reading. The Map concedes it in substance (`free-will` L94, P-A2).

### Dual control with robust probabilities (O'Connor 2000, 2007, 2011; Clarke 2003, 2005; Griffith 2005; Archer 2022 [lead])
- **Core claim**: the probability measures likelihood. The agent's exercise at the time is the control. The cross-world difference is "how Leo exercises his free will".
- **Relation to tenets**: this is the Map's family if the conditionals P-Q2 leaves free are objective propensities, which the born-preserving apex's horn (a) needs. O'Connor is an emergentist and Clarke a sceptic, so no author in it is a dualist ally.

### Not fundamentally explanation (Griffith 2010)
- **Core claim**: luck is a matter of passivity, and agent-caused acts are active. Event-causal views fail; agent-causal ones have not been shown lucky.
- **Relation to tenets**: compatible with the Map's terminus. It does not supply structure.

### No robust probabilities for free choices (Ginet; Lowe 2008; Pink; Buchak 2013; Vicens 2016)
- **Core claim**: the replay's frequency premise is unjustified.
- **Relation to tenets**: it would block the replay at the conditional grain, and P-Q2 as stated does not close it. The price for the Map is the public marginal: Born-exact frequencies would have to arise from propensity-free settlings, which is the wild coincidence unless a conformity law is written.

### Chance is harmless when well placed (Kane; Balaguer; Franklin 2011, 2018; Mele 2006 ch. 5)
- **Core claim**: probability-raising reasons give contrastive explanation, or the luck argument's formulations fail.
- **Relation to tenets**: this is the event-causal rival. The contrastive resource it uses is the same one the Map's contrastive reply borrows.

## Key Debates

### Does agent causation add anything against luck?
- **Sides**: van Inwagen, Mele and Levy say no. O'Connor 2007 says Mele "has not made a persuasive case". Griffith says agent-caused actions "have not been shown to be lucky".
- **Current state**: unresolved. SEP §3.2 states the agent-causal reply only conditionally.

### Can robust probabilities and dual control coexist?
- **Sides**: O'Connor (division of labour) and Bernáth & Tőzsér (no); Vicens and O'Connor 2016 [lead].
- **Current state**: "insufficiently explored to date" (SEP Free Will).

### Is the Mind argument valid, and is it the Consequence Argument's twin?
- **Sides**: van Inwagen 1983 (valid iff the Consequence Argument is, per Finch 2017's abstract); Finch & Warfield 1998 (the Consequence Argument repairable and the Mind argument not); Finch 2017 (a formulation valid iff the Consequence Argument is); Franklin 2011 (it fails).
- **Current state**: the consequence-page P3 owns the twin point.
