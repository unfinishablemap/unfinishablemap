## Executive Summary

Charles Scott Sherrington (1857–1952) — coiner of "synapse," author of *The Integrative Action of the Nervous System* (1906), Nobel laureate 1932 (shared with Edgar Adrian), Waynflete Professor of Physiology at Oxford 1913–1936 — closed his career with the Gifford Lectures (Edinburgh, 1937–38), published as *Man on His Nature* (Cambridge University Press, 1940; entirely revised 2nd edition 1951 — the "Sherrington (1951)" of the SEP dualism entry). Its core doctrine is an **epistemic dualism of two ultimates**: the physics of his day resolved the natural world into forms of energy, and Sherrington argued the mind is not found among them — "The energy-concept of Science collects all so-called 'forms' of energy into a flock and looks in vain for the mind among them." He held that natural science delivers only a "gross correlation in time and space" between thoughts and brain, and (in the 1947 foreword to *Integrative Action*, not in *Man on His Nature* — a standing misattribution trap) that two fundamental elements are no more inherently improbable than one. Crucially, and unlike his students, Sherrington's dualism is **agnostic and negative**: a verdict about where natural science runs out, not a positive interaction mechanism. The lineage the Map already cites (SEP: Sherrington → Penfield → Eccles) is genuinely a teacher-student lineage — Penfield trained under Sherrington at Oxford from 1915, Eccles took his DPhil under Sherrington in 1929 and co-authored eight papers with him — and it terminates in the Beck–Eccles quantum-exocytosis proposal, the Map's own congenial mechanism family. Critical literature (Arshavsky 2006; Smith 2001) reads the lineage's dualism as religiously and culturally rooted rather than evidence-driven; an article must state that critique at full strength and keep Sherrington's own verdict-direction (agnostic, not confessional) clearly distinct from Eccles's overtly religious substance dualism.

## Key Sources

### Man on His Nature (primary subject)
- **Publisher**: Cambridge University Press, 1940 (Gifford Lectures, Edinburgh 1937–38; also Macmillan, New York 1941 printing, 413 pp per the Psychoanalytic Quarterly's 1941 review listing). 2nd edition 1951, "entirely revised and reset" per the [Cambridge University Press page](https://www.cambridge.org/universitypress/subjects/philosophy/philosophy-general-interest/man-his-nature-2nd-edition) (page snippet via search; direct fetch 403'd).
- **Type**: Book (primary)
- **Structure**: A sustained dialogue between modern physiology and the 16th-century physician Jean Fernel, framed as natural theology (Gifford remit). Chapter titles attested via quote locators: ch. 6 "A Whole Presupposed of its Parts"; ch. 7 "The Brain and its Work"; ch. 9 "Brain Collaborates with Psyche"; ch. 11 "Two Ways of One Mind" (chapter numbering shifts in the 1951 edition — Wikiquote notes ch. 9 becomes ch. 10).
- **Key points**:
  - The natural world resolves into forms of energy; mind does not appear among them, so science confronts **two ultimates** it cannot reduce to one.
  - The mind–brain relation is, for natural science, bare correlation — no mechanism, not even the beginning of one.
  - The brain as a "million-fold democracy whose each unit is a cell" (PARAPHRASE via Wikipedia; exact wording and locator unverified) — integration without a central executive.
  - Reads throughout as reverent agnosticism, not confessional theism; the Sunday Times blurb ("one of the landmarks in the history of man's speculation about his own place in the universe") marks its reception as a humanist classic.
- **Tenet alignment**: Aligns with Tenet 1 (irreducibility) in epistemic form; supplies Tenet 5's parsimony point (see 1947 foreword below); does NOT by itself assert Tenet 3 (interaction) — Sherrington states the correlation and stops.
- **Quotes** (with verification level):
  - "The energy-concept of Science collects all so-called 'forms' of energy into a flock and looks in vain for the mind among them." **[PR-SECONDARY]** — transcribed in Pepperell 2018 (below), cited to Sherrington 1940, no page given.
  - "Is the mind in any strict sense energy?" / "…thoughts, feelings, and so on are not amenable to the energy (matter) concept." **[PR-SECONDARY]** — Pepperell 2018, cited to Sherrington 1940.
  - "The brain is waking and with it the mind is returning. It is as if the Milky Way entered upon some cosmic dance. Swiftly the head-mass becomes an enchanted loom where millions of flashing shuttles weave a dissolving pattern, always a meaningful pattern though never an abiding one." **[AGGREGATOR]** — Wikiquote and todayinsci concur: 1940 ed., p. 225 (ch. 7). The single most famous image in 20th-century neuroscience prose; ancestor of the Map's loom/filter/interface imagery.

  - "As followers of natural science we know nothing of any relation between thoughts and the brain, except as a gross correlation in time and space." **[AGGREGATOR]** — Wikiquote (ch. 9, p. 290) and todayinsci (p. 290) concur on wording.
  - "Biology cannot go far in its subject without being met by mind." **[AGGREGATOR — WORDING VARIES]**: todayinsci has "without being met"; Wikiquote has "before being met." Do not quote until resolved against print (pp. 290–291).

### The Integrative Action of the Nervous System, 1947 reissue Foreword (primary; the misattribution trap)
- **Publisher**: Cambridge University Press reissue, 1947 (foreword dated June 1947, written for the Physiological Society's republication); original book Yale/Scribner 1906.
- **Type**: Book foreword (primary — Sherrington's most explicit late dualist statement)
- **Key points**: The two most-quoted "dualist Sherrington" sentences live HERE, not in *Man on His Nature*, though secondary sources routinely misattribute them:
  - "That our being should consist of two fundamental elements offers I suppose no greater inherent improbability than that it should rest on one only." **[AGGREGATOR]** — todayinsci: 1947 Foreword, p. xx. (Punctuation varies across carriers: some print "offers, I suppose, no greater…".) This is the **Tenet 5 anchor**: an explicit refusal to let monist parsimony decide an open question.
  - "we have to regard the relation of mind to brain as still not merely unsolved, but still devoid of a basis for its very beginning" **[AGGREGATOR/scholarly echoes]** — same foreword; multiply attested in the secondary literature (e.g., the PMC survey "How to house a mind inside a brain," https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3327533/).
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1 (two elements) and Tenet 5 (parsimony has no purchase here) directly; honest about the interaction gap (the "basis for its very beginning" line concedes the interface problem the Map's Tenet 2 tries to address at the quantum level).

## Major Positions

### Sherrington's epistemic two-ultimates dualism
- **Core claim**: Natural science resolves nature into energy; mind is not among energy's forms; therefore the thinking subject confronts two irreducibles, with their relation not merely unsolved but lacking any basis for a solution.
- **Verdict-direction (the originating review demanded this be settled)**: **Agnostic-epistemic, not confessional-ontological.** Sherrington asserts a limit of natural science and declines to convert it into a theology or a mechanism. The Nature-search summary formulation — he "continued to believe throughout his life that dualism was as reasonable an assumption to make as was monism" — captures the modality: *as reasonable as*, not *proven*. Any Map article should say plainly: Sherrington is the lineage's agnostic pole.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Aligns with Tenet 1 read epistemically; Tenet 5 directly (parsimony can't settle it); silent on Tenets 2–3 (no mechanism, no asserted interaction); nothing on Tenet 4.

### Penfield's clinical interactionism (already covered)
- See [[penfield-interactionist-dualism]] and the 2026-07-12 research note. Role in the lineage article: the middle node — Sherrington's student who supplied *clinical* evidence where the teacher had only the conceptual gap. Mystery of the Mind (1975) = SEP's "Penfield (1975)".

### Eccles's escalating interactionism: liaison brain → trialism → psychons
- **Core claim**: Mind is a distinct entity that acts on the brain — first via the "liaison brain," then within Popper's three-worlds trialism, finally via quantum-probabilistic action on exocytosis (Beck–Eccles) with psychons paired to dendrons.
- **Key arguments**: unity of experience vs distributed brain; the self's continuity; quantum indeterminacy as the causal opening (no energy-conservation violation claimed at the level of probability amplitudes).
- **Verdict-direction**: The lineage's opposite pole from Sherrington — explicitly religious substance dualism (divine creation of each soul). The teacher's agnosticism became the student's confession; an article gains credibility by displaying the gradient rather than flattening it.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Beck–Eccles is the direct ancestor of the Map's Tenet 2 (minimal quantum interaction) and Tenet 3; but the Map's tenets do not require Eccles's theology, and the article should decouple them explicitly.

### The critics' counter-position
- **Proponents**: Ritchie (1941), Arshavsky (2006), Smith (2001), Pepperell (2018) — different generations, same family of replies.
- **Core claims**: (a) nothing incorporeal shows up in the biology (Ritchie); (b) the dualism has extra-scientific roots and functions as an obstacle (Arshavsky — abstract-level; verify full text); (c) the quantum move does not solve Descartes' problem, it relocates it (Smith); (d) mind is the organization of energy, dissolving the two-ultimates argument (Pepperell).
- **Relation to site tenets**: The standing opposition; Tenet 5 is the Map's principled reply to (d)-style dissolutions, and the interface-problem material is its reply to (c).

## Key Debates

### Is "distinguished dissent" evidence?
- **Sides**: The lineage is routinely cited (including by SEP, and by the Map's Penfield article) as showing dualism remained scientifically respectable; critics reply that three eminent dissenters against a consensus is sociology, not evidence, and that their eminence was in synaptic physiology, not in adjudicating metaphysics.
- **Current state**: Ongoing. An article should use the lineage as *history and provenance of arguments* (the loom image, the stimulation data, the quantum proposal), never as an authority count. This is also the honest frame for the Map's own reliance on the SEP sentence.

### Agnostic limit-claim vs positive interactionism
- **Sides**: Sherrington (limit-claim) vs Penfield/Eccles (positive interaction) — a within-lineage debate usually papered over.
- **Core disagreement**: whether "science finds only correlation" licenses asserting a second causally active element, or only suspension of judgment.
- **Current state**: The Map's own architecture (Tenet 1 + Tenet 2 as *minimal* mechanism) is best presented as taking Sherrington's gap seriously and Eccles's mechanism minimally — without Eccles's theology.

### Did the quantum move solve Sherrington's "no basis for a beginning"?
- **Sides**: Beck–Eccles (yes, at exocytosis probability) vs Smith 2001 and mainstream (no — relocated, not solved).
- **Current state**: Live; the Map's interface-problem and psychophysical-control-law articles are exactly this debate's home turf.
