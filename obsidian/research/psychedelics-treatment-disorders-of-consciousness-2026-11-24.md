## Executive Summary

**Verdict: `fold` into [[entropic-brain-hypothesis]], as a new closing paragraph of "Where the Measure Is Contested" (~154 words + ~81 words of references).** Both sections that could host a standalone page are full (concepts 370/370, topics 370/370). The subject does not need a page of its own anyway. It is a single clinical test of the claim that EBH's single scalar tracks conscious level, and the EBH page already holds the measurement debate it belongs to.

What the literature shows. Scott and Carhart-Harris (2019, *Neurosci Conscious* niz003, an "Opinion Paper") proposed testing psilocybin in vegetative and minimally conscious patients on the ground that brain complexity indexes conscious level. They named their own falsifier: if complexity could rise "without increasing conscious awareness", that "would negate our hypothesis". The critique is Peterson, Tagliazucchi and Weijer (2019, *Neurosci Conscious* niz013), also an "Opinion Paper". It is an **ethics analysis** that applies the Value-Validity Framework and Component Analysis. Its scientific-validity section argues that LZC is not a clinically validated index for DoC and that ketamine raises complexity in unresponsive participants. That ketamine argument is the source of the objection already on the EBH page (L62), which reaches the page through Rankaduwa and Owen (2023). The first patient data came out in 2025. In one woman in a minimally conscious state, Lempel–Ziv complexity rose after an unblinded dose of psilocybin, while her bedside scores dropped to unresponsive wakefulness and showed no gain on validated scales. The authors, with Carhart-Harris among them, read her as "disconnected" rather than unconscious. They also named the *opposite* dissociation as the one that would have falsified the hypothesis. By 2026 Carhart-Harris ties EBH to "phenomenal consciousness" and suggests that such a case could "add validity to the two-component model".

For the Map, the critique and the defence read **one pattern in opposite ways**: complexity goes up while responsiveness goes down. So both depend on the premise the Map's disruption page says a materialist must surrender, namely whether unresponsiveness marks absent experience. Neither side has supplied the independent test (covert command-following) that the 2019 proposal itself said might be needed. None of this discriminates for dualism.

## Key Sources

### Scott & Carhart-Harris (2019), "Psychedelics as a treatment for disorders of consciousness"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niz003 (PMC6475593, PMID 31024740; CC BY)
- **Type**: Opinion Paper, *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2019(1), niz003. Authors (Crossref): Gregory Scott; Robin L. Carhart-Harris. Scott is the lead author ("G.S.: conceptualization, drafting of and revision of the manuscript"), so do not call it "Carhart-Harris's proposal" alone.
- **Key points**:
  - The rationale rests on "the twin discoveries that measures of brain complexity reliably index conscious level, and that brain complexity can be increased by psychedelics". The evidence is PCI (Casali et al. 2013), spontaneous-EEG LZC in DoC (Sitt et al. 2014), and Schartner et al. (2017a), on which Carhart-Harris is second author.
  - It does not claim that complexity is causal: "Note that this hypothesis does not require that brain complexity be the cause of conscious awareness." Complexity is an "explanatory correlate" in Seth & Edelman's sense.
  - **Self-stated falsifier**: "a key question for our proposal is whether it is possible to increase measures of brain complexity without increasing conscious awareness. If it were possible, then this would negate our hypothesis and call into doubt the relationship between consciousness and brain complexity, at least as we define it here."
  - They read LZC as tracking *content*, not arousal: "Studies of impaired consciousness suggest that LZC and related measures of complexity chiefly index conscious content rather than arousal". They also say "the evidence overwhelmingly suggests that psychedelic-related elevations in LZC or information entropy ... reflect an increased richness of conscious experience".
  - They frame the proposal as a test of unidimensionality: "we see our proposal (to explore psychedelics as a treatment in DoC) as a challenge to the unidimensional conception of conscious level as indexed by brain complexity".
  - On outcome measures: "behavioural indices like the Coma Recovery Scale - Revised (CRS-R) must ultimately be used as the primary outcome measure". The caveat follows: "it may be necessary in fact to test for covert awareness, i.e. identifying consciousness in the absence of any behavioural response" (citing Owen et al. 2006).
  - The proposed sequence is sedated healthy volunteers, then non-REM sleep, then a dose-escalation study in DoC patients. A historical precedent is cited: Apter (1958) reported that LSD reversed pentobarbital sedation in cats (lead, not checked).
  - Conflict statement: "R.L.C.-H. is currently advising Compass Pathways".
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. It is a physicalist-framed clinical hypothesis. The "explanatory correlate" hedge leaves it compatible with a filter reading.
- **Quotes**: all verbatim. Each has one hit in the raw PMC efetch text after tag stripping, `html.unescape` and whitespace collapse, with a nonsense control at 0.

### Peterson, Tagliazucchi & Weijer (2019), "The ethics of psychedelic research in disorders of consciousness"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niz013 (PMC6785661, PMID 31616570; CC BY-NC)
- **Type**: Opinion Paper, *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2019(1), niz013. Authors (Crossref): Andrew Peterson (George Mason; Rotman Institute), Enzo Tagliazucchi (Buenos Aires/CONICET), Charles Weijer (Western). It is **not** labelled a reply or commentary. It is a free-standing opinion paper that analyses Scott and Carhart-Harris's programme by name: "We focus on Scott and Carhart-Harris's proposal". Cite it as a response to that paper, and as an ethics analysis rather than a critique of EBH.
- **Key points**:
  - It is broadly supportive in tone: "We applaud Scott and Carhart-Harris for seeking out innovative therapies for DoC patients." It finds clinical and social value in the proposal.
  - **Scientific validity**: "to our knowledge, an LZC value is not a clinically-validated index for classifying DoC patients". So validity "would thus hang on the strength of associations between an LZC value and a clinical diagnosis".
  - **The ketamine objection (the source of EBH L62)**: "brain complexity can increase, rather than decrease, when a participant is unconscious". Subanaesthetic ketamine raises LZC (Schartner 2017). "an increase in PCI values has also been elicited in participants with anesthetic doses of ketamine, which render participants entirely unresponsive (Sarasso et al. 2015)". They conclude: "This raises the question as to whether a reliable baseline complexity-based metric can even be established to assess the effect of a drug."
  - **Note the bridge premise.** The objection equates "entirely unresponsive" with "unconscious". Sarasso et al. (2015), the paper cited for it, read the same data as possible disconnected consciousness (see below).
  - Psilocybin "should be regarded as a nontherapeutic procedure for DoC patients" early on, so the minimal-risk threshold applies (Declaration of Helsinki ¶28).
  - There is a disanalogy between the controls and the patients: "The mechanisms underlying the loss of consciousness in the former are likely to be distinct from those affected in the latter" (sedated or sleeping healthy volunteers versus brain injury).
  - On the risk profile, they assert flatly that "VS patients are not conscious and thus—at least, initially—would not be at risk of adverse psychological events". That assertion leans on the same premise; Rankaduwa and Owen later qualify it with the covert-awareness literature. They add: "Not knowing that a participant is experiencing an adverse psychological event makes this risk worse."
  - On the DBS precedent: "This is a bad argument, for it assumes that the investigational implantation of DBS electrodes in DoC patients is, in fact, ethical."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Its ketamine argument silently uses the behavioural-absence premise that the Map rejects (disruption L104).
- **Quotes**: all verbatim, one hit each in PMC efetch text. **Control**: the phrase "fully compatible with overall reductions in conscious awareness" has **0 hits** in Peterson et al. It is Rankaduwa and Owen's wording and must not be quoted as Peterson's.

### Rankaduwa & Owen (2023), "Psychedelics, entropic brain theory, and the taxonomy of conscious states"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niad001 (PMC10072236)
- **Type**: Review Article, *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2023(1), niad001. This is the secondary source that carries the exchange onto the EBH page.
- **Key points**:
  - Their relay of Peterson reads: "To put further pressure on indices of complexity, Peterson et al. (2019) highlight that increases in both LZC (Schartner et al. 2017a) and PCI (Sarasso et al. 2015) values have been observed in cases of low and anesthetic doses of ketamine; contrary to Scott and Carhart-Harris's (2019) assumptions, an increase in complexity seems to be fully compatible with overall reductions in conscious awareness."
  - **The target is narrower than the EBH page says.** R&O wrote "contrary to Scott and Carhart-Harris's (2019) assumptions". EBH L62 says "contrary to what the entropy literature generally assumes". The page has widened a claim about one paper into one about a literature (see [[#defects|Defects noticed]]).
  - They add a set-and-setting argument and draw on covert awareness: "Research on covert awareness in this population, however, seems to suggest that a considerable proportion of patients are able to retain some residual awareness". They are "not in principle opposed to Scott and Carhart-Harris's (2019) proposal".
  - They attribute the "raising" question to Carhart-Harris (2018): "could psychedelics then offer a way of effectively 'raising' a DOC patient's level of consciousness (Carhart-Harris 2018)?" Whether Carhart-Harris (2018) itself floats DoC treatment is a **lead, not checked**.
- **Quotes**: verbatim, one hit each.

### Sarasso et al. (2015), "Consciousness and Complexity during Unresponsiveness Induced by Propofol, Xenon, and Ketamine"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2015.10.014 (PMID 26752078); *Current Biology* 25(23), 3099–3105. Checked at **abstract level only**.
- **Key points**: In 18 healthy volunteers, TMS-evoked responses were low-complexity under propofol and xenon and showed "a wakefulness-like, complex spatiotemporal activation pattern during ketamine anesthesia". After ketamine, subjects "reported long, vivid dreams unrelated to the external environment". Their conclusion: "brain complexity may be sensitive to the presence of disconnected consciousness in subjects who are considered unconscious based on behavioral responses".
- **Bearing**: "Wakefulness-like" means PCI is preserved under anaesthetic ketamine relative to the other agents. It does not rise above waking. Peterson's phrase "an increase in PCI values" stretches this, and EBH L62 inherits the stretch.

### Farnes et al. (2020) and Ort et al. (2023): spontaneous versus evoked complexity
- Farnes, Juel, Nilsen, Romundstad & Storm (2020), *PLoS One* 15(11), e0242056 (PMID 33226992; abstract). In 10 volunteers, "no significant difference was found in TMS-evoked complexity (PCI) between the sub-anaesthetic ketamine condition and normal wakefulness", while LZc, ACE and SCE all rose. The authors read this as follows: "sub-anaesthetic ketamine may increase the complexity of the conscious content and the brain activity underlying it, while the level or general capacity for consciousness remains largely unaffected."
- Ort et al. (2023), *iScience* 26(5), 106589 (PMID 37138774; abstract). They report that psilocybin "produces a state of increased chaotic brain activity which is not a result of altered complexity in the underlying causal interactions between brain regions". Cardone et al. (2024) summarise both papers as showing increases in LZC "but not evoked-complexity (PCI)". The PCI-null reading of Ort rests on that summary plus the abstract wording; the PCI values themselves were not seen.
- **Bearing**: the two measures behind the 2019 rationale come apart under the drugs proposed. Spontaneous LZC rises. Evoked PCI, the DoC gold standard, does not rise at subanaesthetic doses and stays wake-like at anaesthetic ketamine.

### Cardone et al. (2024), "Psychedelics and disorders of consciousness: the current landscape and the path forward"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niae025 (PMC11179162); *Neurosci Conscious* 2024(1), niae025. Coma Science Group, Liège.
- **Key points**: The paper addresses the LZC/PCI split above and defends using LZC as an index of richness. It poses "resonance versus creation": do psychedelics need an already-complex (MCS) brain? It predicts that EMCS patients would show "an increase of complexity without necessarily a richer behavioural repertoire", which decouples complexity from behaviour in advance. It also proposes "an optimal amount of complexity", above which cognition might be impaired in a damaged brain. On ethics, it agrees with Peterson et al. and with Rankaduwa & Owen.

### Cardone et al. (2025), "Psilocybin for disorders of consciousness: A case-report study" (THE KEY DATUM)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2025.02.264 (PMID 40147181). *Clinical Neurophysiology* 173, 181–189. Authors: Cardone, Núñez, Alnagger, Martial, van der Lande, Sandell, **Carhart-Harris**, Gosseries. Full text was read from the ORBi copy, CC BY-NC-ND.
- **Facts (verbatim-verified in the PDF text)**:
  - "41-year-old woman diagnosed with MCS+ one-year post-traumatic brain injury". She received psilocybin at home in Colorado, where it is decriminalised.
  - "The patient was not naive to psilocybin, as she experienced it once before suffering from DoC." Before the session she had microdoses and smaller doses (highest 1.35 g).
  - The caregiver gave "a single dose of 2.5 g liquid tincture (estimate psilocybin: 25 mg) via the gastric tube". "As this was an observational study, the authors had no role in deciding the dose that was given." "the drug administration was not blind and there was no placebo condition". The Liège ethics committee "could not offer an opinion" because the session took place outside Belgium, but did not object to publication.
  - Abstract: "We report no increase in overt behavioural repertoire with validated scales, yet new spontaneous behaviour not previously seen, and increased brain complexity". LZC went from a mean of 0.30 before to 0.47 after (t18 = 6.64).
  - Behaviour. Before the dose she scored MCS+ on SECONDs (score 6: she responded to "Look up"). After the dose, "the patient was diagnosed with UWS (eyes opened: score of 1) in both SECONDs" and "the patient was unresponsive for the first two SECONDs assessments" (the second at +2h30). Later assessments returned MCS− with visual pursuit. The new spontaneous behaviour was lifting both legs; the right leg had not been lifted before without psilocybin.
  - The authors' reading: "If that was the case [psilocybin pushing her into UWS], psilocybin would act in DoC as an anaesthetic which, given the information we have, is extremely unlikely." "it is more probable that the patient was just disconnected from the environment, meaning that there was an internal experience, but it was detached from the world or unable to interact with it". Also: "there is the possibility that psilocybin induced a state of disconnected consciousness, rather than unconsciousness."
  - **The falsifier inverts.** "While we expected a higher state of consciousness, which would have resulted in a more varied behavioural repertoire, our findings do not disprove the hypothesis that brain complexity and consciousness are tightly linked." Then: "Had we observed richer behaviour with no substantial change in brain complexity, this would have falsified our hypothesis." Compare the 2019 condition (complexity up *without* awareness up).
  - Measures used: SECONDs, NCS-R, four resting EEGs and an auditory oddball paradigm (oddball results are in an appendix that was not retrieved). **No covert command-following** (motor-imagery fMRI or EEG) was run; a grep for `command|imagery|fMRI|covert|CMD` found only SECONDs command items and reference titles.
  - Limitations the authors list: a single case; no blinding or placebo; possible muscle artefact in gamma power; one dose only; possible habituation from earlier doses; music played only after intake (a confound for LZC); diagnosis fluctuates from day to day.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The authors' "disconnected" reading is the same move as the Map's disruption L104. The Map can agree with the logic, but cannot count the case as evidence for EBH (see [[#map-question|below]]).

### Patient data on ketamine and esketamine (corroborating pattern; abstracts only)
- Cardone et al. (2025b), "A pilot human study using ketamine to treat disorders of consciousness", *iScience* 28(1), 111639 (PMC11780106). Three patients, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover, subanaesthetic IV ketamine. The abstract reports "increased LZC during ketamine infusion but no change in ECI", plus more time with eyes open and reduced spastic paresis, but "no positive change in diagnosis".
- Liang et al. (2026), *J Neurosurg Anesthesiol*, doi:10.1097/ANA.0000000000001124 (PMID 42290311; NCT06473285). Twenty-two prolonged-DoC patients were analysed after esketamine infusion. LZC rose in parietal and occipital regions and the rise persisted in MCS patients. "No behavioral improvements were observed on the Coma Recovery Scale-Revised assessments."
- **Pattern across three datasets (1 + 3 + 22 patients)**: complexity rises and behavioural diagnosis does not improve. Ketamine and esketamine are "atypical" psychedelics, and all three studies are acute and small.

### Modelling, trials and reviews (leads; abstracts only)
- Mindlin et al. (2024), *Commun Biol* 7, 1176 (PMC11413186). In whole-brain models, serotonergic and opioid stimulation shifted DoC models "towards a dynamical behaviour associated with a healthier state". **Tagliazucchi**, a co-author of Peterson et al., is an author.
- Alnagger et al. (2026), "A Virtual Clinical Trial of Psychedelics to Treat Patients With DoC", *Adv Sci* 13(11), e11780 (PMC12931207; Carhart-Harris and Nutt among the authors). Simulated LSD and psilocybin "shifted the brain activity of patients with DoC closer to criticality", with a greater effect in MCS.
- Eigenbrodt et al. (2026), *BMJ Open* 16(9), e124508 (PMC13599874). This is the protocol for an authorised phase 1b open-label trial in 80 ICU patients, targeting "arousal with apomorphine and awareness with psilocybin" at doses of 1, 10 and 25 mg. "Primary outcome is time to awakening within 30 days." Last author Kondziella. Results are pending.
- Du, Peng & Wang (2026), *Mol Psychiatry*, doi:10.1038/s41380-026-03918-4 (perspective): "controlled clinical evidence demonstrating sustained restoration of consciousness remains absent".
- Moses, Doss & Tagliazucchi (2026), "A critical review of brain entropy as a biomarker of the psychedelic state", *Neurosci Biobehav Rev* 106720. **Metadata only, no abstract retrieved.** It may matter for EBH in general and should be the next lead for the EBH page.

### Carhart-Harris (2026), "The entropic brain today"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag206 (PMC13634631); *Brain* 149(10), 3362–3378. Full text read.
- **Key points**:
  - It restates EBH "along a dimension of 'the size of phenomenal consciousness'".
  - On the case report: "Novel behaviours, such as never-seen-before bilateral leg-lifts were observed ... However, no improvements in standard assessments of conscious level were observed, either acutely or sub-acutely, thereby tempering optimism for the hypothesis." He describes the dose as "psilocybe ('magic') mushrooms" and the study as "an uncontrolled experiment". That matches the report's "liquid tincture", but note the wording.
  - **The relocation**: "One critical angle to consider here is that the EBH relates most closely to 'phenomenal consciousness'". The enrichment "could occur with this intervention, but in the absence of an adjacent improvement in reflective awareness or 'access consciousness'". Such a failure could "add validity to the two-component model of consciousness" and "imply that greater effort is needed to achieve the translation from one (phenomenal) to the other (access)".
  - On ketamine, he describes an inverse-U in rodents and cats: "Entropy decreases when the dosage of ketamine is sufficiently high to cause unconsciousness." That is spontaneous-signal entropy. It does not answer Sarasso's wake-like *PCI* under anaesthetic ketamine in humans.

## Major Positions

### Complexity restoration (Scott & Carhart-Harris 2019; Cardone, Gosseries, Martial and colleagues)
- **Core claim**: DoC brains have low complexity, psychedelics raise complexity, so psychedelics may raise conscious awareness.
- **Arguments**: the PCI and LZC ordering across VS, MCS and EMCS; complexity under psychedelics exceeding waking levels; 5-HT2A density in the DMN and in layer-5 pyramidal cells; plasticity.
- **Relation to tenets**: The empirical claim is neutral between production and filter readings. On a filter reading, complexity is a mark of interface bandwidth, and pharmacologically raising the mark need not restore the channel (the interface's output to behaviour) if the channel is damaged. The production theorist says the same with different nouns.

### Ethics-and-validity critique (Peterson, Tagliazucchi & Weijer 2019; extended by Rankaduwa & Owen 2023)
- **Core claim**: the proposal has value, but its endpoint (LZC) is not clinically validated. Complexity can rise in unresponsive (on their reading, unconscious) participants. Healthy-control models are mechanistically unlike brain injury. Risk to non-communicative patients is hard to detect.
- **Relation to tenets**: the ketamine step assumes that unresponsiveness means unconsciousness, a premise the Map rejects. The validation, disanalogy and detection points stand regardless.

### Multidimensional critique (Bayne, Hohwy & Owen; Rankaduwa & Owen 2023)
- **Core claim**: "levels" of consciousness are an inadequate frame, and complexity measures do not map onto specific dimensions. Scott and Carhart-Harris themselves accept the proposal as a test of this.
- **Relation to tenets**: consistent with the Map's Occam's-limits caution about single scalars (EBH page L84).

### The disconnection and phenomenal/access defence (Cardone et al. 2025; Carhart-Harris 2026)
- **Core claim**: complexity up with behaviour flat or down is possible disconnected experience, or phenomenal without access consciousness. It is not a falsification.
- **Relation to tenets**: logically the same move as disruption L104, which the Map endorses. Its cost is that behaviour can no longer test the hypothesis; only covert command-following or later report can.

## Key Debates

### Has the falsifier moved?
- **Sides**: the 2019 proposal says complexity up without awareness up "would negate our hypothesis". The 2025 report says complexity up with no behavioural gain does "not disprove" it, and that the converse, richer behaviour with no complexity change, "would have falsified" it.
- **Core disagreement**: whether "conscious awareness" in 2019 meant behaviourally assessed awareness (CRS-R as primary outcome) or experience (the covert-awareness caveat). The 2019 text contains both, so "moved goalposts" is too strong. The defensible statement is narrower. The behavioural form of the 2019 falsifier has now occurred, and the response has been to read the falsifier as being about experience, which behaviour cannot check.
- **Current state**: open. No study has used covert command-following. The Copenhagen ICU trial (primary outcome time to awakening) will produce behavioural data, not a test of covert experience.

### The responsiveness/experience hinge
- **Sides**: Peterson et al. read ketamine's complexity in unresponsive participants as complexity without consciousness. Sarasso et al., Cardone et al. and Carhart-Harris read the same pattern as disconnected experience.
- **Core disagreement**: whether behavioural unresponsiveness marks absent experience.
- **Current state**: the same hinge that the open EBH L62/L66 task and disruption L104 address. Bajwa et al. (2025), the subject of the sibling research task "Neural complexity measures versus reported experience within one anaesthetic or psychedelic state", supply the within-state report test that bears on it. Leave that material to the sibling note.

### Spontaneous LZC versus evoked PCI
- PCI is the validated DoC instrument, and psychedelics do not raise it in healthy participants at the tested doses. LZC is what rises. The proposal's endpoint is the measure that is less validated for DoC, which is Peterson et al.'s point, sharpened by later data.

### Does the ethical risk calculus depend on the same hinge?
- Peterson et al.: "VS patients are not conscious" initially, so they carry no psychological risk at first. Rankaduwa & Owen: covert awareness means some are, and a "bad trip" could go undetected. If the disconnected-consciousness reading of the case report is right, that patient may have had an unreportable psychedelic experience for over two hours. The ethics and the science turn on one question. (A possible link to [[ethics-under-dualism]]; not proposed for this fold.)

## The Map-Specific Question {#map-question}

**What each side assumes about complexity.** The 2019 proposal assumes that complexity tracks conscious level and content, and that raising the mark pharmacologically can raise what it marks. The critique assumes that behaviour is the arbiter of consciousness, at least where it turns against complexity (ketamine). The defence assumes that complexity can be the arbiter when behaviour turns against it (the case report).

**Where the Map stands, without overclaiming.**
1. The Map's own disruption argument (L104) says behavioural unresponsiveness cannot mark conscious absence. Consistency therefore puts the Map closer to the defence's logic than to the critique's ketamine step. The Map should say so and not use Peterson et al. as an ally against EBH on that point. The open EBH L62/L66 task makes this repair for the existing ketamine sentences.
2. The Map should **not** let the case count as evidence for EBH. The authors infer "an internal experience" from rising complexity plus the theory: the alternative is "extremely unlikely" because psilocybin would then "act ... as an anaesthetic". That inference uses the hypothesis to interpret the datum. Unless an independent channel is used (covert command-following, a later report, within-state report tests), a case where complexity and responsiveness diverge cannot confirm the measure either way.
3. **Filter/interface reading.** Read through [[filter-theory]], the case looks like a channel widening while its output stays shut, or even closes further. That fits the Map's picture of an interface whose bandwidth and output can come apart. But Carhart-Harris's two-component (phenomenal/access) gloss gives the physicalist the same description, and the covert-consciousness page's physicalist reply ("The production argument never rested on behaviour") applies directly. **No discrimination.** Mark any filter gloss as interpretive per [[evidential-status-discipline]].
4. **Occam's limits.** The proposal was the single scalar's clinical test, and its authors said so. The scalar has not failed cleanly: n = 1, uncontrolled, an MCS+ baseline. It has, however, yielded a result its own framework can absorb only by re-describing the target. That supports the EBH page's existing caution about single-scalar accounts, with no need to strengthen it.
5. **Over-concession watch.** Do not write that a non-reporting patient's experience is "in principle undetectable". Covert command-following and recovered-patient report are partial routes, and the 2019 paper named one of them.
