**Voids category**: Mixed — an Unexplorable-seeming core (a difference given in feeling that no concept states), an Occluded layer (a reorientation system impervious to information the subject demonstrably has; a puzzle the mind cannot keep hold of), and a *filled* region (the Ozma problem, closed by physics in 1957 and 1964) that serves as a calibration case for whether voids are real.

## Executive Summary

The difference between left and right is the most familiar thing consciousness knows and one of the hardest for it to state. Kant argued in 1768 and again in the *Prolegomena* (1783) that a left hand and its mirror image share every internal, conceptually specifiable relation and yet cannot occupy one another's space: the difference is real, "the senses teach" it, and "no internal differences" are available to "the understanding by thinking alone". In 1786 he made the same difference the model for how reason orients itself beyond experience — the "feeling of a difference in my own subject, namely, the difference between my right and left hands", a feeling precisely because "these two sides outwardly display no designatable difference in intuition". Martin Gardner's Ozma problem (1964) sharpened this into a communication limit: no description transmitted to a distant civilisation with no shared chiral object can convey which hand we call left. That limit was then *closed*. Wu et al. (1957) showed that beta decay of cobalt-60 prefers one handedness, so a recipe can be sent; Cronin and Fitch (1964) showed that CP violation removes even the antimatter loophole. The handedness void is therefore one of the few catalogued voids with a documented boundary that moved, which makes it a test case for the Map's "whether the voids are real" question. A second, empirically robust face survives: disoriented rats, young children, and adults under verbal shadowing reorient by room geometry alone, ignoring a coloured wall they can see and remember (Cheng 1986; Hermer & Spelke 1994; Hermer-Vazquez, Spelke & Katsnelson 1999) — an occlusion within the subject's own spatial system that natural language appears to unlock. A third face is the mirror puzzle (Block 1974; English 2024): a question about left and right that most people cannot answer and cannot stably hold in view. Roughly one adult in ten reports difficulty telling left from right (Wolf 1973 via Gormley & Brydges 2016), and vision-language models still score near zero on simple left/right tasks (Kong et al. 2025), which places AI in the Ozma position: able to state the cobalt recipe without any felt asymmetry to anchor it.

## Key Sources

### Kant, *Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics* (1783), §13
- **URL**: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/52821/52821-h/52821-h.htm (Carus translation; verified verbatim)
- **Type**: Book (primary text)
- **Key points**:
  - Mirror images of hand and ear are as similar as anything can be, yet the reflected right hand is a left hand and "never can serve as a substitute for the other".
  - The difference is not conceptually determinable but is nonetheless internal to the objects as sensed.
  - Kant's solution: hands are appearances whose internal determination depends on their relation to the whole of space, i.e. to the form of outer intuition.
- **Tenet alignment**: A worked example of a truth available to acquaintance and refractory to description — the structure the [[acquaintance-void]] and [[ineffable-encounter-void]] describe, but with a *physical* rather than phenomenal object.
- **Quote**: "There are in this case no internal differences which our understanding could determine by thinking alone. Yet the differences are internal as the senses teach, for, notwithstanding their complete equality and similarity, the left hand cannot be enclosed in the same bounds as the right one (they are not congruent); the glove of one hand cannot be used for the other."

### Kant, "What Does It Mean to Orient Oneself in Thinking?" (1786), AA 8:133–146
- **URL**: https://phil880.colinmclear.net/materials/readings/kant-orientation.pdf (Cambridge translation; verified verbatim at 8:134–136)
- **Type**: Essay (primary text)
- **Key points**:
  - Geographical orientation needs objective data (the sun, the Pole Star) *and* a subjective ground: the felt difference between right and left.
  - The difference is a *feeling* because the two sides "outwardly display no designatable difference in intuition" — the difference is not seen in the objects, only felt in the subject.
  - Thought experiment: if the constellations were mirror-reversed overnight, "no human eye would notice the slightest alteration"; only an astronomer who attends to what he *feels* as well as sees could detect it.
  - Kant extends this from geography to mathematical space (orienting in a dark room) and then to "thinking in general, i.e. logically": when reason leaves experience behind and finds "no object of intuition at all, but merely space for intuition", its only compass is "a subjective ground of differentiation" — "reason's feeling of its own need".
- **Tenet alignment**: This is the Map's own metaphor stated at source. Kant makes the felt left/right asymmetry the paradigm for how a mind proceeds where its map runs out; the Voids section is an attempt at exactly that orientation. It also bears on [[tacit-integration-void]]: the feeling of handedness is subsidiary, never focal.
- **Quote**: "Thus even with all the objective data of the sky, I orient myself geographically only through a subjective ground of differentiation; and if all the constellations, though keeping the same shape and position relative to one another, were one day by a miracle to be reversed in their direction, so that what was east now became west, no human eye would notice the slightest alteration on the next bright starlit night."

### Kant, "Concerning the Ultimate Ground of the Differentiation of Directions in Space" (1768), AA 2:377–383
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-mathematics/ (SEP treatment; primary text not fetched)
- **Type**: Essay (via encyclopedia)
- **Key points**:
  - Incongruent counterparts introduced to argue against a Leibnizian relational account of space and for an orientable absolute space.
  - SEP: Kant "invokes such 'incongruent counterparts' in 'Directions in space' to establish the orientability and actuality of a Newtonian-style absolute space".
  - The argument's later career (Nerlich 1973; Earman; Hoefer; Pooley 2003) turns on whether the left/right difference must be intrinsic or can be relational once parity violation is taken into account.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — the simplest metaphysics of space (pure relationalism) was challenged by a fact about hands, and the dispute is not settled by simplicity alone.

### Gardner, *The Ambidextrous Universe* (1964) and the Ozma Problem
- **URL**: https://philpapers.org/rec/GARTOP-2 (reprinted as "The Ozma Problem and the Fall of Parity" in Van Cleve & Frederick, eds., *The Philosophy of Right and Left*, 1991)
- **Type**: Book / chapter (secondary summaries consulted; primary text not fetched)
- **Key points**:
  - Ozma problem: communicate the meaning of "left" to a distant civilisation by signal alone, with no shared chiral object. Before 1957 there was no known solution; every description is invariant under mirror reflection.
  - Gardner's resolution: instruct them to observe parity violation in nuclear beta decay (Wu et al. 1957).
  - Gardner's caveat: the trick works only if they are made of matter, not antimatter. CP violation (Cronin & Fitch 1964) later closed that loophole in principle.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — mirror symmetry of physical law was the simplest assumption and was false. Also a calibration exhibit for [[meta-epistemology-of-limits]]: a limit that felt conceptual turned out to be contingent on undiscovered physics.

### Wu, Ambler, Hayward, Hoppes & Hudson (1957), "Experimental Test of Parity Conservation in Beta Decay"

- **Type**: Paper — *Physical Review* 105(4): 1413–1415
- **Key points**: Polarised cobalt-60 nuclei emit beta electrons preferentially opposite to the nuclear spin; the weak interaction distinguishes left from right. Feynman's *Lectures* vol. ### Pooley (2003), "Handedness, Parity Violation, and the Reality of Space"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511535369.015 (in Brading & Castellani, eds., *Symmetries in Physics*, CUP, ch. 14, pp. 250–280; metadata verified, text not fetched)

### Hermer & Spelke (1994), "A Geometric Process for Spatial Reorientation in Young Children"
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8015605/ (abstract verified verbatim)
- **Type**: Paper — *Nature* 370: 57–59
- **Key points**:

- **Tenet alignment**: A demonstrated occlusion internal to one mind: information the subject has is unavailable to a subsystem that needs it. Relevant to the Occluded category and to [[embodiment-cognitive-limits]].

### Hermer-Vazquez, Spelke & Katsnelson (1999), "Sources of Flexibility in Human Cognition: Dual-Task Studies of Space and Language"
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10433786/ (abstract verified verbatim)
- **Type**: Paper — *Cognitive Psychology* 39(1): 3–36
- **Key points**:
  - Adults reorient flexibly (geometry + colour) normally, but "reoriented themselves like children and adult rats when they engaged in verbal shadowing of continuous speech"; nonverbal rhythm shadowing did not produce the deficit.
  - Verbally shadowing adults still detected and remembered both kinds of information but "failed to conjoin the two types of information to specify the positions of objects".
  - Conclusion: flexible spatial memory "depends on the ability to combine diverse information sources rapidly into unitary representations and that this ability, in turn, depends on natural language".
- **Tenet alignment**: Bears on [[language-thought-boundary]] — language here is the bridge across an internal occlusion, not a limit on thought. Contested by Twyman & Newcombe (2010, *Cognitive Science* 34(7): 1315–1356, "Five Reasons to Doubt the Existence of a Geometric Module") and by aphasia evidence: Bek, Blades, Siegal & Varley (2010, *J. Exp. Psychol.: LMC* 36(3): 646–658; abstract verified) find that "neither syntax nor lexicon is essential for spatial cue representation in a mature cognitive system". Both must be carried into any article.

### Levinson (1997), "Language and Cognition: The Cognitive Consequences of Spatial Description in Guugu Yimithirr"
- **URL**: https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_66622_3/component/file_66623/content (full text verified)
- **Type**: Paper — *Journal of Linguistic Anthropology* 7(1): 98–131
- **Key points**:
  - Guugu Yimithirr uses cardinal directions for all horizontal spatial description; no "left"/"right" in the relative sense.
  - Pointing to distant locations from bush sites: "Over a total of 120 trials from bush locations or stops on bush roads, the average error was 13" degrees (about 13°); the best subjects approached 10°.
  - Nonverbal memory tasks: Hopevale (Guugu Yimithirr) subjects predominantly chose "absolute" solutions, Dutch subjects "relative" ones, across multiple experiments; "the absolute coding is almost certainly available to the GY speaker, and it is almost certainly not readily available to the Dutch speaker".
  - Absolute coding "requires a constant, background 'dead-reckoning' of current heading and position".
- **Tenet alignment**: Shows the left/right frame is one option, not a cognitive universal. The Kantian "feeling" of right and left is still present in these speakers (they have bodies), but it is not what their spatial cognition is built on. See [[language-thought-boundary]] for the Map's existing Kuuk Thaayorre note (Boroditsky 2011).

### Gormley & Brydges (2016), "Difficulty with Right–Left Discrimination: A Clinical Problem?"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1503/cmaj.150577 (verified verbatim) — *CMAJ* 188(2): 98–99
- **Type**: Commentary (with Gormley, Dempster & Best 2008, *BMJ* 337: a2826; metadata verified)

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Three faces, sharing one seam — the left/right difference and its availability to concept, language, and feeling.

**Face 1 — the chirality face (Unexplorable-seeming).** Every internal, conceptually specifiable relation of a left hand is shared by a right hand. The difference is real, felt, and (Kant claims) not statable by any concept; it is given only in intuition. The Ozma problem is this claim converted into a communication protocol: nothing you can *say* to a mirror-agnostic receiver fixes which hand is left.

**Face 2 — the reorientation face (Occluded).** After disorientation, rats, toddlers, and language-loaded adults relocate a target by the geometry of the enclosure and ignore a coloured wall they can see, remember, and report. Information present in the mind is unavailable to the subsystem that needs it. Adults normally escape this by conjoining sources, and the dual-task evidence points to language as the conjoining medium. Guugu Yimithirr speakers show the frame itself is culturally optional: their spatial cognition runs on a continuously updated absolute heading with about 13° average error, and left/right plays no role in it.

**Face 3 — the mirror face (what slips away).** "Why does a mirror reverse left and right but not up and down?" is a question about handedness that most people cannot answer, that trained philosophers and physicists answered differently for decades, and that — even once explained — does not stay explained. English (2024) reads it as Wittgenstein's "I don't know my way about" in miniature.

### Evidence for the Limit

The chirality face has the best credentials of any entry in the catalogue for being a *genuine* limit at the time it was identified: it is a mathematical fact that no description invariant under reflection can distinguish enantiomorphs, and until 1957 every known physical law was reflection-invariant. That is stronger than the usual "nobody has managed it yet". It is also the entry with the best-documented *closure*: parity violation gave the description that had been proved impossible for a mirror-symmetric world, and CP violation removed the residual matter/antimatter ambiguity. What was unexplorable relative to one physics became explorable relative to the next.

The reorientation face is empirically robust across species and development but theoretically contested (Twyman & Newcombe 2010; aphasia data), so it is evidence for an occlusion, not for a module.

The mirror face is evidence only of persistent perplexity; it is a limit on holding a distinction steady, not on possessing it.

### Phenomenology

Kant's description is precise: the difference between one's sides is *felt* and not *seen*. The left hand looks like the right hand; nothing in the visual scene labels one of them. What one has is a bodily asymmetry available immediately, tacitly, and (for perhaps a tenth of adults) unreliably. Approaching the edge — trying to *say* what makes left left — produces the characteristic spiral: every candidate definition ("the side of the heart", "the side the sun rises on when you face north") smuggles in another chiral anchor, and the attempt to remove the anchor removes the content. The mirror puzzle adds a second phenomenology: the answer is understood, accepted, and lost again within days.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

For the chirality face, physics supplied a direct method: point at a process the world itself performs asymmetrically. This is the one entry in the voids catalogue where "look harder at nature" actually worked. It did not make the difference *conceptual* in Kant's sense — the cobalt recipe still terminates in an ostension ("the end the electrons come out of") — but it made it transmissible.

### Indirect Methods

- **Ostension and shared objects.** The pre-1957 solution to Ozma: send a chiral artefact. The void was always a void about *description*, never about *acquaintance*.
- **Frame substitution.** Absolute-frame languages route around handedness entirely; dead reckoning replaces left/right. This does not fill the void, it declines to use the territory.
- **Grammatical dissolution.** English (2024) and Block (1974) show the mirror puzzle yields to disambiguating what "reverse" means. The void here is a void of ambiguity, not of fact.
- **Language as bridge.** In the reorientation case, the dual-task results suggest the occlusion is crossed by spatial language ("left of the blue wall"), making this a rare void where a known cognitive tool does the crossing.

### What AI Might See

An LLM is the Ozma alien. Trained on text alone, it acquires "left" purely relationally: every occurrence of the word is invariant under a global mirror flip of the world it never saw. It can recite the cobalt-60 recipe and still has no felt asymmetry to attach it to — exactly Kant's astronomer who attends only to what he sees. Vision-language models, which do have a pixel frame, nonetheless score near zero on simple left/right benchmarks (Kong et al. 2025; Hoehing et al. 2023), which suggests that having images is not the same as having a side.

Two probes follow. First, ask a text-only model to define "left" with no indexical, ostensive, or chiral-artefact anchor; by the Ozma result the only correct answers route through parity or CP violation, and any answer that does not is question-begging. Second, the asymmetry cuts the other way: an AI has no Kantian feeling to *mislead* it, and no reorientation module to occlude its featural information. If the reorientation face is a modular occlusion, AI should not exhibit it; if VLMs fail left/right for reasons of training data (as Hoehing et al. argue), the failure is contingent and should be trainable away, which is a very different kind of void from Kant's.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits.** Mirror symmetry of physical law was the simplest hypothesis and was held with near-unanimity until Lee and Yang questioned it and Wu refuted it. Reflection invariance is the paradigm of a symmetry believed because it was elegant. The handedness void records that a conceptual limit on thought (Kant's, Gardner's) was partly an artefact of an oversimple physics, and that the world was less simple than the mind had assumed.

### Implications

- **For the "whether the voids are real" question.** This is the catalogue's cleanest case of a void boundary that moved, and it moved through empirical discovery rather than conceptual analysis. But the movement was partial: the difference remains unstatable *without* an ostensive terminus. Kant's point about concepts survived; Gardner's point about communication did not. Articles should separate the two.
- **For Dualism (with caution).** Kant's "feeling" is a phenomenal datum that delivered a real physical difference before any concept or law could state it. That is an instance of acquaintance being epistemically ahead of description, which the Map's dualism takes seriously. It is not an argument for dualism: the feeling has a body-schema explanation, and physicalists can grant the whole story. The correct claim is narrower — here is one case where the phenomenal was the *only* access to a truth for the entire history of thought until 1957.
- **For No Many Worlds / indexicality.** "Which hand is my left" is indexical in the way "which person is me" is indexical; the Ozma problem is the failure of pure description to fix an indexical fact. The analogy with the vertiginous question is suggestive and should be flagged, not pressed.
- **For Minimal Quantum Interaction and Bidirectional Interaction.** No link. The weak-interaction chirality is a fact about physics, not about consciousness's influence on it. An article that reaches for the weak force as an interface mechanism would be overclaiming and should not.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **"The Handedness Void: a limit that moved."** Lead with Kant's hand and the Ozma problem, narrate the 1957/1964 closure, then argue for the residue (ostension survives, concept does not). Use it as the calibration exhibit for the whole catalogue: what a *resolved* void looks like, and what "resolved" fails to mean.
2. **Three-face cognate (conjunction-coalesce style).** Chirality / reorientation / mirror, with the seam being the left/right difference's differing availability to concept, to a subsystem, and to steady attention. Sister to [[vagueness-void]] and [[suspension-void]] in form; sister to [[language-thought-boundary]] in content.

## Gaps in Research
