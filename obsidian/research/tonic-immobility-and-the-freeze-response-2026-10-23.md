## Executive Summary

**Two immobilities, not one.** The defence-cascade literature separates an early, attentive *freeze* from a late, passive *tonic immobility* (TI), and popular and some clinical usage blurs them.
- Roelofs (2017): "Tonic immobility is yet another defensive state that should be distinguished from freezing." Freezing is "a state of attentive immobility serving to avoid detection by predators and to enhance perception", whereas TI "occurs later, for example, in case of circa-strike physical contact, when fight, flight and freezing are no longer optimal for survival".
- Kozlowska et al. (2015) say human freezing is "Typically a short-lived phenomenon (often lasting only a few seconds), it is accessible for conscious processing and subjective representation". TI is "a response to inescapable threat, or a strategy of last resort", and their "collapsed immobility" is a variant "in which muscle tone is lost and consciousness is compromised".
- Lloyd et al. (2019) write that "TI, sometimes referred to as freezing", and their scale uses the word "freezing" for the lay audience. The vocabularies do not line up (see [Vocabularies](#vocabularies)).

**Awareness during freezing has a concurrent, behavioural measure; awareness during TI does not.**
- In the laboratory, freezing is indexed by reduced body sway and heart-rate deceleration, and perception can be tested while it happens. Lojowska et al. (2015) found that "stronger decrease in heart rate, a parasympathetic physiological index of freezing, was linked to better perception of LSF" (coarse visual features).
- For TI, no study this search found probes perception or awareness during the state. Everything known about awareness in TI comes from later report.

**What the reports say about awareness in TI points in two directions.**
- *Retained awareness*: Kozlowska et al. list "fear, immobility, coldness, numbness and analgesia, uncontrollable shaking, eye closure, and dissociation". They add, hedged: "What is (presumably) different is that human victims also typically retain a vivid memory of the event."
- *Progressive shutdown*: Schauer and Elbert's model, as summarised by Schalinski et al. (2015), holds that the shutdown "consists of functional sensory deafferentation, motor paralysis, alterations of the consciousness, and loss of speech perception and production", with "shutdown of physiological arousal and memory functions" at its flaccid end. Lloyd et al.'s scale keeps an amnesia item ("I have difficulty remembering the experience") and says the stages beyond TI "are thought to result in eventual loss of consciousness".
- *First-person accounts* (14 women interviewed within 12 weeks of rape; Gbahabo & Duma 2021) include a theme the authors call "mental paralysis" ("I could not think"). Some participants "felt pain", against the analgesia picture.

**The reports are collected after the fact, and the main instrument asks nothing about awareness.**
- The 10-item Tonic Immobility Scale (TIS), the field's first instrument, rates freezing or paralysis, inability to move or call out, trembling, "numb or no pain", cold, fear, fear of death, and detachment from self and surroundings (Reichenheim et al. 2014, Table 1). None of its items asks whether the respondent was aware of, perceived or remembers the event.
- Remembering is presupposed by answering, so a TI episode the person cannot recall cannot be scored. That is a survivorship filter on any "retained awareness" claim.
- Timing ranges from minutes to decades. The one near-concurrent paradigm (script-driven imagery with posturography; Volchan et al. 2011, Norte et al. 2019) asks only four motor items: "(i) froze or paralyzed; (ii) unable to move; (iii) unable to call out or scream, and (iv) unable to escape."
- Roelofs (2017): "the only studies in which freezing was actually linked to development of anxiety disorders such as post-traumatic stress disorder were based on retrospective self-reports of immobility during trauma". This is the [[peritraumatic-dissociation#after-the-fact|after-the-fact]] problem in its strongest form.

**The predictive record parallels peritraumatic dissociation's.**
- Coimbra et al.'s (2023) meta-analysis of 27 articles found TI associated with PTSD symptom severity at "r = 0.39, 95% CI: 0.34-0.44". But they had "limited longitudinal data to perform a meta-analysis" of development or course.
- Abrams et al. (2009) found that "peritraumatic dissociation scores alone accounted for 51% of the variance in TI scores".

**What follows for the Map.**
- The defence cascade predicts the shutdown, its triggers and its circuitry with no experiential term. Kozlowska et al. report that TI "can be induced in animals without a cerebrum" (as they claim it; source not read).
- The Map can describe TI as a reflexive closure of the output side with input partly open. That is the same two-arm description it gives [[locked-in-syndrome-as-the-negative-case-where-filter-loosening-does-not-apply#the-two-arms-of-the-interface|locked-in syndrome]], and here it is a *fit*, not evidence.
- That awareness can persist while action is blocked shows that awareness is not sufficient for action under an active motor inhibition. It does not show that awareness is causally idle when the channel is open. Epiphenomenalism is not conceded.
- The awareness half of the "awareness intact, motor channel closed" picture is itself contested inside the cascade literature, and the shutdown models predict graded dimming.

## The Two Immobilities and Their Vocabularies {#vocabularies}

The single biggest trap for the article is terminology. "Freeze" means attentive immobility in the animal and laboratory literature, but it is often used for TI in clinical and lay writing.

| Source | Early, attentive immobility | Late, passive immobility | Beyond it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roelofs 2017; Hagenaars et al. 2014 (figure "adapted from") | **freezing** ("attentive immobility"; bradycardia; "a parasympathetic brake on the motor system") and **orienting** (habituates; freezing does not) | **tonic immobility** ("playing dead"; "hypotension and unresponsiveness") | — |
| Kozlowska et al. 2015 | **freezing**, "a flight-or-fight response put on hold"; "also referred to as attentive immobility, hyper-reactive immobility, and reactive immobility" | **tonic immobility** (last resort) | **collapsed immobility** (loss of muscle tone, compromised consciousness); **quiescent immobility** (rest and healing) |
| Bracha 2004 | **freeze** | **fright** | **faint** |
| Schauer & Elbert 2010, per Schalinski et al. 2015 Table 1 | **Freeze**: "During attentive immobility (orienting response)" | **Fright**: "During unresponsive immobility" | **Flag/faint**: flaccid immobility, analgesia, "shutdown of physiological arousal and memory functions" |
| Volchan et al. 2017 | **attentive immobility**; **immobility under attack** | **tonic immobility** ("robust tachycardia") | — |
| Lloyd et al. 2019 (STOP) | — | **TI**, "sometimes referred to as freezing"; items say "freezing" for lay readers | flag/faint items kept as a severity indicator |

**Guard.** The article must define both terms in its first section and keep them apart. Where a source uses "freezing" for what is plainly TI, say so. Roelofs's own retrospective-studies sentence says "freezing" and cites three TI papers (Marx et al. 2008; Bovin et al. 2008; Heidt et al. 2005, her refs 28, 103 and 104).

### B. Instruments, and when they are administered

#### Fusé, T., Forsyth, J. P., Marx, B., Gallup, G. G. & Weaver, S. (2007). "Factor structure of the Tonic Immobility Scale in female sexual assault survivors." *Journal of Anxiety Disorders* 21(3): 265–283. DOI 10.1016/j.janxdis.2006.05.004 [V-abs]
- The TIS (Forsyth, Marx, Fusé, Heidt & Gallup 2000, unpublished; PsycTESTS 10.1037/t12763-000 [META]) "is comprised of two independent factors: physical immobility and fear" (EFA N = 88; CFA N = 191).

#### Reichenheim, M., Souza, W., Coutinho, E. S. F., et al. (2014). "Structural validity of the tonic immobility scale in a population exposed to trauma: Evidence from two large Brazilian samples." *PLoS ONE* 9(4): e94367. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0094367 [V-full, PMC3991625]
- **The item list (Table 1)**, each rated on a seven-point scale:
  1. "Rate the degree to which you froze or felt paralyzed during your most recent experience."
  2. Unable to move though not restrained.
  3. Trembling or shaking.
  4. Unable to call out or scream.
  5. "Rate the degree to which you felt numb or no pain during the event."
  6. Felt cold.
  7. Fear or panic.
  8. Feared for one's life.
  9. "Rate the extent to which you felt detached from yourself during the event."
  10. "Rate the extent to which you felt detached from what was going on around you during the event."
- The proponents call these "10 face valid items that were derived from the animal literature" (as quoted by Reichenheim et al.).
- In 3,223 trauma-exposed adults from São Paulo and Rio, "The original model did not hold". A one-factor solution fitted best, with residual redundancies.
- **What this means for the awareness question.** No item asks about awareness, perception or memory of the event. Item 5 merges numbness with analgesia. Items 9 and 10 are detachment items, which if anything index *reduced* presence. The instrument presupposes that the respondent remembers the event well enough to rate it.

#### Abrams, M. P., Carleton, R. N., Taylor, S. & Asmundson, G. J. G. (2009). "Human tonic immobility: measurement and correlates." *Depression and Anxiety* 26(6): 550–556. DOI 10.1002/da.20462 [V-abs]
- The Tonic Immobility Questionnaire. In 78 undergraduates reporting a TI experience, EFA gave three factors, "physical immobility, fear, and dissociation".
- "peritraumatic dissociation scores alone accounted for 51% of the variance in TI scores".
- The authors' reading: "TI may represent an extreme behavioral expression of trauma-induced peritraumatic dissociation".

#### Lloyd, C. S., Lanius, R. A., Brown, M. F., Neufeld, R. J., Frewen, P. A. & McKinnon, M. C. (2019). "Assessing Post-Traumatic Tonic Immobility Responses: The Scale for Tonic Immobility Occurring Post-Trauma." *Chronic Stress* 3: 2470547018822492. DOI 10.1177/2470547018822492 [V-full, PMC7219877]
- **What the STOP is**: a self-report scale, refined from a 41-item pool, for TI that *recurs* after trauma, in response to reminders. N = 462 trauma-exposed adults with a TI history, recruited by crowdsourcing (713 pooled).
- **How the items were built**: "STOP items were developed based on themes identified across first-person reports ( N = 6), descriptions in the literature, and clinical expertise."
- **Awareness-relevant items** (verbatim):
  - b1 "I cannot move";
  - b9 "I am still able to move my eyes";
  - b17 "I feel safe";
  - b34 "I have difficulty remembering the experience".
- "The final stages in the defense cascade, beyond TI, are thought to result in eventual loss of consciousness."
- **Authors' protective reading** (Lanius laboratory): "Dissociating the body from conscious awareness is inherent to TI and can function as an adaptive strategy under imminent threat (e.g., reduced pain)."
- **Their own limits**: "a reliance on retrospective, self-report data", self-selection on a prior TI experience, and crowdsourced samples.
- **Physiology**: "whereas one body of evidence suggests TI is characterized by tachycardia or heart rate acceleration, 30 another suggests that it is associated with bradycardia or heart rate deceleration". Their refs 30 and 31 are Volchan et al. 2017 and Hagenaars et al. 2014.
- **Scope**: "At present, no validated self-report measures assess post-traumatic tonic immobility responses specifically" (abstract, 2019).

#### Schalinski, I., Schauer, M. & Elbert, T. (2015). "The Shutdown Dissociation Scale (Shut-D)." *European Journal of Psychotraumatology* 6: 25652. DOI 10.3402/ejpt.v6.25652 [V-full, PMC4431999]
- A 13-item structured interview (225 patients, 68 controls). Items include fainting, transitory blindness or deafness, numbness, and "Have you felt as though you couldn't move for a while, as though you were paralyzed?"
- The time frame is set by the interviewer: "The interviewer should select a time frame within the past 6 months".
- So the Shut-D measures a *proneness* to shutdown episodes over months, not awareness during any one episode.

### C. Prevalence and outcome (all by retrospective report)

- **Möller, Söndergaard & Helström (2017)**, *Acta Obstet Gynecol Scand* 96(8): 932–938, 10.1111/aogs.13174 [V-abs; already cited on the Map].
  - The TIS was given to 298 women at an emergency clinic "within 1 month of a sexual assault".
  - "70% reported significant tonic immobility and 48% reported extreme tonic immobility". PTSD at 6 months: OR 2.75.
- **Coimbra, Hoeboer, van Zuiden, Williamson, D'Elia, Mello, Mello & Olff (2023)**, "The relationship between tonic immobility and the development, severity, and course of posttraumatic stress disorder: Systematic and meta-analytic literature review", *J Anxiety Disord* 97: 102730, 10.1016/j.janxdis.2023.102730 [V-abs].
  - 27 articles. TI and PTSD symptom severity: "r = 0.39, 95% CI: 0.34-0.44; p < .0001".
  - TI was more severe in females (d = 0.37) and more often elicited by interpersonal violence.
  - "We found limited longitudinal data to perform a meta-analysis of the association between TI and the development and/or course of PTSD."
  - Their definition: "Tonic immobility (TI) is a reflexive, involuntary response that causes motor inhibition, vocal suppression, and analgesia."
- **Kalaf et al. (2015)**, *Compr Psychiatry* 60: 68–72, 10.1016/j.comppsych.2015.04.001 [V-abs].
  - A representative sample of 3,231 trauma-exposed adults in Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo. TIS scores "more than double" in those with PTSD.
  - Limits: "The cross-sectional and retrospective design may have given rise to recall bias."
- **Kalaf et al. (2017)**, *J Affect Disord* 215: 71–76, 10.1016/j.jad.2017.03.009 [V-abs]: "PTI scores in child sexual abuse and adult sexual violence were almost twice as high as in other types of traumatic events".
- **Heidt, Marx & Forsyth (2005)**, *Behav Res Ther* 43(9): 1157–1171, 10.1016/j.brat.2004.08.005 [V-abs].
  - 39 undergraduates and 41 inpatients reporting childhood sexual abuse: "Over fifty-two percent of all participants reported TI in response to CSA."
  - The retrospective interval here is years to decades.
- **Bovin, Jager-Hyman, Gold, Marx & Sloan (2008)**, *J Trauma Stress* 21(4): 402–409, 10.1002/jts.20354 [V-abs].
  - 176 undergraduates: "tonic immobility fully mediated relations between perceived inescapability and overall PTSD symptom severity".
  - The design is cross-sectional, so "mediated" is statistical, not causal.
- **Humphreys, Sauder, Martin & Marx (2010)**, *J Interpers Violence* 25(2): 358–373, 10.1177/0886260509334412 [V-abs]: "37% to 52% of sexual assault survivors report experiencing a set of peritraumatic responses, which include gross motor inhibition, analgesia, and fixed or unfocused staring".
- **Fiszman et al. (2008)**, *J Affect Disord* 107(1–3): 193–197, 10.1016/j.jad.2007.07.015 [V-abs].
  - In 23 victims of urban violence with PTSD, peritraumatic TI was "reported by both genders in 43%" and predicted poor drug response.
  - Limits: "This study probed PTI retrospectively and was based on a small sample recruited in a tertiary clinic."
- **Maia et al. (2015)**, *Braz J Psychiatry* 37(1): 49–54, 10.1590/1516-4446-2013-1267 [V-abs]: a prospective police-recruit cohort (n = 132), with the TIS taken in training and after the first year. Each TIS point went with a 9% higher PCL-C mean.
- **Kalaf et al. (2022)** (*Psychiatr Danub*, 10.24869/psyd.2022.447) [V-abs]: quality of life. Not needed.

### D. Near-concurrent and laboratory measures

- **Volchan, E., Souza, G. G., Franklin, C. M., et al. (2011)**, "Is there tonic immobility in humans? Biological evidence from victims of traumatic stress", *Biological Psychology* 88(1): 13–19, 10.1016/j.biopsycho.2011.06.002 [V-abs].
  - 18 trauma-exposed participants with PTSD and 15 without listened to a script of their own trauma.
  - "Reports of script-induced immobility were associated with restricted area of body sway and were correlated with accelerated heart rate and diminished heart rate variability, implying that tonic immobility is preserved in humans as an involuntary defensive strategy."
  - **The closest to a concurrent measure this search found.** The report is taken minutes after re-elicitation and is checked against an objective motor measure.
- **Norte, C. E., Volchan, E., et al. (2019)**, "Tonic Immobility in PTSD: Exacerbation of Emotional Cardiac Defense Response", *Frontiers in Psychology* 10: 1213, 10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01213 [V-full, PMC6543833].
  - The same paradigm. After the script, "the psychiatrist applied the TI questionnaire to assess signs of immobility elicited by the trauma script".
  - It is a four-item motor measure: "(i) froze or paralyzed; (ii) unable to move; (iii) unable to call out or scream, and (iv) unable to escape."
  - **No awareness item.** This is the key datum for the article's measurement section.
- **Fragkaki, I., Stins, J., Roelofs, K., Jongedijk, R. A. & Hagenaars, M. A. (2016)**, "Tonic immobility differentiates stress responses in PTSD", *Brain and Behavior* 6(11): e00546, 10.1002/brb3.546 [V-full, PMC5102644].
  - 12 PTSD patients and 12 controls. "retrospective self-reported TI was associated with lower body sway increases in PTSD" from eyes open to eyes closed.
  - The authors' framing: "However, the evidence on TI in humans derived from retrospective self‐report measurements and did not include physiological measurements under experimental manipulations." The hyphen in "self‐report" is U+2010 in the source.
- **de Kleine, R. A., Hagenaars, M. A. & van Minnen, A. (2018)**, "Tonic immobility during re-experiencing the traumatic event in posttraumatic stress disorder", *Psychiatry Research* 270: 1105–1109, 10.1016/j.psychres.2018.06.051 [V-abs].
  - 184 patients with chronic PTSD: "During re-experiencing the traumatic event, 35.3% (n = 65) reported moderate TI, and 37.0% (n = 68) extreme TI."
  - TI during re-experiencing mediated the peritraumatic TI–severity link. **TI recurs**, which is what makes post-traumatic measurement possible.
- **Lojowska, M., Gladwin, T. E., Hermans, E. J. & Roelofs, K. (2015)**, "Freezing promotes perception of coarse visual features", *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 144(6): 1080–1088, 10.1037/xge0000117 [V-abs].
  - Under threat of shock, "stronger decrease in heart rate, a parasympathetic physiological index of freezing, was linked to better perception of LSF".
  - **Concurrent performance evidence for perception during *freezing***. It is the template a TI study would need.

## What the Reports Say About Awareness {#awareness-reports}

This section organises the evidence the article will turn on. Every item is a report given after the state.

1. **Motor and vocal inability is the core report and the best-supported.** The TIS items 1–4, Lloyd's b1 "I cannot move", and Gbahabo's "log of wood" all concern it. The only near-concurrent check (Volchan et al. 2011) validates the *motor* report against body sway.
2. **Eye movement and gaze.** The STOP keeps "I am still able to move my eyes". Kozlowska lists "eye closure", and Humphreys et al. list "fixed or unfocused staring". The motor closure is not total, and what it spares varies.
3. **Retained awareness and memory are asserted, hedged, by Kozlowska ("presumably", "typically").** They are presupposed by every instrument and directly measured by none.
4. **Reduced presence is reported alongside it.**
   - The TIS detachment items (9–10).
   - Abrams's dissociation factor, and the 51% overlap with the PDEQ.
   - The STOP's "Somatic Detachment and Amnesia" subscale and its amnesia item.
   - Gbahabo's "mental paralysis" ("I could not think") and "It felt like I was dreaming".
   - Schauer and Elbert's "functional sensory deafferentation ... alterations of the consciousness".
5. **Analgesia is reported but not isolated.** TIS item 5 asks "numb or no pain" as one rating. The animal antinociception is opioid-mediated (Kozlowska). In humans this search found no separate measure of pain during TI, and some of Gbahabo's participants "felt pain". Do not write "TI produces analgesia in humans" as a finding.
6. **Felt volition without execution.** "I wanted to stand up and run, but my legs were numb and heavy" (Gbahabo). Lloyd's original item pool had a "Loss of Agency" domain. These are reports of intention without movement, retrospective and few.
7. **Fear and safety.** The TIS has fear factors, and fear is the trigger. Yet the STOP keeps b17 "I feel safe", which Lloyd et al. read as "possibly reflecting the protective function of TI". On the Map, the momentary-protection reading has a null in [[peritraumatic-dissociation#four-senses|sense 2]]. Do not let the STOP item reopen it.
8. **Timing.**
   - Freezing lasts "often ... only a few seconds" (Kozlowska).
   - TI duration in humans was not found measured. Gbahabo's participants "lay there for a while", and Kozlowska's soldier vignette (an amalgam) gives "an indeterminate period".
   - Gap: no human TI duration data were found.
9. **Collapse.** Beyond TI, the cascade's flaccid end compromises consciousness (Kozlowska's collapsed immobility; Lloyd's "eventual loss of consciousness"; Schauer and Elbert's faint). The picture of "awareness intact, motor channel closed" holds, if at all, for a window between freezing and collapse.

**Net reading for the article.** The reports show a motor closure that is well attested and checked against an objective measure. With it comes an awareness that is variously described as vivid, detached, dreamlike or thought-stopped, together with memory that is sometimes impaired. What awareness was like during the state is the least measured element in the literature.

## How the Reports Are Collected {#collection}

| Method | Example | Interval to the event | What it asks about awareness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retrospective scale, clinic | TIS in Möller et al. 2017 | up to 1 month | nothing directly; detachment items only |
| Retrospective scale, community or student | TIS in Heidt 2005; Bovin 2008; Kalaf 2015 | months to decades | the same |
| Retrospective scale, clinical PTSD | TIS-A in Fragkaki 2016; de Kleine 2018 | years | the same |
| Prospective cohort | TIS in police recruits (Maia et al. 2015) | after the first year's incidents | the same |
| Laboratory re-elicitation | script-driven imagery plus posturography and ECG; 4 motor items (Volchan 2011; Norte 2019) | minutes after a *re-elicited* state | none (motor items only) |
| Recurrent post-traumatic TI | STOP (Lloyd 2019); Shut-D (past 6 months) | episodes over months | amnesia and detachment items; no probe during an episode |
| Qualitative interview | Gbahabo & Duma 2021 (≤12 weeks); TeBockhorst 2015 | weeks to years | open narrative |

**Two structural points for the article.**
1. **Survivorship filter.** A scale can be completed only by someone who remembers the episode well enough to rate it. Episodes with dense amnesia or collapse drop out, so "victims typically retain a vivid memory" is partly guaranteed by the method. Say "partly": the filter does not show that memory is usually poor either.
2. **Re-elicitation is the only route near the moment, and it measures movement.** The design is already there for an awareness probe (cf. Lojowska's perceptual task during freezing), but no study this search found has added one.

This is [[peritraumatic-dissociation#after-the-fact|the peritraumatic page's after-the-fact problem]] with one difference. For TI the *motor* half has an objective check, so the asymmetry is sharper: the motor closure is measured, while the awareness that accompanies it is not.

## Major Positions {#positions}

### The defence-cascade account (Kozlowska; Roelofs; Hagenaars; Volchan; Lanius laboratory)
- **Core claim**: TI is an evolved, reflexive, brainstem-mediated (vlPAG) last-resort defence, triggered by fear plus restraint or inescapability.
- **On awareness**: Kozlowska says it is retained, with a vivid memory ("presumably"). Lloyd and Lanius say the body is dissociated "from conscious awareness", with eventual loss of consciousness beyond TI.
- **Relation to tenets**: needs no experiential term to predict the shutdown. It is the Map's rival here, as on the subtype page.

### The shutdown-dissociation account (Schauer & Elbert; Schalinski)
- **Core claim**: a six-stage cascade in which the fright, flag and faint stages progressively shut down sensory, emotional, nociceptive and memory functions, with parasympathetic dominance.
- **On awareness**: graded dimming, not preservation.
- **Relation to tenets**: a dissociation model. It is the opposite of the "intact observer" picture, and the Map must carry it.

### The evolved-predator-defence account of rape-related TI (Suarez & Gallup 1979; Marx et al. 2008; Fusé; Heidt; Bovin)
- **Core claim**: sexual assault supplies the eliciting conditions for TI found in animals: "fear, contact, and restraint" (Marx et al. as quoted by TeBockhorst et al.).
- **On awareness**: the body of Marx et al. was not read, so their position on awareness is unknown here.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. Its clinical and legal point, that immobility is not consent, is outside the Map's scope and must be stated, if at all, with care.

### TI as extreme peritraumatic dissociation (Abrams et al. 2009)
- **Core claim**: TI may be "an extreme behavioral expression" of peritraumatic dissociation.
- **Relation**: bears on the Map's construct guard. On this account TI and peritraumatic dissociation overlap heavily (51% shared variance) but are not identical. Lloyd et al. also note: "Some authors caution against a unified conceptualization of these constructs (i.e., attributing dissociative symptoms to the cognitive experience of TI)."

## Key Debates {#debates}

### 1. Is awareness preserved or dimmed during TI?
- **Sides**: Kozlowska ("typically retain a vivid memory", hedged) against Schauer and Elbert (sensory deafferentation, memory shutdown) and Lloyd's amnesia item.
- **Core disagreement**: whether TI is "awareness without action" or the start of a graded shutdown of awareness itself.
- **Current state**: unresolved and largely untested. No instrument this search found probes awareness during the state.

### 2. Tachycardia or bradycardia?
- **Sides**: Volchan et al. (TI "presents robust tachycardia" in humans; Norte et al. found sustained heart-rate acceleration) against the animal parasympathetic model (bradycardia; Kozlowska) and Hagenaars et al.
- **Lloyd et al.'s proposal**: co-activation of both branches.
- Roelofs: TI's "physical features ... remain largely unclear and are often contradictory".
- **Current state**: open. This matters because a "parasympathetic shutdown" story of awareness dimming (via bradycardia and cerebral hypoperfusion) would not apply to a tachycardic TI.

### 3. Is TI a dissociation, a motor state, or both?
- **Sides**: Abrams (extreme dissociation); Kozlowska ("sometimes—but not always" counted among peritraumatic reactions); the Map's 10-22 construct guard (a motor state that often includes dissociative experience).
- **Current state**: the overlap is large but incomplete. The Map's wording on `peritraumatic-dissociation` L41 is consistent with the evidence found here.

### 4. Freeze and TI: one immobility or two?
- **Sides**: Roelofs, Hagenaars and Volchan (two or more, with different physiology) against clinical and lay usage (Lloyd's "sometimes referred to as freezing").
- **Current state**: settled in the animal and laboratory literature as distinct. The confusion persists in clinical writing.

## The Map's Question: What the Evidence Can and Cannot Show {#maps-question}

**The Map's interest.** TI looks like a natural experiment in what the [[locked-in-syndrome-as-the-negative-case-where-filter-loosening-does-not-apply|locked-in]] page calls the interface's two arms. The output arm is reflexively closed in an intact brain, and the input arm is reported partly open. It completes a family:

| Case | How output is lost | Duration | When awareness is reported | Map page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Locked-in syndrome | ventral pontine lesion | chronic | concurrently, by eye code or BCI | `locked-in-syndrome…` |
| Sleep paralysis | REM atonia outlasting arousal | seconds to minutes | immediately afterwards | `sleep-paralysis-and-interface-reassembly` |
| Awareness under neuromuscular blockade | drug at the neuromuscular junction | the procedure | afterwards, sometimes much later | `anesthesia-void` |
| Freezing (attentive immobility) | parasympathetic brake, not paralysis | seconds | concurrently, by task performance | none |
| Tonic immobility | reflexive brainstem motor inhibition | uncertain (unmeasured) | weeks to decades later, by scale or interview | none (this note) |

**What it can show.**
- Tier one of [[logic-of-dissociation-inference#three-tiers|the dissociation tiers]], for the motor side. People report being unable to move while threatened, and in the laboratory those reports covary with objective immobility (Volchan 2011; Fragkaki 2016).
- That reported experience during the state includes fear, detachment and sometimes vivid recollection. That is *reported* awareness.

**What it cannot show.**
- **That awareness was intact during the state.** The instruments do not ask, the near-concurrent paradigm asks only about motor items, and the survivorship filter guarantees that scored episodes were remembered.
- **That the interface reading beats the cascade.** The cascade predicts the trigger (fear plus restraint or inescapability; Bovin), the circuitry (vlPAG; amygdala deactivation) and the motor closure without any experiential term. Kozlowska reports that TI can be induced in decerebrate animals. Persisting cortical processing during a brainstem-mediated motor inhibition is what a production view predicts as well.
- **That awareness is causally idle.** That awareness can persist while action is blocked shows only that awareness is not sufficient for action against an active motor inhibition. It is silent on whether awareness contributes when the channel is open, as in freezing, where perception and action preparation are measured together (Roelofs's "freeze for action"). The Map never concedes epiphenomenalism. Fikretoglu-style "epiphenomenon" language does not appear in this literature, but watch for "awareness without control" being glossed as "awareness without causal role".
- **Anything about animal minds.** TI occurs in "insects, crustaceans, fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals" (Kozlowska), is reported in decerebrate preparations, and is used in poultry science as an operational fear index. That makes it a poor sentience marker. If anything it is a *defeater* for reading immobility as experience.

**The symmetric cut, in the Map's own voice.** The weakness of first-person evidence about TI cuts against the Map as much as for it. The Map's two-arm description would be strongest exactly where the evidence is weakest, in what awareness was like during the closure. The Map records this as a limit on its own evidence ([[evidential-status-discipline]]). This mirrors the closing paragraph of `peritraumatic-dissociation`'s Relation to Site Perspective, and the article must say it.

**What would decide something.**
- A re-elicitation study (script-driven, with posturography) that adds a concurrent perceptual or attentional probe (a Lojowska-type discrimination task, or a near-threshold detection task) during reported TI, compared with the same probe during freezing.
- The cascade-with-shutdown model predicts degraded perception during TI. Kozlowska's "vivid memory" picture predicts preserved perception.
- Either result is compatible with both the interface and production readings. It would settle the *awareness* half of the picture, not the metaphysics.
- Ethical constraints on inducing TI rule out the real event, and the article should say so.

## Guards for the Article Writer (Binding) {#guards}

### Author stances

- **Roelofs; Hagenaars.**
  - Neurobiologists of human and animal freezing.
  - Roelofs makes no claim about awareness ("aware"/"conscious": 0 hits).
  - Her "freeze for action" thesis is about action preparation. Do not enlist it for mental causation.
- **Kozlowska et al.**
  - Clinicians presenting a management framework.
  - The "vivid memory" sentence is hedged ("presumably", "typically").
  - The opioid sentence is a hypothesis ("it is probable"), as the 10-22 note already records.
  - Their claim that human experience "parallels that of animals" is theirs. Do not adopt it.
  - Their TI vignettes are amalgams.
- **Lloyd, Lanius et al.**
  - The Lanius laboratory holds a *protective* reading of TI-dissociation ("can function as an adaptive strategy"; "possibly reflecting the protective function of TI").
  - Report it as their hypothesis. The Map's [[peritraumatic-dissociation]] found no measured momentary relief, and the article must not let the STOP reopen that.
- **Schauer & Elbert; Schalinski.**
  - Shutdown-dissociation theorists from a treatment tradition (Narrative Exposure Therapy).
  - Their model is a functional evolutionary account. Do not read "alterations of the consciousness" as a claim about consciousness's nature.
- **Volchan, Figueira and the Rio group.**
  - Psychophysiologists. They concluded that TI is "preserved in humans as an involuntary defensive strategy".
  - Their measure is motor.
- **Marx, Forsyth, Gallup, Fusé, Heidt, Bovin.**
  - Clinical researchers in an evolutionary-psychology frame.
  - The TI–rape link has an explicit legal and clinical purpose (immobility is not consent; de Heer & Jones 2024 [V-abs] makes the legal case).
  - Body of Marx et al. 2008 not read.
- **Gbahabo & Duma.** Nursing researchers using narrative inquiry. The "mental paralysis" label is their interpretation of the narratives.
- **Abrams et al.** Psychometricians. "TI may represent ..." is a hedged suggestion.
- **No author in this note argues for dualism, or engages the metaphysics at all.** Do not enlist any of them. None is enlisted for physicalism either. Write "the cascade model needs no experiential term" as the Map's observation about the models, not as any author's thesis.

### The calibration rule (binding)

1. **Later report only.** Awareness during TI is known only from report given minutes (re-elicitation), weeks (clinic) or years (community, clinical) afterwards. Treat it as [[peritraumatic-dissociation#after-the-fact|measuring a state after the fact]], and link that section.
2. **Fit, not evidence (Tenet 3).** The defence cascade predicts the shutdown, its triggers and its circuitry with no experiential term. Any Map reading, including the two-arm description, is a fit. **Do not present TI as consciousness persisting without motor control in a way that evidences dualism or interactionism.**
3. **No animal tier moves.** Animal TI, in any taxon, must not raise any animal's evidential tier on the five-tier scale ([[birch-edge-of-sentience-and-the-five-tier-scale#five-tier-scale|the Map's five tiers]]; rule: "a tenet may remove a defeater, but it must not upgrade the evidence level"). Do not use Kozlowska's "parallels that of animals" sentence for animal experience. If animal TI is mentioned at all, note that its occurrence in invertebrates and decerebrate preparations makes immobility a poor marker of experience.
4. **Never concede epiphenomenalism.** That awareness persists while action is blocked does not show that awareness is causally idle; see [The Map's Question](#maps-question). Also do not argue the converse, that "the appraisal of inescapability triggers TI, so consciousness causes it". Bovin's mediation is cross-sectional, and on the rival the appraisal is a brain state. Symmetric.
5. **Keep the symmetric reading** the peritraumatic page holds. The evidence's weakness cuts against the Map's two-arm gloss as much as for it, and neither reading gains. The article must contain a sentence to this effect in the Map's voice.
6. **Locked-in comparisons keep the disanalogy explicit.** TI is a reflexive, time-limited state in an intact nervous system. Locked-in syndrome is a lesion state, chronic, with concurrent report possible. Sleep paralysis is a state-boundary atonia recalled immediately. Neuromuscular blockade acts at the junction. Do not count the four as independent confirmations of the two-arm architecture. The sleep-paralysis page's own L88 warning about the [[common-cause-null]] applies.
7. **Awareness is contested even inside the cascade literature.** Present both the "vivid memory" and the "shutdown" pictures. Never write "TI victims remain fully aware".
8. **Absence claims scoped** (see [Absence and Exclusivity Claims](#absence)).

### Evidence not to lean on

- **Kozlowska's "vivid memory".** Hedged by its authors and confounded by the survivorship filter. Use it as one side of a debate, never as a finding.
- **Volchan et al. 2011 as evidence about awareness.** It validates the *motor* report only.
- **The STOP "I feel safe" item, or Lloyd's "adaptive strategy"**, as momentary protection. It is a recalled, self-selected, crowdsourced item, and the peritraumatic null stands.
- **"TI produces analgesia in humans."** No isolated human measure was found. TIS item 5 merges numbness and "no pain", and some participants report pain.
- **Bovin's mediation as causal.** It is cross-sectional.
- **Animal TI** as evidence of animal awareness, or of anything about human awareness.
- **The decerebrate claim as settled.** Kozlowska cites an EEG paper (Schwarz & Bickford 1956) for it. Write "Kozlowska et al. report", or omit it.
- **Prevalence figures as awareness data.** Möller's 70%, de Kleine's 72% (moderate plus extreme TI during re-experiencing, summed here) and Heidt's 52% count reported immobility, not awareness.
- **Lojowska as evidence about TI.** It concerns *freezing*. Use it only as the template for the missing TI test.

### Coiner and populariser

- **"Defence cascade"**: do not credit Kozlowska et al. as originators (the 10-22 guard). Kozlowska write that sequential responses were "termed the defense cascade", citing several earlier works.
- **"Freeze, flight, fight, fright, faint"**: Bracha 2004. "Flag" is Schauer & Elbert's addition (2010).
- **"Attentive immobility"**: in use by Roelofs, Kozlowska and Volchan, with no coiner identified. Do not credit one.
- **"Rape-induced paralysis"**: Kozlowska credit the usage to the sexual-assault literature (their ref 107, Foa & Rothbaum 1998), and Heidt et al.'s title uses it. Do not credit a coiner.
- **The TI–rape hypothesis**: Suarez & Gallup 1979 by title [META]. Say "first proposed, on this search, by", not "coined by", and keep it out of the lede.
- **Tonic Immobility Scale**: Forsyth, Marx, Fusé, Heidt & Gallup 2000 (unpublished; the PsycTESTS record exists).

### Term traps

- **"Freeze"** is ambiguous (see [Vocabularies](#vocabularies)). Define it on first use.
- **"Shutdown"** means parasympathetic shutdown (Schauer and Elbert) or the cascade's "shutdown response" (Kozlowska). It has no relation to the Map's anaesthesia "shutdown" usages.
- **"Dissociation"** in Abrams and Lloyd is clinical dissociation (depersonalisation, derealisation, amnesia), not the neuropsychological dissociation of [[logic-of-dissociation-inference]]. The article uses both senses, so it must flag which.
- **"Protective" or "adaptive"**: always say which of the [[peritraumatic-dissociation#four-senses|four senses]] is meant. For TI, the survival sense (4) is the cascade's own claim, untestable in humans.

### Sensitivity

- Much of the human literature is about sexual assault. Write without graphic detail, quote survivors sparingly, and keep the article on the consciousness question.
- Mention the clinical and legal significance, that immobility is involuntary and is not consent, in one sentence at most and with care. Do not develop it.
