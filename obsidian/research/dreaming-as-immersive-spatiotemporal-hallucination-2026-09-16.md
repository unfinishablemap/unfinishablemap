## Executive Summary

Jennifer Windt's *immersive spatiotemporal hallucination* (ISTH) model (2010; expanded in *Dreaming*, 2015) proposes a **minimal** definition: dreaming is immersive spatiotemporal hallucination occurring in sleep or at sleep–wake transitions, with reportability assumed; the core phenomenal feature is the sense of presence or self-location in a spatiotemporal frame of reference, and this is taken to be necessary and sufficient for dreaming. It belongs to a family of *simulation views* (Revonsuo's world-simulation, Metzinger's self-model account, Hobson and Friston's virtual-reality generator) that the SEP describes as "largely complementary" and as having "forged unity in a field that was previously hampered by lack of agreement about the definition of dreaming." Its rivals are the *imagination view* (Sosa, Ichikawa, McGinn, Thompson) — now under empirical pressure from aphantasics who dream visually (Whiteley 2020) — and *anti-experience / report-skeptical* views (Malcolm, Dennett, Rosen), which Windt answers with a transparency defence of laboratory dream reports.

Two findings matter most for the Map. First, the outer reviewer's framing is partly off: ISTH is a **floor**, not a claim of phenomenal completeness. Windt argues that bodily self-experience in dreams is "both more variable and typically more weakly expressed than in waking," that bodiless dreams still count as dreams, and (2018) that dreams are *weakly* phenomenally-functionally embodied, with vestibular, thermal and touch content systematically under-represented. This directly qualifies the "Phenomenal Completeness" signature in `topics/lucid-dreaming-and-dualist-rendering` (L110: "integrated multisensory experience (vision, audition, touch, proprioception, emotion)"), which currently overstates what the standard philosophical model licenses. Second, the ISTH definition is *metaphysically neutral about the substrate*: it is a phenomenological characterisation that physicalists (Revonsuo's "biological realism") deploy toward internalism and that the Map can deploy toward its filter reading; Windt herself (2015, 2018) argues dreaming supports neither internalism nor externalism cleanly. A concepts article can therefore state the definition canonically, then set out precisely what each horn of the rendering dilemma owes: the production reading must explain why the *minimal* core (self-location) is what survives when modal content drops out; the filter reading must explain why loosening sensory constraint yields a *less* embodied, not a richer, self.

### Windt (2010), "The immersive spatiotemporal hallucination model of dreaming"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11097-010-9163-1 (Crossref-verified: *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 9(2), 295–316; published online 6 May 2010)
- **Type**: Paper
- **Key points** (paraphrased from the Monash portal's abstract rendering — verbatim text not obtained, Springer 403):
  - Proposes defining dreaming as "immersive spatiotemporal hallucination (ISTH) occurring in sleep or during sleep-wake transitions" under the assumption of reportability; ISTH is presented as necessary and sufficient, and as "a constitutive condition of dreaming" that any future extension of the concept (beyond sleep, or independently of reportability) must retain.

  - Citation count 132 (Scopus, via Monash) / 136 (OpenAlex).
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. A phenomenological definition; commits to nothing about whether the brain produces or transmits the experience.

## Major Positions

### The simulation view — ISTH variant (Windt)
- **Proponents**: Windt (2010, 2015, 2018, 2019); endorsed as complementary by Revonsuo, Tuominen & Valli (2015) and Metzinger (2013).
- **Core claim**: dreaming is "the immersive experience of a virtual world centered on a virtual self" (Windt 2019); the necessary and sufficient core is spatiotemporal self-location ("here-and-now" presence), which can persist even when a body, visual imagery, or specific thought content is absent.
- **Key arguments**: (i) bodiless dreams and asomatic OBEs show self-location without body ownership; (ii) the definition unifies the field and gives a principled boundary between dreams and non-immersive (dreamless) sleep experience; (iii) it is phenomenologically plausible and empirically tractable (hot-zone findings, microdream studies).
- **Relation to site tenets**: the definition is neutral. What is *not* neutral is the framing the Map imports: "phenomenal completeness" is not part of ISTH and Windt's 2018 "weak embodiment" thesis contradicts the lucid article's L110 list. A concepts article should state ISTH as the floor and treat richness as a separate, empirically variable dimension.

### The simulation view — world-simulation / biological realism (Revonsuo)
- **Proponents**: Revonsuo (2000, 2006, 2015); Valli, Tuominen.
- **Core claim**: consciousness is "a virtual reality in the brain"; dreaming is the paradigmatic model system because it is "phenomenality pure and simple" (2006: 75, via SEP); Threat Simulation Theory and Social Simulation Theory give functions.
- **Relation to site tenets**: **Conflicts** on Dualism — Revonsuo draws the internalist conclusion that the phenomenal level is "in the brain." The Map's reply is that the same envatment observation licenses only "brain-dependent," not "brain-constituted"; Windt's own refusal of the internalist inference (SEP; 2015, 2018) and Thompson (2015) are the citations for this.

### The virtual-reality generator / predictive-processing view (Hobson & Friston; Bucci & Grasso; Sinn & Borner)
- **Core claim**: "The brain is genetically equipped with a model that generates a virtual reality during sleep and is entrained by sensory input during waking"; REM "powerfully amplified" the system.
- **Relation to site tenets**: **Conflicts** (production model), but it is the strongest production-side account of both the vividness surplus and the degradation asymmetry, and the lucid article already engages it. The ISTH article should route to `concepts/predictive-processing-and-dualism` rather than re-derive the reply.

### The self-model view (Metzinger)
- **Core claim**: dreams are transparent world-models with a phenomenal self-model that lacks several waking layers (agency, autobiographical memory, metacognition); waking is "online dreaming."
- **Relation to site tenets**: Conflicts on Dualism (representationalist physicalism) but supplies the MPS research agenda the filter reading can adopt.

### The imagination view (Sosa, Ichikawa, McGinn, Thompson)
- **Core claim**: dreams are (propositional and sensory) imaginings, not hallucinations; dreamers imagine believing rather than believe.
- **Current state**: under pressure from Whiteley's aphantasia argument; the survivable version (Whiteley 2020; Thompson 2014) makes dream imagination *spontaneous and non-voluntary*, which erodes the original contrast with hallucination.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Neutral-to-awkward. The Map's intention-responsiveness signature presupposes that dream content is *not* under ordinary voluntary imaginative control (lucid control is partial and effortful), which sits closer to the hallucination side.

### Anti-experience and report skepticism (Malcolm, Dennett, Rosen)
- **Core claim**: (strong) no experience occurs in sleep — reports are waking constructions; (moderate, Rosen) experiences occur but reports are systematically fabricated.
- **Current state**: strong form widely regarded as refuted by lucid-dream signalling and hot-zone correlates; moderate form live and unresolved.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Rosen's moderate view is a constraint the Map should state: every dream-phenomenology argument in the corpus inherits report unreliability.

### The filter precursor (Bergson 1901)
- **Core claim**: sleep is disinterest; the dream is "the entire mental life minus the tension, the effort and the bodily movement"; content is preserved memory incarnated in residual sensation.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Aligns with Filter Theory and Occam's Limits. It also gives the filter reading its own prediction: dream content should be *memory-derived* and *sensation-triggered*, which the ISTH data partly confirm (dream body as template of the waking body) and partly complicate (bizarreness, novel scenes).

## Key Debates

### Is ISTH a floor or a ceiling? (the "phenomenal completeness" question)
- **Sides**: the outer reviewer (2026-09-16) treats ISTH as "the standard philosophical model of exactly the 'phenomenal completeness' the article foregrounds"; Windt (2015, 2018) treats it as the *minimal* condition and argues dream embodiment is typically weaker than waking; Revonsuo says dream self-experience is "identical to waking".
- **Core disagreement**: whether integrated multisensory richness is constitutive of dreaming or an optional, variable overlay on a minimal spatiotemporal core.
- **Current state**: Windt's position is dominant in the philosophical literature; the Map's lucid article currently sides with Revonsuo without saying so. This is the single most useful correction the research yields.

### Hallucination versus imagination
- **Sides**: hallucination/perceptual (Hobson, Revonsuo, Windt's ISTH by name) versus imagination (Sosa, Ichikawa, McGinn, Thompson).
- **Core disagreement**: whether dream experience has perceptual (transparent, world-presenting) or imaginative (opaque, agentive) character.
- **Current state**: Whiteley (2020) shifted the burden onto the imagination side; Windt's *Dreaming* ch. 6 ("Dreaming as Imaginative Experience") engages it directly. Ongoing.

### Internalism versus enactivism/externalism
- **Sides**: Revonsuo (dreaming as cranial envatment supports internalism) versus Noë (2004), Thompson (2014, 2015); Windt (2015, 2018) says neither inference is licensed because dreams are not sealed from the sleeping body.
- **Relevance**: this is the debate the Map's rendering dilemma actually sits inside. The physicalist "concession" the lucid article quotes is, in Revonsuo's hands, an argument *for* the brain as sole substrate.

### Report transparency versus narrative fabrication
- **Sides**: Windt (2013) versus Rosen (2013); Dennett's cassette theory as the limiting case.
- **Current state**: the moderate skeptical view is unrefuted; Windt concedes the transparency assumption is a methodological default justified by inference to the best explanation.

### Dreamless sleep, white dreams and the overabundance problem
- **Sides**: Windt, Nielsen & Thompson (2016) and Alcaraz-Sánchez (2021, 2024) argue for sub-ISTH experience; Fazekas et al. (2019) reinterpret white dreams; Nielsen (2017) and Windt (2019) contest whether microdreams count as dreams.
- **Relevance**: determines whether self-location really is the minimum, or whether "objectless awareness" states go below it — the Map's `voids/minimal-consciousness-void` territory.
