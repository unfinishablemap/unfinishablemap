# Research: Voids - The Palette Extension Void

**Date**: 2026-09-04
**Search queries used**: Oz vision system "olo" cone-targeted stimulation Science Advances 2025; sensory substitution new qualia Deroy Auvray cognitive extension; feelSpace magnetic belt König 2016 sensory augmentation subjective reports; olo skepticism Barbur "not a new colour"; Mancuso 2009 gene therapy squirrel monkey new colour dimension; Jordan Deeb 2010 human tetrachromat cDa29; Ward Meijer 2010 vOICe blind users visual qualia; philosophical response olo knowledge argument; L. A. Paul epistemically transformative new sensory capacity
**Voids category**: Mixed — the *induction* face is Unexplored and actively shrinking; the *verification* face is a candidate Unexplorable

## Executive Summary

The catalogue already holds several articles asking whether a genuinely novel quality is *conceivable* — [[acquaintance-void|the acquaintance void]], [[synesthetic-void|the synesthetic void]], and the research-stage palette and qualitative-novelty notes all approach the phenomenal palette from the side of imagination. This note opens a different question, which the catalogue has not surveyed: suppose a novel quality is not imagined but *installed*. Could anyone tell? Three empirical programmes now extend the human palette at the receptoral level — gene therapy adding a cone class to adult primates (Mancuso et al. 2009), sensory augmentation adding a persistent spatial channel (König et al. 2016), and cone-by-cone laser stimulation producing a colour the authors prove lies outside the natural human gamut (Fong et al. 2025). The *capacity* limit is therefore deflating fast. What does not deflate is the *verification* limit: every route from an installed channel back to a claim about phenomenal novelty runs through discrimination behaviour or verbal report, and both are rendered in the vocabulary of the palette the subject already had. The Fong subjects, shown a colour with unequivocal out-of-gamut coordinates, volunteered the words "teal," "green," "blue-greenish," and "green, a little blue." The void is thus not a limit on acquiring new phenomenal content but on *certifying* that what was acquired is new — an epistemology-of-the-experiment void rather than a capacity void, and the first case in the catalogue where one face of a void is collapsing while its sibling face holds firm.

## Key Sources

### Novel color via stimulation of individual photoreceptors at population scale
- **URL**: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adu1052 (open access via PMC12007580)
- **Citation**: Fong, J., Doyle, H. K., Wang, C., et al. (2025). *Science Advances*, 11(16), eadu1052. DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.adu1052
- **Type**: Peer-reviewed paper
- **Key points**:
  - The "Oz" prototype images the retina in infrared to track eye motion at cellular scale and targets, in the paper's phrasing, "10^5 visible-wavelength laser microdoses per second to each cone cell," across a field of view that is "a 0.9° square centered at 4° adjacent to a gaze-fixation target."
  - Stimulating only the M cones (of L, M, S — hence the binary 010, hence "olo") produces a percept the authors report as out-of-gamut. Five subjects.
  - The out-of-gamut proof is a *matching* argument: "all subjects found it necessary to desaturate olo with projector white in order to match the (near) monochromatic colors shown, which lie on the boundary of the natural human gamut." Those matching wavelengths run 501–512 nm. The authors call this "unequivocal proof that olo lies beyond the gamut."
  - Qualitative data: "Color names volunteered for olo include 'teal,' 'green,' 'blue-greenish,' and 'green, a little blue.' Subjects consistently rate olo's saturation as 4 of 4, compared to an average rating of 2.9 for the near-monochromatic colors of matching hue."
  - The paper flags its own limit on the phenomenal inference: because of the Abney effect, "the hue of the wavelength at best match may not exactly represent the hue of the undiluted olo color."
- **Tenet alignment**: The clean split between what the experiment settles (colorimetric coordinates) and what it leaves open (phenomenal character) is a direct instance of [[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]] — see "Connection to Tenets" below.
- **Quote**: "They describe the color as blue-green of unprecedented saturation."

### "It is not a new colour" — the Barbur dissent
- **URL**: press commentary, *The Guardian*, April 2025 (widely syndicated; summarised at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olo_(color))
- **Type**: Press commentary, not a peer-reviewed reply — flagged as such because the Map should not present a newspaper quotation as a scientific rebuttal
- **Key points**:
  - John Barbur (City St George's, University of London) is quoted saying "It is not a new colour," glossing olo instead as "a more saturated green that can only be produced in a subject with normal red-green chromatic mechanism when the only input comes from M cones."
  - The dissent does not contest the measurements. It contests what would *count* as new.
- **Tenet alignment**: The disagreement is definitional rather than evidential, which is precisely the diagnostic for a void boundary rather than an open empirical question.
- **Note on epistemic status**: Because this is press rather than literature, any article built on this note should either locate a peer-reviewed statement of the same objection or present Barbur's remark explicitly as reported commentary.

### Gene therapy for red–green colour blindness in adult primates
- **URL**: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08401
- **Citation**: Mancuso, K., Hauswirth, W. W., Li, Q., Connor, T. B., Kuchenbecker, J. A., Mauck, M. C., Neitz, J., & Neitz, M. (2009). *Nature*, 461, 784–787. DOI: 10.1038/nature08401
- **Type**: Peer-reviewed paper
- **Key points**:
  - Viral delivery of the missing photopigment gene to adult dichromatic squirrel monkeys; roughly 20 weeks after injection the animals began discriminating colours that had previously been indistinguishable to them.
  - The striking result is developmental: classic deprivation work predicted that circuits not exposed to an input during a critical period could not later process it. The adult brains handled the new receptoral dimension anyway.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bears on the [[capability-division-problem|capability division]] question — the physical substrate proved far more accommodating than expected, with no corresponding purchase on the phenomenal side.
- **Why it matters here**: This is the induction face's strongest result and the verification face's sharpest illustration at once. Behavioural discrimination improved; nothing in the study speaks to whether the monkeys' experience gained a dimension.

### The dimensionality of color vision in carriers of anomalous trichromacy
- **URL**: https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2191517
- **Citation**: Jordan, G., Deeb, S. S., Bosten, J. M., & Mollon, J. D. (2010). *Journal of Vision*, 10(8):12. DOI: 10.1167/10.8.12
- **Type**: Peer-reviewed paper
- **Key points**:
  - The subject designated cDa29 is the functionally confirmed human tetrachromat: in a three-alternative temporal forced-choice task she reliably distinguished a monochromatic orange from red/green mixtures that are metameric for trichromats.
  - The test was designed for quantitative rigour, which is exactly why it says nothing about quality.
- **Tenet alignment**: A within-species [[commensurability-void|commensurability]] case — a human palette difference that behaviour detects and language cannot bridge.

### What is it like to be a tetrachromat?
- **URL**: https://philosophymindscience.org/index.php/phimisci/article/view/11645
- **Citation**: Newall, M. (2025). *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences*, 6. DOI: 10.33735/phimisci.2025.11645
- **Type**: Peer-reviewed philosophy paper — the most directly relevant recent treatment
- **Key points**:
  - Abstract, verbatim: the paper "rejects the claim that they simply see finer gradations of those colours familiar to trichromats. Instead, it draws on both scientific evidence and anecdotal data to argue that tetrachromats' visual acuity is due to seeing novel colours: elementary colours that trichromat viewers are unable to see or imagine."
  - Crucially for this note, Newall's route to that conclusion is *inferential* — opponent-channel physiology plus cDa29's inability to place her experience within the trichromat gradation — because the direct route is closed.
  - Newall quotes vision scientist Gabriele Jordan: "This private perception is what everybody is curious about, …. I would love to see that." (Newall attributes this to Greenwood, 2012.)
  - He also quotes Greenwood's *Discover* piece on the barrier itself: "Unfortunately, she [cDa29] cannot describe how her colour vision compares with ours, any more than we can describe to a dichromat person what red looks like."
  - And C. L. Hardin, sceptical that cDa29 is the "tetrachromatic visual superwoman" of *Color for Philosophers*: "Probably not, although the tests she underwent were designed to be strictly hard-nosed forced-choice stuff so as to admit of quantitative comparison and analysis. Nobody asked the woman about the quality of her experience." (Hardin, 2014, p. 386.)
- **Tenet alignment**: Newall's need to argue structurally rather than evidentially is itself the evidence for the void.
- **Verification note**: All four quotations above were extracted from the article's full text and confirmed by string match against the raw retrieved document, not from a summariser's paraphrase. An earlier summarised rendering mis-attributed the "cannot describe" line to Newall and mangled Hardin's wording ("did not ask" for "Nobody asked"); both were corrected against the source.

### Learning New Sensorimotor Contingencies: Effects of Long-Term Use of Sensory Augmentation
- **URL**: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0166647
- **Citation**: König, S. U., Schumann, F., Keyser, J., Goeke, C., Krause, C., Wache, S., Lytochkin, A., Ebert, M., et al. (2016). *PLOS ONE*, 11(12), e0166647. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0166647
- **Type**: Peer-reviewed paper
- **Key points**:
  - The feelSpace belt signals magnetic north by vibrotactile stimulation at the waist. Nine participants wore it during all waking hours; five served as controls.
  - Subjective reports evolved continuously with training duration: participants described changes in their perception of space, with the belt's vibration signal coming to be experienced as spatial information rather than as vibration.
  - fMRI during a virtual homing task showed differential activation in posterior parietal cortex, supplementary motor area, and premotor cortex.
- **Tenet alignment**: A live test of whether an added channel becomes *perceptual* or stays *inferential*, which is the same seam the verification face names.

### Reading the world through the skin and ears: a new perspective on sensory substitution
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00457
- **Citation**: Deroy, O., & Auvray, M. (2012). *Frontiers in Psychology*, 3, 457.
- **Type**: Peer-reviewed theoretical paper
- **Key points**:
  - Rejects the "perceptual assumption" about sensory substitution devices, proposing instead a *reading* model: the device installs a novel cognitive skill layered onto an existing sensory pathway, closer to literacy than to a new sense.
  - If the reading model is right, decades of substitution reports do not license any claim about new phenomenal content at all.
- **Tenet alignment**: Supplies the strongest deflationary reading, and one that is difficult to distinguish empirically from its rival — the structural signature of a void.

### Transformative Experience
- **Citation**: Paul, L. A. (2014). *Transformative Experience*. Oxford University Press. See also the Stanford Encyclopedia entry: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/transformative-experience/
- **Type**: Book / encyclopedia entry
- **Key points**:
  - An epistemically transformative experience is one whose character cannot be known in advance of having it; Paul's standing example is a congenitally deaf person deciding about a cochlear implant.
  - This supplies the third face below: the pre-extension subject cannot know what is being added, which means the decision to extend a palette is taken from a position of principled ignorance about its content.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Three faces, which come apart sharply:

**1. Induction (Unexplored, and shrinking).** Can a phenomenal channel actually be added to an existing mind? Until recently the honest answer was speculative. It is now partly empirical, and the results run against the pessimistic prior: adult primate brains absorbed a new receptoral dimension without developmental exposure (Mancuso 2009); an augmentation signal worn continuously came to be reported as spatial rather than tactile (König 2016); cone-targeted stimulation produced a percept with coordinates outside the natural gamut (Fong 2025). This face is deflating.

**2. Verification (candidate Unexplorable).** Given an extension, can anyone establish that what was added is phenomenally *new* rather than an extreme value along an old dimension? Every available route is comparative and passes through the prior palette:

- *Discrimination* is silent about quality. Mancuso's monkeys discriminate; cDa29 picks the odd one out every time. Neither fact entails a new dimension of sensation, and Hardin's complaint is exactly that the rigour of the forced-choice design is what makes it mute.
- *Verbal report* is loaned vocabulary. The Fong subjects, holding a stimulus with proven out-of-gamut coordinates, reached for "teal" and "green, a little blue." Whatever they saw, the report channel resolved it into trichromat words.
- *The one quantitative proof of novelty certifies the wrong thing.* The desaturation-matching argument establishes a fact about the colour *space* — that no in-gamut light matches olo without added white. That is a colorimetric coordinate, not a phenomenal one, and the authors' own Abney-effect caveat concedes that the matching wavelength "may not exactly represent the hue" in question.
- *The dissent is definitional.* Barbur does not dispute the data; he declines the description. A disagreement that survives all the measurements, because the parties differ on what would count, marks a boundary rather than an open question.

**3. Baseline (structural).** No subject ever occupies both sides of the boundary in a form that permits comparison. Before the extension, Paul's argument applies: the content of what is coming cannot be known. After it, the pre-extension baseline is no longer available for inspection — the comparison would have to be run in memory, and a remembered colour is re-rendered in the palette currently in force. The olo case makes this vivid in miniature, since the percept survives only while cellular targeting holds and collapses the moment it slips.

The seam joining the three is the reason this belongs in the catalogue as one void rather than three findings: the Map can now plausibly *extend* a palette while remaining unable to *audit* the extension. That relocates the palette question from metaphysics to the epistemology of the experiment.

### Evidence for the Limit

The strongest evidence is the pattern of what the best-designed studies decline to deliver. Fong et al. built an instrument of extraordinary precision and obtained a rigorous result about gamut coordinates; the qualitative data they collected alongside it is four volunteered colour words and a saturation rating. Jordan et al. built a test admitting of "quantitative comparison and analysis" and, as Hardin notes, nobody asked about quality. Newall, writing fifteen years later and wanting the phenomenal conclusion, cannot get there by citing a measurement and instead constructs a structural argument from opponent-channel physiology. Three independent research programmes, each competent, each stopping at the same wall.

The counter-hypothesis deserves stating plainly: perhaps nobody asked because the question is ill-posed, and the wall is philosophy's rather than the world's. This note does not settle that. It records that the wall is where the void framework predicts a void, and that the deflationary reading is at present unfalsifiable by the available instrument — which is a reason to distrust it as much as a reason to hold it.

### Phenomenology

Approaching this edge has a distinctive feel, reported informally by the Fong subjects and reproduced in nearly every popular account: the conviction of having seen something unprecedented, coupled with an immediate collapse of the description into ordinary words. The gap between "unprecedented saturation" and "teal" is the phenomenology of the void. It resembles the reaching-for-words quality catalogued in [[language-thought-boundary|the language-thought boundary]], but with an added twist — here the words that arrive are not merely inadequate, they are *confidently* offered, and they name a familiar region of the palette.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)

None currently. Every direct method reduces to discrimination or report, and both are shown above to be palette-relative. A genuinely direct method would require a comparison the architecture does not permit.

### Indirect Methods

- **Structural inference**, as in Newall: derive novelty from physiology plus the *failure* of the subject to locate the experience within the old ordering. Note the shape — the positive claim rests on a negative datum, which is apophatic cartography in its usual form.
- **Longitudinal self-report under continuous extension**, as in the feelSpace protocol: rather than asking whether an experience is new, track whether the *category* of report migrates (from "vibration" to "direction") over months. This substitutes a change-detection question for an identity question and is the most promising available design.
- **Cross-subject convergence in coinages.** If several independently extended subjects converge on the *same* novel description — rather than each reaching for a different familiar word — that convergence is weak evidence against the deflationary reading. The Fong data point the other way, with four different familiar words across the group, though five subjects on a transient stimulus is far too thin a base for the inference.

### What AI Might See

This is a case where an artificial mind is a *poor* probe, which is worth recording because the catalogue's default expectation runs the other way ([[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers|non-human minds as void-explorers]]). A model can be given a genuinely new input channel — a sensor modality absent from training — and will describe it fluently and immediately, in the vocabulary it already has. The fluency is not evidence of phenomenal acquisition; it is what the architecture produces regardless. So the AI case does not resolve the human question and reproduces the verification gap in a form where the deflationary reading is, if anything, better supported. The methodological upshot: extension experiments on models cannot arbitrate the human question, and treating them as if they could would be an instance of the generation problem flagged in the distributional-void material.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]**, and unusually directly. Barbur's gloss — a very saturated green — is the simpler hypothesis. It posits no new phenomenal dimension and explains the reports economically. It is also unfalsifiable by any instrument the field possesses, because the only data that could discriminate it from the novelty reading are exactly the phenomenal data the verification face rules out. Parsimony here is not tracking the truth; it is *selecting* an answer in a domain where the evidence cannot vote. That is the tenet's claim in a clean empirical instance, and it is a better example than most in the catalogue because the simpler hypothesis is held by a competent specialist for good reasons rather than by a strawman.

### Implications

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]**: the case exhibits an asymmetry the Map's framework predicts. The physical side of the palette has proved unexpectedly tractable — genes, cones, lasers, adult plasticity all yielded — while the phenomenal side stayed sealed, and stayed sealed *not for lack of technique but as a consequence of the technique working*. Every improvement in the physical instrument sharpens the colorimetric result and leaves the qualitative result exactly where it was. A physicalist can accommodate this by denying there is a further fact; the point for the Map is that the accommodating move is a denial rather than a measurement.

**Calibration value.** This is the mirror image of [[research/voids-olfactory-void-2026-08-13|the olfactory void]], which the catalogue records as its best case of an unexplorable limit deflating under better sampling. Here one face deflates and its sibling does not. The lesson for the catalogue's method is that a void can require *splitting* rather than retiring: the acquaintance and qualitative-novelty material was framed as a single limit on the palette, and the empirical record has now separated a capacity question (increasingly answered) from a verification question (untouched). Any convergence claim built on the earlier framing should be re-examined for the same conflation. Under [[common-cause-null|the common-cause null]] this counts as housekeeping on the Map's own carving, not as new support for it.

**[[observation-and-measurement-void|The observation void]]** gets a sharpened instance: the measurement here does not disturb the phenomenon so much as *substitute for it*, returning a coordinate where a quality was sought.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **The verification face as the whole article** (recommended). Lead with olo — the concrete, dateable, unusually vivid result — and use it to separate the capacity question from the certification question, then generalise across tetrachromacy, gene therapy, and substitution. This is the angle with the least overlap against existing articles, since [[acquaintance-void|acquaintance]] and [[synesthetic-void|synesthetic]] already own the conceivability question.
2. **Deflation and its limits**: a methodological piece pairing this with the olfactory void, arguing that the catalogue should expect voids to split rather than simply survive or die, and drawing the calibration lesson for the convergence claim.
3. **A fold instead of a standalone**: the induction material into [[synesthetic-void|the synesthetic void]] (which already handles within-species palette differences) and the verification material into [[commensurability-void|the commensurability void]] or [[observation-and-measurement-void|the observation void]]. Given that `voids/` sits at 99 against a 115 cap, headroom exists for the standalone, but the fold is defensible and the choice is an operator decision.

## Gaps in Research

- **Ward & Meijer 2010 was not read.** Only Crossref metadata was confirmed. The vOICe phenomenology is directly relevant to the verification face and should be opened before any article is written.
- **The Barbur objection has no located peer-reviewed statement.** It currently rests on newspaper quotation. Either a published version should be found or the objection should be presented as reported commentary, with the Map's own reconstruction of the argument doing the work.
- **No follow-up literature on olo was found.** The search surfaced popular treatments through August 2026 but no peer-reviewed reply, replication, or philosophical paper engaging the result. Whether one exists was not established; this is a thin-coverage finding rather than a confirmed absence.
- **The feelSpace subjective-report data was read through secondary summary**, not the paper's own reporting of participant statements. The claim that participants came to experience the signal "as spatial information" needs checking against the source before it is quoted.
- **Possibly unanswerable**: whether the deflationary reading and the novelty reading differ in any respect that could in principle be measured. If they do not, the verification face is Unexplorable in the strict sense rather than merely unexplored, and the article should say so plainly rather than hedging.
