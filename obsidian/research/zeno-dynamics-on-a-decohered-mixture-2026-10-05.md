## Executive Summary

The standard theory answers three of the task's four questions and half of the fourth. (1) The Zeno theorem holds for density matrices and for projectors of any rank; with a multi-dimensional projector the state is confined to the subspace and keeps evolving inside it, and for a spatial region the confined dynamics has hard walls. Stapp's sentence that mixedness "makes no difference at all" is correct as a statement about the *state*. (2) Zeno confinement differs from conditioning a classical stochastic process exactly when the leak out of the subspace is still quadratic in time. Once the environment resolves "inside" from "outside", the leak becomes a constant rate, and Kiefer and Joos state plainly that a rate process "is not influenced by repeated observations". (3) Inside the quadratic window the effect shows in the unconditional, non-selective density matrix, so it is not a post-selection artefact; in the rate regime nothing changes, conditionally or unconditionally. (4) Neither Stapp nor Georgiev is right without qualification. Stapp's derivation evolves the brain's reduced state unitarily between projections, which drops the environment during the interval; with it restored, the mind's projections add something only if they come faster than the environment resolves the same distinction. Georgiev's redundancy charge is correct in the opposite regime. A timing requirement therefore survives for Stapp's model, in a different form from the one the Map used to attribute to him. No source computes where a brain sits between the regimes. That part stays open.

## Key Sources

### Facchi & Pascazio (2002), "Quantum Zeno Subspaces"
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0201115 (PRL 89, 080401; DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.89.080401)
- **Type**: Paper, read in full
- **Key points**:
  - The theorem is stated for an initial density matrix ρ₀ and a complete set of orthogonal projectors of arbitrary rank, with the *non-selective* map ρ → Σₙ PₙρPₙ applied N times in time t.
  - In the N → ∞ limit the population of each subspace is constant: pₙ(t) = Tr(ρ(t)Pₙ) = pₙ(0).
  - Inside each subspace the dynamics continues, generated by PₙHPₙ.
  - The same result follows with no projections at all, from strong continuous coupling to an apparatus; "decoherence-free subspaces" are treated as a case.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Standard physics.
- **Quotes** (verbatim): "It is important to stress that the quantum Zeno effect does not necessarily freeze everything. On the contrary, for frequent projections onto a multi-dimensional subspace, the system can evolve away from its initial state, although it remains in the subspace defined by the measurement." And: "probability is conserved in each subspace and no probability “leakage” between any two subspaces is possible. The total Hilbert space splits into invariant subspaces and the different components of the wave function (or density matrix) evolve independently within each sector."

### Facchi & Pascazio (2008), "Quantum Zeno dynamics: mathematical and physical aspects"
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.3297 (J. Phys. A 41, 493001; DOI 10.1088/1751-8113/41/49/493001)
- **Type**: Topical review, relevant sections read (§§1–4, 10, 13)
- **Key points**:
  - Defines the vocabulary the Map lacks. A measurement is "incomplete" when outcomes are lumped together, so that the projector is multi-dimensional; it is "nonselective" when the apparatus "does not select the different outcomes, but simply destroys the phase correlations between some states, provoking the transition from a pure state to a mixture" (verbatim).
  - §13.1 extends Misra and Sudarshan's theorem to incomplete, non-selective measurements; an initial "incoherent superposition" evolves sector by sector.
  - Position projections: "if the measurements ascertain whether a quantum particle is in a given spatial region, the evolution is unitary and the generator of the Zeno dynamics is the Hamiltonian with hard-wall (Dirichlet) boundary conditions" (verbatim, abstract). This is the case Stapp's 2012 "interval in coordinate space" falls under. The underlying paper is Facchi, Pascazio, Scardicchio & Schulman, "Zeno dynamics yields ordinary constraints" (arXiv:quant-ph/0101037; journal metadata not confirmed here).
  - The effect needs no collapse: "the main physical features of the Zeno effect are a consequence of the dynamics and need not be ascribed to the “collapse” of the wave function" (verbatim).
  - The Zeno time τ_Z is "a (sometimes very inaccurate) quantitative estimate of the duration of the short-time quadratic behavior" (verbatim).
  - States that as of 2008 "No experiments have been performed so far in order to check the multidimensional Zeno effect and the Zeno dynamics" (verbatim). Later experiments exist but were not searched for; do not carry this sentence into an article as current.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.

### Kiefer & Joos (1998/1999), "Decoherence: Concepts and Examples"
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9803052 (in *Quantum Future*, Lecture Notes in Physics, Springer, pp. 105–128; DOI 10.1007/BFb0105342; Crossref gives no year, the arXiv preprint is March 1998)

## The Four Questions

### 1. Mixed states and multi-dimensional projectors
Settled in the literature. The theorem is formulated for density matrices and for projectors of any rank (Facchi and Pascazio extend Misra and Sudarshan to the incomplete, non-selective case). The result is confinement and not freezing; within the subspace the state evolves under PHP, and for a spatial interval that means reflection from hard walls. Initial mixedness is irrelevant: a state already block-diagonal in {P, 1−P} is the theorem's starting point. On this point Stapp 2000 is right, and Georgiev's Theorem 4 does not say otherwise.

### 2. Is confinement of a classical mixture distinct from classical conditioning?
It depends on one comparison: the interval between projections against the time over which the leak from P to 1−P stays quadratic (the Zeno time, or Koshino and Shimizu's jump time). The quadratic law is the quantum content. It exists because population leaves the subspace only by first building coherence between inside and outside. If the environment destroys that coherence faster than the next projection arrives, the leak is a rate, and a rate process conditioned on repeated observation is unchanged (Kiefer and Joos; Koshino and Shimizu). In that regime nothing quantum is left for the extra projections to act on. A second limit follows from the same sources: any transport across the boundary that is already a classical rate (thermal noise, Markovian hopping) is untouched by projections at any frequency.

### 3. The unconditional density matrix
Inside the quadratic window the Zeno effect is an unconditional effect. Facchi and Pascazio's theorem is stated for the non-selective map and concludes that subspace populations are constant; no outcome is selected. Stapp's equation (3) and Georgiev's "unconditional probabilities" agree that this is the register in which the effect is judged. It follows that a Zeno hold, where it operates, would *not* be an instance of what the Map calls ensemble-level epiphenomenalism: the ensemble statistics differ from the unmeasured case. Two things come with that. The difference is produced by the non-selective asking, so which answer nature gives plays no part and any physical dephasing of the same strength does the same work. And outside the window the unconditional matrix is unchanged, which is Georgiev's diagonal case.

Theorem 4 is not in tension with this. It compares entropy immediately before and after a projection. A Zeno-held ensemble never has its entropy lowered by a projection; its entropy simply grows more slowly than the unmeasured ensemble's. (This note's reading of the theorem's scope, checked against the toy model below and not against a published statement.)

### 4. The note's own derivation: a two-state toy model

*This section is a derivation by this note. It is not a literature finding and should be labelled as the Map's working if used.*

Let "in" and "out" stand for P and 1−P, coupled by H = (Ω/2)σₓ, with the environment dephasing in the same {in, out} basis so that the in–out coherence decays at rate γ. This is Stapp's conceded case: environment and mind share a basis. Take γ ≫ Ω.

- Environment alone. Eliminating the coherence gives a rate equation with hop rate Γ = Ω²/(2γ). The environment has already slowed the transition from the coherent rate Ω to Γ. This is Georgiev's "decoherence alone can achieve the quantum Zeno effect".
- Add non-selective projections {P, 1−P} at interval τ. After each one the state is diagonal. To second order in Ω, the coherence built during the next interval is c(s) = −i(Ω/2)(1 − e^(−γs))/γ, and the population transferred is p(τ) = (Ω²/2γ)[τ − (1 − e^(−γτ))/γ].
- Effective hop rate with projections: R(τ) = p(τ)/τ = Γ·[1 − (1 − e^(−x))/x], with x = γτ.

Limits: for x ≪ 1, p ≈ Ω²τ²/4, the quadratic law, and R ≈ Γx/2 → 0. For x ≫ 1, R → Γ and the projections do nothing. Numerical check (Lindblad evolution, Ω = 1, γ = 20, total time 4): R/Γ = 0.902, 0.693, 0.368, 0.136, 0.048 at x = 10, 3, 1, 0.3, 0.1, against 0.900, 0.683, 0.368, 0.136, 0.048 from the formula. These are the unconditional populations.

Reading: the mind's projections reduce the hop rate by half or more only when x ≲ 1.6, that is, when they arrive at intervals comparable to or shorter than the time the environment takes to dephase "in" from "out". Slower than that, they are redundant with the environment to within a factor 1 − 1/x. Because Stapp's equation (4) has no γ, it sits at x = 0 by construction, which is why his linear terms vanish for any d.

**What the toy model does not show.** It is two states. For Stapp's interval in coordinate space the relevant γ is not Tegmark's rate for macroscopically distinct states: position decoherence suppresses coherence between points farther apart than the coherence length ℓ of the "narrow Gaussians", and leaves coherence over shorter distances. A packet within about ℓ of the interval's edge is one the environment has *not* resolved as in or out, so there the mind's projector does distinguish something the environment does not. The window in which that matters is roughly the time a packet takes to cross a distance ℓ; after that the flux through the boundary is classical. This is a scaling argument by this note, with no number attached, and no source consulted computes ℓ or the crossing time for the collective field modes Stapp names. It gives the "interval grain against strip width" comparison on the Process 1 page a definite form: the grain does not matter, the strip width sets the window.

## Major Positions

### Stapp: the hold works on a mixture
- **Core claim**: the Zeno effect "is not destroyed by the fact that S(t)b is a mixture: that makes no difference at all" (2000); the mind "rolls back the brain-based process of evolution" (2012 draft).
- **What the literature supports**: mixedness of the state is irrelevant; the effect is unconditional; a classical model cannot reproduce it.
- **What it does not support**: indifference to ongoing environmental monitoring. Only the coherent, quadratic part of the brain's spreading can be rolled back, and the environment Stapp grants converts that part to a rate on its own timescale. Stapp's stated repetition scale (tens of milliseconds) against his stated decoherence scale leaves the hold in the rate regime unless the relevant window is far longer than the decoherence figures he accepts. He has not addressed this in any text read. His 2015 rejoinder claims "a simple proof that environmental decoherence does not nullify the quantum Zeno effect"; only its abstract has been located, and it may contain the missing argument.
- **Relation to tenets**: a model on which the mind's lever is measurement *rate* fits Bidirectional Interaction and is Born-intact. It is not minimal in the sense of escaping a timing requirement.

### Georgiev: same basis means redundant
- **Core claim**: where mind and environment share a basis, "the action of the mind becomes redundant with the action of the environment".
- **What the literature supports**: exactly this, in the rate regime. The toy model gives it a number (1 − 1/x).
- **What it does not support**: redundancy as a general theorem. Theorem 4 bounds entropy and is silent on rates. In Georgiev's own simulations the mind projects at ξ → 0 against an environment acting every τ, a regime where the mind is doing the holding and the environment is the slower monitor. The redundancy sentence is an assertion about the other regime and is not derived in the paper.

### The physics community's default (Joos, Kiefer, Facchi, Pascazio, Koshino, Shimizu)
- **Core claim**: Zeno suppression and rate behaviour are two ends of one dynamical continuum set by how strongly and how fast the system is monitored; collapse is not needed for either.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral on dualism. It removes any special role for *selection* in the Zeno effect, which matters for how the Map describes Stapp: the work is done by Process 1, and nature's answer is idle.

## Key Debates

### Does Stapp's model carry a timing requirement?
- **Sides**: Stapp says the model was designed to be unaffected by decoherence. The corridor's earlier wording said he must outpace it. The optimistic review of 2026-10-05 corrected the Map toward Stapp's self-description.
- **Core disagreement**: which coherence matters. The coherence of the initial superposition is irrelevant, as Stapp says. The in–out coherence rebuilt between projections is what the hold feeds on, and the environment attacks it.
- **Current state**: open in print as far as this note can find. The honest wording is that Stapp denies needing a surviving superposition, and that standard Zeno theory still requires his projections to fall inside the quadratic window, whose length in a brain nobody has computed. Today's edits ("measurement need not outpace decoherence", reported as Stapp's position on `forward-in-time-conscious-selection` L97) remain accurate as attribution; they should not be read as the physics.
