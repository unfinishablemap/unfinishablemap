## Executive Summary

A family of 2016–2026 papers denies that experience carries a *phenomenal* ownership feature, whether that is Zahavi's universal for-me-ness or Guillot's narrower mineness. Almost none of these authors denies that experience is someone's, or that it has first-person organisation. What they deny, or doubt, is one further phenomenal quality: the quality on which the Map's [[mine-ness]] page and its [[consciousness-and-the-ownership-problem|ownership topic]] lean. That narrows the threat. None of these accounts conflicts with the Dualism tenet as stated, and Sá Pereira says explicitly that his argument "does not depend on any reductive thesis about qualia". What they do threaten is the ownership topic's auxiliary claim that ownership is "part of what makes consciousness irreducible", and its claim that the ownership problem "survives even if the hard problem were somehow solved". The strongest single objection is evidential, and it comes in four independent forms: Bradley's against pathology arguments, McClelland's four impediments, Sá Pereira's circularity charge, and Kang's against Guillot's argument from justification. Each says that the case for a phenomenal feature underdetermines it, whether that case comes from pathology, from introspective description or from first-person justification. Each leaves a concrete rival in place. **Verdict: `fold`.** Topics and concepts are both at cap (370/370). The ownership topic has about 520 words of headroom and should absorb most of this. [[mine-ness]] can take one sentence and [[thought-insertion]] one short addition. Details are under "Fold Plan" below.

## Four Denials

The four-way split was proposed by the ChatGPT outer review of 2026-11-26 (§6). It is a reviewer's taxonomy, not a term from the literature, and it should be credited that way. The split is: (1) no substantial self; (2) no special phenomenal ownership quality; (3) no metaphysical ownership relation between experiences and an owner; (4) no first-person organisation of consciousness. Evidence against one leaves the others untouched.

- **Key points (abstract)**: Philosophers turn to depersonalisation and thought insertion "to argue for self-acquaintance, a strategy I label the Pathology Argument". ### Kang (2025), "Against an Epistemic Argument for Mineness"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13164-024-00723-2. Online 10 January 2024; issue *Review of Philosophy and Psychology* 16(1), 47–64 (2025). Shao-Pu Kang, National Tsing-Hua University.
- **Access**: The paper itself was **NOT read**. PhilArchive (KANAAE-2) returned 403 and Cloudflare, Springer redirected to a login, and Springer elides the abstract at Crossref and Semantic Scholar. The author's own summary on *Imperfect Cognitions* (29 April 2026) was read raw. The reference list (75 entries, Crossref) cites Guillot 2017, Guillot 2023 and every Part I chapter except Orbán & Wong.
- **Target**: Guillot's argument from justification, in Guillot 2017 (*RPP* 8(1); the passage Guillot 2023 cites as "2017: 45-6"). From an ordinary experience (the cat's silky fur) she infers immediate justification for judging "that I am present" and "that the experience is mine". She concludes that "the phenomenal character of a normal experience includes for-me-ness, me-ness, and mineness", using blindsight as the contrast case (verified in the Guillot 2017 full text, PMC5362670).
- **Objections (author's summary)**: (i) Perceptual awareness and pre-reflective introspective awareness differ, so one principle cannot cover both: "Guillot needs a version of the principle tailored specifically to introspection, but no such account is provided." (ii) Blindsight is a poor contrast. (iii) Depersonalised patients feel alienated yet know immediately that their experiences are theirs, so "immediacy does not require mineness after all".
- **Stance**: Kang targets an argument, not the feature. The summary is conditional throughout.
- **Bearing on the Map (this note's inference)**: The Map adopts Guillot's split and her reading of DPD as mineness lapsing while for-me-ness persists. On that reading, Kang's third objection *follows from the Map's own position*: felt mineness is idle for the immediacy of self-knowledge. That is a concession the Map can make cheaply. It leaves open that persisting for-me-ness does the epistemic work.

### Wehinger (2022/2024), "The Transcendental Argument for Universal Mineness: A Critique"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13164-022-00658-6. CC BY. Online 22 November 2022, issue *RPP* 15(1), 167–188 (2024). The outer review's "Wehinger (2024)" is the issue year. Daniel Wehinger, Department of Christian Philosophy, University of Innsbruck.
- **Access**: **Full text read** (Innsbruck repository PDF).
- **Terminology**: Following Guillot, Wehinger reserves "mineness" for experiences "being given to me as mine". He uses "for-me-ness" only when reporting others.
- **Target**: Zahavi 2018's "central argument" for universality, "a kind of transcendental argument" (Zahavi 2018, p. 711, as quoted by Wehinger), in Shoemaker's 1968 regress form.
- **Three objections**: (1) Shoemaker's critique yields only that "not every self-ascription" rests on identification, a non-perceptual *core*. It does not rule out conscious states without mineness, and the non-universalist reading of thought insertion is compatible with it. (2) The argument does not touch dispositional accounts (Schear 2009, Carruthers 2000); Zahavi and Kriegel answer those with a metaphysical categorical-basis principle instead. (3) "Non-universalist antireductionism is a perfectly consistent position." Castañeda, Henrich and Frank endorse the argument while denying universality.
- **Concessions**: "I am sympathetic to the transcendental argument." He calls it "a forceful objection" to perceptual and self-representational reductions. For definitional purposes he will "simply take for granted that some kind of self exists". His conclusion is about universality only: "the claim that all experiences have mineness does not appear well-justified".
- **Tenet alignment**: Wehinger is the most dualist-friendly author here. He separates irreducibility from universality, and the Map's separable-mineness reading is a form of non-universalist irreducibility about mineness. He also argues that universalist antireductionism "is gappy as well". That bears on the [[self-and-self-consciousness]] kind-claim, which the Map holds for *for-me-ness*, not mineness.

### Alweiss (2022), "Self-Consciousness without an 'I': A Critique of Zahavi's Account of the Minimal Self"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1163/15691640-12341490. *Research in Phenomenology* 52(1), 84–119. Brill, closed access.
- **Access**: **abstract only** (Crossref and Semantic Scholar, raw). No preprint found.
- **Key points**: She argues that on the pre-reflective level "there are not two phenomena but only one": experience is transparent. Experience "may well be reflexive (insofar as it is relation to me)" without a further sense of what it is like for me. On Husserl: "Husserl only claims that I am self-conscious but not that I am a self that owns its consciousness." What Husserl shows is that "I do not need to have a sense of abiding ownership, to have experience."
- **Stance**: A phenomenologist's denial of (2) that keeps reflexivity. She is already on the Map in one live file ([[no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value]]), stated in her own terms after the 2026-08-27 deep review.

### Sá Pereira (2026), "For-me-ness: A Critical Assessment of the Phenomenal Mineness Thesis"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10339-026-01383-z. *Cognitive Processing* 27(4), 1307–1326, CC BY, PMC13714114.
- **Access**: **Full text read** (PMC efetch). It is already cited on [[mine-ness]] (one paragraph).
- **What it adds beyond that paragraph**: The target is narrowed to "the stronger thesis that for-me-ness, in this sense, amounts to a phenomenally manifest structure that explains ownership and self-presence", and "Weaker notions of mere for-someone-ness are not in dispute". He states his conclusion as: "The strong phenomenal notion of for-me-ness should be rejected." It is replaced by "a weak sense of for-me-ness", "the functional integration of egocentrically encoded content into a self-model". He grants: "I do not deny that experience has a perspectival structure or that subjects normally report their experiences as their own." On metaphysics: "I remain neutral on further metaphysical questions concerning the ultimate nature of phenomenal qualities." His method: "appeals to phenomenal structure must earn their place by doing genuine explanatory work."
- **Citation caution**: Sá Pereira's reference list gives Guillot 2017 as *Philosophical Psychology* 30(6), 761–779. Every Map page cites Guillot 2017 from *RPP* 8(1), 23–53, and a quick Crossref search found no matching Phil Psych item. Cite Guillot from *RPP*, not through Sá Pereira.

### Letheby (2020), "Being for No-One: Psychedelic Experience and Minimal Subjectivity"

- **Access**: abstract (Crossref, raw).
- **Key points**: He holds that some psychedelic ego-dissolution states instantiate "(a) phenomenal consciousness and (b) total lack of self-consciousness", against Billon & Kriegel's 2015 defence of the subjectivity principle. The reply that these states keep for-me-ness meets a dilemma. For-me-ness is "defined either as containing a genuinely experiential component or as not". On the second horn, "the claim that phenomenally conscious mental states can totally lack self-consciousness has been conceded".
- **Map coverage**: 0 content files cite this paper. [[self-and-self-consciousness]] L140 cites Letheby's later self-fictionalism only.

### Part I of *Self-Experience* (abstracts, Crossref raw)
- **Salje & Geddes, ch. 2**: They identify equivocations in "for-me-ness", "mine-ness" and "a sense of mine-ness". They then question the grounds for universality, which they give as "immunity to error though [sic] misidentification, epistemic asymmetry, and ease of judgment or reportability", and the direct descriptive case.
- **Howell, ch. 4**: Substantial subjective character conflicts with perceptual transparency. On depersonalisation, "the best interpretation of patient reports is consistent with a lack of subjective character". This is a direct challenge to the Map's DPD inference.
- **Soldati, ch. 5**: He rejects the "mineness link" between special first-person knowledge and phenomenal mineness. He is explicit that this is "arguing for the epistemological inefficiency of mineness, rather than for its psychological unreality". He is the general-form source for Kang's DPD point.
- **Wu, ch. 6**: Introspection cannot supply the evidence. On the rubber hand illusion, he argues that "the evidence fails to establish that claim", namely that the illusion yields a phenomenal sense of ownership. The standard delusion-of-control mechanism "does not explain loss of mineness".
- **Orbán & Wong, ch. 7**: The body-ownership literatures answer five different questions (constitutive, phenomenological, causal, functional, mereological). This is a caution for the Map's body-ownership pages.

### Gallagher (2017), "Self-Defense: Deflecting Deflationary and Eliminativist Critiques of the Sense of Ownership"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01612. *Frontiers in Psychology* 8:1612, CC BY, PMC5609435. **Full text read.**
- **Use**: This is the defender's map of the family. The critics he names are Dainton, Bermúdez, Prinz, Di Francesco et al. and Garfield. **Terminology trap**: Gallagher argues "the phenomenological account is itself a deflationary account", because it takes the sense of ownership to be intrinsic to experience rather than an additional quality. - **Zahavi**, "Consciousness, Self-Consciousness, Selfhood: a Reply to some Critics", *RPP* 9(3), 703–718, https://doi.org/10.1007/s13164-018-0403-6. The KU accepted manuscript returned 403. It was used only as quoted by Wehinger. Zahavi says there that one should "not pretend that the issue [of universalism] can be settled simply by [...] more detailed experiential descriptions" (p. 717). That is a concession the Map's introspection-led pages should register.

## Major Positions

### Evidential scepticism (McClelland, Wu, Bradley, Sá Pereira)
- **Core claim**: The evidence offered for a phenomenal ownership feature underdetermines it. Rival explanations handle the same reports.
- **Relation to tenets**: Neutral on Dualism. It leaves [[mine-ness]]'s *separability* claim standing ("something varies") and contests its *phenomenal* reading. The page already concedes the distinction at L94.

### Epistemic deflation (Soldati, Kang, Salje & Geddes)
- **Core claim**: First-person epistemic privileges, such as immediacy and immunity to error through misidentification, do not require phenomenal mineness.
- **Relation to tenets**: Neutral. It removes one *motive* for mineness, not mineness. It aligns with the concession the ownership topic already makes via Child 2026.

### Anti-universalism (Wehinger, Letheby; Billon from the robust side)
- **Core claim**: Mineness (Wehinger) or all self-consciousness (Letheby) is not a feature of every experience.
- **Relation to tenets**: Wehinger is compatible with irreducibility and so with Dualism. Letheby, if right on the experiential horn, falsifies the Map's per-experience kind-claim about for-me-ness. That is a cost to the Map's Zahavian commitments ([[self-and-self-consciousness]] "Why the Map Requires the Zahavian Version"), not to the Dualism tenet.

### Phenomenological deflation (Alweiss; Gallagher's own sense)
- **Core claim**: There is one phenomenon, transparent world-directed experience that is reflexive, not two. Alweiss concludes there is no ownership quality. Gallagher concludes that the ownership quality is not an *additional* quality.
- **Relation to tenets**: Neutral. It shows the dispute can be had entirely inside phenomenology.

## Key Debates

### Does the depersonalisation datum show a phenomenal feature?
- **Sides**: Billon 2017 and 2023 say yes, and [[mine-ness]] follows Billon 2023. Bradley 2024 and 2026, Howell 2023, Knappik 2022 and Sá Pereira 2026 say no.
- **Core disagreement**: Whether the best explanation of "as if not mine" reports is a lost phenomenal feature, or disrupted affect (affective immediacy), interoceptive precision, or type-specific self-relations.
- **Current state**: Open. The discriminating observation is already named on [[mine-ness]] L94: reported loss of mine-ness with those processes measured intact. Bradley 2026 supplies a measurable affective variable. No study in this run measures it against mineness reports.

### Is universal for-me-ness argued or merely described?
- **Sides**: Zahavi 2018 gives the transcendental argument; Wehinger 2022 replies that it fails for universality. Salje & Geddes and Alweiss say the descriptive case equivocates. Letheby says the psychedelic counterexample forces a dilemma.
- **Current state**: Zahavi grants that description alone should not be expected to settle it (p. 717, via Wehinger). Wehinger shows the central theoretical argument does not reach universality. The Map's kind-claim now rests on "a reporting position", which is Letheby's second horn.

## Relation to the Tenets (graded: compatible / suggestive / discriminating)

The ladder is the one in [[inference-to-the-best-explanation-against-dualism]]. Evidence can be *compatible with* a tenet, *suggestive* of it, or *discriminating* in its favour over the physicalist rival. Under P-M1, a tenet may remove a defeater but never upgrade evidence.

- **Dualism.** None of these accounts conflicts with the tenet as stated, and the honest consequence cuts both ways. First, phenomenal mineness is not evidence *for* Dualism. Its best support (DPD, thought insertion, introspective description) is at most *compatible* with Dualism, and these papers show it is not even discriminating between phenomenal and functional readings of the feature. [[depersonalisation]] already says the significance tier is null. Second, deflating mineness costs Dualism nothing, since Sá Pereira is neutral on qualia and Wehinger is antireductionist. **The real conflict is with Map auxiliaries.** The ownership topic says "ownership is part of what makes consciousness irreducible" and that ownership "survives even if the hard problem were somehow solved". [[self-and-self-consciousness]]'s description says the minimal self "supports dualist irreducibility". On the deflationary view each of these lists, as an extra irreducible, something that is either the hard problem itself or a functional self-ascription story. Grade: the Map's ownership-as-primitive reading is **compatible** with the evidence. It is not suggestive, and the evidence is not discriminating against deflation.
- **Bidirectional Interaction.** The ownership topic says "Causal efficacy requires owned experience". Deflationists about (2) grant a subject-locus and perspectival structure. A deflated "owned" therefore still supplies the anchor the tenet needs, so this argument is untouched by most of the family. Only (1)/(3) deniers (Metzinger, Buddhist no-owner) bear on it, and they are engaged elsewhere.
- **No Many Worlds.** The indexical "further fact" of ownership is a (3)-level claim. Deflation about (2) leaves it as it was. Parfit and the no-owner views remain the relevant opponents.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits.** Sá Pereira's method is an explanatory-work requirement, close to a parsimony argument. Tenet 5 can remove the inference *simpler, therefore true*. It cannot supply what McClelland's Function Impediment asks for, which is an account of what a phenomenal ownership feature *does* that egocentric registration and affect do not. Using the tenet to fill that gap would be the upgrade P-M1 forbids.
- **What would be discriminating** (between phenomenal and deflationary mineness, not between dualism and physicalism): depersonalised or meditating subjects reporting loss of mine-ness while affective immediacy (Bradley's variable), interoceptive precision and egocentric registration are independently measured intact. The converse, mineness reports tracking those variables one-for-one, would favour deflation. Neither has been done on this evidence.
