**Can individual vices yield collective epistemic benefit?** Yes, in idealised models and in a few reconstructed case histories, under conditions that are often not met:

1. **The positive results.** Kitcher (1990) showed that credit-seeking scientists can divide effort between rival methods better than "their epistemically pure cousins" under some conditions. Zollman (2007, 2010) showed that a community can be more reliable when its members see fewer of each other's results, or when some hold extreme priors, because rival hypotheses survive misleading early data. Peters (2021) argued from the psychology of reasoning that confirmation bias can raise group reliability: advocates develop their own view thoroughly and criticise rivals well. Morton (2014) and Smart (2018) named the pattern "Mandevillian".
2. **The conditions.** The benefit needs partiality distributed across rival positions (Solomon; Peters), "a diversity of viewpoints, an equal distribution of power" (Peters, who grants that these conditions "are frequently not met"), and diversity that is transient: "the real epistemic goal is not diversity but transient diversity" (Zollman 2010). It also needs evidence that can eventually decide between the rivals, which every model assumes.
3. **The negative results.** Too much isolation combined with dogmatism means the community never converges (Zollman 2010). The less-connectivity effect occupies "a relatively small niche" of parameter space (Rosenstock et al. 2017), and connectedness effects are "highly context-dependent" (Frey & Šešelja 2020). The epistemic-landscape case for diversity "largely derive[s] from implementation errors" (Alexander et al. 2015, submitted version). Stable polarisation arises when agents discount evidence from those whose beliefs differ, with no confirmation bias needed (O'Connor & Weatherall 2018). One intransigently biased agent can stop a community converging (Holman & Bruner 2015), and selective sharing of honest results can mislead rational policymakers (Weatherall et al. 2020). Career incentives can select for bad methods with "no conscious strategizing" (Smaldino & McElreath 2016).

**Consciousness science** shows the partisan vice in measurable form. Across 412 experiments, "supporting a specific theory can be predicted solely from methodological choices, irrespective of findings" (Yaron et al. 2022). It meets the diversity condition: the field surveys report that "many views and opinions currently coexist" (Francken et al. 2022). COGITATE is a deliberate attempt to install Peters's conditions: rival proponents, preregistered divergent predictions, and theory-neutral laboratories. The 2023 "pseudoscience" letter and its replies show the trust condition under strain. Nothing found tests whether the field will converge.

**For the voids admission test (level 3)** the literature supplies conditions, not a verdict. Interested parties checking one another is a sound corrective in these models when tilts are spread across rival camps and the evidence can decide. Whether it reaches the interested party void's survival and fundamentality questions is open. The void's existential stake is shared by every mortal inquirer, so it may act as a common cause rather than as a camp-specific bias, and those questions lack the discriminating evidence the models assume. The models also do not show that the tilt survives correction. See [[#admission-test|Bearing on the admission test]].

**Tenet grade: no bearing** on Dualism, Minimal Quantum Interaction, Bidirectional Interaction or No Many Worlds. These are results about community dynamics, and their agents are minimal Bayesian or search-rule learners that a physicalist can accept without change. Tenet 5 is not engaged either way (`tenets.md` L145: the tenet "binds the Map's use of parsimony, not only its critics'").

**Relation to the morning's note.** The argumentative-theory note (`research/argumentative-theory-of-reasoning-and-the-argument-from-reason-2026-11-27`) covers the psychology. Peters 2021 builds on that same psychology: "Mercier" occurs 25 times in his text, and his evidence is Trouche et al. 2016 and Mercier & Sperber 2017. The earlier note's limits therefore carry over to Peters: the gains are clearest on problems with demonstrable answers, and like-minded groups polarise. This note adds the formal and social-epistemology side, which the earlier note did not cover.

## Key Sources

### Peters, U. (2021). "Illegitimate Values, Confirmation Bias, and Mandevillian Cognition in Science." *The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 72(4): 1061–1081. doi:10.1093/bjps/axy079
- **Bibliography verified**: Crossref gives vol. 72, issue 4, pp. 1061–1081, issued 1 Dec 2021. Advance access: OpenAlex gives 18 Dec 2018, and the PDF footer reads "© The Author(s) 2018". The outer review's details are correct.
- **Text read**: the OUP advance-access PDF (24 pp., "Brit. J. Phil. Sci. 0 (2020), 1–24"), grepped. Page numbers below are that version's and **do not match the 72(4) pagination**. Cite by section.
- **Key points**:
  - Target: the "Confirmatory Value (CV) view", that values directing inquiry to predetermined conclusions are always illegitimate. He attributes versions of it to Anderson 2004, Douglas 2009/2016, Brown 2013, Elliott 2017 and Longino 1990/2002.
  - Argument (§4.2): a five-scientist thought experiment on the origin of life. Biased advocates search harder for support and, faced with counter-evidence, tend to revise "auxiliary assumptions to V rather than the proposal itself". Because people are "more critical of other people's arguments than of their own" (quoting Trouche et al. 2016), group criticism corrects each advocate. The group explores the hypothesis space more deeply than impartial members would.
  - **No agent-based model.** "agent-based" occurs 0 times. The single "simulation" hit reports Zollman 2010. The Gemini outer review's statement that Peters "deploys agent-based modeling" is wrong (Side Finding 1).
  - Conditions (§5.1, verbatim): "It sets aside many aspects of the social context in which science actually takes place and assumes scientific environments with (inter alia) a diversity of viewpoints, social criticism, and an equal distribution of power and resources among scientists. These conditions are frequently not met in actual scientific research." And: "the argument applies only where there exist within the group a diversity of viewpoints, an equal distribution of power, and so on."
  - He grants that industry-capture cases (pharmaceutical, chemical, fossil-fuel interests with "disproportionate power to fund research and suppress or obscure evidence") are where restricting values "is highly plausible".
  - Against Longino (§5.1): "comprehensive uptake, which seems to be what Longino calls for, is not required", and "Longino's proposal of comprehensive uptake is likely to be too strong". Uptake is needed only from "most of the group, or the group as a whole".
  - Dogmatism (§5.2) can provide "(i) protection against premature scientific convergence and consensus, (ii) motivation for opponents to better develop their objections and alternative theories, and (iii) a way to ensure all research avenues are explored". He calls these "Mandevillian effects" and cites Smart 2018, Rowbottom 2011 and Popper.
  - Conclusion: such values "in some cases ... contribute to the reliability of scientific belief-formation at the group level".
- **Coiner** (Peters §1): "Epistemically imperfect mental processes or states that have such grouplevel benefits have been called 'Mandevillian' cognitions (Morton [2014]; Smart [2018])" (the PDF text reads "grouplevel"; restore the hyphen when quoting). He credits Mandeville with first proposing that private shortcomings "may promote public goods".
- **Abstract (verbatim, OpenAlex = PDF)**: "Values that drive inquiry to the confirmation of predetermined conclusions can contribute to the reliability of scientific inquiry at the group level, even when they negatively affect an individual's cognition." It ends: "this proposal assumes a narrow, individualistic account of science that threatens to undermine the project of ensuring reliable belief-formation in science." (The outer review's quotation "a narrow, individualistic account of science" is verbatim.)
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral. The argument concerns cognitive bias and has no phenomenal premise.
- **Do not confuse** Uwe Peters (this paper) with Megan A. K. Peters, a co-author of Michel et al. 2018 and of the 2023 IIT letter.

### Morton, A. (2014). "Shared Knowledge from Individual Vice: the Role of Unworthy Epistemic Emotions." *Philosophical Inquiries* 2(1): 163–172 (published 20 Jan 2014)
- **URL**: https://philinq.it/index.php/philinq/article/view/82 (publisher abstract read; full text not read; Crossref has no record for the DOI the search reported).
- **Abstract (verbatim)**: "nosiness, obsessiveness, wishful thinking, denial, partisanship. The explanation for their desirable effect is Mandevillian: because of the division of epistemic labour individual epistemic vices can lead to shared knowledge. In fact it is sometimes essential to it."
- **Coiner caution**: Morton 2014 is the earliest epistemological use of "Mandevillian" found here. It predates Smart's paper (online 17 May 2017) by three years. The SEP "Social Epistemology" entry credits Smart with the term "Mandevillian intelligence", which is accurate for that phrase. A page that says "Smart coined 'Mandevillian'" overstates. Older uses outside epistemology were not searched.

### Smart, P. R. (2018). "Mandevillian intelligence." *Synthese* 195(9): 4169–4200 (online 17 May 2017). doi:10.1007/s11229-017-1414-z
- **Abstract (verbatim, OpenAlex)**: "Mandevillian intelligence is a specific form of collective intelligence in which individual cognitive vices (i.e., shortcomings, limitations, constraints and biases) are seen to play a positive functional role in yielding collective forms of cognitive success." It adds that "the exploitation (or even the accentuation) of individual cognitive shortcomings could, in some situations, provide a productive route to collective forms of cognitive and epistemic success."
- **SEP "Social Epistemology"** (verbatim): "Smart (2018) calls one direction of this claim—that sometimes individual cognitive vices can improve group performance— 'Mandevillian intelligence.'"
- Full text not read.

### Kitcher, P. (1990). "The Division of Cognitive Labor." *The Journal of Philosophy* 87(1): 5–22. doi:10.2307/2026796
- **Text read**: JSTOR scan (OCR) hosted at https://mail.joelvelasco.net/teaching/120/kitcher90-divisioncognitive.pdf.
- **Key points**: the paper opens with the phlogiston case. It asks whether "highminded inquirers, following principles of individual rationality, should do a poor job of promoting the epistemic projects of the community that they constitute? Might those with baser motives actually do more to advance their community's epistemic endeavors?" It models "Hobbesian" scientists motivated by a prize. The result (verbatim): "Moral: there are conditions under which the Hobbesians do better than their epistemically pure cousins, even conditions under which they come as close as you please to the ideal." Also: "we may be grateful to the stubborn minority who continue to advocate problematic ideas."
- **Guard**: keep "conditions under which". Kitcher does not claim that credit-seeking always improves the distribution of effort.
- *The Advancement of Science* (OUP 1993), ch. 8, develops the model. Not read.

### Zollman, K. J. S. (2007). "The Communication Structure of Epistemic Communities." *Philosophy of Science* 74(5): 574–587. doi:10.1086/525605
- **Abstract (verbatim, OpenAlex)**: "in some contexts, a community of scientists is, as a whole, more reliable when its members are less aware of their colleagues' experimental results. Second, there is a robust tradeoff between the reliability of a community and the speed with which it reaches a correct conclusion." The model is a bandit problem after Bala and Goyal (1998).

### Zollman, K. J. S. (2010). "The Epistemic Benefit of Transient Diversity." *Erkenntnis* 72(1): 17–35 (online 22 Oct 2009). doi:10.1007/s10670-009-9194-6
- **Text read**: full text (journal PDF mirror at gwern.net), grepped.
- **Abstract (verbatim)**: "a division of labor can be maintained in two different ways: by limiting information or by endowing the scientists with extreme beliefs. If both features are present however, cognitive diversity is maintained indefinitely, and as a result agents fail to converge to the truth. Beyond the mechanisms for creating diversity suggested here, this shows that the real epistemic goal is not diversity but transient diversity."
- **Conclusion (verbatim)**: "This diversity should be around long enough so that individuals do not discard theories too quickly, but also not stay around so long as to hinder the convergence to one action." On peptic ulcer disease (PUD): "In the actual history, 30 years were wasted by pursuing a sub-optimal treatment. Had the scientists been both uninformed and dogmatic, we might still be debating the bacterial and hypoacidity hypothesis today."
- **Kuhn via Zollman (secondary)**: Zollman p. 18 quotes Kuhn 1977, p. 332: "Such a mode of development, however, requires a decision process which permits rational men to disagree, and such disagreement would be barred by the shared algorithm which philosophers have generally sought." Kuhn's own text was not reached (archive.org item restricted). Zollman's reference list titles the essay "Collective belief and scientific change". Course pages give "Objectivity, Value Judgment, and Theory Choice", ch. 13, pp. 320–339, which is the standard title (Side Finding 7).

### Rosenstock, S., Bruner, J., & O'Connor, C. (2017). "In Epistemic Networks, Is Less Really More?" *Philosophy of Science* 84(2): 234–252. doi:10.1086/690717
- **Text read**: author's final version with journal pagination (cailinoconnor.com), grepped.
- **Key points (verbatim)**: "parameters for which there is a notable benefit to decreased network connectivity occupy a relatively small niche of the total space". The effect occurs when learning is "'difficult'": the two theories have similar success rates, the population is smaller, and less data is collected each round. Also: "we cannot say with confidence that we expect real world epistemic communities to generally fall under the area of parameter space where the Zollman effect occurs." They cite "two experiments with some evidence for a similar effect" (Mason, Jones & Goldstone 2008; Jönsson, Hahn & Olsson 2015), which were not read here.
- **SEP gloss** (verbatim): "Adjust the number of nodes or the probabilities assigned to the alternative strategies/hypotheses and the Zollman effect disappears."

### Frey, D., & Šešelja, D. (2020). "Robustness and Idealizations in Agent-Based Models of Scientific Interaction." *BJPS* 71(4): 1411–1437. doi:10.1093/bjps/axy039

### Weisberg, M., & Muldoon, R. (2009). "Epistemic Landscapes and the Division of Cognitive Labor." *Philosophy of Science* 76(2): 225–252. doi:10.1086/644786
- **Abstract (verbatim)**: "pure populations of mavericks, who try to avoid research approaches that have already been taken, vastly outperform the other strategies". In mixed populations, "mavericks stimulate followers to greater levels of epistemic production, making polymorphic populations of mavericks and followers ideal in many research domains."

### Alexander, J. M., Himmelreich, J., & Thompson, C. (2015). "Epistemic Landscapes, Optimal Search, and the Division of Cognitive Labor." *Philosophy of Science* 82(3): 424–453. doi:10.1086/681766
- **Abstract (verbatim)**: "we argue they have not shown epistemic reasons exist for the division of cognitive labor, identifying the errors that led to their conclusions. Furthermore, we generalize the epistemic landscape model, showing that one should be skeptical about the benefits of social learning in epistemically complex environments."
- **Body (LSE submitted version, so paraphrase in articles)**: "the apparent benefits of cognitive diversity reported largely derive from implementation errors in two of the three search strategies they discuss."

### Thoma, J. (2015). "The Epistemic Division of Labor Revisited." *Philosophy of Science* 82(3): 454–472. doi:10.1086/681768
- **LSE accepted version** (it says "Please quote the published version", so paraphrase in articles): division of labour "is only beneficial when scientists are not too inflexible in their choice of new research topic, and too ignorant of work that is different from their own", and "the more flexible and informed scientists are, the more beneficial is division of labor".
- Note the tension with Zollman, for whom less information can help. The two models assume different things about evidence and search, which is the context-dependence Frey & Šešelja report.

### Pöyhönen, S. (2017). "Value of cognitive diversity in science." *Synthese* 194(11): 4519–4540. doi:10.1007/s11229-016-1147-4
- Metadata only (an open copy is listed at philpapers.org/archive/PYHVOC.pdf but was not fetched). A third reworking of the Weisberg–Muldoon model.

### Mayo-Wilson, C., Zollman, K. J. S., & Danks, D. (2011). "The Independence Thesis: When Individual and Social Epistemology Diverge." *Philosophy of Science* 78(4): 653–677. doi:10.1086/661777
- **Abstract (OpenAlex)**: "epistemically rational individuals might form epistemically irrational groups and that, conversely, rational groups might be composed of irrational individuals. We call the conjunction of these two claims the Independence Thesis". The OpenAlex abstract's next sentence reads "We defend the inconsistency thesis", which looks like a transcription error. The SEP says they "defend the 'independence thesis'". Do not quote the abstract's second sentence.
- This is the formal backbone of the Mandevillian claim, and it cuts both ways. Rational individuals can make an irrational group.

### Solomon, M. (1992). "Scientific Rationality and Human Reasoning." *Philosophy of Science* 59(3): 439–455. doi:10.1086/289680; and *Social Empiricism* (MIT Press, 2001)

### O'Connor, C., & Weatherall, J. O. (2018). "Scientific polarization." *European Journal for Philosophy of Science* 8(3): 855–875. doi:10.1007/s13194-018-0213-9
- **arXiv v2 (1712.04561, 19 Dec 2018)**, grepped: "The key mechanism that generates polarization involves treating evidence generated by other agents as uncertain when their beliefs are relatively different from one's own." - **Consequence**: polarisation can arise among evidence-gathering Bayesians without confirmation bias. Its presence does not diagnose individual vice, and its cure is about trust, not debiasing.

### Weatherall, J. O., O'Connor, C., & Bruner, J. P. (2020). "How to Beat Science and Influence People: Policymakers and Propaganda in Epistemic Networks." *BJPS* 71(4): 1157–1186. doi:10.1093/bjps/axy062

### Smaldino, P. E., & McElreath, R. (2016). "The natural selection of bad science." *Royal Society Open Science* 3(9): 160384. doi:10.1098/rsos.160384

## Major Positions

### Mandevillian social epistemology (Morton, Smart, Peters; Solomon's social empiricism as precursor)
- **Core claim**: some individual epistemic vices (partisanship, confirmation bias, dogmatism) raise group reliability when the group's structure turns them into thorough development and cross-criticism.
- **Status**: a possibility claim with stated conditions. Peters: "in some cases". Its evidence is psychological (Mercier & Sperber; Trouche et al.) plus historical reconstructions (plate tectonics; peptic ulcer).
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral.

### Formal division-of-labour and network epistemology (Kitcher, Strevens, Zollman, Weisberg & Muldoon, Mayo-Wilson et al.)
- **Core claim**: individual and group rationality come apart (the independence thesis). Credit incentives, limited communication, extreme priors or maverick search can keep a community exploring rival options.
- **Status**: results are fragile under robustness analysis (Rosenstock et al.; Alexander et al.; Frey & Šešelja). The stable conceptual lesson is Zollman's: what helps is *transient* diversity.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. The agents are minimal learners.

### Pessimistic network results (O'Connor, Weatherall, Bruner, Holman; Smaldino & McElreath)
- **Core claim**: rational agents can polarise stably, be steered by selective sharing, or be blocked by one intransigent agent. Incentives can degrade methods with no individual fault.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. The polarisation result shows that a split community is not evidence of individual bias.

### Longino's critical contextual empiricism (the Map's current reference point)
- Objectivity comes from criticism within and between communities, with an "uptake" condition. Peters argues uptake is needed only from most of the group. The Map cites Longino at `topics/methodology-of-consciousness-research` L146 and `voids/interested-party-void` L80.

## Key Debates

### Does less communication help? (the Zollman effect)
- **Sides**: Zollman 2007/2010, Kummerfeld & Zollman 2016 (yes, in some contexts) vs Rosenstock et al. 2017 ("a relatively small niche"), Frey & Šešelja 2020 ("highly context-dependent"), Thoma 2015 (more information helps division of labour in her landscape model).
- **Current state**: no robust general recommendation. The effect lives where learning is hard: similar-success rivals, small communities, sparse data.

### Do epistemic-landscape models show that diversity pays?
- **Sides**: Weisberg & Muldoon 2009 (mavericks; mixed populations) vs Alexander et al. 2015 (implementation errors; social learning doubtful on rugged landscapes) vs Thoma 2015 (yes, if scientists are flexible and informed) vs Pöyhönen 2017 (not read).
- **Current state**: contested. The original headline result does not survive.

### Is partisanship a virtue at group level, or only tolerable?
- **Sides**: Peters, Biddle 2009 (via Peters: progress comes from "letting each of these programs doggedly pursue its own course") vs Longino (uptake), Anderson, Douglas (the CV view). de Melo-Martín 2024 defends demarcation on epistemic-integrity grounds.
- **Current state**: open. Even Peters accepts restriction where power is unequal.

### Does polarisation indicate bias?
- **Sides**: motivated-reasoning accounts vs O'Connor & Weatherall (trust that tracks belief similarity suffices).
- **Current state**: the models show that polarisation needs no bias. Whether real cases are bias-driven or trust-driven is an empirical question for each case.

## Application to Consciousness Science {#consciousness-science}

Peters's conditions and the network results let the field be read condition by condition. Each item below is the Map's reading unless a source is named. No paper applying Mandevillian or division-of-labour models to consciousness science was found (one control search, whose results covered only the adversarial-collaboration literature and Negro 2024).

1. **The vice is present and measured.** Yaron et al. 2022: theory support predictable "solely from methodological choices", with most interpretation post hoc. This is the confirmation-bias pattern at field scale. On Peters's account it is not automatically a defect. On the CV view it is.
2. **Diversity of viewpoints: met.** Francken et al. 2022 ("many views and opinions currently coexist") and Michel et al. 2018 show no single theory dominating. Melloni et al. 2023 describe theories that "developed in parallel, with little to no cross-talk", which is diversity without the cross-criticism Peters needs.
3. **Social criticism: deliberately installed.** COGITATE adds what point 2 lacks: rival proponents, preregistered divergent predictions, theory-neutral labs. It is the cleanest institutional match to Peters's model in the field. The proponents' later behaviour fits his account too. GNWT's proponents revised an auxiliary (offset ignition needs attention to the offset; Naccache et al. 2025, already on the Map) rather than abandoning the theory. Peters expects exactly this of advocates. Negro 2024 asks whether such revisions are progressive.
4. **Equal power and resources: unclear.** The 2023 letter's complaint was IIT's prominence in "news articles in both Nature and Science". The IIT-Concerned 2025 comment speaks of "misrepresentation ... amplified by media coverage". IIT's defenders read the letter as an incumbent paradigm's reaction (Tononi et al. 2025). Each side alleges that the other holds disproportionate influence. Neither claim was assessed here.
5. **Trust across camps: under strain.** O'Connor & Weatherall's mechanism, discounting evidence from those with different beliefs, is the route to stable polarisation. A field where one group calls a rival theory "pseudoscience" and a commentator says "The signatories should apologize ASAP" shows that mechanism's preconditions. Whether it has produced evidence-discounting is untested.
6. **Transient diversity: undecided.** Rosenstock et al.'s "difficult" region (rivals with similar success, small population, sparse data per round) resembles consciousness science. That is where community structure matters most in the models, but they warn the model–world match is loose. COGITATE "challeng[ed] key tenets of both theories" and eliminated neither. Whether diversity here is transient, or Zollman's "too stable" case, will only show over further collaborations.
7. **Discriminating evidence: the deepest limit.** The models converge because payoffs eventually separate the options. The consciousness-science debate between IIT and GNWT has such evidence for its neural predictions (COGITATE supplies it). The IIT-Concerned group's claim is that IIT's *core* claims are "untestable even in principle". If so, those claims fall outside what any division-of-labour model can promise to settle. The same limit applies, more strongly, to the metaphysical questions behind the debate (Duhem–Quine at `topics/duhem-quine-underdetermination-consciousness`).

## Bearing on the Admission Test and the Interested Party Void {#admission-test}

`project/voids-circularity-discount` §A Proposed Admission Test, level 3, asks whether a tilt "resists named procedures (public criticism, adversarial collaboration, preregistration, cross-cultural replication, instruments)". This literature gives the conditions under which those procedures work:

| Correction works when | Source | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| Partiality is spread across rival positions | Solomon (via SEP); Peters §4.2 | The tilt is shared by everyone, a common cause ([[common-cause-null]]) |
| Viewpoints are diverse and power roughly equal | Peters §5.1 | One faction controls funding or evidence (Peters's industry cases; Weatherall et al. 2020) |
| Most of the group takes up criticism | Peters §5.1 vs Longino | Agents are intransigent (Holman & Bruner 2015) |
| Diversity is transient | Zollman 2010 | Isolation and dogmatism combine and diversity becomes permanent |
| Evidence is weighted regardless of its source's beliefs | O'Connor & Weatherall 2018 | Trust tracks agreement, producing stable polarisation |
| Incentives reward finding the truth | Smaldino & McElreath 2016 | Incentives reward output, and methods degrade |
| Discriminating evidence eventually arrives | All bandit and landscape models (built-in assumption) | The question has no evidence that separates the rivals |

- No study of social correction on existential or metaphysical questions was found, here or in the argumentative-theory note.
- No paper applying these models to consciousness science was found (one control search). Section [[#consciousness-science|Application to Consciousness Science]] is the Map's own reading.
- The "8% of 60" IIT survey could not be traced (Side Finding 4).
