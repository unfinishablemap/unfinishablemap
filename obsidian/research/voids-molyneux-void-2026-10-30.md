## Executive Summary

A sighted adult who picks up a cube she is looking at gets one cube. Molyneux's question, put to Locke in 1688 and printed in the second edition of the *Essay* (1694), asks whether that oneness is given or learned. Could someone born blind, who knows cube from sphere by touch, tell them apart by sight alone on first seeing? Three hundred and thirty years later the answer is still contested. The reason is not lack of interest: the SEP calls it the problem in the philosophy of perception that has "provoked more reflection, refinement, and experimentation" than any other [raw]. The record shows a void with three independent faces.

1. **The seam face.** The mature perceiver cannot tell from inside whether seen and felt shape share one content or two contents fused by habit. Locke says the work of judgement is done "without our taking notice of it" [raw]. Berkeley says the ideas of sight and touch are "most closely twisted, blended, and incorporated together" [raw]. Green (2024) argues that real-world Molyneux tests "are irrelevant" to whether sight and touch are rationally connected in normally sighted perceivers [abstract].
2. **The probe face.** Only a subject without the history can answer, and the conditions of a fair test defeat each other. Held et al. (2011) found "a lack of immediate transfer, but such cross-modal mappings developed rapidly" [abstract]. Schwenkler (2012) showed that the failure could be a purely visual deficit. Clarke (2016) showed that success would not have settled the question either, because subjects could infer the match. That leaves the paradigm "doubly inadequate" [raw]. The SEP's 2026 revision concludes that a pure Molyneux experiment "may turn out to require a scientific miracle" [raw]. Newborn humans and dark-reared chicks give "yes" answers, but only to modified questions.
3. **The question face.** Molyneux's question is not one question. Matthen & Cohen (2020) split it in two and conclude that it "resists any monolithic answer" [abstract]. Green (2022) argues that perspectival aspects of shape experience are modality-specific while some constant aspects are "constitutively multisensory" [abstract]. Which precisification an experiment tests is fixed by theory, not by data.

**Calibration for the Map.** The void is neutral between dualism and physicalism. Green (2024) argues that the all-possible-perceivers version of the question "cannot be used to adjudicate debates about whether the phenomenal character of perceptual experience is primarily determined 'externally' ... or 'internally'" [abstract]. A dualist and a physicalist describe the same blank. What the void does supply is a third calibration exhibit, after [[handedness-void]] and [[palette-extension-void]]. Handedness is a void that physics closed. Palette extension is a void that split into a closing question and an open one. Molyneux is a void where the crucial experiment was run, in 1728, 2011 and 2024, and the boundary did not move, because the experiment could not isolate the variable it was meant to test.

## Key Sources

### William Molyneux (1688 letter; 1693 letter, printed by Locke 1694)
- **Source read**: SEP "Molyneux's Problem" §1 (raw), which reproduces both letters from de Beer's *Correspondence of John Locke*. The 1693 version was also read in Locke's *Essay* II.ix.8 (Gutenberg 10615, raw).
- **Key points**:
  - The 1688 letter adds a second question, about distance: whether the newly sighted man "Could know by his Sight, before he stretch'd out his Hand, whether he Could not Reach them, tho they were Removed 20 or 1000 feet from Him?" [raw, SEP transcription]. Locke never replied to it. The 1693 version drops distance.
  - The 1693 question as printed by Locke [raw, Gutenberg]: "whether BY HIS SIGHT, BEFORE HE TOUCHED THEM, he could now distinguish and tell which is the globe, which the cube?" (the small capitals of the Gutenberg text are rendered as uppercase).
  - Molyneux answers no: the man "has not yet obtained the experience, that what affects his touch so or so, must affect his sight so or so" [raw, Gutenberg].
- **Tenet alignment**: none directly. This is the origin of the void.

### John Locke, *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, 2nd ed. (1694), II.ix.8–10
- **URL**: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10615
- **Type**: Book (primary)
- **Key points**:
  - The section title is "Sensations often changed by the Judgment". Its first sentence: "the ideas we receive by sensation are often, in grown people, altered by the judgment, without our taking notice of it" [raw].
  - Locke agrees with Molyneux that the blind man "at first sight, would not be able with certainty to say which was the globe, which the cube, whilst he only saw them". He offers the case "as an occasion for him to consider how much he may be beholden to experience, improvement, and acquired notions, where he thinks he had not the least use of, or help from them" [raw].
  - II.ix.9 states the seam face exactly. By "a settled habit" the judgement "is performed so constantly and so quick, that we take that for the perception of our sensation which is an idea formed by our judgment" [raw]. The heading of §10: "How, by Habit, ideas of Sensation are unconsciously changed into ideas of Judgment" [raw].
- **Caution**: whether Locke's "no" is consistent with his treatment of figure as a common sensible is a live historiographical dispute (SEP §4 cites Mackie 1976, Levin 2020, Bruno & Mandelbaum 2010, Ayers 1993, Vaughan 2018, Ott 2020, Glenney 2024). An article should not present Locke's answer as simple.

### George Berkeley, *An Essay Towards a New Theory of Vision* (1709), §§51, 108, 129, 132–133
- **URL**: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/4722
- **Type**: Book (primary). Section numbers were checked against the Gutenberg text; its edition was not identified.
- **Key points**:
  - §108: "the things I see are so very different and heterogeneous from the things I feel that the perception of the one would never have suggested the other to my thoughts, or enabled me to pass the least judgment thereon, until I had experienced their connexion" [raw].
  - §129: "It is therefore a direct consequence that there is no idea common to both senses" [raw].
  - §51, which is the seam face in Berkeley's idiom: we "find it so difficult to discriminate between the immediate and mediate objects of sight" because they are "as it were, most closely twisted, blended, and incorporated together. And the prejudice is confirmed and riveted in our thoughts by a long tract of time, by the use of language, and want of reflexion" [raw].
  - §132 cites Molyneux's problem as "A farther confirmation of our tenet" [raw].
- **Tenet alignment**: Berkeley is an idealist, not a dualist, and should not be enlisted. His relevance is structural. He is the first to say that the join is invisible *because* it is habitual, which is the void's seam face.

### G. W. Leibniz, *Nouveaux essais* II.ix.8 (written 1704, published 1765)
- **Source read**: SEP §2 and Clarke 2016. **Leibniz was not read at source.**
- **Key points (paraphrase)**: Leibniz answered yes, conditionally. Per the SEP, he held that the similarities could be "reasoned out", and he resisted Locke's "at first sight" amendment by insisting on time for full visual recovery. Per Clarke, citing the Remnant & Bennett translation p. 135, the subject might reason from the cube's distinguished points against the sphere's absence of them. On this reading Leibniz's "yes" is a yes by inference, not by shared perceptual content. That is the root of Clarke's 2016 objection.

### William Cheselden (1728), "An account of some observations made by a young gentleman, who was born blind…", *Phil. Trans.* 35(402): 447–450
- **DOI**: 10.1098/rstl.1727.0038 (Crossref-verified)
- **Source read**: archive.org OCR of vol. 35, raw. The quotations below normalise the long s and fix obvious OCR misreads (for example "fird" for "first"). **Variant**: the OCR reads "making any Judgment about Distances", while the SEP's transcription reads "making any judgment of distances". The printing the SEP used was not identified, so an article should quote the OCR wording or paraphrase.
- **Key points**:
  - "When he first saw, he was so far from making any Judgment about Distances, that he thought all Objects whatever touch'd his Eyes, (as he express'd it) as what he felt, did his Skin" [raw].
  - "He knew not the Shape of any Thing, nor any one Thing from another, however different in Shape, or Magnitude" [raw].
  - The cat episode: having forgotten which was the cat and which the dog, he caught the cat "(which he knew by feeling)", looked at her steadfastly, set her down and said "So Puss! I shall know you another Time" [raw].
- **Why it matters**: the cat episode is the probe face in miniature. The first newly sighted subject on record taught his sight with his touch, which is exactly the contamination route that makes any later test impure. The SEP notes that Cheselden's report "intensified" the debate rather than settling it, because Voltaire, Diderot, Condillac and Reid read it as ambiguous [raw].

### SEP, "Molyneux's Problem", Glenney, Ferretti & Degenaar (first published 2005; substantive revision 8 January 2026)

- **Type**: Encyclopedia (raw HTML downloaded and grepped)
- **Key points**:

### Held, Ostrovsky, de Gelder, Gandhi, Ganesh, Mathur & Sinha (2011), "The newly sighted fail to match seen with felt", *Nature Neuroscience* 14(5): 551–553
- **DOI**: 10.1038/nn.2795 (Crossref-verified). The corrigendum at 10.1038/nn0911-1217d corrects only the spelling of de Gelder's name [raw, nature.com].
- **Source read**: abstract [Europe PMC], SEP §4, Schwenkler 2012 and Clarke 2016. **Full text not read.**
- **Key points**:

### Schwenkler (2012), "On the matching of seen and felt shape by newly sighted subjects", *i-Perception* 3(3): 186–188; and Schwenkler (2013), "Do things look the way they feel?", *Analysis* 73(1): 86–96
- **DOIs**: 10.1068/i0525ic; 10.1093/analys/ans137 (Crossref-verified)
- **Source read**: 2012 full text (Europe PMC, raw). 2013 metadata only.
- **Key points**:
  - The subjects "may not have been able visually to represent 3D shape in the perspective-invariant manner required for intermodal matching" [abstract]. Success on the vision-to-vision task could rest on "a more simple visual representation (eg of approximate overall appearance, object outlines…)" [raw].
  - Supporting evidence is Ostrovsky et al. (2009). Their newly sighted subjects, given shaded images of a cube or pyramid, "were unable to integrate the facets [of the shape] into the percept of a single three-dimensional object" [via Schwenkler 2012].
  - Remedy: re-run with the objects "presented on a rotating platform", because motion cues help newly sighted form perception [raw].

### Ostrovsky, Meyers, Ganesh, Mathur & Sinha (2009), "Visual parsing after recovery from blindness", *Psychological Science* 20(12): 1484–1491
- **DOI**: 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02471.x (Crossref-verified)
- **Key points**: In three newly treated congenitally blind individuals, "prominent figural cues of grouping, such as good continuation and junction structure, were largely ineffective for image parsing", while "motion cues were of profound significance". Performance on static scenes improved after "10 to 18 months of visual experience" [abstract].

### Clarke (2016), "Investigating what felt shapes look like", *i-Perception* 7(1)
- **DOI**: 10.1177/2041669515627948 (Crossref-verified)
- **Source read**: full text (Europe PMC, raw).
- **Key points**:
  - Success would not have settled the question either. A newly sighted subject "might only do so on the basis of a capacity for rational inference, rather than the recognition of a familiar spatial structure in experience" [raw]. Held et al.'s paradigm "seems doubly inadequate, incapable of delivering either a positive or negative answer to Molyneux's question with any certainty" [raw].
  - Lowering the visual demands, as Schwenkler 2012/2013 and Connolly 2013 (*i-Perception* 4(8): 508–510, doi:10.1068/i0623jc, metadata only) propose, may make inference *easier* and so worsen the problem [abstract].

### E. J. Green (2022), "The puzzle of cross-modal shape experience", *Noûs* 56(4): 867–896; and Green (2024), "Newly sighted perceivers and the relation between sight and touch", *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences* 5

- **Key points**:
  - Meltzoff & Borton, *Nature* 282: 403–404: one-month-olds looked longer at the shape they had sucked (smooth versus nubby pacifier). Source read: SEP §4 and Maurer et al.'s abstract; metadata only.

### Versace, Freeland & Emmerson (2024), "First-sight recognition of touched objects shows that chicks can solve Molyneux's problem", *Biology Letters* 20(4): 20240025
- **DOI**: 10.1098/rsbl.2024.0025 (Crossref-verified)
- **Key points**: Chicks hatched in darkness were given 24 h of tactile exposure to smooth or bumpy stimuli. At first light, those exposed to smooth stimuli "approached the visual smooth stimulus significantly more". The authors conclude that "cross-modal recognition does not require previous multimodal experience" and that "supra-modal brain areas appear functional already at birth" [abstract]. The second claim is the authors' inference, not a measurement.
- **Why it matters**: it is the cleanest probe yet, since there is no immature-vision confound and no prior visual experience. But the answer is a precocial bird's approach response, which the SEP classes as a "modified 'action' version" of the question [raw]. It shows that a system can be built to answer yes. It does not show that a human mind's join is given.

### Held & Hein (1963), "Movement-produced stimulation in the development of visually guided behavior", *Journal of Comparative and Physiological Psychology* 56(5): 872–876
- **DOI**: 10.1037/h0040546 (Crossref-verified)
- **Key points**: In ten pairs of neonatal kittens, one member's visual stimulation varied with its own locomotion and its yoked partner received equivalent stimulation through passive motion. Afterwards, "visually guided paw placement, discrimination on a visual cliff, and the blink response were normal for A but failing in P" [abstract].
- **Why it matters**: the same Richard Held, forty-eight years before the Prakash study, showed that vision acquires spatial use through self-produced movement. Together with Cheselden's cat, this is why the purity paradox has no easy exit. The route by which sight gets its space runs through the agent's own action and touch.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The void claims something narrower and stronger than "nobody yet knows the answer to Molyneux's question". The claim has three parts:

- No mind that has both senses can find out, by attending to its own experience, whether its seen cube and its felt cube are one content or two.
- No subject who lacks the history can be tested without either an immature visual system or contamination of the probe.
- What would count as an answer depends on which of several questions is meant.

Each face would hold without the others. Together they make a void that a crucial experiment was expected to close and repeatedly did not.

### Evidence for the Limit

- **Seam face.** Locke II.ix.8–10 and Berkeley §51 independently describe a join that habit makes invisible. Their positive theories differ: Locke works with judgement and Berkeley with suggestion. The modern form is Green 2024's distinction, under which no experiment on newly sighted subjects bears on the normally sighted case. The Map's own [[three-dimensional-world-representation-problem]] page reports the phenomenology the seam produces: cross-modal spatial unity "is so seamless that it takes philosophical reflection to notice it is puzzling at all" (L89). The void adds that reflection, once it notices, cannot tell whether the seamless arena is given or built.
- **Probe face.** The record from 1728 to 2024 is a sequence of probes, each answering a different question. Cheselden: no spatial extension at first. Held 2011: no at first, yes within days. Schwenkler: perhaps a visual deficit. Clarke: a yes would have been an inference. Meltzoff & Borton: yes by looking time, which failed exact replication. Streri: yes in one direction only. Versace: yes for an approach response in a precocial bird. The SEP's "scientific miracle" is the 2026 consensus statement of the paradox.
- **Question face.** Matthen & Cohen's split, Glenney's cluster concept and Green's perspectival/constant distinction. Levin (2008, *Philosophical Studies* 139(1): 1–28, doi:10.1007/s11098-007-9072-5; metadata only) raises the timing question: should the subject be tested at once or after visual recovery?

**Whether the limit is real.** The strongest deflationary reading is that the question is empirical and merely hard. Better sight-restoration cohorts, gene therapy or optogenetics would let a naïve subject with mature vision exist. The SEP notes such technologies, but also that "even such conditions may fail to provide a satisfactory answer" once blindness is reconceived as atypical visual development [raw]. Even granting the miracle subject, the seam face survives: the result would tell us about *that* subject, and nothing in the ordinary adult's experience would change. The probe face is therefore possibly deflatable. The seam face is not, by the void's own structure.

### Phenomenology

For the ordinary perceiver there is nothing to approach. The join is not felt as a join, and that absence is the void's signature, in the "no felt edge" family of [[apex/taxonomy-of-voids]]. The only first-person reports from near the boundary come from the newly sighted:

- Cheselden's boy thought objects "touch'd his Eyes" and "knew not the Shape of any Thing" [raw].
- Ostrovsky's subjects saw a shaded cube as several objects [via Schwenkler].
- Fine et al.'s MM still recognised forms by reasoning two years on [via Clarke].

These reports describe sight without a shared space, not the moment a shared space is acquired. Held's subjects acquired the mapping within days, apparently without reporting the change. The transition is a [[transit-void]]-type crossing, with the endpoints available and the passage not.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

None for the mature perceiver. Any introspective attempt to separate seen shape from felt shape is made with both already joined. Closing the eyes does not help: what remains is touch plus visual imagery of the felt object, and the imagery is itself post-join. This connects to [[imagery-void]].

### Indirect Methods

- **Naïve subjects**: sight-restoration cohorts, newborns, dark-reared animals. Each trades purity against comparability.
- **Lowered visual demands**: rotating platforms, 2D figures, motion cues (Schwenkler; Connolly). Clarke warns that these raise the risk of inference.
- **Speed and judgement-independence as markers**: Clarke suggests that perceptual recognition should be fast and "judgement independent in a way that rational inference is not" [raw], which gives a way to tell a perceptual yes from an inferential one.
- **Sensory substitution devices**: per the SEP, SSD learning delays have been read both ways (Morgan 1977 "no"; Hillenbrand et al. 2018 "yes"), and Auvray et al. 2009 treat SSD use as augmentation and so only an approximation. Metadata via the SEP only.

### What AI Might See

AI changes the probe face and leaves the seam face alone.

- **No purity paradox for machines.** A vision encoder can be trained to robust 3D shape recognition with zero tactile data, and a haptic or point-cloud encoder trained separately. Zero-shot matching can then be tested at once. The two human confounds (immature vision, touch-taught vision) do not arise. Such an experiment answers Matthen & Cohen's (a) and (b) *for that architecture*.
- **Convergence as a third answer.** If Huh et al.'s hypothesis holds, separately trained modalities converge on alike geometry because the world they model is shared. That would be neither Berkeley's associative learning between the senses nor an innate link between them, but independent convergence on one world. The hypothesis is the authors', and they flag counterexamples.
- **The limit on what this shows.** Representational alignment is a third-person, structural fact. Whether a system with aligned encoders has one shape-experience or two, if it has any, is the question the seam face is about. On the Map's reading, no structural probe settles that, which is a dualist interpretation, not a finding. The AI case also yields an asymmetry the Map can use: in a machine the join is externally inspectable (by alignment and probing), while in a human it is inspectable from neither side. That is the familiar "recoverable third-personally, not first-personally" pattern from [[modality-void]] and [[fusion-void]], with the difference that here the human third-person route is also blocked by the purity paradox.
- **A text-trained system is itself an odd Molyneux subject.** It has shape knowledge by description, without touch or sight. Whether a language model "recognises" a cube in an image it is later given is a question about its training mixture, not about Molyneux. The note records this as a curiosity, not evidence.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits.** Both historical camps claimed economy. Berkeley's "no" needs only the proper objects of each sense plus association; the rationalist "yes" needs only one geometry shared by both. The experimental record shows simplicity failing in a specific way: neither economy picked out a question the experiment could answer, because the question had not been individuated (Matthen & Cohen). Tenet 5 is *illustrated* here, as a warning against settling by parsimony a question not yet split. It is not *vindicated*, since nothing here shows that a complex answer is true. The tenet binds the Map's own parsimony reasoning. It is not a charge against Berkeley or Leibniz.

### Other Tenets

- **Dualism.** Neutral, and the article must say so in its lead. Green 2024's final claim rules out using the question to decide internal versus external determination of phenomenal character. The void bears on one Map page, though. The [[cross-modal-capability-division]] apex claims a "supramodal mind-side boundary" from shared *access* signatures (Sanchez et al., MEG). The Molyneux record shows that supramodal *access* does not imply supramodal *content*: the newly sighted have conscious access to seen and felt shape, and still no immediate shared shape. An article should keep these apart and should not read Versace et al.'s "supra-modal brain areas" as support for the apex's mind-side claim.
- **Bidirectional Interaction.** Held & Hein 1963 and Cheselden's cat show the shared space being acquired through the agent's own movement and touch. That is congenial to the Map's emphasis on agency, but a physicalist sensorimotor account predicts it just as well (see [[tactile-consciousness-and-the-interface]], which already concedes this for touch). No evidential weight.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction; No Many Worlds.** No bearing. An article should not manufacture one.

### Implications

The Map's claim that the experienced world is a single spatial arena, made in [[three-dimensional-world-representation-problem]], [[binding-void]] and [[dualist-perception]], describes the mature perceiver. The Molyneux void shows that whether that arena is given or acquired is closed to the very perceiver who has it. The cross-modal record suggests it is at least partly acquired in humans, and quickly. That leaves the Map's unity claims intact, but they hold for the adult, and an article can record the developmental qualification without weakening them.

## Gaps in Research

- **Held et al. 2011 full text not read.** Per-task percentages, the four-versus-five subject discrepancy, and the exact delay before the post-experience retest are unverified. Do not give numbers.
- **Green 2022/2024, Spence & Di Stefano 2024 and Ferretti & Glenney 2024** are read at abstract level only (bot walls). Spence & Di Stefano is the most current experimental review and should be read before drafting.
- **Evans 1985** ("Molyneux's Question", *Collected Papers*, OUP, pp. 364–399; Crossref lists the 1996 reissue, doi:10.1093/oso/9780198236276.003.0013) was not read. The SEP attributes the phosphene variant to him. The recollection that Evans answers *yes* for subjects whose spatial concepts are grounded in egocentric behavioural space is a **lead from memory, unverified**.
- **Leibniz's text** was not read. The paraphrase comes via the SEP and Clarke (Remnant & Bennett p. 135). An article quoting Leibniz must read *New Essays* II.ix.8 first.
- **Gregory & Wallace 1963 (S.B.) and Sacks's "Virgil"** are classic first-sight phenomenology. They are named in Schwenkler's references but not read. Leads only.
- **Versace et al. 2024**: only the abstract was read; replication status is unknown.
- **Machine Molyneux experiments**: no paper was found that trains vision and touch encoders separately and tests zero-shot shape transfer under the Molyneux label. Barbano et al. 2024 is a text-to-vision variant. A targeted search (`"Molyneux" visuo-tactile zero-shot`) was not run.
- **Possibly unanswerable**: whether the mature join is one content or two may have no first-person answer at all, which is the seam face's claim. Whether it has a fact of the matter at all depends on the question face.
