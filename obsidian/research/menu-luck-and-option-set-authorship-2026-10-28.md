## Executive Summary

1. **"Menu luck" and "option-set luck" are the reviewer's coinages.** Two targeted searches found zero uses of either phrase in the philosophical literature. The nearest literature treatments go under three other names, each with a verified source: Nagel's second kind of luck, "luck in one's circumstances—the kind of problems and situations one faces" (1976: 140, raw), together with his question how one can be responsible "for the circumstances that pose one's moral choices" (1976: 146, raw); Levy's present luck applied to candidate generation, "Which considerations come to mind is – apparently – a matter of luck for an agent" (2011: 90, as quoted in Garrett 2013: 213, raw); and Dennett's consideration-generator, the two-stage model in which "a consideration-generator whose output is to some degree undetermined produces a series of considerations" and the agent's freedom lies in "the intelligent selection, rejection, and weighing of the considerations that do occur" (1978: 295, as quoted in Doyle 2011, raw). The SEP groups the Dennett, Mele 1995 ch. 12, Mele 2006: 9–14 and Ekstrom accounts under "deliberative" indeterminism (§2.1, raw). Credit the phenomenon to those three; credit the label to the review.
2. **"Option-set luck" invites a conflation that the article writer must block.** Dworkin's "option luck" is the luck of "deliberate and calculated gambles" an agent chose to run (1981: 293, as quoted in SEP Moral Luck §4.2.3 and secondaries; Sovereign Virtue 2000: 73 per Fleurbaey 2006, raw). It is the luck one is answerable for, opposed to brute luck. The reviewer's "option-set luck" is almost its contrary: luck in the set one never chose. Two live Map pages use Dworkin in other senses (`precommitment-and-the-diachronic-agent` L76 cites his critical-interests view on dementia; `co-optimization-reply-to-the-correlation-problem` carries no Dworkin at all — the harvest note's claim that both pages carry "option luck" / "brute luck" is false; a case-sensitive grep over live articles for those phrases returns zero). Do not use "option luck" for the reviewer's objection anywhere on the Map.
3. **Menu luck is a composite of lucks the Map already concedes, not a fourth kind.** The option set at a selection event is fixed by (a) the agent's constitution — what it can conceive and what weights it brings — which is Strawson's and Nagel's constitutive luck; (b) its circumstances — what the situation offers — which is Nagel's circumstantial luck; and (c) chance in the generator — which considerations come to mind at the moment — which is Levy's present luck located before the decision rather than at it. The reviewer's own four requirements (adequate menu, access to reasons, an option corresponding to the ought, authored character) partition the same way: the first three are (b) and (c), the fourth is (a).
4. **What the objection adds is structural, and it is a cost of the selection-only posit.** Dennett, Mele and Ekstrom could answer "you did not author the generator's output" by moving indeterminism, or by making preference formation an action (Ekstrom's "The formation of a preference ... is an action", SEP §2.1). A selection-only channel forecloses both: by `concepts/selection-only-channel` L42 mind contributes "nothing to the alternative set itself", and L84 bounds the input alphabet by the brain-generated output alphabet. So the Map holds Dennett's architecture with the stages inverted in metaphysical weight — the generator is wholly physical, the selector wholly mental — and inherits Kane's complaint against Dennett with no Kanean escape. The Map's only reply is the recursive one the reviewer himself supplies (present selections curate future candidate sets), which has exactly the shape, and exactly the limit, of the self-shaping reply to constitutive luck already on the host page: the first menu, like the initial character, was not chosen, and the curating power is itself constitutive luck.
5. **The Map already concedes menu luck under two other names.** `topics/responsibility-gradient-from-attentional-capacity` L67–69 ("Scope"): consequences outside the attentional field "were never phenomenally available for selection", and the agent "did not choose to ignore distant consequences". `concepts/moral-responsibility` L79: "We're not responsible for our initial characters or for the power that reshapes them". `concepts/basic-argument-and-constitutive-luck` L81 (parity point 1) lists "its attentional capacities and the weights it brings to a torn choice" among the unchosen nature. The reviewer's "the responsibility-gradient material partly acknowledges constrained option availability" is accurate. What is missing is the sentence that names the three authorships and says which the selection-only channel can claim.
6. **"Menu adequacy" belongs on the gradient page, not on `moral-responsibility`.** The gradient's "Scope" dimension already is menu adequacy under another name; the page has 721 words of headroom; `moral-responsibility` has 4 (3,495 against 3,500) and can take only a pipe. ### Q4. The token / menu / character authorship distinction, and where "menu adequacy" goes

- **Token authorship** — which option is actualised at the event. Literature antecedent: the agent-causal "the agent himself causes the choice" (Clarke, as the SEP Free Will entry summarizes him; already on the host page). This is all the selection-only channel claims. Mele's "proximal control" (SEP §2.1) is the compatibilist cousin.
- **Menu authorship** — which options are on the table. Antecedents: Nagel's constitutive luck and "may be the product of earlier choices" (already on the host page); Kane's self-forming actions; Mele 2006 ch. 5 and O'Connor 2009 "come in degrees and grow over time" (already on the host page). ## Major Positions

### Circumstantial luck is real and undermines desert in proportion (Nagel 1976; Levy 2011; Zimmerman 2002 [lead])
- **Core claim**: one cannot be more culpable "than one is for that fraction of it which is under one's control" (Nagel 1976: 140), and the circumstances "that pose one's moral choices" are outside it.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral on mind. The Map accepts the premise for the menu and locates its remaining claim in token authorship and recursive curation.

### Circumstantial luck is real and compatible with desert (Hartman 2017; Lang 2021 [lead])
- **Core claim**: luck in results, circumstance and character "can partially determine the degree of a person's blameworthiness" (Routledge blurb, via search); Lang's anti-anti-luckism restrains situational luck by praising dispositions (abstract).
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. This is the position the fold passage's "options never phenomenally available were not refused" sits closest to, and the writer should cite Hartman for it rather than present it as the Map's invention.

### Luck in the generator is where freedom should be sought (Dennett 1978; Mele 1995; Ekstrom 2000)
- **Core claim**: indeterminism in which considerations occur, determinism (or compatibilist control) in the selection.
- **Relation to tenets**: the Map inverts the weighting (physical generator, mental selector) and so cannot use their reply; see Q2.

### Luck at the moment of decision undermines libertarian and compatibilist control alike (Levy 2011; Pérez de Calleja 2014; McKenna 2025; Mele and Kearns 2014)
- **Core claim**: parity; the compatibilist pays for present luck too.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral; supports the host page's existing parity accounting.

## Key Debates

### Is menu luck a kind or a composite?
- **Sides**: the reviewer treats it as a distinct form of the luck objection; Nagel's taxonomy places it under circumstances, Levy's under present luck, Strawson's regress under constitution.
- **This note's verdict**: composite (Summary 3); the structural cost under selection-only is the only new element.

### Does the counterfactual view dissolve it?
- **Sides**: Zimmerman 2002 (blame for what one would have done); Hartman 2017 ch. 4 and Lang 2021 ch. 5 against (SEP §4.2.5; OpenAlex abstract).
- **Current state**: open. Nagel's own text already rejects the counterfactual move: "We judge people for what they actually do or fail to do, not just for what they would have done if circumstances had been different" (1976: 146, raw). The Map should not adopt the counterfactual view without a page.
