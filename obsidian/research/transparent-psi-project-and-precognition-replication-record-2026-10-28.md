## Executive Summary

The Transparent Psi Project (TPP; Kekecs et al. 2023, *Royal Society Open Science*) is the largest adversarially co-designed replication of Bem's 2011 Experiment 1 (precognitive detection of erotic stimuli). Ten laboratories in nine countries collected 37,836 erotic trials from 2,115 participants with valid data under a protocol approved by a 29-expert consensus panel (15 ESP proponents, 14 opponents). The preregistered primary analysis (one frequentist test plus three Bayesian proportion tests, each with a BF01 threshold of 25) returned 49.89% correct guesses against Bem's 53.07% and chance 50%; all four tests supported the null, the stopping rule fired at the minimum sample, and the paper reports that the data are "72 times more likely" under chance. A 2025 PLOS ONE follow-up using the TPP procedure online (Walleczek et al.; three studies, 26,483 participants, 420,472 critical trials) found no above-chance effect in any study, with one below-chance anomaly that failed to re-replicate. A separate 2021 preregistered pair of time-reversed priming replications co-authored by Bem himself (Schlitz et al.) also failed its confirmatory hypotheses. Bem-style behavioural precognition therefore now has a preregistered multi-site record that is null across proponent-involved designs, which narrows the experimenter-effect escape the host page describes.

Physiological presentiment is a different literature with a different record. Mossbridge et al. (2012) and the Duggan & Tressoldi (2018) update still stand as the proponents' meta-analyses (overall effects 0.21 and 0.28); the published critique is Schwarzkopf (2014) on study quality and extraordinary-evidence standards, with a reply by Mossbridge et al. (2015, arXiv). No TPP-scale preregistered multi-site presentiment replication was found. The host page currently files Bem-precognition critics (Wagenmakers 2011, Galak 2012) and the Bem et al. 2016 meta-analysis under "presentiment", which contradicts its own §Two Distinct Claims; the fold should relabel those sentences and add the TPP record. None of this changes the Map's stance: it does not rest on presentiment, a null record is compatible with its tenets, and a positive one would not be evidence for conscious selection.

  - Consensus design: "The panel of this consensus design process included 29 experts (15 proponents and 14 opponents of the ESP interpretation of the original results)." (§2.1)
  - Hypotheses and thresholds: "M0 assumed a success rate of 50% in the population, while M1 assumed a success rate higher than 50%. If Bayes factors (BF01) is lower than 0.04 (1/25), the test would support M1, while if it was higher than 25, the test would supports M0." (§2.5.3; "supports" is the source's own typo.) The primary analysis bundled a mixed-effects logistic regression with three Bayesian proportion tests under different priors; a conclusion required all four to agree.
  - Sample: "In total, 2220 individuals participated in the study." (§3.2.3) "Data collection was carried out in 10 laboratories from nine different countries, during which 2115 participants provided valid data for analysis." (Discussion) The 2,220 figure sometimes quoted in secondary sources is recruitment; the analysis is on 2,115.
  - Result: "Overall, we observed 49.89% successful guesses within a total of 37 836 erotic trials. As a comparison, Bem reported 53.07% successes among 1560 trials in his original experiment. All four tests used for the primary hypothesis testing supported M0, thus the stopping rule was triggered with our minimum sample size achieved." (§3.2.5) "meaning that the probability of correct guesses in this task was no higher than chance." The 99.75% CI for the population success rate is "between 49.11% and 50.67%"; the robustness test "can reject the hypothesis that the probability of successful guesses in this task is higher than 51%" (§3.2.6).
  - Abstract verdict: "We found 49.89% successful guesses, while Bem reported 53.07% success rate, with the chance level being 50%. Thus, Bem's findings were not replicated in our study."
  - Verbal Bayes factor (§5): "Observing this percentage of successful guesses is 72 times more likely if the guesses are successful at random than if they have a better than chance success rate." Numeric BF01 values per prior are shown in Figure 1 (all pass BF01 > 25), not tabulated in the text.
  - Interpretation (§5): "The failure to replicate previous positive findings with this strict methodology indicates that it is likely that the overall positive effect in the literature might be the result of recognized methodological biases rather than ESP. However, the occurrence of ESP effects could depend on some unrecognized moderating variables that were not adequately controlled in this study, or ESP could be very rare or extremely small, and thus undetectable with this study design."
  - Scope disclaimer (§4): "our results cannot rule out the existence of ESP itself, and the goal of the project was not to prove or disprove its existence."
  - Sheep-goat check (§4): "we also explored the potential association of belief in psi and performance in the experimental task. We did not find any indication of such an association in our data."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The Map does not rest on Bem-style precognition. Do not enlist the consortium for any Map position; the paper's own framing is methodological ("pure bias theory" versus "ESP theory").

### Walleczek et al. (2025) — AMP-TPP follow-up replications
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0335330 (PLOS ONE 20(11): e0335330, published 2025-11-05; publisher page fetched and grep-verified)
- **Type**: Paper. Authors: Jan Walleczek, Nikolaus von Stillfried, Stefan Schmidt, Marc Wittmann, Kirsten A. Kirmse, Jürgen Moll, Zoltan Kekecs. Funding statement discloses Walleczek directed the Fetzer Franklin Fund; the group is a psi-research-adjacent metascience team, not a sceptic consortium.
- **Key points** (grep-verified):
  - "three replication studies with a total of 26,483 participants resulting in N = 420,472 critical trials" (abstract). All participant studies were online (Bilendi panel), unlike the in-lab TPP.
  - "Study 1 failed to replicate the precognitive effect. An exploratory analysis of Study 1 suggested an effect in the opposite direction than was originally predicted" (49.48%, exploratory p = 0.042; preregistered confirmatory p = 0.979). "Study 2 confirmed this exploratory result" (49.65%, p = 0.013). "Study 3 was unable to replicate the result from Study 2" (50.07%, p = 0.496).
  - "In short, none of the three replication studies was able to detect the precognitive effect (53.1%) reported by Bem" (Discussion).
  - On the Study 2 below-chance anomaly: "either (i) a psi-derived anomaly that defies known physical laws, or (ii) a method-derived anomaly, e.g., a false-positive statistical finding. Using conventional standards, based on the lack of replicability in Study 3 and absence of an accepted scientific theory, the second scenario appears more plausible."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral; same caution as above.

### Schlitz et al. (2021) — Two preregistered time-reversed priming replications
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.31275/20211903 (Crossref-verified: *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 35(1), 65–90). Abstract read on the Institute of Noetic Sciences listing (https://noetic.org/publication/two-replication-studies-of-a-time-reversed-psi-priming-task/); the JSE PDF itself was not fetched, so quote only the abstract.
- **Type**: Paper. Authors include Marilyn Schlitz, Daryl Bem, Etzel Cardeña, Susan Blackmore, Patrizio Tressoldi, Dick Bierman, Jacob Jolij, Arnaud Delorme — a mixed proponent/sceptic team.
- **Key points**: Experiment 1's preregistered confirmatory hypothesis was not supported; an exploratory English-language subgroup showed an effect. ### Bem, Tressoldi, Rabeyron & Duggan (2015/2016) — the 90-experiment meta-analysis and its critics
- **URL**: https://f1000research.com/articles/4-1188 (F1000Research 4:1188; v1 2015, v2 2016; PMC4706048 grep-verified)
- **Key points** (verified): "90 experiments from 33 laboratories in 14 countries"; Hedges' g 0.09; "A Bayesian analysis yielded a Bayes Factor of 5.1 × 10^9"; "When DJB's original experiments are excluded, the combined effect size for replications by independent investigators is 0.06, z = 4.16, p = 1.1 × 10^-5, and the BF value is 3,853"; p-curve estimate 0.20. Closing line: "Meanwhile the data increasingly compel the conclusion that it really is like that." Acknowledgements thank Daniel Lakens "for his collaboration in preparing the p-curve analysis and critical examination of effect sizes. These helpful colleagues do not necessarily concur with our conclusions."
- **Peer review status**: the two formal F1000 referees (Paul Grigoriev; Ina Vitalevna Vasileva) both marked "Approved". The substantive criticism sits in the article's open comment thread, not the referee reports:
  - **Lakens** (comment 8 Jan 2016, reproducing his earlier review; blog post of 4 April 2015, "Why a meta-analysis of 90 precognition studies does not provide convincing evidence of a true effect", https://daniellakens.blogspot.com/2015/04/why-meta-analysis-of-90-precognition.html, grep-verified): "the estimated effect size when correcting for publication bias is 0.008"; "there is no good reason to assume that anything more than publication bias is going on in this meta-analysis"; "No researcher should be convinced by this meta-analysis that psi effects exist."   - **Schwarzkopf** (comments 2 Nov 2015 and 11 Jan 2016, grep-verified): notes the abstract/text Bayes-factor mismatch (1.4 vs 5.1 × 10^9; corrected in v2) and asks "how does the robustness analysis look when Bem's own data are excluded? ## Major Positions

### Pure-bias reading (Kekecs consortium framing; Lakens; Schimmack; Francis)
- **Core claim**: positive Bem-paradigm results are the product of questionable research practices and publication bias; corrected effect is about zero (Lakens 0.008).
- **Relation to site tenets**: no conflict. The Map's retrocausality rests on physics; a null behavioural-precognition record removes nothing it uses.

### Proponent reading (Bem; Tressoldi; Mossbridge; Radin)
- **Core claim**: meta-analytic effects survive bias corrections; nulls under hyper-strict protocols reflect unselected participants, rigid procedure, or experimenter/sheep-goat moderators (Tressoldi's and Radin's stated reservations about the TPP, per the SPR Psi Encyclopedia entry — secondary, not verified at source).
- **Relation to site tenets**: the Map does not need this reading to be true; its §What Presentiment Would Mean If Real already says a confirmed presentiment effect would weigh against the Minimal Quantum Interaction tenet's content constraint rather than for the Map.

### Methodological-reform reading (Kekecs et al. themselves; Walleczek et al.)
- **Core claim**: the result's main value is demonstrating consensus design, born-open data and real-time reporting; the paper's stated goal "was not to prove or disprove" ESP.

## Key Debates

### Does the TPP close the experimenter-effect escape?
- Proponents co-designed the protocol (15 of 29 panel members) and Tressoldi ran the Padova site; Schlitz 2021 had Bem as co-author; belief in psi did not moderate TPP outcomes. The escape has narrowed from "sceptics' labs fail" to "strict protocols suppress the effect", which Kekecs et al. themselves list as a residual possibility ("unrecognized moderating variables"; "very rare or extremely small").

### Presentiment versus Bem-style precognition
- The 2023/2025 nulls are about behavioural guessing of erotic-image position. They say nothing directly about skin-conductance presentiment, whose record remains the proponents' meta-analyses versus Schwarzkopf's quality critique. The host page's §Two Distinct Claims already draws the line; its critics sections do not yet respect it.

## Bearing on the Map's Position

The Map's retrocausal commitments rest on time-symmetric physics, so a null behavioural-precognition record is compatible with every tenet and removes nothing the Map uses. A positive record would not have been evidence for Tenet 3 either: Bem-paradigm guessing and physiological presentiment are unconscious or near-chance responses to external stimuli, not conscious selection among internal neural possibilities, and the host page already argues that presentiment as claimed would import content no neural candidate encodes and so weigh against the Minimal Quantum Interaction tenet. The correct update is calibration only: the page should stop describing the Bem-paradigm literature as "genuinely contested" between symmetric camps and should keep the presentiment literature's narrower, still-contested status separate.
