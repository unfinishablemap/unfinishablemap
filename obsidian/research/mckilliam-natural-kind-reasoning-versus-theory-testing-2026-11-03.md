## Executive Summary

McKilliam's alternative does **not** answer the divergence objection that `philosophy-of-science-under-dualism` L110 records against the Map's Lakatosian/Bayesian appraisal machinery. Relative to the Map's use of that objection, it **sharpens** it.

- **It runs under dualism only on a processing kind.** In the thesis that is the paper's precursor, the posited kind is "conscious processing", a form of brain information processing. The thesis also lists lawfully correlated non-physical properties among the metaphysical views it leaves open. A dualist can therefore read the method's output as the physical relatum of a psychophysical law. **Stated plainly: the method cannot run on a kind individuated by phenomenal character.** It finds where the kind is, not what it is. That is a cost to the Map.
- **The same neutrality makes it idle for the Map's question.** If it converges, it settles which systems belong to the processing kind, an answer physicalism and dualism can share.
- **Divergence recurs inside the method.** McKilliam argues that Bayne and Shea's version can yield different kinds "for perfectly rational reasons". His own escape needs introspection to be correctable by third-person evidence, a condition the Map's observation void doubts. Chang (2017), the source of his model of iteration, allows that iteration may settle on local minima.
- **Beni (2026) compounds the objection.** One paper targets McKilliam by name: inference to the best explanation imports theory, so the method cannot be theory-neutral. Another charges Lakatosian demarcation itself with theory-ladenness and concludes that no methodological recipe is likely to settle the underdetermination.

The verdict is neutral between the programmes. Nothing here is evidence for dualism.

## Key Sources

### McKilliam (2025), *Noûs* — abstract-level
- The paper explains "why a theory‐driven approach to consciousness science, even one sensitive to Lakatosian norms and updating in a Bayesian manner, may lead to divergence rather than convergence".
- It says: "I draw on the history of thermometry and natural kind reasoning to sketch a theory‐neutral route towards progress in consciousness science."
- OpenAlex lists 32 citing works, including four Beni papers and Kammerer's illusionist *House of Mirrors* (2026, not read).

### Mckilliam (2023), thesis — full text
- **Divergence (ch. 3 §1).** "Competing theories of consciousness associate consciousness with different but equally real features of our neurocognitive mechanisms." Priors over detection rules correlate with which theory a researcher favours. He hedges: "I do not take the arguments above to definitively show that a theory-driven approach cannot succeed." His targets are HOT, GWT, local recurrence and midbrain theories. He applies the argument to theory choice within consciousness science, **not to dualism**.
- **Method (ch. 3 §3–4).** Thermometers first validated against felt heat came to correct it. Likewise, the best explanation of the clustering of introspectability, report and planning "is that a distinct form of processing—conscious processing—causally facilitates" them. On this method, "it is conscious processing, rather than subjective experience itself, that is doing the explanatory work."
- **Bridge to experience.** "Since, in my own case, this kind of processing comes along with a subjective experience, the simplest hypothesis is that it does so in their case too."
- **Phillips's starting-point objection (ch. 3 §5).** "Rather than identifying properties that correlate with consciousness, the first step is to identify properties that correlate with our initial line of evidence about consciousness—namely introspective judgments."
- **Against Bayne & Shea's "attractor".** "If researchers have sufficiently different prior confidences in the initial criteria used to draw inferences about consciousness from behavioural data, then causal modelling may actually lead them to converge on different natural kinds for perfectly rational reasons." His own escape: "I suspect that the possibility of breaking out of Phillips’ objection and converging toward consensus in consciousness science depends on it being mistaken". Here "it" is the intuition that how something feels cannot be in error.
- **Metaphysics (ch. 1).** "This brand of realism does not commit one to any particular view on the metaphysics of subjective experience." Among the options he lists is "the instantiation of non-physical properties that systematically come and go with certain neural processes by virtue of irreducible laws of nature (Chalmers, 1996)".

### Beni (2026b), *NoC* — full text
- Lists "Bayne and Shea 2020, Birch 2022, Mckilliam 2024" as theory-light NKR, but engages only Bayne & Shea in detail. This confirms the 08-21 review's "principally Bayne and Shea".
- Argues from predictive processing and the Bogen–Woodward data/phenomena distinction that observation and design are theory-laden, and defends theory-guided Bayesian adversarial collaboration.
- Its coda turns the charge on Lakatos: "Whether this takes the form of demarcating progressive from degenerative strands of a research programme, or distinguishing theoretically significant commitments from dispensable ones, such prescriptions themselves rest on substantive theoretical commitments rather than on neutral methodological ground."
- It concedes: "The view developed here offers no quick resolution of that underdetermination; indeed, the argument indicates that no philosophical or methodological recipe is likely to settle it."

### Beni (2026a), *NIP* — abstract-level
- This is **the Beni paper aimed at McKilliam by name**, and it is new relative to the 08-21 review. It reports "a methodological incompatibility arising from McKilliam's (2024) proposal: deploying Inference to the Best Explanation (IBE) within NKR directly contradicts the aspiration for theory-neutrality".
- Beni's positive alternative (*JGPS* 2026) is structural realism over predictive processing, "shifting the focus from identifying a natural kind of consciousness to understanding invariant structural relationships across diverse theories". It is itself FEP-committed.

### Chang (2017) — abstract-level
- "Inquiry can begin in the absence of assured foundations, using the results to correct and refine its starting point."
- "If the iterative process converges, the pattern may be regarded as cumulative progress." Convergence may be to a "local minimum", and Chang proposes that "all local minima should be appreciated as achievements with realist significance".
- The iteration McKilliam borrows was designed by a pluralist.

### Supporting (abstracts)
- **Kob & Marvan (2026)** ask "how can consciousness constitute a unified natural kind if realized by fundamentally distinct NCC kinds?" This is a live route to several endpoints.
- **Negro & Mudrik (2025)** call analogical abduction "the strongest method for extrapolating consciousness" to nonstandard systems, "despite limitations in current consciousness science".
- **McKilliam (2025, *AJP*)** treats the bridge as "analogical inferences grounded in explanatory considerations".
- **Overgaard et al. (2025)** likewise urge "construct-based, data-driven, and iterative approaches".

## Analysis

### Does it answer the objection?

**No, not as the Map uses it.** McKilliam's divergence concerns rival first-order theories. L110 applies it to the appraisal of physicalist against dualist programmes. The transfer is fair, since rational updating from divergent priors need not converge, but it is the Map's application and not McKilliam's own target. If natural-kind reasoning works, it delivers convergence on the processing kind's extension. Both programmes can accept that, so the method relocates convergence away from the appraisal question.

It sharpens the objection in two ways:
- (a) divergence recurs inside the method, as the Bayne–Shea critique, the conditional escape and Chang's local minima all show;
- (b) Beni (2026b) extends the theory-ladenness charge to Lakatosian demarcation and grants that nothing settles the underdetermination.

### Can it run when the kind is individuated by phenomenal character?

**No.** It runs under dualism on a processing kind only.

- **The pull toward yes.** Interactionism (Tenet 3) makes phenomenal states candidate common causes of the markers. That fits the thermometry model better than epiphenomenalism, under which the posited cause could never be phenomenal.
- **Why not.** Under the corridor reading of Tenet 2, the non-physical contribution "preserves the aggregate Born measure by construction" (PoS, Relation to Site Perspective). Iterated IBE over public markers therefore cannot tell a phenomenally individuated kind from the processing kind that co-occurs with it. This is the Map's own point about thermometry in `topics/consciousness-and-the-problem-of-measurement-standards`: there is "no equivalent of the shared water bath", and operationalising "measures neural states, not phenomenal states".

Two further costs fall on the Map:
- **The bridge is a simplicity inference.** [[problem-of-other-minds]] already says behavioural similarity "furnishes weaker evidence under dualism" and that analogy "must also assume that physical similarity tracks phenomenal similarity". In hard cases (AI, invertebrates, borderline patients) "the simplest hypothesis" crosses the boundary in dispute, which is where the fifth tenet withholds the licence.
- **Graded evidence against a sharp joint.** [[is-conscious-being-a-natural-kind]] and [[coupling-engagement-condition]] posit a sharp on/off fact. Cluster evidence is graded, so in borderline cases the method cannot by itself locate that joint. This gives the coupling-engagement "trigger problem" a methodological face.

**Correction to `reviews/optimistic-2026-11-02-map-as-science-wing`.** It hoped that a kind "individuated by phenomenal character and detected by co-varying markers" could make the method "the dualist's best instrument rather than a threat", and "an alternative to parsimony-driven theory choice". McKilliam individuates by processing, which is where the neutrality comes from. The bridge step is a parsimony step. The method is compatible with dualism and is not its instrument.

## Relation to Site Perspective (Tenets)

- **Dualism**: compatible. The method's output can be read as the physical relatum of a psychophysical law. No evidential bearing.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction**: the corridor reading is why phenomenal individuation is invisible to the method. A cost.
- **Bidirectional Interaction**: phenomenal states are candidate common causes of the markers, but not detectably so.
- **No Many Worlds**: no bearing.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits**: the bridge step is a simplicity inference, so do not present the method as parsimony-free.
