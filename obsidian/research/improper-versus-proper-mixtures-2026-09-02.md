## Executive Summary

The improper/proper mixture distinction is canonical, originating with d'Espagnat (1966 paper; developed in *Conceptual Foundations of Quantum Mechanics*, W.A. Benjamin, 1st ed. 1971, 2nd ed. 1976 — the edition the Map cites). It remains standard vocabulary in the decoherence literature through Schlosshauer's 2019 Physics Reports review. The corpus deploys it in roughly 35 live files (16 topics, 9 concepts, 5 apex, 2 positions, 2 project, tenets.md — full census below) with no page owning it. Externally, the landscape has four poles: (1) d'Espagnat's own argument, which he defended into the 2000s on explicitly *logico-semantical* rather than mathematical grounds; (2) the FAPP lineage — Bell coined FAPP in "Against 'measurement'" (1990) as an *accusation* of imprecision, and Wallace later inverted it into a *defence* (branching structure as emergent-but-real); the two uses run in opposite directions and the corpus should not conflate them; (3) a published "distinction is empty" attack line (Kirkpatrick 2001; Castellani 2022) which is an operational-indistinguishability argument, not a theorem; and (4) the insolubility theorems (surveyed by Bacciagaluppi 2012), which cut the other way — within unitary quantum mechanics no proper mixture over outcomes can be produced, so the no-go defeater the quantum-darwinism article names cannot arrive as a theorem *inside* unitary QM. That sharpens the defeater into three specific routes (empirically confirmed objective collapse; Everett; epistemic reinterpretation of states), each of which the Map already engages elsewhere. Two corpus quote-fidelity defects surfaced during grep-verification and are flagged as binding findings below.

## Key Sources

### Bell, "Against 'measurement'" (Physics World 3(8), 33–40, August 1990)
- **URL**: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2058-7058/3/8/26 (paywalled); raw text verified from the mirror at https://www.informationphilosopher.com/solutions/scientists/bell/Against_Measurement.pdf
- **Type**: Article (Bell's last paper; also in the Erice proceedings *Sixty-Two Years of Uncertainty*)
- **Key points**:
  - Coins the acronym: "FOR ALL PRACTICAL PURPOSES = FAPP" **[grep-verified]**.
  - The vagueness charge: "The LL formulation, with vaguely defined wavefunction collapse, when used with good taste and discretion, is adequate FAPP. It remains that the theory is ambiguous in principle, about exactly when and exactly how the collapse occurs, about what is microscopic and what is macroscopic" **[grep-verified]**.
  - Concedes the operational point while refusing the inference: "I am fully convinced of the practical elusiveness, even the absence FAPP, of interference between macroscopically different states" **[grep-verified]**.
  - The forbidden words: "Here are some words which, however legitimate and necessary in application, have no place in a formulation with any pretension to physical precision: system, apparatus, environment, microscopic, macroscopic, reversible, irreversible, observable, information, measurement." **[grep-verified]**
  - "The first charge against 'measurement', in the fundamental axioms of quantum mechanics, is that it anchors there the shifty split of the world into 'system' and 'apparatus'." **[grep-verified]**
- **Tenet alignment**: Aligns with Tenets 1 and 4 in spirit — Bell insists the FAPP-adequate story is ambiguous *in principle*, which is exactly the Map's claim that operational equivalence does not settle ontology. Caution: Bell's own preferred exits were Bohm and GRW, both of which the Map rejects; he is an ally on the diagnosis, not the cure.
- **Note for the article**: Bell's FAPP is an *accusation*. The "FAPP rejoinder" the QD article gestures at is the *inverted* use — treating FAPP-adequacy as good enough — which Bell explicitly wrote the paper against. The canonical FAPP *defence* is Wallace (below), not Bell.

### d'Espagnat — the original argument and his late defences
- **Books** (all **[catalogue-copy]** — no raw text obtainable): the distinction is introduced in a 1966 paper and developed in *Conceptual Foundations of Quantum Mechanics* (W.A. Benjamin; 1st ed. 1971, 2nd ed. 1976) and *Veiled Reality* (Addison-Wesley, 1995). The corpus's standard citation "(d'Espagnat, 1976)" matches the 2nd edition.
- **Raw-verifiable primary artefacts** (all pdftotext + grep this run):
  - "Reply to K A Kirkpatrick" (arXiv:quant-ph/0111081, 2001): the reason the improper mixture cannot be given an ignorance interpretation "is, as we shall see, not mathematical but logico-semantical" **[grep-verified]**; and: "the 'improper mixtures' represented by the partial traces of a composite system pure state statistical operator cannot be given an ignorance interpretation. It is therefore this claim that my critics should aim at disproving." **[grep-verified]**
  - "A note on measurement" (arXiv:quant-ph/0101141, 2001): argues that in the relevant post-measurement situation "proper mixtures with the requested properties do not exist" **[grep-verified]** — i.e. the ignorance reading is not merely unlicensed but unavailable.
- **Tenet alignment**: The distinction is the formal core of the Map's Tenet 1/Tenet 4 reading of decoherence; d'Espagnat's own metaphysics ("veiled reality", open realism) is adjacent to but not identical with the Map's interactionism.
- **Important nuance for the article**: d'Espagnat's late defence relocates the argument from mathematics to semantics — what the word "ignorance" can coherently mean when the global state fixes no local fact. An article that presents the argument as a formal theorem overstates it; d'Espagnat himself did not.

### Kirkpatrick, "Indistinguishability and improper mixtures" (arXiv:quant-ph/0109146, 2001)
- **Key points**: the direct attack d'Espagnat answered. Abstract: "All quantum mixtures are what d'Espagnat has termed 'improper.' His 'proper' mixture cannot be created — if welcher weg, or distinguishing, information exists, an improper mixture results, while in the absence of such information, the resulting 'mixture' is a pure state." **[grep-verified]** Argues d'Espagnat's inconsistency claim "fails to account for the indistinguishability of the pure-state subensembles" **[grep-verified]**.
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with the Map's reading — if no proper mixture is ever physically preparable, the contrast class the Map's argument leans on is empty in one direction.

### Castellani, "All quantum mixtures are proper" (arXiv:2201.04143; philsci-archive 20113, 2022)
- **Key points**: the modern emptiness attack, titled in the *opposite* direction from Kirkpatrick. Abstract: "It is argued that proper and improper quantum mixed states have no observable differences, and hence should not be distinguished." **[grep-verified]** Section 4 conclusion: "the proper/improper distinction is unphysical, i.e. not detectable by any experiment on S" **[grep-verified]**.
- **Instructive asymmetry**: Kirkpatrick says all mixtures are improper; Castellani says all are proper. They agree on operational indistinguishability and disagree on which label survives — evidence that the *deflationary* conclusion is underdetermined even among deflationists, a point the article can use.
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with Tenets 1 and 4 as deployed; a worked reply is exactly what the new page owes.

### The in-principle-testability response (Zeh; Fortin & Lombardi)
- Zeh, "Roots and Fruits of Decoherence" (arXiv:quant-ph/0512078, Séminaire Poincaré 2005): "The conceptually important difference between true and apparent ensembles was clearly pointed out by Bernard d'Espagnat [ref] when he distinguished between proper and improper mixtures. In the case of virtual (reversible) decoherence, this difference can even be observed as recoherence (a relocalization of the superposition, that would be impossible for a proper mixture)." **[grep-verified]**
- Fortin & Lombardi, "Partial traces in decoherence and in interpretation: What do reduced states refer to?" (arXiv:1404.3264; *Foundations of Physics* 2014): carries d'Espagnat's point that there is no theoretical bar to accessing "some of the traced over degrees of freedom, and this access would permit us to show that the proper mixture and the improper mixture are, in principle, testably different" **[grep-verified]**; argues reduced states are coarse-grained states, not quantum states of subsystems.
- **Why this matters**: the emptiness attack holds only in the irreversible/FAPP regime. In reversible regimes the difference is *observable as recoherence*. So the honest statement of the FAPP boundary is: detectable in principle and at small scales, undetectable FAPP at macroscopic scales — which is precisely the shape [[topics/forward-in-time-conscious-selection]] L81 already gives it ("identical predictions for any physical apparatus with finite resources"). The new page can ground that sentence in Zeh's recoherence point.

### Schlosshauer — the standard-reference statements
- 2004 RMP review (arXiv:quant-ph/0312059v4, raw TeX): proper terminology — "an 'ignorance-interpretable,' or 'proper' ensemble" **[grep-verified]**; the FAPP passage: "the measurement problem appears to be solved, at least for all practical purposes. However, the crucial difficulty in the above reasoning is justifying the second step … one still needs to impose an interpretive framework that explains why only one of the localized states is realized and/or perceived" **[grep-verified]**; and: "the resulting reduced density matrix would represent an improper mixture, with no outcome having been singled out" **[grep-verified]**. These are the correct replacement quotes for the misattributed sentence flagged below.
- 2019 Physics Reports review (arXiv:1911.06282, raw TeX): "mixed reduced density matrices for systems entangled with an environment are not *ignorance-interpretable* … they do not describe a situation in which the system is in a pure state but one does not know which" **[grep-verified]**. Footnote in the same passage: "The degree to which this distinction between proper and improper ensembles is considered fundamental, or even relevant, depends in some measure on one's interpretation of quantum states" — with QBism as the worked example, where "the above cautionary note … would consequently appear unnecessary" **[grep-verified]**.
- **Tenet alignment**: the 2019 footnote is the strongest modern statement that the distinction's *weight* is interpretation-relative — direct support for the QD article's admission (its hinge status is a commitment, not a theorem), and a Tenet 5 exhibit: the distinction is an Occam boundary case where "simpler" (deflate it) and "correct" come apart depending on prior commitments about what quantum states are.

### The anti-FAPP lineage (decoherence does not solve the outcome problem)
- Adler, "Why decoherence has not solved the quantum measurement problem" (arXiv:quant-ph/0112095; *SHPMP* 2003): "We discuss why, contrary to claims recently made by P.W. Anderson, decoherence has not solved the quantum measurement problem." **[grep-verified]**
- Hance & Hossenfelder, "What does it take to solve the measurement problem?" (arXiv:2206.10445, 2022): "decoherence alone simply will not evolve a system into a single detector eigenstate, and hence does not agree with what we observe" **[grep-verified]**; also notes decoherence "is still a unitary and linear process … It can therefore not give rise to the measurement update" **[grep-verified]**.
- Tomaz, Mattos & Barbatti, "The Quantum Measurement Problem: A Review of Recent Trends" (arXiv:2502.19278v3, 2025; the corpus elsewhere associates it with *Philosophical Magazine*, DOI 10.1080/14786435.2025.2601922 — publisher page returned 403 this run, so the DOI pairing is **[catalogue-copy]**): "After the basis is chosen and quantum superpositions are suppressed, the system remains in a mixture of possible outcomes. Decoherence does not tell how and why only one of these outcomes is measured." **[grep-verified]** — this is the true source of the corpus's most-quoted sentence on the topic (see findings below).
- SEP, "The Role of Decoherence in Quantum Mechanics" (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qm-decoherence/): "while decoherence *does* explain why we do *not* observe superpositions of measurement results, it does *not* explain why we *do* observe measurement results in the first place" **[catalogue-copy — WebFetch summary, phrasing consistent with the entry's §2.1]**. Note: the summariser reported the entry does not itself discuss proper/improper vocabulary; treat that absence claim as unverified.

### The FAPP defence (the position the new page must answer at its strongest)
- Wallace, "Decoherence and Ontology (or: How I learned to stop worrying and love FAPP)", in Saunders, Barrett, Kent & Wallace (eds.), *Many Worlds?* (OUP 2010) **[catalogue-copy]**; and "Decoherence and its role in the modern measurement problem" (arXiv:1111.2187; *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A* 2012), downloaded this run — chapter-title reference grep-verified in its bibliography.
- **Core move [catalogue-copy]**: the branching structure decoherence defines is "for all practical purposes" relative to the microscopic formalism yet emergent and real, in the same way higher-level ontology is real elsewhere in science. On this view nothing needs to convert the improper mixture into anything; all elements are actualised in branches.
- **Why the Map must engage this rather than Bell**: Wallace's is the philosophically serious version of the L66 rejoinder. The Map's answer runs through Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds / indexical identity), not through denying the operational equivalence — the corpus already takes this line, but no page states that the FAPP rejoinder, pressed hard, tends to *become* Everett or an epistemic view rather than remaining a free-standing deflation.
- Grangier, "Revisiting the Interpretations of Quantum Mechanics: From FAPP Solutions to Contextual Ontologies" (arXiv:2601.20488, Jan 2026): "Bell introduced the acronym 'FAPP' to describe a certain attitude toward the measurement problem: the view that quantum mechanics works so well in practice that conceptual difficulties can be set aside. Decoherence theory has greatly reinforced this attitude" **[grep-verified]**. Organises the whole landscape as FAPP-solutions versus ontological solutions; his own CSM framework is offered as "a non-FAPP ontology" **[grep-verified]** with no role for consciousness — a live rival ontological closure worth a sentence in the article.

### The no-go direction: insolubility theorems
- Bacciagaluppi, "Insolubility Theorems and EPR Argument" (philsci-archive 9121; *EJPS* 2012): generalisations of von Neumann's result are "collectively known in the foundations literature as 'insolubility theorems' for the measurement problem" **[grep-verified]**; "All more recent theorems focus on the impossibility of reproducing (the statistics of) definite pointer readings" **[grep-verified]**. Family includes Fine 1970, Shimony 1974, Brown 1986, Busch & Shimony 1996 (arXiv:quant-ph/9604013), Bassi & Ghirardi 2000 (*Phys. Lett. A* 275, 373 — "A General Argument Against the Universal Validity of the Superposition Principle") **[metadata catalogue-copy]**.
- **Research finding of direct value**: the defeater [[topics/quantum-darwinism-and-consciousness]] L116 names — "a no-go theorem demonstrat[ing] that decoherence alone necessarily produces definite outcomes" — would have to *contradict* this theorem family, since decoherence is unitary dynamics and the insolubility theorems show unitary dynamics cannot deliver definite outcomes (a proper mixture over pointer readings) from arbitrary initial states. So the feared no-go cannot arrive as a theorem within unitary QM. What CAN close the gap: (a) empirically confirmed objective collapse — proper mixtures produced *physically*, by new dynamics (this route is live and testable; the corpus already tracks it in [[topics/testing-consciousness-collapse]]); (b) accepting Everett — no single outcome to select (Tenet 4 territory); (c) epistemic reinterpretation of states (QBism-style) — the distinction becomes inapplicable rather than false (Schlosshauer 2019 footnote). The article should restate the defeater as this trilemma; "physics might prove decoherence suffices" is not among the live options, and saying so precisely *strengthens* the honest-defeater posture rather than weakening it.

## Major Positions

### Ontological-weight realism (the Map's position)
- **Proponents**: d'Espagnat; Schlosshauer (with the 2019 interpretation-relativity caveat); Adler; Fortin & Lombardi; Hance & Hossenfelder (operationally); Bell as ancestor.
- **Core claim**: mathematical identity of density matrices does not entail physical identity of situations; tracing out delivers no fact of the matter about which outcome obtains.
- **Relation to tenets**: grounds Tenets 1 and 4's reading of the outcome gap; the Map's whole post-decoherence corridor stands on it.

### Operational deflation (the distinction is empty)
- **Proponents**: Kirkpatrick 2001; Castellani 2022; the "sufficiently thorough decoherence" physicists the QD article L66 gestures at.
- **Core claim**: no experiment on the system distinguishes the two; a distinction with no observable difference should not be drawn.
- **Weaknesses found this run**: (a) the two flagship papers deflate in opposite directions (all-improper vs all-proper); (b) reversible-regime recoherence is an observable difference (Zeh), so the emptiness claim must be scoped to the irreversible regime, at which point it is a FAPP claim, and FAPP claims are exactly what is in dispute; (c) pressed hard it tends to collapse into Everett or an epistemic view, which are separately-priced positions, not free deflation.
- **Relation to tenets**: conflicts with Tenets 1 and 4; a Tenet 5 case study (parsimony judged with incomplete knowledge).

### Emergent-branching FAPP realism (Wallace)
- **Core claim**: FAPP structure is emergent and real; no conversion problem exists because all branches are actual.
- **Relation to tenets**: direct collision with Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds, indexical identity). The Map's reply route already exists in [[topics/quantum-darwinism-and-consciousness]] §Why Not Everett.

### Interpretation-relative dissolution
- **Proponents**: QBism (per Schlosshauer 2019 footnote); Grangier's CSM as a distinct non-FAPP contextual ontology.
- **Core claim**: whether the distinction matters depends on what a quantum state is; on epistemic readings the cautionary note lapses.
- **Relation to tenets**: orthogonal rather than opposed; the Map's realism about states is an unstated premise the new page should surface.

## Key Debates

### Is the distinction empirically empty?
Sides: Kirkpatrick/Castellani (yes, on the system alone) vs d'Espagnat/Zeh/Fortin–Lombardi (no in principle — access to traced-over degrees of freedom, recoherence in reversible regimes). Current state: both live; the emptiness claim is secure only FAPP and only for embedded observers with finite resources — which the corpus's own L81 framing already says. The honest scorecard: locally and FAPP, undetectable; globally and in principle, detectable; the Map's wager is that ontology follows the global description.

### Whose FAPP is it?
Bell coined FAPP as a charge of imprecision; the deflationary rejoinder wears it as a badge; Wallace makes the badge philosophically respectable at the price of Everett. The QD article's L66 rejoinder paragraph currently blends these; the new page should separate them, because the Map's answer to each differs (to Bell: agreement; to the deflationist: recoherence + scoping; to Wallace: Tenet 4).

### Can physics alone close the outcome gap?
The insolubility-theorem family says not by unitary dynamics. Objective collapse could close it empirically (and is independently constrained — Gran Sasso bounds on Diósi–Penrose, tracked elsewhere in the corpus). Everett and epistemic readings dissolve rather than close it. Resolution state: open, and the openness is structural, not merely current.
