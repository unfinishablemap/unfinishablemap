## Executive Summary

The subject is worth covering but not as a standalone article. In the closing parts of *Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind* (EPM, §§48–63), Sellars tells a "myth of my own". Jones postulates thoughts, modelled on speech, and impressions, modelled on "inner replicas". He then trains his fellows to use the theory on themselves, so that "What began as a language with a purely theoretical use has gained a reporting role" (§59). This is the founding text for the view that the concepts of introspection are theory-shaped and that privileged access is a trained, derivative reporting use. SEP's *Eliminative Materialism* names Sellars 1956 as a main root of eliminativism, and Churchland (1985) says the direct-introspection idea arose "in a theoretical environment prepared largely by Sellars 1956".

The note makes four findings the corpus lacks. (1) **Sellars drew a realist moral.** He warns that calling concepts pertaining to thinking theoretical concepts would be "most misleading" (§59), and he says the language of impressions "embodies a 'discovery' that there are such things" (§62). Eliminativism needs an extra premise, possible wholesale falsity, which Churchland supplies (1981, p. 70) and Sellars did not hold. (2) **Churchland's subjects shift.** His 1981 introspection argument concerns "beliefs and desires". On qualia in 1985 he is a *reductionist* who would retrain introspection in neuroscience's terms, not an eliminativist. (3) **The Map's astronomy analogy is Churchland's own.** It appears verbatim at 1981 p. 70 but goes unattributed on `concepts/constitutive-vs-referring-observation` and aimed at attitudes rather than qualia. (4) **One Map page glosses Sellars inaccurately.** `topics/eliminative-materialism` L78 makes Sellars say self-evidence is "an artefact of long enculturation".

## Key Sources

### Sellars, "Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind" (1956), §§48–63
- **URL**: https://www.ditext.com/sellars/epm.html (frames; parts at `epm12.html`–`epm16.html`)
- **Type**: Primary text
- **Key points**:
  - The Rylean ancestors start with a language whose "fundamental descriptive vocabulary speaks of public properties of public objects" (§48). They gain semantic discourse (§§49–50) and crude theory (§52).
  - §51 contains the crucial qualifier. Theory/observation is "involved in" the logic of inner-episode concepts, "for it would be paradoxical and, indeed, incorrect, to say that these concepts are theoretical concepts".
  - §53: Jones is introduced as a methodological behaviourist. Methodological behaviourism does not deny "privileged access" or that introspection is "on some topics, at least, quite reliable". Its "essential point" is "that we introspect in terms of common sense mentalistic concepts".
  - §54: behaviourism that restricts itself to explicitly definable concepts would be "unduly restrictive", because "nothing in the nature of sound scientific procedure requires this self-denial".
  - §56: thoughts are modelled on overt speech. On the Jonesean theory, "the true cause of intelligent nonhabitual behavior is 'inner speech.'"
  - §58(1): the theory "need not be given a Socratic or Cartesian form". §58(2): the episodes are "in" language-users "as molecular impacts are 'in' gases, not as 'ghosts' are in 'machines.'" Their purity is "a methodological purity". The text adds that "many" would say thoughts are to be "identified" with complex events in the cerebral cortex, and that "Jones, of course, has no such idea." §58(5): thoughts "are not introduced as immediate experiences."
  - §59: Dick "can be trained to give reasonably reliable self-descriptions, using the language of the theory, without having to observe his overt behavior". "What began as a language with a purely theoretical use has gained a reporting role." The concepts are "as intersubjective as the concept of a positron". The reporting role "is built on and presupposes" that status. "this privacy is not an 'absolute privacy.'"
  - §60: impressions are posited as "the end results of the impingement of physical objects and processes on various parts of the body".
  - §61(1): impressions are "states of the perceiving subject, not a class of particulars", and the model is "a domain of 'inner replicas'". §61(3): the analogy's essential feature is a resemblance system that "is structurally similar to the ways in which the colors and shapes of visible objects resemble and differ". §61(4): the view that the content of immediate experience is incommunicable "is but another expression of the Myth of the Given". §61(5): a micro-theory of sentient organisms with "variables peculiar to sentient things". The same section treats "physical theory" covering behaviour as "a truism" or "a mistake", which bears on the host's physical₁/physical₂ material.
  - §62: Jones trains the reporting use "when, and only when, according to the theory, they are indeed having the impression of a red triangle". "the language of impressions embodies a 'discovery' that there are such things". Of the sense-datum theorist: "He construes as data the particulars and arrays of particulars which he has come to be able to observe … It is in the very act of taking that he speaks of the given."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral on Dualism; Sellars is a scientific realist. The myth is congenial to Tenet 5, since it argues against parsimonious restriction to observational concepts (§54). It presupposes causally efficacious inner episodes (§56), which fits Tenet 3. It challenges the Map's first-person evidential base (see below).

## Major Positions

### Sellars: realist theory-origin (EPM 1956)
- **Core claim**: concepts of inner episodes are intersubjective in origin, introduced on the model of overt speech or of inner replicas. Their reporting role is a real but derivative dimension of their use. Such episodes are real, as molecules are.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral on substance (§58(1)). It does not deny privileged access (§§53, 59). It denies only *absolute* privacy and the Given. The Map can share the anti-Cartesian epistemology of concepts without sharing the ontology Sellars ends with (the host already marks that he is not a dualist).

### Eliminativism about attitudes (Churchland 1981; roots in Feyerabend, Rorty)
- **Core claim**: folk psychology is a theory and may be radically false. Introspective judgement is a trained conceptual response whose integrity depends on that theory's.
- **Relation to site tenets**: the Map is conciliatory on attitude *structure* (`topics/eliminative-materialism`, "The Map's stance on classical eliminativism"). The argument becomes a threat only when extended to phenomenal character.

### Reduction with retrained introspection (Churchland 1985)
- **Core claim**: qualia are brain states. Introspection, already learned, could be retrained to report them non-inferentially in neuroscientific terms.
- **Relation to site tenets**: conflicts with Tenet 1. It concedes the structural point the Map needs: the framework shifts while the states discriminated, and the discriminating mechanisms, stay put.

### Eliminativism about phenomenal character (illusionism)
- **Core claim**: introspection misrepresents quasi-phenomenal states as phenomenal (Frankish 2016, already on the Map).
- **Relation to site tenets**: the Map's bedrock disagreement, already marked under "Honest Limitation" on `concepts/constitutive-vs-referring-observation`.

## The Strongest Eliminativist Reading (for the writer to state fairly)

1. Every concept deployed in an introspective report, including "experience", "impression" and "what it is like", originates as a posit introduced to explain behaviour (§§56, 60).
2. Non-inferential reporting is a trained use of those posits, calibrated by a teacher against the theory (§§59, 62). So the reliability of reports is reliability *relative to the framework*. Churchland puts it this way: "the integrity of any particular response is always contingent on the integrity of the acquired conceptual framework (theory) in which the response is framed" (1981, p. 70).
3. Theories can be false as a whole. A posit with a reporting use can be eliminated like any other, as the crystal spheres were.
4. The felt *givenness* of experience is predicted by the story, not evidence against it. The sense-datum theorist "construes as data" what he has learned to report, and "It is in the very act of taking that he speaks of the given" (§62). Gopnik's version: expertise "provides an illusion of direct perception" (1993, abstract).
5. Training evidence therefore cuts both ways. Churchland (1985, p. 54) predicts trained introspective precision ("for the most part learned"). The Map's evidence that meditators show greater metacognitive precision is what a theory-laden view expects as well.
6. So even layer 1 ("that experience exists") deploys a Jonesean concept, and the claim that it is apodictic has the form Sellars diagnosed as the Myth of the Given.

## The Map's Honest Answer

- **Concede 2 and 5 at layers 2–3.** The Map already holds that introspective *judgement* is theory-shaped there (`topics/phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence` L118; `concepts/constitutive-vs-referring-observation` L61). Trainability is consistent with the Map's model but does not discriminate it from the theory-laden rival.
- **Deny that 1–2 yield 3.** Possible falsity is an added premise, and Sellars himself rejected the eliminative moral (§62 "discovery"; SEP Ramsey). The myth separates report from episode. Training is keyed to when "they are indeed having the impression" (§62). Churchland's own formula is a response "to one's internal states" (1981), and his 1985 retraining leaves "native discriminatory mechanisms" unchanged. Theory-ladenness falls on the conceptual response, not on what it responds to.
- **Use the causal premise, carefully.** Jones's posits are introduced as causes of behaviour (§56), so the myth cannot be run on behalf of epiphenomenalism. This is the Map's inference about the myth's structure. EPM does not discuss epiphenomenalism. It agrees with Sellars 1981, on which σ-ings are "not being epiphenomenal" (host ¶126). Do not overstate it into a proof of non-physical causation.
- **Mark the bedrock at 6.** Jones shows that a *referring* model of introspection is coherent, modelled on molecules and positrons (§§59, 62). It does not show that introspection is *only* referring. The Map's constitutive claim for layer 1 has the shape of what Sellars rejects as the Given. Neither side can establish its model from premises the other accepts. That is the same bedrock as illusionism, and the existing pages already state it honestly. The fold should say so and not claim a win.

```markdown
## The Myth of Jones

The closing parts of *Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind* (§§48–63) supply the positive half of the attack on the Given. Sellars calls it "a myth of my own" (§48), and commentators call it the Myth of Jones. "Our Rylean ancestors" start with a language for public objects and behaviour. To explain silent intelligent behaviour, the genius Jones postulates thoughts, inner episodes modelled on overt speech (§§56–58). He then postulates impressions, modelled on "inner replicas" of perceived objects (§§60–61). Once self-applied, "What began as a language with a purely theoretical use has gained a reporting role" (§59). Privileged access is real but derivative, a dimension of these concepts "which is built on and presupposes" their intersubjective role (§§59, 62). Sellars drew a realist moral: the language of impressions "embodies a 'discovery' that there are such things" (§62). He also warned it would be "most misleading" to call concepts pertaining to thinking theoretical concepts (§59).

Eliminativism adds a premise Sellars did not share, that the theory may be false as a whole. For Churchland an introspective judgement is "an instance of an acquired habit of conceptual response to one's internal states", sound only if its framework is (1981, p. 70). He meant beliefs and desires; for qualia he defended reduction (1985).

The eliminative reading presses the Map on phenomenal character. Trained reliability holds inside the trained framework and cannot certify it. Of the sense-datum theorist, who construes a theory's items as data, Sellars writes: "It is in the very act of taking that he speaks of the given" (§62). The Map grants theory-shaped judgement at the [[phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence|second and third layers]] of phenomenal authority and resists at the first, that experience is occurring. The myth itself keeps report and episode apart. Jones trains his fellows to report an impression "when, and only when, according to the theory, they are indeed having" it (§62), and Churchland's formula keeps the internal states the judgement responds to. Theory falls on the response, not on its object. Jones posits thoughts as causes, inner speech being "the true cause of intelligent nonhabitual behavior" (§56), so the myth gives epiphenomenalism no support. It cannot settle the first layer. Its model is referring observation, with inner episodes known as molecules are, and the Map's claim that observing bare experience partly constitutes it has the shape of what Sellars rejected as the Given, the bedrock dispute of [[constitutive-vs-referring-observation]].
```

## Guards for the Writer

- **Never call Sellars a dualist or enlist him for dualism.** Do not quote §58(1) ("primitive peoples may have had good reason to suppose that humans consist of two separate things") as support. Sellars grants the primitives reasons and says the theory "need not be given a Socratic or Cartesian form". The episodes are "in" us "not as 'ghosts' are in 'machines.'"
- **Do not write that EPM identifies thoughts with brain events.** §58(2) attributes that to "many" and adds "Jones, of course, has no such idea". Keep the host's ¶91 wording ("leaves open"; "might be complex physical₂ states").
- **Do not write "mental concepts are theoretical posits" as Sellars' thesis.** He calls that "most misleading" for thinking (§59) and "paradoxical and, indeed, incorrect" for inner-episode concepts generally (§51). The posits were *introduced* as theoretical and then gained a reporting role.
- **Originators.** The theory-theory name is Morton's (1980). Its application to self-knowledge is Gopnik's (1993). The direct-introspection suggestion was "first identified and explored" by Feyerabend and Rorty according to Churchland (1985, p. 55). "Theory-laden" is Hanson-era vocabulary, absent from EPM. Churchland 1981 does not cite Sellars, so do not write "Churchland, following Sellars, argued (1981)…". The lineage is sourced to SEP Ramsey and to Churchland 1985, p. 55.
- **Churchland's subjects.** In 1981 the subject is beliefs and desires. In 1985 he is a reductionist about qualia. Do not call 1985 eliminativist.
- **Never concede epiphenomenalism.** The causal point is the Map's inference about the myth's structure. Do not cite it as Sellars' anti-epiphenomenalism argument, because EPM does not make one.
- **Absence claims are scoped.** "Myth of Jones" and "theory-laden" are absent from the ditext transcription of EPM (all 16 parts). "Sellars" is absent from the Churchland 1981 OCR only.
- **Do not place Jones under the host's "Congenial" bullet.** It is a challenge the Map answers.

## Key Debates

### Is the myth a "theory theory"?
- **Sides**: SEP Ramsey calls the Sellarsian thesis the theory-theory and notes it is endorsed by realists "like Sellars". O'Shea 2012 (lead) examines how far Sellars' original aims match the modern theory theory.
- **Core disagreement**: whether "theoretical" in the myth means inferential theorising, or only intersubjective, model-based concept introduction with a non-inferential reporting role (§§51, 59).
- **Current state**: open. Garfield 1989 says "considerable confusion reigns concerning the central moral".

### Realist or eliminativist moral?
- **Sides**: Sellars (realist; posits as "discovery") against Feyerabend, Rorty and Churchland, who take possible falsity seriously.
- **Core disagreement**: whether theory-origin makes the posits revisable wholesale.
- **Current state**: the lineage is agreed (SEP; Churchland 1985, p. 55). The moral is disputed.

### Thoughts half versus impressions half
- Modern parity accounts took up the *thoughts* half. Gopnik's subject is intentional states. Carruthers' ISA keeps sensory states introspectible (`concepts/interpretive-sensory-access-theory` L48). The *impressions* half (§§60–62) bears on phenomenal character and runs to Churchland 1985 and illusionism. This explains why the Map's ISA page can concede attitude parity without touching phenomenal authority.
