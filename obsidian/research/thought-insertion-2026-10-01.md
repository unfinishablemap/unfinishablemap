## Verdict (read first)

**1. A dedicated concepts page is warranted, and a fuller mine-ness section is not viable.** `concepts/thought-insertion`, recommended **2,400 words** (range 2,200–2,600). Three reasons. (a) Eight pages use thought insertion as an established datum, "ownership preserved, agency lost", which is one contested reading among five in the literature, and two of them build an inference on it (agency-vs-passivity L125 toward Tenet 3; the two-tier project page L67 calls the double dissociation "robust"). (b) The corpus's only description of Billon 2013 (research note `depersonalisation-2026-09-30` L207, repeated in the optimistic review as "the strongest published counterexample to the kind-claim") gets his conclusion backwards: Billon *saves* the principle that consciousness entails subjectivity (details below). (c) The kind-claim analysis this note produces needs a home. [[mine-ness]] measures 3,496 words against a 3,500 gate (3 words of headroom), and [[self-and-self-consciousness]] is already over the gate at 3,538; neither can absorb it.

**2. What the case does to the kind-claim at [[self-and-self-consciousness]] L148.** The claim survives: no reading of thought insertion in the literature, including the readings of the authors who use it *against* for-me-ness universality, describes an inserted thought that is not given to the patient first-personally. Three findings go against the Map, and the page should state them plainly:
- **The falsifier cannot be met by any report.** "No position from which the lack is registered" excludes every case a patient could report. L148 says the falsifying scenario is "specifiable but absent from the evidence", but the clinical record *cannot* contain it, so its absence there carries no evidential weight. The same falsifier recurs at [[self-opacity]] L147 ("Evidence to date supplies only the latter") and [[cotard-delusion]] L64 (Cotard "passes").
- **The kind-claim is safe only because it is scoped to the feature nobody disputes.** Every party, including Lane, grants that a thin first-personal givenness survives; what the counterexample authors deny is *mineness*. **[Correction 2026-10-06: the ZK sentence continues "but claims that this has no bearing on the issue of for-me-ness" (p. 44) — Lane grants a trivial first-person perspective, not for-me-ness. The kind-claim's survival is on shared ground under a disputed name: every party grants the thought's epistemic asymmetry (accessible to the patient alone); the universalists call that for-me-ness, Lane denies it bears on for-me-ness. Matches the corrected [[thought-insertion]] article.]** On mineness the Map is already a generalist ([[mine-ness]] calls it separable and able to lapse). But the corpus's vocabulary puts the universal feature's name on the separable one: [[mine-ness]] L48 says phenomenologists call mine-ness "*for-me-ness* (Dan Zahavi)", and [[depersonalisation]]'s title is "Depersonalisation and the Separability of For-Me-Ness". Read in the Map's own vocabulary, Guillot's reading of thought insertion is a per-experience counterexample to L74 ("no genuinely anonymous experiences exist").
- **L148's supporting inference has a published objection that has not been answered.** "Every reported case is reported from somewhere" infers ownership from the thought's being *in* the reporter's mind. Billon 2013 §4.1 argues at length that apparent inner location does not entail subjectivity. The three universalist replies the corpus relies on restate that inference and do not engage his argument (0 hits for "inner location" or "Cartesian" in any of them).

**3. Tier: Tenet 1 *compatible*.** The case does not *suggest* or *discriminate*. A self-model account predicts the full profile: the thin perspectival feature is architectural, and a content-specific mineness tag can drop out. The only physicalist weakness is a missing mechanism (Frith 2012: "the most mysterious symptom of all"), which is a gap in neuroscience and cannot count as evidence for the Map.

**4. Coinage checks.** Schneider did not coin thought insertion. He grouped it among the first-rank symptoms (proposed 1939), and the phenomenon is described in psychiatric texts from 1810 on. The thought-insertion split between subjectivity and agency is Graham and Stephens's (1994); Gallagher's 2000 papers give the first-order "sense of ownership / sense of agency" version, and Gallagher says himself that his distinction differs from theirs.

## Executive Summary

Thought insertion is the complaint "there is a thought in me which is not mine", a Schneiderian first-rank symptom of schizophrenia that is also reported in other psychoses. Philosophers read it in five ways. On the **agency** reading (Graham and Stephens 1994; Gallagher 2000, 2004) ownership is preserved and agency lost; this is the reading every corpus page uses. On **ownership-loss** readings (Metzinger 2003; Lane 2012; Martin and Pacherie 2013) ownership itself is lost, and the agency loss is downstream. **Billon 2013** offers a distinctive variant: the inserted thought lacks subjectivity *and* phenomenality, and the patient has only "good reflexive awareness" of it. **Extra-element** readings (Billon and Kriegel 2015; Zahavi and Kriegel 2016; Ratcliffe and Wilkinson 2015) hold that nothing is missing and something, a mark of alienness, has been added. The **universalist disturbance** reading (Henriksen, Parnas and Zahavi 2019) holds that for-me-ness is "neither lacking nor unaffected but disturbed". Guillot's (2017) three-way split between for-me-ness, me-ness and mineness organises the field: thought insertion "lacks mineness, but retains both for-me-ness and me-ness", while depersonalisation lacks mineness and me-ness. On that split the corpus's "double dissociation" between thought insertion and depersonalisation is not the contrast between ownership and agency that the corpus describes. The HPZ 2019 formula comes very close to the Map's kind-claim (L148), but [[self-and-self-consciousness]] does not cite it. The mechanism literature (comparator, efference copy to inner speech, predictive coding, context integration) is active, and its own authors say it has not yet explained the symptom. None of it discriminates between the Map and physicalism.

## Key Sources

### Billon (2013), "Does consciousness entail subjectivity? The puzzle of thought insertion"
- **Citation**: *Philosophical Psychology* 26(2): 291–314. DOI 10.1080/09515089.2011.625117. Crossref confirms author, title, volume, issue and pages. Online first 2 November 2011 (iFirst pp. 1–24).
- **Retrieval**: **[F]** iFirst PDF via Wayback (`web.archive.org/web/20240829034612/https://philpapers.org/archive/BILDCE.pdf`), 12,569 words. Print page numbers below are iFirst page + 290. HPZ 2019 cites print 299 for the phenomenological constraint, H&P 2019 cites 301 for "They explicitly deny this!" and 310 for the ownership-not-agency claim, and all three fall at iFirst 9, 11 and 20.
- **The puzzle**: the "Cartesian principle": "If a thought is conscious then it is subjective" (p. 293), where subjective means "it appears to me to be mine 'from the inside'".
- **Against the agency reading**: classical accounts "save the Cartesian principle by interpreting thought insertion as a problem of the sense of agency for thought rather than as a problem of subjectivity. I argue that those accounts fail and that thought insertion really is a problem of subjectivity" (abstract, p. 291). The decisive objection is that agency loss cannot tell inserted thoughts from obsessional intrusions. Even when the intrusion feels irresistible, "Even then, patients with OCD will acknowledge that the intrusive thoughts are really theirs" (p. 296). On the reports themselves he writes: "Patients do not say that, although they have no agency for the thought, it nevertheless seems theirs. They explicitly deny this!" (p. 301).
- **Method**: a "phenomenological constraint": "If the patient says that an occurrent thought is not his, then it is not subjective" (p. 299).
- **The argument the universalists have not answered (§4.1)**: the standard rescue runs from the thought's being reported as "in him" to its being "his". Billon calls this premise INNER-LOC (SUB), denies that it is analytic, and accuses its users of "'sorite' shifts in meaning" through spatial metaphors ("within his ego boundaries", "within his stream of consciousness"). He names Campbell 1998, Gallagher 2000, Graham and Stephens 2000 and Synofzik et al. 2008. His positive account: "Apparent inner location is a good availability to reflexive awareness" (p. 303), a functional property that does not entail subjectivity.
- **The solution, which defends the principle**: "In a nutshell, inserted thoughts really lack subjectivity but they are not conscious either, at least in the usual sense of the term" (p. 294). "The patient would have a good reflexive awareness of their inserted thoughts but those would lack phenomenality and subjectivity altogether"; "Thought insertion would thus fundamentally be a disorder of phenomenal consciousness"; and the account "is, first of all, compatibilist and thus conservative: it is consistent with the Cartesian principle" (all p. 306). He concedes: "It would, however, benefit from an empirical confirmation" (p. 308). Supporting evidence cited: congenitally deaf patients who report alien voices; Miller (1996), in which only 10 of 50 patients attributed auditory phenomenology to their voices; Moritz and Larøi (2008). None of these were checked here.
- **Closing thesis**: there are two "dimensions of the mind", the phenomenal and the spatial or functional, and "It has been a common ambition of philosophers of mind to try to reduce the phenomenal dimension to the spatial dimension" (p. 311). The case, he argues, shows the risk of that ambition.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 1 *compatible*, and closer to the Map than any other source here. He treats subjectivity as tied to phenomenality and not to access, and he warns against reducing the phenomenal to the functional. But he does not argue for non-physicality, and his later work (Billon 2023) locates mineness's explanation at the neurophysiological level.
- **Correction to the corpus**: Billon 2013 is no counterexample to "consciousness entails subjectivity". The title's question is answered *yes* for phenomenal consciousness. Henriksen and Parnas (2019, p. 10) list "Billon 2013" among the counterexample authors and never mention his non-phenomenality move ("unconscious", "Cartesian" and "reflexive" each return 0 relevant hits in their text), which is the probable source of the corpus misreading.

### Billon and Kriegel (2015), "Jaspers' Dilemma: The Psychopathological Challenge to Subjectivity Theories of Consciousness"
- In R. J. Gennaro (ed.), *Disturbed Consciousness*, MIT Press, pp. 29–54. DOI 10.7551/mitpress/9780262029346.003.0002 (Crossref: authors, title, pages, 2015).
- **Retrieval**: **[A]** (the OpenAlex chapter summary, written in the third person). It reads: "They argue that at least some subjectivity theories can accommodate these pathologies", covering thought insertion, delusions of alien control, somatoparaphrenia and depersonalisation.
- **Content via Billon 2023 [F]**: "With Uriah Kriegel, we have suggested elsewhere that thought insertion might be better explained by an extra sense of disownership (explained, itself, by an extra mark of alienness) rather than by a missing sense of ownership (explained itself by a missing mineness)".
- **Why it matters**: Zahavi and Kriegel (2016, p. 43) cite it as support for retained minimal ownership. Billon therefore co-authored a defence of subjectivity theories two years after the 2013 paper the corpus treats as the attack on them.

### Billon (2023), "What Is It Like to Lack Mineness?"
- In M. García-Carpintero and M. Guillot (eds), *Self-Experience*, OUP, pp. 314–342. DOI 10.1093/oso/9780198805397.003.0014 (resolved in `depersonalisation-2026-09-30`).
- **Retrieval**: **[F]** PhilArchive preprint via Wayback (`BILWII.pdf`), 12,686 words. OUP paragraph markers (C14P…) were stripped before grep.
- **On thought insertion**: he argues that the standard objections to a lacking sense of ownership fail for depersonalisation "even if they were cogent in the case of thought insertion, which I very much doubt". The objections he lists are Jaspers' rationality objection, the agency objection (Gallagher 2000; Graham and Stephens 2000), the endorsement objection (Fernández 2010), the ownership* objection (Campbell 2004; Bortolotti and Broome 2009), and his own extra-disownership account with Kriegel.
- **On scope**: generalism ("some authors have put forward a weaker claim, to the effect that at least in non-pathological cases, all conscious mental states come with mineness—call this claim generalism"); he names Gallagher (2004) and Zahavi (2008) as universalists. His conclusion: mineness is "a general, if not universal" feature.
- **Why it matters**: Billon's position moved between 2013 and 2023. In 2013 he saved the Cartesian principle by denying inserted thoughts phenomenality. In 2015 he and Kriegel proposed that thought insertion adds an alienness mark. By 2023 he holds a generalism on which conscious states in pathology can lack mineness, so the Billon who threatens a per-experience mineness claim is the 2016/2023 depersonalisation Billon, already treated in [[depersonalisation]], and not the 2013 thought-insertion Billon.

### Zahavi and Kriegel (2016), "For-Me-Ness: What It Is and What It Is Not"
- In D. O. Dahlstrom, A. Elpidorou and W. Hopp (eds), *Philosophy of Mind and Phenomenology*, Routledge, pp. 36–53. No Crossref record (Routledge chapter).
- **Retrieval**: **[F]** PhilArchive page proof via Wayback (`zahfwi.pdf`, 8,252 words). The proof's running page numbers (42–44) match the published range.
- **Key quotes**: "What-it-is-like-ness is properly speaking what-it-is-like-for-me-ness" (p. 36). On thought insertion, inserted thoughts "cannot lack minimal ownership altogether, since the afflicted subject is aware that it is she herself rather than somebody else who is experiencing them" (p. 43), citing Billon and Kriegel (forthcoming) and Zahavi 1999. ZK report that "Lane actually allows that there is an utterly trivial sense in which the first-person perspective is retained even in pathological cases" (p. 44). **[Correction 2026-10-06: the ZK sentence continues "but claims that this has no bearing on the issue of for-me-ness" (p. 44) — Lane grants a trivial first-person perspective, not for-me-ness. ### Henriksen, Parnas and Zahavi (2019), "Thought insertion and disturbed for-me-ness (minimal selfhood) in schizophrenia"
- *Consciousness and Cognition* 74: 102770. DOI 10.1016/j.concog.2019.102770. Open access (CC BY-NC-ND). Henriksen and Parnas (2019) list it as "in press".
- **Retrieval**: **[F]** ORA PDF, 8,300 words.
- **Thesis**: "Although thought insertion does not involve a lack of for-me-ness, we do argue that thought insertion involves a disturbed for-me-ness" (abstract). They reject two "misinterpretations": total absence, and an untouched core (the "artichoke"). "Fruit syrup might consequently be a better analogy than an artichoke" (p. 7). "In short, we maintain that for-me-ness (minimal selfhood) is neither lacking nor unaffected but disturbed in schizophrenia spectrum disorders" (p. 7). Their criterion for retained for-me-ness: the experiences "are never intersubjectively accessible"; "The epistemic asymmetry is preserved" (p. 7).
- **Methodological critique**: Mellor 1970 and Frith 1992 are "Two of the most used and recycled examples" (p. 3). Reports are polysemic and conceptualised after the fact, so "clinicians should not take patients' reports at face value" (p. 5), which is aimed directly at Billon's constraint. Thought insertion is decontextualised from the schizophrenic Gestalt: "Putting it bluntly, there are no TI patients" (p. 5). It is an "end phenomenon" (Klosterkötter 1988) of a prodromal self-alienation (p. 6).
- **Clinical material (first-hand, their own patients)**: "Sometimes, I get thoughts about death that are not my own thoughts. But I can't say that they are yours or someone else's." (p. 4). They also report patients who deny "insertion" but describe alien thoughts "simply being there" (Jansson and Nordgaard 2016, cited), and note that inserted thoughts rarely leave "no other thoughts".
- **Why it matters**: this is the closest primary-source statement of the Map's kind-claim ("attenuate, distort, or destabilise — never … fully dissolves"), and the Map does not cite it. Its for-me-ness criterion (the thought is not publicly accessible; it is "in his own mind") is the inner-location premise Billon §4.1 attacks, and the paper does not engage that argument ("inner location" 0, "Cartesian" 0, "reflexive" 1 incidental).

### Henriksen and Parnas (2019), "Experiences without for-me-ness? Reconsidering alleged counter examples from psychopathology and psychedelics"
- *Thaumàzein* 7: 6–20 (journal PDF header "THAUMÀZEIN 7, 2019"). DOI 10.13136/thau.v7i0.99 (DataCite; corrected 2026-10-06 from "no DOI").
- **Retrieval**: **[F]** journal PDF (font-encoding corruption in some lines; only clean spans quoted). Page numbers are read from the running footers.
- **Key quotes**: if the counterexample accounts are correct "they demonstrate the fallaciousness of the claim that phenomenal consciousness necessarily entails for-me-ness" (p. 10). Reply: "if the sense of ownership or forme-ness had effaced entirely, the experience of thought insertion could simply not articulate itself" (p. 12). Conclusion: they "refuted them all".

### Guillot (2017), "I Me Mine: on a Confusion Concerning the Subjective Character of Experience"
- *Review of Philosophy and Psychology* 8(1): 23–53. DOI 10.1007/s13164-016-0313-4 (Crossref; online 27 May 2016). Open access, PMC5362670.
- **Retrieval**: **[F]** Europe PMC XML (no page numbers in that rendering).
- **Key claims**: "there is no conceptual implication from for-me-ness to me-ness or mineness" (abstract). For-me-ness is the first-personal mode of givenness, me-ness is awareness of oneself in having the experience, and mineness is awareness of the experience as one's own. "I thus propose to describe thought insertion as a condition in which experience lacks mineness, but retains both for-me-ness and me-ness." "I thus propose to describe the depersonalisation syndrome as a condition in which experience lacks both mineness and me-ness, but retains for-me-ness."
- **Clinical report (second-hand)**: an Allison-Bolger 1999 patient said her "own thoughts might say the same thing" "but the feeling isn't the same", "the feeling is that it is somebody else's" **[2nd via Guillot; Allison-Bolger not traced]**. On Guillot's reading this report describes a felt absence of mineness.
- **Why it matters**: it supplies the vocabulary that resolves the optimistic review's Seam (c). The Map's kind-claim concerns for-me-ness (Guillot), its separability thesis concerns mineness (Guillot), and thought insertion and depersonalisation differ in *me-ness*, not in whether ownership or agency is lost. It also removes the premise of the corpus's "double dissociation", because on this reading both conditions sit on the mineness side.

## Clinical Phenomenology: What Is Reported

- **The canonical report is quoted unstably.** Mellor's patient (1970, p. 17) is transcribed four ways. Billon 2013 has "He treats my mind like a screen and flashes onto it like you flash a picture." HPZ 2019 and Guillot 2017 have "flashes his thoughts on to it". Gallagher 2007 has "I look out my window", "There are no thoughts there" and "flashes his thoughts onto it". The original was not consulted. A page should quote the HPZ/Guillot form, which two independent transcriptions agree on, mark it second-hand, and avoid the opening clause.
- **First-hand reports exist in HPZ 2019** (their own patients). One patient could not identify the source of thoughts "not my own". Another, asked about "inserted" thoughts, denied them until interview showed alien thoughts "simply being there". These cut against the "inserted from outside" image the philosophical literature recycles.
- **Reports of a missing feeling** (Allison-Bolger patient via Guillot: "the feeling isn't the same") and reports of an *added* or spatialised presentation (Billon via Parnas and Sass 2001: thoughts located "slightly behind my skull"; HPZ's patients who "see" thoughts as subtitles) both occur. The first suggests a missing element, the second an extra one, and the record supports both shapes.
- **Clinical framing**: reliably identified when narrowly defined but not specific to schizophrenia (Mullins and Spence 2003). Whether it is an experience or only a belief is open (ibid.). On HPZ's account it typically arises late, after a prodromal self-alienation, and is embedded in a Gestalt (Schneider's "a 'lowering' of the 'barrier' between self and the surrounding world, the loss of the very contours of the self" [2nd via HPZ, Schneider 1959, 134]).
- **Prevalence**: Billon gives "approximatively one fifth" of patients with schizophrenia, without a source. Not verified; do not use.

## Major Positions

### 1. Agency lost, ownership preserved (the corpus's reading)
- **Proponents**: Graham and Stephens 1994; Stephens and Graham 1994, 2000 (higher-order, narrative version); Gallagher 2000a/b, 2004 (first-order, pre-reflective version); Frith's comparator as the mechanism.
- **Core claim**: the patient experiences the thought in her stream (ownership, subjectivity) but not as self-generated (agency).
- **Best objection**: Billon's: OCD intrusions and unbidden thoughts lack felt agency yet are owned, so agency loss does not individuate thought insertion. Gallagher's reply (2007 [F]) is that in the unbidden case "I know at a second-order level that this unbidden thought was generated by me".
- **Relation to tenets**: if correct, it supports the corpus's architecture-tier separability of agency and ownership. It is the most contested of the five readings, not the settled datum the corpus presents.

### 2. Ownership lost (with agency loss downstream)
- **Proponents**: Metzinger 2003 (the "simple account", per Guillot); Lane 2012, 2015; Martin and Pacherie 2013 (context-integration); López-Silva 2017 (loss of mineness but not for-me-ness, per HPZ's summary; **[M]**, RPP 10(2): 319–335, DOI 10.1007/s13164-017-0366-z, online 31 October 2017); Guillot 2017 (mineness lost, for-me-ness and me-ness retained).
- **Relation to tenets**: Metzinger's and Lane's versions, read as claims about phenomenal consciousness, contradict any per-experience universality of mineness. Guillot's and López-Silva's versions explicitly retain for-me-ness, so they leave the Map's kind-claim untouched and contradict only a mineness-universal reading.

### 3. Billon 2013: subjectivity and phenomenality both lost; reflexive access retained
- **Core claim**: the patient has "good reflexive awareness" of a thought vehicle that is phenomenally unconscious. The thought feels located in him but is not lived. The Cartesian principle stands.
- **Relation to tenets**: the most anti-reductive reading on offer, because it separates phenomenality from access. It has an evidential cost for the Map. If an item can be in the stream, accessible and reportable without being phenomenally conscious, then first-person reports of "my thought" do not certify phenomenality. That weakens any argument that leans on reports at Layer 3 of [[phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence]].

### 4. Something added, not something missing
- **Proponents**: Billon and Kriegel 2015 (an extra mark of alienness); Zahavi and Kriegel 2016 (a "phenomenology of alienation", consistent with "nothing missing"); Ratcliffe and Wilkinson 2015 (thought contents presented quasi-perceptually as alien).
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. It removes the case from the subtraction evidence altogether, which hurts the corpus's double dissociation more than reading 2 does.

### 5. Universalist disturbance
- **Proponents**: Zahavi and Kriegel 2016; Henriksen and Parnas 2019; Henriksen, Parnas and Zahavi 2019.
- **Core claim**: for-me-ness (perspectival ownership, epistemic asymmetry) is never lost but is "disturbed"; what is lost or altered is personal ownership (Albahari 2006), the Jamesian "warmth and intimacy", or a higher layer.
- **Relation to tenets**: matches the Map's kind-claim almost exactly. The Map should cite HPZ 2019 at L148 rather than present the kind-claim as its own.

### Also present: endorsement accounts
Fernández (2010), *Mind & Language* 25(1): 66–88, DOI 10.1111/j.1468-0017.2009.01381.x **[M]**, and Bortolotti and Broome (2009), *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 8(2): 205–224, DOI 10.1007/s11097-008-9109-z (online 2008) **[M]**, hold that what fails is endorsement of or commitment to the thought. Content is known only via Billon 2023 and Guillot 2017. Billon notes that Fernández "does not speak of the sense of ownership in our sense".

## Key Debates

### Face value versus contextual interpretation
- **Sides**: Billon 2013 (the phenomenological constraint) and Metzinger and Lane (literal readings) against HPZ 2019 and Henriksen and Parnas 2019 (reports are polysemic, metaphorical and conceptualised after the fact; assess them in clinical context).
- **State**: unresolved. HPZ's critique is strongest against reliance on two recycled quotations. It cuts both ways, though: the universalist's own premise ("she is aware that it is she herself … who is experiencing them") is also a reading of reports.

### Does "in my mind" entail "mine"?
- **Sides**: Billon 2013 §4.1 (no; inner location is a functional availability to reflexive awareness) against ZK 2016, H&P 2019 and HPZ 2019 (yes; the thought is present "within her own stream of consciousness", so perspectival ownership is retained).
- **State**: the exchange has not been joined. All three universalist texts were checked for "inner location", "Cartesian", "reflexive" and "unconscious" and contain no engagement with §4.1. **This is the gap a Map page could honestly fill**, and it is where L148's "reported from somewhere" argument stands or falls.

### Which is primary, agency or ownership?
- **Sides**: Gallagher (agency) against Billon, Martin and Pacherie, and Guillot (ownership); Ratcliffe and Wilkinson regard the question as ill-posed.
- **State**: the agency reading is still "now the standard account" on Guillot's (2017) survey, but it no longer goes unchallenged. Gallagher's 2015 revisiting is unread here (see Gaps).

### What does the comparator explain?
- **Sides**: Feinberg 1978 and Frith 1987/1992 (failed self-monitoring); Synofzik et al. 2008 (cannot extend to ownership or to agency for thought); Frith 2012 ("still do not understand"); predictive-processing successors (Sterzer et al. 2016; Skipper et al. 2026) and new measurements of corollary discharge in inner speech (Whitford et al. 2017; Jack et al. 2019).
- **State**: the mechanism for agency in overt action is well supported. For thought, a forward-model signal for inner speech now exists, but no model accounts for the alienness of particular thoughts.

## The Physicalist Reading at Full Strength

A self-model theorist (Metzinger 2003; see [[self-model-theory-of-subjectivity]]) can grant every datum. On that view mineness is a representational property the phenomenal self-model attaches to contents. An inserted thought is a globally available content that the model fails to tag as self-generated or self-owned. Candidate mechanisms converge on inner speech as a motor act with a forward model: a measured efference copy (Whitford et al. 2017; Jack et al. 2019), aberrant precision and salience (Sterzer et al. 2016), failed context integration (Martin and Pacherie 2013), and spectrum-wide structural differences in inner-speech and monitoring regions (Skipper et al. 2026). The account predicts two things. The thin perspectival feature, the "weak first-person perspective" that Lane, following Blanke and Metzinger, treats as trivial (ZK 2016, p. 44), is architectural and present whenever the model runs. The thick mineness tag, by contrast, is content-specific and can drop out. That is the profile all five readings of the clinical record report.

The physicalist's real weakness is explanatory. The comparator's author says the symptom is not understood (Frith 2012), its critics say the comparator cannot reach ownership or thought (Synofzik et al. 2008), and the predictive-coding proposal starts from the admission that no account existed (Sterzer et al. 2016). But treating a missing mechanism as evidence for a non-physical one would be a gap argument, which [[evidential-status-discipline]] rules out. Billon's 2013 warning against "reduc[ing] the phenomenal dimension to the spatial dimension" is a warning against identifying subjectivity with access, not an argument for dualism. His 2023 chapter suggests mineness "can only be explained in purely neurophysiological" terms (quoted from the same preprint in [[depersonalisation]]).

**Tier: compatible.** The interface reading and the self-model reading both predict a retained thin feature and a mineness that can lapse for particular contents. Nothing in thought insertion tells them apart.

## What the Case Does to the Map's Kind-Claim

**The claim** ([[self-and-self-consciousness]] L148): "Clinical variation shows the asymmetry can attenuate, distort, or destabilise — never that it fully dissolves while phenomenal life continues … The falsifying scenario is specifiable but absent from the evidence: not attenuation but clean elimination — phenomenal life continuing without any residue of for-me-ness, no position from which the lack is registered." L74 states the per-experience form: for-me-ness is "necessary (no genuinely anonymous experiences exist)".

**Test 1: does any reading of thought insertion meet the falsifier?** No. The agency reading, the universalists and Guillot all retain first-personal givenness. Billon 2013 denies the inserted thought phenomenality, so it is no instance of "phenomenal life … without for-me-ness". Even Lane, the strongest counterexample author, concedes "an utterly trivial sense in which the first-person perspective is retained" (as ZK report him). **[Correction 2026-10-06: the ZK sentence continues "but claims that this has no bearing on the issue of for-me-ness" (p. 44) — Lane grants a trivial first-person perspective, not for-me-ness. The kind-claim's survival is on shared ground under a disputed name: every party grants the thought's epistemic asymmetry (accessible to the patient alone); the universalists call that for-me-ness, Lane denies it bears on for-me-ness. Matches the corrected [[thought-insertion]] article.]** **The kind-claim survives thought insertion.**

**Test 2: why it survives.** The disputed feature is mineness: Metzinger's "phenomenological mineness", Lane's "meish quality", Guillot's mineness, Albahari's personal ownership. The kind-claim's object is the thin first-personal givenness, which nobody in the debate denies. **[Correction 2026-10-06: Lane does deny that the trivial retained perspective bears on for-me-ness (ZK 2016 p. 44, continuation); "nobody denies" holds only for the epistemic asymmetry under whatever name — shared ground, disputed name.]** So the case confirms the kind-claim only in the sense that the claim has been scoped to uncontested ground. On the live question the Map is a *generalist*: [[mine-ness]] says mineness is separable and can fade while consciousness continues. That is coherent, but only if the Map keeps Guillot's two words apart. Its own text does not. [[mine-ness]] L48 equates mine-ness with Zahavi's for-me-ness. [[depersonalisation]]'s title speaks of the "Separability of For-Me-Ness". And on L74's per-experience form, Guillot's reading of an inserted thought (lacking mineness) would be an "anonymous" experience in the mine-ness page's vocabulary. Thought insertion is where the optimistic review's Seam (c), "for-me-ness" naming both a feature that can lapse and a position that cannot, stops being a matter of wording and produces a contradiction.

**Test 3: the falsifier.** "No position from which the lack is registered" excludes every case that a subject could report, and clinical evidence consists of reports. The scenario is therefore unreportable, not merely "absent from the evidence". Its absence is guaranteed by how the evidence is gathered, and so confirms nothing. This is the verdict that goes against the Map. The falsifier is real only as a conceptual specification. If it is to be empirical, the test must be third-person: a dissociation between independently validated markers of phenomenal presence and of first-personal access. Only the direction "phenomenal without first-personal access" would count, and Billon's direction, access without phenomenality, would not. No such marker pair exists, so the claim should be presented as a framework commitment at the coherence tier, as the "Distinction-Without-Empirical-Difference Check" in [[evidential-status-discipline]] requires for empirically inert distinctions. [[cotard-delusion]] L66 already concedes that passing "settles less than it appears to"; what it does not say is that the test cannot be failed.

**Test 4: the inference.** "Every reported case is reported from somewhere" (L148; [[self-opacity]] L145) runs the transcendental argument of H&P 2019 ("could simply not articulate itself"). Billon 2013 §4.1 has argued that what a report requires is good reflexive access ("apparent inner location"), which is a functional property, and not subjectivity. The physicalist will add that a self-model gives a functional "position" for free. Until the Map answers §4.1 its own way, the inference shows that a reporting *position* survives, not that *phenomenal* for-me-ness does. Those are different claims, and the gap between them is where the physicalist reading lives.

**Net verdict.** Thought insertion does not falsify the kind-claim. It shows that the claim (i) survives only on its thin scope, (ii) has a falsifier no clinical report could meet, and (iii) rests on an inference with an unanswered published objection. The honest repair keeps the claim and changes how it is presented. State it with Guillot's terms: for-me-ness is universal and mineness general. Cite HPZ 2019 as its primary statement. Relabel the falsifier as conceptual (coherence tier) and say why a report cannot meet it. Engage Billon §4.1 in one sentence. Because [[self-and-self-consciousness]] is over its gate, this repair belongs on the new thought-insertion page, with net-negative pointer edits in the hosts.

## Relation to Site Perspective

- **Tenet 1 (Dualism)**: *compatible*; neither suggestive nor discriminating (above).
- **Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction)**: nothing positive, and one caution. [[phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity]] L125 moves from the double dissociation to "genuine conscious selection" as what full agentic phenomenology requires. The premise is contested (readings 2–4), and on the agency reading itself felt agency is absent for self-generated thought. Felt agency is therefore an unreliable indicator of causal origin, which any Tenet 3 argument from agency phenomenology must allow for. The comparator family explains the loss without any phenomenal causation.
- **Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds)**: coherence only. "Not mine" presupposes a determinate subject; no evidence either way.
- **Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits)**: the self-model account explains the thin-retained, thick-lapsing profile with one structure. The Map should not answer it with parsimony claims in either direction. The disagreement is over what the data are (face value or context), not over which account is simpler.
- **Map-congenial material that must not be over-read**: Billon 2013's phenomenal/spatial distinction and his warning against reduction. Use it as a structural ally at the architecture tier, mark it "recruited-by" for anything stronger, and note that Billon 2023 points to neurophysiology.

## Coinage and Attribution Checks

- **Thought insertion as a first-rank symptom**: Kurt Schneider *proposed* first-rank symptoms in 1939 (Kendler and Mishara 2019) and included thought insertion (*Gedankeneingebung*). He did not discover or name the phenomenon. Texts from 1810 to 1932 describe it; Jaspers (1913) separated "thought insertion in a narrower sense" from "made thoughts" (Koehler and Witter 1976); and the early Heidelberg School (Gruhle, Mayer-Gross, Beringer) "first systematically described" the *Ichstörungen* that Schneider absorbed. Who introduced the English phrase "thought insertion" was **not traced** (see Gaps). Safe wording: "one of Schneider's first-rank symptoms".
- **The subjectivity/agency split for thought insertion**: Graham and Stephens (1994) and Stephens and Graham (1994), as "sense of subjectivity" versus "sense of agency". **The "sense of ownership / sense of agency" pair in its first-order form**: Gallagher 2000a/b, who says his distinction is "not the same distinction found in Graham and Stephens (1994)" and that Stephens and Graham 2000 make "a very similar one". Corpus pages that write "Gallagher's distinction" are right about the terminology they use. A page should not present Gallagher as the originator of the agency reading of thought insertion.
- **Efference copy applied to thought**: Feinberg 1978, before Frith.
- **"For-me-ness" / "mineness" as distinct notions**: the corpus credits for-me-ness to Zahavi, which is correct for the term. The three-way disambiguation is Guillot's (2017).

## Gaps in Research

- **Gallagher 2015** (*RPP* 6(4)): metadata only. This is his later statement on how agency and ownership relate in thought insertion, and it may revise the 2000/2004 position the corpus relies on. Read it before any page attributes a *current* view to Gallagher.
- **Gallagher 2000a (TICS)** full text was not read. Its definitions are known here only via Gallagher 2007 and search-snippet paraphrase, so do not quote it.
- **Billon and Kriegel 2015**: abstract only. Its content is known only through Billon 2023's one-sentence summary and Zahavi and Kriegel's citation.
- **Metzinger 2003, Lane 2012, Lane 2015**: claims known only as quoted by ZK 2016 and HPZ 2019. Lane's own text, including the exact "utterly trivial" concession, is unread.
- **López-Silva 2017, Fernández 2010, Bortolotti and Broome 2009, Coliva 2002, Stephens and Graham 1994/2000, Campbell 1999**: metadata only; content via secondary reports.
- **Primary clinical sources**: Mellor 1970, Frith 1992, Jaspers 1913, Schneider 1959 and Allison-Bolger 1999 were not consulted. Every patient quotation here is second-hand except HPZ's own patients. The four Mellor transcriptions disagree.
- **Who coined the English "thought insertion"**: not traced. Mellor 1970, the Present State Examination and Hamilton's 1959 translation of Schneider are candidates; none was checked.
- **Billon's empirical support** for non-phenomenal inserted thoughts (Miller 1996; Moritz and Larøi 2008; deaf voice-hearers) was not checked. Nor was whether anyone has tested his proposed "controlled interviews".
- **A direct reply to Billon §4.1**: none found in the universalist texts read. A targeted search ("inner location" thought insertion reply) was not run. Absence across three texts is not absence from the literature.
- **2020s philosophy**: the search turned up "Thought insertion without thought" (*RPP*, 2023, DOI 10.1007/s13164-023-00689-7) and "On the rationality of thought-insertion judgments" (*Philosophical Psychology*, 2023, DOI 10.1080/09515089.2023.2296580) as titles only. They are unread and unverified, and the expand step should check them.
- **Prevalence** figures: none verified.
