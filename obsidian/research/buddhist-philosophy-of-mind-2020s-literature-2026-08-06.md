# Research: Buddhist Philosophy of Mind — the 2020s Literature

**Date**: 2026-08-06
**Scope**: the citation-currency gap in the three-article Buddhist sub-cluster, flagged by `reviews/optimistic-2026-08-06-cross-cultural-cluster` ("Ideas for Later" and "Medium Priority")
**Method**: Crossref and OpenAlex metadata and abstract verification; SEP entries read at source for bylines and revision dates; one publisher-side review read in full; corpus-wide greps over `obsidian/`, `archive/` and `hugo/content/`. No claim below is sourced from unfinishablemap.org.

## Executive Summary

Four findings carry the weight, and **three of them run against the Map**.

**First, the strongest paragraph in the sub-cluster now has a named 2025 opponent, and it is the Map's own principal Buddhist source.** `buddhism-and-dualism` L119 argues a *principled* asymmetry: what licenses dropping the permanent self does not license dropping phenomenal experience. Mark Siderits — cited twelve times across the corpus, always from 2007 — published *Buddhist Physicalism? Non-self Metaphysics and Phenomenal Consciousness* (Oxford, 2025), whose chapter 8 runs the no-self machinery straight through to the conclusion that "**qualia are thereby shown to be artifacts of the deployment of a sort of useful fiction**." That is exactly the illusionism L119 declines, derived from exactly the premises L119 accepts. Siderits does not claim the position is true — Garfield's review confirms he advances only "the more modest claim that Buddhist materialism is a reasonable option to consider, hence the question mark in the book's title" — but the asymmetry the Map states as principled is now a contested claim with a book-length case against it.

**Second, the Map's "Madhyamaka resolution" is contested by the leading contemporary Madhyamaka philosopher, in the direction that costs Tenets 2 and 3.** `buddhism-and-dualism` L127 answers the Yogācāra relatum problem by holding that Madhyamaka keeps mind and matter "conventionally real and distinct". Jan Westerhoff's *The Non-Existence of the Real World* (Oxford, 2020) argues from Madhyamaka, in analytic terms, that **we should reject the external world and that there is no substantial internal self either** — chapter 1 kills the first relatum, chapter 2 the second. The Map declines "restricted dualism" at L129 precisely because it cannot pay for Tenets 2 and 3; Westerhoff's route arrives at the unpayable place anyway, from the school the Map nominated as its escape hatch.

**Third — and this corrects the task title — the material is not undiscovered. It is un-propagated.** Corpus greps show `topics/eastern-philosophy-consciousness`, linked from all three target articles' Further Reading, already cites Thompson 2020, Metzinger 2024, Yang et al. 2025 and Demirel et al. 2025. `voids/void-as-ground-of-meaning` already cites Westerhoff (2009, not 2020). *Buddhist Physicalism?* has been sitting flagged in **two research notes since February 2026** and was never consumed. The defect is a link-neighbourhood isolation, not a discovery failure, and that changes what the fix is.

**Fourth, the sub-cluster's only empirical citation is its most exposed.** `buddhism-and-dualism` L56 cites Fox et al. 2012 for a *perceptual* introspective-accuracy result. Baird et al. 2014 — a randomised trial with an active control, already cited elsewhere in the Map — found the enhancement "in a memory but not a perception domain", and a 2026 *Mindfulness* study found no metacognitive-monitoring advantage at all. The article's careful hedging survives; the citation's one-sidedness does not.

**Recommendation: refinement, not a new article.** See [Article or Refinement](#article-or-refinement).

## Finding 1 — Siderits 2025, *Buddhist Physicalism?* (Grade B; the finding that runs hardest against the Map)

**Bibliographic**: Siderits, Mark (2025). *Buddhist Physicalism? Non-self Metaphysics and Phenomenal Consciousness*. Oxford University Press, New York. ISBN 9780197799666. All nine chapters verified at Crossref (pp. 1–208) under book DOI prefix `10.1093/9780197799697`.

**Why this is the headline.** The Map cites Siderits twelve times, always *Buddhism as Philosophy* (2007). He is the sub-cluster's principal authority on what Buddhism claims. In 2025 he published a book-length exploration of whether Buddhism is compatible with physicalism.

**Chapter 8, "The Problem of Phenomenal Consciousness" (pp. 169–186), abstract read in full:**

> "This chapter addresses the 'hard problem' of phenomenal consciousness that confronts any form of physicalism. After describing the problem and discussing some common approaches that physicalists have offered in response, it attempts to develop a specifically Buddhist approach to its resolution. This turns on the key point that first-person application of theory of mind involves the development of inner speech, something that fosters a kind of mirroring of the subject's representational system. Since a mirror image is an illusory appearance, this reveals the subject-object dichotomy to be a kind of illusion. **Qualia are thereby shown to be artifacts of the deployment of a sort of useful fiction.**"

**Where this lands.** `buddhism-and-dualism` L119 holds that "classical eliminativism about folk-psychological selfhood is congenial; illusionism about phenomenal consciousness is not", because "phenomenal evidence has a different epistemic standing than theoretical posits about what underlies it". The optimistic review called this "the strongest paragraph in the wing" and praised its "*principled* asymmetry rather than an ad hoc one". Siderits chapter 8 is a Buddhist-internal argument that the asymmetry does not hold — the same deconstructive move, applied to the subject-object dichotomy, delivers qualia-as-useful-fiction. The Map's paragraph does not need to be withdrawn; it needs to stop presenting the asymmetry as uncontested.

**Chapter 9, "Assessing the Plausibility of a Buddhist Physicalism" (pp. 187–208), abstract read in full**, is the survey of objections, and one of them is precisely Finding 2:

> "Finally, a Mādhyamika would object that their global anti-realism has already established that consciousness can be no more than a useful fiction, so that a metaphysical realism like Buddhist physicalism is neither necessary nor philosophically defensible."

The same abstract lists three further concerns: that physicalism renders karma-rebirth implausible; that "physicalism's illusionism would seem to be incompatible with the central role of meditation in Buddhist practice, given the common understanding of meditation as a technique that facilitates the objective observation of inner mental states"; and that "physicalism strips the world of all meaning and value." The second bears on `buddhism-and-dualism` L56; the third on the whole of `buddhist-perspectives-on-meaning`.

**Chapter 5, "Meta-Cognition and the Reflexivity Thesis" (pp. 81–126), abstract read in full**, examines "the attempt by the New Yogācāra philosophers Dignāga and Dharmakīrti to show that occurrences of consciousness are the only ultimately real entities", argues "their approach has difficulty avoiding solipsism", and offers "an abductive-inferential account of meta-cognition" as an alternative to reflexive self-awareness. This runs *for* the Map: the Yogācāra position `buddhism-and-dualism` L83 names as "the Map's principal Buddhist rival" has an internal problem the article does not mention. Record it for symmetry.

**Chapter 6 (pp. 127–142)** raises "the 'too-many-subjects' objection to a physicalist account of consciousness, with a response built around the Buddhist theory of two truths" — the same two-truths machinery `buddhism-and-dualism` L115 uses for its "practical equivalence" reply, deployed for the opposite conclusion.

**Siderits' own verdict (Grade B, from Garfield's review read in full).** Garfield in *Tricycle*: "Siderits does not defend Buddhist materialism as a true doctrine, nor does he argue outright that materialism is fully consistent with Buddhism," advancing instead "the more modest claim that Buddhist materialism is a reasonable option to consider, hence the question mark in the book's title" — an argument Garfield grants "considerable force" even for readers who reject it. Siderits' own introduction (ch. 1, abstract read) says the book "is intended to do little more than start a conversation". **The hedge must travel with any use of this source.**

**Honest statement of where this leaves the Map.** Not refuted — Siderits withholds endorsement, and a compatibility thesis survives a rival compatibility thesis. But `buddhism-and-dualism` presents the property-dualist reading as the natural Buddhist position with the emptiness challenge as its one live rival. There is a second live rival, argued in 2025 by the article's own most-cited authority, and it is the physicalist one.

## Finding 2 — Westerhoff 2020: Madhyamaka Against Both Relata (Grade B)

**Bibliographic**: Westerhoff, Jan (2020). *The Non-Existence of the Real World*. Oxford University Press. DOI `10.1093/oso/9780198847915.001.0001`. ISBNs 9780198847915 / 9780191882548. Westerhoff edits the OUP *Buddhist Philosophy for Philosophers* series in which Siderits' *How Things Are* appears.

**Book abstract, read in full**, states the thesis and the four theses that compose it:

> "The main thesis defended is that we have good reason to deny the existence of such a world. … I argue specifically that: (a) we should reject the postulation of an external world behind our representations; (b) the internal world is not as epistemically transparent as is usually assumed, and there is no substantial self acting as central unifier of our mental lives; (c) there are good reasons for adopting an anti-foundational account of ontological dependence; and (d) ontology, and philosophy more generally, must not be conceived of as providing an ultimately true theory of the world."

**Chapter 1 (pp. 1–80) abstract**: argues "that we do not need to postulate an external world in order to justify the illusion–reality distinction, or to explain the coherence, intersubjectivity, and efficacy of our perception", concluding that "a kind of brain-based representationalism (referred to as irrealism) works best as a theory of perception but that this, somewhat surprisingly, also undermines the justification of a mind-independent world of material objects."

**Chapter 2 (pp. 81–151) abstract**: "our introspective capacities do not give us any more of a secure grasp of an internal world than our five senses perceiving the external world."

**Where this lands.** `buddhism-and-dualism` "Yogācāra and the External World" (L121–L129) poses the relatum problem — Bidirectional Interaction needs two relata, Yogācāra dissolves one — and offers the **Madhyamaka resolution** at L127: Madhyamaka "denies inherent existence to both mind and matter without reducing either to the other: both are empty of independent essence but conventionally real and distinct". The Map then declines "restricted dualism" at L129 because it "cannot pay for Tenets 2 and 3, which need an independent physical world with indeterminate outcomes to bias".

Westerhoff argues from Madhyamaka that there is no such independent physical world. The reply the Map nominated as its way of keeping both relata is, in its leading contemporary exponent's hands, a route to keeping neither. Note also that his thesis (b) — no substantial unifying self, introspection not transparent — cuts at the minimal-self material in Finding 4, and thesis (d) bears on `buddhist-perspectives-on-meaning`'s grounding-project defence at L139.

**A qualification the note must carry.** Westerhoff presents the argument "in a purely Western philosophical framework" (per the publisher's description), drawing on Madhyamaka rather than exegeting it. He is not claiming Nāgārjuna held global irrealism, and the Map should not report him as saying so. What he establishes is that the *contemporary analytic development* of Madhyamaka reaches irrealism about the external world — which is what makes L127's "conventionally real and distinct" a contested reading rather than a settled one.

**Symmetry note, in the Map's favour.** Siderits chapter 9 puts the Mādhyamika global-anti-realist objection on the *physicalist's* head too. The Map is not uniquely exposed here; the Madhyamaka position is corrosive of metaphysical realisms generally, which is roughly what `buddhism-and-dualism` L109 already says. What the article lacks is the observation that this corrosion reaches its own Madhyamaka escape hatch.

## Finding 4 — The Minimal-Self Paragraph Is Six Years Out of Date (Grade B/C)

`buddhism-and-dualism` L103 reads:

> "**Minimal self**: Phenomenological analysis (Zahavi, Gallagher) identifies a 'minimal self'—the pre-reflective 'for-me-ness' present in all experience… Buddhism might accept it conventionally while denying it ultimate substantial reality, and the Map can accept that compromise: first-personal structure doesn't require permanence."

Three 2020s developments bear on it, and the paragraph's picture of a settled compromise does not survive any of them.

**(a) Garfield 2022 (Grade C).** *Losing Ourselves: Learning to Live without a Self*, Princeton University Press, 2022, DOI `10.1515/9780691220291` (also JSTOR `10.2307/j.ctv23r3gh1`). Argues, from Candrakīrti and Hume, that we are not selves but *persons*, and that belief in the self undermines ethics. Genuinely absent from the corpus — see the homonym correction above. **Content not read this run**; the Map should cite it as existing and orienting, not for a specific argument.

**(b) Zahavi 2024 (Grade B, abstract read in full).** "Being You — Or Not: A Challenge for Garfield and Seth", *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 31(5):206–220, DOI `10.53765/20512201.31.5.206`. Abstract:

> "Whereas Garfield is a Buddhist scholar who advocates a no-self view, Seth is a neuroscientist who defends a radical form of representationalism. But is it really possible to speak meaningfully of a *you* (and of a *we*) if one denies the existence of the self…? In the following, I will criticize both accounts. I will argue that they both, in different ways, are unable to deal with a real other, and, drawing on the work of Husserl, I will argue that a proper understanding of the *you* doesn't merely require accepting the reality of the self, but also the existence of a shared lifeworld."

Zahavi — cited in the Map from 2005 — has an explicit 2024 objection to the Buddhist no-self position that L103 treats as compatible with his minimal self. **This also lands on `cross-cultural-phenomenology-of-agency` L89**, which builds its intersubjectivity argument on Husserlian appresentation without knowing that the Husserlian who owns that argument deploys it *against* the Buddhist no-self the same article relies on at L73.

**(c) Gallagher et al. 2023 (Grade B, abstract read in full).** Gallagher, S., Raffone, A., Berkovich-Ohana, A., Barendregt, H. P., Bauer, P. R., Brown, K. W. et al., "The Self-Pattern and Buddhist Psychology", *Mindfulness* 15(4):795–803, DOI `10.1007/s12671-023-02118-3`. The abstract relates Buddhist insights "to the notion of the self-pattern developed in the pattern theory of self", emphasising "the dynamic, temporal and enactive characteristics of the self-pattern, **consistent with the core Buddhist notion of non-self**", while noting "there is no one-to-one mapping of Buddhist psychological concepts onto the pattern theory of self".

The Map pairs "Zahavi, Gallagher" as joint proponents of a minimal self. By 2023 they are on visibly different trajectories: Zahavi defending the reality of the self against Buddhist no-self, Gallagher developing a pattern theory he presents as *consistent* with non-self. `self-pattern`, `pattern theory of self` and the JCS and *Mindfulness* DOIs all return **zero** hits across the corpus.

## Finding 5 — The Sub-Cluster's Only Empirical Citation Is One-Sided (Grade B/C)

`buddhism-and-dualism` L56: "experienced meditators give more accurate introspective reports of tactile sensitivity during body-scanning meditation… than novices (Fox et al. 2012)". The article hedges well — "narrower than a claim that meditators introspect consciousness as such more accurately" — but the evidential picture is incomplete in a way the hedge does not cover.

- **Fox, K. C. R., Zakarauskas, P., Dixon, M., Ellamil, M., Thompson, E. & Christoff, K. (2012)**, *PLoS ONE* 7(9):e45370, DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0045370` — metadata confirmed at Crossref, six authors as listed. **Note that Evan Thompson is a co-author**, which is worth recording given Finding 7. The design is cross-sectional: experts versus novices, correlational.
- **Baird, B., Mrazek, M. D., Phillips, D. T. & Schooler, J. W. (2014)**, "Domain-specific enhancement of metacognitive ability following meditation training", *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 143(5):1972–1979. A two-week randomised program with an **active control**, which "significantly enhanced introspective accuracy in a memory but not a perception domain." Fox's result is in the perception domain.
- **Neuenschwander, R., Bryce, D. & Eberhart, J. (2026)**, "Mindfulness and Metacognitive Monitoring Accuracy in Young Meditation Practitioners Compared to Non-Practitioners", *Mindfulness*, DOI `10.1007/s12671-026-02938-z`, published 13 July 2026, n = 124 (61 practitioners). Abstract read in part: motivated by the claim that "metacognition [is] one of the underlying cognitive mechanisms of mindfulness", noting "only few studies have examined metacognition objectively with laboratory tasks". Reported null on monitoring accuracy and on practice-feature correlations.

**What this does and does not establish.** It does **not** show Fox 2012 failed to replicate — no direct replication was found, and two designs measuring different things can both be right. What it establishes is that the *controlled* test in the same domain came out null, and that the Map already carries the Baird citation elsewhere while flattening its central qualifier: `phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence` L166 says only "Baird et al. (2014) found meditation training enhanced metacognitive accuracy", dropping "in a memory but not a perception domain". **That flattening is a defect in its own right and sits in a different article from the one this task targets** — a live instance of the fix-by-file hazard.

## Finding 7 — The Soteriological/Metaphysical Distinction Has a Live 2020s Dialogue (Grade B/C)

Two Map articles turn on the distinction between Buddhism's soteriological aims and metaphysical claims — `buddhism-and-dualism` L158 ("Buddhist urgency about *this* life is soteriological, not metaphysical indexicality") and `buddhist-perspectives-on-meaning` L105 ("Liberation vs. explanation"). > "The exploration, which proceeds in critical dialogue with suggestions made by Donald Lopez Jr. and Evan Thompson, covers the topics of the role of mindfulness as a means for progress to awakening, the path to and the realization of awakening, **the implications of the doctrines of not self and of the four noble truths**, and the centrality of meditation in early Buddhism. The Map cites his encyclopedia entry and not his journal-length engagement with the exact question two of its articles are about. **His position is unverified — do not characterise it from the title.**

## Article or Refinement

**Refinement, unambiguously.** Five reasons, the first three transferred from the African note and the last two specific to this cluster.

1. **Cap arithmetic, re-measured with the gating function on 2026-08-06**: `topics/` **319/320** (1 slot), `concepts/` **315/320** (5 slots), `voids/` **99/100** (1 slot). Spending the last `topics/` slot on a fourth Buddhist article would be poor use of scarce headroom. *(Note the optimistic review's figures — topics 320/320, concepts 316/320 — are already stale; use `tools.evolution.state.count_section_files`.)*
2. **This is a sourcing gap, not a subject gap.** Every finding attaches to an existing paragraph in an existing article. Nothing here lacks a home.
3. **A new article would worsen the diagnosed defect.** The convergent review finding was over-aggregation and a tenet-section support register. A fourth page assembling more Buddhist material into more compatibility is the wrong direction.
4. **Finding 3 rules it out specifically.** The sub-cluster's problem is that it does not know what its neighbours already cite. A new page starting from the same isolation would inherit it. The corrective is *inbound links*, which the optimistic review's cross-linking table independently asks for.
5. **`buddhism-and-dualism` already carries open review work.** Per the same-file pileup hazard, adding an article compounds rather than relieves it.

**Suggested sequencing for the downstream `refine-draft` tasks**, most load-bearing first:

1. **Record the Siderits 2025 rival at L119** — one or two sentences saying that a Buddhist route to illusionism about qualia has been argued at book length in 2025 by the article's own principal source, and that the asymmetry is therefore contested rather than principled-and-settled. Carry the "reasonable option to consider" hedge. This is the highest-value change and it is length-neutral.
2. **Qualify the Madhyamaka resolution at L127** — note that the contemporary analytic development of Madhyamaka (Westerhoff 2020) argues *against* the external world the resolution preserves, so the resolution is available rather than established. Length-neutral; it strengthens the article, because the Map's honesty about bedrock disagreement is what the reviewers praised.
3. **Update L103** — the Zahavi/Gallagher pairing has come apart, and L103 currently presents a compromise as if both parties still held it. Add Zahavi 2024 and Gallagher et al. 2023; name Garfield 2022 as the no-self position being argued against.
4. **Balance L56** — add Baird 2014's domain-specificity beside Fox 2012. Two clauses. **And mint a separate small task for `phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence` L166**, which drops the same qualifier in a different article: the locus list here is a sample, not the population.
5. **Source L158** — the soteriological/metaphysical sentence is the sub-cluster's most reused line and has no citation. Anālayo 2021 and Thompson 2020 between them give it a two-sided evidential base.
6. **Cross-link before adding references** — `buddhism-and-dualism` → `eastern-philosophy-consciousness` (which already holds Thompson 2020, Metzinger 2024, Yang 2025), and `buddhist-perspectives-on-meaning` → `void-as-ground-of-meaning` and `cross-traditional-convergence-on-consciousness-irreducibility`. This is the actual repair for Finding 3.
7. **Date the SEP references**, noting that *Ethics in Indian Buddhism* was substantively revised in 2024; consider replacing the *Encyclopedia of Buddhism* entry.

Steps 1–3 are the ones that matter philosophically. Steps 4–7 are hygiene, and step 6 is the one that stops the gap recurring.
