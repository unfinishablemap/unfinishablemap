## Executive Summary

**Mad-Dog Everettianism** (Carroll & Singh 2019) is the most austere ontology on offer for quantum mechanics: the world *is* a vector in Hilbert space plus a Hamiltonian, with no preferred algebra of observables, no configuration space, and no fundamental spacetime — space, fields and particles all emergent. Carroll (2022) restates the thesis solo and concedes that "Hilbert Space Fundamentalism" (HSF) would be an equally accurate name. **The reviewer's dating is wrong and the corpus should not inherit it**: the anchor paper is **2019**, not 2021 (the 2021 Carroll & Singh paper is the separate *quantum mereology* paper). The subject is genuinely absent from the corpus and genuinely worth an article.

**The literature is much richer than a bare "Carroll and Singh say X" would suggest, and it is live as of March 2026.** There is a sustained no-go programme against HSF (Stoica 2021–2026, one leg peer-reviewed in *Advances in Theoretical and Mathematical Physics*), a 2025 counterexample claiming the no-go is only valid in a weaker form (Soulas, Franzmann & Di Biagio), and a 2026 reply (Stoica). The dispute is about whether a *unique* tensor-product structure — and therefore a unique subsystem decomposition, preferred basis, and branch structure — can emerge from the Hamiltonian and state alone. That is a technical, adjudicable question with direct bearing on Tenet 4, and it is unresolved.

**On the Tenet-4 question the driver posed: it cuts both ways, and the asymmetry is the interesting part.** Against HSF the Map's *existing* indexical objection gets *weaker*, because HSF hands the Everettian the deflationary route the tenets page already concedes is available — on HSF branches are emergent bookkeeping, so "why am *I* this branch?" asks about a level HSF never claimed was fundamental. But HSF simultaneously opens a *different and structurally novel* line the Map does not currently run: if Stoica is right, HSF cannot supply a determinate branch structure for branch-relative identity to be relative *to*, and that pressure is **internal** — it uses only resources HSF accepts. The corpus at `qm-interpretations-beyond-many-worlds` and register entry P-I5 both state flatly that the Map "does not claim to show branch-relative identity false on the Everettian's own resources." Against HSF specifically there may be an exception. It must be scoped narrowly: it is a no-go about *uniqueness of emergent structure*, not a refutation of branch-relative identity; it binds HSF only, leaving Wallace-style Everettians who take spacetime and fields as given untouched; and it is contested by a live 2025 counterexample.

**Scope recommendation: a standalone `topics/` article.** All three articles in the existing MWI cluster are at or past their length ceilings, and there is ample cap headroom. Details in "Consumption Verdict" below.

**The absent thing is the ontological thesis**, and the six keys the driver checked all still return zero across the content sections. **A closer neighbour than the MWI cluster, missed at harvest.** [[wavefunction-realism-vs-primitive-ontology]] frames the fundamental-ontology question as a **two-way** split: wavefunction realism (`Ψ` as a field on `3N`-dimensional configuration space — Albert, Ney, Loewer, North) versus primitive ontology (matter in 3D/4D, `Ψ` nomological — Dürr, Goldstein, Zanghì, Allori, Tumulka). ## The Thesis, in the Authors' Own Words

All quotations **[grep-verified]** against the downloaded `pdftotext` output of arXiv:1801.08132v1 (Carroll & Singh) and arXiv:2103.09780v1 (Carroll).

**The minimal ontology.** From the abstract: "the most basic description of the world is given by the spectrum of the Hamiltonian (a list of energy eigenvalues) and the components of some particular vector in Hilbert space", and "Everything else — including space and fields propagating on it — is emergent from these minimal elements." The name is self-consciously immodest: "We call this approach 'Mad-Dog Everettianism,' to emphasize that it is as far as we can imagine taking the program of stripping down quantum mechanics to its most pure, minimal elements."

**Carroll's solo restatement** (2022, ch. 15 of Allori's volume): "the fundamental ontology of the world is completely and exactly represented by a vector in an abstract Hilbert space, evolving in time according to unitary Schrödinger dynamics. Everything else, from particles and fields to space itself, is rightly thought of as emergent from that austere set of ingredients. This approach has been called 'Mad-Dog Everettianism' (Carroll & Singh, 2019) although 'Hilbert Space Fundamentalism' would be equally accurate." Note that **Carroll himself supplies the "2019" date** and the alternative name. Since the only intrinsic data attached to a Hilbert space is its dimension, the theory reduces to a list of numbers: "A specification of a quantum theory consists entirely, therefore, of this list of real numbers, the energy eigenspectrum" *(reassembled across a `pdftotext` page-number interpolation; both halves matched separately)*.

**The programme for recovering space.** Locality is claimed to be spectrum-fixed: "It turns out that a generic Hamiltonian will not be local with respect to any decomposition, and for the special Hamiltonians that can be written in a local form, the decomposition in which that works is essentially unique [10]" — reference [10] being **Cotler, J. S., Penington, G. R. & Ranard, D. H. (2019), "Locality from the Spectrum," *Communications in Mathematical Physics* **368**(3), 1267–1296, DOI `10.1007/s00220-019-03376-w`** (verified at Crossref; journal_ref confirmed in the arXiv record for 1702.06142). Geometry is then recovered from entanglement entropy / mutual information between factors. The uniqueness clause carries the whole load-bearing weight of the emergence programme, and is exactly what the critics attack.

**What the authors concede.** Two concessions are quotable and unusually candid. On the split into system/apparatus/environment: "Two things do get assumed: an initially unentangled state, and a particular factorization into object/apparatus/environment", and "there exists an infinite unitary freedom in the choice of our system and environment"; "This question remains murky at the present time, but substantial progress is being made." Carroll (2022) closes: "This has been an overly concise discussion of an ambitious research program (and one that may ultimately fail)."

**The passage with the most direct bearing on Tenet 4**, and it is Carroll's own: "Clearly, 'observers' are not represented directly by a vector in Hilbert space, nor is the world that they observe." Also: "What can be observed in practice will depend on how we split further split Hilbert space into systems and observers" *(the doubled "split further split" is verbatim in arXiv:2103.09780v1; quote it with the slip or paraphrase, do not silently repair it)*.

## The No-Go Programme Against HSF — and the Live 2025–26 Exchange

This is the strand the corpus most needs, and it is where the philosophical action is.

**Stoica, O. C. (2022).** "3D-space and the preferred basis cannot uniquely emerge from the quantum structure." *Advances in Theoretical and Mathematical Physics* **26**(10), 3895–3962. DOI `10.4310/ATMP.2022.v26.n10.a12`. Preprint arXiv:2102.08620 (v1 Feb 2021, v9 Mar 2024). **Peer-reviewed** — verified at Crossref (International Press of Boston, vol 26 iss 10, pp. 3895–3962) and in the arXiv record's `journal_ref`. From the abstract **[grep-verified]**: "whenever such a structure emerges from the Hamiltonian and the state vector alone, if it is physically relevant, it is not unique", and "HSF leads to strange effects like 'passive' travel in time and in alternative realities, realized simply by passive transformations of the Hilbert space." The abstract names its target explicitly — "in particular the version of Everett's Interpretation coined by Carroll and Singh 'Mad-dog Everettianism'".

Crucially, Stoica does **not** dispute Cotler et al.; he argues their result cannot do the job Carroll & Singh need it to do. "We do not contest their results, but we will see that, no matter how restrictive is the algorithm to obtain a local TPS from the spectrum of the Hamiltonian, it violates one of the Conditions 1 and 2, so either it is not unique, or it is not physically relevant" **[grep-verified]**. The targeted result is **Theorem 6**: "The emergent 3D-space of Carroll and Singh [1, 29] either is physically irrelevant, or it is not unique (not even essentially unique)" **[grep-verified]**, with the surrounding verdict "Their results are interesting, but unfortunately neither the resulting 3D-space nor any other preferred structure in their program can emerge in a unique or essentially unique way from the Hamiltonian or its spectrum" **[grep-verified]**. The shape of the argument is a **trilemma/dilemma on invariance**: a structure built only from `H` and `|ψ⟩` is invariant under the theory's symmetries, and anything genuinely invariant cannot distinguish physically distinct situations — so it is either non-unique or physically idle.

**Two further Stoica papers are arXiv-only and must be flagged as not peer-reviewed.** (a) "Refutation of Hilbert Space Fundamentalism," arXiv:2103.15104 (v1 Mar 2021, v3 Jul 2022), 2 pages — a short popular account of the ATMP proof; **no journal reference in the arXiv record**. (b) "The prince and the pauper. A quantum paradox of Hilbert-space fundamentalism," arXiv:2310.15090 (v1 Oct 2023, v3 Feb 2025) — argues that "even if a tensor product structure is assumed along with the unit vector and the Hamiltonian, concrete physically distinct worlds can be described by the same structures"; **no journal reference**. The peer-reviewed citation to lean on is the ATMP paper; these two are useful for framing and should be cited as preprints.

**The exchange is open as of March 2026.** Both of the following are **arXiv-only preprints with no journal reference**, and the corpus should say so:

- **Soulas, A., Franzmann, G. & Di Biagio, A. (2025).** "On the emergence of preferred structures in quantum theory." arXiv:2512.07468 (v1 8 Dec 2025, v2 22 Dec 2025), 14 pages. From the abstract: the paper "reviews, clarifies, and critically examines two apparently conflicting theorems by Cotler et al. and Stoica. We resolve the tension, show how the former has been widely misinterpreted and why the latter is correct only in some weaker version," concluding that "a Hamiltonian and a state are enough structure to uniquely select a preferred tensor product structure." It also uses "Hilbert space fundamentalism" as the settled name for the position.
- **Stoica, O. C. (2026).** "Comment on 'On the emergence of preferred structures in quantum theory' by Soulas, Franzmann, and Di Biagio." arXiv:2603.07674 (8 Mar 2026). Replies that "Soulas et al.'s construction of a unique TPS can't be both invariant and compatible with physical observations, so it can't be a counterexample," and that "since Soulas et al.'s method to construct preferred structures consists of choosing their invariants, by the same logic one could claim as well that the masses of elementary particles emerge uniquely just by fixing their values by hand."

**One adjacent paper is commonly mis-attributed and the corpus must not inherit the error.** Searching "quantum mereology and subsystems from the spectrum" surfaces DOI `10.1007/s10701-024-00813-2` in contexts that read as though it were Stoica's. It is not. Crossref gives the authors as **Nicolas Loizeau and Dries Sels**, *Foundations of Physics* **55**(1), article 3 (2024-12-21), arXiv:2409.01391. It is a constructive contribution to quantum mereology, not a critique of HSF, and attributing it to Stoica would put a critic's name on a non-critical paper.

## The Tenet Interface — the Part Requiring Care

### Tenet 4: the objection gets weaker and a new line opens

The Map's Tenet 4 rejects MWI on the **indexical** objection, and the tenets page already books the objection's precondition honestly: it "is meaningful only on a *non-deflationary* conception of personal identity — an 'I' whose branch-assignment is a real further fact, not a convention". [[qm-interpretations-beyond-many-worlds]] states the calibration flatly: "The Map cannot show, using only resources MWI accepts, that branch-relative indexicality is false." Register entry **P-I5** ([[individuation-and-subjecthood]]) says the same in meta form: all three anti-MWI arguments bear on *branch-egalitarian* Everettianism, but "Even there the verdict is a framework boundary, not an in-framework refutation."

**Reading A — austerity weakens the Map's existing objection.** HSF is the strongest available version of the deflationary reply. If branches, systems, observers and space are all *emergent* descriptions rather than fundamental furniture, then "why am *I* this branch?" is a question about an emergent bookkeeping level that HSF never claimed was fundamental — and Carroll says so in terms: "'observers' are not represented directly by a vector in Hilbert space, nor is the world that they observe." The Map's own tenets page concedes a deflationary route stays open at the framework boundary. HSF is that route with a physics programme attached.

**Reading B — austerity opens a different, sharper line.** The Map's objection needs a determinate branch for the indexical to fail to be fixed by. If Stoica's Theorem 6 stands, HSF cannot supply one: the emergent structure is either non-unique or physically idle, so there is no determinate fact about *which* branch structure obtains, and *a fortiori* none about which branch the subject occupies. This is a pressure the Map can press **from inside**, using only what HSF grants, which is precisely what the corpus says it cannot do against Wallace.

**My read: it cuts both ways, and Reading B is the one worth developing — under four hard constraints, all of which an article must state.**

1. **It is not a refutation of branch-relative identity.** Stoica's result is about uniqueness of *emergent structure*. Branch-relative identity could be perfectly coherent in a framework that takes the branch structure as given; the no-go says HSF is not such a framework. Writing it up as "the indexical objection now succeeds" would be the over-claim the corpus's own review history repeatedly catches.
2. **It binds HSF only.** Wallace-style Everettians who help themselves to spacetime, fields and a preferred algebra are untouched. **P-I5 therefore needs no re-scoping** — HSF is a branch-egalitarian variant and all three arguments bear on it, as the entry already says — but the *strength* clause acquires a variant-specific exception candidate, and that is a register-relevant refinement rather than a correction.
3. **It is contested.** Soulas et al. (2025) claim the no-go holds only in a weaker form and that uniqueness *is* achievable; Stoica (2026) replies. Both are preprints. The honest verdict is "live technical dispute, unresolved", which is also the corpus's habitual calibration for the Born-rule derivations.
4. **It is a double-edged result.** Non-uniqueness of emergent structure is embarrassing for *any* programme that recovers classicality from decoherence in a spectrum-fixed factorization — and the Map's own [[post-decoherence-selection-programme]] uses environmental einselection to fix the menu consciousness selects among. The apex already books the parallel discount for einselection and for envariance. An article pressing Stoica against HSF must check whether the same non-uniqueness bites the Map's own factorization assumption, and say so. This is the highest-risk item in the whole subject and the one most likely to be missed.

### Tenet 1: the strongest form of structure-is-all, but the hook is thinner than the task note implies

The harvested task note frames HSF as "the strongest form of the structure-is-all-there-is claim Tenet 1 denies". That is fair as far as it goes, but **neither Carroll & Singh (2019) nor Carroll (2022) discusses consciousness at all**. Measured: the word `consciousness` occurs **exactly once in each text**, and in both cases it is inside the *title of Tegmark's paper in the reference list* — zero occurrences in either body. An article should therefore construct the Tenet-1 confrontation rather than report it, and should not imply Carroll has taken a position on phenomenal consciousness in these texts. The honest framing: HSF is a maximally austere physicalist base, so it makes the explanatory-gap question maximally sharp (what in a list of energy eigenvalues could be a phenomenal state?), but the confrontation is the Map's to stage. The nearest real hook is that Carroll & Singh cite **Tegmark, M. (2015), "Consciousness as a state of matter," *Chaos, Solitons & Fractals* **76**, 238–270, DOI `10.1016/j.chaos.2015.03.014`** (verified at Crossref this run) as reference [16] for the system/environment factorization criterion — a consciousness paper doing real work in the physics of the Map's opponent, and absent from the corpus entirely.
