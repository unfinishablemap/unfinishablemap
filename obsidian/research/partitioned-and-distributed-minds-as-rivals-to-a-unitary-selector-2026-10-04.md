## Executive Summary

Five rival families explain akrasia, failed resolutions and self-opacity without one personal selector. Davidson's interpretive **partition** places the irrational step between "quasi-independent structures" and denies that the parts are little agents. Ainslie's **intertemporal bargaining** treats the person as a population of reward-seeking interests whose hyperbolic discounting makes preferences reverse. On his account the "will" is recursive self-prediction and owes nothing to an organ. **Planner–doer** and **hot/cool** models split the person in two, while **multiple-controller** models add an explicit computational arbitration step. Dual-process typology is itself contested, but its critics reject the two-type taxonomy without arguing for a unitary controller. Korsgaard supplies a *non-naturalist* rival framing, in which unity of agency is a practical requirement that "is not based on a metaphysical theory". No discriminating prediction favours the Map's selector. The one discriminating result in the literature tells against an organ-style will. And a unitary agent-cause makes akrasia harder to explain, because it has no other structure in which to locate Davidson's surd.

## Key Sources

### Davidson, "Paradoxes of Irrationality" (1982) [S]
- Original publication: Wollheim & Hopkins (eds), *Philosophical Essays on Freud*, CUP 1982, pp. 289–305, doi 10.1017/CBO9780511554636.018 (Crossref; the reprint's provenance note gives the same pages). Reprint: *Problems of Rationality* (OUP 2004), pp. 169–187 per the SEP; Crossref gives 169–188. The pages below are the reprint's.
- The puzzle: "The explanation must, it is evident, contain some feature that goes beyond the Plato Principle; otherwise the action is perfectly rational" (p. 177).
- The diagnosis: irrationality is "characterized by the fact that there is a mental cause that is not a reason" (p. 179).
- The partition: "quasi-independent structures that interact in ways the Plato Principle cannot accept or explain" (p. 181). "To constitute a structure of the required sort, a part of the mind must show a larger degree of consistency or rationality than is attributed to the whole" (p. 181). "The picture I want is of overlapping territories" (p. 181). "What is called for is organized elements, within each of which there is a fair degree of consistency, and where one element can operate on another in the modality of non-rational causality" (p. 182).
- Akrasia: "semi-autonomous departments of the mind, one that finds a certain course of action to be, all things considered, best, and another that prompts another course of action" (p. 181).
- **Why the parts are not homunculi**: "The breakdown of reason relations defines the boundary of a subdivision." "The idea of a quasi-autonomous division is not one that demands a little agent in the division; again, the operative concepts are those of cause and reason" (p. 185). Davidson describes the homuncular picture only to set it aside, as the picture that makes partition look unintelligible: each part would "become a little woman, man, or child", and the mind "a battlefield where opposed forces contend, deceive one another, conceal information, devise strategies" (p. 171).
- SEP "Weakness of Will" n. 14 [2nd, raw text]: the later Davidson's idea "that we can make the most sense of akrasia when it involves distinct sub-systems within the mind operating to some degree autonomously".
- **Tenet alignment**: Davidson is a token physicalist, and his partition is interpretive rather than a further entity. It rivals the selector's unity and touches no tenet directly.

### Davidson, "How Is Weakness of the Will Possible?" (1970): the "surd" [S]
"What is special in incontinence is that the actor cannot understand himself: he recognizes, in his own intentional behaviour, something essentially surd" (*Essays on Actions and Events*, 1980, p. 42). Note 25 on the same page reads: "Of course he has a reason for doing a; what he lacks is a reason for not letting his better reason for not doing a prevail." **Attribution guard**: "surd" comes from the 1970 essay, not from the 1982 one.

### Davidson, "Deception and Division" (1986); Pears (1984) [2nd]
"Deception and Division" appeared in Elster (ed.), *The Multiple Self*, CUP 1986, pp. 79–92, and was reprinted in *Problems of Rationality* at pp. 199–212 (SEP; Crossref confirms the reprint). The SEP "Self-Deception" entry calls Davidson's division "relatively modest", one "where there need only be a boundary between conflicting attitudes and intentions". It contrasts Pears (*Motivated Irrationality*, OUP 1984), whose "deceiving part still constitutes a separate center of agency". The same entry reports a "consensus even among intentionalists" against divisions "not already used to explain non-self-deceptive behavior", which Talbott calls "innocent" divisions. **[Lead]**: the brief's 1985 date, probably the LePore & McLaughlin volume, is unverified.

### Ainslie: *Breakdown of Will* (CUP 2001) [S]; *Picoeconomics* (CUP 1992) [M]; "Précis" (*BBS* 28(5): 635–650, 2005) [A]; "Willpower with and without effort" (*BBS* 44, e30, 2021) [A]
- Metadata: *Breakdown* doi 10.1017/CBO9781139164191. *Picoeconomics* is confirmed by reviews (Kavka 1994, *Econ. & Phil.* 10(2), "Cambridge University Press, 1992, xvi + 440 pages"). The 2021 paper's doi is 10.1017/S0140525X20000357; it was online 26 Aug 2020, and the SEP cites it as 2021, 44(e30): 1–16.
- **The person as a population**: the model has "no ego or judge or other philosopher-king, no organ of unity or continuity, although it will predict how such an organ may appear to operate. In this model the person comprises a variety of interests – groups of reward-seeking processes – that have been shaped by a natural selection process" (p. 62).
- **The bargaining is self-prediction, with no literal selves**: "The threat that weighs on your current self's choice in a repeated prisoner's dilemma is not literally retroactive retaliation by a future self, but the risk of losing your own current stake in the outcomes" (p. 93). Recursive self-prediction answers "the age-old question of what the will consists of" (p. 90).
- **The direct test.** Chapter 8 sets five theories of will against eight observed properties: "null", "organ", "resolute choice" (discussed alongside Bratman's planning view, p. 81), "pattern-seeking" and bargaining. The organ theory holds "that the will is an entity characterizable as strong or weak in general and directed rather like a muscle by an independent intelligence" (p. 118). His verdict: "The organ model, which rather vaguely depicts the will as a faculty deployed by a person like an arm or leg, is silent on most tests but is contradicted by observations that weakness of will can be specific to particular kinds of choice and by evidence of recursive choice-making" (p. 139). The scoring table (p. 125) is the author's own, so it counts as advocacy, not as an independent test.
- Précis [A]: "In conventional theories, a governing faculty like the ego evaluates future choices consistently over time … *Breakdown of Will* (Ainslie 2001) presents evidence that contradicts this model." The side-effects of will include "intractable but circumscribed failures of self-control".
- **Free will**: "you only discern incentives. By contrast, a basic characteristic of cognitivism is the reservation of ultimate choice to an imponderable ego" (p. 130). The "imponderable ego" is the slot the Map's agent-cause occupies.
- The 2021 paper [A]: willpower is "resolve and suppression", and "Resolve is based on interpretation of a current choice as a test case for a broader set of future choices".
- **The bundling prediction, tested.** Kirby & Guastello 2001 [A] (*JEP: Applied* 7(2): 154–164, n = 60 and n = 34): "One third of participants in Experiment 1 who could choose independently on each pair in the series reversed their previous preference and chose the larger reward in the context of the series." Hofmeyr, Ainslie, Charlton & Ross 2011 [A] (*Addiction* 106(2): 402–409, 30 smokers and 30 non-smokers): smokers chose the larger, later reward more often when bundling was suggested or forced; "Non-smokers showed neither pattern"; the authors add "If replicated". Tier: *live hypothesis*.
- **Sibling seam**: personal rules appear here only as the way a will emerges without an organ. The P3 "Self-control as skill and scaffolding" note should treat them as a self-control *strategy*: Ainslie's 2021 split between resolve and suppression, his claim that effortless habit is "a successful result of resolve", and the rigidity costs (misers and anorectics, p. 152), alongside Duckworth, Kennett, Sripada, Bermúdez and Mylopoulos & Pacherie. The hot/cool "cool coding" strategies belong there as well.

### Planner–doer, time inconsistency and the neural split [A]/[M]
- Thaler & Shefrin 1981, *JPE* 89(2): 392–406, doi 10.1086/260971 [A]: they model "the individual as an organization", "both a farsighted planner and a myopic doer", in a conflict "fundamentally similar to the agency conflict between the owners and managers of a firm".
- Strotz, *RES* 23(3), 1955, doi 10.2307/2295722 [M], is the origin of time inconsistency. Laibson 1997, *QJE* 112(2): 443–478 [M], gives the quasi-hyperbolic model.
- McClure, Laibson, Loewenstein & Cohen 2004, *Science* 306: 503–507 [A]: "two separate systems are involved in such decisions". Limbic areas are "preferentially activated" by immediate rewards, while lateral prefrontal and parietal regions are engaged "irrespective of delay". Kable & Glimcher 2007, *Nat. Neurosci.* 10: 1625–1633 [A]: one set of regions "tracks the revealed subjective value of delayed monetary rewards". **[Lead]**: the usual reading, that Kable & Glimcher count against the two-system view, was not checked in their text. Whether valuation is unified below the personal level is open, and either answer is physical.

### Hot/cool systems (Metcalfe & Mischel 1999) [A]
*Psych. Rev.* 106(1): 3–19. The model has "A cool, cognitive 'know' system and a hot, emotional 'go' system". The cool system "is the seat of self-regulation and self-control", and the balance depends on "stress, developmental level, and the individual's self-regulatory dynamics". The model **relocates** the controller into a subsystem rather than eliminating it.

### Multiple controllers with arbitration (Daw, Niv & Dayan 2005) [A]
*Nat. Neurosci.* 8: 1704–1711. The brain "contains multiple systems for behavioral choice", and "such a surfeit of control raises an additional choice problem: how to arbitrate between the systems when they disagree". They propose "a Bayesian principle of arbitration between them according to uncertainty". The distributed architecture therefore has its own selection step, specified computationally.

### Dual-process theory and its critics [A]
Evans & Stanovich 2013 (*PPS* 8(3): 223–241) defend a view on which Type 1 processes "yield default responses unless intervened on by distinctive higher order reasoning processes (Type 2)". Keren & Schul 2009 (*PPS* 4(6): 533–550) find that "two-system models currently provide little scientific advance". Melnikoff & Bargh 2018 (*TiCS* 22(4): 280–293) say the typology pairs "intentional, controllable, conscious, and inefficient" against their opposites, and call it "a convenient and seductive myth". In philosophy, Altehenger (*Theoria* 87(3): 542–558, 2021) combines the "hydraulic model" with a "divided mind" account to *make room* for deliberative agency. Henden 2016 and Heather 2016 are in Heather & Segal (eds), *Addiction and Choice*, OUP [2nd].

## Major Positions (RQ1)

| Rival | Core claim | Predicts, where a unitary selector only accommodates | Relation to tenets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interpretive partition (Davidson) | The irrational step is a non-reason cause *between* structures; the parts are functional and are not agents | No new data. It is a constraint on explanation: the irrational transition cannot be a reasoned act of the whole | Neutral on Tenet 1, rival to the selector's unity |
| Agentive partition (Pears; Rorty 1988 [2nd]) | Subsystems are centres of agency | Self-deception's selectivity | Under the "innocent divisions" consensus |
| Intertemporal bargaining (Ainslie) | Interests discount hyperbolically, and the will is recursive self-prediction | Preference reversal; bundling effects; domain-specific lapses; precedent-sensitivity; rigid rules as side-effects; a shift "from judging amounts to judging reasons" (p. 95) | Physicalist; calls the agent-cause slot an "imponderable ego" |
| Planner–doer / hot–cool | Two subsystems in conflict | Demand for commitment devices; stress and developmental effects | Relocates the controller rather than eliminating it |
| Multiple controllers (Daw et al.) | Arbitration by uncertainty | Which system dominates as training and uncertainty vary | The selection step is computational |
| Practical unity (Korsgaard; Bratman) | Unity is a requirement or achievement of agency | Nothing empirical | Supplies the unity ethics needs, with no posit |
| Unitary naturalism (Mele 1987) | One agent whose wants' motivational force is out of line with its evaluations | Akrasia without partition | Shows that *unity* is not the contested property: a unitary **physical** agent is available |

The last row matters for framing. The objection to the Map is partly about unity and partly about redundancy. Any unitary selector faces the fragmentation data. A non-physical one faces the further question of what it adds to a physical arbitration step.

## Key Debates

- **Explanation or redescription?** Davidson's parts are individuated by "the breakdown of reason relations" (p. 185), so they are defined by the phenomenon they explain. Revisionists such as Mele avoid divisions altogether. The question is open.
- **Is valuation unified or plural?** McClure et al. 2004 report two systems; Kable & Glimcher 2007 report one subjective-value representation; Daw et al. 2005 model arbitration. The question is open, and every answer on the table is physical.
- **Two types, or no clean types?** Evans & Stanovich defend the distinction, while Keren & Schul and Melnikoff & Bargh attack the strict typology. Neither critique, at the level of its abstract, argues for a unitary controller.
- **Is the will an organ, or something that emerges from bargaining?** This is Ainslie's chapter 8, which is advocacy. The independent tests are the two small bundling studies.

## The Objection at Full Strength (RQ2)

**The data.** (O1) Agents show temporary preference reversals that they predict and regret. (O2) Resolutions fail, and lapses can be specific to particular kinds of choice. (O3) A choice changes when the same option is framed as one of a series. (O4) Agents are opaque to the basis of their own choices (`concepts/interpreter-module-narrative-construction-unity`: Nisbett & Wilson, choice blindness). (O5) Agents disavow their choices after the fact or confabulate reasons for them.

**What a distributed architecture predicts.** It derives O1–O5 from independently measured parts. Hyperbolic valuation gives O1 and O3. Multiple controllers with arbitration give O2. A late, separate reporting system gives O4 and O5.

**What a unitary selector needs.** It accommodates each datum only with an auxiliary assumption. O1 and O3 need a present-weighted currency, which is ad hoc unless the felt-valence currency that P-VS1 leans toward is independently shown to be hyperbolic. O2 needs a low-bandwidth, fallible selector, which is independently motivated by the control-theoretic will. O4 needs P-A4's verification limit, also independently motivated. O5 needs the interpreter distinction, also independently motivated. The likelihood comparison therefore runs against the unitary selector on O1–O3 and is roughly even on O4–O5. The sharpest datum is Ainslie's pair, domain-specific weakness and recursive choice. Neither fits a will "deployed by a person like an arm or leg".

**The reverse question.** What does a unitary selector predict that its rivals do not? The akratic owns the act, unlike the anarchic hand. But ownership is a computed signal that every party accommodates, and felt ownership is not evidence (see below). Responsibility practices presuppose one agent, but Korsgaard's practical unity supplies that without a posit. **Nothing remains.**

## Map Relevance (RQ3, P-M1 tiers)

**The phenomena.** Preference reversal under delay is *strongly supported*, though the exact functional form is contested (Read 2001, subadditivity, is a [Lead]). The bundling effect is a *live hypothesis*. The two-system neural split and the strict dual-process typology are both *contested*.

**Discrimination: none in the Map's favour.** A unitary selector can be read in three ways, and each has a cost:

1. **An organ-style will**, meaning a general capacity that is strong or weak and that the person deploys. Domain-specific weakness and recursive choice disconfirm it at modest strength. No Map page checked holds it: `concepts/mental-effort`, `concepts/control-theoretic-will` and `topics/volitional-control` have no general-strength language. **Constraint for the article**: describe the selector as candidate-relative, never as a faculty of general strength.
2. **A candidate-relative selector**, which chooses among options a distributed system supplies (`concepts/control-theoretic-will` L33: "The brain generates options, runs simulations, and prepares competing action plans"). This coexists with every rival, but it faces a trilemma in the akratic case. (i) If it picks what the supplied values favour, the akratic pattern belongs to the valuation and the selector explains none of it. That is P-Q3's nearness to epiphenomenalism, restated at the level of psychology. (ii) If it departs from those values, the departure has no reason, and that is Davidson's surd relocated into the subject. (iii) If it tracks felt valence, the hyperbolic shape must lie in the felt currency, so the selector supplies the medium of choosing but not the explanation of the failure.
3. **Interface relocation**, the split-brain move, in which the fragmentation lies in the expression channel and the selector stays whole. This move is always available, so it discriminates nothing. `topics/split-brain-consciousness` L78 already concedes that agency-unity "is what a coordinated substrate would produce whether or not the phenomenal subject is single".

**Verdict: non-discriminating; framework-internal coherence only.** The selection reading of akrasia supplies a vocabulary and a locus for agent causation, and no prediction. On O1–O3 the evidence mildly favours distributed architectures over any unitary selector, and over the organ-style one most of all. Under P-M1, no tenet upgrades this. Tenet 5 at most blocks a parsimony dismissal of the selector; it does not count in its favour.

**A unitary agent-cause makes akrasia harder (the luck objection).** Davidson's partition keeps the irrational step from being a reasoned act of the whole person, because the non-reason cause runs *between* structures. A unitary agent-cause has nowhere else to put it. The akratic has a reason for *x* and no reason for *x*-rather-than-*y* (1970, n. 25). The agent-causal reply at `concepts/agent-causation` L101 (point 3: selection on reasons is "contrastively explicable") fails for exactly that contrast. Kane's torn decisions are easier on this note's reading: the agent is torn, and under plural voluntary control either outcome is endorsable (`topics/event-causal-libertarianism` L48). Akrasia is selection *against a settled ranking*. Whether Kane's moral-versus-ambition cases already carry such a ranking is a [Lead] to check in Kane before the article leans on the contrast. **Correction to the review's phrasing**: the agent-cause selects against the agent's all-things-considered ranking, not against "all of the agent's reasons". Two replies are possible, and both are leads with costs. (a) Accept the surd as the price of sourcehood, taking agent causation as a terminus (L101). (b) Confine agent-causal selection to cases with no settled ranking, which concedes that akratic acts are not agent-caused against the ranking and so withdraws the selection reading of akrasia. The akrasia page records the objection as "unanswered here", and the article should leave it there unless it argues one reply in full.

**Felt unity is not evidence.** Korsgaard's p. 377 states the Kant page's first exposure for agency: felt unity "is nothing more than the lack of any perceived difficulty in the coordination of psychic functions". The akratic's two pulls are co-present in one field, which is compatible with a partitioned will. `concepts/unity-of-consciousness` L145 ("a synchronically unified locus of selection, which phenomenal unity supplies") infers a unified *selection* locus from *phenomenal* unity. Akrasia is the case of phenomenal unity with divided control, so the line needs a hedge.

**Korsgaard as the rival framing.** Practical unity gives the Map everything its ethics needs from a unified agent, without the posit. The Map can adopt it and keep the posit on its independent grounds of agent causation and indexicality, as the Kant page requires. The cost is that unity of agency then gives the posit no support.

**Guards.** Do not settle the Tenet 3 quantifier, and stay in the [[tenets#^tenet-3-standing|available, not actual]] register. Make no universal claim of efficacy. Leave the tension between aspects and a persisting subject unresolved. That deserves one sentence at most.
