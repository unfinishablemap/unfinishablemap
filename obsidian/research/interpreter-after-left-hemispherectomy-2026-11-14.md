# Research: The Interpreter After Left Hemispherectomy

**Date**: 2026-11-14
**Question** (from `reviews/optimistic-2026-11-14-unity-under-fracture-wing.md`, Ideas for Later): after childhood left hemispherectomy the right hemisphere can take over language. Does post-hoc confabulation of reasons persist, and does it move with language into the right hemisphere? An answer would show whether the interpreter is a lateralised module or a function that follows language.

## Verdict: `fold`. The direct question is unanswered: no study found

**No study found** tests interpreter-type confabulation (post-hoc reasons for one's own behaviour) in left-hemispherectomy patients, or asks whether it relocates with language. This is not an inference to absence from thin searching. A positive-control PubMed query found the interpreter literature, and the matched hemispherectomy query returned zero (see [Search log](#search-log)). There is a structural reason for the gap. The split-brain paradigm needs a second hemisphere to produce behaviour the speaking hemisphere cannot see, and after hemispherectomy there is none. A test would need intact-brain paradigms such as choice blindness or Nisbett–Wilson-style reason reports. No study found has run one in a hemispherectomy population.

The adjacent record still calibrates `concepts/interpreter-module-narrative-construction-unity` (INT). INT L34 describes the interpreter as "associated with the language-dominant (typically left) hemisphere". That parenthetical makes language dominance the defining association and left the typical case. The record cannot support that ordering:

1. **Left and language-dominant have never come apart in a case where confabulated reasons were tested.** Every case where the studies trace reason-confabulation to a hemisphere is a split-brain left hemisphere (or an intact left hemisphere after right-hemisphere damage). That hemisphere is both left and language-dominant.
2. **In split-brain patients, partial right-hemisphere language did not carry the left's capacities with it.** In Gazzaniga & Smylie 1984 and in J.W. (Gazzaniga et al. 1996), right-hemisphere language or speech did not become a second interpreter. On the authors' reading, J.W.'s emerging right-hemisphere speech fed the *left* interpreter.
3. **The lateralisation is not absolute.** Probability matching, a guessing strategy Gazzaniga's group credits to the interpreter, appeared in one split-brain patient's *right* hemisphere when the stimuli were faces (Miller & Valsangkar-Smyth 2005). Corballis (2003) separately postulates a visuospatial "right-hemisphere interpreter".
4. **Why the split-brain cases cannot answer the hemispherectomy question.** Split-brain right-hemisphere language is partial: J.W.'s right hemisphere could not use syntax to comprehend sentences, on the 1996 paper's account. It also sits beside an intact, dominant left hemisphere. After early left hemispherectomy the right hemisphere is the only one, and in the one fMRI case found its language network "conforms to a left-like linguistic neural blueprint" (Danelli et al. 2013, n = 1). A lone right hemisphere that rebuilds a left-like language network might rebuild a left-like interpreter too. No one has checked.

**The answer does not discriminate between the Map and its rival.** This is the reviewer's point and it stands. If confabulation moves with language, a reorganisation account absorbs that. If it does not move, the same account absorbs that too, and so does an interface reading. Treat this as calibration of INT's lateralisation sentence and as a missing HEM→INT link. It is not tenet evidence.

## Key Sources

Every quote below was grep-verified in raw text (see [Quote Ledger](#quote-ledger)). Every citation's metadata was checked against its Crossref record.

### Gazzaniga, Eliassen, Nisenson, Wessinger, Fendrich & Baynes (1996): J.W.'s emerging right-hemisphere speech
- **Citation (Crossref-verified)**: Brain 119(4):1255–1262, doi:10.1093/brain/119.4.1255. PMID 8813288.
- **Read**: full text (author-hosted PDF, UCSB Gazzaniga lab) and the PubMed abstract.
- **Finding**: J.W. had a language-dominant left hemisphere. His right hemisphere could understand speech and print but could not speak. Fourteen years after surgery it named ~25% of left-visual-field stimuli, and a year later ~60%. The extended verbal responses, however, "seem to result from a collaboration between the hemispheres and to involve the left hemisphere interpreter." The mechanism is the left hemisphere elaborating one- or two-word right-hemisphere "clues": "Speech generated by the right hemisphere can provide the left with additional clues which the interpreter utilizes."
- **Guard**: the authors hedge their own mechanism: "We can only speculate that the right hemisphere is generating just one- or two-word clues." Present it as their reading, not as a demonstrated result.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. It is evidence about the interpreter's architecture, not about dualism.

### Gazzaniga & Smylie (1984): Dissociation of language and cognition
- **Citation (Crossref-verified)**: Brain 107(1):145–153, doi:10.1093/brain/107.1.145. PMID 6697151.
- **Read**: PubMed abstract only.
- **Finding**: two split-brain patients "who differ in their right hemisphere language capacity" were given simple cognitive tasks, and "Both isolated right hemispheres performed poorly on most tests." Conclusion: right-hemisphere language "need not necessarily confer the full complement of cognitive skills associated with the language processing skills of the left hemisphere."
- **Guard**: a later review describes this study's causal-inference task (match + log → bonfire): "Even though both hemispheres possessed a sophisticated lexicon, only the left hemisphere could infer the causal relationship between the two items" (Marinsek et al. 2014). That detail comes from the **secondary** review, though Gazzaniga co-authored it. It is not in the 1984 abstract. Do not attribute it to the 1984 paper's own words.

### Gazzaniga, Smylie, Baynes, Hirst & McCleary (1984): Profiles of right-hemisphere language
- **Citation (Crossref-verified)**: Brain and Language 22(2):206–220, doi:10.1016/0093-934X(84)90090-7. PMID 6430465.
- **Finding** (abstract): V.P., a **callosotomy** patient, had right-hemisphere syntactic competence and "expressive language from the right hemisphere".
- **Trap recorded**: a web-search summariser asserted from "background knowledge" that V.P. was a *hemispherectomy* case. That is false. The abstract says the two patients "had undergone staged callosal section".

### Miller & Valsangkar-Smyth (2005): Probability matching in the right hemisphere
- **Citation (Crossref-verified)**: Brain and Cognition 57(2):165–167, doi:10.1016/j.bandc.2004.08.038. PMID 15708210.
- **Finding** (abstract): probability matching "has been attributed to an "interpreter," a mechanism for making interpretations and forming hypotheses, thought to reside exclusively in the left hemisphere." With face stimuli, which this patient's right hemisphere processed preferentially, "the right hemisphere matched the frequency of the previous occurrences of a face-type, but the left hemisphere did not."
- **Why it matters**: this is the one finding that bears directly on *module vs function*. An interpreter-attributed behaviour followed the stimulus domain into the right hemisphere. It is n = 1, a guessing strategy and not verbal reason-confabulation, so it qualifies the lateralisation without settling it.

### Gazzaniga (2005): Forty-five years of split-brain research
- **Citation (Crossref-verified)**: Nature Reviews Neuroscience 6(8):653–659, doi:10.1038/nrn1723.
- **Read**: full text (author-hosted PDF).
- **Gazzaniga's later lateralisation remarks**: the interpreter "creates a schema or 'story' about events that goes beyond the actual available information", and "sense of self is constructed by the left hemisphere interpreter on the basis of the input from these distributed networks." The same essay credits Corballis with "the concept of a right hemisphere 'interpreter'" for visuospatial construction. The essay says nothing on hemispherectomy.

### Gazzaniga (2000): Cerebral specialization and interhemispheric communication
- **Citation (Crossref-verified)**: Brain 123(7):1293–1326, doi:10.1093/brain/123.7.1293. PMID 10869045.
- **Read**: abstract only. The OUP full text is paywalled.
- **Quote**: "These phenomena appear to be related to our left hemisphere's interpreter, a device that allows us to construct theories about the relationship between perceived events, actions and feelings."
- **Side finding**: this check closes a gap in `research/the-interpreter-module-narrative-unity-2026-07-16.md`, which flagged the 123(7):1293–1326 pagination as unverified. INT does not cite this paper, so no page needs fixing.

### Corballis (2003): Visuospatial processing and the right-hemisphere interpreter
- **Citation (Crossref-verified)**: Brain and Cognition 53(2):171–176, doi:10.1016/S0278-2626(03)00103-9. PMID 14607141.
- **Read**: full text (course-hosted PDF).
- **Finding**: the paper postulates a right-hemisphere interpreter "dedicated to constructing a representation of the visual world". This is a *perceptual* interpreter, and a different function from reason-confabulation. Do not conflate the two.

### Marinsek, Turner, Gazzaniga & Miller (2014): Divergent hemispheric reasoning strategies
- **Citation (Crossref-verified)**: Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 8:839, doi:10.3389/fnhum.2014.00839.
- **Read**: full text (open access).
- **Finding**: a review across split-brain, lesion, delusion and imaging studies. It proposes "that the left hemisphere specializes in creating hypotheses and representing causality, while the right hemisphere specializes in evaluating hypotheses". It reports no hemispherectomy data.

### Roser, Fugelsang, Dunbar, Corballis & Gazzaniga (2005): Causal perception vs causal inference
- **Citation (Crossref-verified)**: Neuropsychology 19(5):591–602, doi:10.1037/0894-4105.19.5.591. PMID 16187877.
- **Finding** (abstract): in two split-brain patients, "the direct perception of causality and the ability to infer causality depend on different hemispheres of the divided brain." The abstract does not say which hemisphere does which. Marinsek et al. assign inference to the left.

### Hemispherectomy studies of language and discourse: the nearest on-topic tests
None of these measures confabulated reasons for one's own behaviour.
- **Lovett, Dennis & Newman (1986)**, Brain and Language 29(2):224–251, doi:10.1016/0093-934X(86)90046-5 (Crossref-verified; abstract read). Narrative discourse in three hemidecorticate adolescents: "Neither left hemidecorticate planned narration in extended discourse units", and "only the right hemidecorticate narratives conveyed suggestion and implication as well as explicit statement." n = 3.
- **Caplan, Siddarth, Shields, Mathern & Asarnow (2002)**, American Epilepsy Society abstract 1.188 (no DOI; text read from the raw aesnet.org page). Conversational reasoning and cohesion in 19 children (13 left, 6 right hemispherectomy) against 314 controls. The authors predicted "impaired reasoning and use of lexical ties" after left hemispherectomy, but found that "irrespective of the side of surgery, the patients had significantly more illogical thinking". The isolated right hemisphere was more impaired on cohesion. This is a conference abstract, not a peer-reviewed paper. Its "illogical thinking" is a thought-disorder rating, not confabulation.
- **Caplan, Curtiss, Chugani & Vinters (1996)**, Brain and Cognition 32(1):45-66, doi:10.1006/brcg.1996.0057 (Crossref-verified). Social communication in four children before and after *right* hemispherectomy. Off-side for this question.
- **Danelli et al. (2013)**, Neurocase 19(3):209–231, doi:10.1080/13554794.2011.654226 (Crossref-verified; abstract read). EB had a left hemispherectomy at 2.5 years. His lone right hemisphere's language network "conforms to a left-like linguistic neural blueprint", though "a lone right hemisphere may not be sufficient to guarantee full blown linguistic competences after early hemispherectomy". No interpreter or confabulation testing.
- **Bulteau et al. (2015)**, Epilepsy & Behavior 53:51–57, doi:10.1016/j.yebeh.2015.07.044 (Crossref-verified; abstract read). Six children after left hemispherotomy: right-hemisphere lexico-semantic recovery, but "Syntactic abilities and phonology remain low and support the hypothesis of an early left hemispheric specialization".
- **Social inference, which concerns other people's minds, not one's own reasons**: Fournier et al. 2008, Epilepsy & Behavior 12(3):460–471, doi:10.1016/j.yebeh.2007.12.009. J.H., after anatomic *left* hemispherectomy, "was highly skilled interpersonally, despite evident language-related limitations". Kliemann et al. 2021, Brain Sciences 11(8):965, doi:10.3390/brainsci11080965, found typical theory-of-mind networks in four single-hemisphere adults (one left). Borne et al. 2025, Cortex 191:140–153, doi:10.1016/j.cortex.2025.07.014, found that 14+ years after hemispherotomy "working memory, syntax, and theory of mind remain impaired in the long term" (18 patients, 11 left, not split by side in the abstract). All Crossref-verified.

### Considered and set aside
- **Mayor & Schneider (2023)**, Clinical Neuropsychologist 37(8):1787–1808, doi:10.1080/13854046.2022.2163921 (Crossref-verified). Confabulations in a teenager after a **right frontal** hemispherotomy. The left hemisphere was intact, so the case cannot test relocation. It is memory confabulation, not reasons for actions. It is the only PubMed hit for confabulation × hemispherectomy/hemispherotomy. Its own note: "the literature on confabulating children/teenagers is nearly non-existent".
- **Lu et al. (1997)**, Neurology 49(5):1316–1322, doi:10.1212/wnl.49.5.1316 (Crossref-verified). In Wada testing, "confabulation" was a pointing response on no-touch trials, and it occurred under anaesthesia of either hemisphere. It is sensory, not reason-confabulation, and the abstract does not stratify by language dominance.
- **Joseph (1986)**, J Neurol Neurosurg Psychiatry 49(6):628–634, doi:10.1136/jnnp.49.6.628 (Crossref-verified). A callosotomy patient with *reversed* dominance, the right hemisphere dominant for language. In principle this is the one case type where "left" and "language-dominant" come apart in a disconnected brain. **Full text not read** (PMC scan behind a proof-of-work gate; BMJ PDF blocked), and the abstract mentions no interpreter or confabulation testing. A Europe PMC full-text hit for "confabul*" on this record is probably the reference-list title "confabulatory responding in children" (ref. 22). Do not cite it as evidence either way. It is worth a hand-read if anyone pursues the question.

## Gaps in Research

- **The direct test does not exist** in any study found: choice blindness, false-feedback or Nisbett–Wilson-style reason reports in left-hemispherectomy (or right-language-dominant) patients. The cleanest design would compare left- and right-hemispherectomy adults on choice blindness. That is a suggestion, not a study.
- **The Joseph (1986) reversed-dominance callosotomy case was not read in full.** It is the only disconnected brain found where language dominance and left come apart.
- **Wolford, Miller & Gazzaniga (2000)**, J Neurosci RC64, the original probability-matching finding, was not read directly. It is cited here only through Miller & Valsangkar-Smyth 2005 and Marinsek et al. 2014.
- **The Gazzaniga (2000) full text is paywalled.** Its body may discuss right-hemisphere-speech patients and the interpreter. Only the abstract was read.
- **The Caplan et al. 2002 discourse study** seems to exist only as a conference abstract. No peer-reviewed full paper was found.
