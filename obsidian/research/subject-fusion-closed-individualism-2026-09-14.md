## Executive Summary

The gap is real, doubly self-declared, and worth a standalone concept article. [[moral-census-opacity]] says "The Map has no worked account of subject fusion"; [[positions/individuation-and-subjecthood|P-I4]] registers fusion as "the unresolved case … a live hole." What the corpus does not say — and what this note works out from its own commitments — is that closed individualism plus the fission verdict already *fixes both ends* of the fusion question and leaves only the middle open. Partial coupling (the Hogan twins, brain-to-brain interfaces) mirrors the split-brain verdict: the channel is shared, the census is unchanged, two subjects remain. Complete fusion (Parfit's two bodies growing into one) mirrors P-I4's fission verdict: neither original persists, one new subject begins. Between them lies a transition the framework predicts is *determinate* (never 1.34 subjects), *third-person unreadable* (P-I3), and — the one genuinely new result — *first-person unreadable too*, since the post-fusion subject's q-memories of both lives cannot tell it whether it is A continued, B continued, or new. So the answer to the harvest's question ("impossible, unknowable, or merely unmodelled") is: possible, doubly unknowable at the threshold, and unmodelled only as to *where* the threshold sits, which is the pairing debt P-SC2 already books.

The 2026-08-20 harvest claim "zero 'fusion' hits in the corpus" is **no longer true as a string**: [[fusion-void]] (created 2026-09-04) exists, but it is about *perceptual cue fusion* (Ernst & Banks, McGurk), not subjects. The slug is taken; the subject gap is untouched. The new article must not be named "fusion-void" or "fusion", and should disambiguate in its first paragraph.

## Key Sources

### Parfit, "Personal Identity" (1971)
- **URL**: https://www.pdcnet.org/phr/content/phr_1971_0080_0001_0003_0027 (text extracted from the UVM course PDF, NFKC-normalised)
- **Type**: Paper, *Philosophical Review* 80(1), 3–27
- **Key points**: fusion introduced as "the converse of Wiggins' case"; the physical case is trivial, the psychology is a compromise; survival becomes a matter of degree.
- **Verified quotes** (pp. 18–19 per Cochrane's locator; page not read off the scan): "Physically, fusion is easy to describe. Two people come together. While they are unconscious, their two bodies grow into one. One person then wakes up." — "The one person who results from a fusion can, similarly, q-remember living the lives of the two original people. None of their q-memories need be lost. But some things must be lost." — "If we were about to undergo a fusion of this kind, would we regard it as death? Some of us might. This is less absurd than regarding division as death." — "I have suggested that fusion, while not clearly survival, is not clearly failure to survive, and hence that what matters in survival can have degrees." Also the marriage analogy ("the choice of a partner for fusion can be just as well considered as the choice of a marriage partner … 'trial fusion'") and the autumn/spring fusion-and-division beings, with footnote 31 to Shoemaker, "Persons and Their Pasts".
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with Tenet 4's presupposition (a non-deflationary subject). This is the canonical statement of the *degree* view the Map must reject.
- **Gap**: *Reasons and Persons* (1984) revisits the cases in Part Three ch. 12; the fusion locus there was **not verified at page level** — cite 1971 unless a page is checked.

### Roelofs, *Combining Minds* (2019), ch. 8 "What It Is Like for Two to Become One"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190859053.003.0008 (crossref: pp. 270–293; book DOI 10.1093/oso/9780190859053.001.0001; ch. 7 "Composite Subjectivity and Psychological Subjects" pp. 229–269)
- **Type**: Book chapter (OUP)
- **Key points**: the chapter takes "person-fusion" as its case — two people brain-interfaced and gradually integrated into "a single, integrated, person combining the traits of both" — and asks whether that is "the destruction or the survival of the original subjects of experience" (OUP chapter abstract as reproduced in search; Schwitzgebel's NDPR review of 2021-02-02 calls it "a detailed treatment of slow merging" run on both a panpsychist and a functionalist picture). Companion: Roelofs, "Can We Sum Subjects?" (Routledge Handbook of Panpsychism, 2019, pp. 245–258, DOI 10.4324/9781315717708-21), which compares "experience-sharing, phenomenal bonding, and subject-fusion" and favours the first two.
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts — experience-sharing between overlapping subjects is the manyist bet the Map already declines. But the chapter is the only book-length *phenomenology* of gradual fusion and the article should engage it rather than the dissertation-era fusionism.
- **Caveat**: chapter body not read; claims above rest on publisher abstract + NDPR.

### Cochrane, "A case of shared consciousness" (Synthese, 2021)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-020-02753-6 (199(1–2), 1019–1037; accepted manuscript read in full via Flinders repository)
- **Type**: Paper
- **Key points**: the only peer-reviewed philosophical treatment of the Hogan twins. Three possibilities — "i) that the girls have one unified set of conscious experiences overall; ii) that the girls' sets of experiences are partially shared or; iii) that the girls enjoy two entirely distinct sets of experiences"; argues for (ii) from *locality of content processing*: "each twin accesses processing stages in the other twin's brain, at least up to the thalamic level … Thus both twins are accessing the same processing that (by whatever process) has been made conscious." Defines shared consciousness as "a case where neural connection falls short of bringing about either a singular subject of experience or a wholly unified consciousness. Rather, the conscious experiences of two subjects overlap or fuse such that there are some single token experiences that both simultaneously possess."
- **Verified quote on the evidence base**: "There has yet to be a thorough first-hand scientific report of their case published in the academic literature. Our primary sources remain several television documentaries and newspaper articles, the most detailed of which remains Susan Dominus' write up for the New York Times (2011)." — This is the citation the three corpus articles that hedge "if accurate" should carry.
- **Also useful**: Cochrane's survey line that Parfit "assumed" a singular mind results, with "Similar assumptions abound in the personal identity literature (e.g. Lewis 1976; Unger 1990; Van Inwagen 1995; Dainton 2008; Hershenov 2013 …)".
- **Tenet alignment**: partially compatible. Two subjects with shared *content* is exactly what [[multi-mind-collapse-problem]] already licenses for external observations; shared *token experiences* is the contested step (Kang, below).

### Kang, "Shared consciousness and asymmetry" (Synthese, 2022)

### Schwitzgebel & Nelson, "When counting conscious subjects, the result needn't always be a determinate whole number" (2026)

- **Verified abstract quote**: "Could there be 7/8 of a conscious subject, or 1.34 conscious subjects, or an entity indeterminate between being one conscious subject and seventeen? … Whole-number-countability might be an accident of typical vertebrate biology."
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with P-I1/P-I3. Fusion is the diachronic case where their view and the Map's make opposite predictions about the *shape* of the count (continuous vs. step). ## Major Positions

### Degree survival (Parfit 1971; Roelofs 2019 ch. 8; Zohny & Savulescu 2024)
- **Core claim**: fusion is neither clearly survival nor clearly death; what matters comes in degrees, and a gradual merger has no privileged moment.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Rejected by P-I1/P-I3 — but the rejection *costs* a step: somewhere on the coupling gradient the census goes 2→1 at a point nothing physical marks. This is the diachronic form of the sorites cost [[phenomenal-sorites-problem]] already books, and Tenet 5 governs how it is written: the graded story is simpler and the Map declines it on incomplete-knowledge grounds.

### Multiple occupancy / occasional identity (Lewis 1976; Langford & Ramachandran 2013)
- **Core claim**: fusion is two persons coming to share stages, or two objects becoming identical.
- **Relation to site tenets**: pattern views; rivals to the haecceitist particular. Useful as the *formal* shape of the "both persist" option without its metaphysics.

### Fusionism about micro-subjects (Mørch 2014; contrast Roelofs's experience-sharing)
- **Relation to site tenets**: already read as emergence relabelled ([[manyism]]). For persons it is the "new subject" option, which is the Map's own verdict at the *complete*-fusion end — the article should say so plainly and distinguish it from endorsing fusionism as a combination story.

### Partial sharing, distinct subjects (Cochrane 2021; Kang 2022)
- **Relation to site tenets**: the Map's default at the *partial* end, by parity with split-brain (connection no more fuses than disconnection splits). The Map needs only shared *content*; it can stay neutral on shared *tokens*.

## The Entailment (what the article should actually argue)

1. **Both ends are fixed by parity.** Partial coupling ↔ split-brain: the channel is shared, the census unchanged (two subjects; Hogans, B2B). Complete fusion ↔ P-I4 fission: the perspective "does not survive substrate division," so by mirror it does not survive substrate *union* — neither original persists, one new census entry begins. Coleman's dilemma supplies the exhaustive options and rules out "C composed of A and B."
2. **The count never passes through 1.34.** [[coupling-engagement-condition]]'s existential reading applied diachronically; this is the Map's discriminating prediction against Schwitzgebel & Nelson and Parfit alike — and, per P-I3, an unobservable one.
3. **Double unreadability at the threshold.** Third-person: P-I3. First-person: Parfit's own q-memory symmetry — the post-fusion subject remembers both lives and cannot tell whether it is A, B, or new. The fission subject at least knows it is *not* the original (P-I4 says no successor is); the fusion subject does not even have that. This is the one result no corpus article states.
4. **What remains owed** shrinks to P-SC2's pairing law: *where* on the coupling gradient the two channels' pairings terminate and a new one begins. Not a new debt; the same debt, now with a named case.
5. **Ethics corollary** for the census: at the complete end, fusion is two deaths and one birth (P-AC3 additivity run backwards), harsher than Parfit's "only the very self-satisfied would think of this as death" and than Zohny & Savulescu's "death of an existing psychological identity." Precaution over count *ranges* ([[moral-census-opacity]]'s counsel) extends to fusion consent.
