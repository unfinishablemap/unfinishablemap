## Executive Summary

The outer reviewer was right that a process-internal literature on the dominant occasion exists and is absent from the Map. It was wrong about most of its pointers.

- **The literature found.** It is the 1969–1974 *Process Studies* debate on "Whiteheadian psychological physiology": Sherburne, Cobb, Gallagher. It continues in Cobb (1984) on Hartshorne's advance, Bracken (1989) on fields, and Ford's sub-occasions and inclusive occasions. Moses (2024) surveys it as "the problem of compound individuals".
- **The explicit Cartesian charge.** Its sharpest form is Rem B. Edwards, "Process Thought and the Spaciness of Mind" (*Process Studies* 19(3), 1990): "We seem to be perilously close to Descartes once more!" The reviewer did not name him. Edwards addresses Griffin by name. On a 1988 statement of Griffin's he says "Griffin does not tell us which regions mind occupies, how large the regions are, or whether they are externally identifiable".
- **The reviewer's four pointers, checked.**
  - McHenry's 1999 review contains no dominant-occasion critique at all. It is a sympathetic "fellow traveller" review.
  - The Weber & Weekes volume is real, though the reviewer reverses the editor order. Its text could not be read.
  - Ford's and Bracken's contributions are about the size, inclusion and agency of societies. They do not press the disguised-ego objection as such, though Bracken charges Whiteheadians with residual "entitative" (substance) thinking.
  - Hartshorne's "reservations" are amendments to Whitehead: a psyche roughly as large as the active brain, and undetectable from outside. Edwards calls that amended mind "pseudo-Cartesian".
- **Griffin.** *Unsnarling* pre-empts the substance reading (pp. 43, 48, 134, 172) and asserts that the dominant occasions have a determinate location (p. 228). Within the scope [below](#absence), it engages none of these critics.
- **What it means for the Map.** The objection presses the Map's posited subject at least as hard as Griffin's mind ([explained below](#symmetric)):
  - the Map holds a persisting subject as a further fact (Background Posit One), where Griffin's mind is a serial society;
  - it holds subject boundaries determinate yet unreadable off physical structure (P-I3);
  - it owes a pairing law it lacks (P-SC2).
  - The process thinkers' own escapes, which deflate, enlarge or identify the unifier, are mostly closed to the Map.
  - The objection cannot establish the kind claim, which stays at bedrock.
- **Verdict.** The material fits as a fold of about 300 words into `concepts/nondualistic-interactionism` plus about 200 words into `concepts/process-philosophy`.

## Key Sources {#key-sources}

### A. Whitehead's terms and hedges: *Process and Reality* (1929) [V-ocr]
- **"Regnant" qualifies nexūs, not the soul.** Part II, ch. III, sect. VIII (1929 p. 157): "There will be the 'subservient' nexus and the 'regnant' nexūs within the same structured society." And: "in practice a society is only called 'living' when such nexūs are regnant." These are the "entirely living" nexūs, marked by "a certain social deficiency" (sect. X). The soul is a different thing, the "living person".
- **Two hedges.** Sect. XI (1929 pp. 166–167):
  - "in an animal body the presiding occasion, if there be one, is the final node, or intersection, of a complex structure of many enduring objects";
  - "This route of presiding occasions probably wanders from part to part of the brain, dissociated from the physical material atoms. But central personal dominance is only partial, and in pathological cases is apt to vanish."
- **Locus and personality.** Sect. X (1929 p. 161): "Life lurks in the interstices of each living cell, and in the interstices of the brain." Ch. IV, sect. V (1929 p. 182): "The enduring personality is the historic route of living occasions which are severally dominant in the body at successive instants."
- **Use**: Griffin's p. 186 triad "presiding, regnant, or dominant" fuses Whitehead's presiding occasion with his regnant nexūs. The parent note's guard stands: attribute the triad to Griffin.

### B. The unity debate, 1969–1974
**Cobb & Sherburne (1973), *Process Studies* 3(1): 27–40, doi:10.5840/process1973314** [V-xref; text V-mirror]. It continues Cobb & Sherburne 1972 (*PS* 2(4): 277–295) and Sherburne 1969 (*SJP* 7(4): 401–407, not read).
- **Cobb's dilemma**, against a single soul-thread: "either my experience does not have (at any moment) the unity of an actual entity (i.e., it is then in fact and finally many discrete experiences and not an experience at all), or my experience is an actual occasion including, or contributed to by, both the ego and the other occasions of the nexus." Then: "The second alternative requires a doctrine of regional inclusion."
- **Sherburne's reply**:
  - "Since I believe that regional inclusion is an incoherent concept, I there provided a model … without making any appeal to regional inclusion."
  - "My view is that the unity of my conscious experience is the unity of experience in the occasions of the regnant society."
  - He handles experience in several senses at once through "dominant" and "oblique" inheritance. He turns Cobb's model against itself with examples of forgetting and inattention.
- **Sherburne 1969 (p. 403), as quoted by Cobb**: the regnant, personally ordered society "taken by itself" is "as irrelevant an answer to the problem of life, which is a bid for freedom, as is the doctrine of substance".

**Gallagher (1974), *PS* 4(4): 263–274, doi:10.5840/process19744431** [V-xref; V-mirror]. Gallagher distributes the integrative work:
- "The dominant occasion would have too burdensome a job if it alone were responsible for the integration of bodily experience."
- "I conclude that human mentality is not correctly identified solely with the one nonmaterial enduring object generated by the dominant series of occasions."
- The soul "contains subordinate nonconscious 'living persons'", and "The dominant occasion is not necessarily conscious."
- He names Cobb's worry "the Hermes-quality of the dominant occasion, as it supposedly races around the brain".

**Cobb (1965), *A Christian Natural Theology*, ch. 2** [V-mirror]:
- "In opposition to Whitehead's view, I suggest that the soul may occupy a considerable region of the brain including both empty space and the regions occupied by many societies."
- "The soul is not an underlying substance undergoing accidental adventures."

### C. The explicit Cartesian charge: Edwards (1990), *PS* 19(3): 156–166, doi:10.5840/process199019331 [V-xref; V-mirror]
Edwards studied metaphysics with Hartshorne at Emory (Winter 1961, by his account).
- **Detectability**: "We seem to be perilously close to Descartes once more! What is the difference between a spatially extended mind as large as a brain that cannot be detected externally and a mind that is not spatially extended at all?"
- **Against Hartshorne**: the view "looks suspiciously like either Cartesianism or privileged objectification in disguise"; it leaves "Hartshorne's pseudo-Cartesian mind that is extended in name only".
- **The trilemma**: "process thought must either give an account of the magnitude, locus, and external detectability of the stream of human consciousness, revert to Cartesianism, or cease believing in consciousness altogether." He hopes "the last two alternatives can be avoided".
- **On Griffin by name.** The target is Griffin's essay in *The Reenchantment of Science* (1988, p. 151): "Obviously, Griffin does not tell us which regions mind occupies, how large the regions are, or whether they are externally identifiable."
- **On Hartshorne**: "Hartshorne also rejected Whitehead's account of the magnitude of the occasions of human experience for much the same reasons as Cobb." Edwards quotes *RSP* (1953) p. 36: "the human mind, at any given moment, is not drastically different in size and shape from the pattern of activity in the nervous system with which at that moment it interacts" [V-sec].
- **On Ford**: mind-occasions "pervade the entire region of the brain", yet are imperceptible (PIC 132) [V-sec].
- **His repair**: identify conscious occasions with "the internal synthetic unity of the whole pattern" of brain-wave activity correlated with consciousness. This is identity-leaning; **he is not an ally**.

### D. Substance residue and collective agency: Bracken, Cobb on Hartshorne, Ford
**Bracken (1989), "Energy-Events and Fields", *PS* 18(3): 153–165, doi:10.5840/process198918341** [V-xref; V-mirror]
- **The charge**: "a fair amount of substance-oriented or 'entitative' thinking still lingers among the disciples of Whitehead".
- **Named targets**:
  - Hartshorne "seems paradoxically to be thinking of occasions in thing-like terms within his oft-quoted article on 'The Compound Individual'". On that picture "one mini-entity (actual occasion) is regnant over all the other mini-entities (occasions)".
  - Note 1 extends this to Griffin's "Charles Hartshorne's Postmodern Philosophy": "if one draws a close parallel between the interplay of Leibnizian monads and the interrelatedness of Whiteheadian actual occasions, as does Griffin (and by implication Hartshorne) …, then it is not surprising that one thinks of Whiteheadian societies as aggregates of mini-things rather than as fields".
- **Alternative**: "The unity of the structured society, in other words, might lie in the society itself rather than in its regnant constituent occasion." And: "the agency of the structured society is a genuinely collective agency, not just the agency of its dominant subsociety".
- **What he keeps**, a soul for humans: "Where a given field (society) appears to be a unified subject of experience (e.g., a human being or some other animal species), it is so only through higher-order actual occasions making up its soul or central mechanism of organization and control."
- **On Ford.** Ford's 1973 Leclerc review treats natural compounds as "single strands of personally ordered actual occasions" (Ford p. 109, as quoted). Bracken says this "implicitly confirms the entitative or substantialist understanding of actual occasions".

## Major Positions {#positions}

These are five process answers to "where is the dominant occasion, and what does it do?", plus one outlier.

1. **Microscopic wanderer** (Whitehead as read by Sherburne). It unifies by dominant and oblique inheritance, and Cobb's dilemma presses it. It comes closest to a localised seat.
2. **Regionally inclusive psyche** (Hartshorne; Cobb 1965 and 1984; Griffin according to Moses, unverified). Sherburne calls it incoherent; Edwards calls it undetectable, "extended in name only".
3. **Distributed soul** (Gallagher 1974). It deflates the unifier, an exit that P-I1 closes to the Map.
4. **Field** (Bracken 1989 and 1994). Agency is collective, with a soul kept for humans. Tenet 3 closes this exit to the Map's subject.
5. **Inclusive occasions** (Ford): first sub-occasions, later included occasions.
6. *Outlier*: **identification** (Edwards 1990). It conflicts with Tenet 1.

None of the six concludes for dualism.

## Key Debates {#debates}

### The unity dilemma
- **Sides**: Cobb against Sherburne (1973), with Gallagher's third view (1974).
- **Core disagreement**: one moment of experience across several senses is either one occasion, which then needs regional inclusion, or many occasions, which are then not one experience.
- **Current state**: unresolved. Moses leans "very provisionally" toward a layered ontology.
- **Bearing on the Map**: this is the process form of the question the host already concedes about the Map's own subject. The Map has no better answer: [[unity-of-consciousness]] and [[binding-problem]] treat unity as a datum, not as a solved coupling.

### Locus, size and detectability
- **Sides**:
  - Whitehead: the interstices; the route "probably wanders";
  - Hartshorne and Cobb: brain-sized;
  - Ford: brain-pervading yet imperceptible (via Edwards);
  - Edwards: such a mind must be detectable, or it is Cartesian.
- **Bearing on the Map**: Edwards's question reaches any located but externally unreadable subject.
  - [[positions/individuation-and-subjecthood|P-I3]] holds subject boundaries "determinate" but "not recoverable from the physical or functional organisation of the system".
  - [[tenets/background-commitments]] accepts that the corridor bias is invisible to unconditioned aggregate tests.
  - In Edwards's terms the Map has taken the Cartesian horn openly. Whether that cost is acceptable is settled at the framework level, not by this debate.

### Substance residue and collective agency
- **Sides**: Bracken against Hartshorne, Ford and Griffin.
- **Bearing on the Map**: the Map's subject is *more* substantial than Griffin's mind, since Posit One makes persistence "a real further fact". Bracken's charge would land harder on the Map. The Map holds that subject as a commitment, so the charge does not refute it, but it cannot be turned against Griffin as a point for the Map.

## The Objection Stated, and What It Presses on Both Sides {#symmetric}

**The objection.** On Griffin's view the mind is numerically distinct from the brain, unifies its data, has a determinate location, and exerts more power over the body than any other member does. Those are the Cartesian ego's working features, kept while the ego's name and its difference in kind are removed. The process literature raises four prongs:

1. **Unity**: a single unifier is either overloaded or needs regional inclusion (Cobb, Gallagher).
2. **Locus**: either a wandering point or a brain-sized region (Whitehead, Cobb, Hartshorne).
3. **Detectability**: if it is located yet undetectable, what separates it from an unextended mind? (Edwards)
4. **Substance residue**: a regnant mini-entity as the principle of unity is substance thinking (Bracken, Sherburne's "as irrelevant … as is the doctrine of substance").

**Against Griffin.** In *Unsnarling* Griffin answers the substance prong in advance: the mind is a serial society, not "numerically one through time" (pp. 43, 134, 172). He answers the label prong with the numerical thesis, which does not carry "all the problems of Cartesian (ontological) dualism" (p. 48). He asserts the locus without specifying it (p. 228). On this note's sources he does not address unity in Cobb's form, or locus and detectability in Edwards's form.

**Against the Map, symmetrically.** Each prong lands on the Map's posited subject at least as hard:
- **Substance.** [[tenets/background-commitments|Background Posit One]] posits primitive subject individuation and diachronic subject persistence, the latter as "a real further fact rather than a convention over successive states". That is a stronger persistence claim than Griffin's serial society, which Griffin presents as *less* Cartesian for being serial. The Map holds the posit as a commitment, "chosen starting points rather than proofs", not as an argued result.
- **Detectability.** [[positions/individuation-and-subjecthood|P-I3]] holds boundaries determinate but unreadable off physical structure, which is Edwards's target in Map form. The corridor bias is invisible to unconditioned aggregate tests. A conditioned test remains the register in which this could be relieved ([[positions/quantum-interface#^mechanism-debt|P-Q3]]).
- **Locus and pairing.** [[positions/subject-census|P-SC2]]: "The Map owes a subject-pairing law and does not have one." On the substance-leaning reading, [[interactionist-dualism]] makes pairing "a live unpaid debt" should the interface-fixed location fail. Griffin's co-membership answer (host L81) is not worse.
- **Unity.** The host's concession already covers this (the Rickabaugh discussion in [[emergent-dualism]]).

**Escape routes.** The process tradition answers the objection in three ways:
- *deflate* the unifier (Gallagher's distributed soul, Bracken's collective agency);
- *enlarge* it (Cobb's and Hartshorne's regional inclusion, Ford's inclusive occasions);
- *identify* it with detectable brain activity (Edwards).

Deflation conflicts with P-I1 and Posit One. Identification conflicts with Tenet 1. Enlargement presupposes a spatial occasion the Map's substance-leaning subject is not, while its property-leaning reading "locates the mind too, at no cost" (host L81). **The Map therefore has fewer exits from this objection than Griffin has.**

**What the objection cannot do.** It does not show that Griffin's dominant occasion differs *in kind* from neurons, and no process critic argues that it does. All of them, Edwards included, seek non-Cartesian repairs. The kind disagreement stays where the host puts it, at bedrock. Showing that Griffin carries Cartesian burdens removes nothing from his view that the Map needs removed, and adds nothing to the Map's case.
