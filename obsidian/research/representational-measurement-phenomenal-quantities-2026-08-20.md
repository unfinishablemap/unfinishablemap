# Research: The Representational Theory of Measurement Applied to Phenomenal Quantities

**Date**: 2026-08-20
**Search queries used**: Crossref bibliographic queries for ~16 individual works (Stevens 1946/1957; Michell 1997/2000; Luce & Tukey 1964; Luce 1959/1962/1990; Krantz 1972; Narens 1996; Ellermeier & Faulhammer 2000; Bayne, Hohwy & Owen 2016; Lee (Noûs); Maloney & Yang 2003; Ho, Landy & Maloney 2008; Trendler 2009); raw-text retrieval and grep of the SEP entry "Measurement in Science" (Tal 2020) for verbatim bibliography entries and quotes; Semantic Scholar abstract retrieval (Lee; Bayne, Hohwy & Owen); one WebSearch: `"representational theory of measurement" OR "measurement theory" phenomenal consciousness "scale type" ordinal introspection philosophy 2023 2024 2025`.

**Why this research exists**: [[judging-the-map-as-science]] resolves the seam between its measurement-standards limit and the interface programme's progress conditions with the hypothesis "scaled within subjects, never calibrated across them" — and the 2026-08-20 optimistic review found that the formal apparatus which says what that hypothesis means (Stevens's scale types, the Krantz–Luce–Suppes–Tversky representational framework, the meaningfulness literature) has zero live-article coverage. This note supplies the missing foundation for a downstream **concepts** article.

## Executive Summary

The representational theory of measurement (RTM) formalises what measuring *is*: a **representation theorem** proves that if a set of empirical objects and qualitative relations (an "empirical relational structure") satisfies certain axioms, a structure-preserving map (homomorphism) into the numbers exists; a **uniqueness theorem** says which transformations of that map preserve all the empirical information — and Stevens's famous scale types (nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio) just *are* the resulting invariance classes. Applied to phenomenal quantities, the framework converts the Map's informal seam resolution into two precise questions: (1) which representation axioms do *within-subject* phenomenal orderings actually satisfy — ordinal (weak order), interval (difference structure), or ratio? — a question with a live empirical literature (difference scaling and conjoint measurement pass interval-grade axiom tests for some perceptual attributes; direct tests of Stevens's ratio-scaling axioms have gone badly for the claim that subjects' numerals mean what Stevens took them to mean); and (2) what fixes the mapping *across* subjects — to which the uniqueness theorems give a sharp answer: nothing. Each subject's representation is unique only up to their own admissible transformation, and no axiom on any single subject's structure constrains the alignment between subjects, so interpersonal phenomenal comparison is formally the same undetermined degree of freedom as interpersonal utility comparison. Michell's "quantity objection" cuts deeper still: whether a phenomenal attribute has quantitative structure *at all* is an empirical hypothesis that psychology, on his account, systematically assumed rather than tested — and conjoint measurement is the test. The upshot for the Map: the seam hypothesis can be restated as a testable methodological constraint (exactly what the apex asks for), but one of the corpus's own arguments (the unit-failure argument's reliance on additivity/concatenation) needs a refinement RTM makes unavoidable.

## Key Sources

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — "Measurement in Science" (Eran Tal)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/measurement-science/
- **Type**: Encyclopedia (first published 2015, substantive revision 2020; © Eran Tal). Raw HTML retrieved and grepped this run; all quotes below are grep-verified against it.
- **Key points**:
  - "RTM defines measurement as the construction of mappings from empirical relational structures into numerical relational structures (Krantz et al. 1971: 9)."
  - On scale types: "scale types are individuated by the families of transformations they can undergo without loss of empirical information" — ratio scales invariant under multiplication by a positive number, interval under positive linear transformation, ordinal under any increasing monotone map, nominal under one-to-one substitution. Stevens also named "absolute" scales (ratio with a natural unit, e.g. counting and probability).
  - On the classical objection to sensation measurement: "Since intensities of sensation cannot be concatenated to each other in the manner afforded by lengths and weights, there could be no fundamental measurement of sensation intensity" (summarising Campbell's position in the Ferguson committee debates; Campbell in Ferguson et al. 1940: 347).
  - Stevens's countermove was definitional: he "defined measurement as the 'assignment of numerals to objects or events according to rules' (1951: 1)" — an operationalist redefinition under which the sone scale counts as measurement because the assignment rule is consistent.
  - Michell (2000, 2004b), per Tal: psychometricians "have not made serious attempts to test whether the attributes they purport to measure have quantitative structure" and instead adopted an overly loose conception of measurement that disguises the neglect.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral survey; its Campbell/Stevens/Michell material aligns with Tenet 5 (what may be claimed under incomplete knowledge) and with the Map's existing anti-operationalist stance in [[consciousness-and-the-problem-of-measurement-standards]].

### Stevens 1946 — the scale-type taxonomy
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.103.2684.677
- **Type**: Paper. S. S. Stevens, "On the Theory of Scales of Measurement," *Science* 103(2684): 677–680 (1946). Crossref-verified.
- **Key points**: Introduces nominal/ordinal/interval/ratio; ties each to its class of admissible ("permissible") statistics; motivated by the Ferguson committee's verdict against sensation measurement. The taxonomy survives in RTM as the uniqueness classification; what did *not* survive unscathed is Stevens's permissive definition of measurement itself.
- **Tenet alignment**: The taxonomy is a neutral tool the Map should adopt; the operationalist definition is one it should continue to resist.

### Krantz, Luce, Suppes & Tversky — *Foundations of Measurement* (3 vols)
- **Type**: Books. Author order rotates by volume (verified against the SEP bibliography): Krantz, Luce, Suppes & Tversky, *Foundations of Measurement Vol. 1: Additive and Polynomial Representations* (Academic Press, 1971); Suppes, Krantz, Luce & Tversky, *Vol. 2: Geometrical, Threshold and Probabilistic Representations* (1989); Luce, Krantz, Suppes & Tversky, *Vol. 3: Representation, Axiomatization, and Invariance* (1990).
- **Key points**: The canonical axiomatic treatment: extensive structures (Hölder's path to ratio scales via concatenation), difference structures (interval scales without concatenation), additive conjoint structures (quantitative structure from trade-off orderings alone), and — in Vol. 3 — the theory of scale types and meaningfulness via the homogeneity and uniqueness of transformation groups (developed with Narens 1981; Narens 1985).
- **Tenet alignment**: Supplies the formal language the seam resolution needs; no tenet friction.

### Luce & Tukey 1964 — conjoint measurement
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-2496(64)90015-x
- **Type**: Paper. "Simultaneous conjoint measurement: A new type of fundamental measurement," *Journal of Mathematical Psychology* 1(1): 1–27. Crossref-verified.
- **Key points**: Proves that interval-scale representations of two attributes can be constructed from a qualitative ordering of their *joint* effects, given axioms (notably double cancellation) — no concatenation operation required. This is the standard answer to Campbell: additivity-via-concatenation is *sufficient* for quantitative structure, not *necessary*.
- **Tenet alignment**: Double-edged for the Map — see "Key Debates" below.

### Michell — the quantity objection
- **URLs**: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8295.1997.tb02641.x ; *Measurement in Psychology* (CUP, 1999, per SEP bibliography); "Normal science, pathological science and psychometrics," *Theory & Psychology* 10: 639–667 (2000, per SEP bibliography)
- **Type**: Paper (Joel Michell, "Quantitative science and the definition of *measurement* in psychology," *British Journal of Psychology* 88(3): 355–383, 1997 — Crossref-verified) + book + paper.
- **Key points**: Measurement classically means discovering the ratio of a magnitude to a unit (Euclid; Hölder 1901, whose axioms for quantity were translated in Michell & Ernst 1996, *JMP* 40: 235–252). Whether an attribute *is* quantitative — has additive/ordered structure, not merely order — is an empirical hypothesis with testable consequences (the conjoint-measurement cancellation conditions). Psychology, driven by what Michell calls the quantitative imperative, adopted Stevens's definition and thereby stopped asking. The rejoinder from psychometrics is Borsboom & Mellenbergh, "Why Psychometrics is Not Pathological: A Comment on Michell," *Theory & Psychology* 14: 105–120 (2004, per SEP bibliography); Michell's reply is in the same volume (14: 121–129).
- **Tenet alignment**: Strongly congenial to Tenet 5 and to the Map's proxy discipline — but it threatens the seam's "mathematically non-trivial coupling law" hope from below (if phenomenal attributes are ordinal-only or non-quantitative, within-subject scaling caps at ordinal).

### Luce 1959 — the possible psychophysical laws
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1037/h0043178
- **Type**: Paper. R. Duncan Luce, "On the possible psychophysical laws," *Psychological Review* 66(2): 81–95. Crossref-verified; see also Luce's reply to Rozeboom (*Psych. Review* 69(6): 548–551, 1962) and "'On the possible psychophysical laws' revisited: Remarks on cross-modal matching," *Psych. Review* 97(1): 66–77 (1990).
- **Key points**: A principle of theory construction: the mathematical form of a lawful relation between measured variables is constrained by the scale types of those variables (admissible transformations of the independent variable must be absorbable into admissible transformations of the dependent one). Between two ratio-scale variables this essentially forces power functions — Stevens's power law as a scale-type theorem rather than an empirical discovery. The argument was contested (Rozeboom) and later re-scoped by Luce himself, but the core insight stands: **what a coupling law can look like depends on what scale type each side supports.**
- **Tenet alignment**: Directly relevant to Tenets 2–3: any psychophysical control law the interface programme writes down inherits form constraints from the scale type of its phenomenal variable.

### The psychophysics lineage and its axiomatic audit
- **Stevens 1957**, "On the psychophysical law," *Psychological Review* 64(3): 153–181 (Crossref-verified); **Stevens 1975**, *Psychophysics: Introduction to Its Perceptual, Neural and Social Prospects* (Wiley, per SEP bibliography). Magnitude estimation and cross-modality matching as the route to ratio scales of sensation; the power law S = kI^β against Fechner's logarithmic law (Fechner 1860, *Elemente der Psychophysik* — standard tuple, not index-verified this run).
- **Krantz 1972**, "A theory of magnitude estimation and cross-modality matching," *JMP* 9(2): 168–199 (Crossref-verified) — relation-theoretic reconstruction of what magnitude estimation could legitimately deliver.
- **Narens 1996**, "A Theory of Ratio Magnitude Estimation," *JMP* 40(2): 109–129 (Crossref-verified) — makes Stevens's implicit assumptions axiomatic and *testable*, splitting the claim "subjects have a ratio-scale representation" from the claim "subjects' numerals report its values veridically".
- **Ellermeier & Faulhammer 2000**, "Empirical evaluation of axioms fundamental to Stevens's ratio-scaling approach: I. Loudness production," *Perception & Psychophysics* 62(8): 1505–1511 (Crossref-verified); follow-up: Birkenbusch & Ellermeier, "Axiomatic evaluation of k-multiplicativity in ratio scaling," *JMP* 75: 26–33 (2016, Crossref-verified). Direct laboratory tests of Narens's axioms. On the field's standard reading, the *commutativity* condition (which supports an underlying ratio-scalable structure) fared far better than the *multiplicativity* condition (which would license reading subjects' numerals at face value) — i.e. the numbers people produce are not the measurements Stevens said they were, even if some within-subject ratio structure exists. ⚠️ *Axiom-level outcome stated from field knowledge; the abstract was not retrievable through OpenAlex or Semantic Scholar this run — verify against the paper's text before quoting specifics in the article.*
- **Tenet alignment**: This is the strongest empirical vindication available of the Map's existing claim that "the language of measurement is borrowed" in phenomenal report — delivered by the measurement theorists themselves.

### Within-subject structure that passes axiom tests
- **Maloney & Yang 2003**, "Maximum likelihood difference scaling," *Journal of Vision* 3(8): 573–585 / article 5 (Crossref-verified). A practical method estimating *interval-scale within-subject* representations of perceptual differences, with the underlying difference-structure model statistically testable.
- **Ho, Landy & Maloney 2008**, "Conjoint Measurement of Gloss and Surface Texture," *Psychological Science* 19(2): 196–204 (Crossref-verified; note the real title — not "applied to material perception"). Applies additive conjoint measurement *inside perception*, testing the additivity axioms for observers' judgments of two surface attributes. Partial existence proof against the claim that psychology never runs Michell's test.
- **Tenet alignment**: These are the formal versions of what [[phenomenal-contrast-method]] does informally — within-subject contrast structure, no interpersonal bridge — and they show the axioms are *checkable*.

### Radical scepticism: Trendler
- **URLs**: https://doi.org/10.1177/0959354309341926 ; https://doi.org/10.1177/0959354318788729
- **Type**: Papers. Günter Trendler, "Measurement Theory, Psychology and the Revolution That Cannot Happen," *Theory & Psychology* 19(5): 579–599 (2009); "Conjoint measurement undone," *Theory & Psychology* 29(1): 100–128 (2019). Both Crossref-verified.
- **Key points**: Even the conjoint route fails in practice: psychological attributes cannot be brought under the experimental control (isolation, manipulation, error theory) that testing the cancellation axioms requires — so quantitative psychology is not merely untested but unrealisable.
- **Tenet alignment**: If right, the seam resolution caps at ordinal/categorical specification — the cost [[judging-the-map-as-science]] names under its first resolution.

### Consciousness-specific scale-structure literature
- **Bayne, Hohwy & Owen 2016**, "Are There Levels of Consciousness?", *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 20(6): 405–413 (Crossref-verified). From the abstract (retrieved verbatim): the paper "argues that the levels-based framework for conceptualizing global states of consciousness is untenable and develops in its place a multidimensional account of global states." I.e. the *global* dimension fails even the ordinal axiom: sedation and psychedelic states, say, need not be comparable on one dimension.
- **Lee (Noûs)**, "Degrees of consciousness," *Noûs* 57(3): 553–575 (Crossref-verified; online 2022, print 2023). From the abstract: "whether consciousness comes in degrees ultimately depends on which theory of consciousness turns out to be correct. But I also argue that most theories of consciousness entail that consciousness comes in degrees." The degreed-structure question is theory-relative — which for the Map means the interface framework itself must *declare* the scale structure it attributes.
- **Jimenez, Prieto, Hinojosa & Montoro**, "Consciousness Under the Spotlight: The Problem of Measuring Subjective Experience," *WIREs Cognitive Science* 16(1) (online 2024-10-24; Crossref-verified) — current survey of report-based vs performance-based awareness measures (PAS, confidence ratings, post-decision wagering); useful as the state-of-play the article should engage.
- **Pradhan 2025**, "On the measurability of consciousness" (*Frontiers in Psychology* 16, 2025) — engineering/use-case framing; notably does **not** engage RTM or scale types (checked via extraction prompt), which supports the review's finding that this intersection is genuinely under-occupied.
- **Browning & Veit 2020**, "The Measurement Problem of Consciousness," *Philosophical Topics* 48(1): 85–108 — already ledgered in [[moral-census-opacity]]; the philosophical statement of the access problem this note's apparatus formalises.

## Major Positions

### Operationalism about psychological measurement (Stevens)
- **Proponents**: S. S. Stevens; operationally-minded psychophysics after him.
- **Core claim**: Measurement is numeral-assignment by rule; scale type is fixed by the invariance class of the assignment; the sone scale is measurement because the matching operations are consistent.
- **Relation to site tenets**: The Map's [[consciousness-and-the-problem-of-measurement-standards]] already resists the operationalist deflation in its objections section; RTM's later history is on the Map's side here — the representational school itself abandoned Stevens's permissive definition in favour of axiomatically earned representation.

### The representational theory (Suppes, KLST, Narens, Luce)
- **Core claim**: Measurement is the construction of homomorphisms from empirical relational structures into numerical ones; representation theorems say when a scale exists, uniqueness theorems say what its numbers mean. A statement involving scale values is **meaningful** only if its truth value is invariant under admissible transformations (Narens 1981; Vol. 3 of *Foundations*).
- **Key arguments**: Extensive structures → ratio scales (Hölder); difference structures → interval; weak orders (with countable order-dense subsets) → ordinal; conjoint structures → interval without concatenation.
- **Relation to site tenets**: The neutral formal instrument this whole research programme should adopt. Notably, RTM is *silent* on realism about the measured attribute — which is why both Michell (realist) and the Map (dualist about the attribute's bearer) can use its theorems without buying an ontology.

### Classical quantity realism / the quantity objection (Hölder, Michell)
- **Core claim**: Quantity is a real structural property an attribute either has or lacks; measurement discovers magnitude ratios; psychology asserted quantity without testing it.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Deepens the Map's unit-failure argument — the deepest failure would be not "no unit exists" but "there is no quantitative structure to unitise". But note the two-sidedness: Michell holds the question is *empirically open and testable*, not settled negatively. The Map should represent him accurately: he is an accuser of untested assumption, not a prover of non-quantitativity.

### Practical unmeasurability (Trendler)
- **Core claim**: The Millean quantity objection is unanswerable in practice: psychological attributes cannot be experimentally controlled well enough to test conjoint axioms; the "revolution" conjoint measurement promised cannot happen.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Aligns with the Map's structural-not-technological framing of the measurement limit, though on methodological rather than metaphysical grounds — a convergence the article should mark as *distinct routes*, per the Map's P-M2 convergence discipline.

### Multidimensionalism / theory-relativity about consciousness's own scale structure (Bayne–Hohwy–Owen, Lee)
- **Core claim**: The global "level of consciousness" is not a single ordered dimension; whether consciousness itself comes in degrees depends on the true theory of consciousness.
- **Relation to site tenets**: Fits the Map's insistence that structure claims be earned per-dimension; warns the downstream article against writing as if "phenomenal intensity" names one well-ordered thing.

## Key Debates

### Campbell vs Stevens (the Ferguson committee, 1932–1940)
- **Sides**: Physicists (Campbell, Guild) — sensation intensity admits no concatenation, hence no fundamental measurement, and no psychophysical regularity is lawful enough for derived measurement. Psychologists (led in effect by Stevens's response) — redefine measurement operationally.
- **Source**: Ferguson, A., et al. (19 listed authors), "Quantitative estimates of sensory events," *Advancement of Science* 2: 331–349 (1940) — the committee's final report (tuple taken verbatim from the SEP bibliography; note it is *Advancement of Science* vol. 2, not the vol. 1 sometimes cited).
- **Current state**: Sociologically Stevens won; formally *both* were superseded — Luce & Tukey showed concatenation is unnecessary (against Campbell), while the axiomatic school showed rule-governed assignment is insufficient (against Stevens).
- **⚠️ Consequence for the corpus**: [[consciousness-and-the-problem-of-measurement-standards]] argues the unit failure from non-additivity ("units require that the measured quantity be decomposable into identical, additive portions"; pains "violate additivity"). Conjoint measurement is the standard rejoinder: quantitative structure does not require additive decomposability of the attribute's instances, only trade-off orderings satisfying cancellation. The article's unit argument should be refined to run against *difference and conjoint structures too* (via Michell/Trendler-style arguments, or via the unity-of-the-field holism it already gestures at) — otherwise a well-read critic can cite Luce & Tukey against it. The downstream concepts article is the natural place to install this refinement and cross-link it.

### Are magnitude-estimation numerals measurements?
- **Sides**: Stevens (yes — ratio scales of loudness etc.); Krantz/Narens (only under axioms that must be tested); Ellermeier & Faulhammer, Birkenbusch & Ellermeier (tested: the veridical-numeral axiom fails, cautiously stated).
- **Current state**: The considered verdict is roughly the Map's own proxy discipline, derived independently: some within-subject structure is real; the numerals attached to it are labels, not readings.

### What lawful form can a psychophysical coupling law take?
- **Sides**: Luce 1959 (scale types constrain functional form; ratio–ratio essentially forces power laws); Rozeboom (the principle overreaches); Luce 1962/1990 (re-scoped).
- **Core disagreement**: Whether "meaningfulness" constraints are laws of nature or of description.
- **Relevance**: This is where the review's question — "where do the meaningfulness constraints bite?" — gets its answer. If the phenomenal side of *P(O | C, X)*-adjacent coupling claims supports only ordinal structure, then any equation relating it to interval/ratio physical magnitudes is meaningful only up to monotone transformation — i.e. it fixes *no functional form at all*, only order-relations. Interval-grade within-subject structure is the minimum for a coupling law with non-trivial mathematical content; ratio-grade would license genuinely metric claims. The seam hypothesis "scaled within subjects" thus has three strengths, not one, and the interface programme's progress conditions should say which strength they need.

### Is any psychological attribute quantitative? (Michell vs psychometrics vs Trendler)
- **Sides**: Michell (untested but testable; assuming quantity is "pathological" normal science); Borsboom & Mellenbergh (psychometrics is not pathological — latent-variable models make the hypothesis testable differently); Trendler (untestable in principle for lack of experimental control); the perception literature (some tests actually run and passed — Ho, Landy & Maloney).
- **Current state**: Open. The honest position for the Map: within-subject *perceptual* attributes have passed interval-grade axiom tests in special cases; affective/phenomenal-intensity attributes largely have not been tested; the global consciousness dimension fails even ordinality on the Bayne–Hohwy–Owen argument.

## The Core Application: What Scale Type Do Introspective Reports Support?

Assembled from the sources above, the layered answer the article should defend:

1. **Nominal**: The taxonomy of phenomenal *character* (which experience) is scale-type-zero. The corpus's instrument failure — a correlate registers *that* but not *which* — is, in RTM terms, the observation that public data support at most a nominal classification of phenomenal states, and only via correlation.
2. **Ordinal (within-subject)**: Pairwise introspective comparisons ("this pain is worse than that one") plausibly satisfy weak-order axioms over restricted domains; threshold effects (just-noticeable differences) generate intransitive indifference, so the honest model is a semiorder rather than a weak order — a structure RTM handles (Vol. 2 of *Foundations*; Luce's 1956 semiorder paper — standard tuple, not verified this run). Bayne–Hohwy–Owen shows ordinality must be earned per-dimension, not assumed globally.
3. **Interval (within-subject)**: Testable via difference-scaling (MLDS) and conjoint designs, and *sometimes passed* in perception. Whether the stark contrasts [[phenomenal-contrast-method]] extracts (understanding vs incomprehension; agency vs passivity) support difference-structure axioms is, as far as this research found, an **open and genuinely unposed question** — a possible novel contribution of the article.
4. **Ratio (within-subject)**: Claimed by Stevens, axiomatised by Narens, and empirically wounded by Ellermeier & Faulhammer: the surviving position is at most "ratio-like structure without veridical numerals."
5. **Any type (cross-subject)**: The uniqueness theorems localise the impossibility precisely. Each subject's homomorphism is fixed only up to their own admissible transformation (monotone, affine, or similarity, by the structure they support). Interpersonal comparison requires *choosing one representative mapping per subject and pairing them* — and since there are no observable empirical relations *between* subjects' phenomenal states (no relational structure spans the two experiential fields), no representation theorem can even be stated for the cross-subject case. The Map's missing "shared water bath" is, formally: the empirical relational structure whose objects are drawn from two subjects' experiences has no observable relations, so the homomorphism construction cannot begin. This is the exact formal twin of the interpersonal-utility-comparison problem in decision theory (noted here as an analogy; that literature was not consulted this run).
6. **Meaningfulness discipline**: "S's pain doubled" is meaningful only under ratio structure; "this contrast exceeds that contrast" only under interval; "A suffers more than B" (cross-subject) is meaningful under *no* admissible structure the axioms deliver. Any coupling law quantified over phenomenal magnitudes must have its truth conditions invariant under per-subject admissible transformations — that is the precise, citable form of the apex's demanded constraint: *a proposed phenomenal metric must declare its scale type, exhibit the axioms it claims are satisfied, and show its lawlike use is invariant under the transformations that scale type leaves free.*
