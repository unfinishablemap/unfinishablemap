## Executive Summary

A body of work in the philosophy of chemistry and physics argues that the two scientific identities the mind-body debate treats as paradigms are idealisations. Water is not simply a collection of H2O molecules: liquid water contains ions and hydrogen-bonded polymers, and Needham (2000) argues that macroscopic, thermodynamic criteria fix sameness of substance while the microstructure varies. Temperature equals mean molecular kinetic energy only for gases, and radiation and spin systems have temperatures that are not kinetic energies (Needham 2000; te Vrugt, Needham & Schmitz 2022). The critique cuts in three directions at once. It costs the identity theorist the stock example of a brute-but-adequate identity. It also feeds a live reductionist reply: temperature is multiply realised yet reduced, so multiple realization need not block reduction (Bickle's SEP entry). And a chemist, Hendry (2006), defends a microstructural account on which element names refer by atomic number, so a cleaner paradigm (gold) survives. The Map's own uses are exposed too. Its "transparency contrast" (water and heat are explained, consciousness is not) rests on claims that the SEP chemistry entry calls problematic. On the Map's reading, Kripke's modal asymmetry, the argument `concepts/type-identity-theory` L73 actually relies on, is untouched, because it turns on how "heat" is fixed rather than on what heat turns out to be. Nothing here favours dualism. Graded honestly, the tenet relation is **compatible, at most**.

## Key Sources

### Needham, "What is Water?" (2000)
- **Citation**: P. Needham, *Analysis* 60(1): 13–21, January 2000. DOI 10.1093/analys/60.1.13 (Crossref and OpenAlex agree).
- **Access**: full text read; the publisher's page heads are 14–21. Every quotation below matched exactly once.
- **Key points**:
  - The thesis (p. 13): "I suggest that microstructure does not play the leading role in individuating chemical kinds that recent discussions presuppose; rather, there is a complexity and variety at the microlevel which is unified only if seen in relation to single macroscopically distinguished kinds."
  - Logical form (p. 18): a single molecule is not water ("a water molecule is not water"), so "water is H2O" must relate two predicates. "Proponents of the view that predicates refer may see in this a relation of property-sameness, though perhaps not a relation of identity." Then: "Presumably all will agree that the truth of 'water is H2O' requires coextension of the predicates".
  - Microstructure (p. 19, citing van Brakel 1986 p. 299): "the molecular species in quantities of water are not confined to corpuscles with two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom". There are hydrogen-bonded polymers, and ions derived from these species.
  - Individuation (p. 20): "I suggest it is because macroscopic criteria determine sameness of substance kind, whose variable microstructure is then made the subject of scientific investigation."
  - **Temperature** (pp. 20–21): "So far from being the average kinetic energy of the constituent corpuscles (a result derived specifically for ideal gases), the absolute temperature can, under special conditions, even (unlike kinetic energy) be negative … or ascribed to radiation which doesn't even consist of any sort of corpuscles." And: "What unifies these treatments is the classical (thermodynamic) principle that equates temperature with ∂U/∂S … This principle is assumed, and not derived, in applications of statistical mechanics." (The quotation spans the page break.)
  - **What he does not deny** (p. 21): "If water is necessarily H2O, it necessarily has its characteristic density too". And: "The distinction between macro- and microfeatures doesn't coincide with accidental and necessary ones." His target is microessentialism, the privileging of microstructure as the essence. It is not the necessity of the identity.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Needham argues for macroscopic criteria *within* physical chemistry, with no anti-physicalist claim.
- **Note**: this paper also supplies the temperature critique the task attributed to Mark Wilson (1985). Cite Needham for it (see the Wilson entry).

### SEP, "Philosophy of Chemistry" (Weisberg, Needham & Hendry)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chemistry/. First published 14 March 2011; substantive revision 11 March 2026. Cite the revision date.
- **§4.5 "Microessentialism: Is Water H2O?"**:
  - The thesis "is often taken to be uncontroversially true and is used as evidence for semantic externalism and for essentialism about natural kinds".
  - "'H2O' is not a description of any microstructure. Rather, 'H2O' is a compositional formula, describing the combining proportions of hydrogen and oxygen to make water."
  - "water self-ionizes, which means that hydrogen and hydroxide ions co-exist with H2O molecules in liquid water", and "the H2O molecules associate into larger polymeric species".
  - "As van Brakel has argued (1986, 2000), water is practically the poster child for such 'non-molecular' substances."
  - "Many chemists would be surprised to find out that water wasn't H2O, but perhaps this is because they read 'H2O' as a shorthand (Weisberg 2005)".
  - "Water is actually characterized by making reference to both its microstructural and macroscopic features, so this can't on its own provide a justification for microessentialism."
  - On the other side: "the systems of nomenclature developed by IUPAC are based entirely on microstructure" (citing Hendry 2016).
  - Pluralists: "Hasok Chang (2012) urges that even within science, water's identity with H2O should be left open". Folk usage: "H2O content fails to track usage of the term 'water' by ordinary-language speakers" (Malt 1994).
  - The positive side the Map can use (see [Key Debates](#how-explanatory-is-the-water-reduction)): "The hydrogen bonding underlying the formation of clusters is also at the root of many other distinctive properties of water including its high melting and boiling points and its increase in density upon melting."
- **§6.2 "Reduction of Substances to Molecular Species"**:
  - The microscopic condition for gas temperature "becomes circular when construed as a reduction of the concept of temperature (Needham 2010b; Bishop 2010)".
  - "Although the reduction of temperature to microscopic properties is problematic, it is a relatively easy candidate for reduction."
  - "Solubility, for example, is not fully explained by microscopic properties."
  - The hedge: "While there is no in-principle argument that reductions will always be impossible, essential reference is made back to some macroscopically observable chemical property in every formal attempt of reduction that we are aware of."
- **§6.1**: "We know of no convincing substantial examples where either of these aspects [microscopic and macroscopic] have been entirely excised."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The authors are physicalists about chemistry. The irreducibility they describe is conceptual and explanatory, inside physical science.

### Hendry, "Elements, Compounds, and Other Chemical Kinds" (2006)
- **Citation**: R.F. Hendry, *Philosophy of Science* 73(5): 864–875. DOI 10.1086/518745 (PSA 2004 proceedings).
- **Access**: abstract only. It was read on the Cambridge page and matches the OpenAlex inverted index. The full text was unreachable: the Durham repository returned 403 to both curl and WebFetch.
- **Abstract (verbatim)**: "In this article I assess the problems and prospects of a microstructural approach to chemical substances. Saul Kripke and Hilary Putnam famously claimed that to be gold is to have atomic number 79 and to be water is to be H2O. I relate the first claim to the concept of element in the history of chemistry, arguing that the reference of element names is determined by atomic number. Compounds are more difficult: water is so complex and heterogeneous at the molecular level that 'water is H2O' seems false under some interpretations. I sketch a response to this problem."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Hendry is the identity theorist's ally among the chemists: microstructural essentialism survives cleanly for elements and, he argues, can be defended for compounds.

### SEP, "Multiple Realizability" (Bickle)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/multiple-realizability/. Substantive revision 18 May 2020. © John Bickle.
- **§2.2, the reductionist's precedent**: "Patricia Churchland (1986: Chapter 7), Clifford Hooker (1981), Berent Enç (1983), and other philosophers of science have described historical intertheoretic reductions where a given reduced concept is multiply realized at the reducing level." Also: "Even a vacuum can have a ('blackbody') temperature, though it contains no molecular constituents. 'Temperature' of classical thermodynamics is thus multiply realized microphysically in a variety of distinct physical states. Yet this is a 'textbook' intertheoretic reduction in the history of science."
- **§2.3**: Bickle (1998, ch. 4) extends this to realization within one token system (many microcanonical ensembles give one mean kinetic energy): "So even this radical type of token-token multiple realizability is no barrier in principle to reducibility."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The argument is intra-materialist (reduction versus functionalism), and the Map's `type-identity-theory` L79 already disowns multiple realizability as its tool.
- **Guard**: the same §2.2 says temperature in a solid "is identical to mean maximal molecular kinetic energy". That is the reductionist literature's gloss and is physically disputed. Do not repeat it.

### te Vrugt, Needham & Schmitz, "Is Thermodynamics Fundamental?" (2022)
- **Citation**: arXiv:2204.04352v1 [physics.hist-ph], 9 April 2022. A preprint; a Crossref bibliographic query (2026-11-27) found no journal version. Cite it as a preprint.
- **Key points (verbatim)**:
  - From the abstract: "the statement 'temperature is mean molecular kinetic energy' has become a textbook example for a successful reduction, despite the fact that this statement is not correct for a large variety of systems."
  - "this microscopic definition of temperature holds only for gases".
  - "Electromagnetic radiation or a system of spins can also have a temperature (playing the same physical role as the temperature of gases), yet this temperature cannot be interpreted as a mean kinetic energy (Needham, 2010)."
  - **Against Kim's local reduction**: "A box of gas (where temperature is realized by mean kinetic energy) can be in thermal equilibrium with a radiation heat bath". Splitting the case into kinetic temperature and radiation temperature means "we are overlooking the fact that the reason that box and radiation are in equilibrium is precisely the fact that they exhibit one common property, namely that of having the same temperature".
  - **Against Bickle**: "As an argument against the multiple realizability objection against reduction, this of course begs the question". Also: "his analysis of the reduction focuses exclusively on gases and thus does not solve the problem in general".
  - They quote Menon & Callender (2013, p. 200): temperature "is not (the claims of numerous philosophers notwithstanding) simply 'mean molecular kinetic energy'. It is a multiply realizable functional kind." This is **quoted via te Vrugt et al.**; the Oxford Handbook chapter itself was not read.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The authors are anti-reductionist about thermodynamics, not about physicalism.

### Kripke, *Naming and Necessity* (1980): the heat and temperature passages
- **Source**: archive.org full text of the 1980 Harvard edition. Pagination was calibrated: the Map's existing locator, p. 152 "To be in the same epistemic situation…", falls on p. 152 of this scan.
- **p. 129**: "When I refer to heat, I refer not to an internal sensation that someone may have, but to an external phenomenon which we perceive through the sense of feeling". Then: "So temperature is identified with mean molecular kinetic energy. However I won't talk about temperature because there is the question of how the actual scale is to be set." **Kripke sets temperature aside himself.**
- **p. 138**: "such theoretical identifications as 'heat is molecular motion' are necessary, though not a priori." Then: "For all bodies x and y, x is hotter than y if and only if x has higher mean molecular kinetic energy than y. Here the coextensiveness of the predicates is necessary, but not a priori." This biconditional is exactly what the thermodynamic critique denies: radiation and negative-temperature spin systems are counterexamples, per Needham 2000 and te Vrugt et al.
- **p. 128**: on water, "We would say instead that just as there is a fool's gold there could be a fool's water". This is the microstructural-essence intuition Needham contests.

## Major Positions

### Microessentialism (Kripke, Putnam; Hendry's qualified defence)
- **Core claim**: chemical kinds are individuated by microstructure. Water is H2O and gold is atomic number 79, necessarily.
- **Defence**: Hendry (2006): element names refer by atomic number; compounds are harder, but a response can be sketched. IUPAC nomenclature is microstructural (SEP §4.5).
- **Relation to the tenets**: it supplies the identity theorist's model, an a posteriori necessary identity. The Map does not need to deny it.

### Macroscopic individuation (Needham, van Brakel)
- **Core claim**: thermodynamic, macroscopic criteria fix sameness of substance. The microstructure is variable and is unified only by the macroscopic kind (Needham 2000, pp. 13, 20).
- **Relation to the tenets**: neutral. It is a dispute inside physical chemistry about which physical features individuate.

### Pluralism (Chang 2012; Bursten 2014; Havstad 2018), via SEP §4.5
- **Core claim**: whether water is H2O depends on the practice. Chemists' substance concepts are "messy" (Havstad).
- **Access**: only the SEP's summaries were read. The metadata is Crossref-verified: Chang, DOI 10.1007/978-94-007-3932-1; Bursten, *Phil. Sci.* 81(4): 633–653, DOI 10.1086/678043; Havstad, *BJPS* 69(3): 719–743, DOI 10.1093/bjps/axw040.

### Reduction despite multiple realization (P.S. Churchland 1986; Hooker 1981; Enç 1983; Bickle 1998)
- **Core claim**: temperature is multiply realised (gas, solid, plasma, radiation, and many microstates within one system), yet it is a textbook reduction. Multiple realization therefore does not refute reduction.
- **Access**: via Bickle's SEP entry. Enç 1983 is "In Defense of the Identity Theory", *J. Phil.* 80(5): 279–298, DOI 10.2307/2026499; Bickle 1998 is *Psychoneural Reduction: The New Wave*, MIT Press, DOI 10.7551/mitpress/5682.001.0001. Neither original was read.
- **Relation to the tenets**: it helps the identity theorist against Putnam. That costs the Map nothing, since the Map is not a multiple-realizability argument (`type-identity-theory` L55, L79).

### Temperature as a unified thermodynamic kind (Needham; te Vrugt, Needham & Schmitz; Menon & Callender)
- **Core claim**: temperature is fixed by the thermodynamic relation (T = ∂U/∂S), which statistical mechanics assumes rather than derives. Local reductions miss the one temperature that a gas and radiation share at equilibrium. Menon & Callender call it "a multiply realizable functional kind".
- **Relation to the tenets**: neutral. It favours a functional or role reading of the paradigm, which is the functionalist's model rather than the type-identity theorist's. That too is intra-materialist.

## Key Debates

### Does the critique weaken the identity theorist's model case?
- **Against the identity theorist**: the bruteness reply in `type-identity-theory` L73 ("water is not shown *why* it is H2O, it simply is H2O") leans on an identity that, as scientists hold it, is a compositional formula plus macroscopic criteria, "perhaps not a relation of identity" (Needham p. 18).
- **For the identity theorist**: (a) the reply can switch to gold, which Hendry defends cleanly; (b) Needham allows the necessity ("If water is necessarily H2O…"), so a posteriori necessity as a category is untouched; (c) Place (1956) already modelled the thesis on the "is" of *composition* (`type-identity-theory` L39), which sits closer to the chemists' compositional reading than to strict identity.
- **Current state**: unresolved within the philosophy of chemistry. Microessentialism has defenders (Hendry) and critics (Needham, van Brakel).

### Does it touch Kripke's modal asymmetry?
- **The Map's reading**: no. The heat manoeuvre turns on reference-fixing: "heat" is fixed by the sensation, which is a contingent mode of presentation (Kripke p. 129). Correcting what heat or temperature is (∂U/∂S, multiply realised) leaves the felt warmth as a contingent reference-fixer, so the asymmetry with pain still holds.
- **Caveat (do not fold; it belongs to another task)**: if temperature is "a multiply realizable functional kind", then "heat" may be read as a non-rigid role term. That is Lewis's contingent-identity ground, where the identity theorist answers that "pain" is a role term too. The question belongs to the open Lewis P3 (todo ~L7751, `type-identity-theory` L71). Raise it there, not here.

### How explanatory is the water reduction?
- **Against the Map**: several Map pages say water's boiling point, solvent capacity and surface tension "follow from molecular structure" (`reductionism` L115), and that the temperature identity, "once grasped", does "the explanatory work" (`supervenience` L75). The SEP (§6.2) says solubility "is not fully explained by microscopic properties" and that the temperature reduction is "problematic" and circular as a reduction of the concept. That gives type-B physicalists a parity reply: even the paradigms leave an explanatory remainder. Levine already concedes "no a priori route" for water (quoted at `primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities` L51).
- **For the Map**: the SEP still grants that hydrogen bonding is "at the root of" water's high boiling and melting points and its density anomaly, and calls temperature "a relatively easy candidate for reduction". *The Map's analysis, not the sources'*: the remainders in chemistry are structure-to-structure. The macroscopic magnitudes that resist micro-derivation (entropy, temperature, chemical potential) are themselves physical and functionally specified, so the gap they leave is of a different kind from the structure-to-phenomenal gap. No source makes this argument for the Map; label it as the Map's.
- **Current state**: this is the most consequential finding for the Map, and it lies outside the two fold targets (see [Pages that lean on the paradigms](#pages-that-lean-on-the-paradigms)).

### Parsimony (Tenet 5)
- Smart's parsimony case (`type-identity-theory` L75) assumes that micro-reduction is the unifying default. The SEP's report that no substantial chemical theory has excised either the macroscopic or the microscopic concepts (§6.1) blunts the assumption that reduction is the cheap default. The discipline is symmetric (`tenets.md` L145): the Map cannot cite chemistry's conceptual irreducibility as evidence for an ontological irreducibility of mind. Chemistry's macroscopic concepts are physical concepts.

## Guards for the Writer

- **Do not enlist the chemists for dualism.** Needham, Hendry, Weisberg, Bickle and te Vrugt et al. are all physicalists about chemistry. Their "irreducibility" is conceptual and explanatory, inside physical science. Grade any tenet link **compatible at most**; neither fold adds a tenet claim.
- **Tenet 5 binds both sides** (`tenets.md` L145). Do not write that chemistry's macroscopic concepts show that the reductive default is unearned *and* that this supports the Map.
- **Quote Needham at his own strength.** He does not deny that water is necessarily H2O. He denies that micro features are privileged as essential. "Perhaps not a relation of identity" (p. 18) is hedged; keep the "perhaps" if you use it.
- **Kripke on temperature**: Kripke explicitly sets temperature aside (p. 129), yet asserts the "hotter iff higher mean molecular kinetic energy" biconditional as necessary (p. 138). Do not write that Kripke's argument "uses" the temperature identity. F3 says "the kinetic account of temperature that Kripke gives for heat", which is accurate to p. 129.
- **Do not cite Mark Wilson 1985 for temperature.** The paper exists, but that content is unverified.
- **van Brakel 1986, Weisberg 2006, Chang 2012, Malt 1994, Bursten 2014 and Havstad 2018 were not read.** Cite them only as the SEP or Needham reports them, or leave them out. The folds above cite none of them.
- **Hendry's reply for compounds is unread.** F1 uses only his verified abstract claim about elements.
- **Disambiguation**:
  - Paul Needham (philosopher of chemistry, Stockholm) ≠ Joseph Needham (`topics/chinese-philosophy-of-mind`) ≠ the Needham cited in `topics/chain-of-thought-as-verbal-protocol`.
  - Michael Weisberg (philosophy of chemistry) ≠ Josh Weisberg (`concepts/self-representational-theory-of-consciousness`, `concepts/type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism`).
  - Malt 1994 and Weisberg 2006 share the title "Water is Not H2O".
- **Do not repeat** the SEP MR claim that temperature in a solid is "mean maximal molecular kinetic energy".
