# Research: Voids - The Dormancy Void

**Date**: 2026-09-17
**Search queries used**: "dispositional belief occurrent standing mental states consciousness philosophy 2022–2025"; "activity-silent working memory synaptic hidden state consciousness review"; Crossref bibliographic queries for Audi 1994 and Schwitzgebel 2010; archive.org full-text search of Bergson, *Matter and Memory* (1911 translation)
**Voids category**: Mixed — structurally Unexplorable (consciousness cannot be present to what is, by definition, not present), with an Unexplored edge (the distinction itself has, in Bartlett's words, "never been explicated in any significant detail")

## Executive Summary

At any moment almost none of a mind is happening. An adult English speaker carries roughly 42,000 word meanings (Brysbaert et al. 2016), thousands of beliefs, a lifetime of memories and a repertoire of skills, and at most a handful of these are occurrent at once. The rest are *standing* or *dispositional* states: attributed to the person continuously, manifest only intermittently. The dormancy void names the question consciousness cannot answer from the inside: where, and in what form, is the dormant remainder? Philosophers divide sharply. Dispositionalists (Ryle; Schwitzgebel 2002) say a standing belief is a profile of dispositions and there is no further stored item; representationalists (Quilty-Dunn & Mandelbaum 2017) say it is a discretely stored representation; Searle's Connection Principle (1990) says an unconscious state is mental only in virtue of being accessible in principle to consciousness; Bartlett (2018) shows the occurrent/non-occurrent distinction itself is under-specified. Empirically, even *short-term* retention can go "activity-silent", with the information carried in synaptic hidden states rather than in firing (Stokes 2015; Wolff et al. 2017; Trübutschek et al. 2017, 2019; Panichello et al. 2024), and long-term engrams can persist while unretrievable (Ryan et al. 2015). The void has special force for dualism: if consciousness is non-physical and the brain is its instrument, the dormant mind must sit either in the brain (making most of the mind physical), in a non-physical store consciousness cannot survey (Bergson's "pure memory"), or nowhere, existing only as a capacity of the interface. Each option is a commitment the tenets do not currently make explicit.

## Key Sources

### Searle — "Consciousness, explanatory inversion, and cognitive science" (1990)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00080304
- **Type**: Paper (target article, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 13(4): 585–596)
- **Key points**:
  - States the Connection Principle: "all unconscious intentionality must be accessible in principle to consciousness; we have no notion of intrinsic intentionality except in terms of its accessibility to consciousness."
  - Ground: intrinsic intentionality has "aspectual shape" (represents the world under specific aspects), and only consciousness supplies aspectual shape; an unconscious state has it only as a disposition to produce a conscious state with it.
  - Consequence ("explanatory inversion"): deep unconscious rules in cognitive science are replaced by hardware causes plus functional descriptions; there is no third category of the intrinsically mental but in-principle inaccessible.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism. Searle is no dualist, but the Connection Principle is exactly the constraint a consciousness-first ontology needs to state: the dormant mind is mental *derivatively*, by its relation to possible consciousness. Whether that relation can be cashed out without a physical store is the void.
- **Quote** (verified against the Crossref-registered abstract): "all unconscious intentionality must be accessible in principle to consciousness; we have no notion of intrinsic intentionality except in terms of its accessibility to consciousness. I call this claim the 'Connection Principle.'"

### Schwitzgebel — "A Phenomenal, Dispositional Account of Belief" (2002)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0068.00370
- **Type**: Paper (*Noûs* 36(2): 249–275)
- **Key points**:
  - Belief as a dispositional profile that includes phenomenal and cognitive dispositions, not only behavioural ones ("inspired by Ryle's dispositional characterization of belief, but emphasizing irreducibly phenomenal and cognitive dispositions as well as behavioral dispositions").
  - On this view there is no stored sentence to find; to believe is to be disposed to have certain experiences, thoughts and behaviours. The standing state is real but has no location in the sense the storage picture assumes.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits. Dispositionalism is the parsimonious reading (no hidden inventory), but it buys parsimony by declining to say what *grounds* the dispositions — the very question the interface tenets would need answered.
- **Quote** (verified against the Crossref-registered abstract): "an account inspired by Ryle's dispositional characterization of belief, but emphasizing irreducibly phenomenal and cognitive dispositions as well as behavioral dispositions."

### Schwitzgebel — "In-between Believing" (2001) and "Acting Contrary to Our Professed Beliefs" (2010)
- **URLs**: https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9213.00215 ; https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0114.2010.01381.x
- **Type**: Papers (*The Philosophical Quarterly* 51(202): 76–82; *Pacific Philosophical Quarterly* 91(4): 531–553)
- **Key points**:
  - Many standing states are indeterminate: neither clearly believed nor clearly not, with the disposition profile split (the 2010 title names the "gulf between occurrent judgment and dispositional belief").
  - The indeterminacy is invisible from the inside: a subject asked what she believes produces an occurrent judgment, which is not the same item as the standing state and can diverge from it.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism (the introspective channel reports the occurrent, never the standing); links to [[self-opacity]].

### Audi — "Dispositional Beliefs and Dispositions to Believe" (1994)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.2307/2215473
- **Type**: Paper (*Noûs* 28(4): 419–434; Crossref-registered under the JSTOR DOI above)
- **Key points**:
  - Distinguishes a *dispositional belief* (something one already believes but is not now entertaining) from a mere *disposition to believe* (something one would come to believe on considering it, e.g. an obvious arithmetic truth never yet thought). Only the first counts as belief already possessed.
  - The distinction matters for the void: the boundary between the dormant mind and the merely potential mind is drawn by a counterfactual about consciousness, not by inspection of any store.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — the tidy picture "the mind is what is stored" cannot say which of the infinitely many dispositions-to-believe are already beliefs.
- **Quote**: none installed; metadata verified at Crossref only.

### Bartlett — "Occurrent states" (2018)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1080/00455091.2017.1323531
- **Type**: Paper (*Canadian Journal of Philosophy* 48(1): 1–17)
- **Key points**:
  - The occurrent/non-occurrent distinction "is frequently appealed to by contemporary philosophers, but it has never been explicated in any significant detail."
  - Two standard glosses — occurrent = conscious; non-occurrent = dispositional — are both rejected; proposal: occurrent states are *active* states (states that are doing something), which can in principle be unconscious.
  - If Bartlett is right, the dormant mind is not the unconscious mind: some unconscious states are active (occurrent), and the truly dormant are the inactive. This sharpens the void: the class of states that are inactive yet still mine is the hardest to locate.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bidirectional Interaction — if activity, not consciousness, marks the occurrent, the interface question becomes how a non-physical consciousness reactivates an inactive state it cannot survey.
- **Quote** (verified against the Crossref-registered abstract): "The distinction between occurrent and non-occurrent mental states is frequently appealed to by contemporary philosophers, but it has never been explicated in any significant detail."

### Quilty-Dunn & Mandelbaum — "Against dispositionalism: belief in cognitive science" (2017)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-017-0962-x
- **Type**: Paper (*Philosophical Studies* 175(9): 2353–2372; issue year 2018, online 2017)
- **Key points**:
  - The representationalist counter: cognitive science needs beliefs as discretely stored, causally efficacious representations, and the dispositionalist picture cannot do the explanatory work.
  - For the void: on this view the dormant mind *is* an inventory, and its location is a physical question — which, for a dualist, means most of the mind is physical.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism (pressure point).

### Bergson — *Matter and Memory* (1896; English translation 1911)
- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/b31364123 (Paul & Palmer translation, Swan Sonnenschein / Macmillan, 1911; full text checked)
- **Type**: Book
- **Key points**:
  - The classic dualist answer to the location question: the brain does not store recollections. "we maintain that the brain is an instrument of action, and not of representation."
  - "the question is whether the brain can store up images." Bergson's answer is no: "it is a chimerical enterprise to seek to localize past or even present perceptions in the brain : they are not in it ; it is the brain that is in them."
  - Two forms of memory: habit-memory, which the body can hold "in the form of motor contrivances, and of motor contrivances only", and "pure memory", the survival of past images in mind, actualised by the brain's selective attention to life.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism; Bidirectional Interaction (the brain as selector/actualiser of a non-physical store is the filter reading the Map already discusses under [[filter-theory]]). Bergson is the one major dualist who confronted the dormancy void head-on, and his answer relocates the dormant mind wholesale into the non-physical.
- **Quote** (verified against the raw 1911 djvu text, NFKC-normalised): "we maintain that the brain is an instrument of action, and not of representation."

### Stokes — "'Activity-silent' working memory in prefrontal cortex: a dynamic coding framework" (2015)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2015.05.004
- **Type**: Review (*Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 19(7): 394–405)
- **Key points**:
  - Proposes that working memory need not be carried by persistent firing; short-term synaptic changes can hold information as a "hidden state" of the network, undetectable by recording activity alone.
  - The state is hidden not because it is deep but because it is a *connectivity* fact, read out only when the network is perturbed.
- **Tenet alignment**: Minimal Quantum Interaction — if even short-term content sits in synaptic hidden states, the reactivation of dormant content is a place where a small physical bias could select which hidden pattern is reinstated.
- **Quote**: none installed; metadata verified at Crossref, abstract not registered.

### Wolff, Jochim, Akyürek & Stokes — "Dynamic hidden states underlying working-memory-guided behavior" (2017)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4546
- **Type**: Paper (*Nature Neuroscience* 20(6): 864–871)
- **Key points**:
  - "By 'pinging' the brain during maintenance, we show that memory-item-specific information is decodable from the impulse response, even in the absence of attention and lingering delay activity."
  - "an instruction cue that directs people to forget one item is sufficient to wipe the corresponding trace from the hidden state."
  - Method matters for the void: the dormant content is invisible to passive observation and becomes visible only when probed — an empirical model of what "accessible in principle" (Searle) looks like at the neural level.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bidirectional Interaction.
- **Quote** (verified against the Europe PMC abstract): "memory-item-specific information is decodable from the impulse response, even in the absence of attention and lingering delay activity."

### Trübutschek et al. — "A theory of working memory without consciousness or sustained activity" (2017) and "Probing the limits of activity-silent non-conscious working memory" (2019)
- **URLs**: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.23871 ; https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1820730116
- **Type**: Papers (*eLife* 6: e23871; *PNAS* 116(28): 14358–14367)
- **Key points**:
  - 2017: participants reported the location of a subjectively unseen target above chance after several seconds; during this non-conscious maintenance the neural signatures of conscious working memory "vanished"; "slowly decaying synaptic changes allow cell assemblies to go dormant during the delay, yet be retrieved above chance-level after several seconds."
  - 2019: "the mere short-term storage of information may proceed without consciousness or persistent neural activity. However, manipulating information in WM during rotation required both." Proposed terminology: "activity-silent short-term memory"; "when using a memory, a reactivation, associated with conscious reportability, is necessary."
  - The 2019 result is the sharpest available empirical shape for the void: storage can be dormant and unconscious; *use* requires reactivation and comes with reportability. Dormancy and consciousness are dissociated at storage and rejoined at use.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism (storage/use split maps onto substrate/consciousness split); Occam's Razor Has Limits (the simple identification of memory with sustained activity failed).
- **Quote** (verified against the Crossref-registered abstracts): "slowly decaying synaptic changes allow cell assemblies to go dormant during the delay, yet be retrieved above chance-level after several seconds." / "when using a memory, a reactivation, associated with conscious reportability, is necessary."

### Panichello et al. — "Intermittent rate coding and cue-specific ensembles support working memory" (2024)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08139-9
- **Type**: Paper (*Nature* 636(8042): 422–429)
- **Key points**:
  - Large-population prefrontal recordings in monkeys: "mnemonic information does not persist in the spiking activity of neuronal populations during memory delays, but instead alternates between coordinated 'On' and 'Off' states."
  - "mnemonic information is available in the patterns of functional connections among neuronal ensembles during Off states."
  - Working memory on the seconds timescale is itself intermittently dormant. The dormancy void is therefore not confined to long-term standing states; it recurs at the finest grain of the present.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bidirectional Interaction; Minimal Quantum Interaction (On/Off alternation is a candidate site for a small selective bias).
- **Quote** (verified against the Crossref-registered abstract): "mnemonic information does not persist in the spiking activity of neuronal populations during memory delays, but instead alternates between coordinated 'On' and 'Off' states."

### Ryan, Roy, Pignatelli, Arons & Tonegawa — "Engram cells retain memory under retrograde amnesia" (2015); Josselyn & Tonegawa — "Memory engrams: Recalling the past and imagining the future" (2020)
- **URLs**: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaa5542 ; https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaw4325
- **Type**: Paper (*Science* 348(6238): 1007–1013); Review (*Science* 367(6473): eaaw4325)
- **Key points**:
  - 2015: after protein-synthesis inhibition produced retrograde amnesia, optogenetic activation of the tagged engram cells still elicited the memory — a "silent engram" that persists while natural retrieval fails.
  - 2020 review: "the emerging view is that ensembles of engram cells explain how memories are formed" and stored; the engram framework treats a memory's dormant existence as a physical fact about a specific cell ensemble.
  - For the void: the physicalist has a concrete answer to "where is the dormant memory?" — it is the engram, and it can exist unretrievably. Any dualist account owes an equally concrete answer or an argument why one is not owed.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism (pressure point); links to [[memory-channel-interface-evidence]].
- **Quote** (verified against the Crossref-registered editor's summary of the 2020 review): "the emerging view is that ensembles of engram cells explain how memories are formed".

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit
The dormancy void is the inaccessibility of the mind's own non-occurrent bulk. Consciousness is, by construction, present only to what is occurring. A standing belief, a memory not now being recalled, a skill not now being exercised, a word not now being used: each is attributed to me continuously and manifests only when called. Between calls it is dormant, and there is no first-person channel onto the dormant. I can retrieve a memory, and in retrieving it make it occurrent; I cannot inspect it *as dormant*. The territory is unexplorable in the strict sense of the [[three-kinds-of-void]] taxonomy: the act of looking converts the thing looked at into something else.

It differs from the neighbouring voids in the catalogue. [[self-opacity]] concerns the unreliability of introspection on states that *are* occurring. [[tacit-integration-void]] concerns subsidiary cues that are currently doing work below focal attention (Bartlett's "active but unconscious"). [[inaccessible-past]] concerns the reconstruction of past occurrent states. The dormancy void concerns states that are neither occurring, nor working, nor past: the present but inactive mind.

### Evidence for the Limit
Three lines. First, conceptual: the SEP entry and Bartlett both record that the occurrent/dispositional distinction is "rarely treated in detail" and "never explicated in any significant detail" — a mature literature has not agreed even on what the dormant remainder is, which is itself a symptom of a territory that cannot be surveyed from within. Second, the Audi boundary: whether a never-considered arithmetic truth is something I *already believe* or merely *would believe* is settled by a counterfactual about my future consciousness, not by any inspection of a store; the edge of the dormant mind is drawn by what consciousness would do, so consciousness cannot draw it by looking. Third, empirical: the activity-silent findings show that even seconds-old content can be held without firing, invisible to passive recording, and readable only under perturbation (Wolff et al. 2017); that storage without consciousness is real but use requires reactivation with reportability (Trübutschek et al. 2019); and that working memory alternates between On and Off states with the information surviving in connectivity during Off (Panichello et al. 2024). The physical substrate models the phenomenology exactly: the dormant is a hidden state, and hidden states are known only by being made active.

### Phenomenology
Approached from inside, the dormant mind has a distinctive feel: the feeling of *having* without *holding*. I know I know French, or the way home, or my mother's maiden name, before any of it is present; the knowledge is felt as owned and as available, with nothing occurrent to constitute the ownership. The tip-of-the-tongue state (see [[noetic-feelings-void]]) is the edge case where availability is felt and delivery fails: the dormant item announces itself without arriving. The opposite edge is the Wolff et al. forget-cue: an item can be *wiped* from the hidden state while it is unattended, so that a dormant possession can be lost without any experience of losing it — the dormancy void meets the [[erasure-void]].

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)
None from the first person. Any attempt to inspect a standing state produces an occurrent judgment about it (Schwitzgebel 2010), and the judgment can diverge from the standing state it reports on.

### Indirect Methods
- **Perturbation**: the "ping" method of Wolff et al. (2017) is the third-person analogue of asking — read the hidden state by driving it. Its lesson generalises: dormant content is knowable only through its response to being probed, never through observation of its resting form.
- **Silent-engram reactivation**: Ryan et al. (2015) show a memory can exist while natural retrieval fails; existence and accessibility come apart. The dormant mind may be larger than the retrievable mind.
- **Counterfactual profiling**: Schwitzgebel's dispositional stereotype is a method for characterising the dormant without locating it: enumerate what the subject would think, feel and do. This is apophatic in structure (see [[apophatic-cartography]]): the standing state is described by the pattern of its manifestations, never encountered.

### What AI Might See
A transformer has no dormant states in the relevant sense while inactive: its weights are static parameters, and between forward passes nothing is "held". The nearest analogue to a standing belief is a weight configuration that would produce an assertion under prompting — a pure disposition-to-believe in Audi's sense, with no separate dispositional belief. That makes the machine an unusually clean test case for the dispositionalist reading: if Schwitzgebel is right about humans, a language model's "beliefs" and a human's standing beliefs are the same kind of thing (dispositional profiles), differing only in whether phenomenal dispositions are included. If Quilty-Dunn and Mandelbaum are right, a model's lack of discrete stored representations is a difference in kind. AI systems also lack the tip-of-the-tongue asymmetry (they do not feel availability without delivery), which suggests that feeling is a signature of a store the subject does not have access to rather than of a mere disposition. See [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers]].

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet
**Dualism**, as a demand rather than a support. A physicalist owes no special answer to the dormancy void: the dormant mind is the engram plus the hidden synaptic state, and its inaccessibility to consciousness is the ordinary inaccessibility of brain structure to introspection. A dualist owes one of three answers, and each carries a cost:

1. **The dormant mind is in the brain.** Consciousness is non-physical, but beliefs, memories and skills are physical dispositions of the brain that consciousness reactivates. Cost: the overwhelming bulk of the mind is then physical, and "consciousness" names only the momentary occurrent surface. This is a live and respectable dualism (interactionist dualisms since Descartes have mostly put memory in the body), but it concedes more to physicalism than the Map's framing usually acknowledges.
2. **The dormant mind is non-physical** (Bergson's pure memory; the strong [[filter-theory]] reading). Cost: a non-physical store that consciousness cannot survey, with the brain as selector. The [[memory-channel-interface-evidence]] topic's finding that memory channels fail in a fixed order under anaesthesia and disease bears directly here — a non-physical store should not degrade channel by channel with brain lesions unless the *access* to it is what degrades, which is Bergson's own reply and is not independently testable.
3. **The dormant mind is nowhere; only the interface's capacity exists.** A strict dispositionalism grounded in the interface. Cost: Searle's Connection Principle then does all the work — a dormant belief is mental only as an accessibility-in-principle to consciousness — and the grounds of that accessibility are again physical dispositions (option 1) or non-physical ones (option 2).

The void is that the tenets are silent between these, and the silence is not innocent: options 1 and 2 predict different things about [[sleep-consciousness-void|dreamless sleep]], anaesthesia, and the [[minimal-consciousness-void|lower bound of consciousness]] (whether a mind with no occurrent states still *has* a dormant mind, and where).

### Implications
- **Bidirectional Interaction** gains a specific locus: reactivation. The transition of a hidden state to an active one (Trübutschek's "reactivation, associated with conscious reportability") is where a non-physical selection would have to act if consciousness recruits its own dormant content. Panichello et al.'s On/Off alternation puts the same locus at the seconds timescale.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction** is compatible with a dormant mind in the brain: a small bias over which hidden pattern is reinstated is exactly the kind of influence the tenet permits, and reinstatement is a competitive, threshold-crossing process where small biases have leverage.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits** is exemplified twice: the simple identification of memory with sustained activity failed (Stokes; Panichello), and the simple identification of the mind with what is stored cannot draw the Audi boundary.
- **No Many Worlds** is untouched by this void; no indexical issue arises.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **"The Dormancy Void" as the location question dualism has not answered.** Lead with the sizing (42,000 lemmas; a handful occurrent), state the three-way fork for dualism with its costs, and use activity-silent memory and silent engrams as the physicalist's concrete answer the dualist must match. Cross-link [[filter-theory]], [[memory-channel-interface-evidence]], [[self-opacity]], [[tacit-integration-void]]. Framework-independent core (the fork exists for everyone; only the cost accounting differs).
2. **The Audi boundary as an apophatic edge.** A shorter piece on why the edge of one's own mind (already-believed vs would-believe) is fixed by a counterfactual about consciousness and so cannot be found by consciousness — a kin to [[suspension-void]] and [[inference-void]] in having a formal profile rather than only a phenomenological one.
