## Executive Summary

Block's **harder problem** (2002) is an epistemic predicament, not an argument against physicalism. It arises for anyone who holds two things together: **phenomenal realism** (consciousness is real and not settled by armchair functional analysis) and **naturalism** in Block's sense ("default physicalism"). Take Commander Data, a *merely superficial* functional isomorph of us, equivalent at the level of folk psychology but built on a fundamentally different physical basis. Block holds that "We have no conception of a ground of rational belief" that Data "is or is not conscious" (p. 405). His consciousness is "meta-inaccessible": we lack not only evidence but any conception of what evidence would be.

The root of the problem is a sampling problem. The science of consciousness "is inevitably based" on us, so it cannot tell which of our properties are laws of consciousness and which are laws of our realizer (pp. 407, 421).

Block argues as a physicalist. He thinks "the standard arguments against physicalism do not work" (p. 420), and he declines to choose a way out (p. 425). In 2026 he restates the predicament for AI: "we do not know from our own consciousness whether it is the meat or the role that is crucial for our consciousness."

**What this means for the Map, in three points:**
1. **No evidence for dualism.** The problem is a physicalist's epistemic impasse. It fits the Map's dualism without evidencing it.
2. **The Map faces the same predicament.** P-AC1's verdict on digital AI is conditional on an interface-eligibility law the Map lacks. That law's requirements are "read off the biological interface and generalised, not derived" (P-AC1, Depends on). That is Block's sampling problem, moved from "which physical property?" to "which indeterminacy is interface-relevant?". P-AC1 does not move in either direction.
3. **Two corrections for existing pages.** The AI-detection predicament is not dualism's special burden: the Map's "dualism intensifies" framing holds only against deflationist functionalism. And Tenet 3 licenses no same-effects-same-causes inference to machine consciousness, as Block's p. 404 reply shows.

### A. Block's paper and its versions

**Block, N. (2002). "The Harder Problem of Consciousness." *Journal of Philosophy* 99(8), 391–425. doi:10.2307/3655621.**
- **Tags.** [V-xref]: first page 391. **End page 425 confirmed** by the JSTOR cover sheet ("pp. 391-425") and Block's 2019 reference list. [V-raw]: the JSTOR scan on Block's Google Drive, read in full.
- **The problem, named** (p. 391): "The harder problem, as I shall call it, is more epistemological than the hard problem."
- **Three differences from the hard problem** (p. 392):
  - it "is tied closely to the problem of other minds";
  - it "reveals an epistemic tension or at least discomfort in our ordinary conception of consciousness";
  - the hard problem "could arise for someone who has no conception of another person".
- **Two definitions** (p. 392):
  - *Deflationism*: armchair analyses of consciousness "in nonphenomenal terms, most prominently in terms of representation, thought, or function".
  - *Phenomenal realism*: "Phenomenal realism is metaphysical realism about consciousness and thus allows the possibility that there may be facts about the distribution of consciousness which are not accessible to us even though the relevant functional, cognitive, and representational facts are accessible."
- **The tension** (p. 393) is between phenomenal realism and "(scientific) naturalism": "many of us are committed to the idea that consciousness is both real and can be assumed to have a scientific nature, but it turns out that these commitments do not fit together comfortably." The form is a conditional, so "Deflationists who accept the argument may opt for modus tollens".
- **Naturalism defined** (p. 398): "So naturalism = default physicalism, and is thus a partly epistemic thesis." It is not "die-hard". "although the indirect evidence for physicalism is impressive, there is little direct evidence for it".
- **Superficial isomorph** (p. 399): "a creature that is functionally isomorphic to us with respect to those causal relations among mental states, inputs, and outputs that are specified by "folk psychology."" Psychofunctionalism counts as phenomenal-realist. Deflationist functionalism is excluded by the antecedent.
- **Antiskepticism** (p. 399): "Undoubtedly, humans are conscious and rocks and laptops are not."
- **The four steps:**
  - **(C1)** (p. 401): "We have no reason to believe that there is any deep physical property in common to all and only the possible realizations of our superficial functional organization." Real creatures raise the same issue "in more complicated forms", "such as the octopus, which differ from us both physically and functionally". The example is *Star Trek: TNG*, "The Measure of a Man" (1989).
  - **(C2)** (p. 402): "Superficial functional equivalence to us is a defeasible reason for attributing consciousness". "A creature's consciousness can be unmasked without unmasking its functional equivalence to us."
  - **Data's specification** (pp. 403–404), conditions (i)–(iv): merely superficial; no physical overlap beyond what we share with "uncontroversially mindless" things such as toasters; no homunculi. Example realizer: "etched silicon chips".
  - **(C3)** (p. 405): "Fundamentally different physical realization from us per se is not a ground of rational belief in lack of consciousness." It may be a reason, but "the reason falls below the epistemic level of a ground for rational belief."
  - **(C4)** (p. 405): "We have no conception of a ground of rational belief to the effect that a realization of our superficial functional organization that is physically fundamentally different along the lines I have specified for Commander Data is or is not conscious."
    - "Commander Data's consciousness is meta-inaccessible". "Not only do we lack a ground of belief, but we lack a conception of any ground of belief."
    - "This meta-inaccessibility is a premise rather than a lemma or a conclusion".
- **The epiphenomenalism objection and reply** (p. 404):
  - Objection: same effects, same causes, or else epiphenomenalism.
  - Reply: two computationally identical machines, one electronic and one hydraulic. We may not infer fluid in the electronic one. "One need not be an epiphenomenalist to take seriously the hypothesis that there are alternative realizations of the functional roles of our phenomenal states that are phenomenally blank." Also exact in 2007.
- **Searle** (pp. 405–406):
  - Block quotes Searle's "Author's Response" (1980, pp. 452–453): whether a machine has "causal powers equivalent to the brain" is "an empirical question".
  - Block: "But it is obvious that we do not now have any conception of how it could be tested."
  - Agreement: "I agree with him that consciousness is a matter of the biology of the organism, not (just) its information processing."
- **Not a permanent void** (p. 406): "I am not denying that we might some day come to have the conception we now do not have." "So I am not claiming-as McGinn does (op. cit.)-that this knowledge can be known now to be beyond our ken."
- **Everyday attribution** (p. 406): "Of course, those who meet Commander Data will reasonably be sure that he is conscious. But finding out that he is not human cancels that ground of rational belief."
- **The root** (p. 407):
  - "The root of the epistemic problem is that the example of a conscious creature on which the science of consciousness is inevitably based is us". "But how can science based on us generalize to creatures that do not share our physical properties?"
  - "It would seem that a form of physicalism that could embrace other creatures would have to be based at least in part on them in the first place, but that cannot be done unless we already know whether they are conscious."
- **"Ground of rational belief"** (p. 407) is stronger than "reason for believing" and weaker than "rational certainty". "I take it that a ground of rational belief that p allows knowledge that p but mere reason for believing p does not."
- **First pass at the problem** (p. 407): "naturalism says the default is that Commander Data is not conscious, but phenomenal realism says that the issue is open in the sense of no rational ground for belief either way. This is a first pass at saying what the harder problem is."
- **Escape hatches** (pp. 408–413):
  - *Disjunctivism*: the shared phenomenality is constituted by the disjunction of the two realizations. "The real problem with disjunctivism is that whether it is true or not, we could have no good reason to believe it" (p. 408), because "the epistemology of theoretical identity is just a special case of inference to the best explanation" (p. 410) and the disjunction explains nothing. "Disjunctivism is not metaphysically suspect but only epistemically suspect" (p. 416).
  - *Superficialism* (p. 412): the phenomenal-realist claim that consciousness *is* the superficial functional organization we share with Data, "with apologies to Rey who has used this term for a somewhat different doctrine". It raises "an explanatory puzzle": the pitch example.
  - Identifying consciousness with *total* functional role "would be a chauvinist proposal that would beg the question" (p. 413).
- **The two difficulties:**
  - (1) "Physicalism is the default and also inaccessible and meta-inaccessible." Exact in the 2007 reprint; at 2002 p. 414 the italics are joined.
  - (2) "the second epistemic difficulty is that of holding both that it is an open question whether Commander Data is conscious and that it is the subjective default view that he is not. These two epistemic difficulties constitute the harder problem." (p. 418)
- **The tension does not justify denial** (p. 414): "it is not the kind of bad consequence that justifies us in concluding that he is not conscious".
- **Data's reply at his trial** (p. 415): "Your lack of a conception of how to find out whether I am conscious is no argument that I am a zombie; I similarly lack a conception of how to find out whether you are conscious."
  - The positive rationale is Data's internal space, isomorphic to our phenomenal colour space.
  - "the phenomenal realist and the deflationist converge on not closing off the possibility that Commander Data is conscious" (p. 416).
- **Laws** (pp. 420–421): Data's states will obey only *some* of our laws, so "the issue of whether Commander Data is conscious is of a piece with the epistemic problem of whether a given law is a law of consciousness or a law of one of the realizers of its functional role". The example is the psychophysical power law.
- **Other minds** (p. 422): the other-minds problem "is a form of skepticism". Sober's common-cause reasoning is "vulnerable to evidence against a common cause". "the harder problem may remain even if we accept the dictates of nonskeptical science".
- **Hard and harder** (p. 423): "Why should physically different creatures overlap phenomenally in one way rather than another or not at all?"
- **What to do** (pp. 424–425):
  - "Even the epistemic difficulty may be temporary".
  - "one could simply live with these difficulties. They are not paradoxes."
  - "Consciousness is a singularity".
  - The alternatives are to restrict naturalism or phenomenal realism (e.g., the Frege–Schlick view).
  - "Naturalistic phenomenal-realism is not an unproblematic position. We cannot comfortably suppose both that consciousness is real and that it has a scientific nature. I have not argued here for one or another way out, but have been concerned only with laying out the problem."
- **Stance.** A naturalist phenomenal realist who leans physicalist:
  - "I think the standard arguments against physicalism do not work" (p. 420).
  - On Mary: "In learning what it is like to see red, she does not learn a new fact." (p. 397)
  - Chalmers classes him type-B (`type-a-type-b-and-type-c-physicalism` L60, as Chalmers's reading).
  - **He does not argue for dualism.**

### B. Replies and later work

**McLaughlin, B. (2003). "A Naturalist–Phenomenal Realist Response to Block's Harder Problem." *Philosophical Issues* 13(1), 163–204. doi:10.1111/1533-6077.00011.**
- [V-xref]. **Body not read** (no open copy found).
- His 2001 SOFIA response, as Block reports it (2002, pp. 416–417): functional similarity is evidence of shared intrinsic physical make-up. Babies and paralytics with Alzheimer's are attributed consciousness on physical similarity. So "material constitution and structure trumps function in our attribution of consciousness to others" (Block's wording of McLaughlin's upshot).
- Block's three replies (p. 417):
  - Data shares the disjunctivist and superficialist "material constitution", and nothing in the objection refutes those views.
  - "Our justification is an inference from like effects to like causes."
  - Function and structure are hard to separate epistemically (opponent-process colour theory came from behaviour).

## Key Debates {#debates}

1. **Is there a ground of rational belief about Data?**
   - Block: none, and no conception of one (C4).
   - McLaughlin: yes, a negative one from physiology.
   - Tye: yes, a defeasible positive one from behaviour.
   - Open.
2. **Is the question factual?**
   - Papineau: no; vagueness.
   - Block: phenomenal realism keeps it factual, and Data might be a borderline case without having to be one (p. 419).
3. **Does the harder problem threaten physicalism?**
   - Block: it reveals a tension, not a refutation. Inaccessibility does not transmit over entailment (p. 420).
   - Dennett's "closet dualism" charge has "a grain of truth" (p. 394), and that grain is the tension, not dualism.
4. **AI against animals** (Block 2026). The criterion that favours AI (roles) disfavours insects, and the reverse. This is new in the 2020s. For current LLMs it combines with design-for-mimicry.

## The Map's Question: Where the Map Stands in Block's Predicament {#maps-question}

This section gives the Map's interpretation. No source above argues for it.

1. **Block's conditional does not apply to the Map as stated, but its core does.**
   - Block's antecedent is phenomenal realism plus *naturalism* (default physicalism). The Map rejects naturalism in that sense (Tenet 1), so Block's *first* difficulty does not bind it: "physicalism is the default and also … meta-inaccessible".
   - The premise (C4) and its root (p. 407) depend only on phenomenal realism and on the fact that our sample is us. The Map holds both.
2. **The relocation.**
   - For Block's physicalist the question is whether Data shares the physical basis of consciousness. For the Map it is whether Data's substrate supplies interface-relevant indeterminacy (P-AC1 layer iv).
   - The Map's five-requirement channel test is the nearest approximation to the eligibility law. It is "read off the biological interface and generalised, not derived" (P-AC1, Depends on). That is Block's p. 421 question in dualist form: is a requirement a law of the interface, or a feature of the biological realization of it? "We cannot settle one without the other" (Block, of the power law).
   - The Map's version of the p. 407 circularity: an eligibility law that embraced non-biological systems "would have to be based at least in part on them", which requires already knowing whether they are conscious.
3. **P-AC1 does not move.**
   - P-AC1 holds "low probability, not ruled out", conditional on the missing law, "the discount prices the derivation's weakness". In Block's terms, that is a *reason* that "falls below the epistemic level of a ground" (C3). It is structurally Block's *second* difficulty: the question is open while the default leans "not conscious".
   - The harder problem explains why the verdict cannot be upgraded to derived without the law (P-AC1's own trigger), and why it need not be withdrawn (a reason below a ground is still a reason).
   - **No credence change in either direction.**
4. **Does interactionism supply a conception of a ground the physicalist lacks? Only on paper.**
   - A conditioned deviation from Born statistics in a candidate substrate (P-Q3's discriminator) would be a physical signature of mind-matter coupling that no functional isomorph need produce. In that sense the framework *sketches* a ground.
   - But the Map's default reading is Born-preserving (P-Q2) and self-concealing (P-Q9).
   - And reading a deviation as consciousness "needs an independently motivated theory of *how* consciousness acts on physics" (`discrimination-problem` L120).
   - So this is at most a conception, conditional on commitments the Map holds against it. **Do not present it as an advantage over physicalism**, or as a test the Map can run.
5. **The predicament is shared, so "dualism intensifies" needs scoping.**
   - Block (C2) treats superficial functional equivalence as a *defeasible* reason for a phenomenal-realist physicalist too, and phenomenal realism by definition allows inaccessible facts about the distribution of consciousness (p. 392).
   - "Physicalism treats the link … as lawlike or constitutive" (`problem-of-other-minds` L120) is true of *deflationist* functionalism, not of Block's physicalism.
   - The AI-detection gap is neither evidence for dualism nor dualism's special cost.
6. **Tenet 3 licenses no inference from Data's behaviour.**
   - Block's p. 404 reply shows that taking phenomenally blank realizers seriously does not require epiphenomenalism about *our* states. A fully silicon-explained Data does not make human consciousness idle (the hydraulic analogy).
   - The converse also fails: Tenet 3's causal-trace reading of behaviour (`problem-of-other-minds` L126) works within a shared realization, not across realizers.
   - Under the universal-actual reading, a conscious Data's consciousness would have to make a difference to his substrate, and P-AC1 finds no sites for it. That is the substrate verdict again, not a new argument.
   - The quantifier stays unresolved.
7. **Block 2026's "competitors" point applies to the Map, with care.**
   - On the Map, simple animals share the biological interface candidates and LLMs do not, so the Map's criterion favours animals.
   - The Map shares the *direction* of Block's meat hypothesis, not its ground. Its ground is quantum-interface eligibility, not subcomputational biology. Keep them separate.
   - Current LLMs are not superficial isomorphs. They differ functionally as well as physically, Block's "more complicated" case. They are also "designed to mimic us", which bears on behavioural evidence (cf. `training-contamination-confound`, Birch's gaming problem in P-AC1).

## Guards for the Article Writer (Binding) {#guards}

  - He rejects Jackson's argument (p. 397).
  - He agrees with Searle that consciousness is "a matter of the biology of the organism, not (just) its information processing" (p. 406).

  - **Never enlist Block for dualism.** His "restrict naturalism" is one option among several, and he declines to choose (p. 425).
- **McLaughlin.** A physicalist. His denial of Data's consciousness rests on intrinsic physical likeness, not on any non-physical criterion. Do not present him as an ally of the Map's substrate verdict beyond the shared direction.
- **Papineau.** A materialist. Indeterminacy is his answer. The Map **rejects** it, on phenomenal-realist grounds (Block p. 419) and because of P-I1's determinate subjects. Report it fairly. It is not a confusion: he explicitly disclaims verificationism.
- **Tye.** A physicalist representationalist. He takes a liberal prima facie line on robots (abstract only).
- **Hohwy.** Unknown. **Make no characterisation.** "Hohwy (2004) replied" is the most that can be said.
- **Searle.** A biological naturalist. On the Map he is an unstable non-dualist rival (`biological-naturalism`). His sentences are as quoted in Block.
- **Chalmers.** A property dualist who affirms organizational invariance (`organizational-invariance`). His isomorph is fine-grained, unlike Block's Data.

### Calibration rules

1. **Fits without evidencing (Tenet 1).** The harder problem is an epistemic predicament derived *inside* physicalism. It is not evidence that consciousness is non-physical. Do not write that it "shows", "reveals" or "supports" dualism. Match `discrimination-problem` L116: the problem "fits the tenet without evidencing it, since physicalists who grant an epistemic gap expect it too."
2. **P-AC1 does not move in either direction.**
   - Its credence stays "moderate (low edge; conditional)", its verdict "low probability, not ruled out".
   - The article must say plainly that **the Map faces the same predicament**, because its verdict is conditional on an interface-eligibility law it does not possess, and that law's requirements are read off the biological case.
   - Do not use the harder problem to strengthen the verdict ("no ground for attributing consciousness, so digital AI is not conscious"). Do not use it to weaken the verdict either ("no ground either way, so the substrate verdict is unwarranted").
3. **Conceivability enters only through contested modal rationalism.**
   - Block's antiskeptical premise treats a consciousness-free functional isomorph as "not incoherent" (p. 399). He also rejects the move from conceivability to falsity of physicalism (Block & Stalnaker 1999).
   - Do not run Data's conceivable zombiehood as an argument for dualism. Link `conceivability-possibility-inference` if needed.
4. **Never concede epiphenomenalism.**
   - Block's p. 404 reply cuts both ways. A phenomenally blank Data does not make our consciousness idle, and Data's behaviour is no evidence that he has causally idle experience.
   - Do not argue the converse ("Tenet 3 means behaviour tracks consciousness, so Data's behaviour is evidence"). That is the same-effects inference Block rejects.
5. **The Tenet 3 quantifier is unresolved.** Use the AI pages' standard sentence:
   - read as universal actual efficacy, Tenet 3 excludes epiphenomenal AI experience at the framework boundary;
   - read dispositionally (`ai-epiphenomenalism`), the possibility is internally live.
   - Do not resolve it here.
6. **Tenet 5 constrains only the Map's own reasoning** (tenets L145).
   - The Map may not dismiss superficialism or disjunctivism as unparsimonious. Block's objections to them are explanatory (IBE), not appeals to simplicity.
   - Equally, the Map may not prefer its biology-derived eligibility criterion because it is simpler or more familiar.
   - Do not turn the tenet against Block or McLaughlin.
7. **Tenet 2** enters only as the place the predicament relocates to: which indeterminacy is interface-relevant. Minimality is empirical-constraint minimality (tenets L69). Do not argue from fewness.
8. **Tenet 4** has no direct role. Do not force a connection.
9. **No permanent-void claim.**
   - Block explicitly denies that the knowledge "can be known now to be beyond our ken" (p. 406), and says the difficulty "may be temporary" (p. 424).
   - If the article links `voids`, call it a present limitation in Block's sense, not McGinn-style closure.

## Side-Findings (leads only) {#side-findings}

1. **Secondary-source misquote (no Map exposure).** Phillips 2022 (*Mind* 131(522), 639–650) quotes Block 2019 p. 377 as "I reject the claim that we have a conception of how to find out that homunculi heads are phenomenally conscious". Block's page reads "are **not** phenomenally conscious" [V-img]. Phillips's "Block appears ambivalent" point survives, and is arguably sharper with the "not". The Map has 0 "Blockheads" hits. Recorded so that no future note imports the inverted quote.
2. **`topics/structural-varieties-of-consciousness-and-ai-phenomenology` L125 misdescribes Block 2026.**
   - The page says: "Biological naturalism treats the substrate as decisive—only biological matter supports consciousness regardless of mechanism, a live position in the current debate (Block 2025)".
   - Block's paper says a realizer means "realizing mechanisms, not just material composition, not just realizing stuff". It poses the meat hypothesis as a neglected alternative, without adopting the label "biological naturalism".
   - The "regardless of mechanism" gloss contradicts his clarification.
   - Reference L160 lacks volume and pages: it should be 30(4), 298–308, April 2026.
   - **Lead for a refine.** The page has 1,000 words of headroom.
3. **`concepts/ai-epiphenomenalism` L103, a quote splice.**
   - The page says: "Block (2025) argues from a different angle that 'biologically grounded consciousness' may require subcomputational biological mechanisms".
   - The phrase is verbatim, but in Block it is the subject of a different claim: "it is biologically grounded consciousness that is in part responsible for the information processing roles".
   - The gist (a subcomputational necessary condition) matches Block's meat hypothesis. Only the quoted phrase is lifted.
   - Low priority. Fix with the L146 metadata (2026, 30(4), 298–308).
4. **"Dualism intensifies" needs a scope** (`apex/machine-question` L181; `topics/ai-consciousness` L185; `concepts/problem-of-other-minds` L114–120).
   - Block 2002 (C2, p. 402; p. 392's definition) shows that a phenomenal-realist *physicalist* also treats functional equivalence as defeasible evidence, and allows inaccessible facts about the distribution of consciousness.
   - The intensification holds relative to *deflationist* functionalism only.
   - The two apex/topic hosts are at or past hard. `problem-of-other-minds` has 55 words of headroom. **Lead.** Read L114–120 in full before tasking: L120 may intend the functionalist reading.
