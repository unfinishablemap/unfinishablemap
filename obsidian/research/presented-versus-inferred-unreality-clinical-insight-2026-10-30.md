## Executive Summary

The question was whether the unreality that a patient with "insight" recognises is **presented** in the experience or **judged** from background knowledge. The literature answers it more cleanly than the five Map pages assume. **Clinical insight, as clinicians define and measure it, is a judgement**: David (1990) defines its third dimension as "the ability to relabel unusual mental events (delusions and hallucinations) as pathological", Teunisse et al. (1996) operationalise it as "insightful cognition", and ICD-10 calls it "an acceptance". What that judgement acts on is a separate, presented variable, the felt reality of the experience. Jaspers called it a "primary phenomenon"; Johnson calls reality "not given by experience, but by judgement processes" (both via Farkas 2013, raw preprint). The two vary independently and come apart in both directions:

- **Charles Bonnet syndrome, phantom limbs, lucid dreams: felt real, judged unreal.** In the only qualitative first-person CBS study found, patients say the images "felt real" and the authors state "The crux of insight is that visions look real, but patients use logic to recognise that they are not" (Fisher et al. 2026, raw). In a structured interview, 90% of ten CBS patients rated their complex hallucinations "as real" or "similar to" normal vision (Suzuki, Seth & Schwartzman 2023, raw). ffytche (2007, raw) places "visual hallucinations (with and without insight)" at the same point as veridical percepts on his phenomenological axes. Lucid insight "is separable from ... a change in the subjectively experienced realism of the dream" (Voss et al. 2013, raw).
- **Depersonalisation/derealisation: felt unreal, judged real.** The unreality is the experience and the reality is the judgement. The "as if" is the report-level mark of that judgement (Billon 2023 n. 5, verified in [[depersonalisation-2026-09-30]]; Radovic & Radovic 2002, raw abstract).
- **Kandinsky–Jaspers pseudohallucination: felt unreal *and* the content itself presented as subjective.** This is the one class in which the hallucinatory content itself lacks "objective presence (*Leibhaftigkeit*)" (López-Silva et al. 2022, raw). Its existence as a class is contested (Berrios & Dening 1996).

The word **"pseudohallucination" carried both registers for 150 years**. Telles-Correia et al. (2015, raw) sort its users into those who stress "lack of objectivity" (Kandinsky, Jaspers, Lugaro, Petit) and those who stress "presence of insight" (Bleuler, Sedman, Hare). ffytche (2007) marks both senses in one paper. The term was introduced by **Hagen (1868)**, not Jaspers.

**Verdict on CBS L86** ("Insight is a phenomenal fact: the hallucination presents as unreal"): **judged, not presented.** The sentence runs against the best available evidence. The 10-30 refine task's proposed conditional ("say which the evidence leaves open") is superseded: the evidence does not leave it open. It leaves open only that no study measures felt reality separately from insight in CBS. **For the Capgras/Cotard scope rule**, insight falls on the Factor-2 (judgement) side, so the rule stands. The presented reality-character falls on the Factor-1 (experience) side and is Map territory. Whether Cotard's dropped "as if" is a judgement failure or a change in what is presented stays open, as Cotard L56 already says. **Dualism is unaffected either way**, and no clinician cited here is enlisted for it. **Recommended action: `article`** (topics, 2,800–3,300 words). ## The Two Registers {#two-registers}

The distinction the wing needs is between two things the word "insight" runs together:

1. **Presented reality-character (felt reality).** A feature of how the experience itself presents: real-seeming, life-like, *leibhaftig*, or flat, unreal, picture-like. It has been described as a "primary phenomenon" (Jaspers), an affective or metacognitive feeling (Dokic & Martin 2015, *lead*), and the felt-real signal or reality-stamp (Map: [[perceptual-reality-monitoring-void]] L40/L64; [[phenomenal-quality-void]] L96).
2. **Judged reality-status (insight).** The subject's belief or acceptance that the experienced object is or is not real, formed from cues, background knowledge, testimony and coherence. Clinical insight, reality testing and reality monitoring at the personal level all name this.

Cross-tabulated, the four cells are occupied:

| | **Judged real** | **Judged unreal (insight)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Felt real** | ordinary perception; hallucination without insight | CBS (most cases); phantom limb; lucid dream; drug hallucinosis with insight |
| **Felt unreal** | depersonalisation / derealisation (felt unreality with intact reality testing) | Kandinsky–Jaspers pseudohallucination; ordinary imagery |

The table is this note's construction, but each cell is sourced below. Farkas (2013) is the closest prior treatment of the same distinction (see [[#farkas|Farkas]]). She sides with the judgement register for what she calls "a sense of reality", while conceding that hallucinations with insight may "carry an immediate sense of reality which, however, is overridden by other beliefs" ("a moot point").

### ffytche (2007), "Visual hallucinatory syndromes: past, present, and future", *Dialogues in Clinical Neuroscience* 9(2), 173–189
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.31887/DCNS.2007.9.2/dffytche (PMC3181850, full text). Already in the CBS page's references.
- **Key points** (raw):
  - The neurophenomenological classification has three axes (perceptual locus, agency, vividness): "Veridical percepts, visual hallucinations (with and without insight), visual illusions, and visual afterimages are all located externally, and are devoid of a sense of agency but vary in terms of their vividness." Insight is not one of the axes, and hallucinations with and without it sit together.
  - Two senses of the term, in one paper: specialised-cortex activity occurs "both when insight is present (pseudohallucinations in one sense of the term) and when it is not"; and "pseudohallucinations (in the sense of experiences occurring in the mind's eye) lack a sense of agency".
  - Current taxonomies separate experiences by "their relation to external objects and to insight, with little attention paid to their content".
  - History: Damas-Mora et al. abstracted features including "relation to insight"; Gold & Rabins (1989) set the criteria "used in the current psychiatric literature".
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral.

### Farkas (2013), "A sense of reality", in F. Macpherson & D. Platchias (eds), *Hallucination: Philosophy and Psychology*, MIT Press, pp. 399–416 {#farkas}
- **URL**: abstract at https://research.ceu.edu/en/publications/a-sense-of-reality/ (raw HTML). Full text from the PhilArchive preprint FARASO-3, via Wayback snapshot 2021-03-23. **Quoted wording is the preprint's; the printed chapter was not checked.** Page range: the preprint header and CEU give 399–416; other listings give 399–415 or 399–417.
- **Type**: philosophy chapter
- **Key points** (raw preprint):
  - Thesis: "My suggestion is that a sense of reality is created and sustained by the larger nexus of the subject's beliefs."
  - On CBS: "Past experiences, the reports of others, general considerations of coherence and so on, can subsequently convince one that certain experiences are hallucinatory. This happens in the case of most people with Charles Bonnet syndrome". Insight is preserved "often because it would be completely unlikely that the events depicted by the experience would occur". "On some occasions, the objects appear real on first experiencing them, but are subsequently discovered to be unreal, simply because the subject knows from independent sources that they don't exist". And: "if we considered these experiences in isolation from other mental states – as much as this is possible – the subject would take their objects to be real."
  - Declines the term: "Sometimes hallucinations with insight are called 'pseudohallucinations', but this term is also employed in another sense to denote vivid imagery".
  - Concession: whether such experiences "carry an immediate sense of reality which, however, is overridden by other beliefs? This is a moot point, and I shall not try to resolve it."
  - Sets the two registers against each other in two quotations. **Jaspers** (1959/1997, p. 94, via Farkas) calls the experience of reality "a primary phenomenon which can be conveyed only indirectly", and says perception has a quality that is "something absolutely primary and constitutes sensory reality". **Johnson** (1988, p. 57, via Farkas): "Reality is not given by experience, but by judgement processes." And in the same passage: "Reality testing of ongoing perception and reality monitoring of memories and beliefs are complex judgement processes that are subject to error and more difficult in some situations than others." Farkas sides with Johnson. (Farkas's own block quotations contain ellipses; quote the fragments above separately rather than splicing.)
  - Gives Jaspers's perception/image criteria (1959/1997, p. 69, via Farkas) and Aggernæs's (1972) seven "qualities" of experienced reality (sensation, behavioural relevance, publicness, objectivity, existence, involuntariness, independence). Several are defined as beliefs or feelings of certainty; for example, publicness obtains if the subject believes "that anybody else possessing normal sensory faculties would be able to perceive this something". Aggernæs's operationalisation therefore already mixes the two registers.
  - Quotes Bentall (1990: 82): "the illusion of reality ... is the sine qua non of all hallucinatory experiences".
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral. Farkas makes no metaphysical claim from this.

### Telles-Correia, Moreira & Gonçalves (2015), "Hallucinations and related concepts—their conceptual background", *Frontiers in Psychology* 6, 991
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00991 (PMC4515540, full text)
- **Key points** (raw):
  - "The term appears to have been introduced in 1868 by Hagen". Hagen's sense, as glossed there, covered sensory errors "experienced passively, unintentionally, with a lack of clarity and objectivity and maintaining awareness of their falseness". **Both registers appear in the originating usage.**
  - Figure 2 legend: "some authors emphasize the lack of objectivity as the main difference between hallucinations and pseudo hallucinations (Kandinsky, Jaspers, Lugaro, Petit) but others focus instead on the presence of insight (as Bleuler, Sedman, Hare)."

### David (1990), "Insight and psychosis", *British Journal of Psychiatry* 156, 798–808; Marková & Berrios (1992), "The meaning of insight in clinical psychiatry", *BJP* 160, 850–860
- **URLs**: https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.156.6.798 ; https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.160.6.850 (abstracts, raw)
- **Key points**: David: insight "is not an 'all-or-none' phenomenon" and has three dimensions, the third being "the ability to relabel unusual mental events (delusions and hallucinations) as pathological". Marková & Berrios: insight should be seen "as a continuum of thinking and feeling, affected by numerous internal and external variables".
- **Why it matters**: David gives the canonical judgement-register definition (*relabel*). ### Teunisse et al. (1996), *Lancet* 347, 794–797; Menon et al. (2003), *Survey of Ophthalmology* 48, 58–72; Pang (2016), *Optometry and Vision Science* 93(12), 1466–1478

### Voss, Schermelleh-Engel, Windt, Frenzel & Hobson (2013), "Measuring consciousness in dreams: the lucidity and consciousness in dreams scale", *Consciousness and Cognition* 22(1), 8–21
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2012.11.001 (abstract, raw)
- **Key point**: the LuCiD scale yields eight factors including INSIGHT and REALISM; "realism and negative emotion do not differentiate between lucid and non-lucid dreams, suggesting that lucid insight is separable from both bizarreness in dreams and a change in the subjectively experienced realism of the dream."

## Major Positions

### The judgement view of felt reality (Johnson; Farkas; Bentall; the clinical insight literature)
- **Core claim**: whether an experience is taken as real is the output of a judgement over its features and the subject's other beliefs. "A sense of reality" is downstream of that judgement.
- **Strongest evidence**: CBS insight accrues across episodes (Pang via Gilmour) and is delayed by context-appropriate content (Fisher). Knowledge of the diagnosis helps preserve it (Fisher). It can be lost when hallucinations are "amalgamated with his delusional belief systems" (single-case *BJPsych Open* 2025 abstract, Sebastian; conference abstract, weak).
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. Judgement processes are ordinary cognition on any metaphysics.

### The presented view (Jaspers; Ratcliffe; Dokic & Martin; the depersonalisation literature)
- **Core claim**: felt reality is a feature of experience, primitive and not reducible to judgement (Jaspers's "primary phenomenon"; Ratcliffe's experienced intentional-state type).
- **Strongest evidence**: depersonalisation, where the felt reality withdraws while the judgement stands. Cite DSM-5 Criterion A "experiences of unreality" with Criterion B intact reality testing ([[depersonalisation-nosology]] L39–45, criteria via secondary transcription there), and ICD-10 criterion (c). [[phenomenal-quality-void]] L96 puts "I know it's real but it doesn't feel real" in patients' mouths, but that page gives no source for the wording. Do not quote it as a patient report. Lucid dreams, where realism persists after insight (Voss).
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. A felt-reality variable is equally a predicted output of precision-weighting accounts (Limanowski & Friston 2018, as cited at [[phenomenal-quality-void]] L104/L128).

### Reconciliation (this note's reading; the article should state it as the Map's)
Both views are right about different things. Felt reality is presented, and depersonalisation proves it can vary without the judgement. Insight is the judgement, and CBS, phantom limbs and lucid dreams prove it can run against the felt reality. Farkas's thesis concerns what *makes* an experience feel real (her answer: the belief nexus), which is a third question the Map need not settle. Her own concession ("a moot point") leaves room for the two-variable reading.

## Key Debates

### Is CBS insight presented or inferred?
- **Sides**: Map CBS L86 (presented, unsourced; it originates in [[charles-bonnet-syndrome-generative-model-release-2026-09-16]] L178 as the Map's own synthesis) versus Fisher 2026, Suzuki 2023, ffytche 2007 and Farkas 2013 (images life-like, insight reached by logic, cues and knowledge).
- **Current state**: settled against L86 on the available evidence, with two limits. The evidence is self-report only. And no study separately measures *felt reality* and *insight* in CBS; Suzuki's measure is visual quality, and Fisher's is qualitative. A minority of CBS images are "distorted or cartoon-like" (Suzuki). Even there, the source describes a cue the judgement can use (implausibility), not an image that presents as unreal in Jaspers's sense. No source found describes CBS images as lacking *Leibhaftigkeit*.

### What is the "as if"?
- **Sides**: mark of mere appearance (Billon's majority reading), mark of approximation (Radovic & Radovic, as Billon reports them), and epistemic reservation (Radovic & Radovic abstract).
- **Bearing**: on every reading the "as if" belongs to the *report*. It encodes the speaker's judgement about a presented unreality. Losing the "as if" (Cotard) is therefore at least a change in judgement. Whether it is *also* a change in what is presented is the one-factor/two-factor dispute the Cotard page already carries (L52–56).

### Is pseudohallucination one concept or two?
- **Sides**: objectivity sense (Kandinsky, Jaspers, Lugaro, Petit) versus insight sense (Bleuler, Sedman, Hare), per Telles-Correia. Abolition (Berrios & Dening; Dening & Berrios). Rescue of the inner/outer phenomenology as data (López-Silva et al.). Two types (Turner 2014, *Psychopathology* 47(4): 270–273, abstract: relates Hare's and Jaspers's emphases to "abnormal experience and vivid imagination" and suggests "two types of pseudohallucinations"). Contested placement of Kandinsky (Engmann & Steinberg).
- **Current state**: no consensus. DSM-5 dropped the term (Telles-Correia).

## The Three Objects of Insight

This confirms the optimistic review's diagnosis with sources:
- **The change** (depersonalisation): insight that the alteration is subjective (ICD-10 criterion c). Presented unreality, judged reality.
- **The content** (CBS, hallucinosis): insight that the image is not there. Presented reality, judged unreality.
- **The deficit** (Anton): insight that one is blind. The deficit is never presented. Levine (1990), already quoted at Anton L60, holds that sensory loss "must be discovered by a process of self-observation and inference". So deficit-insight is judged by necessity, and there is no presented counterpart to lose.

Swartz & Brust's patient separates content-insight (kept: "readily acknowledged visual hallucinations") from deficit-insight (lost). The CBS patient keeps both. The Anton page's reading B patient keeps neither content-classification nor deficit-insight. CBS is therefore the right accurate-report control for Anton reading B: a seeming is present and correctly classified by judgement.

A **fourth sense** is in the corpus: [[phenomenal-quality-void]] L96 uses "insight" in Billon's (2024) metaphysical sense, depersonalisation as "seeing through the reality-stamp". That is neither clinical register. A one-clause clarification there would prevent cross-reading; it is optional.

## Where the Scope Rule Falls

The Capgras page (L71) limits the Map's territory to "the anomalous experience" (Factor 1), and Cotard (L48) inherits the rule. On the findings above:

- **Insight is judged**, so its presence or loss is a belief-evaluation fact, on the Factor-2 side. The scope rule stands as written, and the Map has no reason to claim insight-loss.
- **Felt reality is presented**, so a change in it is an experiential fact, on the Factor-1 side, and the Map's territory. Derealisation is the clean case. The Map's existing pages already treat it this way ([[phenomenal-quality-void]]; [[perceptual-reality-monitoring-void]] L64).
- **Cotard's dropped "as if"** is at least a change in the report-level judgement. Whether it is *also* a change in what is presented, an experience so global that a normal evaluator would endorse it, is the dispute between Billon's face-value reading and the revised two-factor account. Cotard L56 states this correctly and leaves it open, and nothing found here closes it. CBS L86 must not be read, or rewritten, as closing it.

## Gaps in Research

- **No study measures felt reality and insight separately in CBS.** A within-patient rating of how real the image looks or feels, taken alongside whether the patient believes it is there, would settle the presented question directly. Suzuki's measure is visual quality.
- **Every datum is self-report**, and CBS samples are filtered by an insight criterion.
- Primary texts **not read**: Hagen 1868; Kandinsky 1885; Jaspers *General Psychopathology* (quoted only via Farkas and López-Silva); Aggernæs 1972 (via Farkas); Johnson 1988 (via Farkas); Hare 1973 and Sedman 1966 (metadata only); Gilmour et al. 2009 full text; Ratcliffe 2017/2018 book; Dokic & Martin full text and abstract; Radovic & Radovic full text; Bleuler 1934; Docherty et al. ## Guards for the Article Writer

- **Do not write that CBS hallucinations "present as unreal."** Write that they are mostly reported as life-like and that insight is reached by inference.
- **Originator**: "pseudohallucination" was introduced by Hagen (1868), per Telles-Correia ("appears to have been") and López-Silva. Do not attribute the term to Jaspers. Jaspers *developed* the objectivity sense from Kandinsky.
- **Kandinsky's criterion is contested**: name both readings and do not choose.
- **The Kandinsky p. 134 translation** appears identically in two secondary sources. It is one carrier, not two.
- **Jaspers and Johnson quotations are via Farkas's preprint.** Mark them "quoted in Farkas 2013" and give Jaspers's English edition pages as Farkas does (1959/1997, pp. 69, 94).
- **Do not quote Telles-Correia's Hare gloss** (apparent slip, "as real").
- **Sedman 1966**: two papers. Cite neither by title unless resolved.
- **Suzuki's 90%** is n = 10 and a visual-quality rating. Do not call it a measure of felt reality.
- **Fisher's quotations** come from 17 participants who all had insight. Quote participants as participants.
- **Pang's 80/8/6%** is secondary (Gilmour full text unread). Cite as "reported by Pang (2016)".
- **Dokic & Martin and Ratcliffe's book**: paraphrase only, flagged. The two Ratcliffe & Wilkinson abstracts may be quoted.
- **Marková & Berrios** cut against an over-cognitive gloss of insight. Include them.
- **Voss**: factor-level result. Do not universalise.
- **Do not enlist clinicians for dualism.** None of Farkas, Johnson, ffytche, Fisher, Suzuki, Voss, Berrios or David makes a claim about dualism, and the article must not imply one.
- **"I know it's real but it doesn't feel real"** ([[phenomenal-quality-void]] L96) is unsourced Map wording. Do not present it as a quoted patient report.
- **Avoid** the "This is not X. It is Y." construct, and do not use "load-bearing" as an intensifier.

## Relation to Site Perspective (for the article)

**Dualism (Tenet 1)**: unaffected either way. If felt reality is presented, physicalist models supply it as a variable (precision-weighting; metacognitive feeling). If insight is judged, it is ordinary cognition. The hard problem applies to the felt-real signal exactly as to any other phenomenal quality, and this subject adds nothing to it. The article should say so in the lead.
**Bidirectional Interaction (Tenet 3)**: nothing. Insight is a belief-forming process on every account, and its causal role in behaviour (touching the "grass" to check, reality-testing by reaching) is equally available to any metaphysics.
**Occam's Razor Has Limits (Tenet 5)**: binds the Map's own tidiness in both directions. CBS L86 chose the presented register for one syndrome without a source, so the simpler single-register story was adopted by assertion. Equally, Farkas's single-register (judgement) account should not be adopted for its economy where depersonalisation shows a presented variable.
**Minimal Quantum Interaction (Tenet 2), No Many Worlds (Tenet 4)**: not invoked.
