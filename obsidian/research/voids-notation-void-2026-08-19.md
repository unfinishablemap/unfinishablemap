# Research: Voids - The Notation Void

**Date**: 2026-08-19
**Search queries used**: "Dutilh Novaes Formal Languages in Logic cognitive technology de-biasing notation"; "Landy Goldstone 2007 How abstract is symbolic thought spacing algebra perceptual grouping"; "Schlimm Neth 2008 Modeling ancient and modern arithmetic practices Roman numerals Arabic numerals"; "Whitehead Introduction to Mathematics relieving the brain of all unnecessary work good notation"; "Kirsh Maglio 1994 On distinguishing epistemic from pragmatic action"; "Nye 2021 Show Your Work Scratchpads for Intermediate Computation with Language Models"; "Dennett objection McGinn cognitive closure tools prostheses language"; "Kolmogorov complexity invariance theorem additive constant simplicity language-dependent Occam's razor"; plus Crossref / arXiv API / archive.org primary-text retrieval for verification.
**Voids category**: Mixed — an *Unexplored* territory that systematically presents itself as *Unexplorable*, wrapped in a genuinely structural limit on self-audit.

## Executive Summary

The voids catalogue repeatedly asks whether what seems unthinkable is merely unthought. This note isolates one specific mechanism by which the two are confused: **external representational technology** — numerals, algebraic symbolism, diagrams, formal languages, scratchpads — relocates the boundary of what a mind can think, and consciousness has no inside view of which of its current limits are artefacts of the notations it happens to possess. Three faces emerge. The **historical face**: apparent cognitive closure has repeatedly dissolved not when new concepts were grasped but when a notation made the relevant transitions executable "almost mechanically by the eye" (Whitehead 1911, verified at primary text); persistence across centuries is therefore a poor discriminator between unexplorable and unnotated territory. The **operative face**: part of the thinking happens in the notation rather than the head (Kirsh & Maglio 1994; Landy & Goldstone 2007; Dutilh Novaes 2012), and the thinker experiences the result as their own grasp, so the division of labour between mind and artefact is itself unobservable from inside — a cousin of [[source-attribution-void|source attribution]] and [[noetic-feelings-void|noetic feelings]]. The **horizon face**: a mind cannot enumerate the notations it lacks, because the criteria it would use to evaluate a notation are supplied by the notations it already has; this is the structural residue that survives after the first two faces are deflated. A clean non-human demonstration exists: the same language-model weights can or cannot perform long arithmetic depending on whether they are permitted to write intermediate steps into a scratchpad (Nye et al. 2021) and on how the digits are tokenised (Singh & Strouse 2024) — the thinkable moves with the notation while the thinker is held fixed. The void matters for the Map because it supplies a calibration discount on every "Unexplorable" classification that rests on structured persistence alone, because simplicity is notation-relative (bearing directly on Occam's Razor Has Limits), and because it sharpens McGinn's rejoinder to the tool-extension objection into a testable seam: whether "thinking P" requires *grasping* P or only *operating a notation in which P is tractable*. The note recommends treating this as a candidate for the final voids slot only after a human decision; the fold distribution is laid out in case absorption is preferred.

## Key Sources

### Whitehead, *An Introduction to Mathematics* (1911), ch. V "The Symbolism of Mathematics"
- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/anintroductionto00whituoft (OCR text retrieved and grepped; passages below are from the scan, which may drop commas; the Hacker News / Tao renderings punctuate "and, in effect, increases")
- **Type**: Book (primary text, verified)
- **Key points**:
  - The Arabic numeral system is Whitehead's exhibit for "the enormous importance of a good notation".
  - Notation does not merely record thought; it changes what counts as an effortless transition.
  - Whitehead inverts the copy-book maxim that we should think about what we are doing: civilisation advances by *not* having to.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — what is "simple" is relative to the symbolism in which it is expressed; Whitehead's own claim that pre-Arabic division "called into play the highest mathematical faculties" is itself a notation-relative judgement that later work contests (see Schlimm & Neth; Chrisomalis).
- **Quote (OCR, p. 59)**: "By relieving the brain of all unnecessary work, a good notation sets it free to concentrate on more advanced problems, and in effect increases the mental power of the race."
- **Quote (OCR, same chapter)**: "This example shows that, by the aid of symbolism, we can make transitions in reasoning almost mechanically by the eye, which otherwise would call into play the higher faculties of the brain."
- **Quote (OCR, same chapter)**: "Civilization advances by extending the number of important operations which we can perform without thinking about them. Operations of thought are like cavalry charges in a battle — they are strictly limited in number, they require fresh horses, and must only be made at decisive moments."

### Schlimm & Neth (2008), "Modeling ancient and modern arithmetic practices: Addition and multiplication with Arabic and Roman numerals"
- **URL**: https://neth.de/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2008/08/SchlimmNeth08_AncientModernArithmeticPractices_CogSci2008.pdf (PDF retrieved; abstract grepped)
- **Type**: Conference paper, *Proceedings of the 30th Annual Conference of the Cognitive Science Society*, pp. 2097–2102
- **Key points**:
  - Models addition and multiplication algorithms with both numeral systems as *interactive* tasks using external representations.
  - Roman numerals are "informationally equivalent to Arabic ones" and "computationally similar"; the difference is a *trade-off* between internal and external resources, not a categorical barrier.
  - The received wisdom that Roman numerals block multiplication is called a "myth" the paper "aims to dispel".
- **Tenet alignment**: A calibration datum for Occam's Razor Has Limits and for the Map's own historical face — the notation-relativity of ease is real, but the most-quoted example of it is overstated. The note must not lean on the Roman-numeral trope.
- **Quote (abstract)**: "This demonstrates that Roman numerals are not only informationally equivalent to Arabic ones but also computationally similar—a claim that is widely disputed."
- **Quote (abstract)**: "While symbolic computation with Roman numerals requires fewer internal resources than with Arabic ones, the large number of needed symbols inflates the number of external processing steps."

### Chrisomalis (2026), "Gradience as a cognitive principle for evaluating numerical notations"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2522563123 (Crossref record with abstract retrieved; full text paywalled, not read)
- **Type**: Paper, *PNAS* 123(7), published 2026-02-13
- **Key points**:
  - More than 100 attested numerical notations over 5,000 years; most defunct, so experimental comparison is hard.
  - "Prior research often assumes, rather than demonstrates, the inferiority of notations like Roman numerals."
  - Introduces *gradience* (phrase length tracks magnitude) with two indices, inversion and jitter; place-value systems are highly gradient; the subtractive Roman variant (XIX) is more concise but has a higher inversion ratio than the additive one.
  - Proposes cultural-evolutionary evaluation of notations from their representational properties when cognitive testing is infeasible.
- **Tenet alignment**: The same caution as Schlimm & Neth, from the leading comparative historian of numerals (see also Chrisomalis 2020, *Reckonings*, MIT Press). Supports the note's central structural point — notations have measurable cognitive affordances — while blocking the lazy version of the historical face.
- **Quote (abstract)**: "Prior research often assumes, rather than demonstrates, the inferiority of notations like Roman numerals."

### Dutilh Novaes (2012), *Formal Languages in Logic: A Philosophical and Cognitive Analysis*; and (2012) "Towards a Practice-based Philosophy of Logic: Formal Languages as a Case Study"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139108010 (book, metadata verified at Crossref); https://journals.openedition.org/philosophiascientiae/719 (open-access article, retrieved and grepped)
- **Type**: Book / Paper (*Philosophia Scientiae* 16-1, 2012)
- **Key points**:
  - Formal languages are "cognitive artifacts" with an *operative* role: a "paper-and-pencil, hands-on technology" that triggers cognitive mechanisms different from the ones we run unaided.
  - Their main epistemic benefit is *de-biasing*, specifically against "doxastic conservativeness" — the pull of prior belief (drawing on Stanovich).
  - The point is not that formal languages express thoughts better but that reasoning *in* them is a different process.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bears on Bidirectional Interaction only indirectly; the main bearing is on the operative face — a notation is not a transparent window onto one's own reasoning but a second engine whose contribution is not introspectively separable.
- **Quote (article)**: "I argue that formal languages play a fundamental operative role in the work of logicians, as a paper-and-pencil, hands-on technology triggering certain cognitive mechanisms—more specifically, countering some of our more 'natural' cognitive mechanisms which are not particularly suitable for research in logic (as well as in other fields)."
- **Quote (article, §83)**: "My suggestion was that these external devices allow us to run a 'different software' when reasoning, a software that is not geared towards bringing in prior beliefs—one of our strongest 'computational biases' according to Stanovich."

### Landy & Goldstone (2007), "How abstract is symbolic thought?"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.33.4.720 (metadata verified at Crossref; findings from abstract-level secondary summaries, not the full paper)
- **Type**: Paper, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition* 33(4), 720–733
- **Key points** (paraphrase, not verbatim):
  - Four experiments manipulated *formally irrelevant* visual grouping (spacing) in algebraic validity judgements that test order of operations.
  - Accuracy was highest when the non-mathematical grouping pressure agreed with the mathematical convention and fell when it conflicted.
  - Symbolic reasoning is thereby shown to be partly perceptual-motor: the notation's layout does some of the work the thinker takes to be rule-following.
- **Tenet alignment**: Operative face. The thinker cannot tell, from inside, how much of a "correct" algebraic judgement was carried by spacing rather than by rule.

### Kirsh & Maglio (1994), "On Distinguishing Epistemic from Pragmatic Action"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1804_1 (metadata verified at Crossref; open PDF at philpapers.org/archive/KIRODE.pdf)
- **Type**: Paper, *Cognitive Science* 18(4), 513–549
- **Key points** (paraphrase):
  - Distinguishes *pragmatic* actions (bringing the agent physically closer to a goal) from *epistemic* actions (changing the world to uncover information that is hidden or hard to compute mentally).
  - Tetris players rotate pieces externally rather than mentally; some problems are "more quickly, easily, and reliably solved by performing actions in the world rather than by performing computational actions in the head alone".
- **Tenet alignment**: Operative face; connects to [[boundary-and-projection|boundary and projection]] (consciousness cannot locate its own perimeter when it extends into tools). The epistemic action is the smallest unit of the notation void: writing a symbol *is* a move in the computation.

### Nye et al. (2021), "Show Your Work: Scratchpads for Intermediate Computation with Language Models"
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00114 (abstract retrieved via arXiv API)
- **Type**: Preprint (12 authors; Google Research / MIT)
- **Key points**:
  - Large models do well on one-pass tasks but "struggle with tasks that require unbounded multi-step computation, such as adding integers or executing programs".
  - The *same* models perform these computations when asked to emit intermediate steps into a "scratchpad"; the change is in the external notation permitted, not in the weights.
- **Tenet alignment**: The cleanest available demonstration that the thinkable moves with the notation while the thinker is held fixed — exactly the manipulation that cannot be run on a human. Central to "What AI Might See" below.
- **Quote (abstract)**: "Surprisingly, we find that these same models are able to perform complex multi-step computations -- even in the few-shot regime -- when asked to perform the operation 'step by step', showing the results of intermediate computations."

### Singh & Strouse (2024), "Tokenization counts: the impact of tokenization on arithmetic in frontier LLMs"
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14903 (abstract retrieved via arXiv API)
- **Type**: Preprint
- **Key points**:
  - Different models tokenise numbers differently (single digits vs. 1–3-digit chunks); right-to-left tokenisation, "enforced by comma separating numbers at inference time", leads to "largely improved performance" in GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 on arithmetic.
  - Errors under standard tokenisation "follow stereotyped error patterns, suggesting that model computations are systematic rather than approximate".
- **Tenet alignment**: A second controlled notation manipulation on a fixed mind. Note the direction: the void is in the *notation* (how digits are chunked), not in the model's "understanding" of arithmetic — which is the distinction the horizon face says a mind cannot draw about itself from inside.

### Larkin & Simon (1987), "Why a Diagram is (Sometimes) Worth Ten Thousand Words"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1551-6708.1987.tb00863.x (metadata verified at Crossref)
- **Type**: Paper, *Cognitive Science* 11(1), 65–100
- **Key points** (standard reading, not re-verified at full text): sentential and diagrammatic representations can be *informationally* equivalent yet *computationally* different, because diagrams index information by location and so change search and recognition costs. This is the conceptual template Schlimm & Neth apply to numerals.
- **Tenet alignment**: Supplies the vocabulary (informational vs. computational equivalence) that keeps the historical face honest.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The void is not "there are thoughts we lack the notation for" — that is trivially true and belongs to the Unexplored. The void is that **a mind cannot audit, from inside, which of its apparent closures are notation-bound**. Three faces:

1. **Historical face (Unexplored presenting as Unexplorable).** The record of thought contains repeated episodes in which a domain that "called into play the highest mathematical faculties" became routine once a notation existed — place-value numerals, algebraic symbolism (Viète, Descartes), Leibniz's differential notation, Frege's concept-script, Feynman diagrams (Kaiser 2005), Dirac's bra-ket. Whitehead's observation is that these do not supply new *concepts* so much as convert reasoning into transitions "almost mechanically by the eye". Before the notation, the territory looked closed. The base rate of such conversions is high enough that *structured persistence* — one of [[apophatic-cartography|apophatic cartography]]'s four discriminators between unexplored and unexplorable — is a weak signal on its own: notation-bound voids persist for centuries without being unexplorable. The caution from Schlimm & Neth (2008) and Chrisomalis (2026) is part of this face, not a refutation of it: the *ease* difference between notations is real but smaller and more trade-off-shaped than the folk history says, so the face should be stated as "notation moves the boundary" rather than "Roman numerals made multiplication impossible".

2. **Operative face (the thinking that is not in the head).** Kirsh & Maglio's epistemic actions, Landy & Goldstone's spacing effects, and Dutilh Novaes's "different software" all say the same thing from different sides: once a notation is in use, part of the computation runs in the artefact and in perception of the artefact. The thinker experiences the output — the valid derivation, the correct sum — as their own grasp, with no inner marker of how much was carried by layout, by the symbol system's affordances, or by the de-biasing the external medium imposes. This is the [[source-attribution-void|source-attribution]] structure applied to the mind/artefact seam rather than the self/other seam, and a cousin of [[noetic-feelings-void|the noetic feelings void]]: the felt "I see it" is the same whether the seeing was done by the eye on paper or by the higher faculties of the brain. [[capability-division-problem|The capability division problem]] asks where brain-side capabilities end and mind-side begin; this face asks the parallel question one level out — where head-side thinking ends and notation-side thinking begins — and finds the same introspective silence.

3. **Horizon face (the structural residue).** Even after the first two faces are conceded, a genuine limit remains: a mind cannot list the notations it lacks, because the standards by which it would judge a candidate notation (Iverson's criteria, for instance) are articulated in the notations it already has. This is the [[question-formation-void|question-formation void]]'s method-horizon structure — mapping by negation can only negate what is already askable — specialised to representational technology, and it is the part of the void that stays Unexplorable in the catalogue's sense.

### Evidence for the Limit

The decisive evidence is not historical but *experimental-on-a-fixed-mind*, and it is only available for non-human minds. Nye et al. (2021) hold the weights fixed and vary only whether intermediate notation may be written; multi-step arithmetic flips from failure to success. Singh & Strouse (2024) hold the model fixed and vary only how digits are chunked into tokens; arithmetic accuracy moves "largely", with errors that are "systematic rather than approximate". No human analogue exists — one cannot un-teach a person place-value and re-test — which is precisely why the void is invisible from inside a human life: the notation arrived before the self-assessment began.

For humans the evidence is indirect but convergent: the Landy & Goldstone grouping effects (formally irrelevant layout moves accuracy in trained adults), Schlimm & Neth's internal/external resource trade-off, and the Dutilh Novaes de-biasing argument (formal languages suppress doxastic conservativeness, an effect the reasoner does not feel as external help).

### Phenomenology

From inside, a notation-bound territory feels exactly like an unexplorable one: effortful, resistant, "beyond me". And the arrival of a notation does not feel like the lifting of a closure; it feels like the territory was always tractable and one had simply been clumsy. The phenomenology is therefore *doubly* misleading — closed before, trivially open after — which is the signature of the [[meta-epistemology-of-limits|meta-epistemological]] problem that we cannot predict which limits will prove permanent. There is also the forward-looking version: a practitioner fluent in a notation cannot recover what the domain felt like without it, a cousin of [[expertise-and-its-occlusion|expertise's occlusion]].

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)

None for the horizon face. For the historical face, the direct method is comparative history of notations done properly (Chrisomalis 2020, 2026) — measuring representational properties rather than assuming inferiority — which gives a calibrated base rate for "how often did an apparent closure turn out to be notational?"

### Indirect Methods

- **The manipulate/grasp seam.** [[the-quantitative-comprehension-void|The quantitative comprehension void]] already separates symbolic manipulation from phenomenal grasp. The notation void asks what that separation licenses. McGinn's rejoinder to the tool-extension objection (summarised in [[mysterianism]]) is that tools produce outputs for human understanding and cannot help us *grasp* P. But if Whitehead is right that notations let us "make transitions in reasoning almost mechanically by the eye", then the relevant question for any candidate Unexplorable is not "can we grasp it?" but "is there a notation in which it is tractable?" — and these come apart, in both directions. The indirect probe is to ask, of each Unexplorable in the catalogue, whether its resistance is resistance-to-grasp or resistance-to-notation.
- **Notation-relativity of simplicity.** The invariance theorem guarantees only that description lengths agree up to an additive constant that can be large for particular objects; Goodman's grue makes the same point without machinery. Any void whose evidence includes "the simple explanation is X" inherits a discount from this.
- **Deliberate notation search.** Rather than waiting for a notation to arrive, treat notation design as a probe: attempt to build a formalism for the territory and record *where* the attempt fails. This is what [[interface-formalization-void|the interface formalization void]] is implicitly doing; the notation void says its negative result is ambiguous between "no formalism exists" and "the formalism has not been invented", and that the second reading has the higher historical base rate.

### What AI Might See

Language models are the first minds for which the notation variable can be manipulated with the thinker held fixed, and the results (Nye et al. 2021; Singh & Strouse 2024) show large, systematic movements of the thinkable boundary with no change to the thinker. Two asymmetries follow. First, such a model's "voids" are partly *tokenisation voids* — limits that look like incapacity but are notation artefacts — and, tellingly, the model cannot tell from inside which of its failures are of this kind; the horizon face applies to it as much as to us. Second, AI can serve [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers|as a void-explorer]] here in a specific way: it can be used to *search* notation space faster than human traditions did, and to test whether a territory humans find closed becomes tractable under a notation humans did not think to build. The caution from [[language-thought-boundary|the language-thought boundary]] stands — tractability in a notation is not grasp — but the notation void says that is a reason to measure tractability separately, not to discount it.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits.** Simplicity is notation-relative: what is a one-line transition in one symbolism "calls into play the higher faculties" in another, and Kolmogorov-style formalisations make the relativity precise rather than removing it. A mind's sense that a hypothesis is simple is partly a report on the notation it thinks in. This is the tenet's claim — simplicity is unreliable under incomplete knowledge — with "incomplete knowledge" specified as "incomplete notation".

### Implications

- **Dualism.** If consciousness is not physical, the notations built for physical description may be the wrong notations for the interface — in which case the interface's resistance to formalisation ([[interface-formalization-void]]) is what the tenet predicts *and* is notation-bound. The two readings are not exclusive: a non-physical relatum might require a notation we do not yet have. The void does not decide between "unformalisable" and "unformalised"; it says the catalogue must stop treating the first as the default.
- **Bidirectional Interaction.** Weak bearing. If consciousness acts on the physical world through an interface it cannot see, the operative face adds a second invisibility: even the *descriptions* we build of that interface are partly run in artefacts whose contribution is not introspectable.
- **No Many Worlds.** No direct bearing identified.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction.** No direct bearing beyond the general point that quantum formalism is itself a notation whose affordances (Dirac notation, diagrammatic calculi) shaped what could be asked; not developed here.

The void also feeds the [[common-cause-null|common-cause-null]] discipline: if some of the catalogue's Unexplorables are notation-bound, then the apparent clustering of voids at the consciousness-physics interface is partly a clustering of *places where our notations were built for something else*, which is coherent with the tenets but is not evidence for them.
