## Executive Summary

1. **Ciaunica, Ayache, Haggard, Nakul, Bonnet & Auvray (2024)**, *Scientific Reports* 14: 15396, DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-65862-z. Online intentional-binding study, non-clinical, French CDS-29 prescreen of 868, final N = 93 (HIGH CDS > 50, n = 46; LOW CDS < 20, n = 47). Abstract: "The results did not reveal significant differences between individuals with low and high occurrences of DP experiences on the implicit and explicit sense of agency." Discussion: "Against our initial hypothesis, no significant differences were found ... both for explicit and implicit sense of agency." The only group effect on the binding index (β = −55.71, p = 0.034) "was no longer significant after Holm Bonferroni corrections (p = 0.80)", and it ran the wrong way for Gemini's reading: "The HIGH DP group (mean = 10.38 ± 114.59) displayed overall higher intentional binding than the LOW DP group (mean = 7.63 ± 119.76)." What survives is interval-dependent: a steeper explicit-agency slope in HIGH DP (interaction F(4, 364) = 3.39, p = 0.010; overall slope difference n.s., p = 0.189), greater baseline temporal distortion at short intervals, and an implicit–explicit correlation present in the whole sample (r = 0.23, p = 0.025) but not in HIGH DP (r = 0.15, p = 0.323). The authors' own gloss is "a discrepancy between implicit and explicit sense of agency in people with high levels of depersonalisation", and their summary sentence is "depersonalisation traits are not directly associated with changes in the implicit and explicit sense of agency, but they seem to be interval-dependent".

2. **Woźniak, McEllin, Hohwy & Ciaunica (2023)**, *JEP: Human Perception and Performance* 49(11): 1447–1459, DOI 10.1037/xhp0001153. Preregistered (AsPredicted vq78u), Prolific, CDS-29 screen of 693, N = 180 (LOW ≤ 20, mean 9.6; HIGH ≥ 50, mean 101.1). Both groups showed standard self-prioritisation for own name and for self-associated shapes. For self-associated **avatar faces** the HIGH group made fewer errors overall (p = 0.024) and in reaction times prioritised self over friend (p = 0.008, BF10 = 9.3) but not over stranger (p = 0.69, BF01 = 4.2). Gemini's summary ("failing to show the normal cognitive facilitation when processing stimuli associated with their own physical body") is half right: the self-over-stranger advantage is absent, but the stimuli are arbitrarily self-associated avatars, not the participant's own body, and the HIGH group processed faces *better*, not worse. The authors offer two readings — social anxiety making strangers salient, or high-DP participants categorising "self in a similar way to how they categorize a stranger" for bodily stimuli — and found no correlation between explicit self-identification and any prioritisation measure.

3. **Lane (2020)**, "The minimal self hypothesis", *Consciousness and Cognition* 85: 103029, DOI 10.1016/j.concog.2020.103029. **Zero occurrences of "depersonal" in the full text.** The paper is about thought insertion, unresponsive wakefulness syndrome, NREM sleep and anaesthesia, and it *defends* the minimal-self hypothesis as an empirical conjecture ("MS might be a necessary precondition for conscious experience"), the opposite of Gemini's "definitive critique". The perspectival/personal ownership distinction appears once, in Lane's exposition of Henriksen, Parnas & Zahavi (2019, *Consciousness and Cognition* 74: 102770 — a different paper from the Henriksen & Parnas 2019 *Thaumàzein* article already in the corpus), and Lane endorses it in his closing extrapolation: "inserted thoughts exhibit for-me-ness, understood as perspectival ownership." Albahari, the coiner of the pair, is not cited by Lane (0 hits).

4. **Answers**: (1) implicit binding is **not** shown disturbed; the installed wording is not too generous and needs no correction, only the record of the null on the agency article. (2) Woźniak et al. support a *differentiated* alteration consistent with the localist reading's structure and with the content-not-fixed recalibration's direction, but they measure a processing bias, not a phenomenal feature, and the direction is loss of a self-over-stranger advantage rather than a bodily deficit; the hub should not take them (at cap, and the support is weak). (3) Lane 2020 does draw the perspectival/personal distinction but for thought insertion, from Henriksen, Parnas & Zahavi 2019, with no depersonalisation content; the hub's universalist paragraph should **not** name him. His proper home is `concepts/thought-insertion` L58, where the Map quotes Lane 2012 denying for-me-ness and can now record the later, softer Lane.

### Ciaunica, Ayache, Haggard, Nakul, Bonnet & Auvray (2024), "Explicit and implicit sense of agency in depersonalisation experiences"

Ciaunica and Ayache contributed equally. Competing interests: "The authors declare no competing interests." Data: OSF (`osf.io/vf9hw`, view-only link in the paper). - **Verification route**: Crossref by DOI (title, authors, volume, article number, date); PubMed `efetch` abstract; Europe PMC `fullTextXML` for the body. All quotations below grep-verified in the XML-derived text.
- **Design** (raw): "868 participants (mean age = 31.32 ± 8.78, 386 men, 472 women, 10 non-binary) were first prescreened by completing a French version of the Cambridge Depersonalisation Scale (CDS-29)". Hypothesis groups: "participants with higher occurrences of DP experiences (CDS scores > 50) ... compared to participants with lower occurrences of DP experiences (CDS scores < 20)". "our final sample consisted of 93 participants divided in HIGH DP (N = 46, mean age = 26.91 ± 5.76 years, 5 men, 41 women) and LOW DP (N = 47, mean age = 33.89 ± 8.14 years, 25 men, 22 women)". "A sensitivity power analysis confirmed that this sample size was sufficient to detect a small effect size (f2 = 0.03)." No preregistration statement (0 hits for "preregist").
- **Hypotheses** (raw): "Considering that DP is commonly linked to feelings of agency loss and to feelings of performing actions on 'automatic pilot', we hypothesised that participants with higher occurrences of DP experiences ... will show lower sense of explicit agency"; "we expected participants with higher occurrences of DP experiences to display a 'lower' intentional binding effect." (The "automatic pilot" source is reference 4, Perkins 2021 *Life on Autopilot*, a self-help guide, not a clinical study.)
- **Results on implicit agency** (raw): "There was indeed no significant difference between HIGH and LOW DP groups (F(1, 89) = 2.93, p = 0.090 and R2 = 0.89) when considering the overall coefficient slopes of intentional binding". However, this difference was no longer significant after Holm Bonferroni corrections (p = 0.80)." Binding itself appeared only at ≥ 500 ms: "the expected temporal compression characterizing the intentional binding effect occurred only for interval durations superior or equal to 500 ms."
- **Results on explicit agency** (raw): "an interaction was observed between interval durations and DP groups (F(4, 364) = 3.39, p = 0.010)"; "Despite a trend for participants with HIGH CDS scores ... to display a steeper coefficient slope ... there was no significant difference between HIGH and LOW DP groups (F(1, 89) = 3.90, beta = 1.05, p = 0.189 ...)". Facet analysis: only "Alienation from Surroundings" predicted steeper explicit slopes (β = 0.17, p = 0.034).
- **Time perception** (raw): "There were no direct effects of DP groups on temporal estimations (beta = 26.07, p = 0.222 ...), but there was an interaction with the 900 ms interval duration"; "Exploratory analyses revealed that this effect was driven by significant differences in temporal distortions ... between HIGH (100.71 ± 87.60) and LOW DP (85.50 ± 73.65) in the BASELINE condition (t(463) = 2.02, p = 0.044 ...)". This exploratory baseline effect is the whole of Gemini's "severe temporal distortions for short intervals in the absence of self-initiated motion".
- **Implicit–explicit decoupling** (raw): "There was a positive correlation between coefficient slopes associated with explicit and implicit agency (r = 0.23, p = 0.025 ...). However, this association displayed a trend for significance in the LOW DP group (r = 0.25, p = 0.096 ...), but not in the HIGH DP group (r = 0.15, p = 0.323 ...)". Authors' reading: "Although typically couched as 'losing' one's sense of self or sense of agency, depersonalisation symptoms, may be linked, on the contrary, to an inability to attenuate self-related inputs and hence to 'forget' the self in the background. Alterations in the ability to attenuate self-related information may further increase reflexivity or 'hyper-reflexivity', which may explain the discrepancy" (citing Ciaunica et al. 2022 and Sass & Parnas 2003).
- **Author's own summary** (raw): "Taken together, the main findings of this study show that depersonalisation traits are not directly associated with changes in the implicit and explicit sense of agency, but they seem to be interval-dependent". Conclusion: "Contrary to other conditions associated with altered sense of self such as schizophrenia, our study did not reveal significant differences between individuals reporting high and low DP experiences on overall indices of explicit and implicit sense of agency."

### Woźniak, McEllin, Hohwy & Ciaunica (2023), "Depersonalization affects self-prioritization of bodily, but not abstract self-related information"
- **Citation (resolved)**: *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance* 49(11): 1447–1459, November 2023. DOI 10.1037/xhp0001153. PMID 37870823. Crossref carries no abstract; PubMed does. No PMC copy. Unpaywall lists an OSF/PsyArXiv author manuscript (`osf.io/gsfy3`) and a Jagiellonian repository copy (`ruj.uj.edu.pl/handle/item/573743`).
- **Verification route**: Crossref by DOI; PubMed `efetch` abstract; OSF author manuscript (38 pp., title and author list match) read in full. Quotations below are from the manuscript; where the published pagination matters, cite the abstract.
- **Design** (raw): "We set our expected sample size to be 200 participants as preregistered under the following link: https://aspredicted.org/vq78u.pdf"; "693 participants completed the Cambridge Depersonalization Scale (CDS-29 ...)"; "eligible for the Low DP group if they scored 20 or lower on CDS-29 ...; and for the High DP group if they scored 50 of higher". Final groups: "low depersonalization group scored on average M=9.6 points on the CDS-29 (SD=6.2, range: 1-20), while participants with high depersonalization scored on average M=101.1 points on the CDS (SD=24.3, range=54-186)". N = 180 (abstract). Stimuli: "(a) one's own name, (b) arbitrarily self-associated abstract stimuli, (i.e. geometrical shapes), and (c) arbitrarily self-associated bodily stimuli, (i.e. avatar faces)"; faces were Basel Face Model avatars, gender randomly assigned. Confirmatory hypothesis: "we hypothesized that DP would influence self-prioritization of bodily self-associated stimuli (avatar faces) to a greater extent than abstract self-associated stimuli (geometrical shapes)."
- **Results** (abstract, verbatim): "We found that both groups demonstrated the standard pattern of results for self-prioritization of one's name and geometrical shapes, but they differed with regard to avatar faces. While people with low depersonalization showed the standard prioritization of avatar faces, people with high depersonalization showed overall better response accuracy with avatar faces, and faster response times for stranger-associated avatar faces." Manuscript detail: accuracy — "participants with high depersonalization made significantly less mistakes in the task with avatar faces than participants with low depersonalization (p=0.024)"; reaction times — "while their responses were faster following self- than friend-associated avatar faces (p=0.008, BF10=9.3), neither the difference between self and stranger (p=0.69, BF01=4.2) nor between friend and stranger (p=0.14, BF01=1.2) was significant." Self-identification: "Participants in both groups indicated that they more strongly self-identified with geometrical shapes than with avatar faces, while the difference between groups was not significant"; and (abstract) "finding no correlation between explicit reports of self-identification with self-associated avatar faces and geometrical shapes, self-prioritization of these stimuli, and prioritization of one's name." Cross-gender avatar preference correlated with CDS total (τB = 0.17, p = .002, BF10 = 31.3).
- **Authors' interpretations** (raw): "the cognitive mechanism underlying this effect must be related to processing of body-related (specifically facial) stimuli"; "An alternative interpretation of these results is that the experience of self-detachment in depersonalization may lead to a 'blurring' of self and other"; "our pattern of results suggests that stranger-associated faces carried increased motivational saliency among high-depersonalization individuals" — explained either by social anxiety or by the possibility that "participants represent themselves literally as 'strangers to themselves', that is, they categorize self in a similar way to how they categorize a stranger", "driven by imprecisions in embodied self-related signalling". Self-described as "this still speculative interpretation". Limitation named by the authors: future work should test "both in the avatar and the participants' real faces conditions".
- **What "bodily" and "abstract" mean here**: bodily = a face avatar arbitrarily paired with "self" minutes earlier; abstract = own name plus an arbitrarily paired shape. Gemini's table glossing these as "somatic mapping, interoceptive integration, physical boundaries" versus "narrative identity ... semantic self-association" is the reviewer's construction, not the paper's.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral; the authors frame the result in bodily-signal-precision terms (an active-inference idiom). It bears on the architecture tier only, and on it weakly (below).

### Lane (2020), "The minimal self hypothesis"
- **Citation (resolved)**: *Consciousness and Cognition* 85: 103029, October 2020 (Epub 19 October 2020). DOI 10.1016/j.concog.2020.103029. PMID 33091792. Author: Timothy Joseph Lane (Taipei Medical University; Academia Sinica). Keywords (PDF metadata): "Minimal self, Thought insertion, Unresponsive wakefulness syndrome, NREM sleep, General anesthesia, Levels of consciousness, Neuroimaging, Default mode network".
- **Verification route**: Crossref by DOI; PubMed `efetch` abstract; publisher-typeset PDF via Wayback copy of the PhilArchive deposit (Unpaywall lists `philpapers.org/archive/LANTMS-2.pdf`; direct fetch 403). Full text extracted and grepped.
- **Control counts in the full text**: "depersonal" 0; "Cotard" 0; "sense of ownership" 0; "Albahari" 0; "Billon" 0; "perspectival" 3; "personal ownership" 2; "thought insertion" many; "Zahavi" 13; "for-me-ness" 30.
- **Thesis** (abstract, verbatim): "These probes, as well as other experimental protocols applied to NREM Sleep, General Anesthesia (GA), and the waking state, provide some evidence to suggest that not only can self and consciousness dissociate, MS might be a necessary precondition for conscious experience." Body: "Conjoined premises 1–6 support phenomenological conjectures about MS—here dubbed the MSH: MS is empirically necessary for consciousness." He proposes neuroimaging of intrinsic activity (DMN, ACC) as the empirical foothold and is explicit that "paradigms such as those that involve facial self-recognition or adjectival self-attribution only probe neural correlates of 'objectifications' of self" (citing Zahavi & Roepstorff 2011) — a point that also limits what Woźniak et al.'s matching task can show about the minimal self.
- **The ownership distinction** (verbatim, in Lane's exposition of Henriksen, Parnas & Zahavi 2019): "Even when a thought is described as having been inserted, it is still 'my' thought: the denial that a thought belongs to me is a denial of 'personal ownership.' But there is a self, more minimal—the individual who has the 1st person experience, and that self retains a 'perspectival ownership' over alien, inserted thoughts. In other words, 'epistemic asymmetry is preserved,' for how I know these thoughts differs from how others know them (cf. Zahavi & Kriegel, 2016, pp. 44-45). In this respect, for-me-ness is preserved in passivity or in other psychopathological experiences, like thought insertion. The authors' point is not that the MS is unaffected in psychopathological experiences; on the contrary, for-me-ness is affected." The "warmth and intimacy" phrase is James's, reported as what "Henriksen et al. claim is absent during thought insertion"; "persisting identity void" is a patient report from Parnas 2005 via the EASE scale. Lane's own closing extrapolation: "it may well be the case that in thought insertion, the ventral mPFC is activated, but its connectedness to the nucleus accumbens, the amygdalae, and the basal ganglia is disrupted. That is to say, inserted thoughts exhibit for-me-ness, understood as perspectival ownership."
- **Relation to the Map's existing Lane citation**: `concepts/thought-insertion` L58 quotes Lane 2012 (via Zahavi & Kriegel 2016) — "Phenomenal consciousness does not entail self-awareness; it is not stamped with a meish quality; and, for-me-ness does not play a determining role in its constitution". The 2020 paper takes the minimal-self hypothesis seriously as possibly true and reads inserted thoughts as retaining perspectival for-me-ness. Whether this is a retraction or a reframing is for the writer to state cautiously ("a softer position than" is safe; "retracts" is not supported — Lane does not say so).
- **Coiner note**: the perspectival/personal ownership pair is Albahari's (2006, *Analytical Buddhism*; Crossref 10.1057/9780230800540_10 for the "two-tiered illusion of self" chapter). The Map already uses Albahari's distinction on `concepts/no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value` L86 and `topics/personal-identity` L181, and the prior research note records Henriksen & Parnas 2019 (*Thaumàzein*) using it for Lane's patient D.P. If any page names the distinction for thought insertion, credit Albahari as coiner and Henriksen, Parnas & Zahavi 2019 as the application Lane expounds — not Lane as originator.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral to Tenet 1; Lane's programme is neuroimaging-first and treats MS as a brain-realised precondition, which the Map should report, not adopt.

Support for localism is therefore structural (differentiated, not uniform) and modest.

- **What Lane 2020 adds**: nothing about depersonalisation. ## Key Debates

### Is felt initiation spared in depersonalisation?
- **Sides**: Gallagher-style phenomenology (spared) versus active-inference modelling (control disturbed) versus the 2024 measurement (no implicit or explicit group difference; decoupling of the two measures in HIGH DP).
- **Current state**: open at the clinical level — the 2024 study is non-clinical trait DP, N = 93, age- and gender-confounded, binding visible only at ≥ 500 ms, and the authors call for "replications" and clinical samples. At the trait level the null is the only direct evidence and it favours the Gallagher-style side on implicit initiation.

### Is the implicit–explicit decoupling a finding or a trend?
- The whole-sample correlation is significant (r = 0.23); the group split is a trend in LOW (p = 0.096) and absent in HIGH (p = 0.323). A difference between "significant in one group, not in the other" is not itself a tested interaction. Report it as the authors do ("suggesting a discrepancy"), not as an established dissociation.

### Does self-prioritisation index the minimal self?
- Lane 2020 (quoting Zahavi & Roepstorff 2011) says face-recognition and self-attribution paradigms probe "objectifications" of self, not "experiencing oneself as a subject"; Woźniak et al. themselves found no link between prioritisation and explicit self-identification. Either way, the self-prioritisation result does not reach the phenomenal feature the hub's architecture claim concerns.

## Bearing on the Map — Answers to the Three Questions

**(1) Ciaunica et al. 2024 and the one-way wording.** The paper does not show implicit intentional-binding agency disturbed in high-depersonalisation-trait participants; it reports no significant group difference on implicit or explicit agency, with the uncorrected binding effect running HIGH > LOW. The installed wording on all four hosts is therefore **not too generous**; no ≤40-word correction is needed. What is owed is the record of the null where the Map discusses the implicit measure, so that `volitional-control`'s and the agency article's use of intentional binding as "a non-introspective measure of agency" is not read as implying DP shows binding loss.
