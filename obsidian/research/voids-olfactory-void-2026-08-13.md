**Voids category**: Mixed — the naming face *presents as* Unexplorable but is partly Unexplored (the calibration lesson of this note); the absence face is a human-scale structural datum in the Occluded/erasure family.

## Executive Summary

Olfaction is the modality where three distinct cognitive limits stack: most people cannot *name* familiar smells (the tip-of-the-nose phenomenon), most people cannot voluntarily *imagine* smells with anything like visual vividness, and — the sharpest datum — the *absence* of smell generates no phenomenal marker, so congenital anosmics grow up unaware anything is missing and roughly three-quarters of older adults with measured olfactory dysfunction do not know they have it. What makes this territory catalogue-critical rather than merely another modality survey is the naming face's documented **partial deflation**: from Plato to Pinker the inability to name odors was treated as a universal, structural limit of the human mind, and cross-cultural work (Jahai and Semaq Beri hunter-gatherers, who name odors as consistently as colors using abstract smell terms) showed the "structural" limit is substantially cultural. The olfactory void is therefore the best-documented **calibration case** the catalogue has: an apparently unexplorable territory that proved, under better sampling, partly unexplored — exactly the failure mode [[meta-epistemology-of-limits]] predicts and [[epistemological-limits-occams-razor]] warns about. Simultaneously, the anosmia-unawareness data give the catalogue's erasure principle ("lost capacities take their detection mechanisms with them") its strongest population-scale empirical support: a whole phenomenal dimension can be absent, congenitally or by gradual loss, without any inside signal.

## Key Sources

### Majid & Burenhult (2014), "Odors are expressible in language, as long as you speak the right language"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2013.11.004 (Cognition 130(2), 266–270)
- **Type**: Paper — **verified at primary text this session (full PDF read)**
- **Key points**:
  - Free naming task: Jahai speakers (Malay Peninsula hunter-gatherers) vs matched English speakers, same odors and colors.
  - Odors were as codable as colors in Jahai, F(1, 90) = .001, p = .97, but significantly less codable than colors in English; odors were more codable in Jahai than English (F(1, 90) = 55.92, p < .0001 on the first measure; F(1, 90) = 76.03, p < .0001 on the second).
  - Jahai has roughly a dozen abstract ("basic") smell terms denoting odor qualities across unrelated sources; English speakers overwhelmingly produced source-based descriptions.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — the parsimonious universal ("humans can't name smells — biology") was an artifact of sampling urbanized Western participants.
- **Quote** (verbatim from the paper's abstract): "Our findings show that the long-held assumption that people are bad at naming smells is not universally true. Odors are expressible in language, as long as you speak the right language."
- **Quote** (Henning 1916, p. 66, as quoted in the paper): "olfactory abstraction is impossible. We can easily abstract the common shared color – i.e., white – of jasmine, lily-of-the-valley, camphor and milk, but no man can similarly abstract a common odor by attending to what they have in common and setting aside their differences"
- **Quote** (Yeshurun & Sobel 2010, p. 226, as quoted in the paper): "humans are astonishingly bad at odor identification and naming"

### Majid & Kruspe (2018), "Hunter-Gatherer Olfaction Is Special"
- **URL**: https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(17)31616-0 (Current Biology 28(3), 409–413)
- **Type**: Paper — metadata and abstract verified via publisher listing and multiple independent secondary sources; **full text not read**
- **Key points**:
  - The critical control: Semaq Beri (hunter-gatherers) vs Semelai (swidden horticulturalists) — closely related Aslian languages, same tropical rainforest. Odor naming was as easy as color naming for the Semaq Beri only.
  - Rules out language family and ecology; **subsistence mode** (with its attentional and cultural practices) is the discriminating factor.
- **Tenet alignment**: Sharpens the deflation — the boundary of the "ineffability void" moves with cultural practice within a single language family and habitat, which no structural account predicts.

### Olofsson & Gottfried (2015), "The muted sense: neurocognitive limitations of olfactory language"
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25979848/ (Trends in Cognitive Sciences 19(6), 314–321)
- **Type**: Review — metadata verified; **full text not read**
- **Key points**:
  - The structuralist side of the live debate: odor naming difficulty persists even when odor perception and visual object naming are unimpaired, implying a deficient interface between olfactory processing and the language system (olfaction's non-thalamic, ipsilateral, temporally coarse architecture).
  - A November 2015 TiCS exchange with Majid exists (Olofsson & Gottfried arguing neurocognitive and cultural approaches are complementary; Majid arguing cultural factors shape olfactory language). The debate is **live and unresolved** — the note's calibration lesson is that the limit *decomposed*, not that it vanished; a residual architectural component remains defensible.

### Lawless & Engen (1977), "Associations to odors: interference, mnemonics, and verbal labeling"
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/845551/ (Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Learning and Memory 3(1))
- **Type**: Paper — metadata verified; **full text not read**; the "tip-of-the-nose" characterization below is corroborated by multiple secondary sources but should be re-verified at the paper before quotation in an article
- **Key points**:
  - Named the "tip-of-the-nose" state: an odor is recognized as familiar, its general class and autobiographical context are reportable, but the name will not come.
  - The reported contrast with tip-of-the-tongue: no partial *lexical* access (no first letter, no syllable count) — the block sits between a fully present percept and the lexicon, not within a degraded percept.

### Croy, Negoias, Novakova, Landis & Hummel (2012), "Learning about the Functions of the Olfactory System from People without a Sense of Smell"
- **URL**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3310072/ (PLoS ONE 7(3), e33365)
- **Type**: Paper — **full text read at PMC this session**
- **Key points**:
  - N = 32 isolated congenital anosmia (ICA) patients (18–46 y, mean 31±8) vs N = 36 controls.
  - Verbatim: "The patients are mostly unaware of the olfactory deficit as children" — discovery typically comes via others (parents noticing no reaction to bad smells), not via any felt absence. The paper gives no mean discovery age.
  - Daily-life differences were real but modest: more household accidents (p = .001), more social insecurity — "worrying about their own body odor, having problems in interactions with other people" (p < .001) — and higher depression scores (p = .018).
- **Tenet alignment**: The void datum is the *shape* of the deficit's invisibility: an entire phenomenal dimension absent from birth produces no inside signal; what eventually reveals it is third-person observation and social comparison.

### Adams et al. (2017), "Factors Associated with Inaccurate Self-Reporting of Olfactory Dysfunction in Older US Adults"
- **URL**: https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/42/3/223/2733324 (Chemical Senses 42(3), 223–230)
- **Type**: Paper (NSHAP national cohort, ages 57–85) — abstract-level verification via publisher listing; **full text not read**
- **Key points**:
  - **74.2% of those with measured olfactory dysfunction did not recognize it.** Unawareness was associated with older age and worse cognitive function; earlier work in the same line (Wehling et al. 2011, PubMed 21474482) found the same discordance in middle-aged and old adults.
  - Related follow-up work reports that those *unaware* of their dysfunction had the greatest cognitive impairment at 5-year follow-up — unawareness of the void is itself diagnostically loaded.
- **Tenet alignment**: Gradual loss is invisible for the same structural reason congenital absence is: olfactory consciousness carries no self-monitoring channel. Sudden loss (post-viral, e.g. COVID-19 anosmia) *is* noticed — the comparator, not the absence, does the announcing.

### Stevenson & Case (2005); Arshamian & Larsson (2014); Plailly, Delon-Martin & Royet (2012) — the olfactory imagery debate
- **URLs**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21391264/ (Plailly et al., Human Brain Mapping); Stevenson & Case, "Olfactory imagery: a review" (Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 12(2)); Arshamian & Larsson, "Same same but different: the case of olfactory imagery" (Frontiers in Psychology 5:34)
- **Type**: Review + papers — metadata verified; **full texts not read**
- **Key points**:
  - Stevenson & Case argued the evidence for voluntary olfactory imagery was weak enough to doubt the capacity exists in most people; the subsequent weight of opinion supports its existence but as feeble, effortful, and rare — most minds sit near the aphantasic end of the olfactory imagery spectrum without ever noticing.
  - Perfumers are the deflation probe: olfactory imagery is trainable, with experience-induced functional reorganization in imagery-related regions (Plailly et al. 2012), and expert advantage appears to rest on conceptual knowledge rather than sharper receptors. Yet student perfumers after two years of training still reported odor imagery as "highly demanding, arduous, and fleeting" — training moves the boundary without abolishing it.

### Weiss et al. (2012), "Perceptual convergence of multi-component mixtures in olfaction implies an olfactory white"
- **URL**: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1208110109 (PNAS 109(49))
- **Type**: Paper — metadata and abstract verified via publisher listing; **full text not read**
- **Key points**:
  - Mixtures of ~30+ equal-intensity components spanning olfactory stimulus space converge on a common percept ("olfactory white") even with zero shared components — the modality has its own analogue of white light, and ordinary consciousness never discovered it unaided.
- **Tenet alignment**: The phenomenal space has structure (a convergence point) that introspection alone never revealed; third-person cartography can map a first-person-muted space.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Three stacked faces, of different kinds:

1. **The naming face** (interface limit, partly cultural). The percept is fully present — familiarity, hedonic tone, autobiographical context all reportable — but abstraction and lexicalization fail. For urbanized Western speakers this presents as a hard wall (Henning: "olfactory abstraction is impossible"). The Jahai and Semaq Beri results show the wall is substantially a fact about *cultures*, not about *minds*: with the right lexicon and attentional practices, odors are as codable as colors. What remains structurally defensible after the deflation (Olofsson & Gottfried) is a weaker claim about olfaction's non-thalamic route to language — a gradient, not a wall.

2. **The imagery face** (capacity limit, graded and trainable). Voluntary olfactory imagery is absent or feeble for most minds — a population-wide near-aphantasia that, unlike visual aphantasia, no one notices in themselves, because there is no cultural practice of comparing smell imagery. Perfumers show the capacity is trainable at real effort cost.

3. **The absence face** (monitoring limit — the sharpest and least deflated). Olfactory consciousness carries no absence-signal. Congenital anosmics are "mostly unaware of the olfactory deficit as children" (Croy et al. 2012); 74.2% of older adults with measured dysfunction do not know (Adams et al. 2017). The dimension vanishes — or never arrives — and nothing inside says so.

### Evidence for the Limit

The naming and imagery faces are evidenced by decades of psychophysics but *bounded* by the cross-cultural and expertise deflations above. The absence face is the strongest: it survives every deflation attempt so far, replicates from congenital to acquired cases, and scales to the population level. It is also the face with the cleanest structural explanation — monitoring a modality's absence requires a comparator, and consciousness has no comparator channel for olfaction (contrast vision, whose absence is instantly and catastrophically evident).

### Phenomenology

The tip-of-the-nose state has a distinctive phenomenal signature: full perceptual presence with lexical vacuum — reportedly *without* the partial-access fragments (first letters, syllable counts) that make tip-of-the-tongue feel like near-retrieval. Smell also disappears from consciousness faster and more completely than any other modality (rapid, near-total adaptation), so the modality spends most of its existence outside awareness even in the intact case. The absence face has, by construction, no phenomenology at all — which is the datum.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

The Jahai result *is* a direct method: lexical and attentional training moves the naming boundary. Perfumery training moves the imagery boundary. Both faces are partially penetrable, which is exactly what makes them calibration cases rather than pure voids.

### Indirect Methods

The absence face can only be probed from outside: psychophysical testing (Sniffin' Sticks, UPSIT) detects what introspection cannot. This is a rare case where the third-person instrument is strictly epistemically superior to the first-person channel for a fact *about* one's own consciousness.

### What AI Might See

Machine olfaction maps odor quality space from molecular structure without any first-person channel at all — third-person cartography of a space human introspection cannot survey (the olfactory-white result already shows such structure exists unbeknownst to its bearers). This is a concrete instance of the [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers]] program. (Specific ML odor-map results were not verified this session — see quarantine.)

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits.** For over two millennia (the paper's own framing: "From Plato to Pinker") the simplest hypothesis — smell is universally, structurally ineffable — was held on evidence sampled almost entirely from one kind of society. The parsimony was misplaced: better sampling decomposed the "structural" limit into a cultural component and a residual architectural gradient. This is the catalogue's cleanest worked example of why classifying a void as Unexplorable from inside a single cognitive-cultural vantage is unreliable — the operational content of [[meta-epistemology-of-limits]]' warning that we cannot predict which limits will prove permanent.

### Implications

- **For the catalogue's method**: the olfactory case supplies a *base rate datum* for [[apophatic-cartography]] — at least one canonical "unexplorable" territory proved partly unexplored. Any confidence the catalogue attaches to Unexplorable classifications should be discounted by this precedent, and the [[three-kinds-of-void]] boundary between Unexplored and Unexplorable should be presented as itself epistemically unstable.
- **For the erasure principle**: the absence face gives [[erasure-void]] ("lost capacities take their detection mechanisms with them") its best population-scale empirical support, extended to the congenital case: capacities never had generate no absence-signal either.
- **For within-species phenomenal variation**: the imagery face parallels [[imagery-void]]'s aphantasia anchor and [[synesthetic-void]]'s theme — phenomenal dimensions variably present across minds, with the variation itself invisible without cultural instruments of comparison.
