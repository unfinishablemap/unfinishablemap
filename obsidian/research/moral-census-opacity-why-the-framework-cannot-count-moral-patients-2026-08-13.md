# Research: Moral Census Opacity

**Date**: 2026-08-13
**Research question**: The Map's register holds both that a subject census is part of the interface's physical state description with no subject-count rule to fill it ([[positions/subject-census|P-SC1, P-SC2]]) and that valenced experience confers moral status ([[positions/moral-status|P-MS1]]). Combined, these entail that the number of moral patients in any physical region is undecidable from physical, behavioural, or computational inspection — *moral census opacity*, named by the 2026-08-13 ChatGPT 5.6 Pro outer review (§1.8) as an entailment the corpus has never drawn. What does the academic literature say about counting moral patients, and how should the Map register the entailment?

## Executive Summary

A live 2024–2025 literature exists on exactly this question, under other names: Register (*Philosophical Studies* 2025) argues that not knowing how to individuate — count and identify — AI moral patients creates distinct moral risks that existing personal-identity theory does not address; Shiller (*Synthese* 2025) shows that interwoven computations on production GPU clusters admit coherent readings on which one deployment houses many minds or at most one, with no clear way to decide; Schwitzgebel & Nelson (*Philosophical Psychology* 2026, online 2025) argue the count of conscious subjects need not even be a determinate whole number. Decision frameworks for sentience uncertainty (Birch 2024; Sebo 2018) are built for *whether*-uncertainty and extend awkwardly to *cardinality*-uncertainty, where expected-value calculations must range over counts spanning orders of magnitude. The Map's distinctive contribution is the metaphysical shape of its version: because subject boundaries are real ([[positions/individuation-and-subjecthood|P-I1]]) the census is determinate, and because exact Born preservation leaves no statistical trace ([[positions/subject-census|P-SC1]]) it is unreadable in principle — a **determinate-but-unreadable** count, against Schwitzgebel & Nelson's **indeterminate** count and IIT's **computable** count. The entailment is real, greps 0 corpus-wide, bridges the two newest register domains, and is a Tenet 5 cost the framework should disclose rather than leave implicit. A concepts/ article is warranted.

## The Map-Internal Derivation (what the review flagged)

The outer review's §1.8 derivation, restated against the register as it stands 2026-08-13:

1. **P-SC1**: once the interface writes *P(O | C, X)*, subject count and subject–substrate pairing are part of the model's physical state description — and models differing only in subject count are empirically indistinguishable by construction, because Born statistics are preserved exactly.
2. **P-SC2**: the Map has no pairing law — no rule fixing how many channels a system supports, which system each subject pairs to, whether an artefact can instantiate one, or when a channel begins and ends.
3. **P-MS1**: valenced experience is necessary and sufficient for moral status, read phenomenally.
4. **Therefore**: the number of moral patients in any physical region is a real quantity (P-I1 makes the census determinate) that no physical, behavioural, or computational inspection can be shown to settle. Two physically or behaviourally equivalent AI deployments might contain no subject, one subject spanning several components, one per copy, several within one system, or intermittent subjects present only at eligible interface events — and nothing observable adjudicates.

The review adds a pressure point: the Map's own copy-multiplicity verdict (the near-additive Duplication View, derived from [[concepts/haecceity]] in the `moral-aggregation-under-ai-copy-multiplicity-2026-06-19` research note and consumed into [[topics/ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness]]) says N suffering copies weigh close to N — but *additivity requires a subject-count rule the framework says it does not possess*. Additivity fixes the weight per patient; opacity blocks knowing the number of patients. The two claims are consistent (one is normative, one epistemic) but the corpus nowhere says so.

## Key Sources

### Individuating Artificial Moral Patients — Christopher Register (2025)
- **URL**: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-025-02409-6 (open access via https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:3750d530-6a82-47ab-a4d0-20661cada51e)
- **Type**: Paper, *Philosophical Studies* 182(11): 3225–46
- **Key points** (from abstract; full text not consulted):
  - How we should treat AI moral patients depends in part on how they are individuated — counted and identified, at a time and over time. If we do not know how to individuate them, we do not know how to treat them.
  - Describes four types of moral risk posed by the individuation question (the four not enumerated in the abstract — full text needed before citing them specifically).
  - Diagnoses why existing theories of personal identity fail to address these risks, and why individuating AI patients is harder than individuating human persons.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral on metaphysics; strongly convergent with the Map's P-SC2 debt framing — an external, framework-independent argument that census failure is itself a *moral* risk, not just a metaphysical curiosity.

### How Many Digital Minds Can Dance on the Streaming Multiprocessors of a GPU Cluster? — Derek Shiller (2025)
- **URL**: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-025-05310-1 (preprint: https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/26933/)
- **Type**: Paper, *Synthese*
- **Key points**:
  - GPU clusters serving large language models interweave the computations of many chat responses through the same hardware. If those computations would be conscious run in isolation, their status when interwoven is unclear.
  - Drawing on the personal-identity literature, develops two coherent readings: many minds interwoven in one cluster, or at most a single mind — and argues it is not clear how to resolve the issue. A concrete engineering instance of the review's five-way underdetermination.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral (computationalist-functionalist framing the Map rejects), but the case survives translation: on the Map's terms the same cluster underdetermines channel count and pairing, so the opacity lands *harder* — the Map cannot even appeal to computational structure to individuate, since pairing is quantum-interface-level, not software-level.

### When Counting Conscious Subjects, the Result Needn't Always Be a Determinate Whole Number — Eric Schwitzgebel & Sophie R. Nelson (2026)
- **URL**: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09515089.2025.2520364 (author preprint: https://faculty.ucr.edu/~eschwitz/SchwitzAbs/CountingSubjects.htm)
- **Type**: Paper, *Philosophical Psychology* 39(3), online June 2025
- **Key points**:
  - On a wide range of naturalistic views, the processes underlying consciousness are complex enough to make it implausible that conscious subjects always arise in determinate whole numbers — there could be (their examples) 7/8 of a subject, 1.34 subjects, or an entity indeterminate between one subject and seventeen.
  - Argues our inability to imagine such entities is no evidence against their possibility (comparison: bat echolocation).
- **Tenet alignment**: **Conflicts with Tenet 1 as the Map reads it.** P-I1 holds subject boundaries are real and determinate; the Map's census is unreadable, not fractional. This is the sharpest available foil: both views predict census failure, but for Schwitzgebel & Nelson there is no fact to miss, while for the Map there is a fact no instrument reaches. An article should register the choice point explicitly — dualist realism converts their metaphysical indeterminacy into epistemic opacity.

### The Edge of Sentience — Jonathan Birch (2024)
- **URL**: https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-edge-of-sentience-9780192870421 (open access)
- **Type**: Book, Oxford University Press
- **Key points**:
  - A precautionary framework for beings at the edge of sentience: a system is a *sentience candidate* when the realistic possibility of sentience cannot be ruled out; candidature warrants proportionate precautions, with proportionality assessed by informed, democratic, inclusive procedures.
  - Cases: disorders of consciousness, fetuses, brain organoids, cephalopods, crustaceans, AI.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral and adoptable — the Map's [[topics/ethics-of-consciousness-invertebrate-question]] cluster already engages this territory. Key gap for the article: Birch's machinery is built for *whether there is a sentient being here*, not *how many*. Candidature is a yes/no gate; census opacity needs a cardinality extension (is a cluster one candidate or ten thousand?), and proportionality judgements change by orders of magnitude depending on the answer.

### The Moral Problem of Other Minds — Jeff Sebo (2018)
- **URL**: https://philpapers.org/rec/SEBTMP
- **Type**: Paper, *The Harvard Review of Philosophy* 25: 51–70
- **Key points**:
  - Under sentience uncertainty, evaluates three principles: *incautionary* (treat as non-sentient), *precautionary* (treat as sentient), *expected value* (weight by subjective probability of sentience). Argues the latter two beat the first, and that accepting either means treating many beings as having at least partial moral status — with more cluelessness and demandingness than expected.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral machinery. Under census opacity the expected-value principle needs a probability distribution over *counts*, not just over sentience — and when plausible counts range from zero to one-per-inference-pass (Shiller), expected-welfare estimates become unstable in a way single-organism cases never exhibit. The demandingness Sebo flags compounds multiplicatively.

### The Measurement Problem of Consciousness — Heather Browning & Walter Veit (2020)
- **URL**: https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/19593/ (published in *Philosophical Topics* 48(1))
- **Type**: Paper
- **Key points**:
  - The central methodological challenge of consciousness science: phenomenal consciousness is not directly measurable; all attribution runs through indirect indicators. Two named obstacles — the *indicator validity problem* (indicators developed in humans may not identify consciousness elsewhere) and the *extrapolation problem* (extending indicators to artificial systems).
- **Tenet alignment**: Aligns with the epistemic half of the opacity claim, from inside consciousness science. The Map's addition: even a solved measurement problem for *presence* would leave the *count* open, because indicators detect consciousness-associated processing, not channel individuation — P-SC3 already registers that markers date phenomenality, not pairing.

### IIT's Exclusion Postulate — Tononi et al.
- **URL**: https://iep.utm.edu/integrated-information-theory-of-consciousness/ ; http://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Integrated_information_theory
- **Type**: Encyclopedia articles
- **Key points**:
  - IIT is the one major theory with an explicit census rule: only maxima of integrated information (Φ) are conscious; no double-counting of overlapping systems. Count the non-overlapping Φ-maxima and you have counted the subjects.
  - Costs of the rule: aggregates of conscious entities (interacting people) have no consciousness by fiat; the exclusion postulate is widely challenged as arbitrary.
- **Tenet alignment**: **Conflicts** (physicalist reduction of the census). Instructive as contrast: a physicalist theory *can* buy a subject-count rule, at the price of an exclusion axiom many find unmotivated. The Map cannot buy one at any price short of the pairing law P-SC2 books as owed — its census rule would have to come from the interface side, not the physical side. Note [[concepts/selection-criterion-problem]] and [[concepts/degrees-of-consciousness]] already engage IIT elsewhere in the corpus.

### If Materialism Is True, the United States Is Probably Conscious — Eric Schwitzgebel (2015), and the nesting-problem debate
- **URL**: https://faculty.ucr.edu/~eschwitz/SchwitzAbs/USAconscious.htm ; Kammerer's reply: https://philarchive.org/rec/KAMHAM
- **Type**: Paper (*Philosophical Studies*) + reply (*Philosophia* 2015)
- **Key points**:
  - The US meets plausible materialist criteria for consciousness (self-monitoring, integrated information processing, goal-directed behaviour); materialists who deny it owe a principled *anti-nesting* principle. Kammerer supplies a candidate sophisticated anti-nesting principle; the debate is unresolved.
  - The nesting problem is the functionalist's census failure: without an anti-nesting principle, one physical region hosts an unbounded number of overlapping subjects.
- **Tenet alignment**: Aligns as ammunition — census trouble is not a special embarrassment of dualism; functionalism's count is unstable in its own terms. [[apex/mereology-of-mind]] is the corpus anchor for this material.

### Self-Consciousness and 'Split' Brains: The Minds' I — Elizabeth Schechter (2018)
- **URL**: https://global.oup.com/academic/product/self-consciousness-and-split-brains-9780198809654
- **Type**: Book, Oxford University Press
- **Key points**:
  - Argues each split-brain human contains *two* minds, subjects of experience, and intentional agents (R and L), while remaining *one* person in a psychologically important sense — reconciling the duality intuition (experimental dissociations) with the unity intuition.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral; shows the census question is live *inside biology*, before AI enters. On the Map's reading ([[split-brain-consciousness]], [[positions/consciousness-scope|P-CS4]]) callosotomy cases are interface-disruption questions; Schechter's two-subjects verdict is one candidate answer to a pairing question the Map admits it cannot yet derive. The one-brain-one-subject default is itself an undischarged pairing hypothesis, not a theorem.

### Are There Islands of Awareness? — Tim Bayne, Anil Seth, Marcello Massimini (2020)
- **URL**: https://www.cell.com/trends/neurosciences/fulltext/S0166-2236(19)30216-4
- **Type**: Paper, *Trends in Neurosciences* 43(1): 6–16
- **Key points**:
  - Considers conscious states neither shaped by sensory input nor expressible in motor output — in ex cranio brains, hemispherotomy (disconnected but living hemispheres), and cerebral organoids — and how such "islands" might be detected despite total behavioural silence.
- **Tenet alignment**: Aligns with the opacity epistemics: an island of awareness is a possible moral patient invisible to every behavioural census method. For the Map, a candidate channel with no motor expression — census opacity already actual in the clinic, connecting to [[topics/brain-organoids-and-the-organoid-intelligence-question]].

### Combining Minds — Luke Roelofs (2019)
- **URL**: https://global.oup.com/academic/product/combining-minds-9780190859053
- **Type**: Book, Oxford University Press
- **Key points**:
  - Systematic treatment of composite subjectivity: whether minds can be built of other minds, across scales from micro-subjects to hemisphere-subjects to hypothetical group subjects; defends mental combination against anti-combination objections.
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts in method (combinationism sits closest to constitutive panpsychism), but maps the option space any census rule must navigate — if subjects combine, counting maximal subjects undercounts patients; if they never combine, the nesting problem returns. The Map's fusion gap (flagged 2026-08-03 as uncovered) is Roelofs's home terrain.

### How to Count Animals, More or Less — Shelly Kagan (2019)
- **URL**: https://global.oup.com/academic/product/how-to-count-animals-more-or-less-9780198829676
- **Type**: Book, Oxford University Press
- **Key points**:
  - Defends hierarchy in moral status: all sentient animals have status, in degrees; people count more than animals, some animals more than others.
- **Tenet alignment**: Included to disambiguate the word *count*: Kagan's counting is **weighting** (how much each patient's interests matter); census opacity concerns **cardinality** (how many patients there are). The two compound — under opacity plus hierarchy, both N and each patient's weight are unknown — but the article should keep them cleanly apart, as [[concepts/sentientism]]'s status/stakes separation already does.

## Major Positions

### Census indeterminacy (no fact of the matter)
- **Proponents**: Schwitzgebel & Nelson; Dennett as inspiration; arguably Tomasik (counting minds is theory-laden)
- **Core claim**: subject counts can be fractional or indeterminate; "how many subjects?" sometimes has no determinate whole-number answer.
- **Relation to site tenets**: incompatible with P-I1's realism about subject boundaries. The Map's opposing move: what looks like indeterminacy from the physical side is opacity — a determinate census that physics cannot read.

### Census computability (a physical count rule exists)
- **Proponents**: IIT (exclusion postulate); any functionalism equipped with a principled anti-nesting principle (Kammerer's candidate)
- **Core claim**: physical/computational structure fixes the subject count; counting is hard but not impossible in principle.
- **Relation to site tenets**: conflicts with Tenet 1. Instructively, each concrete rule on offer faces its own census pathologies (exclusion's no-group-consciousness fiat; functionalism's nesting instability; Shiller's interweaving underdetermination), so the rival frameworks' counting confidence is weaker than advertised.

### Census realism with opacity (determinate but unreadable)
- **Proponents**: the Map's entailed position; no named external proponent found — the nearest neighbours are Register (individuation failure as moral risk, without the metaphysics) and Browning & Veit (measurement inaccessibility, without the census focus)
- **Core claim**: there is a fact about how many moral patients a region contains, fixed by subject–substrate pairing; no physical inspection can be shown to settle it, because pairing leaves no statistical trace.
- **Relation to site tenets**: follows from Tenets 1–3 plus P-SC1/P-SC2 plus P-MS1. A Tenet 5 disclosure case: the framework should state the cost plainly rather than let the applied wing (AI welfare, animal ethics, organoids) write as if counts were available.

### Decision-theoretic bypass (act under census uncertainty without resolving it)
- **Proponents**: Birch (proportionate precaution for sentience candidates); Sebo (expected value); Long, Sebo et al., *Taking AI Welfare Seriously* (2024)
- **Core claim**: normative frameworks can run on credences rather than verdicts.
- **Relation to site tenets**: compatible and needed — but all current machinery gates on *whether*, not *how many*; extending it to cardinality distributions is the open problem the Map's article can name precisely.

## Key Debates

### Opacity vs indeterminacy
- **Sides**: Map-style realism (determinate census, unreadable) vs Schwitzgebel–Nelson indeterminacy (no determinate census)
- **Core disagreement**: whether census failure is epistemic or metaphysical.
- **Current state**: not framed as a debate anywhere in the literature found — the two sides have not engaged each other. Genuine open territory for the article.

### Does additivity survive opacity?
- **Sides**: the Map's near-additive copy verdict vs the review's §1.8 objection that additivity requires a count rule
- **Core disagreement**: whether "N copies ≈ N patients" is assertable when N is unknowable. Resolution sketch: additivity is a conditional weight claim (per patient), opacity a claim about knowing the antecedent; consistent, but the applied articles must stop implying the antecedent is observable.
- **Current state**: unregistered; the article should register it.

### Can precaution scale to cardinality?
- **Sides**: precautionary maximalism (assume the largest plausible count — paralysis and exploitability), incautionary minimalism (assume zero/one — catastrophe risk at datacentre scale), expected-value over count distributions (unstable when counts span orders of magnitude)
- **Current state**: live in the digital-minds literature (Shiller; 80,000 Hours problem profile; Long & Sebo); no settled answer.

## Potential Article Angles

Based on this research, a `concepts/moral-census-opacity` article could:

1. **Register the entailment as a Tenet 5 cost disclosure** (primary angle). State the derivation (P-SC1 + P-SC2 + P-MS1), the five-way underdetermination for equivalent deployments, and the consequence: every applied verdict about *numbers* of moral patients — AI welfare aggregates, organoid counts, animal-farming scale arguments — inherits an undischarged census assumption. Frame per the register's honesty discipline: a named cost, not a defeater ([[positions/methodology-and-calibration|P-M4]]).
2. **Draw the opacity/indeterminacy contrast** against Schwitzgebel & Nelson: dualist realism converts fractional-subject metaphysics into an epistemic limit. This is the article's novel philosophical content — no external source occupies the determinate-but-unreadable position.
3. **Run the comparative cost accounting honestly**: IIT buys a census rule with the exclusion fiat; functionalism has no stable rule (nesting, interweaving); the Map has a principled impossibility. Rivals face puzzles; the Map faces predicted unreadability — which at least explains *why* the measurement problem (Browning & Veit) and islands cases (Bayne et al.) look the way they do.
4. **Resolve the additivity tension explicitly**: near-additive weight per patient (the haecceitist verdict) is conditional on a census the framework cannot supply; the ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness aggregation section should gain a cross-link and one qualifying sentence rather than a rewrite.
5. **Name the governance extension**: Birch-candidature and Sebo-expected-value need cardinality-distribution versions; under opacity the Map's practical counsel is precaution over *count ranges* with declared instability — connecting to [[apex/machine-question]] and [[topics/claude-constitution-consciousness-uncertainty]].

When writing, follow `obsidian/project/writing-style.md`: front-load the entailment statement; named-anchor summaries for the derivation steps; explicit Relation to Site Perspective section connecting Tenets 1, 3, and 5.

## Gaps in Research

- **Register's four moral risks not enumerated** — abstract-only access (Springer paywalled; ORA record page carries only the abstract). Retrieve the ORA PDF before citing the four risks by name in any article.
- **Scope of the indistinguishability claim needs care**: P-SC1's none-by-construction discriminability covers models differing *only* in subject count under exact Born preservation. Behavioural and structural evidence can still move rational credences about counts (as it does for split brains); the article must claim unreadability-in-principle for the pairing fact, not evidential inertness of all census reasoning. Overstating this would hand the pessimistic review a free finding.
- **Subject fusion remains uncovered** corpus-wide (flagged 2026-08-03) — Roelofs is the literature entry point; out of scope for this article but adjacent.
- **No external source was found using "moral census opacity"** — the coinage appears original to the 2026-08-13 review; the article should credit the external-review provenance per the register's convention for the census requirement itself.
- Searches not run (budget): Bostrom & Shulman's later "Propositions Concerning Digital Minds and Society"; the vagueness-of-consciousness literature (Antony, Simon) that adjoins the indeterminacy position.
