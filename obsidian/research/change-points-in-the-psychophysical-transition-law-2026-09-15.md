## Executive Summary

The reviewer's claim is that if subject onset, cessation and fission are real events on the Map's ontology, then the psychophysical law *P(O | C₁…Cₙ, X)* must change **arity** at them — a conditioning variable appears, disappears or becomes several — and a real change in the law's arity should leave a change-point in some consciousness-conditioned distribution, or else the census variable is idle exactly where it is supposed to matter. Research confirms that this is a real, well-posed question with a literature the Map can import: the *change-point* literature supplies detectors, the *causal faithfulness* literature supplies the exact sense in which a real variable can leave no trace, and the *causal abstraction* literature (exact transformations between models of different variable sets) supplies the formal object the reviewer's "theorem" is actually about. The empirical literature is rich but, on inspection, almost entirely about change-points in the **physical substrate at state transitions** (neural inertia, discrete metastable states, edge-of-chaos departure, a PCI cut-off, a gamma surge at death), not about change-points in *conditional* structure keyed to census events. The single most useful finding for the Map is that **its own ontology splits the reviewer's six domains in two**: anaesthesia, disorders-of-consciousness recovery and sleep are, by P-SC3 and P-CS4, interruptions with the *same* subject on both sides — no census event — while developmental onset, death, callosotomy on the two-subject reading, organoid maturation and copying are census events. That split converts the anaesthesia literature from evidence into a **control**: the state-transition change-points the substrate shows at induction are what a census-transition change-point must be *distinguished from*, and a design that finds the same signature at both has found evidence for horn (b) of the census trilemma. A second finding sharpens the reviewer's point: under the *causal minimality* condition, a variable in the state description whose conditionals never differ from the marginal is not "physically real yet invisible" — it is to be deleted from the model — so horn (b) is not compatible with P-SC1 as written, and the article should say which of the two the Map gives up.

## Key Sources

### Change-point detection as a discipline

**Truong, Oudre & Vayatis (2020)**, *Signal Processing* 167: 107299 — a structured review of offline change-point detection organised into cost function, search method and constraint; the standard entry point.
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral — method.
- **Use**: the article can say precisely what "a change-point in the law" means: a change in the parameters of the generating distribution at an unknown time, detected by minimising a cost over segmentations. Under Born preservation the *marginal* cost is flat across a census event by construction; the detector must be run on the **conditional** residuals, which is where [[concepts/selection-only-channel]] already says the channel's information would show.

**Adams & MacKay (2007)**, arXiv:0710.3742 — Bayesian online change-point detection via the run-length posterior. Relevant because a census event is *online* in the clinical settings (emergence, DoC recovery) and the run-length formalism gives a natural "time since the last arity change" variable.

**Green (1995)**, *Biometrika* 82(4): 711–732 — reversible-jump MCMC for "Bayesian model determination, where the dimensionality of the parameter vector is typically not fixed", proposing samplers that "jump between parameter subspaces of differing dimensionality", and illustrated with "multiple change-point analysis in one and two dimensions" (abstract, read raw). This is the statistically exact object for the reviewer's demand: a transition in the *number* of conditioning variables is a trans-dimensional jump, and Green's own illustration is a change-point problem. The article can cite it to say the census change-point is not a metaphor: it is inference over a model whose parameter dimension changes at an unknown time.

**Hamilton (1989)**, *Econometrica* 57(2): 357–384 — regime-switching with a latent discrete state. Useful as the analogue for P-SC1's "latent census": the regime is never observed directly and is inferred from distributional change; if there is no distributional change, the regime is not identified — which is horn (b) restated as an identification failure.

**Scheffer et al. (2009)**, *Nature* 461: 53–59 — early-warning signals for critical transitions (critical slowing down: rising variance and autocorrelation). Relevant as the *rival* reading of any change-point found: a substrate near a bifurcation shows these precursors regardless of any census, so a census signature must be shown not to be a critical-transition precursor.

### Faithfulness and the "physically real yet invisible" question

**Andersen (2013)**, *Philosophy of Science* 80(5): 672–683 — "In complex evolved systems, mechanisms for maintaining equilibrium states are highly likely to violate CF [causal faithfulness]. Even when such systems do not precisely violate CF, they may nevertheless generate precisely the same problems for inferring causal structure from probabilistic relationships in data" (abstract, read raw).
- **Tenet alignment**: bears directly on Tenet 5 — a cancelling structure cannot be read off observational data, so parsimony arguments from "no observed trace" are unreliable, which is the Map's own Occam position.
- **Use**: horn (c) of the census trilemma — census effects that differ but cancel under a Born-balancing law — is a *predicted* faithfulness violation of exactly Andersen's kind: the balancing law is an equilibrium-maintaining mechanism. That names the horn's literature and its consequence: observational change-point detection is blind to horn (c), and only intervention (the "manipulate intention" design in [[apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy]]) can separate it from horn (b).

**Zhang & Spirtes (2008)**, *Minds and Machines* 18(2): 239–271 — "Detection of Unfaithfulness and Robust Causal Inference". Metadata verified at Crossref; abstract not obtainable raw (Springer, PhilPapers and PhilSci-Archive all returned stubs), so cited here only for the title-level claim that unfaithfulness is partly detectable rather than assumed away. The article should either read the paper or lean on Andersen.
- **Derived point (this note's own, not sourced)**: the weakest conjunct of faithfulness is *causal minimality* — no proper sub-model of the true structure satisfies the Markov condition. A conditioning variable whose conditionals never differ from the marginal fails minimality and is to be removed from the model. So horn (b) does not leave the census "physically real yet idle"; under minimality it makes P-SC1's "part of the physical state description" false. The reviewer's dichotomy is therefore sharper than P-SC1 records: the Map cannot hold P-SC1 and horn (b) together. This should be presented as the article's central derivation and checked by a pessimistic pass.

### Causal abstraction — the formal home of the "theorem"

**Rubenstein, Weichwald, Bongers, Mooij, Janzing, Grosse-Wentrup & Schölkopf (2017)**, UAI, arXiv:1707.00819 — *exact transformations* between structural equation models, the conditions under which two models on different variable sets are causally consistent (authors and date verified at arXiv).

**Beckers & Halpern (2019)**, AAAI 33(1): 2678–2685 — "a sequence of successively more restrictive definitions of abstraction for causal models, starting with … exact transformation …, moving to … uniform transformation … [which] does not allow differences to be hidden by the 'right' choice of distribution, and then to abstraction … and strong abstraction" (abstract, read raw).
- **Use**: the reviewer's demand — "an explicit theorem stating whether a census transition can be physically real yet leave every conditioning grain unchanged" — is ill-posed as stated, because *before* and *after* an arity change the law lives on different variable sets and "every grain unchanged" has no meaning without a map between them. The well-posed version is: does the (n+1)-subject model admit an exact transformation to the n-subject model? If it does, the added variable is abstracted away without loss and the census is not part of the state description (horn (b), and P-SC1 fails); if it does not, some conditional differs (horn (a)) or differs-and-cancels in a way a *uniform* transformation would expose (horn (c) — Beckers and Halpern's point that exact transformation can hide differences "by the 'right' choice of distribution" is precisely the balancing-law loophole). The theorem the Map owes is therefore a statement about abstractability, and it can be *written down*, which is more than P-SC1 currently says.

### Anaesthesia — state-transition change-points (the control domain)

**Friedman et al. (2010)**, *PLoS ONE* 5(7): e11903, and **Joiner et al. (2013)**, *PLoS Genetics* 9(9): e1003605 — neural inertia: a conserved barrier separating wakefulness from anaesthetic unresponsiveness, with a genetic basis (four excitability genes in *Drosophila*).

**Proekt & Hudson (2018)**, *British Journal of Anaesthesia* 121(1): 86–94 — "Stochastic state switching accounts for all known features of neural inertia. More than two states are required … This model predicts that hysteresis should collapse with a time scale independent of anaesthetic drug pharmacokinetics" (abstract, read raw via Semantic Scholar). Important for the article: on this model the hysteresis is a *mixing-time* phenomenon, not a physical discontinuity in the law — so the best-documented "change-point" in the field is a change in the *dynamics'* occupancy, not a change in any law's form.

**Hudson, Calderon, Pfaff & Proekt (2014)**, *PNAS* 111(25): 9283–9288 — recovery from pharmacological coma is confined to a low-dimensional subspace of "discrete metastable states persistent on the scale of minutes", with hub states that must be passed "in an orderly fashion" (abstract, read raw). Discrete state jumps at recovery are real and measurable.

**McKinstry-Wu et al. (2019)**, *eLife* 8: e50143 — at a fixed drug concentration "individual responses dynamically fluctuated"; "the amount of noise driving transitions between states … was highly conserved in vertebrates separated by 400 million years of evolution" (abstract, read raw). The physical state is bistable with noise-driven switching — the natural null for any per-trial "signature".

**Kim, Moon, Mashour & Lee (2018)**, *PLoS Computational Biology* 14(8): e1006424 — hysteresis of conscious-state transitions explained by explosive synchronisation as "generic network features"; "the structure and strength of functional brain networks reconfigure differently during the loss vs. recovery of consciousness" (abstract, read raw). Corpus note: the sibling page's hysteresis member could cite this for the network-level mechanism; not required.

**Steyn-Ross, Steyn-Ross & Sleigh (2004)**, *Progress in Biophysics and Molecular Biology* 85(2–3): 369–385 — anaesthesia modelled as a first-order cortical phase transition. Metadata verified; abstract not obtained raw; cited for the model class only.

**Tagliazucchi et al. (2016)**, *J. R. Soc. Interface* 13(114): 20151027 — unconsciousness as "a departure from critical dynamics", with the prediction that "the principles we identified are universal and independent from its causes" (abstract, read raw). **Toker et al. (2022)**, *PNAS* 119(7): e2024455119 — waking cortex sits near the edge-of-chaos critical point and "cortical information processing is disrupted during unconscious states because of a transition of low-frequency cortical electric oscillations away from this critical point" (abstract, read raw).
- **Tenet alignment for the whole block**: neutral-to-conflicting. All of these are production-side, substrate-only accounts of a transition the Map reads as interface disruption. None mentions a subject count. They are what a census signature must be shown *not* to be.
- **Why this block is a control, not evidence**: by P-SC3, the subject is the same on both sides of induction and emergence. So every change-point in this block is a change-point *without* a census event. If the Map's census variable does work, a genuine census event (below) must leave something these transitions do not. That is a discriminating design the article can state; it is also the honest reason the reviewer's list of six domains needs pruning to the census events.

### Disorders of consciousness — recovery without a census event

**Casarotto et al. (2016)**, *Annals of Neurology* 80(5): 718–729 — a PCI cut-off that "discriminated with 100% sensitivity and specificity between the conscious and the unconscious conditions in the benchmark population" and identified "9 of 43" vegetative-state patients with high PCI (abstract, read raw). The 0.31 figure quoted in secondary sources is not in the abstract; the article should either read the paper for it or cite the abstract's wording. **Claassen et al. (2019)**, *NEJM* 380(26): 2497–2505 — EEG evidence of command-following in 15% of clinically unresponsive acute patients. **Bodien et al. (2024)**, *NEJM* 391(7): 598–608 — cognitive motor dissociation in "60 of the 241 participants (25%) without an observable response to commands" (abstract, read raw). **Whyte & Myers (2009)**, *Am. Rehabil.* 88(5): 410–418 — zolpidem: "One of 15 participants (6.7%) demonstrated a clinically significant response, which altered his assessment from the vegetative state to the minimally conscious state", replicated, and "a bimodal rather than a graded response to the drug" (abstract, read raw).
- **Use**: covert consciousness is the strongest reason a *behavioural* change-point is not a *conscious-state* change-point, let alone a *census* change-point — a quarter of behaviourally unresponsive patients have a conscious state the behavioural series never shows. The zolpidem bimodality is the cleanest abrupt-transition datum in the clinical literature and is, on the Map's register, a state transition within one persisting subject.

### Death — a candidate cessation change-point

**Borjigin et al. (2013)**, *PNAS* 110(35): 14432–14437 (rat) and **Xu et al. (2023)**, *PNAS* 120(19): e2216268120 (human) — "Two of the four patients exhibited a rapid and marked surge of gamma power, surge of cross-frequency coupling … and increased interhemispheric functional and directed connectivity in gamma bands", "within the posterior cortical 'hot zone'", "stimulated by global hypoxia" (abstract, read raw).
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral. The surge is a substrate change-point at the *approach* to death; P-SC3 leaves open whether pairing terminates at brain death, and the corpus's terminal-lucidity material (already in the sibling page) is the other cessation-adjacent datum. The article should say plainly that a change-point *in the substrate* at death is expected on every view and is not a census signature.

### Split-brain — the one live human fission case

**Pinto et al. (2017)**, *Brain* 140(5): 1231–1237 (volume and pages confirmed at Europe PMC; the Crossref record lacks them) — "severing the cortical connections between hemispheres splits visual perception, but does not create two independent conscious perceivers within one brain" (abstract, read raw). **de Haan et al. (2020)**, *Neuropsychology Review* 30(2): 224–233 — a twelve-author consensus review including Pinto, Schechter and Bayne: callosotomy produces a broad breakdown of integration that "is not absolute", with action control among the processes that remain unified. **Schechter (2018)**, *Self-Consciousness and "Split" Brains: The Minds' I*, OUP — the two-minds reading.
- **Tenet alignment**: split. P-I4 now books that on the two-subject reading callosotomy entails the pre-operative subject's cessation; Pinto's one-perceiver reading would make callosotomy a non-census event and remove it from the programme.
- **Use**: callosotomy is the only human procedure with a *dated* candidate census event and pre/post recordings in principle. No study has looked at conditional structure across it; all the data are integration-behavioural. The article should register this as the programme's one feasible retrospective and note that the disconnection syndrome's resolution over weeks is a *slow* candidate change-point — no primary source for that timeline was verified here; the article must not assert a number.

### Developmental onset — the cleanest census event

**Bayne, Frohlich, Cusack, Moser & Naci (2023)**, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 27(12): 1135–1149 — "recent research points in favor of early-onset accounts", consciousness "likely to be in place at birth (or shortly after) and may even arise during the third trimester" (abstract, read raw). **Lagercrantz & Changeux (2009)**, *Pediatric Research* 65(3): 255–260 — the fetus "almost continuously asleep and unconscious partially due to endogenous sedation", the newborn awake with "features characteristic of basic consciousness".
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral, and P-SC3 is explicit that markers date phenomenality, not pairing.
- **Use**: developmental onset is the one census event on the Map's ontology that *every* live subject has undergone and that no persistence claim overrides. It is also where the marker/pairing gap bites hardest: a change-point in thalamocortical connectivity or in EEG complexity is a substrate change-point; the census claim needs a conditional change-point on the new subject's state — which requires the independent handle on the subject's conscious state that P-SC1's discriminability note already demands and which no newborn can supply. Honest verdict: the domain is the cleanest *in principle* and the least accessible *in practice*.

### Copying — Bostrom's duplication thesis and the fractional census

**Bostrom (2006)**, *Minds and Machines* 16(2): 185–200 — from the PDF: "If a brain is duplicated so that there are two brains in identical states, are there then two numerically distinct phenomenal experiences or only one? There are two, I argue, and given computationalism, this has implications for what it is to implement a computation. … I show that in some of these cases there can be, in a deep and intriguing sense, a fractional (non-integer) number of qualitatively identical phenomenal experiences." Bostrom also separates the question from personal identity: "The issue is not about personal identity."
- **Tenet alignment**: the duplication half *aligns* with P-I4 (a copy is a new census entry) and with P-AC3's additive rule, from a computationalist premise the Map does not share. The fractional half is a **direct challenge** to the change-point framing: if the census can take non-integer values under unreliable or partially parallel implementation, arity is not a discrete variable and "change-point" is the wrong formalism — the census would drift, not jump, and the relevant statistics are Hamilton's continuous-regime cousins, not Green's trans-dimensional jumps. The article must engage this: the Map's [[concepts/haecceity|primitive-thisness]] reading of subjects gives it a principled reason to reject fractional censuses (a particular is either present or not), and that rejection is what licenses the discrete change-point prediction. Bostrom is thus the sharpest rival and the best foil.

## Major Positions

### The census-realist change-point programme (the reviewer's position, as refined here)
- **Proponents**: ChatGPT 5.6 Pro outer review §4.2; no external author holds it in this form.
- **Core claim**: a real arity change in *P(O | C₁…Cₙ, X)* leaves a conditional change-point, or the census is idle.
- **Relation to site tenets**: it is the Map's own P-SC1 with a time index. Adopting it commits the Map to the *discrete* census (against Bostrom) and to pruning the programme to genuine census events (against the reviewer's own six-domain list).

### Substrate-transition physicalism
- **Proponents**: Proekt, Kelz, Hudson, Lee, Mashour, Tagliazucchi, Toker.
- **Core claim**: all observed change-points at consciousness transitions are network phenomena — bistability, explosive synchronisation, departure from criticality — universal and cause-independent.
- **Relation to site tenets**: conflicts with Tenet 1 only if it claims completeness; as a description of the *state* face it is compatible, and the Map already reads these transitions as interface disruption. Provides the null every census claim must beat.

### Parfitian reductionism about the census
- **Proponents**: Parfit (1971, 1984); [[concepts/parfit-reductionism]].
- **Core claim**: there is no further fact about subject count across fission; the "census" is a convention.
- **Relation to site tenets**: rejects P-I1 and P-I4; predicts *no* census change-point at fission because there is no census. The change-point framing gives the Map/Parfit disagreement an empirical shadow — one event vs. none — even though P-I3 makes it unreadable now.

### Computationalist duplication with fractional experience
- **Proponent**: Bostrom (2006).
- **Core claim**: two identical brains, two experiences; unreliable or partially parallel implementation yields a fractional number of experiences.
- **Relation to site tenets**: half-aligned, half-hostile — see above.

## Key Debates

### Is "leaves every conditioning grain unchanged" even well-formed across an arity change?
- **Sides**: the reviewer poses it as a theorem to be proved; the causal-abstraction literature says the pre- and post-models must first be related by a transformation.
- **Current state**: open, and this note's proposal is that the article state the theorem in abstraction terms (exact vs. uniform transformation) rather than in "grain" terms.

### Discrete or continuous census?
- **Sides**: primitive-thisness (discrete) vs. Bostrom's fractional experience (continuous).
- **Current state**: decides whether change-point detection or regime-drift is the right formalism; the Map has a principled answer but has never stated it in these terms.

### Which of the six domains are census events at all?
- **Sides**: the reviewer lists six; P-SC3/P-CS4 remove three.
- **Current state**: settled by the Map's own register; the article should make the pruning its first structural move.

### Can observational data ever separate horn (b) from horn (c)?
- **Sides**: Andersen — no, equilibrium-maintaining structure defeats observational inference; the born-preserving-causal-efficacy page — yes, by intervention on intention.
- **Current state**: ongoing; converges on the existing "manipulate intention" design.

## Gaps in Research

- **No primary source located** for a quantified timeline of the acute disconnection syndrome's resolution after callosotomy; the article must not assert weeks or months without one.
- **Casarotto's 0.31 cut-off** is quoted only from secondary sources; the abstract gives sensitivity/specificity, not the number.
- **Zhang & Spirtes (2008)** and **Steyn-Ross et al. (2004)** abstracts were not obtainable raw; cited for metadata only. The minimality argument in this note is the note's own derivation and needs a source-check against Zhang & Spirtes's decomposition before it is asserted in an article.
- **No literature at all** applies change-point detection to *conditional* (consciousness-conditioned) outcome structure; every change-point paper found is on the physical time series. The programme is genuinely novel and the article should say so without overclaiming feasibility.
- **AI start/stop/copy**: no empirical literature; the domain is thought-experimental. The Map's five-requirement channel test predicts a *null* at copying of a digital system, which is a stated prediction, not a gap.
- **Davidson/Chalmers on psychophysical laws** were searched but not used: the anomalous-monism literature concerns strict laws vs. none, not arity change, and adds nothing here; recorded so the next researcher does not repeat the search.
