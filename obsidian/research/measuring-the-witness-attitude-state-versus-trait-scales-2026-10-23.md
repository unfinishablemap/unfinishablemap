## Executive Summary

The Map's witness argument and its one empirical test meet at a measurement question that no Map page found by this search addresses (scope: [Coverage](#coverage)). [[brain-stimulation-and-the-witness-mode]] needs a *state* witness profile: non-identification, non-reactivity and non-judging rated for the session, plus retained control (its L86 and L100). [[witness-consciousness]] L113 (under its §Decentering heading, L111) describes decentering with the three processes of Bernstein et al.'s model, without citing the model or any instrument. The field's instruments split on three axes, and each split matters to the Map:

1. **Time-scale.**
   - *Trait or dispositional* measures: the FFMQ, the Experiences Questionnaire (EQ), the Equanimity Scale-16 (ES-16) and the NADA-T.
   - *State* measures, rated for a just-finished session: the Toronto Mindfulness Scale (TMS), the State Mindfulness Scale (SMS) and the NADA-S.
   - *Momentary* measures: experience sampling.
2. **Component.** Bernstein et al. (2015) analyse decentering into "meta-awareness, disidentification from internal experience, and reduced reactivity to thought content". They classify the two decentering subscales, the EQ and the TMS, as "primarily measures of ... disidentification from internal experience". On reactivity, "no items in the EQ clearly reflect reduced reactivity to thought content, and only one item in the Decentering subscale of the TMS reflects this process". By 2019 the same group concluded that "no single or group of existing self-report scales assesses all three proposed metacognitive processes comprehensively".
3. **Method**: retrospective self-report, in-session self-report, experience sampling, and behavioural tasks. The behavioural tasks are breath counting (Levinson et al. 2014), the SES-IAT for self-identification with an experience (Hadash et al. 2016) and an equanimity "decoupling" task (Hadash et al. 2016, [META]).

**The Pons et al. (2026) facets are dispositional.** Non-judging and non-reactivity in Pons et al. are two FFMQ facets rated "from 1 (Never or very rarely true) to 5 (Very often or always true)". Goldberg et al. (2016) call the FFMQ "a widely used self-report measure of dispositional mindfulness".

**No validated session form of those facets was found.** In this search's scope there is no validated session-referenced form of non-judging or non-reactivity. A Waikato thesis by Truong (2020) tried to assemble a state subscale from the FFMQ's least stable items, and it "did not result in acceptable psychometric properties" ([THESIS-ABS]). By contrast, the NADA-S shows that a state form made by rewording trait items *can* be validated: one randomized experiment, N = 53 (Hanley et al. 2018).

**Decentering is theorised as a state that may harden into a trait.** Bernstein et al. "primarily conceptualize the processes subserving these constructs as mental states". They "speculate that if activated repeatedly ... [they] may grow more stable or trait-like". So a trait score reports a disposition, and it cannot certify that any particular episode had the attitude.

**The field's own critique of self-report is sharp.** It also falls hardest on the meditator/non-meditator contrasts that the Map's contemplative evidence uses.
- **Grossman (2011)** lists, among other problems:
  - "inequivalence of semantic item interpretation among different groups";
  - "response biases related to degree of experience with mindfulness practice";
  - "conflation of perceived mindfulness competencies with valuations of importance or meaningfulness".
- **Van Dam et al. (2018)** add three points:
  - different factor structures between meditators and non-meditators;
  - demand characteristics, "a potential conflation of desire to be 'mindful' with actually being 'mindful'";
  - an irony: if training really improved introspective accuracy, that "could increase honest responding, thereby exacerbating between group confounds".
- **Goldberg et al. (2016)**: FFMQ facets rose in an active control without meditation instruction, and the measure "failed to show discriminant validity".
- **Desbordes et al. (2015)**, on equanimity: "most individuals will not be able to accurately assess their own levels of equanimity".
- **The counterweight**: Baer, Samuel & Lykins (2011) found differential item functioning "minimal" in demographically matched meditators and non-meditators. Any article must carry it.

**The only "Witness Consciousness" factor this search found is weak evidence.** It is Factor 12 of Gamma & Metzinger's (2021) MPE-92M. It rests chiefly on two items: "Did you have the experience of a passive observer being present?" and "...an impersonal observer being present?". Respondents answered them about one recalled episode, chosen as "particularly salient and/or one which you can remember particularly clearly", in a self-selected online sample. The authors' own limitations section names:
- theory contamination;
- "response drift";
- the unreliability of memory over "possibly even decades";
- a "logical or a performative fallacy" in any claim to remember a selfless state.

**What follows for the Map.**
- **What the instruments can show:** whether a *reported* attitude was present in a session, whether it changes with training, and whether it covaries with behaviour.
- **What they cannot show:**
  - the witness's irreducibility;
  - the subject–object structure the Map argues from.
- **What they cannot separate:** the production, active-inference and interface readings of [[brain-stimulation-and-the-witness-mode#what-each-reading-predicts|brain-stimulation]]. Each predicts the same scores.
- **The cost to the Map:** the field's response-bias worry cuts against the Map's use of meditators' reports exactly as much as against hype about mindfulness-based interventions. The article must say this plainly.
- **Weak Revelation** ([[revelation-thesis]]): a report that awareness presents as an observer distinct from its contents is a report of a presentation, and the presentation does not certify its nature.

## Key Sources {#key-sources}

### Bernstein, A., Hadash, Y., Lichtash, Y., Tanay, G., Shepherd, K. & Fresco, D. M. (2015). "Decentering and Related Constructs: A Critical Review and Metacognitive Processes Model." *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 10(5): 599–617. DOI 10.1177/1745691615594577 [V-full, PMC5103165 author manuscript]

- **Type**: Review and theoretical model.
- **Key points**:
  - **Model.** Decentering-related constructs "reflect a common mental phenomenon subserved by three interrelated metacognitive processes: meta-awareness, disidentification from internal experience, and reduced reactivity to thought content" (abstract).
  - **The construct family** (the umbrella list): self-distanced perspective, cognitive distancing, cognitive defusion, detached mindfulness, metacognitive awareness, metacognitive mode, mindfulness (Bishop et al. 2004), reperceiving (Shapiro et al. 2006), self-as-context.
  - **Instruments.** "Two self-report instruments were developed to operationalize and measure decentering: the Decentering subscale of the Experiences Questionnaire (EQ; Fresco, Moore, et al., 2007) and the Decentering sub-scale of the Toronto Mindfulness Scale (TMS; Lau et al., 2006)." Then: "no items in the EQ clearly reflect reduced reactivity to thought content, and only one item in the Decentering subscale of the TMS reflects this process (Item 4; Lau et al., 2006). Accordingly, we conceptualize the Decentering sub-scales of the EQ and TMS as primarily measures of the more specific decentering-related process of disidentification from internal experience."
  - **Table 2 classifies the two subscales by time-scale.**
    - EQ Decentering "Measures trait-level capacity to take a metacognitively detached or observant perspective of one's thoughts and emotions".
    - TMS Decentering is "A state measure of 'awareness of one's experience with some distance and disidentification rather than being carried away by one's thoughts and feelings' (Lau et al., 2006, p. 1452; e.g., 'I experienced myself as separate from my changing thoughts and feelings')".
  - **Trait or state.** "Some scholars have conceptualized decentering and related constructs largely as traits ... whereas other scholars have related to them as states ... In the proposed metacognitive processes model of decentering, we primarily conceptualize the processes subserving these constructs as mental states. However, we speculate that if activated repeatedly in time and across contexts, expression of these metacognitive processes may grow more stable or trait-like."
  - **Method limits.** "To date, efforts have been largely limited to self-report measurement ... Laboratory-based behavioral measurement is lacking. Such measurement methods are important to move the field beyond reliance on self-report measures and toward biobehavioral or performance-based indices of decentering."
  - **The observer/observed distinction in their own theory.** "We propose that meta-awareness engenders disidentification from internal experience because the act of observing subjective experience creates a distinction (i.e., disidentification) between the observing self or consciousness and the observed subjective experience". **Read this carefully.** On their model the distinction is *created by* an act of observing, and they propose neural substrates (salience-network meta-awareness; DMN self-referential processing). That is closer to the constructivist reading than to the Map's witness-as-condition reading. It is no ally.
  - **On reperceiving**: "We are not aware of any other empirical studies on reperceiving" (beyond two that used the EQ).
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. A psychological process model with proposed neural substrates and no metaphysics.

### Bernstein, A., Hadash, Y. & Fresco, D. M. (2019). "Metacognitive processes model of decentering: emerging methods and insights." *Current Opinion in Psychology* 28: 245–251. DOI 10.1016/j.copsyc.2019.01.019 [V-full, PMC7459352 author manuscript]

- **Key points**:
  - Earlier science was "limited primarily by reliance on empirically modest methods (e.g., retrospective self-report, cross-section designs)".
  - **Scales.** "At least seven published self-report scales of decentering and related constructs" exist. Two independent psychometric studies (Hadash, Lichtash & Bernstein 2017; Naragon-Gainey & DeMarree 2017) each found two factors:
    - one "reflecting disidentified and non-reactive meta-awareness of experience", labelled "Intentional Decentered Perspective" and "Observer Perspective";
    - one "reflecting reduced reactivity to thought content", labelled "Automatic Reactivity to Thought Content" and "Reduced Struggle with Inner Experience".
  - "First, no single or group of existing self-report scales assesses all three proposed metacognitive processes comprehensively." The scales "are not interchangeable"; several "demonstrated low internal consistencies".
  - **Experience sampling.** "Experience sampling (ES) methods help to address key limitations of traditional retrospective self-report assessment of subjective experience (e.g., retrospective recall bias)." Shoham et al. (2017) found "cumulative elevation in all three metacognitive processes over the course of the intervention", and the predicted relations among them were "significantly stronger in experience samples during mindfulness meditation practice".
  - **Behavioural tasks.**
    - The SES-IAT: lower implicit identification with fear went with "greater levels of self-reported meta-awareness of emotions and trait decentering".
    - A "Simulated Thoughts Paradigm" for reactivity, which was *submitted / conference presentation* at the time. **Not citable as a published measure.**
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.

### Lau, M. A., Bishop, S. R., Segal, Z. V., Buis, T., Anderson, N. D., Carlson, L., Shapiro, S., Carmody, J., Abbey, S. & Devins, G. (2006). "The Toronto Mindfulness Scale: Development and validation." *Journal of Clinical Psychology* 62(12): 1445–1467. DOI 10.1002/jclp.20326 [V-abs; form text AS-QUOTED-IN Kihlstrom page]

- **Key points**:
  - The abstract reports "two factors, Curiosity and Decentering"; "TMS scores increased with increasing mindfulness meditation experience"; and "Decentering scores predicted improvements in clinical outcome". Its conclusion: "Thus, the TMS is a promising measure of the mindfulness state with good psychometric properties and predictive of treatment outcome."
  - **The form** (Kihlstrom's Berkeley page, secondary) instructs: "how well does the statement describe what you just experienced, just now?", rated 0 "Not at All" to 4 "Very much". It lists 13 items. Item 1 is "I experienced myself as separate from my changing thoughts and feelings" (also in Bernstein 2015 Table 2). Item 4 is "I experienced my thoughts more as events in my mind than as a necessarily accurate reflection of the way things 'really' are".
  - **Not verified**: which items load on Curiosity and which on Decentering, beyond Bernstein's count of seven Decentering items.
- **Map link**: Lord et al. (2024) used the TMS. Its Decentering "rose within both groups" (sham and active), per `research/brain-stimulation-and-the-witness-mode-2026-10-03` L58. On Bernstein's classification, that rise was mainly in *disidentification*. It bears on the witness profile's non-identification limb, not on its non-reactivity limb.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.

### Tanay, G. & Bernstein, A. (2013). "State Mindfulness Scale (SMS): Development and initial validation." *Psychological Assessment* 25(4): 1286–1299. DOI 10.1037/a0034044 [V-abs]

- **Key points**:
  - The SMS was developed "to reflect traditional Buddhist and contemporary psychological science models of mindfulness not similarly reflected in extant published measures".
  - Its structure is "1 second-order state mindfulness factor, and 2 first-order factors, one reflecting state mindfulness of bodily sensations and the other state mindfulness of mental events".
  - It showed "incremental sensitivity to change in state mindfulness over time", and the abstract frames mindfulness "as a statelike mental behavior".
- **Not verified**: the item count (21, per a trial protocol: [LEAD]) and the recall window. A search found no confirmation of a "past 15 minutes" instruction. Do not state either.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.

### Fresco, D. M., Moore, M. T., van Dulmen, M. H. M., Segal, Z. V., Ma, S. H., Teasdale, J. D. & Williams, J. M. G. (2007). "Initial Psychometric Properties of the Experiences Questionnaire: Validation of a Self-Report Measure of Decentering." *Behavior Therapy* 38(3): 234–246. DOI 10.1016/j.beth.2006.08.003 [V-abs]

- **Key points**:
  - Definition: "Decentering is defined as the ability to observe one's thoughts and feelings as temporary, objective events in the mind, as opposed to reflections of the self that are necessarily true."
  - The EQ "was designed to measure both decentering and rumination but has not been empirically validated". "A single, unifactorial decentering construct emerged."
- **Attribution guard**: Desbordes et al. (2015) call it "The Experience Questionnaire developed by Teasdale, Segal and Williams". Cite Fresco et al. 2007 as the validation, as Bernstein et al. do.

### Baer, R. A., Smith, G. T., Hopkins, J., Krietemeyer, J. & Toney, L. (2006). "Using Self-Report Assessment Methods to Explore Facets of Mindfulness." *Assessment* 13(1): 27–45. DOI 10.1177/1073191105283504 [V-abs]

- **Key points**:
  - The study pooled items from "five recently developed mindfulness questionnaires" in "Two large samples of undergraduate students". Factor analyses found "five clear, interpretable facets".
  - It also found that "the factor structure of mindfulness may vary with meditation experience".
- **Guard**: the 2006 abstract names neither "FFMQ" nor the five facets. For the facet names, quote Baer et al. 2008 (next entry) or Pons et al. 2026. The FFMQ has 39 items (Baer, Samuel & Lykins 2011 abstract: "18 of its 39 items").

### Baer, R. A., Smith, G. T., Lykins, E., Button, D., Krietemeyer, J., Sauer, S., Walsh, E., Duggan, D. & Williams, J. M. G. (2008). "Construct Validity of the Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire in Meditating and Nonmeditating Samples." *Assessment* 15(3): 329–342. DOI 10.1177/1073191107313003 [V-abs]

- "five component skills: observing, describing, acting with awareness, nonjudging of inner experience, and nonreactivity to inner experience. These elements of mindfulness can be measured with the Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire (FFMQ)."
- "As expected, relationships between the observing facet and psychological adjustment varied with meditation experience."

### Baer, R. A., Samuel, D. B. & Lykins, E. L. B. (2011). "Differential Item Functioning on the Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire Is Minimal in Demographically Matched Meditators and Nonmeditators." *Assessment* 18(1): 3–10 (online 2010-12-30). DOI 10.1177/1073191110392498 [V-abs]

- **What the earlier study found.** The abstract describes Van Dam, Earleywine & Danoff-Burg (2009): "high levels of differential item functioning (DIF) for 18 of its 39 items in meditating and nonmeditating samples that were not demographically matched. In particular, meditators were more likely to endorse positively worded items whereas nonmeditators were more likely to deny negatively worded (reverse-scored) items."
- **The replication.** In matched samples "(n = 115 each)", "evidence for DIF was minimal". Their conclusion: "DIF based on items' scoring direction is not problematic when the Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire is used to compare demographically similar meditators and nonmeditators."
- **This is the counterweight and must appear.** Its scope is DIF *by scoring direction*, in matched samples. It does not answer the semantic-interpretation or valuation worries.

### Goldberg, S. B., Wielgosz, J., Dahl, C., Schuyler, B., MacCoon, D. S., Rosenkranz, M., Lutz, A., Sebranek, C. A. & Davidson, R. J. (2016). "Does the Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire measure what we think it does? Construct validity evidence from an active controlled randomized clinical trial." *Psychological Assessment* 28(8): 1009–1014. DOI 10.1037/pas0000233 [V-abs]

- The FFMQ is "a widely used self-report measure of dispositional mindfulness".

### Van Dam, N. T., van Vugt, M. K., Vago, D. R., Schmalzl, L., Saron, C. D., Olendzki, A., Meissner, T., Lazar, S. W., Kerr, C. E., Gorchov, J., Fox, K. C. R., Field, B. A., Britton, W. - **Key points** (all from §"Problematic aspects of self-report questionnaires" and the two sections after it):
  - **Construct validity.** "Questionnaire-based scales that purport to measure mindfulness offer, at best, modest evidence of nomothetic span." The correlates "may actually be suggestive of a lack of differentiation from broad features of personality and temperament".
  - **Group differences in the instruments.** "several of these scales exhibit different factor structures and response properties between meditators and non-meditators (e.g., ... These findings suggest lack of equivalence on a common underlying latent variable, as well as change in how the items are interpreted."
  - **Demand characteristics.** "one who has practiced mindfulness meditation may understand and value items differently than someone who has not practiced (though see Baer, Samuel, & Lykins, 2011) – a potential conflation of desire to be 'mindful' with actually being 'mindful' (cf.   - **Introspection.** "Self-report based measures of mindfulness may be particularly vulnerable to limitations of introspection because participants may not know exactly which aspects of mental states should be taken into account when making personal assessments."
  - **Social desirability.** "social-desirability biases may be especially pronounced in self-reports about 'mindfulness'. This is because participants/patients often learn to expect/value improved attention, equanimity, and so forth, while experimenters often fail to hide their hopes".
  - **Introspective accuracy.** "While some investigators have suggested that increased mindfulness improves the quality of participants' introspections ..., this claim has not been well established (cf.   - **The irony.** "Ironically, were it shown that mindfulness practice improves the quality of participants' introspections, this might deepen other problems in mindfulness research. For example, if mindfulness-based enhancements of introspective accuracy are real, such enhancements could increase honest responding, thereby exacerbating between group confounds."
  - **Behavioural measures.** "Perhaps because of such pitfalls in introspection, many studies have focused instead on neurobehavioral performance ... However, these studies have inconsistent and sometimes contradictory empirical findings".

## Major Positions {#positions}

### Dispositional measurement (Baer; FFMQ, ES-16, EQ)
- **Core claim**: mindfulness and its attitudinal facets are stable individual differences, measurable by self-report and related to well-being and symptoms.
- **Relation to the Map**: answers "what is this person like", never "what was that episode like". Useful for training effects. It cannot feed the falsifier's state profile.

### State measurement (Bishop/Lau; Tanay & Bernstein; Hanley)
- **Core claim**: mindfulness is a state-like quality that can be rated for a just-ended session. The TMS, SMS and NADA-S are the instruments.
- **Relation to the Map**: the right time-scale for the falsifier. The TMS covers non-identification well and non-reactivity barely (Bernstein).

### The process model (Bernstein, Hadash, Fresco)
- **Core claim**: decentering is three interrelated metacognitive processes, primarily states, possibly trait-like with repetition. Measurement should target processes, move beyond self-report and use experience sampling and behavioural tasks.
- **Relation to the Map**: supplies the components the witness profile needs. Its account of the observer/observed distinction ("creates a distinction") is psychological and leans constructivist; the Map can use the components, but not that account, as support.

### Self-report scepticism (Grossman; Van Dam et al.; Desbordes et al. on equanimity)
- **Core claim**: questionnaire scores of mindfulness have weak construct representation. Meditators and non-meditators may read items differently. Practice-linked response bias, demand characteristics and social desirability are live, and introspective gains are unestablished.
- **Relation to the Map**: cuts both ways. It undermines the hype the Map also distrusts, and it undermines the Map's own reliance on meditators' reports in the same way.

### The FFMQ's defenders (Baer, Samuel & Lykins 2011)
- **Core claim**: scoring-direction DIF is minimal in demographically matched meditator and non-meditator samples.
- **Relation to the Map**: limits the scope of the DIF argument. It leaves the semantic-interpretation and valuation worries untouched.

### Objective and behavioural programmes (Desbordes; Levinson; Hadash & Bernstein)
- **Core claim**: replace or supplement self-report with performance, physiology and implicit association.
- **Relation to the Map**: behavioural footprints of the attitude are what Tenet 3 *permits*. Each rival also predicts them, so they are compatibility evidence only.

### Phenomenological profiling (Gamma & Metzinger)
- **Core claim**: build fine-grained retrospective profiles of rare states from practitioners' reports.
- **Relation to the Map**: closest to the Map's subject (a "Witness Consciousness" factor). By its authors' own account it is also the most exposed to recall, selection and theory contamination.

## Key Debates {#debates}

### 1. Can a trait scale answer a state question?
- **Sides**: Pons et al. use trait facets "to explore whether differences in experience appraisal might reflect trait mindfulness", a legitimate trait question. Brain-stimulation's falsifier asks a state question.
- **Core point**: a disposition raises the probability of a state, but it does not certify one. Bernstein et al.'s model makes the trait the sediment of repeated states, so the inference runs from many states to a trait. Running it the other way requires assumptions about the particular episode that the trait score does not contain.
- **Current state**: the G-theory programme (Medvedev 2017 [LEAD]; Truong 2020 [THESIS-ABS]) aims to quantify how much of a scale's variance is state. The NADA shows state forms can be built and validated. No validated state form of non-judging or non-reactivity was found in this search.

### 2. Do meditators read the items differently?
- **Sides**:
  - Yes: Grossman 2011 (e)–(g); Van Dam et al. 2018; Van Dam, Earleywine & Danoff-Burg 2009 as described.
  - Less than feared, in matched samples and for scoring direction: Baer, Samuel & Lykins 2011.
  - The observing facet varies with experience: Baer 2006, 2008.
- **Current state**: unresolved. Post-2020 invariance work was not found in this search (one WebSearch); treat it as open.

### 3. Does training improve introspection, and would it help if it did?
- **Sides**:
  - Fox et al. 2012 and Baird et al. 2014, already on the Map in `contemplative-epistemology`.
  - Against: Van Dam et al.: "not been well established", and verifying it "would require accurate 'third-person' evidence about the subjective contents of an introspector's 'first-person' consciousness".
- **The twist**: real gains would *worsen* between-group confounds.
- **Relation to the Map**: Van Dam's verification point parallels [[agency-void#The Verification Circularity]], which the Map already accepts for causal power. The article may note the parallel, but must not present Van Dam et al. as making a metaphysical point.

### 4. Can behaviour replace report?
- **Sides**:
  - Desbordes et al. propose objective lab measurement, while conceding that "a self-report component will be useful for developing and validating the interpretation of objective metrics".
  - Van Dam et al. find the neurobehavioural results "inconsistent and sometimes contradictory".
  - Hadash & Bernstein 2019 find four of 12 behavioural measures acceptable.
- **Core point**: a behavioural index of an *experiential* attitude is validated against report. This brings back the circularity that the [[descriptive-experience-sampling#fidelity-validity|DES fidelity/validity distinction]] already names.

## The Map's Question: What the Instruments Can and Cannot Show {#maps-question}

**(a) For the brain-stimulation falsifier (L86, L100).** The design's witness profile has four limbs:

1. **Non-identification for the session.** Covered by TMS Decentering (mainly disidentification, on Bernstein's analysis), and partly by SMS mental-events items.
2. **Non-reactivity and non-judging "rated for the session rather than as traits".** No validated session instrument for these two was found in this search's scope. The options are:
   - an ad hoc state rewording of the FFMQ facets, which carries the instruction-adaptation risk Pons et al. themselves flag for the CDS, and of which Truong's thesis reports one failed item-selection variant;
   - experience sampling within the session;
   - a behavioural reactivity index. The cited one (the Simulated Thoughts Paradigm) was unpublished as of 2019.
3. **State nondual awareness.** The NADA-S exists and has been used in the tFUS programme (Ehmann et al. 2025).
4. **Retained control.** Behavioural by design (a timed, cued switch), so it escapes self-report.

Conclusion: the falsifier is specifiable today only with one limb measured ad hoc. That is the article's most concrete contribution and should be stated as a scoped finding, not a literature-wide absence.

**(b) For the witness-structure argument.**
- [[witness-consciousness]] §The Witness Cannot Be the Brain, and [[the-observer-witness-in-meditation]], argue from structure: the witness "cannot become an object". No instrument here measures structure. They measure *reported relation to contents* (disidentification), *reported reactivity*, and performance.
- Even the MPE-92M factor asks whether "a passive observer" or "an impersonal observer" was *present* in a remembered episode, which is a report of how the episode presented.
- High scores fit the Map's framing. They also fit the self-model reading ([[predictive-self-binding-and-the-naturalist-challenge]]) and active inference, which predict the same reports.
- **Weak Revelation** ([[revelation-thesis]]): the presentation is real data, and it does not certify what presents.

**(c) For the Map's use of meditators' reports: the symmetric cut.** The pages that lean on trained reports would inherit the same exposures:
- `contemplative-epistemology` L60–L62, L103 (Fox et al. 2012 at correlational strength);
- `contemplative-practice-as-philosophical-evidence`;
- `methodology-of-consciousness-research` L134 ("trained meditators produce more consistent and fine-grained phenomenological reports");
- `phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence` L162.

Those exposures are:
- semantic inequivalence across groups;
- practice-linked response bias;
- valuing being mindful conflated with being mindful;
- investment bias ("biases may occur in meditation practitioners due to their large investment of time and effort");
- theory contamination (Gamma & Metzinger);
- the irony that better introspection would sharpen between-group confounds.

The article must say plainly that this worry, raised by the field, cuts against the Map's evidence as much as against mindfulness hype. A practised report is still a report.

**(d) For Tenet 3 and epiphenomenalism.**
- That the attitude is known only through report and performance is a limit on *access*. It does not show the attitude or the experience is causally idle.
- The behavioural measures show the reported attitude *covaries* with performance (breath counting with meta-awareness; the SES-IAT with self-reported meta-awareness). That covariation is what Bidirectional Interaction permits. Every rival on [[brain-stimulation-and-the-witness-mode#what-each-reading-predicts|brain-stimulation]] predicts it too, so it is compatibility evidence, not support.
- Never write that the measurement problem "suggests" epiphenomenalism, or that it is evidence against it.

**(e) What a more decisive measurement would need.**
- State-referenced or momentary ratings of all three Bernstein processes.
- At least one behavioural index per process.
- Instruction-matched sham and active-control arms (Goldberg's HEP lesson).
- Measurement invariance checked between practised and naive groups.
- Blind outcome scoring.

Even then, the result would discriminate between attitude profiles (witness against depersonalisation), not between metaphysics.

## Guards for the Article Writer (Binding) {#guards}

### Author stances

- **Bernstein, Hadash, Lichtash, Tanay, Fresco.**
  - Theirs is a clinical and psychological process model, with neural substrates proposed (salience network for meta-awareness; DMN for self-referential identification).
  - "observing self or consciousness" is their psychological vocabulary. Their claim that the act of observing "creates a distinction" leans constructivist.
  - **Never enlist them for the witness's irreducibility.**
- **Lau, Bishop, Segal et al.** Operationalists: a state measure built on a consensus operational definition. No metaphysics.
- **Baer and colleagues.** Psychometricians of dispositional mindfulness. Baer, Samuel & Lykins 2011 *defend* the FFMQ's cross-group use. Report them as the counterweight, not as critics.
- **Grossman.**
  - A critic of *current self-report questionnaires*, especially trait inventories and the MAAS, from a Buddhist-informed standpoint.
  - His remedy is long practice by researchers, which privileges practised first-person understanding.
  - Do not report him as rejecting measurement or contemplative evidence wholesale. Do not report him as an ally of the Map's first-person authority either.
- **Van Dam et al.**
  - A methodological critique and prescriptive agenda (multimodal first- and third-person assessment). Not anti-meditation; the critique is of hype and poor method.
  - Fox is a co-author; do not say Fox repudiated Fox et al. 2012.
  - Van Dam co-authored Pons et al. 2026.
- **Desbordes et al.**
  - Theravāda-informed definitions and a physiological/neuroimaging measurement agenda.
  - "most individuals will not be able to accurately assess their own levels of equanimity" is their judgement, not a measured result.
- **Gamma & Metzinger.**
  - Metzinger's self-model theory is naturalist. The Factor-12 gloss describes the Advaita concept "on the level of phenomenology"; it is not an endorsement of Advaita metaphysics.
  - **Never enlist** them for dualism.
- **Rogers / Cheever / Cayoun / Shires (ES-16); Hanley, Nakamura & Garland (NADA); Levinson & Davidson; Medvedev group.** Psychometric developers. No metaphysical stances read.
- **No author in this note argues for dualism.** None argues for physicalism in the texts read either; most are silent on metaphysics. Write "their model is psychological" or "their framework is naturalist" only where stated: Metzinger's is, by his wider corpus, and the Map already says so elsewhere.

### The calibration rule (binding)

1. **Scales measure REPORTED attitude.** Every sentence that reports a score must keep the reporting verb, in the form "participants reported" or "scores on X". Never write "meditators were more non-reactive" from questionnaire data alone.
2. **A trait score cannot certify a state.** A dispositional score (FFMQ, EQ, ES-16, NADA-T) raises or lowers the probability of an episode's attitude, but it does not establish it. The brain-stimulation L82 correction stands; do not undo it.
3. **Fits without evidencing.** The witness attitude, as the field measures it, *fits* the Map's two-mode framing and does not evidence it. Every rival reading predicts the same scores.
4. **Weak Revelation only** ([[revelation-thesis]]). The presentation of an observer distinct from contents is data. It does not certify the observer's nature, its irreducibility, or that "it cannot become an object".
5. **The symmetric cut, said plainly.** The field's worries about meditators' response bias, semantic shift, investment bias and theory contamination apply to the Map's use of meditators' reports (contemplative-epistemology, contemplative-practice, methodology L134, phenomenal-authority L162) exactly as they apply to mindfulness-intervention claims. **The article must contain a sentence to this effect in the Map's own voice.**
6. **Never concede epiphenomenalism.** The access limit, that the attitude is known only by report and performance, is not evidence of causal idleness. Behavioural covariation is compatible with Tenet 3, and every rival predicts it. The article claims neither that it supports Tenet 3 nor that the measurement problem favours epiphenomenalism.
7. **Absence claims scoped** (see [Absence and Exclusivity Claims](#absence)).
8. **Tenet 5.** The simpler reading (production, or active inference) is not refuted by any measurement here. The Map may decline to treat parsimony as decisive; it may not claim the rivals are disconfirmed.

### Evidence not to lean on

- **Leigh, Bowen & Marlatt (2005) "binge-drinkers".** Van Dam et al. write that "experienced meditators were less 'mindful' than binge-drinkers". Leigh et al.'s own abstract reports only a positive mindfulness–smoking/binge-drinking association in undergraduates. The meditator comparison is cross-study. Do not use it.
- **MPE-92M Factor 12 as a measure of the witness *state*.**
  - It is two items, answered about one remembered, self-selected, salient episode, in a self-selected online sample.
  - Its 12-factor model fits "moderately well".
  - Use it as the clearest illustration of how far the field's one witness-named factor is from the falsifier's needs. Do not use it as evidence that the witness exists or has a given structure.
- **Truong (thesis) and Medvedev (lead) numbers.** Not verified at the journal.
- **Lord et al. (2024) TMS Decentering rise.** It rose in sham as well as active. It is not witness evidence, and on Bernstein's analysis it reflects disidentification only.

- **Behavioural measures as evidence for interactionism.** They are compatibility only.
- **ES-16 or NADA test–retest figures** as evidence about the witness. They concern scale stability.

## Absence and Exclusivity Claims (Scoped) {#absence}

Per `writing-style.md` §"Absence and Exclusivity Claims", every absence claim carries its search scope.

**On-Map absences (licensed, scoped).**
- Write: "no Map page the 2026-10-23 search found discusses the FFMQ, the Toronto Mindfulness Scale, the State Mindfulness Scale, Bernstein et al.'s decentering model or the self-report critique of Grossman and Van Dam et al."
- Scope: case-insensitive fixed-string search of live topics, concepts, apex, voids, positions and tenets, with positive controls (see [Coverage](#coverage)).
- Also licensed: "no Map page mentions the MPE-92M's 'Witness Consciousness' factor".
- Keep these out of the description and lede.

**Instrument-scope absences (licensed only with the scope).**
- Write: "this search found no validated session-referenced measure of non-judging or non-reactivity".
- Scope: Crossref, Europe PMC, OpenAlex and four WebSearch calls, 2026-10-23. Ad hoc state rewordings of FFMQ items exist in practice; one was attempted in Truong's thesis.
- **Do not write** "no state measure of non-reactivity exists".

**Literature-wide claims: not licensed. Do not write:**
- "Self-report measures of mindfulness are invalid". The critique is of construct representation and group equivalence, and Baer et al. defend cross-group use in matched samples.
- "Meditators' reports are biased". Write "may be biased, by mechanisms the field itself names".
- "There is no behavioural measure of decentering". Breath counting, the SES-IAT and the decoupling task exist; Hadash & Bernstein find four of 12 acceptable.
- "The field agrees that decentering is a state". Bernstein et al. say some scholars treat it "largely as traits".
- "Introspective accuracy does not improve with practice". Van Dam et al. say "not been well established", which is weaker.
- "The MPE-92M shows witness consciousness is a distinct dimension of experience". It reports a factor in one moderately fitting exploratory model.

**Currency.**
- Post-2020 coverage rests on the ES-16 abstracts (2021, 2023), the Truong thesis abstract (2020), Pons et al. (2026) and one invariance WebSearch that surfaced only 2015–2020 leads.
- Treat the self-report critique as **2016–2019-anchored** (Goldberg; Van Dam; Bernstein 2019; Grossman 2019).
- Do not write "recent work has resolved" or "current consensus".
