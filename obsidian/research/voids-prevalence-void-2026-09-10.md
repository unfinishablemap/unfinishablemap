# Research: Voids - The Prevalence Void

**Date**: 2026-09-10
**Also known as**: prevalence-induced concept change (PICC — the literature's term)
**Voids category**: Mixed — primarily **Unexplorable** (architectural), with the mechanism face actively deflating
**Search queries used**: Levari 2018 Science prevalence-induced concept change human judgment; prevalence-induced concept change replication critique criterion shift artifact; "range-frequency" "prevalence-induced concept change" Cognition 2022; prevalence-induced concept change 2024 2025 metacognition awareness; Haslam concept creep expanding harm concepts
**Disposition recommendation**: *Surveyed* — a standalone slot is defensible (see "Disposition"), but the operator decides

## Executive Summary

Every categorical judgement a mind makes is indexed to the recent distribution of instances it has encountered, and the judgement arrives carrying no record of that index. When blue dots become rare, purple dots start looking blue; when threatening faces become rare, neutral faces start looking threatening; when unethical research proposals become rare, innocuous ones start looking unethical (Levari et al. 2018). The verdict presents itself as a first-order fact about the stimulus — *that dot is blue* — never as a fact about the stimulus relative to a base rate the judge cannot inspect. The void has two faces with **opposite empirical fates**, which is what makes it worth the catalogue's attention. The **mechanism face** is deflating fast: Levari (2022) identified the computational process as a range-frequency compromise (Parducci 1963, 1965) and built an intervention that eliminates the effect. The **access face** is untouched, and the deflation sharpens rather than weakens it — the successful intervention works by altering the *stimulus environment*, not by giving the judge any inward view. Forewarning, explicit instruction, monetary incentive, abrupt onset and reversed direction all fail to eliminate the drift (Levari et al. 2018), and the one metacognitive gauge tested points the wrong way: higher pre-shift confidence predicts *greater* susceptibility (Ammalainen & Eppinger 2026, preprint).

Two features suit the Map's calibration discipline. The void is framework-independent and psychophysically measured, so it belongs to the class the [[what-voids-reveal|clustering audit]] treats as carrying what evidential weight the catalogue has. And it is self-implicating in an unusually direct way: the Map is itself a long-running detection task whose operators judge whether articles are good enough, whether a void is real, whether a citation is sound — under a prevalence of defects that its own work is designed to reduce.

## Naming and Coverage Hazards

Recorded so a later scan does not misread the catalogue's position.

**Nearest neighbour, opposite sign.** `research/voids-perceptual-history-void-2026-09-09` (banked one day before this note) surveys *serial dependence*: each percept is pulled **toward** the observer's recent perceptual history, tuned over 1–15 seconds, operating on individual percepts, and manufacturing felt stability. The prevalence void runs the other way. It is **contrastive, not assimilative** — when a category becomes rare its boundary *expands* into the territory it previously excluded, moving away from the prevailing distribution — and it operates on the **category boundary** over hundreds of trials rather than on a single percept over seconds. The two are the assimilative and contrastive halves of one architectural fact: judgement is reference-dependent and the reference is never exposed. That relation is worth an article's attention; it is not an overlap. ## Key Sources

### Prevalence-induced concept change in human judgment
- **Citation**: Levari, D. E., Gilbert, D. T., Wilson, T. D., Sievers, B., Amodio, D. M., & Wheatley, T. (2018). *Science* 360(6396), 1465–1467. DOI 10.1126/science.aap8731
- **Type**: Primary empirical paper (7 studies). Metadata Crossref-verified; quotes grep-verified against the full text.
- **Key points**:
  - Study 1: 1000 dots on a purple–blue continuum; after 200 trials the prevalence of blue was decreased for one condition. Participants began calling blue what they had previously called not-blue.
  - Studies 6 and 7 generalise from simple to complex concepts: 800 computer-generated faces on a threatening/non-threatening continuum, and 240 research proposals judged in the role of an Institutional Review Board reviewer.
  - Study 5 reversed the direction: when blue dots became *more* prevalent, participants *narrowed* the range of colours they called blue. The effect is a boundary tracking the distribution, not a one-way drift toward leniency.
- **Verbatim (abstract)**: "This 'prevalence-induced concept change' occurred even when participants were forewarned about it and even when they were instructed and paid to resist it."
- **Verbatim (results)**: "prevalence-induced concept change seen in Study 1 proved remarkably robust and was not eliminated by forewarning (Study 2), by instructions and incentives (Study 3), by sudden decreases in prevalence (Study 4), or by a reversal in the direction of the change in prevalence (Study 5)."
- **Verbatim (discussion)**: "even well-meaning agents may sometimes fail to recognize the success of their own efforts, simply because they view each new instance in the decreasingly problematic context that they themselves have brought about."
- **Tenet alignment**: The failure of forewarning is the load-bearing datum. A limit that survives being *told about it* is architectural rather than motivational — the discriminator the voids brief demands.

### Range-frequency effects can explain and eliminate prevalence-induced concept change
- **Citation**: Levari, D. E. (2022). *Cognition* 226, 105196. DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2022.105196
- **Type**: Primary — computational modelling plus intervention. Sole-authored by the first author of the 2018 paper. Metadata Crossref-verified; quotes grep-verified against the full text.
- **Key points**:
  - Trial-level computational modelling (Daw 2011 approach) compared candidate mechanisms — Bayesian perceptual models, decision-by-sampling (Stewart, Chater & Brown 2006), range-frequency theory — against human trial data.
  - Range-frequency theory won. Parducci & Wedell (1986) applied to categorical judgement: category boundaries are implicitly constructed from the *range* of stimuli encountered and the *frequency* rank of the current stimulus within it.
  - The intervention that works: making stimuli more intense as they become rare, which holds the range-frequency compromise fixed by compensating in the stimulus itself.
- **Verbatim (abstract)**: "finding that they are best explained as a range-frequency compromise in judgment. Finally, I test an intervention that successfully eliminates prevalence-induced concept change by making stimuli more intense as they become rare."
- **Verbatim (introduction, on the 2018 control studies)**: "None of these interventions were successful, perhaps in part because the mechanism driving the phenomenon was unknown."
- **Tenet alignment**: A textbook [[epistemological-limits-occams-razor|parsimony]] case. The simpler hypothesis — a general-purpose contextual-judgement mechanism, not a special-purpose bias — turned out to be the correct one, and it *dissolved* the mystery while leaving the first-person limit exactly where it was. Parsimony selected well here; the void survives anyway, which is the more interesting result.

### The Role of Confidence in Prevalence-Induced Concept Change
- **Citation**: Ammalainen, A., & Eppinger, B. (2026). PsyArXiv preprint, posted 2026-07-17. DOI 10.31234/osf.io/uxtvr_v2
- **Type**: **Preprint — not peer-reviewed.** Preregistered, N = 81. Metadata and abstract Crossref-verified. Flagged explicitly because the access face leans on it.
- **Key points**:
  - The authors predicted that higher confidence in one's original concept would *protect* against drift. The preregistered result was the opposite.
  - Replicated with a second, non-self-report measure: the decision-boundary parameter from drift-diffusion modelling.
  - Their proposed mechanism: high confidence triggers an intuitive strategy that increases sensitivity to environmental cues, while low confidence produces deliberate choices that stick to the old concept.
- **Verbatim (abstract)**: "Higher self-reported confidence before the prevalence shift is associated with a higher probability of changing concepts after the prevalence shift."
- **Tenet alignment**: A candidate new instance of [[anti-correlated-metacognitive-signal|the anti-correlated metacognitive signal]] in a regime that concept's page does not yet cover. **Scope caution**: this is an association between pre-shift confidence and susceptibility, *not* a demonstration that confidence fails to detect drift as it happens. The stronger claim is not in evidence and must not be written as though it were. The concept's page already insists its anti-correlation claim is regime-conditional and not a universal inversion; that discipline applies here.

### 'Concept creep' in perceptions of mental illness
- **Citation**: Speerforck, S., Jürgensen, V., Göbel, M., Meyer, N., & Schomerus, G. "'Concept creep' in perceptions of mental illness — an experimental examination of prevalence-induced concept change." *European Archives of Psychiatry and Clinical Neuroscience* 276(1), 33–37. DOI 10.1007/s00406-023-01737-0
- **Metadata note**: published online 2024-01-17 but assigned to print volume 276(1). Cite the online year with the print volume, and do not silently "correct" either — the online/print split is real and Crossref records both.
- **Type**: Primary empirical, real-world stigma domain.
- **Key points**:
  - 138 students; statements categorised as mentally healthy / ambiguous / mentally ill across 240 trials. Decreasing condition: illness-statement prevalence fell from 33.3% to 4.12%.
  - Condition × trial interaction: b = 0.51, SE = 0.22, z = 2.33, p = 0.0197, OR = 1.66.
  - **Honest weakness**: Monte Carlo simulations yielded 65.4% power for the interaction term. The authors themselves describe this as just high enough. A later article should not present this as a strong confirmation.
- **Verbatim**: "In the group where the proportion of clearly mentally ill statements was reduced during the experiment, a concept change of mental illness evolved: participants were more likely to identify a statement as denoting a mental illness."
- **Tenet alignment**: Extends the void from laboratory continua to a socially consequential category.

### Prevalence-Induced Concept Change: Universal or Context-Dependent? Implications for Social Psychology and AI Cognition
- **Citation**: Albrecht, R., & Spektor, M. S. (2025). *Proceedings of the Annual Meeting of the Cognitive Science Society* 47. https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5nj10237 (CC-BY; authors at Freiburg and VinUniversity)
- **Type**: Peer-reviewed conference proceedings; computational simulation plus review.
- **Key points**:
  - LSTM networks (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber 1997) trained on shifting category distributions **do not** show PICC. They shift boundaries toward the now-*majority* category — the opposite direction — consistent with an error-minimisation objective. Robust across hyperparameter configurations.
  - The authors read this as evidence that simple bottom-up sequential learning is insufficient, and that structured priors, contextual sensitivity or internal feedback are required.
  - They argue PICC may be domain-*dependent*, citing the Devine body-size results as moderation by socially acquired priors.
- **Verbatim (abstract)**: "We show that a standard AI model for sequential learning, trained on dynamic category distributions, does not display PICC, instead favoring the now-majority category. This opposite-PICC effect suggests that simple sequential learning may be insufficient to produce PICC. Additional mechanisms, such as structured priors, contextual sensitivity, or internal feedback, seem necessary for its emergence."
- **⚠️ Secondary-source compression to avoid propagating**: this paper glosses Devine as PICC "in judgments of female but not male body size." The primary titles say something more precise — for men the effect appears for **muscular** bodies but not thin ones. That is domain-specific moderation, not male immunity. Cite the primaries.
- **Tenet alignment**: Directly serves [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers|non-human minds as void-explorers]], and with an unexpected shape — the artificial system does not merely lack the human limit, it drifts the *opposite way*.

### The body-size series (domain specificity)
- **Citations**: Devine, S., Germain, N., Ehrlich, S., & Eppinger, B. (2022), "Changes in the Prevalence of Thin Bodies Bias Young Women's Judgments About Body Size", *Psychological Science* 33, 1212–1225, DOI 10.1177/09567976221082941. Devine, S., Germain, N., Kasprzyk, A., & Eppinger, B. (2025), "Changes in the prevalence of muscular, but not thin, bodies bias young men's judgments about body size", *Psychology of Men & Masculinities* 26, 129–143, DOI 10.1037/men0000478.
- **Key points**: The effect appears along the dimension that is culturally salient for the group judged — thinness for young women, muscularity for young men — which is the strongest available evidence that the void's *extent* is shaped by acquired priors even if its mechanism is general.
- **Calibration note**: a third-party replication effort (Clearer Thinking, report #11) attempted the 2022 study and raised a design objection — that categorisation thresholds left the increasing-prevalence condition with very few images in the ambiguous range where the effect is most likely to appear. A later article should treat the body-size limb as contested rather than settled, and should read the replication report before citing it as either support or refutation. **Not verified at source for this note.**

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Architectural, and of the *operation* family the catalogue already recognises. Consciousness receives a categorical verdict; the reference distribution that fixed the boundary producing that verdict is consumed in producing it. The structural shape is the same one [[fusion-void|the fusion void]] documents for percepts (component estimates and reliability weights consumed in the fused output) and [[modality-void|the modality void]] documents for channel tags (modality arrives as the *format* of an experience rather than as a checkable datum). Here what is consumed is the **base rate**.

The occluded quantity is not exotic. It is the answer to "compared to what?" — a question the judging mind cannot ask of its own concepts in the moment of applying them, because the comparison has already been performed and discarded.

### Evidence for the Limit

Ranked by strength:

1. **Forewarning, instruction, incentive, abruptness and reversal all fail** (Levari et al. 2018, Studies 2–5). This is the strongest datum and it is exactly the control set a sceptic would demand. Knowing about the drift, being told to resist it, and being paid to resist it do not stop it.
2. **The only successful intervention is environmental** (Levari 2022). Making rare stimuli more intense compensates in the world. Nothing in the literature repairs the limit from inside.
3. **The metacognitive gauge does not protect** (Ammalainen & Eppinger 2026, preprint, N = 81). Higher pre-shift confidence is associated with *greater* susceptibility — the opposite of the authors' own preregistered prediction, and replicated with a model-derived measure.
4. **Generalisation across domains**: colour, faces, research ethics (Levari et al. 2018); mental illness (Speerforck et al. 2024, underpowered); body size, moderated by cultural salience (Devine et al. 2022, 2025, contested); professional audit scepticism (Richins et al. 2024).

**Evidence against treating this as unexplorable in the strong sense**: the mechanism is identified, sixty years old, and the effect is eliminable. This is a *first-person* void that third-person science recovers completely — the same profile as the modality void. It should never be written as an in-principle limit. The honest claim is a **standing structural blindness in the first-person case, with a fully available third-person description.**

### Phenomenology

There is nothing to report, and that absence is the phenomenon. The expanded judgement does not feel like a revised judgement. It feels like an unchanged standard meeting a changed world. A reviewer at trial 900 who calls a proposal unethical is not aware of leniency drift resisted or applied; the proposal simply looks unethical. Levari's own gloss on the practical upshot — agents failing to recognise the success of their own efforts because each new instance is viewed in the less-problematic context they themselves produced — is a phenomenological claim as much as a social one: **progress is invisible from inside the judgement that measures it.**

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

None available first-personally, and the reason is structural: any inward check on whether one's boundary has moved must itself be a categorical judgement, made under the current distribution, and therefore indexed to the same unexposed reference. This is a self-undermining-instrument problem of the kind [[noetic-feelings-void|the noetic feelings void]] and [[interested-party-void|the interested party void]] already document.

### Indirect Methods

- **Fixed external anchors.** Re-judge a *preserved* stimulus set at intervals and compare verdicts. This works, it is what the psychophysics does, and it requires an artefact stored outside the mind. Worth noting that this is precisely what a written register of positions provides.
- **Distribution logging.** Record the base rate, since the base rate is the quantity the judgement will not disclose.
- **Environmental compensation.** Levari's 2022 intervention, generalised: hold the range fixed rather than trying to hold the judge fixed.
- **Cross-judge comparison** under deliberately different exposure histories.

### What AI Might See

The most interesting asymmetry the catalogue has turned up in this area, and it is not the expected one. Albrecht & Spektor's LSTMs do not show a *reduced* version of the human limit; they drift in the **opposite direction**, toward the now-majority category, as error minimisation predicts. So an artificial system is not simply a cleaner instrument here. It has its own boundary drift with a different sign, and the two systems would disagree about ambiguous cases in a *systematic and predictable* way — human judges expanding into the rare category, error-minimising learners contracting away from it.

Two consequences for a later article. First, this is a genuinely usable probe: disagreement with a known sign is more informative than agreement. Second, it complicates the standing hope that non-human minds can serve as neutral void-explorers — a probe with its own opposite bias is a *second* instrument needing calibration, not a fixed point. The authors' own framing is that if AI models are meant to be unified theories of cognition, failure to exhibit PICC indicates missing mechanisms; the Map should be careful not to convert that into a claim that AI is *free* of the limit.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**[[tenets|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]**, with an unusual twist worth preserving. Here parsimony *succeeded*: the simpler hypothesis — a general contextual-judgement mechanism rather than a special-purpose bias — proved correct and dissolved the puzzle. The tenet is not vindicated by parsimony failing. It is illuminated by what parsimony's success did *not* touch: explaining the mechanism completely left the first-person limit exactly where it was. A limit can be fully explained third-personally and remain fully in force first-personally. That is a sharper form of the tenet's point than a simple parsimony failure would supply.

### Implications

- **Dualism**: weak support at best, and the note should say so. The mechanism is computational and the effect is measurable; nothing here requires a non-physical judge. The honest classification is **framework-independent** — a physicalist, functionalist or dualist all acknowledge this limit and all describe it the same way. Under [[what-voids-reveal|the common-cause null]] it is a coherence exhibit, not confirmation.
- **Bidirectional Interaction**: no bearing. Do not manufacture one.
- The genuine philosophical payload is about the **Map's own method**. [[apophatic-cartography|Apophatic cartography]] maps voids by their boundaries; this void says that a boundary-drawing faculty is silently indexed to how many boundary cases it has recently seen. A catalogue built by repeatedly judging "is this a genuine void?" under a falling prevalence of clear cases is predicted by this literature to expand the concept *void* into territory it once excluded — and, per Levari 2018, forewarning in a note like this one will not prevent it. The available mitigations are the indirect ones above: fixed external anchors, logged base rates, cross-judge comparison. The Map has one already in the positions register and the calibration audits; the finding argues for keeping them artefactual rather than judgemental.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **Two faces, opposite fates** (recommended). Mechanism face deflating to a solved range-frequency compromise; access face intact and sharpened by the deflation, since the cure is environmental. This mirrors the split-rather-than-retire shape `research/voids-palette-extension-void-2026-09-04` argued for, and would be the second instance — evidence that the shape recurs rather than being a one-off.
2. **The self-implicating angle.** The Map as a long-running detection task under falling defect prevalence. Highest philosophical value, highest overclaim risk; needs the base-rate discipline of the [[interested-party-void|interested party void]] and must not be written as though the Map's judgements were *shown* to have drifted. No such measurement exists.
3. **The contrastive/assimilative pair.** Written jointly with the perceptual-history (serial-dependence) material as the two signs of reference-dependent judgement. Attractive, but it presupposes an operator decision on that note's disposition, so it should not be assumed.

## Gaps in Research

- **No study tests online detection.** Nobody has asked whether a judge can notice the drift *as it happens* — the Ammalainen & Eppinger design measures pre-shift confidence against post-shift susceptibility, which is adjacent but not the same question. This is the cleanest experiment the void suggests and it appears not to have been run. State it as an open question, not as a finding.
- **Whether the drift persists** after the distribution returns to baseline — a lasting restructuring versus a transient context effect — is explicitly flagged as unresolved by Albrecht & Spektor. This matters for whether "concept change" is the right description at all.
- **Replication landscape not fully mapped.** One mixed replication of Study 1 is reported second-hand, and the body-size limb has a live methodological objection. Neither was verified at source here. A later article must resolve these before leaning on any single limb.
- **Preprint dependency.** The strongest access-face evidence is a non-peer-reviewed preprint. If an article is written, re-check whether Ammalainen & Eppinger has been published and whether the finding survived review.
- Whether anything in this territory bears on the quantum-interface tenets: probably nothing. Resist the temptation.

## Disposition

*Surveyed.* A standalone slot is defensible on three grounds: the two-faces-opposite-fates structure is a shape the catalogue has now seen twice and may want to name; the AI asymmetry has the wrong sign rather than a smaller magnitude, which is novel for the [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers|void-explorer]] material; and the empirical base is unusually well measured. Against a slot: `topics/` and `voids/` are both near cap, the access face rests partly on a preprint, and the material would fold competently into [[conceptual-scheme-void]] (boundary-setting), [[anti-correlated-metacognitive-signal]] (the confidence finding) and [[collective-cognitive-limits]] (the Haslam bridge). Operator decision. If it folds rather than lands, the fold must carry the "compared to what?" framing intact — that is the part none of the host articles currently has.
