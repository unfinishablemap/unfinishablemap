# Research: Representation Axioms for Integrated Information

**Date**: 2026-08-21

**Search queries used**: Direct raw-text retrieval and grep of seven primary sources (listed in the Retrieval Ledger below); OpenAlex bibliographic queries for four works; four WebSearch calls — `Barbosa Marshall Streipert Albantakis Tononi "A measure for intrinsic information" uniqueness theorem`; `integrated information theory Phi "representational theory of measurement" OR "measurement theory" scale type ratio scale critique`; `Bayne 2018 "axiomatic foundations of the integrated information theory" Neuroscience of Consciousness critique axioms`; `"measurement theory" OR "Michell" OR "Trendler" quantitative structure consciousness measures "level of consciousness" scale type ordinal 2023 2024 2025 critique`.

**Why this research exists**: [[scale-types-for-phenomenal-quantities]] closes its meaningfulness-discipline section by turning the discipline outward on the corpus's most quantitatively ambitious rival: "Integrated information theory sits at the opposite pole: Φ is presented as a ratio-grade quantity of consciousness, yet this article knows of no exhibited representation axioms for it." That sentence is an honest report of the article's own reading, not a verified absence. This note tests it against the IIT 4.0 formalism and its surrounding critical literature.

## Executive Summary

**The claim survives in substance but needs narrowing in wording, and the narrowing is the interesting part.** Exhaustive grep of the full text and 66-item bibliography of Albantakis et al. (2023), the IIT 4.0 paper, finds zero occurrences of *representation theorem*, *uniqueness theorem*, *homomorphism*, *relational structure*, *scale type*, *ratio scale*, *interval scale*, *ordinal*, *measurement theory*, *invariance*, *isomorphism*, *admissible transformation*, or *concatenation*, and zero citations of Krantz, Luce, Suppes, Tversky, Stevens, Michell, or Trendler. On the representational-theory-of-measurement (RTM) question as such, the article's report holds for the sources reached here.

But IIT is not axiom-free in the way "no exhibited representation axioms" might be read to imply, and the Map should not let that reading stand. IIT 4.0 rests its measure on a genuine uniqueness result: Barbosa et al. (2020) report as their main result "the existence of a unique function (up to a multiplicative constant" — the constant being any *k* greater than zero — satisfying three stated properties: causality, specificity, intrinsicality. **Uniqueness up to a positive multiplicative constant is exactly the similarity-transformation group that defines a ratio scale.** So Φ's ratio-grade presentation is not a bare numerical convention; on the numerical side it is earned.

The gap is where the earning stops. Barbosa et al. self-locate their result in "the axiomatic characterization of information measures" tradition — the Shannon/Kullback–Leibler lineage, whose axioms constrain a *divergence between probability distributions*. Their domain is distributions over states of a physical substrate, not experiences. RTM's demand is different in kind: axioms on an *empirical relational structure whose objects are the things measured*, and a homomorphism from that structure into the numbers. IIT exhibits no such structure for experiences, because it does not need one — the bridge from the numerical quantity to "the quantity of consciousness" is carried entirely by what IIT calls its explanatory identity, which the paper explicitly *proposes* under "inference to a good explanation" rather than proves.

The sharpened finding, then: **IIT has uniqueness of a formula, not uniqueness of a scale for a phenomenal attribute.** The ratio-grade licence attaches to a measure of intrinsic information; its transfer to consciousness is a postulate. Two further weaknesses show up under the measurement lens that the existing IIT critiques reach only obliquely: Φ aggregates its components by plain summation while IIT simultaneously insists its component measure is deliberately non-additive, and the partition that fixes Φ is selected on *normalised* values while the reported quantity is unnormalised.

## Key Sources

### Albantakis, Barbosa, Findlay, Grasso, Haun, Marshall, Mayner, Zaeemzadeh, Boly, Juel, Sasai, Fujii, David, Hendren, Lang & Tononi (2023) — IIT 4.0

- **URL / DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465 — *PLOS Computational Biology* 19(10): e1011465
- **Type**: Primary theory paper, open access. Retrieved in full.
- **What the greps found (the negative result)**: across 24,361 words and 66 references — `representation theorem` 0, `uniqueness theorem` 0, `homomorphism` 0, `relational structure` 0, `scale type` 0, `ratio scale` 0, `interval scale` 0, `ordinal` 0, `measurement theory` 0, `invarian*` 0, `isomorph*` 0, `admissible transformation` 0, `concatenat*` 0, `monotonic` 0. Author greps across the whole file including the bibliography: `Krantz` 0, `Luce` 0, `Suppes` 0, `Tversky` 0, `Stevens` 0, `Michell` 0, `Trendler` 0, `psychophysic*` 0. Reference-list presence was independently controlled for (Oizumi, Balduzzi, Barrett, Mediano and all sixteen author surnames do appear), so the zeros are absence of the vocabulary, not absence of the bibliography.
- **What IIT means by "axiom"**: "axioms of phenomenal existence: every experience is for the experiencer (intrinsicality), specific (information), unitary (integration), definite (exclusion), and structured (composition)". These are phenomenological self-evidence claims, then reformulated as *postulates* about physical substrates. Neither move is an axiom in the RTM sense — a qualitative condition on an empirical relational structure.
- **The quantity claim**: the sum of the φ values of the distinctions and relations composing the Φ-structure "corresponds to the quantity of consciousness"; elsewhere, the Φ-structure "corresponds to the quality of the experience specified by the substrate" while its Φ value corresponds "to the nature and amount of intrinsic content".
- **The strongest quantitative language in the paper**: once the minimum partition is identified, "the integrated information across it is an absolute quantity, quantifying the loss of intrinsic information due to cutting the minimum partition of the system." Note "absolute" here means unnormalised — not RTM's *absolute scale* type. The homonymy is a trap worth flagging in any article.
- **Why uniqueness matters to IIT itself**: "should be unique and based on quantities that uniquely satisfy the postulates", cited to references [12] and [32] — and [32] is Barrett & Mediano (2019). IIT 4.0's uniqueness programme is in part a direct response to the well-definedness critique.
- **The bridge**: "IIT proposes a fundamental explanatory identity: an experience is identical to the cause–effect structure unfolded from a maximal substrate"; "every property of an experience is accounted for in full by the physical properties of the" Φ-structure. Methodologically the paper files this under "inference to a good explanation". It is proposed, not derived.
- **Tenet alignment**: conflicts with [[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]] (identity, not irreducibility) and with [[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]] (an identity leaves nothing for consciousness to do). Congenial to [[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]] only in the negative sense that its own quantitative confidence outruns its axiomatic base.

### Barbosa, Marshall, Streipert, Albantakis & Tononi (2020) — the uniqueness result

- **URL / DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-75943-4 — *Scientific Reports* 10(1): 18803
- **Type**: Primary formal paper, open access. Main text retrieved in full; **Supplementary Materials, containing the theorem and proof, not retrieved.**
- **The result as stated in the main text**: "The measure satisfies three desired properties" — causality, specificity, intrinsicality — "and is shown to be unique." More precisely: "Our main result is the existence of a unique function (up to a multiplicative constant" — any *k* greater than zero — of the form `D(P,Q) = k · max_α { p_α log(p_α/q_α) }`, "that satisfies all three properties". The authors then set *k* = 1 "without loss of generality". (The raw source renders the constant as inline LaTeX, so it is paraphrased here rather than quoted; the surrounding spans grep contiguously.)
- **Why this is the decisive source for the task**: this *is* a uniqueness theorem, and its invariance class — positive multiplicative constants — is precisely the ratio-scale group. Anyone claiming IIT has "no axioms" for Φ is wrong. The correct claim is narrower and sharper.
- **Why it is nonetheless not an RTM representation theorem**: the authors themselves place it in a different tradition — "The axiomatic characterization of information measures starting from basic properties is an extensive field", citing the Shannon and Kullback–Leibler axiomatisations, and noting that the common properties "monotonicity, continuity and additivity … uniquely characterize the KL measure". This is the tradition of characterising a *function on probability distributions* by desiderata. RTM instead axiomatises a *qualitative empirical structure* and proves a homomorphism into the numbers exists. The objects here are distributions, not experiences; no phenomenal ordering appears anywhere.
- **The unit is a convention**: setting `k=1` "without loss of generality" is exactly the choice of unit that ratio-scale uniqueness leaves free. IIT then reports values in "units of intrinsic bits, or ibits".

### Barrett & Mediano (2019) — Φ is not well-defined

- **URL / DOI**: arXiv preprint https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1902.04321; published as *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 26(1–2): 11–20
- **Type**: Critique. arXiv preprint retrieved in full; published version not retrieved.
- **The framing, which matters**: for IIT's identification of Φ with the quantity of consciousness to be plausible, "there should be no alternative formulae for" Φ "consistent with the axioms of IIT, and there should not be cases of" Φ "being ill-defined." This is a uniqueness demand stated in the IIT-internal sense — which formula do the postulates pick out? — and it is the demand IIT 4.0 answers with the ID measure.
- **Problem 1, the RTM-adjacent one**: "There is no canonical metric on the space of states, nor a canonical metric on probability distribution space". The earth mover's distance requires a metric on states; IIT 3.0 proposed Hamming distance for binary units, but for non-binary states L1, L2 and L∞ are all available "and no canonical 'intrinsic' choice" exists. Conclusion: "in the absence of a well-argued principle by which to uniquely fix the metrics on the space of states and on probability distribution space, Φ is not well-defined." **This is a meaningfulness objection in all but name** — the numerical value depends on a modelling choice the theory does not fix, so claims about its magnitude are not invariant.
- **Scope caveat**: the paper targets IIT 3.0 (Oizumi et al. 2014) explicitly, and disclaims the variant measures. IIT 4.0 has since dropped the earth mover's distance entirely (`earth mover` greps 0 in the 4.0 text) in favour of the ID measure, so Problem 1's distribution-space half is answered. The state-space half and the graining problem are not obviously answered: `grain` still appears 19 times in IIT 4.0.
- **Also contains zero RTM vocabulary**: `representation theorem` 0, `uniqueness` 0, `measurement theory` 0, `Krantz` 0. Its one `scale` hit is "spatiotemporal scale". Its twelve apparent `ratio` hits are entirely substring artefacts of *operational*, *integration* and *configuration*.

### Mediano, Seth & Barrett (2019) — six measures, no agreement

- **URL / DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3390/e21010017 — *Entropy* 21(1): 17
- **Type**: Comparative simulation study, open access. Retrieved in full.
- **Headline finding**: comparing six distinct candidate measures of integrated information on eight-node Gaussian linear autoregressive networks, "We find a striking diversity in the behaviour of these measures" — "no two measures show consistent agreement across all analyses."
- **Bearing on uniqueness**: if several non-equivalent quantities all trade under the name Φ and disagree systematically, then at the operational level Φ does not name one quantity. Whatever a representation theorem might one day be proved about, it would have to be proved about a particular one of them.
- **⚠️ Scope caveat that must not be dropped**: these are *practical* Φ measures in the Gaussian/empirical-distribution framework, not IIT 4.0's Φ. The paper notes that Φ in this family "is often regarded as a poor measure of integrated information because it can be negative" — a fatal problem for a ratio-scale reading, since it destroys the zero. **That negativity point does not transfer to IIT 4.0's Φ**, which is a sum of non-negative φ values and is non-negative by construction. Conflating the two families would be a straightforward error and any article must keep them apart.

## Major Positions

### Position A: Φ's ratio-grade presentation is earned (IIT's own view)

- **Proponents**: Albantakis, Tononi, Barbosa, Marshall and colleagues.
- **Core claim**: the postulates uniquely determine the measure; the ID measure is unique up to a positive multiplicative constant; the explanatory identity then makes that quantity the quantity of consciousness. Nothing further is owed.
- **Strongest form**: this is a real derivation, not hand-waving, and it does deliver ratio-type uniqueness on the numerical side. Critics who say IIT "just asserts a number" are attacking a weaker target than the one that exists.
- **Relation to Map tenets**: the identity move is precisely where [[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]] parts company. But the Map should concede the formal achievement before objecting to the bridge.

### Position B: Φ is not even well-defined, so scale questions are premature

- **Proponents**: Barrett & Mediano (2019); Mediano, Seth & Barrett (2019).
- **Core claim**: alternative formulae are consistent with the axioms; metrics are unfixed; candidate measures disagree with one another. Before asking what scale Φ is on, establish that Φ names one thing.
- **Current state**: partly overtaken. IIT 4.0's ID derivation is a genuine answer to the "alternative formulae" charge, which is why 4.0 cites this critique at exactly that point. The disagreement-among-measures result remains standing for the practical measures used in empirical work.

### Position C (the Map's, and so far unoccupied in the literature): the demand is representational, and it has not been made

- **Core claim**: neither IIT's defenders nor its critics have asked the RTM question. IIT axiomatises an information measure; RTM asks for axioms on the qualitative structure of the attribute being measured, plus a uniqueness theorem fixing the admissible transformations of the resulting scale. For a phenomenal metric the empirical relational structure would have to consist of experiences standing in qualitative relations. IIT supplies no such structure and no representation theorem over one.
- **The honest statement of the gap**: Φ is a ratio-scale quantity *of intrinsic information*. Its status as a ratio-scale quantity *of consciousness* is inherited from the explanatory identity, not proved. The identity is doing measurement-theoretic work that is nowhere audited.
- **Why this is not merely verbal**: on the RTM view, "this system is twice as conscious as that one" is meaningful only if a uniqueness theorem over a phenomenal structure licenses ratio comparisons. Φ's own uniqueness result licenses ratio comparisons of *ID values*. The inference from one to the other is exactly the step the meaningfulness discipline exists to police.

## Key Debates

### Does the aggregation step have any warrant?

- **The issue**: IIT 4.0 defines Φ as the sum of the φ values of a substrate's distinctions and relations. Plain summation is an additive representation, which in RTM has to be *earned* — by an extensive structure with a concatenation operation, or by additive conjoint measurement.
- **The internal tension**: IIT deliberately makes its component measure non-additive. Intrinsic information "is quantified in units of intrinsic bits, or ibits, to distinguish it from standard information-theoretic measures (which are typically additive)", and the intrinsicality property mandates subadditivity under dilution. So the theory rejects additivity at the level of the component measure and then aggregates components additively.
- **Status**: this note found no source raising it. It may have a good answer inside IIT's formalism — S3 Text of the 4.0 paper proves theorems about the sum of relation integrated information, which was not retrieved here. Flagged as the most promising unexamined line, not as an established defect.

### Does the normalisation choice make Φ observer-relative?

- **The issue**: the minimum-information partition is selected using φ values normalised "relative to the maximum possible value it could take for arbitrary TPMs over the units of system S", and the paper says this normalisation "ensures that [integrated information] is evaluated fairly over a system's fault lines". The reported quantity is then the *unnormalised* value across the selected partition. Different normalisation conventions can select different partitions and hence different Φ values.
- **Why it bites**: IIT insists that "the quality and quantity of existence must be observer-independent and cannot be arbitrary". A convention-dependent partition selection is in tension with that, and it is a residue of Barrett & Mediano's Problem 1 surviving into 4.0.
- **Status**: raised here as an inference from the retrieved text. Not found stated in any consulted source.

### Are IIT's axioms axiomatic at all?

- **Sides**: Bayne (2018) against; Tononi and colleagues for. Settled to nobody's satisfaction and largely orthogonal to the measurement question — which is itself the point worth making, since it shows the axiom debate has been conducted entirely on the phenomenological axis.

## Gaps in Research

- **Barbosa et al.'s Supplementary Materials were not retrieved.** The uniqueness theorem's statement and proof live there. This note verifies that the main text *claims* the result; it does not verify the proof. Anyone writing the article should retrieve the supplement before characterising the theorem's exact scope and hypotheses.
- **S3 Text of IIT 4.0** ("Analytical results for the number and integrated information of relations") was not retrieved and may bear directly on the aggregation question above.
- **Pautz (2015) is unverified.** A search summary attributes a ratio-scale point about Φ to it — that a grid with ten times a brain's Φ would have ten times the amount of consciousness. If accurate, someone *has* made a scale-type observation about Φ, and the Map's novelty claim would need trimming. Three retrieval attempts returned HTTP 403. **Resolve this before publishing any "nobody has asked" claim.**
- **The published *JCS* version of Barrett & Mediano** was not retrieved; only the arXiv preprint.
- **No search was run for post-2023 responses to IIT 4.0's uniqueness claim.** The ID result is recent enough that a rebuttal may exist and would materially affect Position A.
- **Absence framing**: everything above is what the searches and retrievals reached. The correct statement is that *the sources consulted for this note exhibit no representation or uniqueness theorem for Φ in the RTM sense* — not that none exists. The seven full-text retrievals include the two places such a result would most naturally appear (IIT 4.0 itself and Kleiner & Tull's formal reconstruction), which makes the absence reasonably well-probed but not established.
