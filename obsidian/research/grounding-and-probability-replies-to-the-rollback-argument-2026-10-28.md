## Answers to the Five Questions

### (a) Archer 2022: grounding, not counterfactuals

Archer's abstract states two moves. Against Mele's cross-world luck: "Mele's argument loses force if facts about which possible world is actual are partly grounded in facts about free choices themselves." Against van Inwagen: "van Inwagen's challenge can be met if facts about free actions are explanatorily prior to any objective probabilities associated with those actions." The endnotes fix the relation. Note 10: Tim does not agent-cause the event *w1's being actual*; rather "the fact that w1 is actual is due to the fact that Tim causes d1 (at t1)", and "is due to" "captures an implicit grounding relation". Note 17 draws the modal consequence: from □(w1 is actual → Tim will cause d1) and "w1 is actual" one cannot infer □(Tim will cause d1), because grounding gives "reason to think □ (w1 is actual) is false" (subscripts rendered inline here and below). Note 20 makes the thesis conditional: "if there are grounding relations between objects/facts, then one can formulate a coherent answer to van Inwagen's rollback argument". Note 16: the strategy "could also be deployed in favor of event-causal libertarianism".

Three findings for the Map:

1. **The grounding relation runs from the token choice to the world's actuality and to the probabilities, not from anything to the token.** Archer grounds the actual world in the choice and makes the choice explanatorily prior to its probability. Nothing in the abstract or notes grounds *why this exercise went to A rather than B*. The token stays the ground, which is the Map's own terminus. So the reply does not restore determinism and does not need to; it reverses explanatory order.
2. **It does not convert the categorical difference into a counterfactual one.** Explanatory priority is a direction-of-dependence claim. It leaves every counterfactual about replays exactly where O'Connor leaves them (see (d)). On the register's wording, P-A2's "which thing is the causal source" is untouched; Archer gives the agent-causalist a reason to say the probability is *posterior to* the source rather than a constraint on it. That is a new way of stating the third family, not a fourth family.
3. **The body is still needed for one thing**: how priority blocks Buchak's (P2) or van Inwagen's "ground-floor" inference in detail. The notes do not show it, and note 19 treats Buchak's (P1) denial as having "some merit" but being "vulnerable to important objections". Label Archer "abstract and notes read; body not reached", no longer "not read".

**Guards for the writer.** Archer is an agent-causalist drawing on Schaffer and Rosen on grounding; he takes "no hard position" on grounding's relata (note 12) and credits Tognazzini 2015 with first framing luck in grounding terms (note 11). He must not be read as answering the type-level question (what constrains the power across like circumstances): his reply is wholly token-level.

### (d) Vicens 2016 versus O'Connor 2016, and where Bernáth and Tőzsér land

**Vicens** (abstract): libertarians "assume that the free choices we might make have particular objective probabilities of occurring"; she examines two motivations, phenomenology and naturalising reasons, and argues "that neither introspective reflection nor the metaphysics of causation gives us reason for thinking there are such particular objective probabilities of our free choices." O'Connor quotes her p. 133: unstructured tendencies' "possibility seems to relieve the burden on libertarians of explaining how a choice with a particular objective probability of occurring could be one which is free". So her paper is an *undercutting* argument against the Objective Probability Thesis plus a hint that its falsity helps against van Inwagen, Pereboom and Loewer; it does not claim probabilities are *incompatible* with control. (The incompatibility claim is Bernáth and Tőzsér's, not hers.)

**O'Connor** keeps both robust probabilities and control, and says how. §1: felt motivation "(partly) ground[s] objective probabilities"; against Vicens's habituation story, "This context-specific tapering points to objective probabilities." §2: an emergent power arising "from an organized system of propensities that are probabilistically structured" being itself unstructured is something "I judge ... to be implausible (I shouldn't say impossible)". §3, on the rollback: "The rolling objective probabilities in play simply measure the relative likelihood of what I shall choose, they do not bring about my choice". He grants convergence under replay, answers "So what? I never could be so situated", says of the replay "Sorry, I really don't understand", and closes: "my control (or not) over an ostensibly free choice of mine supervenes on what occurs then and there, not on what I might do elsewhere and elsewhen."

This is the decisive page for the Map. O'Connor's coexistence claim is an *assertion of the division of labour*, with the token primitive stated in his own words, and he explicitly denies that counterfactuals about other replays bear on control. He therefore does the opposite of turning the categorical difference into a counterfactual one. The page's second "development that would change the verdict" has now been tested and does not move it.

**Bernáth and Tőzsér** cite O'Connor 2000 and 2011 only; neither Vicens 2016 nor O'Connor 2016 appears in their paper. Their quoted target (O'Connor 2011: 326–27, "The probability simply measures relative likelihood") is the sentence O'Connor repeats almost verbatim in 2016, so their argument is aimed at the position O'Connor restates rather than at the 2016 paper by name. Their verdict (p. 49): "This kind of 'division of labor' between agents and ground-floor probabilities is not possible"; (p. 55): "one cannot plausibly claim at once that dual control is a condition of free actions and they have metaphysically ground-floor probabilities." On Vicens they are silent; they list the probability-deniers as Ginet, Lowe, Pink "and see also Buchak (2013) which suggests this strategy to all libertarians" (p. 47). §III (newly read) answers Franklin's "metaphysically impossible" objection by treating rollbacks as "substitutable rhetoric tools"; it adds nothing on O'Connor. They also distinguish their argument from Pereboom's (pp. 55–56): theirs claims the coincidence is *impossible*, not improbable, and they concede O'Connor "has a good chance of explaining" Pereboom's coincidence by constitution, which is exactly the move their own argument is meant to close.

### (e) Buchak 2013: the chance premise, and to whom the denial is offered

Her premise-form (draft p. 3): "(P1) If indeterminism holds, then if God replayed the universe numerous times in the above scenario, it would become increasingly likely, as the number of replays increased, that the ratio of lies to truths would converge to some definite real number." (P2) infers definite objective probabilities from convergence; (P3) is the chance-incompatibilist premise. Her claim: "we have no reason to accept (P1)", because van Inwagen's convergence rests on the law of large numbers, whose antecedent is the very probability at issue; "The rollback argument directly begs the question of whether Alice's lying has an objective probability."

To whom she recommends denial: "It is open to all chance-incompatibilists – proponents of agent-causation and otherwise – to deny that agent-acts have determinate probabilities." Agent causation is "one concrete metaphysical difference that the proponent of agent causation could postulate: agent-caused events lack objective probabilities", and "not the only way". So Bernáth and Tőzsér's gloss ("suggests this strategy to all libertarians") is slightly wide: she offers it to chance-incompatibilists (those who accept P3), and a libertarian who rejects P3 is not addressed. The page's L55 wording ("the replay's premise about chances 'is totally unjustified'") is accurate.

**A finding the page does not yet use.** Buchak's physics section is pitched at the Map's grain. She allows determinate conditional chances on measurements and still denies unconditional p(A): "conditional on at least one of C or ~C the act does not have a determinate objective probability – which I take it is all the chance-incompatibilist needs". Her escape requires denying "that free acts are micro-level events of the type studied by physics", which "is what the proponent of agent-causation denies". For a selection exercised *at* the quantum level, the Map cannot simply borrow that denial: its second branch must say why the selection event is not such an event while the Born marginal over it is fixed. That is the companion-law debt again, seen from Buchak's side.

## The Müller 2023 Item (todo L3112), Verified

Müller's abstract: the wild-coincidence "conclusion is valid for deterministic laws, but that it fails for statistical laws". The mechanism (p. 795): for a class of decisions identified from the agent's point of view, "There is no independent handle on that probability after all", and "The physical probabilities will therefore match what happens by construction." He then concedes the case that matters to the Map (p. 799): "the following would be a wild coincidence: we can single out a class of free human actions such that we know the probabilities of the physical components and we know the agents' propensities for deciding on the actions, which are relevantly different, and we nonetheless observe the agents deciding in accord with the known physical probabilities rather than in accord with their agent-causal propensities." His reply to the "anomic" defence (p. 798) is that "Nothing in the anomic position grounds any contrary expectations", so no coincidence can be *alleged*.

Bearing on the rollback page's L84 ("a cost the Map records as undischarged"): the Born weights are an independent handle, so Müller's "by construction" dissolution does not apply to them. What survives for the Map's second branch is his weaker point: with no propensities there is no contrary expectation, so the match cannot be shown to be a coincidence. That removes the charge as an *evidential* one and leaves the Map's own standard, a law under which the match is lawful, exactly where the page puts it. Rescope "undischarged" to "contested (Müller 2023), not discharged". Müller is a philosopher of physics defending agent-causal libertarianism against one empirical objection; not to be enlisted for dualism. His DOI 10.1093/mind/fzad002 and pagination 789–802 are confirmed from the PDF header.

## Does L96 Need Revision?

**No.** After reading, the sentence is more secure than before: O'Connor 2016 locates control "then and there, not ... elsewhere and elsewhen", and Archer's grounding runs *from* the token. **L98 must be rewritten**, since both "unread" hedges are now false. Replacement for L98:

> Three developments would change the verdict. A reply grounding the token without restoring determinism: Archer (2022, abstract and notes read; body not reached) instead grounds the world's actuality and the probabilities in the choice, so the token stays the ground. A demonstration that the agent's control and robust probabilities coexist: O'Connor's reply to Vicens (2016) asserts the coexistence, holding that the probabilities "simply measure the relative likelihood of what I shall choose, they do not bring about my choice" and that control "supervenes on what occurs then and there, not on what I might do elsewhere and elsewhen", which restates the division of labour Bernáth and Tőzsér (2020) argue is impossible rather than answering them. And a Born-preserving companion law motivated independently of luck, which the Map has not attempted.
