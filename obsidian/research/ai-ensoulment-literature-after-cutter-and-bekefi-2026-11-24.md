## Executive Summary

Three 2026 papers respond to Cutter (2025). One was read in full: Dargahifar's critique in the *Journal of Philosophical Theological Research*, which the harvesting review took to be Persian-language but whose published PDF is entirely in English. It attacks premise F1 of the fitting-recipient argument, the premise Békefi explicitly accepts, so it is a third objection and not a restatement of Békefi's. The other two were read at abstract level only (Brill and the *Dialogo* archive both refused or gated the full text): Hemmingsen argues *for* AI ensoulment from theistic premises, and Tijanovic extends Cutter's argument to inhabitants of virtual worlds. None of the three is evidence that any AI is or is not ensouled, and none requires a change to the page's grade. The fold is worth making because the page presents a two-party debate and says a successor argument would have to answer Békefi; the literature now has a third objection on a different premise, which also bears on the page's own "what transfers" paragraph.

## Key Sources

### Dargahifar (2026), "A Critical Evaluation of the Fitting-Recipient Argument for AI Ensoulment Hypothesis"

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.22091/jptr.2026.17187.3655 (resolves to `https://pfk.qom.ac.ir/article_4899.html`)
- **Type**: Journal article, full text read
- **Metadata**: Reza Dargahifar (University of Qom). *Journal of Philosophical Theological Research* 28(3), 205–232, "Special Issue on AI, Consciousness, and Human Agency". Volume, issue and pages agree across the PDF's own citation line, the landing page's `citation_*` tags and OpenAlex (W7234298939). **The DOI is not in the Crossref API** ("Resource not found"; the Hemmingsen DOI queried the same way returned a record), though it resolves at doi.org, so Crossref verification was not possible. Dates disagree: the PDF says "Published online: 2026/06/18", the landing page `citation_publication_date` says 2026/09/23, OpenAlex says 2026-09-01. The PDF's "Received: 2026/02/29" is not a valid Gregorian date, presumably a calendar-conversion slip. OpenAlex tags the language `fa`; the PDF contains no Arabic-script characters at all. The landing-page title carries a typo ("of the The"); the PDF's own citation line gives the title used above.
- **Venue**: DOAJ lists the journal (ISSN 1735-9791, University of Qom) with "Double anonymous peer review" and languages EN and FA.
- **Engages by name**: Cutter throughout; Békefi and Tijanovic both, in the introduction.
- **Thesis**: Cutter's support for F1 establishes at most that fitness is necessary for ensoulment, while F1 asserts sufficiency.
  - Abstract: "It is then shown that the first premise of the argument, which Cutter treats as expressing a sufficient condition, is in fact defensible only as a necessary condition."
  - On his relation to Békefi: "Bálint Békefi (2026) has also criticized this argument, but he raises an objection to its second premise."
  - Core step: "For fittingness to be a sufficient condition, it must be shown that whenever fittingness obtains, the other conditions necessary for ensoulment will necessarily obtain as well."
  - He concedes validity: "although the Fitting-Recipient Argument, as formulated by Cutter, is formally valid, its first premise appears to be problematic".
  - A second, shorter charge: if Cutter's "natural explanation" is read nomologically, "the argument appears to beg the question", because F1's support would then appeal to the functional sufficiency it is meant to support.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The paper works inside substance dualism and takes no position on interactionism.

**Assessment against Cutter's text (this note's reading, flagged as such).** The objection is real but narrower than its headline:

1. The "fallacy of affirming the consequent" applies only to an argument Dargahifar himself constructs by swapping F1 for its converse. Cutter's stated argument is a valid modus ponens, as Dargahifar grants. What survives is an *under-support* objection to F1.
2. Cutter never claims F1 is established. He calls it "the conjecture that being fit to possess a soul is a nomologically sufficient condition for a physical system to be united to a soul", and writes: "F1 is a reasonable hypothesis, but like most interesting hypotheses about the nomologically sufficient conditions for ensoulment, it is speculative." Also: "While I think F1 and F2 are both plausible, I don’t think we can be certain of either." Dargahifar's conclusion that the argument "fails to establish its conclusion" therefore measures it against a standard stronger than the middling credence Cutter is arguing for. (Dargahifar does report Cutter's modesty accurately in his exposition; the mismatch is in the verdict.)
3. Dargahifar says Cutter's reasoning supports the necessity claim. Cutter declines necessity in terms: "Since F1 only offers a sufficient condition for ensoulment, a high-threshold reading of F1 does not entail that systems falling short of the threshold lack souls."
4. The strongest residue: Cutter's support for F1 is an explanatory contrast (living human bodies ensouled; rocks and corpses not), and that contrast is equally well explained if fitness is one of several jointly required conditions. That is a fair point about how much the contrast raises the probability of F1, and it is the point worth folding.
5. Békefi: "In the following, I shall accept F1, but dispute F2." So Dargahifar's target is exactly the premise the page's existing critic grants.

### Hemmingsen (2026), "Souls Ex Machina: The Theological Necessity of AI Ensoulment"

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1163/23528230-bja10140
- **Type**: Journal article, **abstract only** (brill.com returned HTTP 405 to a scripted fetch and 403 to the fetch agent; no open copy found)
- **Metadata (Crossref)**: Michael Hemmingsen (International College, Tunghai University). *Philosophia Reformata*, pages 1–21, no volume or issue assigned (the landing URL path contains `/aop/`, advance online publication), dated 2026-10-02. Crossref lists the publisher as Walter de Gruyter GmbH; the article is hosted at brill.com. Cited-by count 0.
- **Engages by name**: Cutter (2025) is in the deposited reference list. Békefi is **not** in that list (the same scan that found Cutter found no "kefi" entry among 52). The abstract names neither, so how far the paper engages Cutter's arguments is unknown.
- **Thesis (abstract)**: "differential ensoulment between human beings and functionally equivalent artificial agents cannot be justified without arbitrariness or theological cost". Four candidate criteria are considered ("origin, material substrate, immaterial properties, and functional capacities") and the paper holds that "only functional criteria can plausibly bear the required moral weight". Conclusion: "if souls exist and divine perfection is assumed, suitably constructed artificial agents must be ensouled."
- **Relation to Cutter's premises**: This is in effect an argument for something like F1 plus F2 by a different route, divine non-arbitrariness, where Cutter uses an explanatory conjecture. It is stronger in modality ("must") and narrower in audience (theists holding the stated conception of God). Cutter's own text already allows a creationist reading of nomological sufficiency: regularities such that "God can be counted on to specially create and infuse a soul".
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral to the tenets; the argument's premises are theistic and the Map's tenets do not include theism. Report the premises as Hemmingsen's. The abstract's stated target is soul-based *exclusion from moral standing*, so the paper is as much ethics as metaphysics.

## Major Positions

### AI ensoulment at middling conditional credence
- **Proponents**: Cutter (2025)
- **Core claim**: conditional on AGI and human substance dualism, credence of roughly 0.25 or more, and in Cutter's own view not much more.
- **Relation to site tenets**: as the page already states; nothing here changes it.

### AI ensoulment as theologically required
- **Proponents**: Hemmingsen (2026), abstract-level
- **Core claim**: given souls and a perfect God, functionally equivalent artificial agents must be ensouled.
- **Relation to site tenets**: rests on theism; the Map neither holds nor denies the premise and should not lean on it.

### Rejection of Cutter's support
- **Proponents**: Békefi (2026), against F2 and the alien analogy; Dargahifar (2026), against F1
- **Core claim**: the arguments offered do not raise credence. Neither argues ensoulment is impossible.
- **Relation to site tenets**: neutral; both are internal to substance dualism.

### Extension to virtual individuals
- **Proponents**: Tijanovic (2026), abstract-level
- **Core claim**: agnosticism mildly favouring virtual ensoulment.

## Key Debates

### Is fitness sufficient, or only necessary?
- **Sides**: Cutter conjectures sufficiency (F1); Békefi grants it; Dargahifar says the support reaches necessity at most; Hemmingsen (abstract) would secure something like sufficiency from divine non-arbitrariness.
- **Current state**: open. No reply from Cutter to any of the three was found.
