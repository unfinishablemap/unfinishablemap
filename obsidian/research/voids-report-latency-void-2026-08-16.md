# Research: Voids - The Report-Latency Void

**Date**: 2026-08-16

**Voids category**: Occluded, with an Unexplored face. The occlusion is instrumental rather than architectural — the territory is not closed to minds as such, it is closed to *the channel we happen to be querying it through*. The Unexplored face is that the discount this implies has never been applied to the catalogue's own evidence base.

## Executive Summary

Every claim the voids catalogue makes about an edge state — anaesthesia, deep sleep, mystical absorption, the dissolving insight, the near-death report — arrives through a channel that opens only after the state has ended. This note establishes that the *latency* of that channel is not a minor source of noise but a determinant of what gets recorded at all, and it does so with the one edge territory where all three probe latencies have been run against comparable populations. In anaesthesia, real-time within-state probing (the isolated forearm technique) finds responsiveness to command in **4.6% of patients** after intubation, with five of the twelve responders signalling *pain*; structured interview within 24 hours finds awareness in about **1:800**; spontaneous patient report finds about **1:19,600**. That is a spread of roughly 900-fold across the same clinical population, driven by nothing but when and how the question was asked. The decisive datum sits inside the fastest probe: of 253 patients questioned after surgery in that study, **none** had explicit recall — the retrospective channel returned zero about a state the real-time channel had just detected in one patient in twenty-two.

The finding is sharper than "memory is unreliable", and the honest version has to be stated carefully, because the memory-distortion literature does not support the sweeping claim. Where an episode *is* retained, remembered intensity is only mildly biased: the pooled paediatric estimate is a small overestimation (SMD = 0.28), and the 1990 review of memory for pain concluded recall is "moderately accurate". The failure mode here is different and worse than distortion. It is **all-or-nothing loss of the episode**, and a small bias can be corrected while a zero is invisible — an absent report looks exactly like an absent experience. Sleep supplies the control case that keeps this from being a blanket sceptical thesis: serial awakening gives sleep a near-real-time channel that no other edge territory has, which is precisely why sleep-consciousness claims deserve a different discount from mystical or near-death claims. Meanwhile the Map's own psychedelic evidence runs through an instrument whose validation paper states that its ratings were "provided seven hours after capsule administration", whose persisting-effects ratings came "three to eight weeks" later, and which was itself built on accounts of experiences that "occurred on average eight years before the given ratings".

## Key Sources

### Sanders et al. (2017), "Incidence of Connected Consciousness after Tracheal Intubation" (ConsCIOUS-1)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1097/ALN.0000000000001479 — *Anesthesiology* 126(2), 214–222. PMID 27984262
- **Type**: Paper — abstract verified at the EuropePMC primary record; full body not retrieved
- **Key points**:
  - Prospective international multicentre cohort, 260 adult patients at six sites, isolated forearm technique applied after intubation during intended general anaesthesia.
  - **"The incidence of isolated forearm technique responsiveness after intubation was 4.6% (12/260)"**; "5 of 12 responders reported pain through a second hand squeeze."
  - **"No participant had explicit recall of intraoperative events when questioned after surgery (n = 253)."**
  - Authors' own framing: "Intraoperative connected consciousness occurred frequently, although the rate is up to 10-times lower than anticipated. This should be considered a conservative estimate of intraoperative connected consciousness."
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits, most directly. The parsimonious reading of a null report is that nothing was there; here that reading is wrong in twelve cases out of 260, five of them involving signalled pain. Also touches Bidirectional Interaction: the responders moved a hand to command, so a motor channel from mind to world was open while the memory channel was shut.

### Pandit et al. (2014), NAP5 summary of main findings
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/anae.12826 — *Anaesthesia* 69(10), 1089–1101. PMID 25204236
- **Type**: National audit — abstract verified at the EuropePMC primary record
- **Key points**:
  - Incidence estimated from *spontaneous* patient reports against a parallel national activity survey.
  - **"The incidence of certain/probable and possible accidental awareness cases was ~1:19 600 (95% CI 1:16 700-23 450)."**
  - Wide variation by technique: about 1:8200 with neuromuscular blockade, about 1:135,900 without.
- **Tenet alignment**: the same as above, applied at population scale — the number of edge states on the record is a property of the reporting system, not only of the states.

### Tasbihgou, Vogels & Absalom (2018), "Accidental awareness during general anaesthesia — a narrative review"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/anae.14124 — *Anaesthesia* 73(1), 112–122. PMID 29210043
- **Type**: Peer-reviewed narrative review — abstract verified at the EuropePMC primary record; body not retrieved (publisher returned 402)
- **Key points**: this is the source that places the three latencies side by side, and it does so in the authors' own words.
  - "the ConsCIOUS-1 study showed an incidence of responsiveness around the time of laryngoscopy of 1:25."
  - "In this latter study [NAP5, spontaneous self-report], the incidence of accidental awareness was 1:19,600."
  - "In the recently published SNAP-1 observational study, in which structured postoperative interviews were performed, the incidence was 1:800."
  - **"In almost all reported cases of intra-operative responsiveness, there was no subsequent explicit recall of intra-operative events."**
  - Framing sentence for the whole problem: "As return to consciousness is both difficult to describe and identify, the reported incidence rates vary widely. Similarly, a wide range of techniques have been employed to identify cases of accidental awareness."
- **Tenet alignment**: supplies the general principle in a clinical register — the reported rate is a function of the identification technique.

### Barrett, Johnson & Griffiths (2015), "Validation of the revised Mystical Experience Questionnaire in experimental sessions with psilocybin"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881115609019 — *Journal of Psychopharmacology* 29(11), 1182–1190. PMID 26442957
- **Type**: Paper — **verified at full text this session** (author-hosted PDF, text extracted and read directly)
- **Key points** (all verbatim from the retrieved full text):
  - Abstract: "The 30-item revised Mystical Experience Questionnaire (MEQ30) was previously developed within an online survey of mystical-type experiences occasioned by psilocybin-containing mushrooms. **The rated experiences occurred on average eight years before completion of the questionnaire.**"
  - Introduction: "The conditions under which these experiences occurred were uncontrolled, and reports were based on retrospective accounts of experiences that occurred on average eight years before the given ratings."
  - Figure 2 caption: "**MEQ30 ratings were provided seven hours after capsule administration.**"
  - Methods: "At a period of approximately three to eight weeks (which varied across studies) after each experimental session, participants in each study completed a questionnaire assessing persisting behavioral and psychological changes."
  - Pooled validation sample: five laboratory experiments, n = 184, at least 20 mg/70 kg psilocybin.
- **Tenet alignment**: this is the Map's own exposure. The instrument is well-constructed and its psychometrics are not in question; what the timing establishes is that even the best-controlled mystical-experience evidence is a seven-hour-delayed reconstruction, and the instrument's normative base is an eight-year-delayed one.
- **Note on fairness to the authors**: they flag the delay themselves, in the abstract and again in the introduction, and the whole point of the paper is to move the instrument onto controlled-dose laboratory data. The paper is a model of disclosure. The Map's problem is that it inherits the delay without inheriting the disclosure.

### Redelmeier & Kahneman (1996), "Patients' memories of painful medical treatments"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3959(96)02994-6 — *Pain* 66(1), 3–8. PMID 8857625
- **Type**: Paper — bibliographic record verified at Crossref, abstract read via the EuropePMC record; publisher page paywalled (402)
- **Key points** (paraphrased, not quoted — see Gaps):
  - Real-time pain intensity recorded during colonoscopy (n = 154) and lithotripsy (n = 133), compared with retrospective evaluations of total pain.
  - Retrospective total-pain judgements tracked the peak intensity and the intensity in the final three minutes (both P < 0.005); procedure duration did not make a procedure remembered as more aversive.
- **Tenet alignment**: where the episode survives into memory at all, its *aggregate* is recomputed by a rule that discards duration. Relevant to the Map wherever an edge-state report is treated as a summary of a stretch of experience.

### Cuenca-Martínez et al. (2024), "Pain memory in children: a systematic review and meta-analysis with a meta-regression"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1097/j.pain.0000000000003170 — *Pain* 165(7), 1450–1463. PMID 38314811
- **Type**: Systematic review and meta-analysis — record verified at EuropePMC; abstract only partially rendered verbatim (see Gaps)
- **Key points**:
  - 15 studies (12 observational, 3 randomised), search to 11 February 2022, paediatric populations.
  - Overall pooled effect: small overestimation of experienced pain intensity, SMD = 0.28. Clinical context SMD = 0.33; experimental context SMD = 0.07 (no evidence of change).
- **Why it matters here**: it is the disconfirming control for the strong version of this note's thesis. Remembered magnitude is only mildly biased. The report-latency problem cannot be argued from generic memory unreliability, because on the best-quantified axis memory is roughly right.

### Erskine, Morley & Pearce (1990), "Memory for pain: a review"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3959(90)90002-u — *Pain* 41(3), 255–265. PMID 1697054
- **Type**: Review — full abstract verified verbatim at the EuropePMC primary record
- **Quote**: "There is some evidence that recall is moderately accurate but this conclusion is tentative because of significant methodological problems. There is also some evidence that recall of acute pain is more accurate than recall of chronic pain."
- **Why it matters here**: same role as the meta-analysis, thirty-four years earlier and with the same direction of travel. The literature's own verdict on retrospective phenomenal report is measured, not sceptical.

### Siclari, LaRocque, Postle & Tononi (2013), "Assessing sleep consciousness within subjects using a serial awakening paradigm"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00542 — *Frontiers in Psychology* 4, 542. PMC3747360
- **Type**: Paper — full text retrieved and read this session
- **Key points**:
  - Seven healthy participants, 741 questionings across 44 study nights.
  - Awakenings at "pseudorandom intervals" of 15–30 minutes, "irrespective of sleep stage."
  - Report collected immediately on waking: "Participants were alerted by a computerized sound lasting 1.5 s. They were instructed to signal that they had heard the sound and to then lie quietly on their back with eyes closed. Interviews were conducted via intercom using a structured questionnaire."
  - Rationale includes representativeness: "limiting the analysis to awakenings obtained after a fixed time into a sleep stage may compromise the representativeness of the sample of conscious experiences."
- **Companion**: the same paradigm underwrites Siclari et al. (2017), "The neural correlates of dreaming", *Nature Neuroscience* 20, 872–878, https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4545.
- **Tenet alignment**: this is the positive case. Sleep is the one edge territory where the latency can be pushed to seconds, and the catalogue should treat that as an epistemic privilege rather than a methodological detail.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The limit is instrumental. Nothing about a human mind makes anaesthetic connected consciousness unknowable in principle; a tourniquet and a hand squeeze reach it. What makes it a void is that the channel the Map ordinarily uses — the subject, afterwards, in words — is closed for that state, and closed in a way that returns a confident null rather than an error.

This puts it in the Occluded family alongside erasure-void, but with a different mechanism. In erasure the capacity and its detection mechanism are lost together, so the subject cannot register the absence. Here the *experience* may have been fully present and even outwardly acted upon, and it is the trace that never forms. The subject is not blind to a missing faculty; there is simply no episode to introspect. Both produce absence-of-absence; only one of them can be caught from outside with a faster probe, which is what makes this one tractable.

### Evidence for the Limit

The anaesthesia gradient is the strongest evidence any void in the catalogue has, because it is a within-territory dose-response on the *instrument* rather than on the state:

| Probe | Latency | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated forearm technique (motor response to command, within state) | seconds | 4.6% (12/260), about 1:22 |
| Modified Brice structured interview (SNAP-1) | within 24 h | about 1:800 |
| Spontaneous patient report (NAP5) | days to never | about 1:19,600 |

Two guards on this table. First, the three figures come from different studies with different populations, endpoints and case definitions — IFT responsiveness to command is not the same construct as awareness with recall, and the honest reading is a gradient rather than a controlled comparison. Second, and importantly for the Map's dualism, IFT responsiveness establishes *connected responsiveness*, and the inference to phenomenal consciousness is an inference. The five second-squeeze pain signals make it a strong one, but it is not free.

What survives both guards is the ConsCIOUS-1 internal result, where the comparison *is* controlled because it is within one cohort: twelve patients responded to command, five signalled pain, and none of the 253 questioned afterwards had explicit recall. Latency alone took a rate of 4.6% to a rate of zero.

### Phenomenology

There is none, and that is the finding. The report-latency void has no felt edge. A subject emerging from anaesthesia does not experience a gap where something was; a meditator does not experience an insight receding under a known decay curve — transit-void describes thoughts vanishing "in the act of reaching for them", which is the fastest-decaying case and the only one with any phenomenology at all. In the anaesthetic case the subject's honest, sincere, confident report is that nothing happened, and that report is produced by exactly the same machinery that produces true reports of nothing happening.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

Shorten the latency. This is the whole of the positive programme, and it is already demonstrated twice: the isolated forearm technique for anaesthesia and serial awakening for sleep. Both work by opening a channel *during* the state that does not depend on memory — a motor channel in one case, a near-immediate verbal one in the other. Both are expensive and neither generalises to the states the Map most relies on.

### Indirect Methods

Where latency cannot be shortened, it can be *graded*. The catalogue's edge-state evidence divides cleanly by channel delay: serial-awakening sleep reports (seconds), MEQ30 psilocybin ratings (seven hours), persisting-effects and integration reports (three to eight weeks), near-death reports (months to decades), and the retrospective online surveys on which several instruments were normed (a mean of eight years). Each tier warrants a different discount, and the catalogue currently applies one flat rate.

A second indirect move is to stop treating a null report as evidence of a null state anywhere the state precludes a real-time probe. That is a discipline, not a discovery, but the anaesthesia data give it a number.

### What AI Might See

An artificial mind is in the opposite position: its inner states, whatever their status, are inspectable during processing but its retrospective self-report is generated afresh rather than recalled. A system that can be instrumented mid-computation is the natural home for the fast-probe strategy, and the asymmetry is worth stating in non-human-minds-as-void-explorers — the human bottleneck this note describes is a memory bottleneck, and it is not obvious that machine minds have it in the same place.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits.** The simplest explanation of "the patient reports nothing" is "there was nothing to report", and this literature shows that inference failing at a measurable rate in a controlled setting. Parsimony here is not merely unreliable; it is unreliable in a specific, quantified direction — it systematically under-counts experience, because absence of trace and absence of experience have identical signatures on the retrospective channel.

### Implications

**Dualism and the interface framing.** A state can be conscious and environmentally connected while forming no memory. That decouples consciousness from memory in a way the anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface article already exploits for filter theory. The report-latency material strengthens that argument's empirical base without adding a new commitment, and it does so through a methodological door rather than a metaphysical one.

**Bidirectional Interaction.** In the IFT cases the causal arrow from mind to world was demonstrably open — a command was understood and a hand moved — at a moment when nothing was being written to memory. Whatever the interface is, its input, output and recording channels are separable.

**A caution against over-reading.** None of this shows that retrospective phenomenal report is untrustworthy in general. The meta-analytic and review evidence says the opposite about remembered magnitude. The claim is narrow and should stay narrow: for states that preclude real-time probing, the retrospective channel can return zero while the state occurs, so the *count* moves with the instrument even when the *description* is roughly faithful.

## Gaps in Research

- **Two figures in the central table rest on a secondary source.** The 1:800 (SNAP-1) and 1:25 (ConsCIOUS-1, as rounded by that review) figures are quoted from the abstract of Tasbihgou et al. 2018, a peer-reviewed narrative review. The SNAP-1 primary (Walker et al. 2016, *British Journal of Anaesthesia* 117(6), 758–766, https://doi.org/10.1093/bja/aew381, PMID 27956674) was **not** retrieved: the publisher returned 403 and the EuropePMC record rendered metadata without an abstract. The sample details circulating for SNAP-1 (16,222 enrolled, 15,040 completing postoperative questionnaires, modified Brice within 24 h) come from search summaries and are **unverified at any primary or peer-reviewed source** — do not repeat them in an article without retrieving the paper. The ConsCIOUS-1 figure is independently verified at its own primary abstract as 4.6% (12/260), so the table should prefer that form over the review's rounded 1:25. **[Update 2026-08-20, deep review of the affective-forecasting-gap host]**: the SNAP-1 primary abstract was retrieved at the EuropePMC record this time and verifies the load-bearing details directly — "Within 24 h of surgery, patients completed the Bauer questionnaire ... and a modified Brice questionnaire", and "The incidence of reported AAGA was one in 800 general anaesthetics (0.12%)". The within-24-h modified-Brice detail and the 1:800 figure may now be cited to Walker et al. 2016 directly; the enrolment figures (16,222 / 15,040) are also confirmed in the same abstract. Note the published title carries no "(SNAP-1)" suffix (Crossref-checked) — identify the study in a trailing note, not inside the title.
- **Everything from the anaesthesia literature is abstract-level.** Sanders et al. 2017, Pandit et al. 2014 and Tasbihgou et al. 2018 were all verified at their EuropePMC abstract records, not their bodies. The quoted sentences are safe; anything about method detail, case adjudication or exclusions is not established here.
- **Redelmeier & Kahneman is paraphrased, not quoted.** The bibliographic record is verified at Crossref and the findings were read through the EuropePMC record, but the abstract text arrived through a summarising layer, so the statistics are reported as paraphrase. If an article needs a verbatim sentence from that paper, retrieve it first.
- **The Cuenca-Martínez abstract was not rendered verbatim.** The SMD values (0.28 / 0.33 / 0.07), the 15-study count and the February 2022 cut-off are consistent across the EuropePMC record and two independent search summaries, but the abstract itself was returned in elided form. Treat the numbers as reliable and any surrounding claim about moderators (age and gender were reportedly significant predictors) as unverified.
- **The paediatric scope is a real limitation on the disconfirming control.** The meta-analysis that keeps this note honest is about children. The adult equivalent was registered as a protocol (*Systematic Reviews*, 2019, https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-019-1115-4) and no completed adult meta-analysis was located this session. The calibration point stands on Erskine et al. 1990 for adults, which is thirty-six years old.
- **The inference from IFT responsiveness to phenomenal consciousness is not settled** and this note does not settle it. An article using this material should carry that qualification explicitly rather than treating 4.6% as an incidence of experience.
- **The corpus is not starting from zero, and the note should not be read as if it were.** `obsidian/topics/anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface.md` L91 already states that "Connected consciousness detected by the isolated forearm technique occurs in roughly 5% of patients immediately after tracheal intubation — far above the 0.1–0.2% incidence of awareness with explicit recall." The contrast is therefore present; what is absent is the study identification (`ConsCIOUS-1` greps 0 case-sensitively; `NAP5`, `SNAP-1`, `accidental awareness` and `Pandit` all grep 0 across `obsidian/`), the third tier of the gradient, the zero-of-253 recall result, and the methodological reading. A search control confirms these zeros are genuine rather than an index failure: `isolated forearm` does reach five files, including the topic article above and `research/pci-phi-anaesthesia-recovery-2026-05-27.md`. **An early version of this check was falsely reassuring** — a case-insensitive grep for `ConsCIOUS` returned 7,536 files because it matched "conscious"; the zero is only visible with case sensitivity.
- **No literature was found that states the probe-latency principle in general form.** The clinical sources state it within anaesthesia; the generalisation across edge states is this note's synthesis, and should be presented as the Map's framing rather than attributed to any cited author.
