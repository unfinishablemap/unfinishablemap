## The Instruments Share One Ancestor {#common-ancestor}

The derivation chain is verified at each link except Hood 1975 itself, which was not read at source (see [Gaps](#gaps)).

1. **Stace (1960)** gives the typology. Unity is "the central experience and the central concept of all mysticism". It takes introvertive and extrovertive forms, and experience is sharply separated from interpretation. (The Stace wording is as quoted by Taves 2020, pp. 7–8 of the accepted manuscript. Stace was not read at source.)
2. **Pahnke (1963)**, a Harvard dissertation, turned it into nine categories. Pahnke & Richards (1966) say: "These categories were derived by Pahnke (1963) from a historical survey of the literature of spontaneous mysticism, including the commentaries of scholars such as William James (1902) and W. T. Stace (1960). As Stace has emphasized, such categories attempt to describe the core of a universal psychological experience, free from culturally determined philosophical or theological interpretations." They were used in the Good Friday experiment (1962): a 147-item post-drug questionnaire, a six-month follow-up, and content analysis by independent judges. Subjects were told nothing about the typology: "no mention was made of the characteristics of the typology of mystical consciousness". That is a real mitigation.
3. **The Pahnke–Richards MEQ (Richards 1975)** became the 43 mystical items of the 100-item States of Consciousness Questionnaire used by Griffiths et al. (2006). The other 57 items are distracters. MacLean et al. (2012) say that "the mystical items have remained largely consistent since the inception of the MEQ". MacLean et al. (2012) and Barrett et al. (2015) then reduced it to the **MEQ30**.
4. **Hood (1975)** built the 32-item Mysticism Scale independently, on the same source. MacLean et al. (2012): "Hood's Mysticism Scale … which was developed according to Stace's (1960) framework, is the most widely used quantitative measure of mystical experience." Taves (2020): "In the 1970s, psychologists Ralph Hood and W. A. Richards independently operationalized Stace's narrow definition of mysticism in two widely used self-report scales". In that sentence "independently" refers to authorship only, not to the source.

**Griffiths et al. (2006), the trial the Void's pending audit cites, used both.** At "about 7 h after capsule administration, when the major drug effects had subsided", participants completed "two questionnaires assessing mystical experience". Of the SOCQ/MEQ the paper says: "This questionnaire is based on the classic descriptive work on mystical experiences and the psychology of religion by Stace (1960)". The Mysticism Scale's three factors are described as "corresponding to" Stace's dimensions, and the paper notes the scale "has not previously been used to assess changes after a drug experience". "Complete" mystical experience was defined by Pahnke's criteria: scores of at least 0.6 on each of unity, transcendence of time and space, ineffability, sacredness, noetic quality and positive mood. **On those criteria, "61%, 22 of 36" of psilocybin sessions qualified.** The rate is verified in the full text. The Void's review said "quote no rate" only because the abstract lacks it.

## What the Common Cause Fixes, and What It Does Not {#what-it-fixes}

**What the instrument fixes:**
- **(i) The dimension space.** A respondent can agree or disagree only on Stace's categories, so any convergence the instruments find is convergence in Stace's terms. A shared feature outside the typology cannot register as shared, and a divergence outside it cannot register at all. Taves (2020) says Stace's definition "singles out experiences of unity and isolates them from other – potentially related – alterations in sense of self".
- **(ii) Part of the content.** Several items build an appraisal into the description: the experience was "sacred", "holy", "divine", or "ultimate reality was revealed". (The item wording is as tabulated by Taves 2020, Table 1, from Hood et al. 2001.) A high score therefore requires endorsing an appraisal. Taves's central charge is that the construct "presupposes on theological grounds that experiences of undifferentiated unity are inherently positive, that positive valence is part of the experience itself, and that the experience is culturally unmediated". Her summary: "Reliance on Stace's theory to operationalize mysticism embedded the limitations of his theory in the mysticism scales and undercut comparative investigation of how and why such experiences emerge."
- **(iii) The threshold.** A "complete mystical experience" is a profile that clears Pahnke's six cut-offs. A completion rate therefore counts Stace-shaped profiles; it is not a rate of some independently identified kind.
- **(iv) Convergent validity.** The MEQ "correlated with the Hood Mysticism Scale, indicating convergent validity" (MacLean et al. 2012 abstract). The two scales are descendants of one construct, so their correlation is evidence of reliable operationalisation, not of independent confirmation. P-M2 counts it once.

**What the instrument does not fix**, which is the fair case for Hood and Griffiths:
- **The drug–placebo difference.** Under double-blind conditions against an active placebo (methylphenidate), psilocybin raised scores. The instrument cannot explain why one capsule moved the scores and the other did not. It constrains only the description of what moved.
- **The covariance structure, which has pushed back on Stace.** Hood et al. (2001): "the ineffability he linked with interpretation proved to be as much or even more a feature of the introvertive experience, as hypothesized by Hood." Barrett et al. (2015): for MEQ30 data, "both the Stace and Hood models demonstrated poor model fit", and a four-factor model fitted better. MacLean et al. (2012) report that "results from recent studies suggest a lack of cross-cultural reliability of the composition and number of factors". Inside its own item space, the scale is not a mirror.
- **Hood's own factor analysis separates experience from appraisal.** Taves (2020) concedes: "Ironically, Hood's factor analysis of the M-Scale supports the distinction between the experience of undifferentiated unity and its valence." So the instruments can in part test Stace's experience/interpretation split. They cannot test whether a shared core exists outside the categories they ask about.

**Net.** Agreement on these scales shows how people answer Stace's questions, and that a drug changes the answers. It cannot show that the experiences share a Stace-shaped core beyond the items. **The distinguishing test P-M2 asks for exists in the literature.** It is instrument design that queries "generically worded experiences" separately from "the way they are appraised in terms of valence, significance, cause, and long-term effects" (Taves 2020 abstract), plus item-level response-process validation across languages (Taves et al. 2023, the INOE). Taves et al. (2023) already report a first result: "Our inability to validate some items drawn from other surveys suggests that they are capturing a wider range of experiences than researchers intend."

## Taves: an Attributionist Rival, Not a Constructivist {#taves}

**What she holds (verified wording):**
- The book's programme: Taves "shifts the focus from 'religious experience,' conceived as a fixed and stable thing, to an examination of the processes by which people attribute meaning to their experiences", toward "a naturalistic understanding of the experiences that people deem religious" (2009, publisher abstract as indexed by OpenAlex).
- Its lineage, in her own words: "my goal in writing the book was to update efforts to use attribution theory to bridge between religious studies and the psychology of religion" (2010 abstract). Classical attribution theory is Proudfoot & Shaver (1975), *JSSR* 14(4), 317–330, which the 2008 abstract cites. She groups herself with Proudfoot: "(Proudfoot, 1985; Martin, 2016; Taves, 2009a) have rejected the claim that religious experience constitutes the core of religion-in-general" (2020, accepted manuscript).
- The mechanism that strengthens Proudfoot: "we need to distinguish between attributions of causality and ascriptions of qualities; although the former are invariably conscious, something like the latter may take place below the threshold of consciousness" (2008 abstract). This is aimed at "those experiences that most challenge an attributional model: those that subjects experience as inherently religious". These are exactly the numinous cases the Void relies on.
- The building blocks: "Three more basic processes that interact on multiple levels are proposed: perceiving salience, assessing significance, and imagining hypothetical, counterfactual content" (2015 abstract). "Specialness" is her generic second-order net (2011 abstract).
- Cross-cultural regularity is allowed: "we can still ask if there is empirical evidence to suggest that humans are more likely to set some things apart than others within or across cultures" (2010 abstract). In 2020 she argues that "'positive experiences of undifferentiated unity' are not sui generis, but rather a type of 'ego dissolution'", a generic alteration that appraisal can give positive, negative or neutral valence.

**Why the Map's reply to Katz does not reach her.**
- The Void answers Katz on his own ground: constructivism "predicts agreement only where a tradition supplies the vocabulary", and two features (dissolution under attention, non-manufacturability) resist that sourcing.
- Taves's account predicts no such restriction. Its building blocks are generic and its ascriptions can be subpersonal, so it can accommodate cross-tradition agreement without any shared vocabulary.
- SEP draws the line explicitly: "Taves also rejects constructivism: constructivists see religious and mystical experiences as constructed from the very start by cultural conditioning, but attributionists deny this in favor of a tiered or 'block-building' approach from experiencing something 'special' to a religious or mystical conclusion."
- The two surviving features have not been shown to resist her account.
  - **Non-manufacturability, by the note's own inference (a LEAD, not Taves's text).** On an attributional model, unbidden-ness is part of what gets an experience deemed special at all. Reports of non-manufacturability would then be selected for by the deeming, whatever the traditions' interests in practice.
  - **Dissolution under attention.** Nothing in her verified text addresses it either way.

**What she is not:**
- She is not an ally: she is a naturalist rival.
- She is not a Katzian constructivist.
- She is not someone who denies that mystical-type experiences occur or recur. She relocates what recurs to generic building blocks, and relocates the "mystical" to appraisal.
- Her account entails nothing about consciousness being causally idle. Subpersonal ascription concerns how experiential content is formed, not whether experience does anything.

## Coiner Versus Populariser {#coiners}

- **The "building-block approach"** is Taves's own label: it is the 2009 subtitle (verified at Crossref) and the title of the book's Conclusion ("Religions: A Building-Block Approach", Crossref chapter DOI `10.1515/9781400830978-009`). SEP's paraphrase is "block-building". Use Taves's form.
- **The "common core" *idea*** is older than Stace. Taves (2020) credits James and Underhill with laying "the foundations for the 'common core' idea", and Huxley (1945, 1954) with arguing "that all religions have a common core". Stace (1960) gave what Taves calls "a chastened perennialism", the canonical philosophical statement.
- **The "common core thesis" as a *label*** is the usage of Hood's programme. Examples are Chen, Hood, Yang & Watson (2011), "Mystical experience among Tibetan Buddhists: the common core thesis revisited", and SEP reporting that Hood (2006, 2017) finds "strong empirical support" for it. **Who first used the label is unresolved (LEAD).** Do not write that Stace coined "the common core thesis". Whether Stace's own phrase was "universal core" was not checked; the book is restricted on the Internet Archive.
- **Spillover**: `voids/edge-states-and-void-probes` L79 says "Stace and James articulated a 'common core' thesis: mystical reports converge on unity, ineffability, noetic quality, and transcendence of space-time." That list merges James's four marks (ineffability, noetic quality, transiency, passivity: Taves 2020's list) with Stace's (unity, transcendence of time and space). It is defensible as loose shorthand. Low priority, and the page is over its hard gate (3,664/3,000), so record it only.

### Griffiths, R. R., Richards, W. A., McCann, U., & Jesse, R. (2006). "Psilocybin can occasion mystical-type experiences having substantial and sustained personal meaning and spiritual significance." *Psychopharmacology* 187(3): 268–283. DOI 10.1007/s00213-006-0457-5 [V-full, MAPS-hosted copy of the publisher PDF; abstract also at Europe PMC, PMID 16826400]
- **Design**: double-blind; 30 volunteers took psilocybin (30 mg/70 kg) and methylphenidate (40 mg/70 kg) in counterbalanced order, plus 6 who had an unblinded psilocybin third session. Participants were "hallucinogen-naïve adults reporting regular participation in religious or spiritual activities". Sessions were individual, with eyeshades, music, and encouragement to attend inward.
- **Instruments**: the SOCQ, containing the 43-item Pahnke–Richards MEQ ("based on … Stace (1960)"), and Hood's 32-item Mysticism Scale, whose factors correspond to Stace's dimensions. Both were completed about 7 h post-dose.
- **The authors' own caveats**:
  - "Some expectancy effects are unavoidable because it would be unethical not to inform both the participants and the session monitors about the range of possible effects with hallucinogens."
  - "it seems plausible that the religious or spiritual interest of the participants may have increased the likelihood that the psilocybin experience would be interpreted as having substantial spiritual significance and personal meaning."
  - The second caveat is a Taves-style appraisal point that the authors make themselves.
- **Conclusion** (abstract): "When administered under supportive conditions, psilocybin occasioned experiences similar to spontaneously occurring mystical experiences."
- **Tenet alignment**: neutral. It gives inbound-leg data on which the interface and production readings both fit.

## Major Positions {#positions}

### Common-core psychometrics (Hood; Richards)
- **Core claim**: Factor-analytic stability of Stace's categories across samples supports a shared experiential core that cultures interpret differently.
- **Best evidence**: US and Iranian confirmatory factor analyses, and the drug–placebo elevation.
- **Weakness**: the instrument is built from the thesis under test. The cross-cultural factor results are mixed (MacLean 2012).
- **Relation to tenets**: even if correct, it establishes a recurring experience-type, which is neutral between the interface and production readings. Do not enlist it for dualism.

### Psychedelic-science pragmatism (Griffiths, Barrett, Johnson)
- **Core claim**: Stace's definition is used as an operational definition. Taves notes they "do not actively defend Stace's common core hypothesis".
- **Main results**: MEQ scores predict persisting outcomes.
- **Relation to tenets**: correlations with outcomes are inbound-leg data (fit, not evidence), and they never touch the outbound leg.

### Attributional / building-block naturalism (Proudfoot; Taves)
- **Core claim**: no experience is inherently religious. Specialness is ascribed, sometimes below awareness, to generic building blocks.
- **What it predicts**: convergence at the generic level and divergence at appraisal.
- **Relation to tenets**: the main rival to any reading of numinous reports as contact. It is compatible with a non-epiphenomenal role for consciousness, so it gives no reason to concede epiphenomenalism.

### Constructivism (Katz) and its eliminativist neighbour (Sharf)
- These are covered in [[research/constructivism-about-contemplative-experience-2026-10-05]].
- Katz predicts divergence. Taves does not, which is why the Void's reply transfers to Katz but not to her.

## Key Debates {#debates}

1. **Does the scale test the common core or presuppose it?**
   - **Hood**: the factor structure could fail, and in places it has.
   - **Taves**: the construct embeds valence and immediacy, so it cannot test them.
   - **Current state**: unresolved. The measurable middle ground is generic-item design (INOE).
2. **Is the psilocybin experience "the same" as spontaneous mysticism?**
   - Griffiths (2006) says "similar", on the Stace scales.
   - Similarity outside the template is untested in either direction.
3. **Mysticism versus ego dissolution.**
   - Taves (2020) folds unity experiences into ego dissolution of varying valence.
   - The psychedelic field still reports MEQ "mystical-type" scores as the outcome predictor.
