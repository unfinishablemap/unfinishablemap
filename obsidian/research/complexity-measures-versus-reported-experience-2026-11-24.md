What the literature shows. **Within an unresponsive state**, spontaneous Lempel–Ziv complexity (LZc) has not been shown to follow report. In propofol sedation, Bajwa et al. (2025) found "no evidence" that PCIst or LZc differ between dreaming and non-dreaming awakenings. That null is badly underpowered: there were only 5 reports of no experience, against 24 of experience. In NREM2 sleep, the same Oslo group failed twice to find a dream/no-dream difference in LZc, and the second study failed to replicate the first's one positive correlation (Aamodt et al. 2021; 2023). A TMS-evoked marker of cortical bistability did separate the two within NREM (Nieminen et al. 2016), though there were too few pulses to compute PCI itself. **Within a responsive psychedelic state**, complexity often correlates with intensity ratings: Schartner et al. (2017a), exploratory and uncorrected; Timmermann et al. (2019); Farnes et al. (2020); Mediano et al. (2024). But the most recent time-resolved DMT study found LZc gave "the weakest associations" of the features it tested (Lewis-Healey et al. 2026). LZc also rises with eyes-open sensory input, and under LSD-plus-video it reached its highest values without the highest ratings. **On experience versus responsiveness directly**, Casey et al. (2024) defined responsiveness, connectedness and consciousness separately through serial awakenings. None of ten published EEG markers, complexity among them, tracked reported experience "alone"; each that did also tracked connectedness to the environment.

**The "higher state" framing has a populariser, not an author.** The phrase "higher state of consciousness" has **0 hits** in Schartner et al. (2017a). The paper's abstract says "elevated level of consciousness - as measured by neural signal diversity", and its discussion says calling the state "higher" "needs to be cautiously interpreted and properly qualified". The headline framing comes from the 19 April 2017 Imperial College press release ("first scientific evidence of a 'higher' state of consciousness"). Rankaduwa and Owen (2023) later put "higher state of consciousness" in quotation marks and cited it to Schartner et al. 2017a.

For the Map: the measures sort *states* more reliably than they sort *experiences within a state*, and the second job is the one an index of conscious richness has to do. This supports the EBH page's caution about single scalars. It does **not** discriminate for dualism. "Complexity follows access or connectedness, not phenomenality" is a reading a physicalist with a two-component model accepts as readily as the Map's interface reading does.

## Key Sources

### Schartner et al. (2017a), "Increased spontaneous MEG signal diversity for psychoactive doses of ketamine, LSD and psilocybin"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/srep46421 (PMC5396066, PMID 28422113; CC BY)
- **Type**: Article, *Scientific Reports* 7, 46421 (online 19 April 2017; Crossref). Authors: Michael M. Schartner, Robin L. Carhart-Harris, Adam B. Barrett, Anil K. Seth, Suresh D. Muthukumaraswamy. Seth is corresponding author (Sussex), and Carhart-Harris is second author.
- **Disambiguation (2017a vs 2017b).** In Rankaduwa & Owen's (2023) reference list, "2017a" is this paper. "2017b" is Schartner, Pigorini, Gibbs et al., "Global and local complexity of intracranial EEG decreases during NREM sleep", *Neurosci Conscious* 2017, niw022 (PMC6007155). Scott & Carhart-Harris (2019) use the same a/b assignment. Peterson et al. (2019) cite this paper as plain "Schartner et al. 2017", and Schartner et al. (2015) is the propofol EEG paper (*PLoS One* 10, e0133532). Key on the venue, not the year.
- **Design**: a re-analysis of three earlier placebo-controlled MEG datasets, each with different participants: LSD 75 μg IV (n = 15, recorded 4 h after dosing), ketamine bolus 0.25 mg/kg plus infusion (n = 19, men only), psilocybin 2 mg IV (n = 14). Resting state, eyes open, 2-s segments of 90 source channels. All participants were **awake and gave ratings**. Ratings were retrospective visual-analogue items plus a single "InScanner" intensity rating.
- **Key points**:
  - The ketamine and LSD increases were significant at group level: "ACE, LZc and LZs, all scored on average significantly higher for both KET and LSD than placebo ( p < 0.05, Bonferroni corrected)". **Psilocybin's were not**: "The differences for PSIL versus placebo didn’t reach significance for any of the measures, although a higher value for PSIL was observed for most subjects". The source-level LZsN maps did show regional psilocybin increases (FDR-corrected), so do not write "psilocybin did not raise complexity". The abstract's "For all three, we find reliably higher spontaneous signal diversity" is stronger than the group-level results.
  - **Within-state correlations with report (the subject of this note).** "For KET, strong correlations across participants were found between scores of LZc N , LZs N and ACE N and specific subjective ratings of the psychedelic experience, in particular for ego-dissolution and vividness of the experience". For psilocybin, LZsN correlated with three items, including the total score. For LSD, there were correlations only with the in-scanner rating. The authors' own caveats: "These correlations should be considered somewhat exploratory and are not controlled for multiple comparisons". Correlating across participants "it should be noted that this is an imperfect approach", because each participant "has their own yardstick". They call for experience-sampling designs instead.
  - **The level claim, in the authors' words.** Abstract: "These findings suggest that the sustained occurrence of psychedelic phenomenology constitutes an elevated level of consciousness - as measured by neural signal diversity." Discussion: "While it may be tempting to describe the psychedelic state as a "higher" state or level of consciousness on the basis of our findings, any such description needs to be cautiously interpreted and properly qualified." Their conclusion places the psychedelic state "above conscious states such as wakeful rest and REM sleep on a one-dimensional scale defined by neural signal diversity". They also say spontaneous measures index conscious level "with weaker specificity and sensitivity" than perturbational ones.
- **Bearing on EBH L62.** The LZc half of the "ketamine objection" comes from **awake volunteers who rated their experience**, at a subanaesthetic dose. Their complexity changes correlated *positively* (exploratory) with reported vividness and ego dissolution. These data therefore show complexity rising *with* reported experience while responsiveness is preserved. They are not a case of complexity rising as awareness, or even responsiveness, falls. Peterson et al. (2019) cite the paper only for "subanesthetic doses of ketamine are associated with increases in LZC values" and rest the "unconscious" half of the claim on Sarasso et al. (2015). The merger into "fully compatible with overall reductions in conscious awareness" is Rankaduwa & Owen's.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. It is a physicalist measurement paper, and its hedge on "higher" is the right calibration for the Map to copy.

### The "higher state of consciousness" framing: originator versus populariser
- **The paper**: `higher state` and `higher state of consciousness` have **0 hits** in the PMC full text, which includes captions. There are 15 hits for `higher`, so the search works on this text. The paper's own words are "elevated level of consciousness" (qualified "as measured by neural signal diversity") and the hedge on "higher" quoted above.
- **The press release**: Imperial College London news, "First evidence for higher state of consciousness found", by Ryan O'Hare, 19 April 2017 (https://www.imperial.ac.uk/news/178857/first-evidence-higher-state-consciousness-found/). Its opening line: "Researchers have uncovered the first scientific evidence of a 'higher' state of consciousness." Seth is quoted with a qualifier: "we can say that the psychedelic state appears as a higher ‘level’ of consciousness than normal, but only with respect to this specific mathematical measure." The release adds that "this does not mean that the psychedelic state is a ‘better’ or more desirable state of consciousness". The Sussex release it mirrors is no longer online. A WebSearch summary attributes the same headline to Sussex, but I could not check that at source.
- **The academic relay**: Rankaduwa & Owen (2023, PMC10072236) write: "To understand why psychedelics have been described as inducing a "higher state of consciousness" ( Schartner et al . 2017a )". **The quotation marks attribute to the paper a phrase it does not contain.** Their second citation is accurate: ""elevated level of consciousness" (with respect to specific neural measures) ( Schartner et al . 2017a , 1)". Cardone et al. (2025) reuse the framing in their own voice ("While we expected a higher state of consciousness"; verified by the sibling note).
- **Rule for writers**: never quote "higher state of consciousness" as Schartner's words. If the framing is mentioned, attribute it to the 2017 press coverage and pair it with the paper's own hedge.

### Bajwa et al. (2025), "A repeated awakening study exploring the capacity of complexity measures to capture dreaming during propofol sedation"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-12695-z (PMC12460656, PMID 40993159; CC BY)
- **Type**: Article, *Scientific Reports* 15, 32746 (24 Sept 2025). Authors (Crossref): Imad J. Bajwa, Andre S. Nilsen, René Skukies, Arnfinn Aamodt, Gernot Ernst, Johan F. Storm, Bjørn E. Juel (Oslo).
- **Design**: "in 20 healthy participants during propofol sedation", deep sedation (RASS −4), with repeated awakenings. "Out of the 52 attempted awakenings in this study, 24 produced reports of having had an experience, while there were 5 reports of no experience." The other 23 were unarousable or incoherent. Measures were PCIst (TMS-evoked, computed over several minutes ending one minute before awakening) and single-channel LZc (from the minute immediately before awakening).
- **Result**: "While our study confirms earlier findings that the EEG complexity measures significantly decrease from the awake state to the sedated state, we find no evidence that these measures differ between periods associated with reports of dreaming and non-dreaming, within the sedated state."
- **The authors' own limits (copy these into any brief)**: "This draws the power of the statistical model into question and gives further ground for caution when interpreting the null finding reported here." "a null result does not necessarily allow us to reject the hypothesis of there being a relationship". On timing: "PCI st may essentially not be time-resolved enough". TMS-EEG quality was compromised by movement, scalp contact and artefact.
- **The puzzle they pose**, which is the experience-versus-responsiveness question: "how then can we explain the drop between wakefulness and a state of sedation that is presumably filled with experiences (dreams)?" They offer three readings: degraded but present experience; measures "too coarse"; or a lower frequency of dreaming. They found no evidence of fluctuation.
- **On report**: "Given that subjective report of experience ought to be the gold standard for inferring that a human being is conscious, we should be careful of a priori discounting subjective reports due the state they report in". They tested for confabulation and memory and took vivid reports at face value.
- **Assessment of "the cleanest test the wing has"** (the optimistic review's phrase). It is the cleanest *design*: one agent, one depth, immediate report, both a spontaneous and an evoked measure. It is also among the weakest *evidence*: n = 5 negative reports, and a PCIst window that does not cover the reported interval. Use it as "found no evidence of a difference" and never as "complexity fails to track experience".
- **Quotes**: verbatim, one hit each; nonsense control 0.

### Aamodt et al. (2021; 2023): the NREM2 replication pair (sleep, not anaesthesia; companion evidence)
- Aamodt, Nilsen, Thürer, Moghadam, Kauppi, Juel & Storm (2021), "EEG Signal Diversity Varies With Sleep Stage and Aspects of Dream Experience", *Front Psychol* 12, 655884 (PMC8102678). There were 16 analysed participants, sleep-deprived, with serial awakenings. "Signal diversity decreased with sleep depth, but was not significantly different between dreaming and non-dreaming, even though there was a significant positive correlation between Lempel-Ziv complexity of EEG recorded over the posterior cortex and thought-perceptual ratings of dream contents."
- Aamodt, Sevenius Nilsen, Markhus, Kusztor, HasanzadehMoghadam, Kauppi, Thürer, Storm & Juel (2023), "EEG Lempel-Ziv complexity varies with sleep stage, but does not seem to track dream experience", *Front Hum Neurosci* 16, 987714 (PMC9871639). It was published online 10 Jan 2023 in volume 16 (2022); Crossref "issued" is 2023, and Bajwa's reference list gives both years. Abstract: "We found no significant difference in LZC between dream and non-dream awakenings, nor any significant relationship between LZC and subjective ratings of dream experience, within the same sleep stage (NREM2)." And: "The failure to reproduce our own previous finding ... raises the question of whether EEG LZC is really a reliable correlate of richness of experience as such, within the same sleep stage."
- **Bearing**: this is a self-replication failure by the group most invested in the measure, and Michael Schartner was a reviewer of both papers (per the PMC front matter). The pattern: between stages LZc sorts states, and within one stage it does not follow report.

### Nieminen et al. (2016), "Consciousness and cortical responsiveness: a within-state study during non-rapid eye movement sleep" (the within-state positive)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/srep30932 (PMC4974655); *Sci Rep* 6, 30932. Authors: Nieminen, Gosseries, Massimini, Saad, Sheldon, Boly, Siclari, Postle, Tononi.
- "We recorded TMS-evoked responses in six healthy participants" over 29 nights. Of 187 usable questionings, 70 were experience with recall, 39 experience without recall and 78 no experience. "when subjects reported no conscious experience upon awakening, TMS evoked a larger negative deflection and a shorter phase-locked response compared to when they reported a dream". The negative-deflection amplitude correlated inversely with report length.
- **Caveat that matters**: the number of pulses per awakening "was insufficient for computing the perturbational complexity index". The within-state positive is therefore for **evoked bistability markers**, not for PCI. Lee et al. (2019, *Sci Rep* 9, 5175; abstract) extend this with connectivity measures (lower low-frequency phase-locking and clustering during reported consciousness).
- **Bearing**: the evoked-response family has a within-state positive in sleep, and the spontaneous LZc family has within-state nulls. Bajwa et al. tried to replicate Nieminen in propofol and could not, for methodological reasons.

### Within the psychedelic state: correlations with ratings, and the 2026 reversal
- **Timmermann et al. (2019)**, "Neural correlates of the DMT experience assessed with multivariate EEG", *Sci Rep* 9, 16324 (PMC6864083). Thirteen participants, IV DMT, real-time intensity ratings each minute. Within-subject across time: "Increased signal diversity (LZs and LZs N ) correlated positively with intensity ratings also" (N = 12). Carhart-Harris is last author and Schartner a co-author.
- **Farnes et al. (2020)**, *PLoS One* 15(11), e0242056 (PMC7682856). Subanaesthetic ketamine, n = 10. "This increase in signal diversity correlated with subjective assessment of altered states of consciousness." Also: "spontaneous signal diversity was significantly higher when participants had eyes open compared to eyes closed" in both conditions. Here a sensory-input variable moves LZc without anyone calling eyes-open a higher level of consciousness. Bajwa et al. reproduce the eyes-open/closed drop.
- **Mediano et al. (2024)**, "Effects of External Stimulation on Psychedelic State Neurodynamics", *ACS Chem Neurosci* 15(3), 462–471 (PMC10853937; Seth, Barrett and Carhart-Harris among the authors). LSD under eyes-closed, eyes-open, music and video conditions. "brain entropy changes are consistently associated with subjective ratings of the psychedelic experience, but this relationship is disrupted when participants are viewing a video". "subjects under LSD watching a video had the highest absolute brain entropy but did not give maximal subjective ratings in any of the psychometric items". One of their two readings: "it could be that LZ most faithfully indexes brain activity associated with low-level sensory processing".
- **Lewis-Healey et al. (2026)**, "Time-resolved Neural and Experience Dynamics of Medium- and High-dose N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)", *J Cogn Neurosci* 38(6), 1244–1263 (doi:10.1162/jocn.a.2423, PMID 41468116; **abstract only**). "Nineteen participants received either a 20-mg or a 40-mg dose of freebase DMT" in a blinded, counterbalanced, naturalistic design with time-resolved experience measures (Temporal Experience Tracing). "oscillatory alpha power and permutation entropy most strongly associated with continuous subjective experience dimensions. Strikingly, Lempel-Ziv complexity, previously hailed as a robust correlate of subjective experiences within the psychedelic state, yielded the weakest associations." Conclusion: "the relationship between neural complexity and phenomenology in psychedelic states is less clear than originally hypothesized". **Tagliazucchi** is a co-author, as he was of Peterson et al. (2019). Quotes verified in both the PubMed abstract and the OpenAlex inverted-index reconstruction. Whether "weakest" means non-significant was **not** checked.
- **Leads, abstracts only**: Singleton et al. (2025, *Commun Biol* 8, 631): trajectories of control energy under DMT correlate with EEG signal diversity and intensity ratings. Nikolič et al. (2026, *J Psychopharmacol* 40(11), 1799–1816): psilocybin, N = 20, gamma-band signal diversity correlated with BPRS (r = 0.71). Kroupi et al. (2026, *Brain Res* 1892, 150530): DMT, n = 13; "Complexity metrics positively correlated with subjective intensity", with Higuchi fractal dimension and permutation entropy most sensitive. Larsen et al. (2026, medRxiv preprint): LSD, psilocybin and mescaline versus MDMA and d-amphetamine; "Both drug classes increased Lempel-Ziv and spatial complexity". That is a specificity problem: LZc rises under stimulants too.
- **Pattern**: across participants and across time within a session, complexity usually correlates with reported intensity. But LZc also follows sensory input (eyes open, video), rises under non-psychedelic stimulants, and in the newest time-resolved study is the weakest correlate. Permutation entropy recurs as the stronger candidate (Lewis-Healey 2026; Kroupi 2026; Casey 2024 below). That convergence is a lead, not a finding.

### Sutherland et al. (2026), psilocybin with midazolam: a within-session responsiveness/experience contrast
- *Transl Psychiatry* 16, 160 (PMC13004994; open label). "Eight healthy participants were administered an oral 25 mg dose of psilocybin" plus IV midazolam titrated to mild-to-moderate sedation and memory impairment. Experience was rated **during** the session (six ASC items at 60–360 min), and sedation by observer (OAA/S). "OAA/S improved model fits for alpha power while ASC improved model fits for LZCn and spectral exponent."
- **Bearing**: the one within-session design that pits an observer-rated responsiveness scale against self-rated experience. LZCn sided with experience. It cuts against reading the anaesthesia nulls as "LZc tracks responsiveness". Limits: n = 8, open label, variable midazolam doses, time confounded with drug onset. Use only as a tempering lead.

### Casey et al. (2024), "Evaluation of putative signatures of consciousness using specific definitions of responsiveness, connectedness, and consciousness"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bja.2023.09.031 (PMID 37914581; PMC10808836 is the abstract only). *Br J Anaesth* 132(2), 300–311. Authors: Casey, Tanabe, Farahbakhsh, Parker, Bo, White, Ballweg, Mcintosh, Filbey, Banks, Saalmann, Pearce, Sanders (Wisconsin/Sydney). Data come from NCT03284307: dexmedetomidine, propofol and natural sleep with serial awakenings.
- **Key points (abstract, verbatim)**: "A major limitation of prior studies is the use of responsiveness as an index of consciousness." They "evaluated 10 previously published resting-state EEG-based measures that were derived using unresponsiveness as a proxy for unconsciousness", representing "the complexity, connectivity, cross-frequency coupling, graph theory, and power spectrum measures". Result: "Although many of the proposed markers were associated with consciousness per se (reported subjective experience), none were specific to consciousness alone; rather, each was also associated with connectedness (i.e. awareness of the environment)." Several "showed no association with consciousness and were associated only with connectedness". "the transition from disconnected consciousness to unconsciousness was associated with significant decreases in permutation entropy and spectral exponent". Conclusion: "None of the proposed EEG-based neural correlates of unresponsiveness corresponded solely to consciousness".
- **Not seen**: which complexity measures (LZc or others) were among the ten, and their individual results. The full text was not retrievable.
- **Bearing**: the most direct answer to "experience or responsiveness?" The markers do not track responsiveness *instead of* experience; they fail to separate the two. Casey et al. (2022, *BJA* 128, 1006–1018; abstract) is the derivation study on the same dataset and uses spectral and source-localised signatures, not complexity.

### Other within-state context (abstract level)
- Wong et al. (2020), "The Dream Catcher experiment", *Neurosci Conscious* niaa006: a blinded team classifying 54 one-minute NREM recordings from spectral power: "At no level of blindness did the Analysis Team perform significantly better than chance".
- Wong et al. (2025), "A dream EEG and mentation database", *Nat Commun* 16, 7495 (PMC12350935): 20 datasets, 505 participants, 2643 awakenings. Experience versus no experience was decodable within stage, but weakly: "For NREM, the highest performance was obtained for PSD features (average AUC = 0.586"; REM best AUC 0.700 (catch22 features). Complexity measures were not among the reported features.
- Darracq et al. (2018, *Sci Rep* 8, 16664): evoked alpha power is reduced in disconnected consciousness (REM, ketamine), while evoked low-gamma is reduced in unconscious propofol and NREM states. This is an evoked-spectral marker that tracks disconnection.

## Major Positions

### Complexity as an index of conscious level and richness (Casali, Massimini, Sarasso; Schartner, Seth, Barrett; Carhart-Harris)
- **Core claim**: complexity (evoked or spontaneous) indexes conscious level, and psychedelic elevations index richer experience.
- **Evidence for**: robust between-state ordering; ketamine anaesthesia keeps PCI wake-like alongside dream reports (Sarasso 2015); many within-session correlations with intensity ratings.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral between production and filter readings. Either reads a between-state correlate.

### The within-state sceptics (Oslo group: Storm, Juel, Aamodt, Bajwa; Lewis-Healey, Bekinschtein and Tagliazucchi)
- **Core claim**: complexity sorts states but has not been shown to follow report within a state. Self-replication failed in NREM2; LZc was the weakest DMT correlate.
- **Note**: these are friendly sceptics. The Oslo group still treats complexity as a consciousness measure and reads its own nulls cautiously (underpowered, timing, report reliability).

### Responsiveness, connectedness and consciousness as three variables (Sanders, Casey, Tononi, Laureys; Montupil and colleagues)
- **Core claim**: unresponsiveness is not unconsciousness ("Unresponsiveness ≠ Unconsciousness", Sanders et al. 2012, cited by Bajwa). Measures validated against responsiveness need re-validation against serial-awakening reports, and when re-validated they track connectedness at least as much as experience.
- **Relation to tenets**: the conceptual move is the same as disruption L104 (behavioural absence is not experiential absence), which the Map endorses.

## Key Debates

### Does complexity track experience or responsiveness?
- **Current state**: neither cleanly. Between states it tracks both together. Ketamine anaesthesia is the clearest between-agent case where complexity sides with report against responsiveness (Sarasso 2015, group level). Within unresponsive states, spontaneous LZc has not followed report (Bajwa; Aamodt ×2), while evoked bistability has (Nieminen; sleep only). Re-validated against serial awakenings, markers track connectedness wherever they track experience (Casey 2024). Within a responsive psychedelic state, complexity follows ratings in most studies but is confounded by sensory input and stimulant effects, and was the weakest correlate in the newest time-resolved design. In the one within-session contrast with observer-rated sedation (Sutherland 2026, n = 8), LZCn followed experience.
- **Defensible summary**: spontaneous complexity is a reliable *state* discriminator and an unreliable *within-state experience* discriminator so far. The evidence does **not** support the stronger claim that "complexity tracks responsiveness rather than experience".

### Is the within-state null a measurement failure or a report failure?
- Bajwa discusses both: the measures may be "too coarse", or reports after propofol awakening may be confused, amnesic or confabulated. They controlled for this and took vivid reports at face value. The sleep page's "white dreams" point (experience without recalled content) also blurs the dream/no-dream contrast. Nieminen pooled "experience without recall" with "experience with recall".

### Who first framed psychedelics as a "higher" state?
- The 2017 press release coined the headline and Seth's quoted qualifier limited it. The paper hedged. Rankaduwa & Owen (2023) quote-marked it as the paper's. Cardone et al. (2025) use it in their own voice. The phrase "higher states of consciousness" has a much older popular history (meditation literature, for example) that was not traced here. Do not credit Schartner et al. with coining the phrase.

## The Map-Specific Question {#map-question}

**What the data license.** Spontaneous complexity reliably separates waking from sedation and sleep stages from one another. It has not been shown to separate experience from no experience *within* one of those states. Evoked bistability markers have some within-state success in sleep, and validated against serial awakenings, the markers in use track connectedness wherever they track experience.

**Where the Map stands, without overclaiming.**
1. **Consistency with disruption L104.** The Map holds that behavioural unresponsiveness cannot mark conscious absence. The within-state designs put that premise into practice by using report instead of behaviour, and they are the right tests by the Map's own standard. The Map should cite them as tests of the *measures*, not as findings about consciousness.
2. **No dualist dividend.** Suppose it turns out that complexity follows connectedness or access rather than phenomenality. On the [[filter-theory|interface reading]], that would mean the measure registers the interface's coupling to the world, not the presence of experience. A physicalist with a two-component model (Block; the phenomenal/access split Carhart-Harris invoked in 2026, per the sibling note) says the same with different nouns: the measure tracks the access mechanism, and phenomenal consciousness has other correlates, such as the posterior hot zone or bistability. **No discrimination.** Mark any interface gloss as interpretive per [[evidential-status-discipline]].
3. **Occam's limits.** EBH's single scalar is the economical hypothesis. Within-state evidence is where it would have to earn that economy, and so far it has not. This supports the EBH page's existing caution (L86) and needs no stronger wording.
4. **Over-concession watch.** Do not write that within-state experience is "undetectable" or that "no measure can" separate it. Nieminen (evoked bistability), Siclari (posterior slow-wave activity) and the DREAM database (AUC 0.586–0.700) show partial within-state decoding. The claim is about *spontaneous complexity* specifically.
5. **Overclaim watch.** Do not write that complexity "tracks responsiveness, not experience". Casey shows the two are not separated by the markers. Sarasso's ketamine and Sutherland's midazolam data point the other way, and the anaesthesia nulls are underpowered.
