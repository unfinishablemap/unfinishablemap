## Executive Summary

Purification is the axiom the Map has been leaning on without ever stating. Verified at the publisher of record, Galley and Masanes (2018) prove that every modification of the Born rule violates purification **and** local tomography — so either axiom, holding alone, forces the rule. What today's research adds is that the two are not symmetric partners. The same abstract states that purification alone, together with pure-state structure and reversible dynamics, **derives** the measurement postulates; local tomography gets no parallel sentence. Purification is the axiom that does the forcing, and local tomography is a corollary casualty. The Map's Born-preservation commitment therefore rests principally on purification, an axiom that has never been named in a heading anywhere in the corpus.

The interface question has a sharper answer than expected, and it cuts both ways. Chiribella (2018) shows that subsystem states admit a canonical purification when the enclosing whole is a **closed system with invertible transformations**. So purification across a brain-plus-substrate composite is guaranteed exactly if that composite is closed and reversible — which is precisely what an outcome-selecting interface puts in question. Against that, purification is the one Chiribella-D'Ariano-Perinotti axiom classical theory fails, and Galley and Masanes note that violating it implies "some irreducible classicality." A non-quantum substrate is the textbook way to introduce irreducible classicality into a composite. The Map's own dualism thus supplies a positive reason to expect purification failure at the cut — the first argument the corpus has for the second lock, and the reason this subject is worth writing up.

## Key Sources

### Galley & Masanes (2018) — the theorem, re-verified

- **URL**: https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2018-11-06-104/ · arXiv:1801.06414
- **Type**: Peer-reviewed paper, *Quantum* 2, 104. DOI 10.22331/q-2018-11-06-104
- **Verified at**: publisher of record and arXiv listing, independently of any Map page.
- **The conjunction holds.** Abstract, verbatim: "We prove that in all these theories the purification principle is violated, meaning that some mixed states are not the reduction of a pure state in a larger system." And separately: "We also prove that in all such modifications the task of state tomography with local measurements is impossible." Both, for every modification. Today's correction to the local-tomography article is right, and the contraposition — if either axiom holds, the Born rule is forced — is valid within the classified class.
- **The asymmetry is the new finding.** Verbatim: "This allows us to derive the measurement postulates of quantum theory from the structure of pure states and reversible dynamics, and the requirement that the purification principle holds." Purification is presented as a *derivation base* for the Born rule. There is no corresponding sentence for local tomography, which appears only as a further casualty. The two locks are not equals: purification is the one that does the forcing.
- **Irreducible classicality.** Verbatim: "The violation of the purification principle implies that there is some irreducible classicality in these theories, which appears like an important clue for the problem of deriving the Born rule within the many-worlds interpretation." Directly relevant to the Map twice over — see the interface section, and note the Tenet-4 hook: the authors read purification failure as bearing on the MWI Born-rule derivation problem the Map rejects MWI partly over.
- **Scope condition.** Verbatim: "using the existing classification of all alternatives to the measurement postulates of quantum theory we study the properties of bi-partite systems in these alternative theories." Pure states and reversible dynamics are held fixed; only the measurement postulate varies. The contraposition is valid *inside that class*, not across all GPTs.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral source; strengthens the Map's Born-preservation commitment by identifying which axiom actually carries it.

### Chiribella, D'Ariano & Perinotti (2010) — the canonical statement

- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.1583 · *Phys. Rev. A* 81, 062348. DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.81.062348
- **The axiom, verbatim**: "Every mixed state has a purification, unique up to reversible channels on the purifying system."
- **The equivalence that matters for the interface, verbatim**: purification is "equivalent to the existence of a reversible realization of every physical process, namely that every physical process can be regarded as arising from a reversible interaction of the system with an environment, which is eventually discarded."
- **Consequences derived from purification alone**, per the abstract: no information without disturbance; no joint discrimination of all pure states; no cloning; teleportation; no programming; no bit commitment; a Choi-Jamiolkowski-style isomorphism between transformations and bipartite states. Purification is doing an enormous amount of structural work — which is why an interface that violates it forfeits more than the Born rule.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The reversible-realization formulation is the one the Map should adopt, because it states the axiom in terms of *processes* rather than states, and the interface is a process.

### Chiribella (2018) — purification, subsystems, and the closure condition

- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.01943 · *Entropy* 20(5), 358
- **Why it matters**: this is the source that actually addresses the Map's question. Subsystems are not intrinsic; each agent's subsystem is carved out relative to that agent's experimental capabilities. The paper establishes that "all the states of all subsystems admit a canonical purification" — but within a restriction to closed systems where, per the abstract, "all physical transformations act invertibly and where all states can be generated from a fixed initial state."
- **The interface reading**: purification across a composite is not a brute fact about composites. It is a consequence of the enclosing system being closed and its dynamics invertible. That converts the Map's open question from "does purification hold across the cut?" — unanswerable — into "is brain-plus-substrate a closed system under invertible transformations?", which is a question the Map's own commitments bear on.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral framework; supplies the conditional the Map needs.
- **Caveat**: read from the abstract only. See Gaps.

### Chiribella, D'Ariano & Perinotti (2011) — what purification excludes

- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6451 · *Phys. Rev. A* 84, 012311
- **Abstract, verbatim**: "Five elementary axioms-causality, perfect distinguishability, ideal compression, local distinguishability, and pure conditioning-define a broad class of theories of information-processing that can be regarded as a standard. One postulate-purification-singles out quantum theory within this class."
- **Note for the Map's other axiom**: local distinguishability — local tomography under its other name — sits among the *five*, while purification is the *one*. The two axioms are separately listed and separately needed, which is direct evidence of their logical independence.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Primary citation for purification's discriminating power.

### Winczewski et al. (2023) — what theories without purification have instead

- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02222 · *Quantum* 7, 1159
- **Abstract, verbatim**: "A key postulate is the purification postulate, which we propose to replace by a more generally applicable postulate that we call the complete extension postulate (CEP), i.e., the existence of an extension of a physical system from which one can generate any other extension."
- **Key point**: general no-signalling theories satisfy the CEP, and the complete extension need not be pure. This is the shape of the alternative — a theory can have a well-behaved notion of "the larger system your ignorance comes from" without that larger system being in a *pure* state. A non-Born interface would not be structureless; it would be CEP-satisfying and purification-violating.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral, and useful to the Map as the constructive alternative rather than a bare negation. Uncited in the corpus.

### Torres Alegre (2025) — the mechanism behind the purification dependency

- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12636. Preprint, v1 14 Dec 2025, v3 4 Feb 2026. **No journal reference listed** — not peer-reviewed.
- **Verbatim**: "in any GPT satisfying purification, and therefore admitting steering, the only such relationship consistent with no-signaling is the identity Phi(p) = p," yielding "the Born rule `|<phi|psi>|^2` as the unique causally consistent probability assignment."
- **The mechanism**: purification's role is to guarantee **steering**. Steering is what lets a remote party's choice of measurement shape the ensemble seen locally, and it is that leverage which turns any non-identity probability relationship into a signalling channel. The corpus currently says the purification assumption "is doing the real work" without saying what the work is.
- **Tenet alignment**: Supports the Map's Born-preservation commitment, conditional on an unrefereed preprint and on purification reaching the interface.

## What Purification Says, Operationally

Every mixed state of a system arises as the marginal of a pure state on a larger system, and that purifying state is unique up to a reversible channel acting on the purifying system alone. The informal gloss in the literature: whenever you are ignorant about a system's state, you may always attribute that ignorance to the system being part of a larger whole about which you have complete knowledge.

The uniqueness clause is what gives the axiom teeth. Without it the requirement is nearly free — most theories can embed a mixed state as some marginal of something. With it, the theory must have exactly enough structure that all purifications of a given state are interconvertible by reversible operations on the environment, and no more. This is why the corpus's current bare-existence gloss understates the axiom.

The process form is equivalent and more useful at the interface: every physical process arises from a reversible interaction between the system and an environment which is then discarded. Purification is the statement that irreversibility is always apparent rather than fundamental — that information is conserved, and mixedness is a bookkeeping consequence of tracing out.

## Which Theories Violate Purification, and Why

**Classical probability theory.** The canonical violator. Purification is the one Chiribella-D'Ariano-Perinotti postulate not shared with classical theory — the five elementary axioms hold classically, and purification is what singles out quantum theory within that class. The structural reason: a pure state of a classical composite is a deterministic assignment to both parts, so its marginal is deterministic too. Classical mixedness is irreducible ignorance and there is no larger classical system that removes it. Note this is an entailment from the verified abstract given that classical theory sits inside the five-axiom class, rather than a sentence quoted from the paper; see Gaps.

**General no-signalling theories.** Boxworld and its relatives fail purification and satisfy the weaker complete extension postulate instead, with extensions that need not be pure.

**Born-rule modifications.** By Galley-Masanes, every alternative measurement postulate in the classification. Their diagnosis is the striking one: violating purification "implies that there is some irreducible classicality in these theories."

**What does *not* violate it.** The Map should resist the assumption that the exotic theories cluster. Real-vector-space quantum theory fails local tomography, and the Map's own local-tomography article builds its failure model on it — but a theory can fail local tomography while retaining purification, and the two axioms sit at different places in the CDP list precisely because neither implies the other. Real quantum theory looks like a case in point: the real Schmidt decomposition purifies any real density matrix inside a real tensor product. If that is right, the Map's flagship failure model for local tomography **remains Born-constrained**, because purification survives in it — which is the cleanest possible illustration of why the first lock alone is not enough. Treat this as a Map-side inference, flagged in Gaps; it is not verified at a source that states it.

## Why Purification Is the Decisive Door

Three independent reasons converge, and they are stronger than "it is the one we have not covered."

1. **It is the derivation base.** Galley and Masanes derive the measurement postulates from pure-state structure, reversible dynamics, and purification. Local tomography never appears as a premise anywhere in that derivation; it appears as an additional casualty of modification. Adding purification to the fixed structure gets you the Born rule outright.
2. **It is what the steering argument runs on.** Torres Alegre needs purification to obtain steering, and steering is the engine of the no-signalling argument. The Map's causal-consistency anchor inherits its dependency on purification, not on local tomography.
3. **It is the harder axiom to lose innocently.** Local-tomography failure is comparatively mild — real quantum theory fails it and remains a consistent theory reproducing all bipartite Bell correlations, with "limited holism" as the whole cost. Purification failure costs the no-cloning theorem, no-information-without-disturbance, teleportation, and the transformation-state isomorphism. A composite that fails purification is a substantially stranger object than one that merely fails local tomography.

## The Interface Question

This is the question the Map actually needs answered, and the honest position is that it has arguments in both directions and no resolution.

**The case that purification holds at the cut, forcing the Born rule.** Chiribella's subsystem framework gives purification for free wherever the enclosing whole is closed and its transformations invertible. An interactionist dualism of the Map's kind is naturally read as positing exactly that: brain-plus-substrate is the closed whole, and the apparent mixedness of the brain's state is what you get by tracing out the substrate. On that reading the substrate *is* the purifying environment the axiom postulates, purification holds across the composite by construction, and the Born rule is forced with no further argument. This reading is uncomfortable for anyone hoping the interface escapes the constraint, and comfortable for the Map's actual commitment, which is Born preservation.

**The case that purification fails at the cut, opening the door.** The reversible-realization form is the pressure point. Purification says every process is a reversible interaction with a discarded environment — that information is conserved and irreversibility is apparent. An interface at which consciousness *selects* an outcome looks like a genuinely irreducible, information-non-conserving process, which is what purification forbids. And the Galley-Masanes diagnosis points the same way: purification failure means "irreducible classicality," and a substrate that is not itself a quantum system is precisely how irreducible classicality would enter the composite. Since purification is the one axiom classical theory fails, a substrate with any classical character drags the composite out of the purification-satisfying regime.

**The upshot.** The Map's own dualism supplies the first positive reason the corpus has for expecting the *second* lock to fail — where the local-tomography article could only observe that nothing guarantees the first. The two arguments pull opposite ways and turn on an unstated question: does the Map hold that brain-plus-substrate is a closed reversible system with the brain as a traced-out subsystem, or that selection is an irreducible process within it? That is a question about the Map's own commitments, answerable from the inside, and it deserves to be posed explicitly rather than left implicit.

Two disciplines apply throughout, both already the corpus's practice. Galley-Masanes runs one way only, from modified rule to violated axioms, so purification failure does not construct a non-Born interface. And per the Map's evidential-status discipline, naming the axiom is a coherence move that sharpens where Tenet 2's warrant runs out; it supplies no framework-independent support for the interface reading over its rivals.

## Key Debates

### Is purification the right postulate, or an artefact of the reconstruction programme?

- **Sides**: Chiribella, D'Ariano and Perinotti treat purification as the informational postulate that singles out quantum theory. Winczewski and colleagues argue a more generally applicable postulate — complete extension — should replace it, since no-signalling theories satisfy CEP while failing purification.
- **Core disagreement**: whether the pure-state requirement is fundamental or an over-strong stand-in for "your ignorance has a source."
- **Current state**: ongoing. Relevant to the Map because CEP marks out the space a non-Born interface would occupy.

### Does purification imply local tomography?

- **Sides**: settled in the negative by the structure of the CDP axiom list, where local distinguishability and purification are separately assumed.
- **Why it matters here**: it is what makes "one door with two locks" a substantive claim rather than two names for the same lock, and it opens the possibility that the Map's own local-tomography failure model retains purification.

### Is tomographic locality empirically established?

- **Sides**: Renou et al. (2021) and its 2022 realisations versus Hoffreumon and Woods (2026); Lismer et al. (2025) adds a direct GPT-framework test finding no violation in full quantum mechanics.
- **Current state**: live. Covered by the local-tomography article except for the 2025 test.
