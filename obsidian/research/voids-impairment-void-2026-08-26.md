# Research: Voids - The Impairment Void

**Date**: 2026-08-26
**Search queries used**: Van Dongen 2003 chronic sleep restriction unaware cognitive deficits; sleep deprivation metacognition confidence calibration; drivers unaware falling asleep microsleep awareness; pilots hypoxia self-recognition failure time of useful consciousness; Cable 2003 in-flight hypoxia incidents military aircraft; impaired awareness of hypoglycaemia prevalence Gold Clarke HAAF; Cryer hypoglycemia-associated autonomic failure NEJM 2013; Mellanby effect acute tolerance descending limb; nitrogen narcosis divers self-assessment; frontal lobe paradox knowing-doing dissociation; paradoxical undressing hypothermia Wedin Vanggaard Hirvonen 1979
**Voids category**: Mixed — a *partly deflated* Unexplorable. The general claim is largely false; a narrower structural residue survives.

## Executive Summary

Can a mind detect that it is currently impaired? The catalogue's working assumption — stated outright in [[disappearance-voids]] — is that it cannot, because the monitoring faculty degrades before the faculties it monitors. This survey finds that assumption **substantially contradicted by the literature it implicitly rests on**, and the contradiction is the most useful thing here.

Across five independent impairment domains, the coarse-grained self-report channel works better than the void framing predicts. Sleep-deprived subjects retain calibration, resolution and validity of subjective certainty essentially intact (Baranski, Pigeau & Angus 1994). A pre-registered-scale replication at N = 182 finds a metacognitive-accuracy gap between sleep-deprived and rested groups of 55% versus 59%, with the probability that the difference exceeds 10 percentage points estimated **below 1%** (Sundelin et al. 2026). Drivers cannot fall asleep at the wheel without a sustained, consciously registered run-up to it (Reyner & Horne 1998), a result independently reproduced twice (Williamson et al. 2014; Anderson et al. 2023, where "*No severe drive events occurred without noticeable symptoms beforehand*"). In real in-flight hypoxia incidents, **75.8%** of episodes were recognised by the aircrew themselves (Cable 2003). Narcotised divers make a correct *global* self-assessment that they are impaired (Hobbs 2014).

So the sweeping version fails. What survives is sharper and stranger: the void is not in the *detector* but in the *gauge*, and it opens only where the gauge is transformed rather than merely dulled. Three conditions do this, and each has a different signature. The gauge can **plateau** while the deficit keeps growing — Van Dongen et al. 2003 found subjects "*largely unaware*" of cumulative deficits because sleepiness ratings adapt while performance does not. It can **invert**, falling while impairment persists or worsens: on the descending limb of the blood-alcohol curve subjects rate themselves 29% less intoxicated and become *three times as willing to drive* while driving performance is 96% worse (Holland & Ferner 2017); hypoxia arrives with euphoria; terminal hypothermia produces paradoxical undressing, the sensation of heat while freezing to death. And it can be **deleted by training** — recurrent hypoglycaemia progressively abolishes the warning symptoms through hypoglycaemia-associated autonomic failure, so the episodes the alarm exists to prevent are precisely what dismantle the alarm (Cryer 2013).

This makes the impairment void a **calibration case** of the same species as the olfactory void: an apparently structural limit that better sampling shows to be mostly situational, leaving a narrow real residue. The residue's shape matters — the felt sense of impairment behaves as a **separately generated signal** that can decouple from, or run opposite to, the state it purports to report. That is the identical structural shape the Map has already isolated in [[noetic-feelings-void]], [[perceptual-reality-monitoring-void]], and [[imagery-void]], and which [[anti-correlated-metacognitive-signal]] names as a concept. This note supplies that concept its acute-state limb, which it currently lacks entirely.

⚠️ **Live defect found in a published article.** [[disappearance-voids]] asserts, uncited: "*Self-assessment accuracy declines faster than actual performance under sleep deprivation — people do not know how impaired they are. The monitoring system fails before the execution systems it monitors.*" Baranski et al. 1994 and Sundelin et al. 2026 both directly contradict this; Van Dongen 2003, the only plausible source for it, explicitly declines to draw it. Details in [Correction Required](#correction-required) below.

## Key Sources

### Van Dongen, Maislin, Mullington & Dinges (2003) — the plateau face
- **URL**: https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article-abstract/26/2/117/2709164
- **Type**: Paper (*Sleep* 26(2):117–126)
- **Key points**:
  - n = 48 healthy adults, sleep restricted to 8 h / 6 h / 4 h for 14 days, plus a 3-day total-deprivation arm.
  - Abstract, verified verbatim against the full text: "*Sleepiness ratings suggest that subjects were largely unaware of these increasing cognitive deficits, which may explain why the impact of chronic sleep restriction on waking cognitive functions is often assumed to be benign.*"
  - The mechanism is **adaptation of the gauge, not failure of the detector**. Discussion: "*Surprisingly, by the end of the 14 days of sleep restriction, when performance was at its worst levels, subjects in the 4 h and 6 h sleep period conditions reported feeling only slightly sleepy. Therefore, unlike performance measures, sleepiness ratings appeared to show adaptation to chronic partial sleep deprivation.*"
  - Not a ceiling artefact — the authors rule this out explicitly: "*It is unlikely that this was the result of a ceiling effect or other metric-related artifact, because subjects in the total sleep deprivation condition reported considerably greater levels of sleepiness, and did not show evidence of adaptation.*"
- **⚠️ The authors refuse the inference the Map's article makes.** Discussion, verbatim: "*These findings for subjective sleepiness suggest that once sleep restriction is chronic, subjects either cannot reliably introspect with regard to their actual sleepiness levels, or as long as they are receiving at least approximately 4 h of sleep nightly they do not experience a sense of sleepiness anywhere near the levels found for total sleep deprivation. Regardless of the explanation…*" The second disjunct is **not** a metacognitive failure — it says the subjects report accurately on a phenomenal state that has genuinely adapted. The paper leaves the disjunction open; [[disappearance-voids]] silently picks the first.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits — the simple reading ("monitoring breaks") is the one the source declines.

### Baranski, Pigeau & Angus (1994) — the strongest deflation
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10607107/ · doi:10.1111/j.1365-2869.1994.tb00102.x
- **Type**: Paper (*Journal of Sleep Research* 3(1):36–44)
- **Key points**: 16 subjects, sustained cognitive work across three days. Performance on sequential addition deteriorated as expected and recovered after sleep. But: "*calibration analyses… revealed that the correlation between confidence and performance (calibration), the ability to differentiate correct from incorrect judgments (resolution), and validity of subjective 'certainty', were all unaffected by SD.*"
- **Quote**: "*Hence, in the absence of external feedback from the environment, people have access to fairly reliable internal feedback about their performance during periods of sustained and vigilant cognitive activity.*"
- **Bearing**: This is a direct, sign-reversed contradiction of the claim in [[disappearance-voids]]. It is also the *older* study, which matters: the article's claim is not a stale reading of superseded work, it is unsupported in either direction of the record.

### Sundelin, Jemstedt, Gavel, Schwartz & Axelsson (2026) — the modern deflation
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40810275/ · doi:10.1111/jsr.70141
- **Type**: Paper (*Journal of Sleep Research* 35(2):e70141; epub 2025-08-14)
- **Key points**: N = 182 randomised to one night of total sleep deprivation or three nights of sufficient sleep, then five cognitive tests each followed by a self-rating. Metacognitive accuracy operationalised as correctly identifying whether one performed above or below the median.
- **Quote**: "*The probability was 55% in the sleep-deprived group, and 59% in the rested group, suggesting some decrease in performance awareness during sleep loss. However, the probability that this difference in judgements is practically significant (i.e., exceeding 10 percentage points) is below 1%.*"
- **Honest reading**: a real but small decrement. This is the well-calibrated middle position — awareness is *slightly* degraded, nowhere near abolished.
- **Counter-evidence noted**: Bigica et al. (2021), a *Sleep* supplement abstract, reports metacognitive accuracy declining over 32 h of sustained wakefulness, driven by over-confidence on incorrect trials. Conference abstract only, not weighted heavily here, and the direction of the effect (over-confidence specifically on errors) is worth chasing to full publication.

### Reyner & Horne (1998); Williamson et al. (2014); Anderson et al. (2023) — the driving triple
- **URLs**: doi:10.1007/s004140050131 · doi:10.1016/j.aap.2014.04.007 · doi:10.1093/sleep/zsad136
- **Type**: Papers (*Int J Legal Med* 111(3):120–3; *Accid Anal Prev* 70:225–34; *Sleep* 46(11):zsad136)
- **Key points**: Three independent studies, 28 / 90 / 16 subjects, simulator and instrumented-vehicle, spanning 25 years, all converging against the void.
  - Reyner & Horne: "*It was not possible for our subjects to fall asleep at the wheel and have an 'accident' without experiencing a sustained period of increasing sleepiness, of which they were quite aware.*"
  - Williamson et al.: "*The research shows clearly that drivers can detect changes in their levels of sleepiness sufficiently to make a safe decision to stop driving due to sleepiness.*"
  - Anderson et al.: "*No severe drive events occurred without noticeable symptoms beforehand*"; 90% of severe events were preceded by reports of struggling to keep the eyes open and mind wandering.
- **Bearing**: the popular "you fall asleep without warning" framing is a **folk belief the literature rejects**. Any voids treatment must not repeat it. The residual real phenomenon is the microsleep itself — a *discrete event* the subject may not register — which is a different and much narrower claim than an unmonitored slide into sleep.

### Cable (2003) — hypoxia deflation, from real incidents
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12602449/
- **Type**: Paper (*Aviat Space Environ Med* 74(2):169–172)
- **Key points**: 27 reports, 29 aircrew, Australian Defence Force 1990–2001. Symptoms "*subtle and often involved cognitive impairment or light-headedness.*" Decisive figure: "*The vast majority (75.8%) of these episodes were recognized by the aircrew themselves, reinforcing the importance and benefit of hypoxia training.*"
- **Bearing**: **trained** self-detection of a classically "undetectable" impairment succeeds three times in four, in the field rather than the lab. This is the single most important datum against treating the impairment void as structural: the boundary is *movable by training*, which is the signature of an Unexplored territory, not an Unexplorable one.

### Shaw, Cabre & Gant (2021) — the hypoxia case for the void, with its own hedge
- **URL**: https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2021.665821/full
- **Type**: Review (*Frontiers in Physiology* 12:665821)
- **Key points** (all verified by grep against the raw NLM XML, not a summary):
  - "*Since hypoxia impairs the ability to identify cognitive impairment within oneself (Mitchell et al., 2019), the capacity to recognise hypoxic symptoms is also compromised (Asmaro et al., 2013; Rice et al., 2019).*"
  - "*Moreover, hypoxia can be insidious and include pleasant sensations, such as euphoria, decreased inhibitions, and a strong sense of wellbeing, which will attenuate any perception of urgency.*" — the inversion face.
  - "*in a recent study, more than 20% of participants did not action emergency procedures during hypoxia and 17% actioned emergency procedures without being hypoxic, meaning 37% of participants either misidentified or failed to recognise they were hypoxic (Rice et al., 2019).*"
  - **The review hedges its own claim**, and the hedge must travel with the quote: "*Whilst it is possible to perceive and recognise hypoxic symptoms prior to cognitive impairment (Turner et al., 2015; Pilmanis et al., 2016), this may not occur for all individuals.*"
- **Tension with Cable 2003 is real and unresolved**: 37% failure in a simulator versus 75.8% success in the field. Plausible reconciliations — different populations, trained versus naive, insidious-onset versus mask failure, and a reporting-survivorship effect in the incident database (an unrecognised hypoxia event may never be filed as one). Flagged rather than resolved.

### Holland & Ferner (2017) — the inversion face, quantified
- **URL**: doi:10.1080/15563650.2017.1296576 (accepted manuscript verified at the Birmingham repository)
- **Type**: Systematic review (*Clinical Toxicology* 55(6))
- **Key points**: The Mellanby effect compares responses at equal blood alcohol concentration on the ascending versus descending limb.
  - Subjective: in 19 trials across 12 studies, 229 subjects, "*the mean difference, weighted for study size, in the 9 trials providing numerical data, was 29% [range 24%–74%] less intoxicated subjectively.*"
  - Behavioural: "*willingness to drive increased significantly in 4 of 6 trials. Weighted mean improvement in 52 subjects was 207% [range 79–300%]: that is, they were three times as willing to drive on the descending limb.*"
  - Objective, running the opposite way: driving ability across 200 trials in 57 subjects "*showed worse performance by a weighted mean of 96% [range 3-566%]*"; inhibitory control was "*30% [range 14–65%] worse on the descending limb.*"
- **Quote (conclusion)**: "*It appears then, that these objective tests are likely to be more robust than a person's own perception.*"
- **Why this is the cleanest case in the survey**: the gauge does not merely saturate, it *moves in the wrong direction* while the impairment holds. Same substance, same blood concentration, same subject — only the derivative of the concentration differs, and the felt sense tracks the derivative rather than the state. A signal that tracks the wrong variable is a separately generated signal, demonstrated within-subject.
- **Honest caveat**: the review reports that some minor objective measures *did* improve on the descending limb (maze time 11%, peg-board 71%, arithmetic 10–18%), and that "*Results for several domains were inconsistent between studies.*" The inversion is robust for driving-relevant and inhibitory measures, not universal.

### Cryer (2013) and Baxter et al. (2024) — the deletion face
- **URLs**: doi:10.1056/NEJMra1215228 · doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0297601
- **Type**: Review (*NEJM* 369:362–372) and cross-sectional study (*PLOS One* 19(6):e0297601)
- **Key points**:
  - Hypoglycaemia-associated autonomic failure (HAAF): recent antecedent hypoglycaemia attenuates the sympathoadrenal response, causing hypoglycaemia unawareness, and shifts the glycaemic thresholds for autonomic and symptomatic responses to lower glucose concentrations — a self-reinforcing cycle in which each unnoticed episode lowers the threshold for the next.
  - Baxter et al., N = 189 adults with type 1 diabetes: "*17.5% had IAH and 16.0% reported ≥1 episode of SH in the previous 12 months*"; "*Those with IAH were more likely to report SH (37.5% versus 11.7%, p = 0.001).*"
- **Why this is the structural case**: the other faces are transient states. Here the warning system is **progressively and semi-permanently dismantled by its own triggering condition**, and the resulting blindness measurably raises the rate of the severe events it was there to prevent. Partial reversibility with scrupulous hypoglycaemia avoidance is documented, which keeps it from being a pure erasure.
- **Correction to a secondary source**: search summaries repeatedly attach to Baxter et al. a claim that in IAH "the onset of cognitive dysfunction precedes the onset of autonomic symptoms." That sentence is **not** in Baxter et al. — verified by direct read. It appears to be drawn from other IAH literature. Do not cite it to this paper.

### Hobbs (2014) — the resolution limit
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25329945/ · doi:10.3357/ASEM.4003.2014
- **Type**: Paper (*Aviat Space Environ Med* 85(11):1121–4)
- **Key points**: 20 divers, air versus enriched-air nitrox at shallow and deep. Memory performance significantly worse deep; no difference between gases. Subjective impairment ratings rose deep for both, but were significantly *lower* on EANx30 than air despite identical measured impairment.
- **Quote**: "*divers were able to make a correct global self-assessment they were impaired by narcosis, but were unable to make a finer assessment, leading them to erroneously believe that EANx30 was less narcotic than air.*"
- **Bearing**: the most precise statement of the residue in the whole survey. **The coarse channel is open; the fine-grained channel is closed.** Impairment self-monitoring has a *resolution limit*, not an on/off failure — and the fine-grained errors are systematic (tracking an expectancy about the gas) rather than random.

### Newstead, Lewis, Roderique-Davies, Heirene & John (2022) — the dissociation case
- **URL**: doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2022.913230
- **Type**: Scoping review (*Frontiers in Psychiatry* 13:913230)
- **Key points**: "*The 'frontal lobe paradox' (FLP), otherwise referred to as the 'knowing-doing dissociation,' highlights a phenomenon in which a subset of people who possess frontal lobe damage and exhibit marked impairments in everyday life are still able to perform well in interview and test settings.*" And: "*Individuals are able to verbally describe a logical course of action relating to a task, but then fail to execute it in a real-life scenario.*"
- **Bearing**: a *fourth* structure, distinct from the three faces. Here the propositional self-knowledge is intact and the *deployment* of it fails. It is the impairment-domain twin of Ryle's know-how/know-that gap and belongs with the [[agency-void]] verification face rather than with the gauge faces. The review's title — "the paradox of the frontal lobe paradox" — signals that the phenomenon's own evidential status is contested, so it should be carried as illustrative, not probative.

### Wedin, Vanggaard & Hirvonen (1979); Rothschild & Schneider (1995) — the limit case
- **URLs**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/541627/ · doi:10.1007/BF01245483
- **Type**: Papers (*J Forensic Sci* 24(3):543–553; *Int J Legal Med* 107(5):250–256)
- **Key points**: Paradoxical undressing documented across 33 Swedish hypothermia deaths; Rothschild & Schneider find it in 25% of 69 cases and describe an accompanying "terminal burrowing behaviour". Mechanism: "*paradoxical undressing might be explained by changes in peripheral vasoconstriction in the deeply hypothermic person*" — vasodilatation producing a sensation of warmth.
- **Bearing**: the extreme point of the inversion face — the gauge reports the *exact opposite* of the state, and acting on the reading is fatal. **Caveat that must be carried**: ethanol or other drugs were present in 67% of males and 78% of females in the 1979 series, so this is a confounded population and cannot carry weight alone. Illustrative endpoint, not evidence.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

Not a single void but a **partly deflated cluster**, and the deflation is the headline. The general claim — *consciousness cannot detect its own impairment* — is refuted for the coarse-grained channel in at least four domains (sleep loss, drowsy driving, field hypoxia, gas narcosis). What remains is a narrow structural residue with a specific shape: the felt sense of impairment is a **separately generated signal**, not a readout of the impairment, and it can therefore be decoupled from what it reports along three axes.

| Face | Gauge behaviour | Best evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plateau** | Saturates while deficit grows | Van Dongen 2003 (n=48, 14 days) | Real, but the source leaves the mechanism open between metacognitive failure and genuine phenomenal adaptation |
| **Inversion** | Falls, or points the wrong way, while impairment holds | Holland & Ferner 2017 (229 subjects); hypoxic euphoria; paradoxical undressing | Strongest face; within-subject, same BAC, opposite sign |
| **Deletion** | Trained away by repeated exposure | Cryer 2013; Baxter et al. 2024 (17.5% IAH) | Most structural; partly reversible |
| *(Dissociation)* | Gauge intact, deployment fails | Newstead et al. 2022 | Distinct structure; belongs with [[agency-void]], not here |

Cutting across all three is Hobbs 2014's **resolution limit**: global assessment available, fine discrimination not.

### Evidence for the Limit

The honest tally is mixed, and a voids article must present it that way. **Against** a structural limit: Baranski 1994 (calibration wholly unaffected), Sundelin 2026 (<1% probability of a practically significant decrement), the driving triple, Cable 2003 (75.8% field recognition), Hobbs 2014 (correct global self-assessment). **For** a limit under specific transformations: Van Dongen 2003, Holland & Ferner 2017, Cryer 2013, Rice et al. 2019 (37%).

The discriminator is not how *severe* the impairment is but whether the condition **transforms the gauge**. Sleep loss dulls performance and the gauge tracks it; chronic sleep restriction lets the gauge adapt and it stops tracking. That is why the void opens in some conditions and not others, and it is a considerably more interesting claim than "you can't tell you're impaired."

### Phenomenology

Approaching this edge does not feel like uncertainty. It feels like **fine**. That is the whole difficulty: the plateau face presents as mild sleepiness at the point of worst measured performance, the inversion face as sobering up, hypoxia as euphoria and wellbeing, terminal hypothermia as warmth. The signal is not absent or noisy but *positively reassuring*, and it arrives with the same phenomenal character as an accurate reading — the property [[confabulation-void]] identifies as the phenomenology of confabulation being the phenomenology of accurate report.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

Better supported than the catalogue's usual apophatic default. Cable 2003 shows **training measurably moves the boundary** — three-quarters of real hypoxia incidents were self-recognised by trained aircrew. The general lesson is that where the gauge is dulled rather than transformed, teaching a person the *objective* signature of their own impairment partly substitutes for the missing signal. This is a genuine deflation of the void's scope and should be stated as such.

### Indirect Methods

For transformed-gauge conditions, the only reliable route is **external instrumentation and precommitment**: continuous glucose monitoring for IAH, EEG and ocular measures for drowsiness, breathalysers, oximetry. The structural point is that these work by *not routing through the gauge at all*. Precommitment — deciding while unimpaired what the impaired self will be permitted to do — is the behavioural analogue, and it is the standard clinical and regulatory response in every domain surveyed. Note the recursion: the decision to trust the instrument over the feeling must itself be made by a mind that may already be inside the condition.

### What AI Might See

A genuine asymmetry, and an unusually concrete one. A system whose competence varies with context length, quantisation, or sampling temperature has no interoceptive gauge of its own degradation either — but its state variables are *externally readable* in a way a human's blood alcohol is not, at least to its operator. The interesting question is whether a model's expressed confidence tracks its own degraded state or, like the descending limb, tracks something else entirely. The Mellanby structure supplies a testable prediction: confidence that tracks the *derivative* of a state variable rather than its level. This connects to the existing self-report-reliability line in `topics/cross-architecture-llm-introspection`.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits**, and pointedly turned on the Map itself. The parsimonious story — *the monitor shares substrate with the monitored, so it fails when the substrate fails* — is elegant, generalises beautifully, and is what [[disappearance-voids]] asserts. The measurements do not support it. The actual finding is messier and condition-specific. This is [[epistemological-limits-occams-razor]] operating on the catalogue's own reasoning, and the catalogue got caught.

**Dualism** gains nothing here, and the note should not pretend otherwise. Every mechanism surveyed is straightforwardly physiological: vasodilatation, sympathoadrenal attenuation, receptor adaptation. A physicalist predicts all of it. The one place a dualist reading has any purchase is the Van Dongen disjunction — if the second disjunct holds, the phenomenal state genuinely changed rather than the report failing, which is a claim about what is *present to consciousness* rather than about neural reporting machinery. That is a thin thread and should be presented as one.

### Implications

The catalogue gains a **framework-independent** entry (every framework grants that intoxicated people misjudge their sobriety) whose main value is *disciplinary*: it is a documented instance of the Map asserting a structural limit that measurement contradicts. Under the [[common-cause-null]] this cuts against the convergence reading — a void the Map partly invented is evidence about the Map's habits, not about the territory. [[meta-epistemology-of-limits]] predicted exactly this failure mode; here it is, in a published article, in a domain with abundant data.

## Correction Required

⚠️ **This is the note's primary deliverable and should not be lost in an absorption pass.**

`obsidian/voids/disappearance-voids.md`, section "The Self-Undermining Observer", asserts without citation:

> Self-assessment accuracy declines faster than actual performance under sleep deprivation — people do not know how impaired they are. The monitoring system fails before the execution systems it monitors. By the time the narrowing is significant, the metacognitive capacity to notice it has already been lost.

Three separate problems:

1. **Contradicted.** Baranski et al. 1994 found calibration, resolution and validity of subjective certainty *all unaffected* by sleep deprivation. Sundelin et al. 2026 (N = 182) put the practical significance of any decrement below 1%.
2. **Overstated relative to the only plausible source.** Van Dongen 2003 supports "largely unaware" for *chronic restriction* — not the general claim — and explicitly leaves open whether this is introspective failure or genuine phenomenal adaptation. The article picks one disjunct silently.
3. **"Declines faster than actual performance" is unsupported in either direction.** Van Dongen shows sleepiness ratings *plateauing* while performance declines. That is a dissociation, not a faster decline in self-assessment accuracy, which is a distinct and unmeasured quantity.

The section's closing sentence — "*a specific, measurable instance where the monitoring system fails before the systems it monitors*" — makes measurability the ground of the claim, so the correction is squarely on the article's own terms. The [[self-opacity]] cross-reference in that paragraph inherits the overstatement and should be checked. Suggested repair: keep the fatigue-shedding hierarchy (Killgore 2010 is fine), replace the metacognitive claim with the plateau finding and its disjunction, and add the deflation evidence.

## Gaps in Research

- **Mitchell et al. 2019 does not obviously support the claim attached to it.** Shaw et al. cite "*hypoxia impairs the ability to identify cognitive impairment within oneself*" to Mitchell S. J., Green H. M., Reading S. A. & Gant N. (2019), "The utility and safety of hypoxia experiences for rebreather divers," *Diving Hyperb Med* 49:112–118 (doi:10.28920/dhm49.2.112-118). A diving-safety paper is a surprising home for that claim. Not retrieved; treat the claim as reported-at-second-hand until the primary is read.
- **Rice et al. 2019 read only through Shaw et al.** The 37% figure comes from "Dry-EEG manifestations of acute and insidious hypoxia during simulated flight," *Aerosp Med Hum Perform* 90:92–100 (doi:10.3357/AMHP.5228.2019). Primary not retrieved; n unknown, so the 37% has no denominator here. Do not put a bare "37%" in an article without it.
- **Cable 2003 versus Rice 2019 unreconciled** (75.8% field recognition versus 37% simulator failure). Candidate explanations listed above; none tested.
- **Bigica et al. 2021 is a conference abstract**, and it is the one modern result running *for* the void on sleep-deprivation metacognition. Worth chasing to full publication — if it holds at full length, the sleep-loss picture is more balanced than this note presents it.
- **Holland & Ferner read from the accepted manuscript** at the Birmingham repository, not the published *Clinical Toxicology* version. Page numbers unconfirmed; all figures quoted are from the accepted text.
- **The Van Dongen disjunction may be genuinely undecidable**, which would make the plateau face an instance of the catalogue's structural-opacity pattern rather than merely an open empirical question. Distinguishing "cannot introspect the state" from "the state itself adapted" requires a measure of the phenomenal state independent of the report — precisely what is unavailable. This is the most philosophically interesting thread in the survey and the least resolved.

The tell was semantic: a cerebral-oximeter validation study is an implausible source for a claim about self-assessment. **All reference metadata in this note was verified against raw artefacts** (PubMed E-utilities, publisher XML, or extracted PDF text), never against a model's reading of a page.
