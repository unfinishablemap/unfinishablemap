## Executive Summary

The Paralogisms of Pure Reason (A341–405 / B399–432) are Kant's argument that rational psychology — the Wolffian science that would derive the soul's substantiality, simplicity, personal identity and independence from the body out of the bare "I think" — commits a formal fallacy in each of its four inferences: it reads a *logical* feature of the subject of thought (that "I" is always subject, never predicate; that the thought is one; that the "I" is the same word across time) as a *real* determination of a thing. Kant's own summary of the illusion: "I believe that I cognize what is substantial in myself as a transcendental subject, when I have nothing more in thought than the unity of consciousness, which lies at the basis of all determination of cognition" (B427, Meiklejohn). **[documented textual claim]**

The result is agnostic rather than eliminative. Kant holds that "if materialism is inadequate to explain the mode in which I exist, spiritualism is likewise as insufficient" (B420, Meiklejohn), and that a successful *a priori* proof that thinking beings are simple substances would be "the only stumbling block to our entire critique" (B409, Guyer & Wood). **[documented textual claim]** Whether that agnosticism is the whole story is the main interpretive divide: Ameriks's "modest" reading has Kant retaining deflationary versions of the rationalists' conclusions; Strawson, Sellars and Kitcher read the chapter as a diagnosis of a confusion between the unity of consciousness and a consciousness of unity; Proops and Kitcher disagree, in print, about what the B-edition First Paralogism's fallacy actually is. **[contested historiography]**

For the Map the finding is clean and uncomfortable in equal measure. The Map's subject is *posited* — `tenets.md` L123 calls "the indexical question is meaningful" "itself a posited claim, not a neutral datum" — so it is not an *inference* from the unity of apperception and does not commit the paralogism as a fallacy. But every place the corpus leans on felt unity or felt mine-ness as *evidence* for the determinate persisting subject sits exactly where Kant's diagnosis bites, and the Map's own discipline rule "Phenomenological Datum ≠ Irreducible Metaphysical Datum" is a restatement of Kant's point. The one feature Kant did not consider — a subject index inside a physical selection law (P-SC1's *P(O | C, X)*) — would, if it ever had an empirical signature, turn the subject from a rational-psychology posit into an empirical-psychology determination, which is precisely the route Kant left open (B421: "I exist thinking" is "quite empirical"). Until then it earns no more than *compatible* on the over-reach ladder. **[Map reconstruction]**

### Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, "The Paralogisms of Pure Reason" (A341–405 / B399–432)
- **URL**: Guyer & Wood translation (Cambridge 1998), PDF hosted at `cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/5/25851/files/2017/09/kant-first-critique-cambridge-1m89prv.pdf` (364,441 words extracted); Meiklejohn translation (B edition), Project Gutenberg #4280.
- **Type**: Primary text (public domain; translation copyright for Guyer & Wood).
- **Retrieval status**: **full text, both translations**; every quotation below grep-verified in the extracted text. The A-edition paralogisms are in Guyer & Wood only (Meiklejohn translates B).
- **Key points**:
  - "I think is thus the sole text of rational psychology, from which it is to develop its entire wisdom" (A343/B401, Guyer & Wood). **[documented textual claim]**
  - The four "topics" of rational psychology, in Kant's table (A344/B402): the soul is substance; simple as to quality; "numerically identical, that is UNITY, not Plurality" across the times it exists; "in relation to possible objects in space" (Meiklejohn). From them, "by combination alone", rational psychology derives immateriality, incorruptibility, personality, spirituality, *commercium* with bodies, and immortality. **[documented textual claim]**
  - The empty subject: "Through this I, or He, or It (the thing), which thinks, nothing further is represented than a transcendental subject of thoughts = x, which is recognized only through the thoughts that are its predicates, and about which, in abstraction, we can never have even the least concept; because of which we therefore turn in a constant circle" (A346/B404, Guyer & Wood). **[documented textual claim]**
  - Third paralogism, A edition: "The identity of the consciousness of Myself in different times is therefore only a formal condition of my thoughts and their connection, but it does not prove at all the numerical identity of my subject, in which — despite the logical identity of the I — a change can go on that does not allow it to keep its identity" (A363, Guyer & Wood). The footnote's elastic-ball series: "a whole series of these substances may be thought, of which the first would communicate its state, together with its consciousness, to the second" (A363–364 n.). **[documented textual claim]**
  - B-edition single syllogism (B410–411): "That which cannot be cogitated otherwise than as subject, does not exist otherwise than as subject, and is therefore substance. A thinking being, considered merely as such, cannot be cogitated otherwise than as subject. Therefore it exists also as such, that is, as substance" — "the conclusion is here arrived at by a Sophisma figurae dictionis" (Meiklejohn). The note (B411n, in Proops's translation as reproduced by Kitcher 2024): "in thinking my existence, I can use myself only as the subject of the judgement, which is an identical proposition that discloses absolutely nothing about the manner of my existence." **[documented textual claim]**
  - The agnostic close: "if materialism is inadequate to explain the mode in which I exist, spiritualism is likewise as insufficient; and the conclusion is that we are utterly unable to attain to any knowledge of the constitution of the soul, in so far as relates to the possibility of its existence apart from external objects" (B420, Meiklejohn); the chapter "is nothing more than a discipline, which sets impassable limits to speculative reason in this region of thought, to prevent it, on the one hand, from throwing itself into the arms of a soulless materialism, and, on the other, from losing itself in the mazes of a baseless spiritualism" (B421, Meiklejohn). **[documented textual claim]**
  - The stakes Kant himself names: "It would be a great, or indeed the only stumbling block to our entire critique, if it were possible to prove a priori that all thinking beings are in themselves simple substances" (B409, Guyer & Wood). **[documented textual claim]**
  - B-edition restructuring: the four separate A-edition paralogisms are replaced by one syllogism (B410–411) plus a running commentary; the fourth paralogism's topic (ideality of outer relations) is moved out to the Refutation of Idealism (B274 ff.); Kant adds the Mendelssohn argument against the proof of the soul's permanence (B413–415, the "vanishing by elanguescence" counter-possibility: Meiklejohn's "we can cogitate all the powers and faculties of the soul—even that of consciousness—as diminished by one half, the substance still remaining"). **[documented textual claim]** The SEP entry's judgement that the B version is "much less interesting" for theory of mind is Brook & Wuerth's, not a consensus. **[contested historiography]**
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts with any *derivation* of posit (1) from the unity of consciousness; neutral toward a *posited* subject; the B420 agnosticism is compatible with Tenet 1 and Tenet 5 (Kant's refusal to let parsimony decide between materialism and spiritualism is close to the Map's Tenet 5 use).
- **Quote**: "I cogitate myself in behalf of a possible experience, at the same time making abstraction of all actual experience; and infer therefrom that I can be conscious of myself apart from experience and its empirical conditions" (B426–427, Meiklejohn).

## Major Positions

### The standard deflationary reading (Strawson 1966/1997; Sellars 1970; Kitcher 1982–2024)
- **Core claim**: each paralogism trades a logical feature of the "I think" (always subject; one act; same representation over time) for a real property of a thing. Strawson: confusing "the unity and simplicity of … consciousness with a consciousness of unity and simplicity". Kitcher's variant: the confusion arises because "the I-representation is both empty and necessary for cognition".
- **Relation to site tenets**: the reading says nothing about whether dualism is *true*; it says the "I think" is not evidence for it. It is the direct ancestor of the Map's own "Phenomenological Datum ≠ Irreducible Metaphysical Datum" rule.

### The modest reading (Ameriks 1982/2000)
- **Core claim**: Kant rejects the *proofs* but keeps deflationary versions of the *conclusions* — the thinking I as, in some modest sense, simple, immaterial, persisting — and the B420 agnosticism is real agnosticism, not covert materialism. The SEP's biographical note (Kant "believed that the soul is simple and persists beyond death") is the usual exhibit.
- **Relation to site tenets**: on this reading Kant is a Tenet 5 ally: he refuses to let the failure of a proof count as a disproof, and refuses parsimony as a tiebreaker between "soulless materialism" and "baseless spiritualism" (B421).

### The historical-target reading (Dyck 2010/2014; Proops 2021)
- **Core claim**: Kant's target is Wolff's *impure* rational psychology, which starts from empirical self-observation and tries to ascend to the soul's nature; the "Achilles" argument for simplicity (A351–352) and the "Aeneas" argument for post-mortem personality are the specific Wolffian proofs. Proops: the chapter aims "less to refute the claims of Rational Psychology than to explain why they seem so seductive".
- **Relation to site tenets**: supplies the bridge to the influx page (same milieu, same Wolffian psychology), and implies that a dualism which does *not* claim to derive its subject from the "I think" is simply not Kant's target.

### The dualist rejoinder (Priest 2007; cf. Hasker, Lowe)
- **Core claim**: Priest — the second-paralogism critique is "unsound"; the apperception doctrine "entails propositions about the ontology of the self"; only the fourth-paralogism critique is "conclusive". Hasker runs a Leibniz–Kant unity-of-consciousness argument *for* dualism (i.e., he takes the A352 "Achilles" premise seriously rather than Kant's critique of it). Lowe acknowledges the substitution worry and answers it with simplicity.
- **Relation to site tenets**: these are the moves available to the Map if it ever wants to *argue* for posit (1) rather than posit it; each is contested, and none is currently made on any Map page.

### The neo-Kantian naturalisers (Longuenesse 2017; Zahavi 2005; Cassam 2021)
- **Core claim**: keep reference for 'I', deflate the referent — to a "system" (Longuenesse), to the pre-reflective "for-me-ness" of experience (Zahavi), or to a bodily subject intuitively aware of itself as a physical object (Cassam).
- **Relation to site tenets**: all three are "deflationary, fragmented, or process-only" accounts in the sense of P-I1's shift clause; all three would weaken Tenet 4's indexical objection "correspondingly" (tenets L123). Zahavi's minimal self is the closest to the Map's `mine-ness` and is the one the Map should not mistake for an ally: it is explicitly *not* a persisting substance.

## Key Debates

### What is the fallacy in the First Paralogism?
- **Sides**: Proops (grammatical non-predicability of 'I' entails nothing about the self) vs Kitcher (the "I" is empty-yet-necessary; the illusion comes from treating the condition of cognition as its object) vs Rosefeldt (non-predicability as a logico-semantic feature misread as real) vs Grier (transcendental illusion plus a formal fallacy).
- **Current state**: open; author-meets-critics in *Kantian Review* 2024. For the Map the disagreement does not matter: on every reading, "I" as subject-of-judgement licenses nothing about substance.

### Does the Paralogisms chapter refute, or merely disarm, rational psychology?
- **Sides**: Strawson/Kitcher (disarm, and show the illusion is structural) vs Ameriks (disarm while retaining modest conclusions) vs Priest (fails even to disarm, except for the fourth).
- **Current state**: the modest reading is widely cited but not consensus; Priest is a minority position.

### Is the identity of consciousness over time found or made?
- **Sides**: A363 ("only a formal condition … does not prove at all the numerical identity of my subject") and B133 (identity of consciousness represented "only because I can combine a manifold") vs Priest ("entails synthetic propositions") and the substance dualists who answer the elastic-ball series with simplicity (Lowe) or with a primitive subject (Swinburne, on the usual reading).
- **Current state**: this is the live seam for the Map, because posit (1)'s "diachronic persistence" is exactly what A363 says the "I" cannot deliver.

## The Analytic Question: does the Map's subject fall to the paralogisms?

**Short answer [Map reconstruction]**: it does not *fall*, because it is not *inferred*; but it is *exposed* at three points, and the one feature Kant did not consider rescues it only on promissory standing.

**1. The Map posits; Kant attacks derivations.** Every paralogism is a syllogism from the "I think" to a property of the soul. The Map's register does not run that syllogism: `tenets.md` L121 says the non-deflationary "I" is endorsed "on independent grounds (the agency cluster's substance-leaning sub-reading needs a persisting subject for agent-causation)", and L123 that "'the indexical question is meaningful' is itself a posited claim, not a neutral datum". P-I2 says the same in register form. A posit is not a paralogism. On the historical-target reading (Dyck, Proops) the Map is simply not Kant's target. **[framework-conditional implication]**

**2. Exposure one — felt mine-ness as evidence (first and second paralogisms).** Wherever the corpus lets the *felt* unity, this-ness or ownership of experience count as evidence for a determinate subject, Strawson's diagnosis applies: that is confusing "the unity and simplicity of … consciousness with a consciousness of unity and simplicity". P-I1's calibration already concedes the point — "external-evidence grade D (felt this-ness is compatible with both the primitive-thisness and the perspectival-illusion readings, so no independent evidence discriminates)" — and the discipline's "Phenomenological Datum ≠ Irreducible Metaphysical Datum" rule is a restatement of A346/B404. **Over-reach ladder**: any "Relation to Site Perspective" paragraph that reads felt unity or mine-ness as *supporting* Tenet 1 or posit (1) earns **compatible** only; the perspectival-illusion rival (and Longuenesse's "system", Zahavi's "for-me-ness") predicts the same datum. Likewise the Hasker-style unity-of-consciousness route to a *simple* subject (`unity-of-consciousness`, `apex/mereology-of-mind`) is the A352 "Achilles" inference Kant's second paralogism targets; it stays at compatible unless an independent discriminator is named.

**3. Exposure two — diachronic persistence (third paralogism).** A363 is aimed at the second clause of posit (1): "The identity of the consciousness of Myself in different times is … only a formal condition of my thoughts … but it does not prove at all the numerical identity of my subject." The Map's candidate answer (`personal-identity` L187: "the selector has a unique causal history a replica could not inherit") does not meet the elastic-ball series as stated, because in Kant's series each substance "would communicate its state, together with its consciousness, to the second" — the history is handed on. The Map's reply must therefore be that *selection-history is non-transferable* — which is the `haecceity` posit again, not an argument. **Cost of a Kantian rejoinder**: P-I1 and posit (1) cannot be presented anywhere as *following from* the unity or continuity of consciousness; they are primitive posits, and the register says so. What the rejoinder does *not* cost is the posit itself — B420 is agnostic, and on Ameriks's reading Kant kept a modest version of exactly this conclusion.

**4. Exposure three — the Tenet 4 indexical argument.** `tenets.md` L123 already derives the consequence: "Were the subject deflated, fragmented, or treated as a mere process, the indexical objection would weaken correspondingly". The paralogisms are the canonical argument that nothing in the "I think" *prevents* that deflation. So the Kantian rejoinder costs Tenet 4 its appearance of independence from subjecthood — which P-I2 ("stated unconditionally but argued conditionally") has registered since 2026-08-03. The research adds the name and the text, not a new concession. **[framework-conditional implication]**

**5. What Kant did not consider, and whether it escapes.** Kant's "I exist thinking" is "quite empirical" (B421–422, Meiklejohn: "the proposition is quite empirical, and contains the determinability of my existence merely in relation to my representations in time"); his complaint is that rational psychology wants *more* than empirical psychology can give. P-SC1 proposes something Kant never faced: a subject index *inside a physical law* — *P(O | C, X)* with a census of which subject is paired to which system. If that index ever had a measurable signature, the subject would be determined through its *effects*, i.e. empirically, not through the "I think" — and empirical psychology is the route Kant explicitly leaves open. That is the genuine escape from the dialectic. But Tenet 3 holds downward causation "available, not actual" (P-SC1 L48), P-SC1 carries "external-evidence grade D" and "empirical discriminability … in-principle", and the Map's own measurement page concedes selection within Born probabilities is aggregate-indistinguishable from random collapse. **Ladder**: *compatible* now; *suggestive* only if a conditioned-register signature were reported; *discriminating* only if the signature were subject-indexed (two subjects, two signatures). Until then the escape is a promissory note, and the honest framing is: the Map's subject is a posit that *could become* an empirical determination, which is a status Kant's chapter does not rule out and does not grant. **[Map reconstruction]**

**6. Fourth paralogism.** Not at stake: the Map is a realist about the physical interface, and Kant moved the issue to the Refutation of Idealism in B. Priest, who defends the rationalist on the first three, concedes this one to Kant.

**Transition check (per the 2026-09-30 method-and-history rule)**: "Kant refuted rational psychology ⇒ the Map's subject is refuted" is invalid without the premise that the Map *derives* its subject the rationalist way (it does not); "Kant left the soul agnostic ⇒ the Map may posit it" is valid only with Tenet 5 supplied and named. Neither direction is free.
