### Death (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy) — Steven Luper **[raw]**
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/death/ · copyright © **2026** by Steven Luper (currently maintained)
- **Type**: Encyclopedia. Retrieved raw, 109 KB normalised, grep-verified.
- **Key points**:
  - Names the **harm thesis**, the **Epicurean case**, and the **deprivationist defense**.
  - Canonical comparativism, verbatim: "we are harmed by what makes our lives **as wholes** worse than they otherwise would be, and benefitted by what makes our lives as wholes better than they otherwise would be (early proponents of this view include Nagel 1970, Quinn 1984, and Feldman 1991)."
  - Names the **Epicurean presumption**: "what never makes us worse (better) off than we otherwise would be is not bad (good) for us", and the **timing question**: "if death is bad for us, at what time (or times) does it make us worse off than we otherwise would have been?"
  - Five answers, each retrieved verbatim: **concurrentism** ("a bad death makes us worse off while we die"), **priorism** (Pitcher 1984 — "death may make you worse off before you die"), **subsequentism** (Grey's preference), **indefinitism** (Nagel 1970, Silverstein 1980 — harm "at no determinate time", criticised by Lamont 1998, defended by Grey 1999), **atemporalism** (reject the Epicurean presumption: "there need not be a time at which death makes us worse off").
- **Tenet alignment**: **Neutral / structurally useful.** The taxonomy is framework-independent. It gives the Map a name for the move L113 already makes and a menu for the move it has not made.
- **Why it matters most**: comparativism is stated over **lives as wholes**. That is exactly the commitment a momentary locus cannot discharge, so the Map's gap is not incidental to the standard account — it is the standard account's central quantifier.

## Major Positions

### Comparativism / the deprivation account (Nagel 1970, Feldman 1991, Quinn 1984; Luper's SEP statement)
- **Core claim**: harm is being made worse off than one otherwise would have been, assessed over **lives as wholes**.
- **Relation to site tenets**: This is the analysis `concepts/no-self-objection-to-phenomenal-value` L113 already states as the third rival's premise — correctly, and without the name. **The whole-life quantifier is where the Map's momentary locus fails to fit**, and that is not a detail: it is the account's core. The Map's existing move is to concede the diachronic layer to the persisting subject the agency cluster asserts and keep only the base layer. That move is coherent; it has never been argued.

### Atomism about lifetime welfare (Hersch & Weltman 2022; Dorsey 2015 partly)
- **Core claim**: the prudential value of a life depends **solely** on the prudential value of each of its moments.
- **Relation to site tenets**: **The Map's best route to supplying the missing step**, and it is available without any physicalist commitment. It would let the Map build diachronic welfare *out of* momentary loci rather than borrowing a persisting bearer — which would also make the value chain's cascade isolation (deliberately not sharing the No Many Worlds root) stronger, not weaker. Costs to state: the grain is unaddressed in the source, one narrative bullet is bitten, and it is a minority position.

### Holism about lifetime welfare (Velleman 1991; Raibley 2012; Fletcher 2025; Kauppinen)
- **Core claim**: there is more to how well a life goes than the aggregate of its moments; narrative and order matter intrinsically.
- **Relation to site tenets**: **The live opposition to the Map's diachronic construction — and it concedes the base layer.** Velleman's momentary internalism is the concession. If the Map takes the holist route instead, it needs the persisting subject as a genuine welfare bearer, which is what the agency cluster already asserts — so holism is *also* available to the Map, at the cost of making the value chain depend on the persistence claims it deliberately isolated itself from.

### Gradualism and time-relative interests (McMahan 2002; Bradley's gradualism; Parfit 1984 as inspiration)
- **Core claim**: the badness of a death is discounted by the weakness of the psychological relations between the victim now and the future self who would have had the goods.
- **Relation to site tenets**: **Conflicts with P-I1/P-SC3** (a persisting, non-deflationary subject), and the conflict is instructive. TRIA is what you get if you take prudential concern to track degrees of connection rather than identity. A momentary-locus axiology with no persisting bearer predicts something like maximal discounting — which is *further* than McMahan goes. So the Map cannot adopt TRIA, but it should say why its own momentary base layer does not collapse into it.

### Temporal neutrality vs. time-bias (Sullivan 2018; Parfit 1984)
- **Core claim** (Sullivan): prudential rationality requires indifference to *when* a good falls; near-bias and future-bias are both irrational.
- **Relation to site tenets**: **Unexplored and cleanly relevant.** `time-bias` is 0 in the corpus. If momentary loci are the bearers, the question of why a locus should care about a *later* locus's goods at all is the time-bias question in its most acute form. Parfit's reductionism is the classic route to saying it need not.

### The no-subject / bearership objection (Śāntideva via the sibling note; Parfitian impersonalism)
- **Core claim**: welfare can be constituted by impersonal states, so "who is harmed" is not a well-formed further question.
- **Relation to site tenets**: This is the sibling note's territory synchronically, and it becomes mine diachronically. The Map's distinctive risk is being caught between the two: too much bearership and Śāntideva's impartiality argument breaks (sibling's finding); too little and there is nobody for the deprivation to be a deprivation *of* (this note's finding). **That squeeze is the eventual article's real subject.**

## Key Debates

### Does welfare exist at a time?
- **Sides**: Bramble 2018 (no — only lifetimes) vs. Bradley 2021, Hersch & Weltman 2022, and — notably — Velleman 1991 (yes, defined from the perspective of that moment).
- **Core disagreement**: whether "well-being at *t*" names anything.
- **Current state**: **Ongoing, with the Map's base layer on the majority side.** This is the debate that most directly bears on the Map and it is entirely absent from the corpus.

### Does momentary welfare aggregate into lifetime welfare?
- **Sides**: atomists (Hersch & Weltman, Dorsey partly) vs. holists (Velleman, Raibley, Kauppinen, Fletcher 2025).
- **Core disagreement**: whether narrative/order contribute value over and above the moments.
- **Current state**: **Ongoing; holism is the majority per Dorsey's own report.** The Map needs one side or the other for its diachronic layer and currently has neither.

### The timing puzzle
- **Sides**: concurrentism / priorism / subsequentism / indefinitism / atemporalism (Luper's taxonomy).
- **Core disagreement**: when a deprivation harm is incurred — and whether it must be incurred at a time at all.
- **Current state**: **Unresolved in the literature.** For the Map this is the question with teeth: L113's "needs no second time" is an answer to it, unnamed and unargued.

### Whether reductionism about persons survives punishment and compensation
- **Sides**: the standard objection (only a persisting person can be punished or compensated) vs. Parfit's reply that psychological continuity carries enough.
- **Current state**: Ongoing. Reported from search results only; **not** verified against Parfit's text. Flagged as a gap below.
