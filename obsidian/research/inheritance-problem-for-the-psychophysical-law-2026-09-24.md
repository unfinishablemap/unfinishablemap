## Assessment (read first)

**Verdict: the gap is real and worth an article, and the dilemma mostly comes apart once its parts are separated.** The Map has never said *what* evolution passes on if conscious selection affects fitness. [[evolution-under-dualism]] offers three models (fixed domain, co-evolution, unfolding) but asks what *consciousness* is like in each. It never asks what carries a change from one generation to the next. [[interface-specification-programme]] lists "developmental onset" and "evolutionary interface signatures" as separate open gaps and does not connect them. No Map article discusses the **psychophysical harmony** literature (Cutter & Crummett 2025; Saad 2022; Mørch 2017), which is the modern academic home of this problem. That absence is the main finding.

The reviewer's four horns are not equally serious once three inheritable things are kept apart. They are **R** (the physical receiver/controller), **E** (the physical conditions that make a system eligible to host a subject) and **K** (the psychophysical "codebook", meaning the law's mapping itself):

- Horn (c), *fixed law*, is the standard position (Chalmers 1996; Hasker 1999; the Map's "fixed domain" model). It becomes stable when paired with horn (a), *evolving receiver*. Its difficulty, that one fixed law must serve species-specific and semantically rich intentions, goes away if the law has no semantic content: the meaning sits in the menu the receiver prepares, and the law only governs selection among whatever is presented (see [the thin-law proposal](#the-thin-law-proposal) below).
- Horn (a)'s "underdetermination" worry is really the familiar causal-closure/overdetermination worry again. The dilemma's evolutionary framing adds one new constraint, and it is the most important point in this note: **evolution can only see the selector's contribution through the fitness of outcomes that depend on context.** The Map's long-run Born-preservation commitment limits where that contribution can appear. See [the selectability constraint](#the-selectability-constraint).
- Horn (b), *evolving law*, is what the Map's own **co-evolution model** implicitly relies on, without an inheritance channel. Subjects do not reproduce, so a change in the law carried across generations needs either a law that remembers (Peircean habit-taking; Smolin's evolving laws) or it falls back into (a) or (d). This is a real defect in [[evolution-under-dualism]]. It can be fixed with a paragraph there; it does not need its own article.
- Horn (d), the *learning regress*, stops if learning happens on the receiver side against a fixed, thin law. Developmental and BCI evidence supports that pattern (Blumberg's twitch-driven body mapping; Sadtler et al. 2014). What remains after that is the **onset/eligibility** problem ([[pairing-problem]]), which is a different problem and not a regress.

**Recommended article**: a `topics/` piece, working title "The Inheritance Problem for the Psychophysical Law". Topics has room: `count_section_files('topics')` = 329 against a cap of 360, measured 2026-09-24. The piece would make the R/E/K split explicit, adopt a thin fixed law with an evolving receiver and eligibility as the Map's default, state the selectability constraint, and engage the harmony literature. It should state plainly the costs that remain; see [what stays unpaid](#what-stays-unpaid).

## Key Sources

### Cutter & Crummett — "Psychophysical Harmony: A New Argument for Theism" (2025)
- **URL**: https://philpapers.org/rec/CUTPHA · DOI 10.1093/9780198954712.003.0002
- **Type**: Paper, *Oxford Studies in Philosophy of Religion* (2025), pp. 33–71. Circulated as a preprint from ~2021; Saad (2022) cites it as "Crummett & Cutter (2021)".
- **Key points**:
  - "Psychophysical harmony" names the fact that phenomenal states are correlated with physical states, and with one another, in strikingly fortunate ways. Experiences go with the behaviour they rationalise, and with reports they make true.
  - The argument is modelled on cosmological fine-tuning, but applied to the psychophysical laws instead of the physical constants.
  - On evolution, per a secondary interview summary (not checked against the primary text): selection is a fine *proximate* explanation, but any evolutionary story already assumes the experience–behaviour rationality it would be explaining. Goff's line as quoted there: "Any evolutionary story about why we have the conscious experiences we do presupposes experiential rationality, and if it presupposes it, it can't explain it."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral to aligned. It presses Tenet 1 (dualism) by making the laws substantive. The theistic conclusion is not a Map commitment.
- **Relevance**: This is the literature's direct statement of horn (c)'s cost. A fixed law with the right shape is itself a fact that needs explaining, and evolution cannot supply that explanation.

### Saad — "Harmony in a panpsychist world" (2022)
- **URL**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9684220/ · DOI 10.1007/s11229-022-03974-7
- **Type**: Paper, *Synthese* 200 (2022), open access.
- **Key points**:
  - Rejects selection as an explanation of harmony. Quote verified verbatim in the PMC HTML: "while a selectional explanation can say why—given their causal profiles—experiential properties are distributed in a certain way as a result of selection, such an explanation is of the wrong sort to explain why experiential properties have their causal profiles in the first place."
  - His panpsychist reply is *not* a selection mechanism. It dilutes harmony: if most experiences in nature are not harmonious, the harmonious biological ones cry out less for explanation. The analogy is many monkeys at typewriters.
  - Notes that "biological theorists" cannot take up this reply cheaply.
- **Tenet alignment**: The diagnosis is neutral. The panpsychist remedy sits in tension with the Map's subject-census commitments.
- **Relevance**: **This quote is the cleanest statement of the R/K split in the literature.** Selection acts on R (how physical types are distributed) *given* K (the causal profiles). It never acts on K. Horns (a) and (c) are therefore the default, and horn (b) is the anomaly.

### Mørch — "The Evolutionary Argument for Phenomenal Powers" (2017)
- **URL**: https://philpapers.org/rec/MRCTEA · DOI 10.1111/phpe.12096
- **Type**: Paper, *Philosophical Perspectives* 31(1): 293–316.
- **Key points** (from the abstract and search summaries; the PhilArchive PDF returned 403, so the primary text was not read):
  - Revives James's argument: why does pain go with harm (suffocation, burning) and pleasure with benefit (eating, breathing)? Epiphenomenalism cannot let selection favour these pairings.
  - Recasts it as an argument for the **phenomenal powers view**: phenomenal properties produce and necessitate their effects *in virtue of how they feel*.
  - Argues that this view explains the correlations, given selection, much better than rivals that treat the psychophysical laws as contingent.
- **Tenet alignment**: Strongly aligned with Tenet 3 (bidirectional interaction). It supplies a *non-arbitrary* fixed law.
- **Relevance**: This is the best resource for escaping horn (c)'s harmony cost. If pain's tendency to be avoided is intrinsic to how pain feels, no codebook entry "pain → avoid" exists for anything to inherit or fine-tune. Evolution only has to route harmful states into pain-producing receiver configurations, which is ordinary R-evolution. The Map already touches phenomenal powers in [[causal-powers]] and [[consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions]], but never links it to the evolution question.

### James — "Are We Automata?" (1879) and *Principles* ch. 5 (1890)
- **Type**: Paper (*Mind* os-4(13): 1–22, DOI 10.1093/mind/os-4.13.1) and book.
- **Key points**: The original evolutionary argument against epiphenomenalism. Pleasure goes with the beneficial and pain with the harmful, and that pairing is inexplicable unless feeling makes a difference.
- **Relevance**: This is the historical origin. James assumes a fixed law, evolving organisms, and feeling that is causally efficacious. That is horns (a) and (c) together, with the argument relying on efficacy. Already represented on the Map via [[evolutionary-case-for-mental-causation]].

### Popper — "Natural Selection and the Emergence of Mind" (1978); Popper & Eccles, *The Self and Its Brain* (1977)
- **URL**: DOI 10.1111/j.1746-8361.1978.tb01321.x · *Dialectica* 32(3–4): 339–355.
- **Key points**: Popper holds that mind emerged by natural selection *because* it is causally efficacious. His emergentism leaves open whether "World 2" itself changes over evolutionary time.
- **Relevance**: Popper is the canonical interactionist who takes horn (b) seriously, with mind as an evolved product. He never gives an inheritance channel for it. The reviewer's challenge applies to Popper directly.

### Eccles — *Evolution of the Brain: Creation of the Self* (1989)
- **Type**: Book (Routledge).
- **Key points**: Eccles says openly that each self is "a new Divine creation" implanted in the foetus, and he pairs psychons with ~40 million cortical dendrons. (Source: secondary summaries, Pratt; New World Encyclopedia. Treat the wording as a lead and not a quotation.)
- **Relevance**: Eccles answers the onset question by supernatural fiat and inherits only R, the dendron architecture. This shows that the best-known quantum-interactionist ancestor of the Map left the inheritance and onset problems to theology. The Map cannot borrow that answer, so it has to say something itself.

### Swinburne — *The Evolution of the Soul* (1986; rev. 1997)
- **Type**: Book (Oxford: Clarendon).
- **Key points**: Souls come into being with evolved brains and need brains to *function*. Swinburne argues that no complete scientific explanation of soul–brain connection exists or will exist, because the laws linking brain states to souls are not open to scientific explanation. He ends in theism.
- **Relevance**: A fixed law (c) and an evolving receiver (a), with the explanation of the law itself placed outside science. This is the same structure as Cutter & Crummett's.

### Hasker — *The Emergent Self* (1999)
- **Type**: Book (Cornell University Press).
- **Key points**: Emergent dualism. An individual mind emerges from a sufficiently organised brain, by laws built into matter's nature, and is then a causally efficacious non-physical substance.
- **Relevance**: The clearest non-theistic interactionist story for **E-evolution**. Evolution builds brains that satisfy a fixed emergence condition, and each generation's subjects arise afresh, so no mental inheritance is needed. Hasker offers the Map a template for eligibility without dual inheritance. Its cost is a fixed emergence law with the harmony problem attached.

### Chalmers — *The Conscious Mind* (1996)
- **Key points**: Psychophysical laws are fundamental and fixed. Organisational invariance ties experience to fine-grained functional organisation, so species-specific *richness* sits entirely on the physical input side while the law stays simple. Chalmers expects the basic laws to be simple (p. 276, already quoted in [[the-psychophysical-control-law]]).
- **Relevance**: Chalmers's answer to horn (c)'s "species-specific, semantically rich" objection is to put the specificity in R and keep K simple and universal. The Map can use the same move for the *outbound* (selection) law as well as the inbound one. Chalmers's own laws are inbound only.

### Sadtler et al. — "Neural constraints on learning" (2014)
- **URL**: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13665 · *Nature* 512: 423–426.
- **Key points**: Macaques controlling a BCI cursor quickly learned *arbitrary* decoder mappings that lay inside the neural population's intrinsic manifold. They learned mappings outside it poorly on a timescale of hours.
- **Relevance**: This is an existence proof for learning against a **fixed, arbitrary decoder**. The decoder (analogous to K) never changes. The population (analogous to R) adapts, limited by its existing structure. It is the empirical model for how horn (d)'s "learning the mapping" can happen entirely on the receiver side. *Caution*: it is an analogy about physical-to-physical decoding. It says nothing about a non-physical selector.

### Blumberg — sleep twitches and sensorimotor self-organisation (2015 and later)
- **URL**: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0963721414551362 ("Developing Sensorimotor Systems in Our Sleep", *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, 2015)
- **Key points**: Myoclonic twitches during infant active sleep produce reafference that builds somatotopic maps and joins sensory and motor systems. This comes before and underlies later skilled, embodied control.
- **Relevance**: The developmental answer to the onset regress. The brain's body map and inverse models are bootstrapped from *self-generated movement that is not selected or directed*. Directed control, which is where conscious selection would act, can begin only after a working menu exists. No working mind→brain mapping is needed to get started.

### Lagercrantz & Changeux — "The Emergence of Human Consciousness: From Fetal to Neonatal Life" (2009)
- **URL**: DOI 10.1203/pdr.0b013e3181973b0d · *Pediatric Research* 65: 255–260.
- **Key points**: They place a minimal fetal consciousness after thalamocortical connectivity forms (~24 weeks), with a marked step up at birth.
- **Relevance**: Onset is gradual and tied to R/E milestones. That supports treating onset as an E-question (when eligibility conditions are met), not a K-question.

### Baldwin — "A New Factor in Evolution" (1896)
- ***American Naturalist*** 30: 441–457, 536–554.
- **Relevance**: The standard route by which *learned* behaviour steers *genetic* evolution without Lamarckian inheritance. If subjects learn to exploit a receiver (horn d), the Baldwin effect lets that learning shape R-evolution across generations. This gives horn (d) an evolutionary consequence without requiring K to be inherited.

## Major Positions

### Fixed law, evolving receiver (horns a + c)
- **Proponents**: James (implicitly), Chalmers, Swinburne, Hasker, the Map's "fixed domain" model.
- **Core claim**: The psychophysical law is part of nature and never changes. Evolution acts on physical architecture that the law then applies to.
- **Relation to tenets**: Fits Tenets 1–3. Tenet 2 (minimal interaction) is best served by a *thin* law (see below). The main cost is the harmony problem.

### Evolving law (horn b)
- **Proponents**: Popper (loosely), the Map's co-evolution and unfolding models, Peircean "habit-taking" laws, Smolin-style evolving laws (not researched here; leads only).
- **Core claim**: The mapping itself changes over deep time.
- **Relation to tenets**: No tenet rules it out, but it needs an inheritance channel. Non-physical subjects do not reproduce, so the change must be stored in a *domain-level* law with memory. That is a large addition that Tenet 5 cannot excuse, because it is a cost of specification and not of simplicity. **Recommendation**: the Map should mark the co-evolution model as needing this and treat it as a less favoured option.

### Phenomenal powers (a refinement of a + c)
- **Proponents**: Mørch.
- **Core claim**: The effects of experience follow from its character, so the "law" does not stand apart from its relata.
- **Relation to tenets**: The best fit for Tenet 3. It dissolves the codebook: nothing contingent is there to inherit or fine-tune.

### Emergent eligibility (E-evolution)
- **Proponents**: Hasker; the Map's [[subject-census]] position (in its eligibility-rule component).
- **Core claim**: Evolution builds systems that meet a fixed eligibility condition, and subjects arise anew in each system.
- **Relation to tenets**: Consistent with Tenet 4, since indexical identity is fixed per subject. It inherits the pairing problem.

### Divine implantation / supra-scientific law
- **Proponents**: Eccles, Swinburne, Cutter & Crummett (as an explanation of harmony).
- **Relation to tenets**: The Map takes no theological position. It should say that its best-known predecessors paid this debt theologically, and that the Map does not.

## The Thin-Law Proposal

This is the synthesis the article should develop. The phrase "psychophysical codebook" runs together three things:

1. **Inbound presentation law**: which physical states present which phenomenal options. Chalmers-style. Fixed and structure-preserving (structural coherence).
2. **Outbound selection law**: how the subject's selection biases which presented option is actualised. Fixed and *content-neutral*: it only governs selection among whatever is presented, in line with [[selection-only-channel]].
3. **Menu formation**: which candidate actions the brain prepares and how they are grouped into selectable alternatives. **Entirely physical, evolved and learned.**

Semantic richness and species-specificity belong to (3), and (3) is R. Laws (1) and (2) can be universal and simple. The "semantic mapping problem" in [[interface-specification-programme]] is then *moved*, not solved. The receiver has to prepare menus whose options differ in how they feel, and the selector picks by how they feel (the phenomenal-powers reading). With that, horns (c) and (d) both lose their force: nothing species-specific is in the law, and nothing in the law has to be learned.

## The Selectability Constraint

Here the reviewer's dilemma produces a real, new constraint. Selection can only favour receivers whose fitness depends on what the selector contributes. The Map's [[selection-only-channel]] and Born-preservation commitments hold that the selector leaves the **unconditioned long-run marginal** of outcome statistics unchanged. So:

- If the selector's bias were invisible in *every* fitness-relevant statistic, evolution could not see it, and horn (a) would win: receiver evolution would explain everything.
- For the evolutionary case for mental causation to hold, the bias must show up in **context-conditional** statistics. Choices go differently *given the situation and the valence of the options*, while the marginal over all contexts stays Born. Fitness is computed on outcomes that depend on context, so this is coherent.
- **Tension to check**: recent Map revisions (e.g. the single-system scope P-Q7 added to [[phenomenology-mechanism-bridge]]) say that any selection scheme respecting no-signalling is per-trial indistinguishable from unbiased Born statistics *for a single system*. The article must check that "per-trial indistinguishable" still allows context-conditional correlation between selections and fitness-relevant circumstances. If it does not, the evolutionary argument and the Born-preservation commitment conflict, and horn (a) wins by default. **This is the most important thing for the article to settle, and it should be flagged to the positions register ([[quantum-interface]]) whichever way it comes out.**

## Key Debates

### Can selection explain harmony?
- **Sides**: "Yes, proximately" (the standard biological view; the co-optimisation reply, see [[co-optimization-reply-to-the-correlation-problem]]) against "No, selection presupposes the causal profiles" (Saad; Cutter & Crummett; Goff).
- **Current state**: Ongoing. The two sides are largely talking past each other: selection explains R given K, and the critics ask about K. The Map should accept the critics' point and answer it with phenomenal powers, not with selection.

### Is onset a regress?
- **Sides**: The reviewer's horn (d) against developmental bootstrapping from undirected activity.
- **Current state**: The regress assumes learning needs a *directed* working interface. The developmental evidence says the menu is built first without direction (Blumberg), and a fixed decoder can be learned against (Sadtler). What remains is the question of *when a subject is paired*, which is the pairing/eligibility problem.

## What Stays Unpaid

- **The harmony debt on laws (1) and (2)**. Phenomenal powers dissolves the arbitrariness of the *outbound* law. The *inbound* presentation law, which says why these physical states present these experiences, keeps Chalmers's contingency. The Map should admit this and not claim phenomenal powers covers both directions.
- **Eligibility law unspecified** (see [[subject-census]]). E-evolution only makes sense relative to a condition that has not been written down.
- **The selectability constraint** could go against the Map (see above).
