## Executive Summary

The harvest framed red–green exclusion and the absence of a reddish green as "necessities of quality space". The primary sources do not support that framing without qualification. **Hering**, who coined *Gegenfarben* (opponent colours), held that the exclusion cannot be seen from the qualities themselves. In his words, "Warum sich dies so verhält, ist von vornherein nicht einzusehen, aber es ist so" ("Why this is so cannot be seen from the outset, but it is so"). He judged that, considered as sensations, red is no more opposed to green than to yellow, and that "logisch läßt sich die Thatsache nicht begründen" ("the fact cannot be grounded logically"). The exclusion "must somehow be grounded in the nature of our visual organ", and he thought a red–green sensation might one day be produced (Hering 1878, §38, pp. 109–110). **Wittgenstein** held the opposite throughout, in three different forms. The *Tractatus* calls two colours at one place "logically impossible, for it is excluded by the logical structure of colour" (6.3751). In 1929 the exclusion became irreducible but still not empirical: atomic propositions "may exclude one another". *Remarks on Colour* seeks "die Logik der Farbbegriffe", explicitly not a physiological or psychological theory (BF I §22). The experiments are split. Crane and Piantanida (1983) and Billock, Gleason and Tsou (2001) report reddish greens under retinal stabilisation, the latter in 4 of 7 expert observers as a homogeneous mixture. Hsieh and Tse (2006) report only "intermediate colors", and Ritter (2017) redescribes the reports in ordinary colour concepts. For the Map the question is genuinely open, and it bears on the colour page's split between tractable structure and intractable quality. ## The Research Question, Answered {#question}

*Do the necessities of colour space hold in virtue of the quality or of the structure?* The literature offers **three** candidate grounds, not two:

1. **The visual system (Hering; Billock et al.; Churchland).** On this view the exclusion is a contingent fact about how vision is organised, and the opponent pairs mark only "die Art ihres Vorkommens … ohne jede Beziehung auf irgendwelche Erklärung", the manner of their occurrence, with no reference to any explanation (Hering 1920, §13, p. 49). A sufficiently altered visual system could see a reddish green. On this reading the Map's split *holds and extends*: even colour's apparent necessities are tractable structure, and the residue is what each colour, including a novel one, is like.
2. **The qualities themselves (the Map's reconstruction, closest to Tractatus 4.123 and the 1929 paper).** Colour relations are *internal*, holding *eo ipso*: "It is unthinkable that *these* two objects should not stand in this relation" (4.123); "the relation of difference of degree is an internal relation" (1929, p. 168). If red and green exclude each other because of what they are, part of colour structure is fixed by quality and known through it. "Structure is tractable" then reaches only the contingent, measured part: unique-hue loci, discrimination thresholds, individual variation. This would also argue against metaphysical [[structuralism-about-phenomenal-character|phenomenal structuralism]], since relations would be grounded in the relata.
3. **Colour concepts (late Wittgenstein; Ritter).** "There is no reddish green" is a grammatical, concept-determining sentence. Reports of reddish green are met by redescription or by acknowledging a new concept, never by discovery. RC I §§13–14: even of people who used "rötlichgrün" consistently and showed abilities we lack, "so wären wir dennoch nicht gezwungen anzuerkennen, sie sähen Farben, die wir nicht sehen", since "Es gibt ja kein allgemein anerkanntes Kriterium dafür, was eine Farbe sei, es sei denn, daß es eine unserer Farben ist". On this reading the necessity is real but conceptual. It does not by itself settle whether the qualities or the visual system stand behind the concepts.

The Map's split survives on every reading. What moves is where the line runs, through structure or around it. The record does not decide between readings 1 and 2. Reading 3 is Wittgenstein's own, and it must not be relabelled as reading 2 (see [[#guards|Guards]]).

## Key Sources {#key-sources}

### Wittgenstein, *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus* (Ogden translation, 1922)
- **URL**: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5740 (LaTeX source `files/5740/5740-t/5740-t.tex`; Ogden named as translator in the header)
- **Type**: Primary text, public domain
- **Key points**:
  - 6.3751: "For two colours, *e.g.* to be at one place in the visual field, is impossible, logically impossible, for it is excluded by the logical structure of colour." It continues: "Let us consider how this contradiction presents itself in physics", with particles that cannot have two velocities at once. The *Tractatus* thus expected the exclusion to be analysed *into physics*, which complicates any reading that grounds it in the quality. The German has "zugleich" (at the same time); Ogden's English omits it.
  - The parenthesis: "The assertion that a point in the visual field has two different colours at the same time, is a contradiction."
  - 4.123: "This blue colour and that stand in the internal relation of brighter and darker eo ipso. It is unthinkable that *these* two objects should not stand in this relation."
  - 2.0131: "A speck in a visual field need not be red, but it must have a colour; it has, so to speak, a colour space round it. A tone must have *a* pitch". This links to the colour page's #sound section.
  - 2.0251: "Space, time and colour (colouredness) are forms of objects."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Wittgenstein is no dualist, and the *Tractatus* routes the analysis through physics.

### Proops, "Wittgenstein's Logical Atomism", SEP (rev. 2022), §5.1
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/wittgenstein-atomism/
- **Type**: Encyclopedia
- **Key points**: names the problem "the problem of the manifest incompatibility of apparently unanalysable statements"; reports Ramsey's 1923 objection; quotes Notebooks (August 1916) on particles; reconstructs the 1929 degree-of-brightness analysis; concludes that Wittgenstein "now arrives at the view that some incompatibilities cannot, after all, be reduced to logical impossibilities", so that the impossibility is "irreducibly mathematical", which "contradicts the *Tractatus*'s idea that all necessity is logical necessity (6.37)". The bibliography lists Moss (2012), "Solving the Color Incompatibility Problem" (*Journal of Philosophical Logic* 41(5): 841–51), as a lead.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.

### Wittgenstein, "Some Remarks on Logical Form" (1929)
- **URL**: https://www.wittgensteinproject.org/w/index.php?title=Some_Remarks_on_Logical_Form&oldid=11782; DOI 10.1093/aristoteliansupp/9.1.162 (Crossref: *Aristotelian Society Supplementary Volume* 9(1): 162–171)
- **Type**: Primary text (Wittgenstein's only paper published in his lifetime)
- **Key points (p. 168)**: "the relation of difference of degree is an internal relation"; "The mutual exclusion of unanalyzable statements of degree contradicts an opinion which was published by me several years ago and which necessitated that atomic propositions could not exclude one another. I here deliberately say 'exclude' and not 'contradict' … atomic propositions, although they cannot contradict, may exclude one another." Of "R P T & B P T": it "is some sort of contradiction (and not merely a false proposition)" (pp. 168–169).

### Wittgenstein, *Remarks on Colour* / *Bemerkungen über die Farben* (written 1950–51; ed. Anscombe, bilingual, 1977), via Gierlinger (2015)
- **URL (channel)**: Gierlinger, *Wittgensteins «Bemerkungen über die Farben»*, Peter Lang 2015, open access (CC-BY 4.0), DOI 10.3726/978-3-653-05038-7; PDF at library.oapen.org (bitstream 0f168de7-…/1002734.pdf)
- **Type**: Primary text read in German through a scholarly monograph that cites Anscombe's numbering plus the MS locus
- **Key passages (German, verified in Gierlinger; English is this note's translation)**:
  - **BF I §22** (MS 176, 7r–7v): "Wir wollen keine Theorie der Farben finden (weder eine physiologische noch eine psychologische), sondern die Logik der Farbbegriffe. Und diese leistet, was man sich oft mit Unrecht von einer Theorie erwartet hat." ("We do not want to find a theory of colours, neither a physiological nor a psychological one, but the logic of colour concepts. And this accomplishes what people have often wrongly expected of a theory.")
  - **BF I §1** (MS 176, 1r): "X ist heller als Y" said of two hues expresses "eine interne Relation und der Satz zeitlos" (an internal relation, and the proposition timeless).
  - **BF I §11** (MS 176, 4r–4v): "Wem ein Rötlichgrün bekannt wäre, der sollte imstande sein, eine Farbenreihe herzustellen, die mit Rot anfinge, mit Grün endet und, auch für uns vielleicht, einen kontinuierlichen Übergang zwischen ihnen bildet." Such a person would see as reddish green, on some occasions, a tone we always see as brown.
  - **BF I §§13–14** (MS 176, 4v–5r): the passage quoted under [[#question|reading 3]] above.
  - **BF III §123** (MS 173, 28r–28v): "Kann ich denn auch nur sagen: 'Diese Leute nennen dies (ein Braun etwa) rötlichgrün'?"
- **Not verified**: the McAlister–Schättle English wording, and the commonly quoted "There is no such thing as phenomenology, but there are phenomenological problems" (Wikipedia gives III §248; not checked). **Paraphrase RC in English; do not put English RC wording in quotation marks.**
- **Secondary**: da Silva (ALWS *Agora*) lists Wittgenstein's examples ("There is no transparent white"; "grey cannot be bright"; "there is no reddish green") as grammatical propositions "situated between logic and the empirical".
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. Wittgenstein's view is anti-Cartesian, and the private-language considerations stand behind it.

### Hering, *Zur Lehre vom Lichtsinne* (Gerold 1878; Sechste Mittheilung, presented 15 May 1874), §38 "Das natürliche System der Farbenempfindungen"
- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/b2199755x (djvu text; page headers at the top of each page; p. 109 = lines 5053–5099, p. 110 = lines 5100–5146)
- **Type**: Primary text, public domain, German. English renderings here are this note's.
- **Key passages**:
  - p. 109: "einerseits Roth und Grün, andererseits Gelb und Blau nie gleichzeitig in einer Farbe deutlich bemerkbar sind." / "Warum sich dies so verhält, ist von vornherein nicht einzusehen, aber es ist so." / "Denn die rothe Empfindung zeigt doch (abgesehen von den physikalischen und physiologischen Bedingungen, unter denen sie entsteht) zur grünen so viel und so wenig einen Gegensatz wie zur gelben." ("For the red sensation, apart from the physical and physiological conditions under which it arises, shows as much and as little opposition to green as to yellow.") / "Warum soll es also keine Farbe geben, die zugleich Roth und Grün, oder Gelb und Blau zu enthalten scheint? Eine Antwort hierauf ist vorerst unmöglich."
  - p. 110: "Es muß irgendwie in der Natur unseres Sehorgans begründet sein, daß das Vorhandensein einer deutlich rothen Empfindung die deutlich grüne an derselben Stelle ausschließt … Denn logisch läßt sich die Thatsache nicht begründen, und vorerst ist es ganz gut denkbar, daß es noch einmal gelingt, Bedingungen herzustellen, unter welchen uns eine z. B. aus Roth und Grün gemischte Empfindung entsteht, oder daß es Wesen gibt, welche eine solche Empfindung öfter haben." ("It must somehow be grounded in the nature of our visual organ … For the fact cannot be grounded logically, and for the present it is quite conceivable that we shall one day succeed in producing conditions under which a sensation mixed of, e.g., red and green arises in us, or that there are beings who have such a sensation more often.")
  - p. 110: "will ich sie wegen ihres sozusagen feindseligen Verhaltens zu einander Gegenfarben nennen."
- **1920 restatement** (*Grundzüge*, §13, pp. 48–49): no hue series runs from red to green, and no colour is distinctly reddish and greenish at once (paraphrase; the OCR of this sentence is garbled, so do not quote it without the page image). The naming marks "lediglich die Art ihres Vorkommens … ohne jede Beziehung auf irgendwelche Erklärung". The standard English is Hurvich & Jameson's 1964 translation, *Outlines of a Theory of the Light Sense*, which was not seen.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral to unfriendly. Hering opens the series (Erste Mittheilung, presented 8 June 1872, pp. 1–2) by tracing the inadequacy of the reigning theories to "der spiritualistischen oder, wie man sie euphemistisch bezeichnet hat, „psychologischen“ Behandlung" of questions that "physiologisch untersucht werden müssen" (the OCR reads "psjmhologischen"; corrected here). **Do not enlist him.**

### Crane & Piantanida (1983), *Science* 221(4615): 1078–1080
- **DOI**: 10.1126/science.221.4615.1078 (Crossref-verified; abstract via Europe PMC, PMID 17736657)
- **Key point (abstract)**: "Some dyadic color names (such as reddish green and bluish yellow) describe colors that are not normally realizable. By stabilizing the retinal image of the boundary between a pair of red and green stripes (or a pair of yellow and blue stripes) but not their outer edges, however, the entire region can be perceived simultaneously as both red and green (or yellow and blue)."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. It is evidence for Hering's contingency reading if the reports are taken at face value.

### Billock, Gleason & Tsou (2001), *JOSA A* 18(10): 2398–2403
- **DOI**: 10.1364/JOSAA.18.002398. Full PDF: https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~kjameson/BillockEtAlImpossibleColorsJOSA2001.pdf
- **Key points (full text)**: seven "extremely experienced observers (all but one … are professional psychophysicists)", of whom "The primary subjects were two of the authors". With equiluminant fields, "subjects reported reddish greens or bluish yellows, in violation of Hering's laws". "On occasion (4 out of 7 subjects) the percept was a homogeneous mixture color whose red and green components were as clear and as compelling as the red and blue components of a purple." Observers "were tongue-tied in their descriptions of these colors, using terms like 'green with a red sheen,' or 'red with green highlights.'" Afterwards, "two subjects noted independently that reddish-green and yellowish-blue colors could now be imagined." With non-equiluminant fields they saw "spurious pattern formation". The model is a "soft-wired" cortical opponency, a winner-take-all competition "whose opponency can be disabled".
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. The colours are reportable, and the reporting vocabulary is borrowed, the pattern [[palette-extension-void]] documents.

### Hsieh & Tse (2006), *Vision Research* 46(14): 2251–2258
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.visres.2005.11.030 (abstract via Europe PMC, PMID 16469353; full text 403)
- **Key point (abstract)**: "(3) perceived colors are not 'forbidden colors' at all, but rather intermediate colors". Colour mixing also occurs dichoptically, "implying that color mixing during filling-in is in part a cortical phenomenon". The VSS 2006 abstract (*J. Vision* 6(6): 239) says the C&P claim "has never been empirically tested" and that "the 'forbidden colors' reported by Crane and Piantanida (1983) do not exist". It does not mention Billock et al. (2001).
- **Method dispute (press only)**: Live Science (Wolchover 2012) reports that Hsieh's observers matched their percept on a colour map, landing on "muddy brown", and quotes Billock that Hsieh used steady fixation rather than retinal stabilisation, so "says nothing about colors achieved via more powerful methods". **Unverified in the primary paper; do not state the method difference in an article.** Note the convergence with BF III §123, where Wittgenstein's own example of what people might call "rötlichgrün" is "ein Braun etwa".

### Ritter (2017), "'Reddish Green' – Wittgenstein on Concepts and the Limits of the Empirical", *Conceptus* 42(101–102): 1–19
- **DOI**: 10.1515/cpt-2016-0001 (Crossref, with abstract; PhilArchive blocked)
- **Key point (abstract)**: "A concept-determining sentence such as 'There is no reddish green' registers the absence of such a transition." He proposes "a redescription of the relevant phenomena, invoking only ordinary colour concepts", suggesting "that instead of discovering forbidden colours the experimenters introduced a new use of '-ish'", while allowing "a point in speaking of 'reddish green' in their context" via Wittgenstein's "physiognomy".
- **Note**: the abstract names Crane and Piantanida, not Billock et al. Whether Ritter addresses the 2001 homogeneous-mixture reports is unverified.

### Churchland (2005), "Chimerical colors", *Philosophical Psychology* 18(5): 527–560
- **DOI**: 10.1080/09515080500264115 (abstract via OpenAlex)
- **Key point**: the Hurvich–Jameson network predicts "impossible" colour sensations, "activation-vectors … that lie inside the space of neuronally possible activation-vectors, but outside the central 'color spindle'", whose "accurate descriptions in ordinary language appear semantically ill-formed or even self-contradictory". He aims this against anti-reductionists who call the H–J parallels "just empirical correspondences, constructed post facto, with no predictive or explanatory purchase on the intrinsic characters of qualia proper".
- **Tenet alignment**: Conflicts (reductive). It is the strongest *structural* reply, because it claims predictive purchase on quality. It is about colours outside the spindle, not about reddish green.

### SEP "Color" (Maund 2024) and its supplement "Color Science—Some Complexities"
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/color/ ; https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/color/color-science-complexities.html
- **Key points**: the opponent model "explains why we have experiences of bluish-reds, and yellowish-reds, but not greenish-reds", but it "has the status of a simplified model. Finding solid neurophysiological evidence confirming the theory is proving difficult" (supplement). §2.2: Byrne and Hilbert's hue magnitudes exploit "the opponent-processing model of color vision" to explain "the similarity relations among the hues and the binary/unique distinction". §2.2 also reports W. Wright (2019, *Philosophical Studies* 176(6): 1513–1533, DOI 10.1007/s11098-018-1076-9) that "the unique hues do not play a fundamental role in determining all color appearances", quoting Malkoc et al. (2005) that they "do not alone fully anchor the structure of color appearance for an individual".

### Webster et al. (2000) and Malkoc et al. (2005), *JOSA A*
- **DOIs**: 10.1364/JOSAA.17.001545; 10.1364/JOSAA.22.002154 (abstracts via Europe PMC)
- **Key points**: green, blue and yellow settings cluster "along directions intermediate to the LvsM and SvsLM axes"; "the unique hues appear largely unconstrained by normal individual differences in the cone-opponent axes" (2000). And "a weak tie between the cone-opponent axes and the structure of color appearance" (2005). The phenomenal opponent axes Hering described are not the physiological cardinal axes. The structural explanation of the absent reddish green is therefore owed, not in hand.

## Major Positions {#positions}

### Logical or grammatical necessity (Wittgenstein 1921 → 1929 → 1950–51)
- **Core claim**: the exclusion is not an empirical discovery. Its ground moved from the yet-to-be-completed analysis of the *Tractatus* (6.3751, an analysis expected to run through physics), to irreducible exclusion among statements of degree (1929), to the grammar of colour concepts (RC).
- **Relation to tenets**: no dualist ally. The Map may use the *structure of the problem*, that colour exclusion resists both truth-functional and physiological treatment, but not Wittgenstein's conclusion.

### Organ-grounded contingency (Hering 1874/1878)
- **Core claim**: inspected as sensations, red stands no closer in opposition to green than to yellow. The exclusion is a brute fact grounded in the visual organ and might be overcome.
- **Relation to tenets**: compatible with the Map's concession that structure is tractable. Hering is not an ally on the mind–body question.

### Neural-structural explanation with predictive purchase (Hurvich & Jameson 1957; Churchland 2005; Billock et al. 2001)
- **Core claim**: opponent coding explains colour space's shape, including its apparent necessities. Altering or bypassing the coding yields novel colours.
- **Relation to tenets**: the strongest reductive pressure here, because it claims to predict qualitative character. The Map's reply runs through its existing line that predicting *which* quality is not explaining *that* there is one, and through Webster/Malkoc: the cardinal axes are not Hering's axes, so the neural account is incomplete even on its own terms. Do not overstate this reply. The coding may sit later in cortex, as Billock's "softwired cortical" model proposes.

### Redescription (Ritter 2017; Hsieh & Tse 2006, empirically)
- **Core claim**: the reported reddish greens are ordinary intermediate colours under a new use of "-ish".
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. Note that the redescription strategy has no stopping rule. By RC I §14 any report can be absorbed, which is the [[palette-extension-void]]'s "dissent is definitional, not evidential" in its earliest form.

### Quality-grounded internal relations (the Map's reconstruction)
- **Core claim**: the exclusion holds because of what red and green are. The Map's [[structuralism-about-phenomenal-character#individuation-constitution|individuation/constitution]] view, on which relations locate a character without constituting it, would then gain a positive datum: a relation *flowing from* the characters.
- **Relation to tenets**: congenial to Tenet 1, at the **coherence** tier only. It is the reading Hering's phenomenological report directly contradicts, and Hering knew colour appearance as well as anyone.

## Key Debates {#debates}

### Is the exclusion logical?
- **Sides**: the *Tractatus* (yes, pending analysis); Ramsey (1923: the analysis leaves a synthetic a priori residue); Wittgenstein 1929 (exclusion without contradiction); the Vienna Circle and its critics. Leads only: Schlick's "Gibt es ein materiales Apriori?" (1930; reprinted in *Die Wiener Zeit*, Springer 2008, pp. 447–469, DOI 10.1007/978-3-211-33116-3_25), and Putnam (1956, *Phil. Review* 65(2), DOI 10.2307/2182832), Pap (1957, 66(1): 94ff., DOI 10.2307/2182857) and Putnam (1957, 66(1): 100ff., DOI 10.2307/2182858). Their contents were not read.
- **Current state**: open in the logic literature (Moss 2012 is a lead).

### Has anyone seen reddish green?
- **Sides**: yes, Crane & Piantanida (1983) and Billock et al. (2001, 4/7 homogeneous, 2/7 able to imagine it afterwards); no, Hsieh & Tse (2006: intermediate colours); conceptually reframed, Ritter (2017).
- **Core disagreement**: method (stabilisation versus fixation, press-reported only) and description. Billock's observers were expert psychophysicists, two of them authors, and their descriptions came in borrowed vocabulary.
- **Current state**: unresolved. WebSearch found no peer-reviewed replication between 2015 and 2025. A Northeastern vision-lab poster (Livitz et al., c. 2011) claims opponent-hue combinations in ordinary induction displays (lead).

### Does neural opponency explain Hering's structure?
- **Sides**: the textbook model (SEP supplement); "simplified model"; Webster 2000 and Malkoc 2005 (weak tie to cardinal axes); Wright 2019 (unique hues not fundamental); Matthen 2020 (the function of opponency is non-informational, per SEP §2.2).
- **Current state**: the phenomenal opponent structure is well measured psychophysically (hue cancellation, Hurvich & Jameson 1957). Its neural basis is not settled.

## Guards for the Fold Writer {#guards}

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]**: the subject gives the tenet no evidential support either way, and the fold keeps the colour page's compatible tier. On Hering's reading, colour's apparent necessities are structure, which the Map already concedes is tractable, and the residue is unchanged: what the colours, including any novel one, are like. On the quality-grounding reading the Map gains a datum congenial to its anti-structuralism, a relation that seems to flow from its relata. It pays by narrowing "structure is tractable". On Wittgenstein's own reading the necessity is conceptual and the metaphysical question is left where it was.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]**: the textbook story, hard-wired opponent channels explaining why no reddish green appears, is the simple one. It is the story the data complicate: cardinal axes are not appearance axes (Webster 2000; Malkoc 2005), the model is "simplified" (SEP), and opponency may be "softwired" (Billock 2001). The simple explanation of colour's structure is not yet the true one.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]**: the forbidden-colour debate runs on observers' reports of what they saw. Its being an empirical dispute presupposes that the experiences make a difference to the reports. This is not an argument for the tenet; it marks a debate in which epiphenomenalism would make the evidence idle.
