## Executive Summary

The task's framing needs correcting before it can be answered. In the literature, **"first-person realism" is the name of the privileged-perspective family, not of its egalitarian rival.** Builes's definition: "According to First-Person Realism, one's own first-person perspective on the world is metaphysically privileged in some way" (Builes 2024, abstract). What the task calls "egalitarian first-person realism" is a sub-variety within that family — Fine's "nonstandard" first-personal realism (external relativism or fragmentalism) and Lipman's standpoint pluralism — and it is a live position with named proponents and a 2026 OUP monograph. The genuinely egalitarian rival that stands *outside* first-person realism is the deflationist default (Lewis/Perry; Hellie's target; Merlo's "MVM"), which denies subjective facts altogether. So there are two egalitarianisms, and only one of them is a realism.

The pivot of the whole debate is **Merlo's "question of egalitarianism"**: granted subjective facts, must one identify reality with one's own point of view (Hare, Valberg, Merlo's own SVM), or can one keep parity by making reality incoherent (Lipman's fragmentalism) or irreducibly relative (Fine's external relativism)? Merlo himself is **inegalitarian** — his 2021 paper argues that other minds are *not* like one's own at the subjective level — so the harvester's gloss of Merlo 2021 as the egalitarian source is wrong; it is the best statement of the *problem* egalitarians must solve (parity and multiplicity), from the other side.

For the Map: egalitarian standpoint pluralism is the fully worked-out version of the fragmentalist exit the Map's own quadrilemma analysis says it must take, and it handles other minds at least as well as the Map's posit with far more machinery. What the Map would have to give up to adopt it is the fact *simpliciter* about which subject is this one — the fact P-I2 says Tenet 4's indexical objection presupposes. The egalitarian option does not sit beside the Map's position; it prices it. Article-sized (see Assessment).

## Key Sources

### Builes, "Eight Arguments for First-Person Realism" (Philosophy Compass 19(1), 2024)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/phc3.12959 (preprint read via Wayback copy of https://philpapers.org/archive/BUIEAF.pdf)
- **Type**: Survey paper
- **Key points**:
  - Defines the terms by the modal/temporal analogy. "A subjective fact is a fact that obtains according to some points of view but does not obtain according to others. An objective fact is a fact that obtains according to all points of view." First-Person Realism "states that one's first-person perspective is only subjectively privileged". Three variants listed — List's irreducible first-personal facts, Hare's presence, Merlo's MENTAL-state glow — all privilege one perspective.
  - The explicit foil is "the 'default' metaphysical view that none of us is special: no one's point of view is metaphysically privileged". Argument 2 reports Hellie 2013 against "'egalitarian' views of consciousness, which treat every stream of consciousness as metaphysically 'on a par'". Argument 8 (arbitrariness defence) is where egalitarian realisms enter: List's many worlds and Fine-style fragmentalism "directly address this arbitrariness worry".
  - **Footnote 46 is the hinge**: of those egalitarian realisms, "it is not clear if they do justice to the main motivations behind First-Person Realism, which require that I am special simpliciter (not merely relative to a world or fragment)", citing Merlo 2013.
- **Tenet alignment**: Argument 1 (indexical gap parallel to consciousness gap) and Argument 4 (first-person Castor and Pollux) are the Map's own moves on `indexical-knowledge-and-identity` and `haecceity`. Conflict: the paper's realism is *privileged-subject* realism, which the Map rejects in Hare's form.

### Merlo, "The Metaphysical Problem of Other Minds" (Pacific Philosophical Quarterly 102(4), 2021, 633–664)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/papq.12380 (open access; read via Wayback copy)
- **Type**: Paper (CC BY-NC-ND)
- **Key points**:
  - A third problem of other minds, neither epistemological nor conceptual: from the Appearance/Reality Principle plus a phenomenological exercise — "it is part of how things appear to me from my perspective that only my experiences possess feltness" — it follows that the difference is real: **metaphysical solipsism**.
  - Two things make this unacceptable, and they are the egalitarian's two demands: "Call the problem of doing justice to this aspect of commonsense the problem of parity", and the problem of multiplicity (everyone's solipsistic judgment cannot be true of the *same* property — Merleau-Ponty's "multiple solipsism").
  - Subjective fact defined: something is a fact — "an aspect or component of how reality is, in and of itself" — "even if it is not objectively the case", where objective means "uniformly across all points of view".
  - **The question of egalitarianism**, verbatim: "The question of egalitarianism is the question whether subjectivists are bound to make a similar move with respect to points of view. If one recognizes the existence of subjective facts, as subjectivists do, should one identify reality with one's point of view on it? Some advocates of Subjectivism (including Valberg (2007) and Hare (2009)) have suggested so. Others have been exploring the possibility of retaining egalitarianism by accepting the incoherence of reality (cf. Lipman 2016) or insisting that reality is irreducibly relative (cf. Fine's (2005) 'external relativism')." Note 23: the term is borrowed from Hellie 2013; Fine speaks of "neutrality".
  - Merlo's own resolution is two-level: an objective level at which "All minds are 'on a par'", and a subjective level at which "a spotlight of feltness shines on all and only my experiences". The objective is grounded in the subjective. He concedes the line is thin: "It may be debated whether this fine line can be coherently sustained".
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral-to-conflicting. The Map affirms full parity of consciousness across subjects (it rejects Hare's weak solipsism), so it stands with Merlo's *objective* level and against his subjective spotlight.

### Lipman, "Subjective Facts about Consciousness" (Ergo 10, 2023, art. 19)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.3998/ergo.4649 (preprint read via Wayback copy of https://philpapers.org/archive/lipsas-3.pdf)
- **Type**: Paper. **Metadata correction**: the task lead placed this in *Philosophical Quarterly* 75(3) 1026–; those are List's quadrilemma pages. Lipman's paper is Ergo vol. 10 (2023).
- **Key points**:
  - Subjects are metaphysical *standpoints*, regimented with a hybrid-logic operator @s(p), "at s, p". "Let us call any fact that obtains relative to some but not all subjects a 'subjective fact' (following Merlo 2016: §2)." The relativity is metaphysical, not experiential: "the world itself differs between subjects" — which is what separates the view from Lewis/Perry deflation.
  - §7 lays out the design space by **factivity**. "If one restricts factivity to what obtains relative to oneself, one obtains a solipsist-like or 'metaphysically centered' picture according to which the world is in accord with that it is like from your own standpoint only (defended by Hare 2009; Merlo 2016)." Restricting factivity to appearance facts instead, "one can think of the world as the totality of objective facts with, in addition, each appearance fact that obtains relative to some subject or other." Unrestricted factivity plus strong variation yields contradiction: "If @s1(p) and @s2(not p), and metaphysical relativity is factive, then it would follow that p and not p." Lipman's own route (fn 17) "is not unlike that of fragmentalism".
- **Tenet alignment**: Egalitarian by construction. Dualism-congenial (Nagel's incompleteness argument reframed and endorsed) but officially neutral on physicalism.

### Lipman, *Standpoints: Time and Subjectivity* (OUP 2026) — abstracts only
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/9780198921318.001.0001
- Book abstract: "This book develops a philosophical framework that allows us to accept perspectival facts without needing to privilege one perspective. In particular, it allows us to accept the reality of temporal and subjective facts without needing to privilege a time, such as the present, or a subject, such as oneself. The framework develops a form of fragmentalism, which takes the world to consist in multiple stacks of facts instead of one single stack of facts." And: "Because no time or subject is metaphysically privileged, we are free to adopt any temporal or subjective standpoint in our thinking about the world."
- Ch. 12, "The Subjective Fact of Being Oneself" (pp. 191–225), abstract: taking the appearance of one's own specialness at face value "naturally leads to a solipsist-like theory according to which one really holds a special place in the world by being uniquely involved in facts of a certain type. This chapter proposes a pluralist account of the way we appear to ourselves. I introduce a notion of being the factual standpoint". This is the egalitarian answer to the vertiginous question — see Debates.

### Kinkaid, "A 'Drainage Hole' in Being: Sartre and First-Person Realism" (J. APA 11(4), 2025, 782–799)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/apa.2025.8 (open access, read in full)
- **Type**: Paper — the one source found that *argues for* the egalitarian wing against Merlo.
- **Key points**: Sartre's man-in-the-park vignette is set against Merlo's Exercise 1: "Sartre claims that the man in the park is encountered as having his own world with a quality of its own 'mineness'" — a rival phenomenological datum that, Kinkaid argues, "undermines Merlo's argument for metaphysical solipsism". Egalitarian realists "respond by adopting fragmentalism"; Sartre's "detotalized totality" is read as a kin notion. Kinkaid's Sartre "metaphysically privileges the first-person perspective while rejecting inegalitarian accounts of this privilege" (quoted with a hyphen the PDF text drops).
- **Tenet alignment**: Supplies what the Map's `vertiginous-question` page lacks — a phenomenological counter-datum to the solipsistic reading of first-person presence.

### Merlo, "Subjectivism and the Mental" (Dialectica 70(3), 2016, 311–342)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/1746-8361.12153 (preprint read via Wayback copy)
- **Key points**: "(Subjectivism) Some propositions are true simpliciter without being true from all points of view." SVM's "most striking feature" is "its uncompromising inegalitarian character"; the privilege is itself subjective (it is not claimed that my point of view is first-personal *from every* point of view). **Footnote 19** is Merlo's reply to the egalitarian option: "there might be ways to reconcile the thesis that the totality of facts is oriented towards one point of view with the idea that, most fundamentally, all points of view are metaphysically on a par" — a non-factive conception of the fundamental (Merlo 2013) or fragmentalism (Fine 2005, Lipman 2016); "My own preference goes to the first strategy – the second runs the risk of undermining the sense in which I am, indeed, special vis-à-vis all other subjects." Also a critique of Hare the `egocentric-presentism` page should carry: presence on *perceptual objects* raises questions (the back of the painting; a star that exploded billions of years ago) that locating subjective facts in *mental* properties avoids.

## Major Positions

### Inegalitarian first-person realism (Hare 2009; Merlo 2016, 2021; Valberg 2007)
- **Core claim**: reality is oriented to one point of view; which one is itself a subjective matter. Hare locates the privileged fact in *presence* on perceptual objects, Merlo in MENTAL states.
- **Relation to tenets**: the Map already engages Hare; Merlo's version is the sharper target because it drops object-level presence and states the other-minds cost openly.

### Egalitarian first-person realism (Fine 2005 "nonstandard" realism; Lipman 2016, 2023, 2026; Kinkaid 2025)
- **Core claim**: irreducible subjective facts for every subject, no subject privileged *simpliciter*. Parity is secured by denying that reality is composed from one standpoint. Two routes: **external relativism** (rejects Fine's Absolutism) and **fragmentalism** (rejects Fine's Coherence); Lipman adds a third coherent option, factivity restricted to appearance facts. Deflation is avoided because the variation is in the world, not in experience.
- **Relation to tenets**: the position `vertiginous-question` says the Map's commitments force ("the Map must accept fragmentation") and `egocentric-presentism` calls an unpaid "promissory note". It is the Map's exit, worked out by someone else.

### Deflationary egalitarianism (Lewis 1979; Perry 1979; Hellie's target; Merlo's "MVM")
- All subjects on a par and no subjective facts. The Map has already recorded that it has no argument compelling the deflationist to stop. The taxonomy's point: *this* is the only egalitarianism that rejects first-person realism.

### Many-worlds first-person realism (List 2023)
- Egalitarian across worlds, privileged within each. Already on the Map; Builes files it with fragmentalism as an arbitrariness-defusing variant, and his fn 46 objection applies to both.

## Key Debates

### Does egalitarian realism keep the datum that motivated realism?
- **Sides**: Merlo (2016 fn 19; 2013) and Builes (fn 46): relativising privilege to a fragment or world "runs the risk of undermining the sense in which I am, indeed, special". Lipman (2026 ch. 12): "being the factual standpoint" captures how one appears to oneself without solipsism or a transcendental ego.
- **Current state**: open; Lipman's chapter is the newest move and has not been read here beyond its abstract.

### Does the egalitarian view answer or dissolve the vertiginous question?
- Hellie 2013 charges that egalitarianism leaves "why am I this one?" unanswered. The egalitarian realist's reply is a dissolution: for every s, @s(s is oneself); there is no further fact *simpliciter* to be explained, any more than there is a fact about which time is present simpliciter on a fragmentalist A-theory. Whether a dissolution is an answer is exactly where the Map and Lipman would part.

### Parity vs multiplicity, and who wins the phenomenology
- Merlo's Exercise 1 (only my experiences have feltness) against Kinkaid's Sartre (the other is encountered as a centre with its own mineness). Both appeal to the Appearance/Reality Principle; they disagree about what appears. The Map's `vertiginous-question` page states the solipsistic datum only; it should register the counter-datum.

### Does egalitarian realism require fragmentalism?
- Not necessarily: Fine's external relativism and Lipman's appearance-restricted factivity are both coherent non-fragmentalist egalitarian options. But every author surveyed who actually *endorses* egalitarian realism (Fine's preference, Lipman, Kinkaid's Sartre) ends in fragmentalism, because unrestricted factivity is what makes the subjective facts facts "as such". The Map's corpus names only the fragmentalist route; external relativism is the under-discussed second route.

## Implications for the Map (what MOVES)

1. **Terminology.** The Map uses "first-person realism" in List's sense (irreducible first-personal facts). Compatible with the literature, but the Map should not describe egalitarianism as the *denial* of first-person realism: the deflationist denies it, the fragmentalist does not.
2. **Posit One** ("a fact of *which* subject this is, prior to and independent of any physical or functional criterion"). Under standpoint pluralism this survives *relativised*: subjects remain numerically distinct standpoints with real boundaries (P-I1 closed individualism is untouched — subjective variegation presupposes distinct subjects), but the "which subject is this one" fact obtains only relative to each standpoint, never simpliciter. The primitive "I" within one world becomes a primitive "I" within one *fragment*.
3. **Tenet 4 / P-I2.** The indexical objection to many-worlds presupposes, per P-I2, a non-deflationary fact about which subject is this one. If the Map takes the egalitarian exit, the dissolution it accepts for "why am I this subject?" is available to the Everettian for "why am I this branch?" — each branch-subject is itself relative to itself. P-I5 already records that only the indexical argument engages List's view; this would weaken it against branch-egalitarian Everett too, leaving Tenet 4 on its other two arguments. The Map can still distinguish fragments *within one physical world* from many physical worlds, but the indexical lever shortens. This is the real cost; it belongs on `positions/individuation-and-subjecthood`, not only on the concept page.
4. **Other minds — calibration.** The Map holds every subject fully conscious and rejects Hare's weak solipsism, so on parity it already stands with the egalitarians against Hare and Merlo. But it has no account of *how* parity and the first-personal datum cohere beyond "accept fragmentation", whereas Lipman supplies a logic (internal vs external structure, standpoint-switching inference) and a chapter on self-appearance. Honest verdict: the egalitarian view handles other minds at least as well as the Map's posit, with more machinery; the Map's residual disagreement is only over whether the vertiginous datum needs a fact *simpliciter* — the point Builes fn 46 and Merlo fn 19 press, and the point that keeps Tenet 4's indexical argument alive. The Map should say it keeps the simpliciter fact *because* Tenet 4 needs it, not because egalitarianism fails on other minds.
5. **Tenet 5** untouched. **Tenet 1**: Lipman's framework is officially neutral on physicalism — congenial, not corroborating.
