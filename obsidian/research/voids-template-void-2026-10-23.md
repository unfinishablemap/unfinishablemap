## Executive Summary

Every reader of minds starts from itself. Epley et al. (2004) found that "people adopt others' perspectives by serially adjusting from their own", and that "adjustments tend to be insufficient, in part, because people stop adjusting once a plausible estimate is reached" [raw]. The default survives knowledge of it. Krueger & Clement (1994) found that participants "who had been educated about the consensus bias, or had received feedback about actual consensus, or both showed the bias" [raw]. Keysar et al. (2003) report "a stark dissociation between an ability to reflectively distinguish one's own beliefs from others', and the routine deployment of this ability" [raw]. Between similar minds the template mostly fits, and using it is reasonable. Krueger & Clement themselves grant that the bias "seems statistically appropriate (Dawes, 1989)" [raw].

The void opens where reader and read differ in architecture. Then the template misfits, and from the reader's own position a misfit cannot be told apart from an absence or deficit in the other. Three independent literatures bear on this:

- **Cross-cultural.** A meta-analysis found an in-group advantage in emotion recognition that shrinks with exposure (Elfenbein & Ambady 2002).
- **Cross-neurotype.** This is the double-empathy literature (Milton 2012; Edey et al. 2016; Sheppard et al. 2016; Crompton et al. 2020, 2025).
- **Cross-species and cross-system.** De Waal's (1999) paired errors of anthropomorphism and anthropodenial, and folk attribution of phenomenal consciousness to large language models (Colombatto & Fleming 2024).

The void's distinctive feature is where the darkness gets recorded. Most voids register as a limit of the self: I cannot introspect this, I cannot conceive that. This one is a limit of the reader that can register as a property of the read. Edey et al. put it in their own terms: "'social impairments' exhibited by individuals with ASD may, at least in part, represent a failure by typical individuals to infer the correct mental states" [raw].

The evidence for that displacement is **mixed and must be reported as mixed**:

- The 2025 registered report (N = 311) found "no difference in information transfer between single-neurotype and mixed-neurotype chains" [raw]. This fails to replicate the 2020 result.
- The reading failures are asymmetric, not double. In Edey et al. the autistic participants "did not show this 'same group' advantage" [raw].
- Negative trait impressions of autistic adults are shared by autistic raters (DeBrabander et al. 2019), so those impressions cannot be credited to a reader-template misfit.
- Two recent critiques say the double-empathy construct is poorly derived (Livingston et al. 2025) or under-measured (Fellowes 2026). Its originators now restate it as a sociological rather than social-cognitive account (Kilgallon et al. 2026).

The void therefore rests on the robust egocentric-adjustment literature. The cross-architecture work is where its displacement signature is being tested, so far with mixed results.

## Key Sources

### The general default: egocentric adjustment

#### Epley, Keysar, Van Boven & Gilovich (2004), "Perspective Taking as Egocentric Anchoring and Adjustment"
- **Citation**: *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 87(3), 327–339. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.87.3.327 (Crossref verified.)
- **Type**: Paper (five studies)
- **Stance**: Empirical social psychology. Neutral on metaphysics of mind.
- **Key points**: People estimate others' perceptions by starting from their own and adjusting. Participants were "slower to indicate that another's perception would be different from--rather than similar to--their own" [raw]. Egocentric bias "increased under time pressure (Study 2) and decreased with accuracy incentives (Study 3)" [raw]. Adjustment ends at plausibility, not accuracy.
- **Tenet alignment**: Framework-neutral. This is the core of the void's existence claim.
- **Quote**: "adjustments tend to be insufficient, in part, because people stop adjusting once a plausible estimate is reached" [raw].

#### Keysar, Lin & Barr (2003), "Limits on theory of mind use in adults"
- **Citation**: *Cognition*, 89(1), 25–41. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0277(03)00064-7 (Crossref verified.)
- **Stance**: Psycholinguistics. Adults have a mature theory of mind but do not reliably deploy it.
- **Key points**: In a referential communication game, participants knew the director could not see a hidden object yet often took it as the director's referent, "sometimes even attempting to comply with the instruction by actually moving the bag itself" [raw].
- **Quote**: "These results show a stark dissociation between an ability to reflectively distinguish one's own beliefs from others', and the routine deployment of this ability in interpreting the actions of others." [raw]
- **Counterweight, Rubio-Fernández (2017)**: *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, 24(4), 1121–1128, https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1190-7 (Crossref verified). She finds that "optimal performance in the director task, as established by the standard metrics of interference, is possible by using selective attention alone, and not necessarily Theory of Mind" [raw]. She also finds that pragmatic measures "can reveal that people actively represent the director's mental states" [raw]. **Writer consequence**: cite Keysar for the knowledge/deployment dissociation, flagged with Rubio-Fernández's challenge to what the paradigm measures. Do not lean the void on the director task alone.

#### Nickerson (1999), "How we know—and sometimes misjudge—what others know"
- **Citation**: *Psychological Bulletin*, 125(6), 737–759. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.737 (Crossref verified; abstract via OpenAlex.)
- **Stance**: Review. Own-knowledge imputation is the natural starting point and usually serves well.
- **Quote**: "people impute their own knowledge to others and that, although this serves them well in general, they often do so uncritically, with the result of erroneously assuming that other people have the same knowledge" [raw].
- **Note**: The knowledge-imputation face is owned by [[conceptual-metabolism-void]]. Cite Nickerson here only for the general "starting point" structure.

#### Ross, Greene & House (1977); Dawes (1989); Krueger & Clement (1994): the false-consensus line
- **Citations**: Ross, Greene & House, "The 'false consensus effect': An egocentric bias in social perception and attribution processes", *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology*, 13(3), 279–301, https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1031(77)90049-X. Dawes, "Statistical criteria for establishing a truly false consensus effect", *JESP*, 25(1), 1–17, https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1031(89)90036-X. Krueger & Clement, "The truly false consensus effect: An ineradicable and egocentric bias in social perception", *JPSP*, 67(4), 596–610, https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.67.4.596. (All Crossref verified.)
- **Stances**: Ross et al. named the effect; no abstract was retrievable, so cite by title only. Dawes argued that projecting from oneself can be a rational use of a sample of one; no abstract was retrievable, so his stance is taken from Krueger & Clement's raw abstract, which cites him. Krueger & Clement argue the bias "merely mimics normative inductive reasoning" [raw] and is resistant to correction.
- **Quotes (Krueger & Clement)**: "The bias seems statistically appropriate (Dawes, 1989), but according to the egocentrism hypothesis, it merely mimics normative inductive reasoning." [raw]. "In Experiment 2, Ss attributed bias to another person, but their own consensus estimates were more affected by their own response to the item than by the other person's response." [raw]. "Ss continued to show consensus bias despite the availability of other statistical information." [raw]
- **Why it matters**: This gives the void two of its properties. The template is a reasonable default (Dawes), so the void is no mere error. And the template persists under correction (Krueger & Clement), which is the occluded readout.

#### Meltzoff (2007), "'Like me': a foundation for social cognition"
- **Citation**: *Developmental Science*, 10(1), 126–134. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2007.00574.x (Crossref verified; issued online 2006-12-20.)
- **Stance**: Developmental. Self-other equivalence is where social cognition begins.
- **Quote**: "The 'like me' nature of others is the starting point for social cognition, not its culmination." [raw]
- **Use**: Developmental grounding for "every reader starts from itself". Meltzoff presents this as an achievement. The void concerns its cost at architecture boundaries.

#### Mechanism-neutrality: Goldman (2006) via Wallin (2011)
- **Citations**: Goldman, *Simulating Minds: The Philosophy, Psychology, and Neuroscience of Mindreading*, OUP, https://doi.org/10.1093/0195138929.001.0001 (Crossref verified; not read). Wallin, "Is egocentric bias evidence for simulation theory?", *Synthese*, 178(3), 503–514 (online 2009, issue 2011), https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-009-9653-2 (Crossref verified; abstract from the Lund University portal HTML).
- **Stances**: Goldman's revised simulation theory, per Wallin's raw abstract, "allows mental state attributions containing some or all of the attributor’s genuine, non-simulated mental states" [raw], and so is thought to explain egocentric bias. Wallin denies that this is an advantage. She argues "theory theory and rationality theory can explain egocentricity by appealing to heuristic mindreading and the diagnosticity of attributors’ own beliefs" [raw]. (The source uses curly apostrophes; quote it with them.)
- **Writer consequence**: The template void does not depend on simulation theory. It holds on each of the three mechanism positions in [[empathy]] §"The Contemporary Three-Way Debate". The void concerns the *outcome structure*, not the mechanism.

### The cross-cultural face

#### Elfenbein & Ambady (2002), meta-analysis, with Matsumoto's comment and their reply
- **Citations**: Elfenbein & Ambady, "On the universality and cultural specificity of emotion recognition: A meta-analysis", *Psychological Bulletin*, 128(2), 203–235, https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.128.2.203. Matsumoto, "Methodological requirements to test a possible in-group advantage in judging emotions across cultures: Comment on Elfenbein and Ambady (2002) and evidence", 128(2), 236–242, https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.128.2.236. Elfenbein & Ambady, "Is there an in-group advantage in emotion recognition?", 128(2), 243–249, https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.128.2.243. (All Crossref verified.)
- **Key points**:
  - Emotions were "universally recognized at better-than-chance levels" [raw].
  - "Accuracy was higher when emotions were both expressed and recognized by members of the same national, ethnic, or regional group, suggesting an in-group advantage." [raw]
  - "This advantage was smaller for cultural groups with greater exposure to one another" [raw]. The template is calibrated by exposure, so this face is explorable and corrigible.
  - "Majority group members were poorer at judging minority group members than the reverse." [raw] Minority readers have had more exposure to majority expressions.
- **Dispute**: Matsumoto: "conclusions concerning a possible in-group advantage in judging emotions across cultures are unwarranted" [raw]. Reply: "His analyses had a serious flaw and, once corrected, replicated the original findings." [raw] The reply adds that where Matsumoto treats subtle expression differences "a methodological artifact", the authors "find a core phenomenon worthy of attention" [raw].
- **Why it matters**: This is the void's strongest single evidential anchor. It is meta-analytic, it predates and is independent of the autism literature, and its majority/minority asymmetry matches Edey et al.'s asymmetry (below). Both fit one refinement: **the template is the reader's own architecture plus its exposure history**, not the bare self.

### The cross-neurotype face (the double-empathy literature)

#### Milton (2012), "On the ontological status of autism: the 'double empathy problem'"
- **Citation**: *Disability & Society*, 27(6), 883–887. https://doi.org/10.1080/09687599.2012.710008 (Crossref verified; abstract via OpenAlex.)
- **Stance**: Sociological. Autistic author and self-advocate. Critiques the deficit model.
- **Quote**: the term is introduced as producing "a critique of autism being defined as a deficit in ‘theory of mind’, re-framing such issues as a question of reciprocity and mutuality" [raw]. The piece adopts the identity-first convention ("this piece will refer to ‘autistic people’") [raw]. The note and any article should follow it.

#### Edey, Cook, Brewer, Johnson, Bird & Press (2016), "Interaction takes two"
- **Citation**: *Journal of Abnormal Psychology*, 125(7), 879–885. https://doi.org/10.1037/abn0000199 (Crossref verified.)
- **Key points**: Autistic and typical adults made triangle animations depicting mental states. The autistic participants' animations moved "with increased jerk" [raw]. Typical observers identified mental states better from typical-made animations. The authors' premise: "we are better at interpreting the movements of others who move like us" [raw].
- **Asymmetry (central)**: "The participants with ASD did not show this 'same group' advantage, demonstrating comparable performance for the 2 sets of animations." [raw]
- **Quote (the displacement claim, in the authors' words)**: "'social impairments' exhibited by individuals with ASD may, at least in part, represent a failure by typical individuals to infer the correct mental states from the movements of those with ASD." [raw]

#### Sheppard, Pillai, Wong, Ropar & Mitchell (2016), "How Easy is it to Read the Minds of People with Autism Spectrum Disorder?"
- **Citation**: *Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders*, 46(4), 1247–1254. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-015-2662-8 (Crossref verified; online 2015-11-24.)
- **Key points**: Neurotypical observers were less able to identify which event an autistic target had experienced from video of their reactions. In study 2 they "rated ASD targets equally expressive as neurotypical targets for three of the events" [raw].
- **Quote**: "It thus seems people with ASD react differently but not less expressively to events." [raw]
- **Use**: This is the cleanest statement of the distinction the void turns on: *differently* rather than *less*. Note the scope: the observers here did **not** misjudge expressiveness. The misfit showed up as inaccuracy, not as a misattributed trait.

#### Sasson, Faso, Nugent, Lovell, Kennedy & Grossman (2017), thin-slice first impressions; DeBrabander et al. (2019), the autistic-observer control
- **Citations**: Sasson et al., *Scientific Reports*, 7, 40700, https://doi.org/10.1038/srep40700. DeBrabander, Morrison, Jones, Faso, Chmielewski & Sasson, "Do First Impressions of Autistic Adults Differ Between Autistic and Nonautistic Observers?", *Autism in Adulthood*, 1(4), 250–257, https://doi.org/10.1089/aut.2019.0018. (Both Crossref verified.)
- **Sasson key points**: First impressions of autistic people by typically developing observers were "far less favorable across a range of trait judgments", and the patterns "occur within seconds, do not change with increased exposure" [raw]. The biases vanish for transcript-only content, "suggesting that style, not substance, drives negative impressions of ASD" [raw]. The full text reports that unfamiliar observers "judge expressions made by individuals with ASD as more awkward or odd" [raw full text], with the largest effect for awkwardness (d = 2.96) [raw full text].
- **DeBrabander control (decisive for scoping)**: "autistic raters shared the TD tendency to evaluate autistic adults less favorably than TD adults, but these judgments did not reduce their social interest for interacting with autistic adults as they did for TD raters" [raw]. Disclosure of diagnosis improved TD raters' impressions but not autistic raters'. The sample was "predominantly Caucasian and male" [raw].
- **Writer consequence**: Sasson-type trait judgments **cannot** be cited as evidence that a reader's own template misfits. Readers of both neurotypes make them, so they plausibly track shared cultural norms for social signifiers. What differs by reader is the consequence (interest in interacting), which concerns uptake, not reading.

#### Crompton et al. (2020) and the 2025 registered report
- **Citations**: Crompton, Ropar, Evans-Williams, Flynn & Fletcher-Watson, "Autistic peer-to-peer information transfer is highly effective", *Autism*, 24(7), 1704–1712, https://doi.org/10.1177/1362361320919286. Crompton, Foster, Wilks, Dodd, Efthimiou, Ropar, Sasson, Lages & Fletcher-Watson, "Information transfer within and between autistic and non-autistic people", *Nature Human Behaviour*, 9(7), 1488–1500 (online 2025-05-14), https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02163-z. (Both Crossref verified.)
- **2020**: Nine diffusion chains of eight. The lay abstract reports that "when there are mixed groups of autistic and non-autistic people, much less information is shared." [raw, lay abstract]
- **2025 (N = 311, registered report)**: "We found no difference in information transfer between single-neurotype and mixed-neurotype chains. Non-autistic chains indicated higher rapport, and disclosing diagnosis improved rapport." [raw]. The authors say the result "contrasts with prior findings" and that "Enhanced participant heterogeneity and methodological differences may explain these unexpected results" [raw].
- **Writer consequence**: At the level of information transfer, the cross-neurotype mismatch effect **did not replicate** in the larger, preregistered study. A rapport difference remains. Never cite the 2020 result without the 2025 one.

- **Livingston, Hargitai & Shah (2025)**, "The double empathy problem: A derivation chain analysis and cautionary note", *Psychological Review*, 132(3), 744–757, https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000468 (PubMed abstract, PMID 38829337). The double-empathy problem "represents a striking example of a weak derivation chain in psychological science. - **Kilgallon, Botha, Dwyer, Bottema-Beutel, Milton, Sasson & Crompton (2026)**, "What the Double Empathy Problem Is (and Is Not)", *Autism in Adulthood*, online 2026-06-23, https://doi.org/10.1177/25739581261456653 (Crossref verified, with deposited abstract). "We reassert the Double Empathy Problem (DEP) as a sociological account of autistic-non-autistic (and more broadly, between individuals with differing dispositions) interaction, rather than a social-cognitive theory of autism." [raw] Their framing is "probabilistic", such that "misunderstandings between autistic and non-autistic people shift with context and power" [raw].
- **Bolis, Balsters, Wenderoth, Becchio & Schilbach (2017)**, "Beyond Autism: Introducing the Dialectical Misattunement Hypothesis and a Bayesian Account of Intersubjectivity", *Psychopathology*, 50(6), 355–372, https://doi.org/10.1159/000484353 (Crossref verified). They treat psychopathology as "a dynamic interpersonal mismatch that encompasses various levels of description" [raw]. This is a physicalist predictive-processing account of the same relational structure. Schilbach is already cited in [[voids-between-minds]] §"Encounter".
- **Gernsbacher & Yergeau (2019)**, "Empirical failures of the claim that autistic people lack a theory of mind", *Archives of Scientific Psychology*, 7(1), 102–118, https://doi.org/10.1037/arc0000067 (Crossref verified). They conclude "that the claim that autistic people lack a theory of mind is empirically questionable and societally harmful" [raw]. The baseline they target is Baron-Cohen, Leslie & Frith (1985), "Does the autistic child have a 'theory of mind'?", *Cognition*, 21(1), 37–46, https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0277(85)90022-8 (Crossref verified; not read).
- **Net stance of the field**: Contested. Proponents (Milton, Crompton, Sasson, Kilgallon et al.) read the evidence as relational. Critics (Livingston et al.; Fellowes) say the construct and its measures are not yet fit to test that reading. The *empirical facts* this note relies on are narrower than either side's thesis: same-group advantages in some reading paradigms (Edey; Sheppard; Elfenbein & Ambady), with asymmetries.

### The cross-species and cross-system faces

#### de Waal (1999), "Anthropomorphism and Anthropodenial"
- **Citation**: *Philosophical Topics*, 27(1), 255–280. https://doi.org/10.5840/philtopics199927122 (Crossref verified; no abstract; **not read; not quote-verified**.)
- **Stance (paraphrase, from secondary descriptions; verify before quoting)**: De Waal holds that two opposite errors threaten the reading of animal minds. One is over-applying human categories (anthropomorphism). The other is refusing in advance to grant shared characteristics (*anthropodenial*, his coinage). A secondary source attributes to him the phrase "a priori rejection of shared characteristics", but this was **not** found in raw primary text and must not be quoted until it is.
- **Use**: The template void at species scale runs in both directions, and the reader cannot tell from inside which error it is making.

#### Colombatto & Fleming (2024), "Folk psychological attributions of consciousness to large language models"
- **Citation**: *Neuroscience of Consciousness*, 2024(1), niae013. https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niae013 (Crossref verified; PMID 38618488.)
- **Key points**: In a sample of 300 US residents, "a majority of participants were willing to attribute some possibility of phenomenal consciousness to large language models" [raw]. The attributions were "sensitive to individual differences such as usage frequency" [raw].
- **Use (scoped)**: The study measures attribution rates, not their correctness. Its relevance is structural: here the template fits on the linguistic surface, and exposure moves the attribution. Exposure also shrinks the in-group advantage above. In the cross-system case the reader cannot tell whether a felt presence, or a felt absence, is template-generated.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

**Statement.** A reader of minds has no position from which to read except its own. Its default model of another mind is itself, adjusted from exposure. Where the other's architecture differs, the model misfits. From inside, the misfit has no mark of its own: it arrives as a reading, sometimes as an absence or a trait of the other. The reader's limit is then recorded at the wrong address, in the description of the other mind.

**Kind.** Mixed:
- **Unexplored (principal).** The misfit is measurable from outside. Cross-reading designs compare matched and mismatched reader–target pairs and run the test in both directions. Exposure, incentives and disclosure each move the effect: the in-group advantage is "smaller for cultural groups with greater exposure to one another" [raw]; egocentric bias "decreased with accuracy incentives" [raw]; "disclosing diagnosis improved rapport" [raw]. These are the marks of territory that is dark because it has not been worked, not because it cannot be.
- **Occluded at the readout (natural, not deliberate).** From the reader's own position the default readout delivers a reading and does not deliver the misfit. Knowing about the bias does not remove it (Krueger & Clement; Keysar et al.). This places the void near the operation-void family in [[three-kinds-of-void]] §"Between the Categories", where the product is lit and the operation dark, but it should **not** inherit that family's "unexplorable at the operation" clause. The operation here (adjustment from a self-template) is experimentally tractable through response latencies, time pressure and incentives (Epley et al.).
- **Not unexplorable.** The only candidate unexplorable residue is whether any attribution matches what it is like for the other. That is the general other-minds asymmetry, owned by [[voids-between-minds]], and this void must not claim it.
- **Reason axis** (per [[taxonomy-of-voids]]): *adaptive / naturally occluded*. The template is cheap and usually right among similar minds. Nickerson says imputation "serves them well in general" [raw], and Dawes treats it as rational for a sample of one. Its costs fall at the margins of the reader's exposure.

**A candidate structural sub-kind (the note's own suggestion).** [[inventory-blindness]] says absent capacities produce no signal of their absence. The template void is stronger in one direction: a misfit can produce a *false positive signal about the world*, a reading or a trait, not merely silence. Call it an **exported void**: a limit of the knower recorded as a property of the known. Whether other catalogue entries share this shape (Barfield's logomorphism in [[voids-between-minds]] and `inaccessible-past` is the obvious candidate) is a question for the taxonomy, not something this note settles.

### Evidence for the Limit

**Robust:**
- Egocentric adjustment and false consensus in general populations, which survive education and feedback (Epley et al. 2004; Krueger & Clement 1994; Ross et al. 1977).
- A same-group advantage in reading emotion across cultures, meta-analytically, which shrinks with exposure (Elfenbein & Ambady 2002, against Matsumoto's challenge and their reply).

**Supported, narrower:**
- Same-group advantages in reading mental states across neurotypes, asymmetric in direction (Edey et al. 2016; Sheppard et al. 2016).
- The majority/minority asymmetry in Elfenbein & Ambady matches the Edey asymmetry, which fits an exposure-calibrated template. This match is the note's inference, not either paper's claim.

**Not supported, or contested:**
- That cross-neurotype *communication* is generally degraded. The 2025 registered report found no difference in information transfer.
- That the failure is *double* (symmetric). Edey et al. found it one-directional.
- That negative trait impressions are template misfit. DeBrabander et al. found autistic raters share them.
- That double empathy is a well-specified psychological construct (Livingston et al. 2025; Fellowes 2026; contrast Kilgallon et al. 2026).

**The displacement signature specifically** (misfit recorded as a property of the other) has three sources. Its clearest statement is Edey et al.'s interpretive sentence. Its strongest worked case is historical and community-scale: the deficit model of autism, which Gernsbacher & Yergeau (2019) argue failed on its own evidence. Its cleanest individual-scale support is Sheppard et al.'s "differently but not less expressively". It is **under-tested** at the individual level. No study found in this session asks readers, after a failed cross-architecture reading, whether they experienced the failure as their own limit or as a fact about the target. That gap is a falsifier slot (below).

### Phenomenology

The misfit has no felt edge, which is what keeps the void dark. Epley et al.'s adjusters stop "once a plausible estimate is reached" [raw]: the reading feels finished at plausibility. Keysar et al.'s participants knew the director's ignorance and acted against that knowledge, without, on the paper's account, noticing the conflict. Sasson et al.'s impressions form "within seconds" [raw]. That timing holds whatever the impressions track. DeBrabander's control shows they do not track reader-template misfit, so this item supports only the "no felt edge" point.

This conflicts with the Map's existing phenomenology of understanding others. [[consciousness-and-social-understanding]] L77 describes "limits awareness" as "reaching and not quite touching", and says that across "neurological types ... understanding becomes harder and more approximate". The template-void evidence suggests that at architecture boundaries the default readout is a completed reading, not felt reaching. Limits-awareness looks like an *achievement* under conditions (incentives, disclosure, exposure), not the default (see "Where the Map Is Exposed").

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods
- **Cross-reading designs run in both directions.** Matched versus mismatched reader–target pairs, with the accuracy of both directions reported (Edey et al. did this, and that is what exposed the asymmetry). This is the third-person method that makes the territory explorable.
- **Registered, multi-site replications.** The 2020-to-2025 reversal shows single-site cross-neurotype effects are fragile.

### Indirect Methods
- **Manipulate the template's calibration.** Vary exposure (Elfenbein & Ambady's moderator), accuracy incentives (Epley Study 3) and disclosure (Crompton 2025; DeBrabander 2019), and track which reading errors move.
- **Ask for the readout.** After a failed cross-architecture reading, ask readers what they experienced: their own limit, or a property of the target. This directly tests the displacement signature, and no study found here does it.
- **Apophatic triangulation.** Map where one reader population's confident attributions systematically disagree with the target's self-report *and* with matched-architecture readers. Where two of three disagree, the gap is located in the reader (see [[apophatic-cartography-four-criteria]]).

### What AI Might See (speculative)
- An LLM reader has no first-person template in the human sense. A plausible conjecture is that its default model is its training distribution, so it would inherit majority-norm templates rather than lack one. This is untested here and must be presented as a conjecture.
- The cross-system case runs both ways. Humans reading AI, and AI reading humans, each use a template neither side built for the other. The void applies to the Map's own attributions: a felt absence of mind in a system, or a felt presence, is a template output before it is evidence.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet
**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]**, as illustration, not evidence. "Other minds are like mine" is the simplest model available to a reader with a sample of one, and Dawes's argument makes it rational. The template void is the case where the simplest model is reasonable and systematically misleading at its margins. The deficit model of autism is the institutional version: a single-locus hypothesis read off one-sided data, which relational designs began to qualify. The relational alternative is itself contested (Livingston et al. 2025), so this illustrates Tenet 5 and does not support it.

### Other Tenets
- **[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]**: fits without evidencing. If experience is given from inside and others' only by inference, a self-template is structurally unavoidable, which the Map's account predicts. A physicalist predictive-processing account (Bolis et al. 2017) explains the same structure fully at the level of mechanism. The void is framework-independent and, under the common-cause null in [[voids-as-evidence]], adds no cumulative weight for the tenets.
- **[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]**: not engaged. **Guard**: the void concerns *attributions* of mental states, never their causal role. "The reader cannot tell what the other is undergoing" must not slide into "what the other undergoes does nothing". Do not concede epiphenomenalism.
- **[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]**: a faint link only. The template is indexical (one's own mind as the default case). Do not develop it.
- **[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]]**: no connection.

### Implications
The Map makes consciousness attributions throughout (animals, infants, AI systems, other humans). The template void says each such attribution is made from a template, and its misfit can show up as a fact about the target. This is a calibration demand on the Map's own method: report attributions together with the reader-template they were made from.

## Four-Criteria Scoring ([[apophatic-cartography-four-criteria]])

This is the note's own grading, by one hand, so subject to the same-hand caveat:
- **Signature specificity: moderate.** "A misfit recorded as a property of the target" is recognisable from a description of it. But the individual-level displacement evidence is thin (see "Evidence for the Limit"), and the trait-judgment route is confounded (DeBrabander).
- **Structured persistence: strong** for egocentric adjustment: decades of findings, "ineradicable" in Krueger & Clement's title, and survival under education and feedback. **Moderate** for the cross-architecture face, where one key result has failed to replicate.
- **Cross-observer convergence: moderate-to-strong.** Social psychology, psycholinguistics, cross-cultural emotion research, autism research, primatology and AI-attribution research converge on the egocentric default and on same-group advantages. They do not converge on displacement.
- **Framework independence: strong.** The void holds on simulation theory, theory-theory and rationality theory (Wallin 2011), and on physicalist and dualist accounts alike.

## Potential Article Angles

1. **"The Template Void": a reader's limit recorded at the other's address.** Open with the statement and the exported-void structure. Present the egocentric default (Epley, Krueger & Clement, Keysar with Rubio-Fernández). Then the three faces in order of evidential strength: cross-cultural (Elfenbein & Ambady, with Matsumoto), cross-neurotype (Edey, Sheppard, the 2020/2025 pair, DeBrabander, the critiques and the restatement), and cross-species/system (de Waal by paraphrase; Colombatto & Fleming). **Writer guards:**
- (a) Never present the double-empathy problem as established. Always pair Crompton 2020 with the 2025 non-replication, and cite Livingston et al. 2025 and Fellowes 2026 alongside Kilgallon et al. 2026's restatement.

- (d) Language: identity-first "autistic people" / "non-autistic people" (Milton 2012's stated convention). No deficit framing in the Map's own voice. Report sample limits (DeBrabander: predominantly Caucasian and male; no participants with intellectual disability).
- (e) Never classify the void as unexplorable. The unexplorable residue belongs to [[voids-between-minds]].
- (f) Dualism: fits, does not evidence. Give the physicalist predictive-processing reading (Bolis et al.) a fair statement.
- (g) Epiphenomenalism guard, as above.
- (h) Quote only `[raw]` strings from this note. De Waal 1999, Goldman 2006, Ross et al. 1977 and Dawes 1989 are paraphrase-only. Dawes's stance comes via Krueger & Clement's raw abstract, and Goldman's via Wallin's.
- (i) Scope every absence claim ("no study asks…", "the catalogue lacks…") to the searches named here.
- (j) Leave the state face with [[cross-state-void]], the knowledge face with [[conceptual-metabolism-void]] and the success face with [[boundary-and-projection]]. Link to them and do not re-explain them.
- (k) The AI face is speculative. Colombatto & Fleming measure attribution rates, not correctness.
- (l) Style: avoid the "This is not X. It is Y." construct and the intensifier "load-bearing" (writing-style guide).

**Falsifiers / deflators:**
- Registered, multi-site cross-reading studies find equal accuracy across matched and mismatched pairs once expression style and familiarity are controlled. Then the cross-architecture face deflates to unfamiliarity that exposure removes: still unexplored, but shallow.
- Readers asked after failed cross-architecture readings reliably report the failure as their own limit ("I could not read them"), not as a property of the target. Then the displacement signature fails, and the void collapses into the general asymmetry owned by [[voids-between-minds]].
- Egocentric adjustment is eliminated by brief instruction, contrary to Krueger & Clement and Keysar et al. Then the occluded readout fails.
- Trait and reading errors prove reader-invariant across all reader architectures, as DeBrabander already found for trait judgments. Then they are norm-driven, not template-driven, and the displacement would have to be found elsewhere or abandoned.
