## Executive Summary

David Ray Griffin's *nondualistic interactionism* is the mind–body doctrine of his Whiteheadian panexperientialism. It holds two things:
- the mind is **numerically distinct** from the brain and the two interact both ways;
- the mind is **not different in ontological kind** from the brain's constituents, because neurons, cells and molecules are themselves (low-grade) subjects of experience.

The mind is "that temporal society of dominant occasions of experience" that arises "out of the activities of billions of neurons" (1998, p. 186 [raw-2]). It is the presiding member of a *compound individual*, a term Griffin takes from Hartshorne. A rock, by contrast, is an *aggregational society* "devoid of a dominant member" (p. 181 [raw-2]). Griffin's 1997 statement is the cleanest: "It is interactionism, in that the mind is numerically distinct from brain, both influencing and being influenced by it. But it is not dualism, in that the mind is different only in degree, not in ontological kind, from the neurons comprising the brain" [raw].

**For the Map the view is the hardest close neighbour.** It is the one irreducibility-respecting rival that passes every test `positions/arguments-for-dualism` P-D2 and `positions/arguments-for-mental-causation` P-MC3 name for selecting the Map's dualism:
- it is not an inert-consciousness view;
- it denies the completeness of physics;
- it has two numerically distinct relata, so the idealism exclusion does not reach it.

What separates it from the Map is not Tenet 3. The separation comes from:
- (i) the Map's further claim of a difference *in kind*, which [[tenets#^dualism|Tenet 1]]'s stated neutrality does not supply and which pairing Tenet 1 with Tenet 3 does not deliver either;
- (ii) Tenet 2's minimal, quantum, outcome-selection interface, which Griffin does not need and whose conservation constraint he is prepared to discount;
- (iii) the Map's persisting-subject and independence-with-coupling commitments;
- (iv) the Map's refusal of panexperientialism on combination-problem grounds.

Kim (1999) and Chalmers (2003) supply the two standard published pressure points:
- Kim asks for the composition principle that would turn neuronal experiences into a compound mind;
- Chalmers observes that a high-level-subject view that escapes epiphenomenalism by denying microphysical closure "would be indistinguishable from type-D dualism". By Chalmers's own criterion that blurs the steelman's type-F placement of Griffin.

- **URL**: https://consc.net/papers/nature.html. Crossref: 10.1002/9780470998762.ch5 (pp. 102–142, 2003 confirmed).
- **Type**: Book chapter.
- **Key points**:
  - In §11 Chalmers lists Griffin 1998 among "Versions of type-F monism" [raw]. The Map's steelman L85 and L104 cite exactly this.
  - In the same section he argues that a type-F monist who puts phenomenal properties at a *high* level to dodge the combination problem must deny microphysical causal closure: "The only way to embrace this position would seem to be in combination with a denial of microphysical causal closure, holding that there are fundamental dispositions above the microphysical level, which have phenomenal properties as their grounds. - **Why it matters**: on the Map's reading, Griffin's view meets the condition Chalmers states. Chalmers does not say so, and his list files Griffin under type-F. Griffin's dominant occasion is a high-level subject with its own causal relation to the body: it exercises more power over the society than any other member does (Q9), and neurons take guidance from it by prehension (Q13). Which criterion settles the label is the question of kind, and a type-D placement would not by itself supply the Map's claim of a difference in kind.

## What the Doctrine Claims

**1. Two theses, one kept.** Griffin splits "dualism" into a numerical thesis (the mind is an actuality distinct from the brain) and an ontological thesis (it differs in kind from the brain's constituents) (Q2). Cartesian dualism affirms both. Materialism denies both. Nondualistic interactionism affirms the numerical thesis and denies the ontological one (Q1, Q3). Griffin's complaint about the debate's vocabulary is that treating "interactionism" as a synonym for dualism hides this option (Q3). The Map's own tenet text makes the same move: "Combined with the Bidirectional Interaction tenet below, this commits the Map to [[interactionist-dualism]]—the position that mind and body are distinct yet causally connected" ([[tenets]], Dualism definition [Map]).

**2. The mind as a temporal society of dominant occasions.** The mind is a "distinct actuality" only in a Whiteheadian sense:
- it is a serially ordered society of momentary occasions of experience, each constituted by its prehensions (Q6);
- it is not "numerically one through time", which Griffin says "exaggerates its distinctness from the brain" (Q5);
- its distinctness is numerical distinctness from "the highest-level enduring individuals (the neurons)" (Q4).

The mind arises out of neuronal activity (Q10). Its occasions are "presiding, regnant, or dominant" because they exercise more power over the society than any other member does (Q9).

**3. Compound individuals versus aggregational societies.**
- *Compound individuals* have a dominant member that unifies response and action: animals, cells, perhaps organelles, molecules and atoms (pp. 186–187).
- *Aggregational societies* such as sticks, stones and billiard balls have none (Q8). Their members' spontaneities average out under the "law of large numbers" (p. 186).

This "organizational duality" stands in for ontological dualism (Q11). Griffin places the human mind's novelty in "an extreme exemplification of a kind of causal relation that had become increasingly important in the evolution of the higher animals", the self-correction that completes Q12, and the difference from other minds is "one of degree, not of kind" (Q17). Griffin credits the term "compound individual" to Hartshorne (p. 82).

**4. Interaction through prehension.** Influence runs both ways, by the general Whiteheadian mechanism:
- the mind's occasions prehend neuronal occasions;
- neuronal occasions prehend the mind's appetitions, "just as our brain cells derive guidance from the mind by prehending its appetitions" (Q13).

Griffin affirms supervenience only "in an interactionist sense": changes in the brain change the mind, *and* changes in the mind change the brain (p. 236).

**5. Closure kept, completeness denied.** Ch. 10 recasts the view as "panexperientialist physicalism" (Q22; the chapter title).
- It keeps the closure of the physical domain to *non-physical* causes. Every actual entity is physical-mental, so minds belong to the causal nexus (Q14).
- It denies that physical theory can in principle give a complete account of bodily motion (Q16).
- Griffin is explicit that both materialist and panexperientialist physicalists reject *dualistic* interactionism (Q15).

Kim (1999) replies that the label "physicalism" is misleading. The Map should follow Kim on the label and Griffin on the substance: the view denies physics' completeness, which is what matters for P-MC3.

**6. Conservation of energy.** Griffin does not route interaction through a quantum opening. His answers to the conservation objection are two:
- (a) the conservation law's absolute universality is not known, and the problem "has been greatly exaggerated" (Q18; again at Q20);
- (b) enlarge "energy" to include the mind's "psychic energy", which he equates with Whiteheadian creativity (Q19, following W. D. Hart).

Quantum indeterminacy appears in the book, but in different roles:
- room for an "iota of mentality" in elementary particles, citing Seager and Bohm–Hiley's "rudimentary mental pole" (pp. 199–200);
- the background against which the law of large numbers fails for compound individuals (pp. 186, 214).

The mind's reach into the body's particles gets one footnote (p. 191n [raw-2]). It borrows Walter Elsasser's point that "the laws of quantum mechanics provide in general only necessary conditions for the phenomena and not sufficient ones, that is, they do not uniquely determine the happenings on the microscopic level". From this Griffin concludes that it is "fully consistent with the empirical facts and quantum theory" for living organisms to follow principles beyond those of inorganic matter, which backs the body text's claim that the mind's influence on the body's constituents "violates no laws of physics" (p. 193). No mechanism is given, and the indeterminacy is not developed into a site where the mind acts on the brain.

**7. Freedom.** The book's driver is the "hard-core commonsense" presupposition of freedom as "a choice among alternatives in the moment" (p. 164). Compound individuals with dominant members are the only places where self-determination is not averaged away.

**8. Psi data.** Ch. 5 counts as data "The Apparent Capacity of the Mind for Nonsensory Perception" and "… to Exert Extraordinary Causal Efficacy" (psychokinesis; index: 45, 60). A Map article should mention this scope only in passing, if at all. It should not import it as support.

| Emergent dualism (Hasker, [[emergent-dualism]]) | yes (emergent individual) | yes | two-way | no |
| Panpsychist identity / dual-aspect / type-F ([[russellian-monism]]) | no (the mind is the brain's intrinsic side) | no | none distinct from the physical | yes (the threat is epiphenomenal quiddities) |
| Analytic idealism ([[idealism]]) | no physical relatum independent of mind | n/a | intra-mental | n/a |

- **Against Cartesian dualism**, Griffin keeps distinctness and two-way causation but removes heterogeneity. The pairing and "how can unlike act on unlike" halves of the [[pairing-problem|interaction problem]] lapse, because like acts on like through prehension. What remains is the energy question, which he answers by enlarging energy.
- **Against identity panpsychism and dual-aspect theory**, Griffin asks explicitly "why is a two-aspect view of the relation of mind to brain not adequate?" (Q7). He answers from the unity of experience and from self-determining agency, which a rock's apparent unity lacks (pp. 172–173). The dominant occasion is a *further* actual entity, not the brain known from within. This is why Chalmers's high-level-subject caveat bites: the view escapes epiphenomenalism only by giving that further entity its own causal role, which means denying microphysical closure.
- **Against emergent dualism**, both views make the mind arise from neural activity and act back on it. Hasker's emergent self differs in kind. Griffin's does not (Q12, Q17). On the Map's dependence-versus-independence axis (`emergent-dualism`, "The decisive divergence" [Map]), Griffin sits with Hasker on *dependence*: no brain, no dominant occasions. That is a second point where the Map diverges from him.

## Tenet Assessment

- **Tenet 1 (Dualism / irreducibility): satisfied as the tenet is worded.**
  - The tenet requires that consciousness "is not reducible to physical processes" and is "neutral between substance and property dualism", with "something beyond the material—whether substance, property, or aspect" [Map].
  - Griffin's experience is irreducible to what physics describes, since physics captures only the outer aspect of occasions.
  - His "panexperientialist physicalism" label is the main obstacle, and Kim rejects it.
  - The headline "There is more than just matter involved in reality" also holds on Griffin's view. Matter conceived as vacuous actuality does not exist for him, and every actuality has a mental pole.
  - The steelman's 2026-10-02 stability note is the relevant ruling: "the Dualism tenet does not by itself say 'in kind'" [Map, `reviews/deep-review-2026-10-02-the-steelman-for-process-monism` L126].
- **Tenet 2 (Minimal Quantum Interaction): not satisfied as stated, but not contradicted at the level of possibility.** "Unclear" undersells the divergence. There are three points of friction.
  - (a) The dominant occasion's influence is neither minimal nor quantum-located. It "exercise[s] greater power over the society as a whole than do any of the other members" (Q9).
  - (b) Griffin is willing to doubt the conservation laws (Q18, Q20) where Tenet 2's Rules-out clause forbids "conservation-law violation" outright. His enlargement-of-energy route is a reinterpretation rather than a violation, but it is untested.
  - (c) Tenet 2 speaks of *non-physical* influence. On Griffin's ontology nothing is non-physical in kind.
  - A Whiteheadian *could* graft a quantum locus onto the view, mapping each neuronal occasion's resolution of its own indeterminacy onto outcome-selection. The Map's `subjective-aim` L66 and `process-philosophy` L132 already discuss Stapp's attempt at such a mapping, flagged as contested [Map]. Griffin himself does not make the graft.
- **Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction): satisfied on the headline and on the existential and universal readings.**
  - The mind acts on the brain and the brain on the mind (p. 236; Q13).
  - The Tenet 3 *Definition* goes further: "a mechanism by which the non-physical influences physical outcomes—consciousness biasing quantum indeterminacy in the brain", committing to "*outcome-selection*" [Map, `tenets` L91]. Griffin satisfies that mechanism clause only if the graft above is made.
  - That clause is mechanism content, graded through the quantum-interface register, not the argued core of Tenet 3. The point matters for P-D2.
- **Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds): compatible, and unaddressed.** There are zero hits for "many worlds", "many-worlds" or "Everett" in the book. Whiteheadian concrescence actualises one outcome per occasion, and Griffin's libertarian freedom presupposes real alternatives with one realised (p. 164). The view is a natural single-world ally.
- **Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits): cuts both ways.**
  - Griffin numbers his charges against dualism. The first is interaction: "The chief problem of dualism has always been to understand how two totally different types of things could causally influence each other" (p. 49 [raw-2]). The second is continuity: dualism "violates the principle of continuity in a particularly egregious way" (Q21). He also leans on avoiding "the Great Exception" (p. 92). The continuity charge and the Great Exception appeal are uniformity and parsimony-style heuristics, which Tenet 5 counts as unreliable when knowledge is incomplete.
  - Symmetrically, the Map cannot call panexperientialism unparsimonious.
  - Kim's "wish list" charge is a parsimony charge as well. It applies equally to the Map's posits.
  - Griffin's appeal to "hard-core commonsense notions" as non-negotiable is close kin to the Map's [[self-stultification]] reasoning.

## The P-D2 Question

**What P-D2 says** [Map, `positions/arguments-for-dualism` L55–68]:
- Convergence earns *irreducibility* across "the rivals that respect it (panpsychism, neutral monism, idealism, Madhyamaka emptiness)".
- "[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]] then selects dualism from within that set, on the strength of a separately-supported argument (the self-stultification objection to epiphenomenalism and the evidence for mental causation)".
- The 2026-08-02 update scopes the selector: "The selection step needs only the *existential* claim — that the consciousness doing the theorising is causally efficacious — because what it must exclude is the inert-consciousness reading of the irreducibility-respecting rivals".
- The *Depends on* field names [[positions/arguments-for-mental-causation|P-MC3]] as supplying "the exclusion criterion the selection step operates with".
- P-MC3's diagnostic: any theory that (1) treats consciousness as real and distinct and (2) accepts that physical events always have sufficient physical causes ends with (3) consciousness doing nothing, "unless it identifies a specific point at which consciousness makes a causal difference that physical description misses". The Map escapes by denying (2) [Map, L81].

**Why the selector cannot exclude Griffin's view.** Each exclusion device the register names fails to reach it:
1. **The inert-consciousness exclusion (P-D2's own scoping).** Griffin's consciousness is not inert. He affirms its efficacy as hard-core common sense, rejects even "partial epiphenomenalism", and builds downward causation into the mechanism (Q13, p. 236). The existential claim the selector relies on is one Griffin asserts.
2. **The P-MC3 diagnostic.** Griffin denies premise (2) in the sense that matters: physics cannot in principle give a complete causal account of living bodies (Q16). He also names the difference-making point: the dominant occasion's partly self-determined appetitions, prehended by neurons, in compound individuals where the law of large numbers does not wash them out (Q9, Q13; p. 214). That is the same escape route the Map takes, denying completeness, minus the quantum locus.
   - P-MC3's survey says "panpsychism inherits the problem at the macro level through the combination problem". That does not describe this variant. Griffin's macro-level subject is a *further* actual entity with its own causal role, so the combination problem returns as a *composition* problem (Kim's demand), not as an *inertness* problem. Chalmers 2003 makes the same structural point from the other side.
   - P-MC3's survey conjunct ("no rival surveyed has done it", moderate/C) holds only because this rival was not surveyed. The entry itself concedes the surveyed set is "non-exhaustive".
   - P-MC3's first shift condition does not fire literally: "a rival secured genuine causal work for consciousness while retaining causal completeness". Griffin does not retain completeness. He gets out the way the Map does.
3. **The two-relata exclusion used against idealism** (`idealism` L107–113: "influence requires two relata … its commitment to bidirectional interaction presupposes something genuinely physical for consciousness to interact with" [Map]). Griffin has two numerically distinct relata. Neurons are physical in every sense physics uses, since physics describes their outer, physical pole. The idealism exclusion therefore does not reach him either.

**So, on P-D2's own terms, Bidirectional Interaction selects *interactionism*, not dualism.** Griffin's nondualistic interactionism lies inside the selected set. The phrase "selects dualism among the irreducibility-respecting alternatives" overstates what the argued selector delivers. The step from interactionism to dualism-in-kind needs a further premise. The candidates are:
- **(a) The Map's difference-in-kind claim.** Tenet 1 is officially neutral on it, and the steelman's L85 says it is "drawn from pairing that tenet with Bidirectional Interaction". Griffin shows that the pairing does not deliver it: distinct-yet-causally-connected holds on his view without any difference in kind.
- **(b) Tenet 2's mechanism clause** (non-physical, minimal, quantum outcome-selection). It would exclude Griffin as he states the view. But it is not what P-D2 says does the selecting, and it carries the quantum-interface register's moderate credence and grade D (P-Q1), not P-D2's high credence.
- **(c) The Map's independent refusal of panexperientialism** (combination problem; Kim's composition demand). This is argued on `panpsychism` and `panpsychisms-combination-problem`, not in the P-D2 selector.
- **(d) The persisting-subject and independence-with-coupling commitments** ([[where-the-substance-commitment-enters]]; `emergent-dualism`). These are downstream agency-cluster posits that Tenet 1 states it does not entail.

**Is it "the one rival" the selector cannot exclude?** The review's claim is that P-D2's selection rule cannot exclude this one rival. Read with care, the answer depends on how the selector is read:
- On P-D2's literal 2026-08-02 scoping (exclude only *inert* consciousness), **idealism escapes too**: under analytic idealism consciousness does all the causing. Idealism is then excluded by the separate two-relata argument on the `idealism` page, not by the selector.
- On the fuller reading (two-way causation between numerically distinct relata, plus P-MC3's diagnostic), Griffin's view is the one listed-family member, under "panpsychism", that passes every test.

So the claim is correct for the fuller reading. A positions edit should say which reading it uses.

**What this does and does not disturb in P-D2:**
- Its *counting rule* (the two earnings do not compound) is untouched. If anything, Griffin shows the selection earns even less than the entry says, which strengthens the non-compounding conclusion.
- Its credence (high) attaches to the counting rule and need not move.
- What is at risk is the *Asserts* wording "selects dualism". No listed shift condition fires exactly. The nearest is the second ("the irreducibility-respecting rivals were shown to be excluded by the convergence itself, so that no selection step were needed"), and it points the other way.
- A faithful repair would rescope the clause to "selects interactionism; the step to dualism-in-kind rests on [a]–[d]", or else add Griffin as a named rival the selector leaves standing.

## Key Debates

### Is nondualistic interactionism a form of physicalism?
- **Sides**: Griffin (1997; 1998, ch. 10) says it can be called "panexperientialist physicalism". Kim (1999) says it is "misleading to regard panexperientialism as any form of physicalism", because it violates ontological physicalism, supervenience and closure as intended.
- **Core disagreement**: whether "physical" can be redefined to include experiential actualities.
- **Current state**: terminological, unresolved. For the Map, the substantive point is that the view denies physics' completeness, whatever it is called.

### Can compound individuals be built from experiencing parts?
- **Sides**: Griffin and Hartshorne (the dominant occasion emerges and unifies). Kim (1999), who demands an "independently motivated and defended principle" mapping the component individuals and their structure R to the compound's mentality M. Chalmers (2003), for whom the combination problem is "easily the most serious problem for the type-F monist view".
- **Core disagreement**: whether emergence of a higher-level *subject* is an answer or a relabelling.
- **Current state**: open. This is the Map's best line against the view: [[combination-problem]] and [[panpsychisms-combination-problem]]. The new page must not overclaim it as a refutation, since the Map's own subject-coupling story faces a structurally related "how does a manifold host a simple subject" question (`emergent-dualism`, Rickabaugh paragraph [Map]).

### Type-F or type-D?
- **Sides**: Chalmers lists Griffin under type-F. Chalmers's own analysis says any high-level-subject view that escapes epiphenomenalism by denying microphysical closure is "indistinguishable from type-D dualism".
- **Core disagreement**: whether "dualism" is defined by where fundamental causation sits (Chalmers's structural criterion) or by difference in kind (Griffin's ontological criterion).
- **Current state**: unremarked in the literature consulted. This is the Map's most useful novel observation, and it should be framed as the Map's reading of Chalmers, not attributed to him as a claim about Griffin.

### Does interaction violate conservation of energy?
- **Sides**: Griffin (exaggerated; enlarge energy; freedom is better attested than universal conservation). The Map's Tenet 2 (no conservation-law violation; minimal quantum outcome-selection) and [[conservation-laws-and-mental-causation]].
- **Current state**: this is a real difference in risk tolerance between Griffin and the Map. It gives the clearest empirical-style contrast the new page can draw.
