## Executive Summary

The iconic/discursive debate asks what *kind* of vehicle perception uses. Iconic vehicles are image-like: their parts map onto parts of the scene and each part carries several features at once. Discursive vehicles are language-like: separate symbols combine. Block (2023) holds that perception is "constitutively iconic, nonconceptual, and nonpropositional" and cognition is not. Quilty-Dunn (2020b) argues for *perceptual pluralism*, on which perception also delivers discursive object representations. Both agree that iconic memory is iconic and that working memory is largely discursive (Quilty-Dunn 2020a; Block 2023, ch. 5).

On the richness question, format does offer a third option, but only at the level of representation. Rich iconic content that discursive working memory cannot hold is something rich and sparse theorists can both grant. Block himself splits overflow into two questions. Does perception have greater capacity than cognition? He answers "clearly yes". "Is the excess capacity conscious?" stays in dispute (p. 444). He grants that a global-workspace theorist "can accept a kind of overflow—overflow of perception (whether conscious or not) over cognition" (p. 445). The phenomenal question is untouched, but the cost of each answer shifts. Quilty-Dunn (2020a) shows that a format-based perception–cognition border, combined with the view that only working-memory contents are conscious, implies that "visual representations are never conscious".

For the Map this sharpens two things. In `voids/plenitude-void` it strengthens the "theory-dependence" feature without dissolving the void. In `concepts/phenomenal-overflow` it adds a reading the rivals list lacks: one that grants representational overflow and stays neutral on phenomenal overflow. Every author here is a physicalist or theory-neutral, and Block states "I hold that phenomenal consciousness is reducible to its physical basis" (p. 445). Format does not bear on dualism.

## Major Positions

### Perception is constitutively iconic (Block 2023)
- **Proponents**: Block. Dretske (1981), Carey (2009) and Kulvicki (2015) take format-based or analog approaches, per Quilty-Dunn (2020a).
- **Core claim**: perception is iconic, nonconceptual and nonpropositional by nature. Cognition is paradigmatically discursive and can carry percepts inside a "cognitive envelope". Working-memory "object files" are "conceptualized remnants of perception".
- **Richness**: perceptual capacity exceeds cognitive capacity ("clearly yes"). Whether the excess is conscious is argued separately, now chiefly through infant colour perception.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral on Dualism; Block is a reductive physicalist. The Map can use his account of what access *does* to a percept (conceptualizes it) without taking on his metaphysics.

### Perceptual pluralism (Quilty-Dunn 2020b; Green & Quilty-Dunn 2021)
- **Core claim**: perception delivers iconic representations and discursive, propositional object representations (object files).
- **Richness**: iconic memory is iconic and high-capacity whether or not its contents are conscious. The format of VWM does not settle whether its contents are visual.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral.

### Architectural border (Green 2020)
- The border is fixed by architecture (dimension restriction), not format. Penetration is allowed within strict limits. This is a rival to *both* format views on what the border is.

### Format-based deflation of overflow arguments (Gross & Flombaum 2017; Gross 2018)
- Probabilistic, flexible-resource models of short-term memory undercut post-cue overflow arguments, and they decouple the rich/sparse question from the access question. Quilty-Dunn (2020a) accepts flexible resources but keeps a format distinction between the stores.

## Key Debates

### Is object perception discursive?
- **Sides**: Green and Quilty-Dunn say yes, from OSPB evidence. Block says no: the OSPB probes working memory, not perception (pp. 248–255).
- **State**: ongoing. It was the centre of the 2025 *PPR* symposium (Quilty-Dunn, Beck, Gross, Schellenberg et al.; Block's replies).

### Does format mark the perception–cognition border?
- **Sides**: Block (format), Green (architecture), Beck (format cannot explain the border if icons occur on both sides), Hill (much cognition is iconic).
- **State**: ongoing. Block's own concession that working-memory representations can be iconic weakens the format border, and both Beck and Quilty-Dunn press this point.

### Is the excess perceptual capacity conscious?
- **Sides**: Block (yes, plausibly: infants, and "Report is not the criterion for consciousness"); cognitive theorists (no). Beck and Firestone & Phillips find a gap in the infant argument.
- **State**: unresolved. Block now takes "pointer" cognitive theories "more seriously" (précis).

## The Map's Question: A Third Option? {#map-question}

*Everything in this section is the Map's analysis, not any cited author's claim.*

**1. Yes, at the level of representation.** Three authors each pull a question the Map treats as one into two parts:
- Block separates *capacity* from *consciousness* (p. 444).
- Quilty-Dunn separates *being represented* from *being experienced* (2020a §3; 2020b §3).
- Gross separates the *rich/sparse* question from the *access* question (2018).

Together they make a representational thesis available: perception holds rich, unsegmented iconic content that discursive working memory cannot hold. Sparse theorists can accept it, and Block says global-workspace theorists can. That is a genuine third position. It is richer than access in what is *represented* and silent about what is *experienced*.

**2. No, on the phenomenal question.** Format concerns vehicles. Nothing in the format evidence shows the iconic surplus is felt, and Quilty-Dunn is explicit that his argument is "simply that it is encoded and stored". So format does not answer the question that makes the plenitude void a void. What it does is change the price of each answer:
- *Sparse + format border*: if only working-memory contents are conscious and working memory is discursive, then visual icons are "never conscious", which most theorists find hard to accept (Quilty-Dunn 2020a).
- *Sparse + pluralism*: the price falls, since discursive VWM contents can still be visual (Quilty-Dunn's own route).
- *Rich + format border*: overflow becomes the natural default (Quilty-Dunn's last sentence; Block).
The richness verdict is thus tied to a second disputed verdict, about where the perception–cognition border lies. That compounds the plenitude void's "theory-dependence" feature without resolving it.

**3. A format-specific version of the check-and-find loop.** On Block's account, cognitive access *reformats*. To conceptualize a percept for access is "to turn it into a perceptual judgment" (p. 421), and cognitive use wraps percepts in a discursive "envelope" (précis/replies). A report is then a discursive product of a format change, so introspective report cannot sample the iconic state's format. Even someone who accepts Block's architecture loses the step from "I report seeing every letter" to "every letter was experienced". This supports `voids/plenitude-void`'s self-concealing structure, and `voids/palette-extension-void` already uses the same idea for novel colour channels ("a report is a discursive rendering"). It is the Map's inference from Block's architecture, which Block does not draw.

**4. Two different "format" axes; do not merge them.** `voids/plenitude-void`'s "Ensemble Reframe" uses *format* for **pooled statistics versus item-level** representation (Cohen, Dennett & Kanwisher 2016). The iconic/discursive axis is about **vehicle structure** (analog and holistic versus symbol-combining). The axes interact rather than coincide. Quilty-Dunn reads ensemble coding as computed *from* iconic item encodings, "perceptual icons function as inputs", which points towards representational richness *beneath* the summary statistic. Cohen, Dennett & Kanwisher read ensembles as a sparse resource. Neither reading settles the phenomenal question.

**5. What this does to phenomenal-overflow's rivals list.** The "Rival Readings" section lists two readings that grant Sperling's data and deny overflow: partial awareness (Kouider et al.) and postdictive, cue-sensitive experience (Phillips). The format reading is a different kind of entry. It *grants* overflow of representation, explains it (iconic storage), and stays neutral on overflow of phenomenology. It also differs from partial awareness, whose "levels" are levels of representational hierarchy, not formats. Gross & Flombaum (2017) belong nearby as the challenge to the tiered-store model that Quilty-Dunn answers.

**6. Bearing on the tenets.** None of this favours Dualism. It *lowers* overflow's dualist relevance, since the robust part of the Sperling evidence is now cashed out entirely in representational, physically realisable terms. That matches `concepts/phenomenal-overflow`'s current calibration ("fits the tenet without establishing it"), and the fold should keep that grade. For Occam's Razor Has Limits, the format debate is a case where the simpler story (one capacity, one question) hides two questions.
