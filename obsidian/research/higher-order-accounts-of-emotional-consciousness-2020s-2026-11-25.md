## Executive Summary

The 2020s form of the higher-order account of emotion is a research programme, not a new theory. It is centred on Joseph LeDoux, with Hakwan Lau, Vincent Taschereau-Dumouchel, Stefan Hofmann and Matthias Michel. Its theoretical core is still LeDoux and Brown (2017), which the Map already reports on three pages. Four things are new since 2020, and the Map carries none of them:

1. **The discordance case.** Self-reported fear, defensive behaviour and physiology often come apart. Taschereau-Dumouchel et al. (2022) report that self-report shares "at most" about 27–28% of its variance with other measures. They read the gap as two circuits running in parallel from the same threat.
2. **A terminological rule.** "Fear" names only the experience. The rest is "threat" or "defense" response. The authors present this as following from the view that "subjective fear … is what the emotion fear is".
3. **A softer animal position.** LeDoux (2022) adopts Tulving's anoetic/noetic/autonoetic partition. He allows anoetic, fringe experience in other mammals, possibly back to early vertebrates, while keeping fear itself cortical and cognitive. On anoetic states of the internal milieu he cites Vandekerckhove and Panksepp, so at the anoetic level the two sides are closer than the Map's two-sided framing suggests.
4. **A survey that maps the rivals.** Engelen and Mennella (2023) sort the field into a "cognitive" and a "precognitive" camp. The precognitive camp itself splits: Panksepp holds subcortical emotion states are felt, while Berridge, Anderson and Adolphs, and Fanselow hold emotions "can be fully unconscious". Their verdict is that the evidence does not yet decide between the camps.

**The page that lacks the debate.** [[emotion-and-dualism]] lists Panksepp (1998) and LeDoux and Brown (2017) in its References, but its body names neither. The coalesce of 2026-03-17 (commit 2280ec9825) dropped the "Panksepp vs. LeDoux" section of the archived `topics/emotional-consciousness` but kept both references. Two pages still advertise the page in their Further Reading as "Valence and the Panksepp-LeDoux debate" ([[animal-consciousness]] L200, [[comparative-consciousness-and-interface-differences]] L230). Its open question on unconscious emotions (L163) states LeDoux's terminological move without crediting him.

**Two further pages present Panksepp with no companion.** [[valence-void]] L61 says Panksepp "identified" seven systems "each carrying intrinsic valence as a fundamental property". [[comparative-consciousness-and-interface-differences]] L141 says "Affective neuroscience suggests" subcortical origin, presenting one camp as the field's verdict.

**Tenet bearing, in short.** A higher-order account of *felt* emotion rivals Panksepp's phylogenetically deep, hedonic reading of valence. It adds no pressure on Dualism beyond what the Map already answers for higher-order theories in general. Its one real bite falls on the Map's claim that felt emotion tracks behaviour closely: the discordance data loosen that premise for fear, and the parallel-circuit model is a rival explanation of the alignment. That evidence is *compatible* with Bidirectional Interaction; it is not *suggestive* or *discriminating*.

## What the 2020s Work Adds

1. **Discordance and desynchrony as the evidence base** (Taschereau-Dumouchel et al. 2022, VERIFIED unless marked):
   - Patients can report high subjective fear with normal or low physiological threat responses, and others show the opposite.
   - Beta-blockers "can dampen the hyperreactivity of the autonomic nervous system … without necessarily affecting the subjective experience of anxiety".
   - "successful avoidance will typically lead to a decrease in fear reports while avoidance behaviors can persist over extended periods of time".
   - Self-report shares at most about 27–28% of variance with other measures (from Friedman et al. 2014; the primary is a LEAD).
   - The neural side is the authors' own 2020 decoding study (Taschereau-Dumouchel, Kawato & Lau 2020, abstract VERIFIED). Subjective fear and physiology "were correlated in general", but "the amygdala and insula appear to be primarily involved in the prediction of physiological reactivity, whereas … some areas in the prefrontal cortex, appear to be primarily predictive of the subjective experience of fear".
   - A 2024 follow-up (Taschereau-Dumouchel et al. 2024, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*, abstract VERIFIED) finds the ventral visual stream "may represent affectively neutral visual features … without representing the subjective experience of fear itself". On that reading, subjective fear "may depend on the relevant visual information triggering implicit metacognitive mechanisms in the prefrontal cortex".
2. **The parallel model made explicit.** Threat "often lead[s] to subjective fear via the higher-order circuit, and trigger[s] bodily reactions … via the defensive survival circuit, in parallel" (Fig. 1, VERIFIED). The higher-order model includes "emotion schema and self-schema, as well as meta-representations of semantic and episodic memories". It "can fully account for the subjective experience of fear, even in situations where the amygdala activity and body feedback are absent" (VERIFIED).
3. **The feeling is the emotion.** "subjective fear is not just another factor in the emotion fear; it is what the emotion fear is" (VERIFIED). The authors cite LeDoux (2015), LeDoux and Hofmann (2018) and Lieberman (2019) for this, so it is not new in 2022. Engelen and Mennella read HOTEC as implying that "since emotions can never be unconscious, individuals cannot be mistaken about the emotion that they are feeling" (VERIFIED as their reading; whether LeDoux and Brown say this was not checked). This is the strongest point of contact with emotion-and-dualism's open question at L163.
4. **The metacognitive operationalisation.** In Cushing et al. (2024) the higher-order monitoring of fear is "implicit, in that it happens automatically and largely beyond our volitional control" (VERIFIED). They take confidence as the experimental window. This answers the over-intellectualisation objection for emotion as Brown, Lau and LeDoux (2019) answered it for perception.
5. **The animal position softened.** LeDoux (2022), all VERIFIED:
   - "I do not deny consciousness in other animals. I am perfectly comfortable saying that some animals likely have conscious experiences".
   - Anoetic states are ones for which "non-conscious does not mean completely unconscious"; LeDoux and "others" have "proposed that penumbral/fringe anoetic states also represent the internal milieu". The "others" is ref. 136, Vandekerckhove & Panksepp (2011), *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 35, 2017–2025, "from anoetic affective experiences to noetic knowledge and autonoetic awareness" (metadata read from LeDoux's reference list; LEAD).
   - "orbitofrontal re-representation of olfactory-triggered amygdala states might constitute anoetic experiences", and in early vertebrates medial-pallium re-representation may have supported "amygdala-based anoetic experience of biologically significant stimuli".
   - Fear itself stays "a product of cortical cognitive circuits".
   - The whole section is flagged as "highly speculative".
   
   So the 2020s LeDoux grants a re-represented, non-cognitive affective awareness that Panksepp's tradition also named "anoetic". The live dispute has narrowed to whether *subcortical* activity alone suffices and whether the experience deserves the name of an emotion. The Map's two-sided framing ("Panksepp vs LeDoux") is now less precise than the literature.
6. **The direct answer to the decorticate and hydranencephaly evidence.** LeDoux (2022) on Merker (2007): Merker "used findings from patients with limited cortex due to hydrocephaly, and decorticated rats", and "consciousness is judged by sensory–motor responsiveness, not by evidence of mental states" (VERIFIED). He then suggests that "Such anoetic states may be what Merker had in mind" (VERIFIED). This is the citable 2020s form of the rejoinder that [[minds-without-words]] and [[animal-consciousness]] paraphrase as "reflexive". It is more concessive than "reflexive".
7. **The middle position, stated by its author.** "My model falls between basic and constructionist theories" (LeDoux 2022, VERIFIED). Innate defensive survival circuits stand on one side, a cortical, conceptual feeling on the other, and "arousal and valence" are "activities triggered by the particular innate survival circuit that is active at the moment" (VERIFIED).
8. **The named rivals in the fear literature** (all VERIFIED):
   - Fanselow & Pennington (2018) argue that autonomic, behavioural and cognitive-emotional responses "are best conceptualized as the unitary result of activation of a central fear generator". They warn that the two-system view means "all use of non-human animals to advance translation to the clinic in the study of fear can essentially be thrown out the window".
   - Taschereau-Dumouchel et al. quote Fanselow & Pennington: "the additional machinery needed to generate subjective report probably adds additional noise", and emphasising subjective experience would "push us back well over a century to what was truly the dark ages of psychiatry".
   - In Mobbs et al. (2019), Adolphs defines fear as "a psychological state with specific functional properties, conceptually distinct from conscious experience" and "a latent variable that provides a causal explanation of observed fear-related behaviors". Tye calls fear "an intensely negative internal state". LeDoux, in the same piece: "While other animals may have some kind of experience when in danger, it is not possible to scientifically measure what they experience".

## Major Positions

The 2020s literature crosses two questions, where feeling arises and whether emotion requires feeling, so it does not line up on one Panksepp–LeDoux axis.

### Subcortical and felt (Panksepp; Solms; Damasio & Damasio 2023)
- **Core claim**: Conserved subcortical systems generate phenomenal affect directly; emotional consciousness is phylogenetically deep.
- **Key evidence**: Decorticate behaviour, stimulation reinforcement, hydranencephaly (Merker).
- **Relation to tenets**: Fits the Map's wide distribution of valence, but the fit is not evidence ([[valence]] L39 is already graded this way).

### Cortical, higher-order, emotion = feeling (LeDoux & Brown 2017; Taschereau-Dumouchel et al. 2022; LeDoux 2022)
- **Core claim**: Subcortical survival circuits supply nonconscious inputs. The feeling is assembled by cortical re-representation. "Fear" names only the experience.
- **Key evidence**: Discordance, desynchrony and decoding dissociations.
- **Relation to tenets**: Rival on location and depth. Its identity form meets the Map's general reply to HOT.

### Cortical but first-order or constructionist (Smith & Lane's workspace account; Barrett)
- **Core claim**: Cortical, but no higher-order re-representation is needed. For Barrett, core affect is "probably shared" with animals.
- **Relation to tenets**: Already on the Map via Barrett ([[emotion-and-dualism]] L113–L115).

### Subcortical central states, emotion can be unconscious (Berridge; Anderson & Adolphs; Fanselow)
- **Core claim**: Emotions are central functional states, measured by behaviour; feeling is optional.
- **Relation to tenets**: Opposes Panksepp's phenomenal reading from the other side. [[valence]] carries Berridge.

## Key Debates

### Is the amygdala a fear generator?
- **Sides**: Fanselow & Pennington (yes, one system) vs LeDoux & Pine and Taschereau-Dumouchel et al. (no, a defensive survival circuit in parallel with the feeling).
- **Core disagreement**: Whether discordance is measurement noise in self-report or evidence of two constructs.
- **Current state**: Ongoing. Engelen and Mennella (2023) add that interoceptive and exteroceptive fear partly dissociate in their amygdala dependence (citing Feinstein et al. 2022; LEAD), which may explain mixed lesion findings.

### Self-report as gold standard
- **Sides**: LeDoux & Hofmann (2018) vs Adolphs & Anderson (2018), as quoted by Engelen and Mennella.
- **Current state**: The higher-order programme argues self-report is "the only valid indicator of fear as a subjective experience" by Borsboom et al.'s definition of validity. Michel (2024) generalises this as "validity drift".

### How deep does felt emotion go?
- **Sides**: Panksepp's tradition vs LeDoux.
- **Current state**: Narrowed by LeDoux's anoetic concession (2022). Still open on whether subcortical activity alone suffices. Birch (2024), already on the Map, grades the behavioural evidence.

## Relation to Site Perspective (graded per `project/evidential-status-discipline`)

- **Dualism: compatible.** HOTEC answers *which* brain states are felt and *where* feeling is assembled. That location question is neutral on what feeling is. Its identity form, feeling as cognitively assembled re-representation, is the functionalism [[higher-order-theories]] already says conflicts with Dualism (L158), and that page's reply (L77, L91: re-representation leaves unexplained why the result is felt) applies unchanged. The emotion case adds no new pressure on Dualism.
- **The hedonic reading of valence** (the Map's preferred account on [[valence]]): the 2020s work rivals the *Panksepp-hedonic* combination, intrinsic valence deep in phylogeny. It does not rival the hedonic account as such, since badness could be intrinsic to a cortically assembled experience. It *discriminates* on the location question (lesion, stimulation and decoding work can tell Panksepp's and LeDoux's predictions apart). It is merely *compatible* with both sides of the hedonic-vs-evaluativist question. Keep the question open as [[valence]] L39 states it.
- **Bidirectional Interaction: compatible, with one real counter-pressure.** [[emotion-and-dualism]] L87 and L91 lean on felt emotion tracking behaviour closely. For fear, the discordance data loosen that premise, and the parallel-circuit model explains the fear–threat alignment through a common cause. The alignment is then compatible with felt fear causing behaviour and does not show it (Fold B). LeDoux does not deny conscious emotion a role ("novel forms of behavioural control"). He denies it the defensive one. The asymbolia argument is unaffected. This is the same distinction today's `causal-assumption-critique-of-feelings-driving-behaviour-2026-11-25` research note draws for Key and Brown (2024), seen from the higher-order side.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction; No Many Worlds**: no bearing.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits: weakly illustrative.** The one-system "fear generator" model is the simpler one. Its defenders and its critics both argue from evidence, not parsimony. No tenet support should be claimed.
