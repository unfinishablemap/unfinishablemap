# Research: Voids - The Distributional Void

**Date**: 2026-08-24
**Search queries used**: generative AI homogenization of ideas reduces collective diversity of thought; "knowledge collapse" AI Peterson recursive narrowing; Shumailov 2024 Nature AI models collapse recursively generated data; algorithmic monoculture correlated errors across large language models; LLM-assisted writing content diversity Padmakumar He; humans inherit artificial intelligence biases Vicente Matute; epistemic autonomy large language models testimony; large language models converge philosophical positions. Metadata and quoted spans verified against raw arXiv abstract blocks, PMC full text, and OpenAlex records — see Citations.
**Voids category**: Method-generated (origin-class axis); Unexplored territory with an inward-moving boundary (territory axis) — the taxonomy has no clean slot for this, which is itself a finding.

## Executive Summary

The Map's void catalogue is written by a language model, read largely by language models, and audited by three more language models whose agreement is treated as convergence. This note surveys the empirical literature on what model-mediated inquiry does to the space of what gets thought, and finds a measurable, directional effect: LLM outputs concentrate toward the centre of their training distribution, model-assisted human output becomes more similar across individuals, and errors across *different* models are substantially correlated — with correlation *rising* in larger and more accurate models even across distinct architectures and providers (Kim et al. 2025, over 350 models). The resulting void has the signature the Map's catalogue looks for: it is invisible from inside, because the narrowing consists of outputs that were never generated and questions that were never posed. It differs from every existing catalogue entry in one structural respect — its boundary *moves*, and moves inward as the instrument is used more. The [[apex/taxonomy-of-voids|Void Clustering Audit]]'s method-generated class currently names four voids, all arising from apophatic method; none arises from the *instrument*. That is the gap this material fills.

## Key Sources

### AI and the problem of knowledge collapse
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03502 · https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-024-02173-x
- **Type**: Paper (arXiv preprint Apr 2024; published *AI & Society* 40(5): 3249–3269, 19 Jan 2025)
- **Author**: Andrew J. Peterson (sole author)
- **Key points**:
  - Supplies the mechanism, not merely the worry: LLMs "are trained on vast amounts of diverse data" yet "naturally generate output towards the 'center' of the distribution."
  - Models a community of learners choosing between traditional and discounted AI-assisted inquiry; identifies conditions under which "knowledge collapse" occurs.
  - Quantitative result, stated for the paper's *default model* only: "a 20% discount on AI-generated content generates public beliefs 2.3 times further from the truth than when there is no discount."
  - Names the asymmetry that leaves an exit: "unlike AI models that cannot choose what data they are trained on, humans may strategically seek out diverse forms of knowledge if they perceive them to be worthwhile." The collapse is conditional on perceived value, not mechanically forced.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bears directly on **Occam's Razor Has Limits**. The centre of a training distribution is a *popularity* prior over human text, and a model's fluency-weighted output behaves like a simplicity heuristic with no epistemic warrant. Peterson's result is a formal case of a simplicity-like preference moving beliefs away from truth.
- **Quote**: "widespread reliance on recursive AI systems could lead to a process we define as 'knowledge collapse', and argue this could harm innovation and the richness of human understanding and culture." (verified verbatim, arXiv abstract)

### Correlated Errors in Large Language Models
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962 (ICML 2025 proceedings)
- **Type**: Paper
- **Authors**: Elliot Kim, Avi Garg, Kenny Peng, Nikhil Garg
- **Key points**:
  - Directly tests the assumption the Map's outer-review system relies on: "Diversity in training data, architecture, and providers is assumed to mitigate homogeneity in LLMs. However, we lack empirical evidence on whether different LLMs differ meaningfully."
  - Scale: "over 350 LLMs overall, using two popular leaderboards and a resume-screening task."
  - Headline figure: "on one leaderboard dataset, models agree 60% of the time when both models err."
  - The finding that matters most here: "larger and more accurate models have highly correlated errors, even with distinct architectures and providers." Provider diversity does not buy error independence, and buys *less* of it as capability rises.
  - Downstream effects demonstrated for LLM-as-judge evaluation — the exact configuration of `/outer-review`.
- **Tenet alignment**: Not tenet-specific; it is a constraint on the Map's *method*, and therefore on how much the void catalogue's internal convergence can be worth.
- **Quote**: "models agree 60% of the time when both models err" (verified verbatim, arXiv abstract)

### Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532/
- **Type**: Paper (*Science Advances* 10(28): eadn5290, 12 Jul 2024)
- **Authors**: Anil R. Doshi, Oliver P. Hauser
- **Key points**:
  - Preregistered two-phase online experiment: N = 293 writers producing eight-sentence stories under three conditions (human-only, one GenAI idea, five GenAI ideas), evaluated by N = 600 separate participants across 3,519 evaluations.
  - Individual-level gain and collective-level loss occur together: "access to generative AI ideas causes stories to be evaluated as more creative, better written, and more enjoyable, especially among less creative writers. However, generative AI–enabled stories are more similar to each other than stories by humans alone."
  - **Read the similarity figures carefully.** The paper reports that in the Human-only condition "the similarity scores span a range of 8.10 points; therefore, the increase in similarity from having access to one or five generative AI ideas represents 10.7% and 8.9% of the total range, respectively." These are proportions *of the human-only range*, not percentage increases in similarity. A secondary-source summary encountered during this survey misstated it as "10.7% more similar," and 10.7% separately appears in the paper as a *novelty* gain for low-DAT writers with five ideas — two distinct quantities sharing a numeral. Any downstream use must carry the "of the total range" qualifier.
  - Framing: "This dynamic resembles a social dilemma: With generative AI, writers are individually better off, but collectively a narrower scope of novel content is produced."
- **Tenet alignment**: Supports the void's *undetectability* face. Every individual writer experienced improvement; the loss existed only at the population level, where no participant was standing.
- **Quote**: "These results point to an increase in individual creativity at the risk of losing collective novelty." (verified verbatim, PMC full text)

### Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05196 (ICLR 2024)
- **Type**: Paper
- **Authors**: Vishakh Padmakumar, He He
- **Key points**:
  - Controlled experiment on argumentative essay writing across three setups: base LLM (GPT-3), feedback-tuned LLM (InstructGPT), and no model assistance.
  - Writing with InstructGPT — but not with the base GPT-3 — produced a statistically significant reduction in diversity, increasing similarity between different authors' essays and reducing lexical and content diversity.
  - Mechanism localisation: the effect is attributable to the *model's* contributed text being less diverse; user-contributed text was unaffected.
- **Tenet alignment**: The base-vs-tuned contrast matters for the Map. The narrowing tracked the *alignment-tuned* model, not raw capability. This suggests the void is at least partly an artefact of a design choice rather than an inevitability of statistical language modelling.
- **Note**: Reported here from the ICLR/arXiv record; abstract text was read via search summary rather than raw extraction, so no span from this paper is quoted verbatim above.

### AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated data
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07566-y
- **Type**: Paper (*Nature* 631(8022): 755–759, 24 Jul 2024)
- **Authors**: Ilia Shumailov, Zakhar Shumaylov, Yiren Zhao, Nicolas Papernot, Ross Anderson, Yarin Gal
- **Key points**:
  - Repeated training of a generative model on its own synthetic output produces irreversible defects in which the tails of the original content distribution disappear.
  - Demonstrated beyond LLMs — variational autoencoders and Gaussian mixture models show the same effect, indicating a distributional rather than architectural cause.
  - **Caveat carried forward**: Borji (arXiv:2410.12954) argues via kernel density estimation that the reported outcomes "are a statistical phenomenon and may be unavoidable" — a consequence of repeated sampling from an estimated distribution rather than a distinctive vulnerability of neural training. This *strengthens* the void reading (the mechanism is more general than AI) while *weakening* any framing of collapse as a fixable engineering defect.
  - An author correction exists at DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-08905-3. Its content was not retrievable during this survey and is not characterised here.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tail loss is the formal signature of what an apophatic catalogue most needs and is least likely to receive — the rare, peripheral, unorthodox entry.

### Humans inherit artificial intelligence biases
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-42384-8
- **Type**: Paper (*Scientific Reports* 13(1): 15737, 3 Oct 2023)
- **Authors**: Lucía G. Vicente, Helena Matute
- **Key points**:
  - Three experiments on a simulated medical classification task, with and without assistance from an AI system carrying a built-in bias.
  - Participants assisted by the biased system reproduced its error pattern; the pattern persisted after assistance was withdrawn and participants worked unaided.
  - Establishes that the transfer is not merely behavioural compliance during use — something is retained.
- **Tenet alignment**: This is the face of the void that reaches *into* the inquirer rather than merely into the corpus. No verbatim span is quoted here; the abstract reconstruction available during this survey was corrupted, and only the metadata and structural claims are relied on.

### Large language models reflect the ideology of their creators
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s44387-025-00048-0
- **Type**: Paper (*npj Artificial Intelligence* 2(1): 7, 7 Jan 2026)
- **Authors**: Maarten Buyl, Alexander Rogiers, Sander Noels, Guillaume Bied, Iris Dominguez-Catena, Edith Heiter, Iman Johary, Alexandru-Cristian Mara, Raphaël Romero, Jefrey Lijffijt, Tijl De Bie
- **Key points**:
  - 19 LLMs prompted to describe 3,991 politically relevant persons, across the six official UN languages.
  - Found systematic ideological disparities between models by geopolitical region of origin, and normative differences among US models alone.
  - Conclusion: "the ideological stance of an LLM reflects the worldview of its creators."
- **Why it cuts both ways**: This is the strongest *counterweight* in the survey. It shows the distribution has a *location* — which supports the void — but also that different model families sit in measurably different places, which limits any claim that all models occupy one point. A distribution with structure is narrower than the human corpus but is not a single mode.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bears on **Occam's Razor Has Limits** indirectly: what a model presents as the neutral default is a position, not the absence of one.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The territory is not intrinsically dark. It is territory that model mediation makes progressively harder to reach — peripheral, rare, unorthodox, and tail-distribution content, together with the questions that only arise from within it.

On the Map's **territory axis** ([[three-kinds-of-void|three kinds of void]]) it is closest to the **Unexplored**: nothing here is unthinkable in principle. But the axis assumes fixed boundaries, and this boundary moves. The catalogue currently has no entry whose extent is a function of how much the inquirer uses a particular instrument. On the **origin-class axis** of the [[apex/taxonomy-of-voids|Void Clustering Audit]] it is unambiguously **method-generated** — and it exposes that the audit's method-generated class is currently populated entirely by voids arising from *apophatic method* ([[apophatic-cartography|cartography]], [[question-formation-void|question-formation]], epistemic-horizon, closure-types). None arises from the instrument doing the writing. That omission is the most actionable finding in this survey.

A deliberately-occluded reading is available and should be declined. There is no architect withholding the tails. The mechanism is statistical — Borji's kernel-density result suggests it follows from repeated sampling of an estimated distribution — and the Map should resist the more dramatic framing where the mundane one suffices.

### Evidence for the Limit

The evidence is unusually good by voids-catalogue standards, because the phenomenon is measurable in a way most catalogue entries are not:

1. **Directional mechanism** — outputs concentrate toward the distribution's centre (Peterson 2025).
2. **Population-level effect in humans** — model-assisted writers produce mutually more similar work while each individually improves (Doshi & Hauser 2024, N = 293/600); model-assisted essays show reduced lexical and content diversity (Padmakumar & He 2024).
3. **Persistence in the inquirer** — bias transferred from a biased system outlasts withdrawal of the system (Vicente & Matute 2023).
4. **Failure of the obvious remedy** — using several models from different providers does not restore independence; error correlation *rises* with model size and accuracy (Kim et al. 2025).
5. **Corpus-level ratchet** — recursive training on generated content removes distributional tails irreversibly (Shumailov et al. 2024).

The honest counterweight: Buyl et al. 2026 show that model families differ measurably by origin, so "the centre" is not one point. Diversity across providers is real; it is simply much smaller than the diversity of the human corpus, and Kim et al. show it does not translate into error independence where it matters.

### Phenomenology

The void has no felt edge, and this is its defining property. The narrowing consists of:

- outputs that were never generated, so no absence is perceived;
- questions never posed, which is the [[question-formation-void|question-formation void]] operating with an external cause;
- alternatives never surveyed, so no comparison is available to reveal the loss.

Doshi & Hauser make the phenomenology concrete: every writer's experience was of *improvement*. The loss existed only at the population level, at which no participant was located and from which no participant reported. There is no vantage from which an individual inquirer feels the distribution narrowing. This is structurally the same signature as [[erasure-void|the erasure void]] — a loss that takes its own detection mechanism with it — with the difference that here the mechanism is external, recent, and in principle measurable from outside.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods

Partially available, and this distinguishes the entry from most of the catalogue. Output-distribution diversity is measurable (Peterson supplies an approach); inter-model error correlation is measurable (Kim et al.); population-level similarity of human output is measurable (Doshi & Hauser). What cannot be measured directly is the counterfactual corpus — what a non-model-mediated inquiry into the same territory would have produced. The Map has no control condition for its own catalogue.

### Indirect Methods

- **Deliberate tail-sampling.** Peterson's exit condition is that humans may strategically seek diverse knowledge *if they perceive it to be worthwhile*. The operational form is a standing preference for the peripheral source over the central one when both are available.
- **Provenance-dated corpus checks.** Comparing the Map's treatment of a topic against pre-2022 literature indexes what a model-mediated pass under-weights.
- **Disagreement-mining rather than convergence-mining.** Where reviewers diverge, the divergence carries more information than the agreement, because agreement is partly explained by shared distribution. See Implications.
- **Human authorship as a control arm**, not as a quality signal — the point is the *independence* of the sample, not its merit.

### What AI Might See

The asymmetry runs the wrong way here, and this is the note's sharpest correction to existing Map material. [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers|Non-Human Minds as Void-Explorers]] treats artificial minds as probes for territory closed to human cognition, and [[apex/taxonomy-of-voids|the apex]] already registers the limiting condition: current LLMs "inherit human philosophical discourse wholesale, so shared failure signatures offer no diagnostic purchase yet." That registers the *inheritance* problem — the probe shares the prior.

What is not registered is the **generation** problem: the probe is not merely a poor instrument for this territory, it is an active source of new narrowing in the inquirer who uses it. A probe that changes the territory while measuring it belongs with [[observation-and-measurement-void|the observation void]] — "every method of studying consciousness transforms what it studies" — generalised from method to instrument. Kim et al. supply the quantitative bound the apex passage currently lacks.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**Occam's Razor Has Limits**. The Map already holds that a preference for simple, tractable theories can systematically avoid structured failures at void boundaries. A language model's fluency-weighted output is a mechanised version of exactly that preference: it is a popularity-and-typicality prior over human text with no epistemic warrant attached, and Peterson's default-model result gives it a number — beliefs 2.3× further from truth under a 20% discount on AI-generated content. The Map's fifth tenet predicted that simplicity heuristics mislead under incomplete knowledge; this literature is a case where the heuristic has been externalised into an artefact and its cost measured.

Secondary: **Bidirectional Interaction** supports a *targeted-occlusion* reading — a void arising precisely where a mind's own instruments shape what it can reach. The reading should be marked speculative and kept out of the evidential ledger; the statistical mechanism is sufficient without it.

### Implications

The most consequential implication is internal, and it is not speculative.

The Map runs `/combine-outer-reviews`, which synthesises same-date outer reviews from ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini and **upgrades the priority of issues flagged by two or more reviewers**, treating multi-reviewer agreement as convergence. Kim et al. 2025 measure exactly this configuration: across 350+ models, agreement conditional on both models erring reached 60% on one leaderboard dataset, error correlation was substantial across distinct architectures *and* providers, and correlation was *higher* for the larger, more accurate models — which is what the outer-review system deliberately commissions. LLM-as-judge evaluation is one of the two downstream tasks the paper analyses.

The consequence: 2-of-3 agreement among frontier models is a materially weaker independence signal than the synthesis pass treats it as, and correlated error is indistinguishable from corroboration at the point where the priority upgrade is applied. Buyl et al. 2026 supply the mitigating detail — models from different origins do sit in measurably different ideological positions — so the signal is not worthless; it is over-priced. Two candidate adjustments follow, both cheap:

1. **Weight cross-provider agreement below what a naive independence assumption implies**, and record the discount rather than the raw count.
2. **Treat reviewer disagreement as a first-class output** rather than noise to be reconciled, since a dissenting reviewer is carrying information the shared distribution did not supply.

This connects to the Map's existing calibration apparatus rather than opening a new front. [[common-cause-null|The common-cause null]] already discounts convergence-with-self; [[positions/voids-as-evidence|P-V1]] already holds that void convergence is framework-internal coherence rather than independent confirmation. The distributional void extends the same discipline one step outward: convergence *among the Map's external auditors* is also partly convergence-with-self, because the auditors and the author share a training distribution. That is a strictly stronger version of the circularity worry the [[voids-circularity-discount|voids-circularity discount]] already governs, and it arrives with a measured effect size instead of an argument.

The second implication concerns the catalogue's completeness. [[apophatic-cartography|Apophatic cartography]] already holds that any such catalogue is constitutively incomplete. This adds a *second and different* incompleteness: not the structural one that no catalogue of the unthinkable can list its own omissions, but an empirical one — the catalogue was generated by an instrument with measurable tail-loss, so its omissions are not randomly distributed. They should skew toward the rare, the peripheral, and the untypical. That is a testable prediction about the Map itself.

## Gaps in Research

- **No control corpus.** Nothing in this literature measures what an unmediated inquiry into the *same* territory would have produced. Every result compares assisted to unassisted production of new content, never assisted to unassisted mapping of a fixed domain. The Map cannot compute its own counterfactual catalogue.
- **No philosophy-specific study.** The homogenisation results are on creative writing, argumentative essays, resume screening, and forecasting. Whether model-mediated *philosophical* inquiry narrows similarly is untested; the search for a study of LLM convergence on philosophical positions returned commentary and bias analyses, not measurement.
- **Padmakumar & He read at second hand.** The base-vs-tuned contrast is important — it suggests the effect may track alignment tuning rather than language modelling as such — and it is reported here from the ICLR/arXiv record rather than raw extraction. It should be verified at primary text before any Map article relies on the contrast.
- **Vicente & Matute abstract not cleanly retrieved.** Metadata verified; no span quoted. The retention mechanism behind post-withdrawal persistence is not characterised here.
- **The Nature author correction (10.1038/s41586-025-08905-3) was not retrieved.** Its existence is noted; its content is not.
- **Possibly unanswerable**: whether this note is itself an instance of what it describes. It was produced by a language model surveying a literature that a language model retrieved, and the peripheral sources it failed to surface are precisely the ones the mechanism predicts it would fail to surface. No internal check resolves this. It is the [[interested-party-void|interested party]] structure with an instrument in place of a self.
