# Research: Voids - The Cross-State Void

**Date**: 2026-09-24
**Search queries used**: "Loewenstein hot-cold empathy gap underestimate influence of visceral states"; "Nordgren van der Pligt van Harreveld painful lessons empathy gap pain 2007"; "Read Loewenstein 1999 enduring pain for money cold pressor empathy gap"; "Helton Register hot-cold empathy gaps and the grounds of authenticity Synthese abstract"; "Scarry 'to have great pain is to have certainty…'"; "hot-cold empathy gap replication failure OR meta-analysis 2020..2025 visceral state projection bias"; "philosophy imagining pain without being in pain 'imagined pain' affective imagination recreative Goldman simulation". Metadata verified against Crossref / OpenAlex / publisher pages; Hume quotes verified verbatim against the Project Gutenberg text of the *Enquiry*.
**Voids category**: Mixed. Mostly Unexplorable (a structural limit on representing felt weight from outside the state), with an Occluded element (the limit hides itself: the cold mind judges its own simulation adequate).

## Executive Summary

A mind in one visceral or affective state cannot represent the *felt weight* of a different state, even when that state is one it has had many times and will have again. The sated cannot re-feel hunger, the pain-free cannot re-feel pain, and the calm cannot re-feel rage. What survives the crossing is the *description* of the other state (its location, its rough intensity, the fact that it was bad), not the *pull* that made it drive behaviour. Behavioural economics calls this the **hot-cold empathy gap** (Loewenstein 2005). Its most useful finding for the Map is a dissociation: subjects who had never felt a cold-pressor pain rated its intensity *about the same* as subjects who had just felt it, yet were willing to endure it for markedly less money (Read & Loewenstein 1999). The propositional estimate crosses the gap and the motivational weight does not. Being told about the bias does not remove it (Nordgren et al. 2006). The void is recurrent, self-concealing and bidirectional, and it applies to one's *own* familiar states. That combination marks it off from the transformative-experience void (novel states) and the inaccessible-past void (lost episodes generally).

## Key Sources

### Loewenstein (2005), "Hot-cold empathy gaps and medical decision making"
- **Citation**: Loewenstein, G. (2005). *Health Psychology*, 24(4, Suppl.), S49–S56. https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-6133.24.4.S49
- **Type**: Review paper
- **Key points**:
  - The gap runs in both directions. The cold mind underestimates the hot, and the hot mind "overestimate[s] the stability of their current preferences".
  - It is both intrapersonal (one's own past and future selves) and interpersonal (other people).
  - Practical consequences include clinicians undertreating pain.
- **Quote** (abstract, via OpenAlex): "When people are in an affectively 'cold' state, they fail to fully appreciate how 'hot' states will affect their own preferences and behavior. When in hot states, they underestimate the influence of those states and, as a result, overestimate the stability of their current preferences."
- **Tenet alignment**: Bidirectional Interaction. The felt state does behavioural work that its description cannot do, and the gap is what that work looks like when it is missing.

### Read & Loewenstein (1999), "Enduring pain for money"
- **Citation**: Read, D., & Loewenstein, G. (1999). *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making*, 12(1), 1–17. https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1099-0771(199903)12:1<1::AID-BDM310>3.0.CO;2-V
- **Type**: Experimental paper (cold-pressor pain)
- **Key points**:
  - Willingness to accept pain (WTAP) for money depended on how recently subjects had felt a sample of the pain: moments earlier, a week earlier, or never.
  - Subjects given only a description showed greater WTAP "but similar ratings of pain intensity" compared with those who had felt it. **This is the central dissociation for the void**: the intensity judgement crosses, the motivational weight does not.
  - Attention also mattered. The distraction group showed less WTAP than the sensation-focus group immediately after the pain, but more a week later.
- **Quote** (abstract): "These subjects exhibited greater WTAP, but similar ratings of pain intensity, compared with subjects who had experienced the pain either one week or moments earlier."
- **Caveat**: Popular summaries (e.g. Wikipedia) describe a clean three-level ordering (just-experienced > week-earlier > never). The abstract supports "never experienced → greater WTAP" and the distraction × delay interaction. The fine ordering should be checked against the full text before an article states it.

### Nordgren, van der Pligt & van Harreveld (2006), "Visceral drives in retrospect"
- **Citation**: *Psychological Science*, 17(7), 635–640. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01756.x
- **Key points**:
  - Current state governs how people explain *past* behaviour. Fatigued subjects attributed behaviour to visceral causes, while non-fatigued subjects attributed it to dispositions.
  - Holds for one's *own* past behaviour as well as others'.
  - **Instruction does not correct it**: "participants could not correct for the biasing influence of their current visceral state when instructed to do so."
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits. The cold explanation ("I chose badly because of who I am") is the simpler one, and it is systematically wrong.

### Nordgren, van der Pligt & van Harreveld (2007), "Evaluating Eve"
- **Citation**: *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 93(1), 75–84. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.93.1.75
- **Key points**: Current visceral state shapes moral evaluation of impulsive behaviour. Cold judges condemn lapses they would understand from inside the state.

### Nordgren, Banas & MacDonald (2011), "Empathy gaps for social pain"
- **Citation**: *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 100(1), 120–128. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0020938
- **Key points**:
  - The gap extends from physical to *social* pain (ostracism, shame). People underestimate it unless they are currently experiencing it.
  - It is explicitly **intrapersonal as well as interpersonal**: participants underestimated "the pain participants themselves experienced in the past".
  - Study 5: teachers in whom social pain was induced recommended more comprehensive treatment for bullied students and more punishment for bullies.
- **Quote** (abstract): "a biased judgment that is only corrected when people actively experience social pain for themselves."

### Nordgren, McDonnell & Loewenstein (2011), "What constitutes torture?"
- **Citation**: *Psychological Science*, 22(5), 689–694. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611405679
- **Key points**: In four experiments, people experiencing a mild version of an interrogation pain were more likely to classify the tactic as torture. Experiment 4 located the discrepancy in *underestimation by those not in pain*, not overestimation by those in it. This bears on which state is authoritative: here the hot state was the accurate one.
- **Quote** (abstract): "the discrepancy results from an underestimation of pain by people who are not experiencing it."

### Sayette, Loewenstein, Griffin & Black (2008), "Exploring the cold-to-hot empathy gap in smokers"
- **Citation**: *Psychological Science*, 19(9), 926–932. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02178.x
- **Key points**: 98 smokers were split into hot, cold and comparison conditions. Smokers who made predictions in a low-craving session "underpredicted the value they would place on smoking" in a later high-craving session. Smokers who predicted while craving did not. Addiction is a case where the cold self keeps making plans the hot self breaks.

### Van Boven & Loewenstein (2003), "Social projection of transient drive states"
- **Citation**: *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, 29(9), 1159–1168. https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167203254597
- **Key points**: People project their current drive states (thirst, hunger) onto their predictions of others. Helton & Register cite this as evidence that the mechanism is projection into a simulation. (Metadata verified; findings taken from secondary description, so check before quoting.)

### Loewenstein, O'Donoghue & Rabin (2003), "Projection bias in predicting future utility"
- **Citation**: *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 118(4), 1209–1248. https://doi.org/10.1162/003355303322552784
- **Key points**: Formal economic model. People predict future tastes as if they will resemble current tastes. This is the decision-theoretic form of the gap.

### Kang & Camerer (2013), "fMRI evidence of a hot-cold empathy gap in hypothetical and real aversive choices"
- **Citation**: *Frontiers in Neuroscience*, 7, 104. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2013.00104
- **Key points**: For aversive goods (avoiding eating disliked foods), *real* choices engaged insula and amygdala more than hypothetical ones, and subjects paid more to avoid the bad in real choices. Hypothetical deliberation under-weights visceral aversion. This gives a neural correlate of the missing weight. It does not show that weight is the same thing as insula activity, a gap the Map should keep open.

### Morley (1993), "Vivid memory for 'everyday' pains"
- **Citation**: *Pain*, 55(1), 55–62. https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3959(93)90184-Q
- **Key points**: Pain *events* are vividly remembered. Yet "No subject reported sensory re-experiencing and 41% of subjects were unable to recall the sensory quality of the pain experience memory." Distress, intensity and sensory quality are "separated in their encoding and/or retrieval".
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism. The episode is retrieved, and the felt quality that made it an episode of *pain* is not. The record and the phenomenal content come apart.

### Hume, *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* (1748), §II ¶11
- **Type**: Primary philosophical text (verified verbatim against Project Gutenberg #9662)
- **Quotes**:
  - "Every one will readily allow, that there is a considerable difference between the perceptions of the mind, when a man feels the pain of excessive heat, or the pleasure of moderate warmth, and when he afterwards recalls to his memory this sensation, or anticipates it by his imagination. These faculties may mimic or copy the perceptions of the senses; but they never can entirely reach the force and vivacity of the original sentiment."
  - "The most lively thought is still inferior to the dullest sensation."
  - "A man in a fit of anger, is actuated in a very different manner from one who only thinks of that emotion."
  - "When we reflect on our past sentiments and affections, our thought is a faithful mirror, and copies its objects truly; but the colours which it employs are faint and dull…"
- **Relevance**: Hume states the void's shape exactly. The copy is *faithful* in content ("copies its objects truly") and deficient in force. His word "actuated" names the motivational point that Read & Loewenstein later measured. Hume reads the gap as a difference of degree ("vivacity"). The empirical dissociation suggests something closer to a difference of kind: intensity ratings match while weight does not.

### Radden (2021), "Imagined and delusional pain"; Noordhof (2021), "Explaining impossible and possible imaginings of pain"
- **Citations**: Radden, J. (2021). *Rivista internazionale di Filosofia e Psicologia*, 12(2), 151–166. https://doi.org/10.4453/rifp.2021.0013. Noordhof, P. (2021). *Rivista internazionale di Filosofia e Psicologia*, 12(2), 173–182. https://doi.org/10.4453/rifp.2021.0015
- **Type**: Philosophy papers (forum exchange)
- **Key points**:
  - Radden: feelings of pain and suffering "resist being imagined in a certain, key way" (proprietary imagining). She explains this by the absence of an appearance/reality distinction for pain.
  - Noordhof (abstract): "only basic sensuous imaginings of pain from the first person perspective are, with some qualifications, impossible. Non-basic sensuous imaginings of pain from the first person perspective are possible." He explains the impossibility by what it takes to represent *painfulness*. On his view painfulness is represented by a disposition to respond aversively, and the voluntary control involved in imagining undermines that disposition. His analogy is the impossibility of tickling oneself.
- **Tenet alignment**: The self-tickling analogy gives the void a *structural* explanation rather than a capacity explanation. The act of representing the state from outside removes the feature that made it that state. The limit comes from the representing, not from weak imagery. This fits the Unexplorable category.

### Helton & Register (2023), "Hot-cold empathy gaps and the grounds of authenticity"
- **Citation**: Helton, G., & Register, C. (2023). *Synthese*, 202(5), article 151. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-023-04311-2 (open copy: https://philarchive.org/rec/HELHEG)
- **Type**: Philosophy paper
- **Key points** (from abstract summaries; full text was 403 to WebFetch):
  - Hot-cold gaps arise "when one projects one's own current state into a simulation about another", including one's own other-time self.
  - That process "does not typically confer knowledge, even when the predictions it produces happen to be accurate."
  - They use this to challenge L. A. Paul's requirement that authentic choice rests on experience-based knowledge of one's values. On their account authenticity requires a kind of *understanding* instead.
- **Relevance**: This is the strongest philosophical ally. If simulation across states does not yield knowledge even when it is right, the void is an **epistemic** limit and not only a matter of accuracy. It also links the void to the Map's [[transformative-experience-void]]: Paul's problem arises for novel states, and Helton & Register show a milder form of it for familiar ones.

### Scarry (1985), *The Body in Pain*
- **Citation**: Scarry, E. (1985). *The Body in Pain: The Making and Unmaking of the World*. Oxford University Press. (Already in the Map's bibliography at [[pain-consciousness-and-causal-power]].)
- **Quote** (widely reproduced; **verified only via aggregators, not the page image**. Mark it as a lead before any article quotes it verbatim): "To have great pain is to have certainty; to hear that another person has pain is to have doubt."
- **Relevance**: The interpersonal face. The void extends Scarry's point *within* one person: having been in great pain does not make its later memory certain.

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit
The cross-state void is the inability of a mind in state S₁ to represent the **felt weight** of a state S₂ it is not currently in, where S₁ and S₂ are visceral or affective states (pain, hunger, craving, fatigue, fear, anger, social pain, sexual arousal). The limit applies to one's own past and future states as well as other people's, and it applies to familiar states that recur.

The limit has three structural features.

1. **Content crosses, weight does not.** Read & Loewenstein's matched intensity ratings with unmatched WTAP, Morley's vivid event memory without sensory re-experiencing, and Hume's "faithful mirror" in "faint and dull" colours all show the same split. The *description* of the other state is available. The *pull* it exerts on choice is not.
2. **The gap runs in both directions.** Cold underestimates hot, and hot overestimates its own permanence (Loewenstein 2005). Neither state has a view of the other from which to correct itself.
3. **The gap hides itself and resists correction.** Instruction does not remove it (Nordgren et al. 2006). The cold simulation *feels adequate* from inside. One does not experience oneself as failing to represent hunger. One simply judges that the hungry choice was foolish.

### Evidence for the Limit
- A convergent behavioural literature: pain (Read & Loewenstein 1999; Nordgren et al. 2011a), social pain (Nordgren et al. 2011b), craving (Sayette et al. 2008), fatigue and hunger (Nordgren et al. 2006; Van Boven & Loewenstein 2003), and disgust in real versus hypothetical choice (Kang & Camerer 2013).
- The memory literature (Morley 1993): no sensory re-experiencing of remembered pain.
- The philosophy of imagination (Radden 2021; Noordhof 2021): basic first-person sensuous imagining of pain is argued to be impossible, not merely hard.
- **Honest limits on the evidence**: No dedicated 2020s meta-analysis or large replication of hot-cold gaps turned up in this search. Many foundational studies are single-lab with modest N. A search summary claimed a "2025 ALE meta-analysis" showing insula hypoactivity in cold-state pain prediction. **That claim came from an aggregator (Grokipedia-type) summary with no traceable paper and should be treated as fabricated until located.** The empirical base is solid enough to motivate a void, not to set its magnitude.

### Is the limit genuine or merely difficult?
- **For "merely difficult"**: Recent experience narrows the gap, since subjects who had just felt the pain behaved more like hot subjects. Mental simulation of visceral states can shift preferences somewhat (Steinmetz, Tausen & Risen 2018, *PSPB*, https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167217741315; surfaced in search, findings not verified). Hot-state induction corrects the bias, as in the teachers' study.
- **For "genuine"**: Every correction works by *putting the subject back into a hotter state*, not by improving the representation from the cold side. That pattern is the signature of a limit on representation rather than a skill deficit. You can re-enter the territory, but you cannot map it from outside. Noordhof's argument gives a principled reason: voluntary representation removes the aversive disposition that constitutes painfulness.
- **Classification**: The *existence* of the other state is Unexplored-accessible, since we know it is there. Its *weight* is Unexplorable from the cold side. The void's self-concealment adds an Occluded dimension of the "natural occlusion" kind on the reason axis, not deliberate occlusion.

### Phenomenology
- **The flatness of remembered pain.** Recalling the worst toothache produces a report ("it was unbearable") with no bearing to it. The word "unbearable" is carried forward like a label on a sealed box.
- **Contempt for the hot self.** The sated person reading about their own 2 a.m. binge feels mild bafflement, not recognition. Nordgren et al. show the result: dispositional attributions ("I lack willpower") replace visceral ones.
- **Permanence illusion in the hot state.** In acute grief, pain or rage, the state presents itself as how things *are*, not as a state. Loewenstein's "overestimate the stability of their current preferences" is the behavioural trace of this.
- **The "I remember it being bad" formula.** Language offers a stock phrase precisely because the content is gone. It is a promissory note that cannot be cashed.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods
There are none from the cold side, by the argument above. The only "direct" method is re-entry: controlled re-induction of the state (a cold-pressor sample, a social-exclusion manipulation). That is not mapping from outside. It is visiting.

### Indirect Methods
- **Precommitment and testimony from the hot self.** Ulysses contracts, advance directives, and notes written *during* craving or pain for the cold self to read. These transmit the hot self's *verdicts* rather than its weight. They are an apophatic workaround: one trusts a report one cannot re-feel.
- **Behavioural traces as the map.** The hot self's revealed choices (what it paid, what it broke) are a record of weight, read inferentially. This is how the void can be charted without being crossed.
- **Structural inference.** Knowing the *direction* of the bias (the cold underestimate) allows a correction factor without any restored access. It resembles correcting for a known instrument bias.
- **Contemplative "noting".** Meditative traditions that label states while they occur ("craving, craving") may build a thin bridge of recognition. Whether they carry weight across is an open, testable question and should not be asserted.

### What AI Might See
- An LLM has no hot and cold states in the visceral sense, so it has **no gap and no access**. It can store the hot self's testimony and the behavioural record without the flattening that cold human recall imposes, because it has no current state to project. That might make an artificial system a *better neutral archivist* of cross-state testimony than the person's own cold memory.
- The asymmetry cuts the other way too. An AI's representation of pain is purely the "content that crosses". It is in effect permanently cold. That makes it a clean illustration of the void rather than a way across it.
- Whether a system with persistent, valence-bearing internal states would develop its own cross-state gaps is a useful probe question for the Map's AI-consciousness material.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet
**Bidirectional Interaction.** The void is the negative image of mental causation. What fails to cross between states is exactly the component that *moves behaviour*: WTAP, relapse, punishment recommendations. The descriptive content crosses and changes nothing. The felt weight does not cross, and its absence changes choices in measurable ways. If felt weight were idle, the cold self's accurate intensity ratings would yield hot-equivalent choices. They do not. This mirrors the [[pain-asymbolia]] argument used in [[valence-void]]: information without felt badness produces different behaviour.

### Other Tenets
- **Dualism.** Morley's dissociation (episode retrieved, sensory and felt quality not) and Read & Loewenstein's (intensity matched, weight not) are two more cases where a complete-seeming *physical/informational record* of a state leaves out its phenomenal character. That is the [[knowledge-argument]]'s structure inside a single life: the cold self is a Mary who has seen red before and still cannot see it now. **Caution**: A physicalist can reply that weight simply is a current neural/motivational state (Kang & Camerer's insula/amygdala activity) that recall does not reinstate. The void does not refute that reply. It shows that *representation of* a state and *being in* it come apart, and every theory must accommodate that. The dualist gain is modest: the case adds to the pattern that phenomenal character is not carried by content.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits.** The cold self's explanation of its hot past (disposition, weakness, foolishness) is always the simpler one available from where it stands. Nordgren et al. show it is systematically wrong and uncorrectable by instruction. It is a clean case of simplicity reflecting the vantage point, not the facts.
- **No Many Worlds.** The void is indexical through and through. The same person, at different times, has asymmetric access to "what it is like to be me-now" versus "me-then". The fact about *which* state I occupy does real epistemic work that no third-person description substitutes for. That supports the Map's insistence that indexical facts matter.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction.** No direct bearing. Do not force a link.

### Implications
- **Which self is authoritative?** The void splits authority. Torture judgments (Nordgren et al. 2011a) side with the hot state, and addiction relapse sides with the cold planner. There may be no view from nowhere between one's own states, only a negotiated settlement. This connects to Helton & Register's replacement of experience-based knowledge with *understanding*.
- **Ethics and law are written cold.** Pain standards, torture definitions and bullying policies are drafted by people not in the state. The void therefore has direct institutional consequences, a rare case where a voids article carries practical weight. Link to the Map's testimonial-injustice research note (2026-09-23), which already cites Scarry.
- **For the Map's valence-selection work**: The [[affective-forecasting-gap]] article argues that *anticipatory* felt valence drives selection. The cross-state void identifies a hard floor on that mechanism. Anticipatory valence is felt from within the *current* state, so it inherits projection bias by construction. That sharpens rather than contradicts the concept page, and the article should cross-link it.

## Potential Article Angles

Based on this research, a voids article could:
1. **"The Cross-State Void" (recommended)**: Organise the article around the dissociation *content crosses, weight does not*. Lead with Read & Loewenstein's matched-intensity / mismatched-WTAP result and Hume's "faithful mirror … faint and dull". Build the three structural features (dissociation, bidirectionality, self-concealment). Use Noordhof's self-tickling argument for why the limit is structural. End with the authority question and the institutional consequences.
2. **"The Unbearable, Remembered"**: A narrower pain-only article centred on Morley 1993, Radden/Noordhof, and Scarry. This risks overlap with [[pain-consciousness-and-causal-power]], so angle 1 is better.
3. As a **conjunction-coalesce cognate**: The void has two faces, the *intrapersonal* (own other-time states) and the *interpersonal* (others' states). The seam between them is itself a claim: the same projection mechanism produces both (Helton & Register; Van Boven & Loewenstein). If written this way, it should follow the [[conjunction-coalesce]] seam discipline noted on the voids index.

## Gaps in Research

- The full text of Read & Loewenstein 1999 was not read. The exact WTAP ordering across the three experience conditions needs checking before an article states it.
- The Scarry quote was verified only through aggregators. Check the page (commonly cited as p. 13) or mark it as a lead.
- The Helton & Register full text (PhilArchive) returned 403. Their positive "understanding" account is known only from the abstract.
- Steinmetz, Tausen & Risen 2018 (mental simulation of visceral states) surfaced but was not read. It is a possible counter-example to "no cold-side correction" and must be engaged if the article claims correction requires re-entry.
- No 2020s replication or meta-analysis of hot-cold gaps was found. The "2025 ALE meta-analysis" in one search summary is untraceable and should not be cited.
- Open, possibly unanswerable: whether the *weight* of a state is a phenomenal property that cannot in principle be represented from outside it (the Noordhof/Radden line), or a motivational state that recall simply fails to reinstate (the deflationary line). The behavioural data do not decide between these.
