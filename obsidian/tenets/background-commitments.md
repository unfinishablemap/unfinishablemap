The Unfinishable Map states [[tenets|five foundational commitments]] and presents [[tenets#^dualism|Tenet 1]] as neutral between substance and property dualism. In operation, though, the framework leans on three further posits that no tenet states outright but that several clusters silently require. Naming them is part of the [[project/direct-refutation-discipline|direct-refutation discipline]]'s remit: mark honestly where the framework relies on an unstated commitment rather than one it has earned. This note is the canonical statement; the [[tenets#Three Background Posits the Tenets Rest On|tenets page's summary of these posits]] links here rather than re-deriving it inline.

## Posit One: A Determinate, Persisting Subject

The agency, No-Many-Worlds, personal-identity, copying, and survival treatments all presuppose a *numerically determinate subject that persists through time*. Two components do the work. **Primitive subject individuation**: there is a fact of *which* subject this is, prior to and independent of any physical or functional criterion. **Diachronic subject persistence**: the same subject is present at one time and a later time, and that sameness is a real further fact rather than a convention over successive states.

Each dependent cluster inherits a different component. [[agent-causation|Agent causation]] needs a persisting bearer of causal powers—the *unmoved mover* who originates an action ([[where-the-substance-commitment-enters]] is the canonical treatment of where this lean enters and why it is downstream of agency, not of Tenet 1). The indexical objection to many-worlds ([[tenets#^no-many-worlds|Tenet 4]]) needs a determinate "I" whose branch-assignment is a real further fact ([[many-worlds-argument]]). Personal-identity, mind-copying, and survival puzzles are well-posed only if there is a determinate fact about which post-transformation subject, if any, is the original.

Because [[tenets#^dualism|Tenet 1]] (the substance-leaning sub-reading), [[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Tenet 3]] (the reporting subject whose experience flows into the physical record), and [[tenets#^no-many-worlds|Tenet 4]] (the indexical "I") all draw on this one root, the three are not mutually independent: they share a common posit. "Five *stated* commitments" is precise; "five *independent* commitments" is not. The stated tenets jointly rest on this posit (and on the second, below), and a reader auditing the framework should see the five as a package sharing hidden foundations rather than as five load-bearing pillars that stand or fall separately.

## Posit Two: Objective Single-Outcome Actualization

Rejecting many-worlds ([[tenets#^no-many-worlds|Tenet 4]]) together with the objective-reduction resolution of the [[prebiotic-collapse|prebiotic collapse problem]] jointly commit the Map to *objective single-outcome actualization*: exactly one outcome becomes actual at each quantum event, and physical processes secure this even where no mind is present—stars, chemistry, and prebiotic mutation all resolved to single outcomes before the first observer.

This has a consequence the framework must own. **Once objective collapse already secures definiteness, the measurement problem itself cannot be evidence for conscious selection.** Objective reduction answers the "why *one* definite outcome?" question without invoking any mind. The residual work left for consciousness is narrower—biasing *which* of the already-definite-making collapses lands where the brain provides the interface, within the Born distribution and (on the corridor reading) leaving aggregate statistics undisturbed. So the evidential burden for conscious selection cannot be carried by the definite-outcome puzzle; the openness of "which outcome?" is not itself evidence that a mind closes it, because objective reduction closes the *definiteness* question first. That burden must instead be carried by the agency evidence—the self-stultification and comparative-cognition considerations gathered under [[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Tenet 3]] and the [[agency-void|agency void]]. The framework should not present "decoherence leaves the outcome open, therefore consciousness selects" as though the openness argued for selection; it does not.

## Posit Three: Global Exclusion of Unchosen Alternatives

The agency route to rejecting many-worlds ([[tenets#^no-many-worlds|Tenet 4]]) presupposes a third posit, and one stronger than the corpus has generally stated: *an action is genuinely authored only if every incompatible alternative is globally nonactual—unrealised anywhere, not merely absent from the agent's own history.*

The strength of that condition is easy to miss, because every weaker condition in its neighbourhood is satisfiable inside Everettian quantum mechanics. Branch-relative accounts (Saunders 2010; Wallace 2012) preserve psychological continuity, indexically owned memories and a determinate history along each branch; Sebens and Carroll (2018) formalise the self-locating standpoint of an agent who, after branching, occupies exactly one branch and assigns her credences from inside it. Inside a branch the agent's reasons stand in ordinary causal relations to her action, counterfactual control holds, deliberation is reasons-responsive, and exactly one history persists. Conscious causation is not the point of difference either: an Everettian dualist could grant that mind makes a causal difference and keep the branching, forgoing only the Map's particular mechanism—outcome-selection needs an outcome to be excluded, so a branching dualist would have to route the influence elsewhere. So the No-Many-Worlds conclusion is not delivered by conscious causation, by local counterfactual control, by reasons-responsiveness, by ownership of the actual action, or by persistence along a single history. Each of those is available branch-locally. What separates the Map from the Everettian is the further demand that the alternatives not occur *anywhere*.

This has a consequence the framework must own. **The rejection of many-worlds on agency grounds does not follow from sourcehood; it follows from sourcehood plus a global-exclusion condition the framework asserts rather than argues for.** The Map helps itself to that condition without showing why an outcome's occurring on a branch the agent will never inhabit should subtract anything from her authorship of what she did here. The Everettian's contrary judgement—that authorship is a branch-local relation between an agent's reasons and her action, and that events on branches she has no access to are simply irrelevant to it—is not refuted by anything inside the Map's agency case. It runs counter to the Map's foundational commitments, and that is the honest thing to say about it: the disagreement sits at the framework boundary, closer to it than articles presenting the No-Many-Worlds–agency link as a straightforward entailment of sourcehood have admitted. [[many-worlds-argument|The detailed many-worlds argument]] already marks this for the *indexical* objection, recording global ontological uniqueness as a tenet rather than a derived conclusion; the same marking is owed wherever agency is the route.

The gloss on *nonactual* is doing work, because the word taken on its own is one an Everettian can already satisfy. Wilson (2020) treats Everett branches as genuinely existing worlds whose actuality is indexical—each branch actual only from within, the rest merely possible—so on that account the agent's unchosen alternatives fail to be actual and the branching survives untouched. What the Map needs ruled out is their occurrence: no incompatible alternative may be realised anywhere in the ontology, indexed or otherwise. Spelling that out keeps the posit from passing for a platitude, and it names what the disagreement with Everett turns on.

Naming the condition does not retract [[tenets#^no-many-worlds|Tenet 4]]. Chosen starting points are what the tenets are, and global exclusion is defensible as one: if consciousness cannot be made multiply actual without ceasing to be what it is, an agent whose alternatives are realised elsewhere is not the sole source of anything. What changes is the accounting. The agency evidence that carries the burden for conscious selection does not also carry the burden for rejecting many-worlds; that second rejection rests on this posit, and the framework's dependency structure should show it doing so.

## The Three Coherence Tensions, Named

**Neutrality versus operation.** [[tenets#^dualism|Tenet 1]] advertises neutrality between substance and property dualism, yet the operational framework—agency, MWI-rejection, diachronic persistence—runs substance-dualist. This is not a contradiction: the neutrality is honest *at the tenet level*, where irreducibility is all Tenet 1 asserts, and the substance lean lives one layer down, downstream of [[agent-causation|agent causation]] rather than entailed by the tenet ([[where-the-substance-commitment-enters]]). But it is a place where "neutral Tenet 1" and "substance-dualist operation" coexist in one framework, and the reader is owed the reconciliation rather than left to notice the seam.

**Collapse redundancy.** Objective reduction secures definiteness on its own, which threatens to make conscious selection *redundant* as an explanation of definite outcomes. The Map escapes redundancy only by narrowing consciousness's role to outcome-*biasing* rather than outcome-*making*, and by resting the case for that biasing role on the agency evidence rather than on the measurement problem. Were the agency evidence to fail to carry it, conscious selection would be an idle posit sitting atop objective collapse—doing no explanatory work the physics did not already do. This is consistent with the [[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|falsifiability status]] the Map already records: the corridor bias is empirically indistinguishable from chance, so its warrant, if any, is the agency case and not a statistical signature.

**Agency overreach.** The agency evidence is the Map's strongest asset, and Posit Two hands it the whole burden for conscious selection. It has also been asked to do a second job it cannot do: defeat many-worlds. Everettian branch-local agency is available, so no quantity of agency evidence discriminates between the Map's world and a branching one—only the global-exclusion posit does. One body of evidence has been presented as supporting two conclusions when it supports one, and the second conclusion rests on a chosen commitment rather than on the evidence.

These three posits are held, like the tenets themselves, as chosen starting points rather than proofs. Naming them keeps the framework's dependency structure auditable, prevents the measurement problem from being double-counted as evidence for the mind, and marks the two places where the case rests on posit rather than argument: conscious selection stands or falls with the agency evidence, while the rejection of many-worlds on agency grounds stands or falls with global exclusion.
