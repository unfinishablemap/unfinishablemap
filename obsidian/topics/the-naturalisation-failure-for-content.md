Whether mental content can be naturalised is one of the long-running stress tests for physicalism, and Daniel Hutto and Erik Myin's **Hard Problem of Content** (HPC) is the strongest contemporary argument that the answer is no. Their claim, developed across *Radicalizing Enactivism* (2013) and *Evolving Enactivism* (2017), is that the resources naturalism makes available — causal covariation between internal and worldly states, biological proper function, prediction-error minimisation — cannot deliver the truth-conditional, intensional, referential properties that contentful states are supposed to have. Their positive conclusion is that basic minds are *contentless*, engaging the world through embodied skill rather than representation, and that real content arrives only with linguistically scaffolded, socially instituted claim-making practices.

The Unfinishable Map borrows the diagnosis without endorsing the cure. It accepts that content cannot be reduced to natural relations among physical states; it rejects the conclusion that basic minds are contentless. The Map's alternative is that content is constitutively conscious-mediated — a sister position to the [[consciousness-and-the-normativity-of-reason|normativity-of-reason]] argument that the *ought* of inference also resists physical description.

The structural parallel is what makes the argument matter here. Where the [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard problem of consciousness]] identifies an unbridgeable gap between physical processes and *experience*, the HPC identifies an unbridgeable gap between physical processes and *aboutness*. Both gaps emerge inside the naturalist's own project of explanation; both resist closure by the resources naturalism makes available. The Map reads this convergence as evidence that physicalism keeps encountering the same boundary from different directions.

## The Naturalising Project

To naturalise content is to give an account of how a physical state — a neural pattern, a functional configuration, a dynamical trajectory — comes to *say* something about the world. The state must be capable of being true or false, accurate or inaccurate. It must be *about* particular objects or properties under particular guises. And the account must use only resources a working scientist would recognise: causal relations, statistical correlations, evolutionary history, dynamical coupling, computational role.

Three families of resources have been pressed into this service. Fred Dretske's *informational semantics* derives content from reliable causal correspondence: a state means *that p* when, under specified conditions, it would not be tokened unless *p* obtained. Ruth Millikan's [[teleosemantics]] adds biological function: a state means what consumer mechanisms downstream of it were selected by evolution to use it as a sign of. Andy Clark's [[predictive-processing|predictive processing]] casts the brain as a hierarchical generative model whose internal states represent the world by encoding parameters that minimise prediction error. Each story leans on a different feature of the natural order, and each is sophisticated enough that it cannot be dismissed by gestures.

What unites the three is a commitment that content is grounded in something the natural sciences already track. If naturalism in cognitive science is to be a substantive thesis rather than a label, at least one of these stories — or some descendant — must work. Hutto and Myin's argument is that none of them does, and the failures are structural rather than detail-level.

## The Argument: Covariance Does Not Constitute Content

Hutto and Myin's clearest formulation is that covariance does not constitute content. In their words, "if covariance is the only scientifically respectable notion of information that can do the work required by explanatory naturalists, it follows that informational content does not exist in nature" (*Radicalizing Enactivism*, p. xv). The argument can be presented as a dilemma that catches each naturalising resource at the same chokepoint.

### The Covariational Horn

Reliable correspondence between an internal state and a worldly state explains *when* the state will be tokened. It does not explain what the state *says*. A thermometer reading correlates with ambient temperature without representing it; a neural state characterised purely by its covariation profile is in the same predicament. The relation captured by "S correlates with P" is too weak to ground "S means *that P*", because the meaning relation is normatively loaded — it admits of correctness and error, and naturalised correlation does not.

Hutto and Myin grant the existence of such covariational relations. What they deny is that covariational facts add up to semantic facts. A state reliably correlating with food can be characterised as carrying food-information, but not, on their reading, as *saying* that food is present. Saying-that requires correctness conditions, the possibility of misrepresentation, and intensionality — none of which the bare covariational story delivers. As Tom Roberts puts the point in his review of *Radicalizing Enactivism*, a relation of correspondence or indication "explains nothing more than the conditions under which an internal state will be tokened; it reveals nothing about what the state 'says' or 'means'".

### The Teleosemantic Horn

Adding evolutionary proper function gives the theorist a way to specify *which* covariations the consumer system was selected to track, and so introduces a minimal normative dimension: the state can misfire by being tokened in the absence of the condition for which the consumer was selected. Hutto and Myin grant this much. Their objection is that proper function does not deliver intensionality — the difference between representing something *as* food and representing it *as* the nutritionally optimal nearby item. As Roberts puts the worry, "biology lacks the resources for specifying under which guise such states might represent what they target". Selection history can fix what consumer systems track; it cannot fix the conceptual mode under which they track it.

The dilemma Hutto and Myin press: emphasise the covariational component of teleosemantics and inherit covariation's problems; emphasise the consumer-function component and lose intensionality. Neither horn delivers what content was supposed to be.

### The Predictive-Processing Horn

More recent representationalist programmes often appear to escape the dilemma by recasting representation as the encoding of generative-model parameters tuned by prediction-error minimisation. Hutto and Myin's response, central to their *Evolving Enactivism* programme, is that these accounts inherit the same chokepoint. Calling a parameter a "representation" because it figures in a model that minimises error does not change the fact that what the parameter does is covary with something. The predictive-processing literature, on their reading, is doing distinguished engineering work without showing how prediction-error dynamics deliver content rather than reliable behaviour.

The Map's [[content-vocabulary-as-derived-feature]] is the calibrated-weaker companion to this horn. It holds that predictive-processing content-talk *summarises* the dynamics rather than grounding them, and stops short of Hutto and Myin's universal claim that *no* naturalistic theory can deliver content.

The dilemma's joint pressure is what gives the HPC its bite. Whichever family of natural relations the naturalist leans on, the chokepoint is the same: causal and historical facts cannot, on their own, deliver the normative-cum-intensional facts that content requires.

### The Family Not Caught by the Dilemma

One naturalising family escapes these horns because it does not start from covariance at all. [[conceptual-role-semantics|Conceptual role semantics]] constitutes content out of a state's inferential role rather than its world-tracking, and so must be answered separately: the dedicated treatment argues that inferential role constitutes content only by presupposing already-contentful norms of correct inference (a regress) or by collapsing into interpretationist deflation, reproducing the covariance dilemma one level up.

## The Positive Alternative: Contentless Basic Minds

The negative argument motivates Hutto and Myin's positive programme, Radical Enactive (or Embodied) Cognition (REC). If naturalistic theories cannot deliver content for basic cognition, the conclusion they draw is that basic cognition does not have content. Basic minds engage the world through embodied skill, dynamic coupling, and ecological responsiveness rather than through the manipulation of representations.

In *Evolving Enactivism* they extend the account to explain how content-involving cognition emerges. Cognition becomes content-involving only "when special sorts of sociocultural norms are in place" — norms that depend in turn on "practices involving the use of public symbol systems through which the biologically inherited cognitive capacities can be scaffolded in particular ways" (p. 145). Linguistic communities institute claim-making practices; participation in those practices is what gives certain cognitive episodes correctness conditions. Content is real on this picture, but as a sociocultural achievement rather than a feature of basic biological cognition.

REC remains within naturalism. Hutto and Myin reject [[dualism]] and treat the explanatory gap deflationarily. Their move is to *contain* content — restricting its reach to the level of cognition where sociocultural scaffolding has done its work.

## Replies and Pressure Points

**The functionalist reply.** A response owed broadly to Dennett's heterophenomenology and to functionalist semantics accepts the bare causal-informational picture but adds that "saying-that" is itself a functional posit — a useful pattern of interpretation rather than a deep metaphysical fact. Normativity is not something a physical state needs to *contain* but something the interpreter ascribes when it predicts behaviour better than non-content-involving descriptions do. Hutto and Myin reply that this slides from explaining content to explaining it away: if correctness conditions are projected by the interpreter rather than possessed by the state, the state has no content of its own, and the question of how *interpreters* acquire correctness conditions reproduces the original problem one level up. The Map agrees with the diagnosis — the functionalist move buys naturalism nothing the eliminativist's move does not already buy, and incurs the same self-referential pressure [[status-of-content|Boghossian]] presses against eliminativism.

**The teleosemantic reply.** Stephen Mann and Ross Pain (2022) argue that the HPC objection narrows what teleosemantics is required to deliver in a way the naturalist need not accept. They distinguish two criteria for genuine content: truth-evaluability (the state can be true or false) and intensionality (content can differ while reference holds fixed). Teleosemantics meets the first through its consumer-function machinery; they concede it does not meet the second, but contend that intensionality is a personal-level desideratum rather than a requirement on sub-personal cognitive states.

The reply concedes the HPC's substance rather than refuting it — naturalistic semantics cannot deliver intensional content — and argues the concession is acceptable. Readers who accept the narrowing will find the Map's argument less compelling.

**Pressure on the positive programme.** The negative argument and REC are independently assessable. Lawrence Shapiro (2014) objects that the case for discarding content is not strong enough to justify abandoning a notion with that much explanatory work behind it. Ash and Welshon, and Markus Pantsar, press the programme on empirical grounds. Subitization — rapid numerosity perception of small sets — appears to involve representational structure in basic cognition, including in pre-linguistic infants and non-human animals. If so, REC's claim that *all* representational processes require sociocultural scaffolding is empirically false. Hutto and Myin reply that these capacities admit non-representational descriptions in terms of skilled coupling. The dispute often turns on what counts as "representation." Evan Thompson (2018) presses a different gap: Hutto and Myin "do not explain how social cognition and public symbol systems can come into being without the prior existence of mental contents." These disputes target REC's positive thesis; they leave the negative argument against naturalising content largely untouched.

## A Distinct Hard Problem

The HPC is structurally similar to the [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard problem of consciousness]] but logically independent of it. Both have respectable defences from the other side and have not been refuted.

The independence changes the dialectical landscape. A physicalist who reads Chalmers and remains unmoved — judging the felt unbridgeability of the [[explanatory-gap|explanatory gap]] a cognitive illusion or a temporary shortfall — still faces the HPC as a separate front. Conversely, a dualist persuaded by Chalmers acquires the HPC as an independent piece of evidence: physicalism failing in two places, on two topics, with two argument structures.

But the independence is dialectical, not metaphysical. The Map's [[intentionality|phenomenal intentionality]] tradition argues that content is grounded in consciousness — that genuine aboutness requires "what it is like" to be in a mental state. If that view is right, the two hard problems are deeply connected at the metaphysical level even as they are independent at the argumentative level. Both possibilities stay live here. The HPC tells us where physicalism fails on content; whether the failure is a separate metaphysical fact or a downstream consequence of the consciousness gap is a further question. The parallel could also be read the other way — as two instances of one over-demanding standard for "accounting for" — and which reading is correct is not settled by the parallel alone.

## Parallel to the Normativity of Reason

The HPC sits naturally alongside the Map's treatment of [[consciousness-and-the-normativity-of-reason|the normativity of reason]]. Both arguments share a diagnostic structure: a feature of mind that is normatively loaded — content has correctness conditions, inference has prescriptive force — meets a description language that is purely descriptive — covariation, function, causation, dynamical coupling — and falls through.

The normativity-of-reason argument identifies the [[rational-normativity|*ought* of inference]]: given certain premises you ought to draw a particular conclusion, and this *ought* is not captured by any account of what the brain *will* do. The HPC identifies the *aboutness* of content: an internal state is *about* something specific, under a specific guise, and this aboutness is not captured by any account of what the state *correlates with*. Different topics, same structural diagnosis. In each case, naturalisation gets the descriptive shape right and misses the normative-intensional dimension that makes the phenomenon what it is.

This is the same joint the [[argument-from-reason|argument from reason]] presses from the side of inference: where the HPC finds aboutness undelivered by covariation and function, the argument from reason finds normative *grasping* undelivered by the same resources — sister failures of one naturalising programme. Where physicalism keeps hitting boundaries — phenomenal experience, rational normativity, mental content — the boundaries form a consistent pattern. The Map's claim that consciousness is the common ground for all three (developed in the [[intentionality]] and [[consciousness-and-normative-force|normative-force]] articles) is one available unification. It is not the only one, but it is the unification that fits the Map's [[tenets]].

## The Map's Position: Content as Conscious-Mediated

The negative half — that the standard naturalising resources cannot deliver content — the Map takes to be substantially correct. The HPC succeeds against the strong reading of what naturalised content is supposed to be: truth-evaluable, intensional, referential. Where the argument has been resisted, most notably by Mann and Pain, the resistance has come at the cost of conceding that intensionality is not deliverable and reframing what naturalists should hope for. The Map reads this as a victory for the diagnosis: if the surviving naturalist position is "content without intensionality," then the kind of content folk psychology and most philosophical traditions take intentional states to bear is not on the table for physicalism. Naming the loss is what matters.

The positive half — that basic minds are contentless and content is a sociocultural achievement — the Map declines, for two reasons connected to its tenets. First, REC's claim that basic minds are entirely contentless eliminates intentionality at the level where the Map locates conscious experience, conflicting with the dualist commitment that conscious states have intrinsic [[intentionality|aboutness]] not exhausted by behavioural skill. A pre-linguistic infant attending to her mother is *experiencing the mother as her mother*, rather than engaging in skilled coupling that becomes contentful only when it later participates in claim-making practices; the empirical pressure points on REC's positive thesis align with this reading. Second, REC's deflationary treatment of the hard problem of consciousness — which asks that the gap be dissolved by denying the metaphysical distinction between the physical and the phenomenal (Kirchhoff & Hutto 2016) — is contested in its own terms (Shani 2020) and incompatible with the Map's tenets that consciousness is irreducible and bidirectionally interactive with the physical.

The Map's alternative is that content is constitutively conscious-mediated. Naturalising content keeps failing because the resources naturalism allows are restricted to physical-causal facts, and content is a feature of the conscious subject's relation to the world that is not exhausted by such facts. This is the same move the Map makes for the normativity of reason: the *ought* is real, and it is grounded in what conscious grasping is, rather than in what brains do. Substituting "content" for "ought" yields the parallel diagnosis.

The HPC also joins Boghossian's [[status-of-content|status-of-content]] argument as a structural challenge physicalist semantics has not answered. Together they form a pincer: Boghossian shows that content irrealism cannot be coherently asserted; Hutto and Myin show that content realism cannot be naturalised. The dualist response accepts both halves — content is real, content has non-physical grounding for conscious creatures, and the failure of naturalisation is a feature of the metaphysical landscape rather than a temporary explanatory shortfall. From the other side, [[content-externalism|content externalism]] supplies a complementary result: intrinsic physical state underdetermines content, pressing the same gap from the reference direction rather than the intensionality direction.

## Honest Limitations

Four limitations should be flagged.

**REC remains a live alternative** the Map cannot dismiss by stipulation. Hutto and Myin are sophisticated naturalists who have anticipated most of the moves the Map makes, and their preferred response is precisely to deny that "phenomenal aboutness from the bottom up" is the right starting point. The argument that conscious experience comes already content-laden is contested both by REC's anti-representationalism and by autopoietic enactivism (Thompson, Di Paolo), which locates intentionality in embodied biological organisation rather than in phenomenal consciousness. The Map's position is principled but not undisputed.

**The Mann-Pain reply shows the HPC is not knock-down.** Teleosemantics can be kept by narrowing what content must deliver, and the Map's response — that the narrowing concedes the substantive point — is dialectically reasonable rather than decisive.

**Hutto and Myin would reject the Map's conclusion.** They are naturalists who take their negative argument to *dissolve* rather than vindicate non-physicalism. Using their argument as an ally of dualism is a structural alliance, not a philosophical one. The Map borrows the diagnosis without inheriting the cure.

**A Dennettian "intentional stance" reply may sidestep the HPC** by treating content as a pattern that emerges under interpretation rather than a metaphysical kind to naturalise — deflation by export, with naturalism preserved by removing content from the inventory. The [[intentionality]] page carries the deeper engagement: phenomenally grounded aboutness is what the intentional stance is responsive *to*, making the stance derivative on something physicalism still cannot ground.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The naturalisation failure for content connects to three of the Map's [[tenets]].

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]].** If naturalised content theories cannot deliver truth-conditional, intensional content, and content is a real feature of conscious mental life, then the grounds for content lie outside the natural-physical order as currently described. This does not by itself establish dualism — further inquiry might yet produce a naturalising story that works — but it provides evidence congruent with the Map's commitment that mind is irreducible. The convergence with the [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|phenomenal hard problem]] and the [[consciousness-and-the-normativity-of-reason|normativity of reason]] strengthens the case: physicalism is not failing once but repeatedly, and at the same kind of joint each time.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]].** Content shapes behaviour. What an organism takes the world to be, under what guise, drives what the organism does. If content is genuinely conscious-mediated, the causal pathway from content to behaviour is a pathway from consciousness to physical action — instantiating the bidirectional structure the tenet requires. A purely epiphenomenal consciousness could not anchor content, because it could not figure in the causal explanation of why an organism reaches for one thing rather than another under one description rather than another.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]].** REC is itself a parsimony move whose cost can be named precisely: to save naturalism about cognition, REC eliminates content from basic minds. The Map's [[parsimony-epistemology|treatment of parsimony]] argues that simplicity claims should be weighed against what they leave unexplained. REC trades a difficulty (where does content come from?) for a deeper one (how can a creature with no contentful states still engage the world *as* a world of meaningful objects?) — a tension Shani presses from another angle, arguing that radical enactivism is inherently unstable, blending strong deflationary currents with an implicit commitment to substantive metaphysical revisionism. The trade is not obviously favourable, and it is the kind of trade Tenet 5 cautions against making too quickly.

The HPC is therefore one of the Map's standing arguments — borrowed from a position whose conclusions the Map rejects, deployed in service of a position those originators would reject in turn. The borrowing is honest because the diagnosis is portable: the failure to naturalise content is a feature of the explanatory landscape that survives whatever the diagnostician's preferred remedy.
