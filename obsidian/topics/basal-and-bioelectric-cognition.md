Michael Levin's research programme demonstrates sophisticated goal-directed agency in systems with no brain and, in some cases, no nervous system at all: flatworms that store an altered body plan in a rewritable voltage pattern that overrides their genome's default, frog cells that self-assemble into novel organisms called **xenobots**, and a general framework—"basal cognition," "cognition all the way down"—that treats memory, anticipation, and error-correction as scale-free competencies. The point the Map draws from this is a *decoupling*, explained below in [Agency Without Experience](#agency-without-experience-the-decoupling), and it is drawn against Levin rather than with him. What the programme *measures* is competency: what a system can do, in the vocabulary of goals, memory, and error-correction. What Levin *infers* from those measurements is that consciousness is graded and very widely distributed—TAME holds that it "is definitely incompatible with binary views that cut off consciousness at a particular sharp line" (Levin 2022; passages under [Basal Cognition and the Cognitive Light Cone](#basal-cognition-and-the-cognitive-light-cone)). The Map takes the measurements and declines the inference. If robust agency-, memory-, and goal-language applies all the way down to voltage gradients in a cut worm, then such language cannot by itself license attributions of consciousness—and Levin's willingness to follow it that far down is the clearest demonstration available of the step the Map is refusing, not a witness for it.

This article is the agency-side counterpart to [[plant-cognition-and-the-plant-neurobiology-debate|the plant rung]] of the [[marginal-organism-scope-of-value-sensitive-selection|marginal-organism ladder]]. Plants show information processing without a nervous system; Levin's systems show something stronger—engineering-grade agency—with no phenomenal measurement anywhere in the experimental record to set beside it. The design-side companion, [[synthetic-minimal-agents-and-the-engineered-decoupling|The Engineered Decoupling]], widens the xenobot case into the full built class—anthrobots grown from adult human cells, minimal synthetic genomes, self-modelling robots—where the designer can say which competencies were installed and still cannot say whether anything is felt.

## The Bioelectric Substrate: Memory Written in Voltage

Levin's empirical work centres on **developmental bioelectricity**: the standing patterns of resting membrane voltage (Vmem) that cell collectives maintain across gap junctions, and which appear to store and impose anatomical target states. Two results anchor the case.

**Planarian pattern memory.** Durant and colleagues briefly interrupted gap-junctional communication in planarian flatworms with octanol, transiently resetting the animal's bioelectric circuit. Some worms then regenerated as *two-headed*—and, crucially, continued producing two-headed regenerates in later rounds of cutting with no further treatment (Durant et al. 2017). The altered body plan is stored in global patterns of cellular resting potential, with no change in the expression of key polarity genes; bioelectric properties, in the authors' formulation, "stably override genome-default target morphology"—leaving the genome supplying the default, not out of the picture. It is experimentally resettable to wild-type, though stochastically: treatment yields a constant *ratio* of two-headed to normal rather than a clean toggle. *Stable attractor* is a model of the voltage circuit (Grodstein & Levin 2022), not a demonstrated mechanism. This is a rewritable, largely non-genetic *memory*—a clean case of information storage that no one is tempted to call an experience. Which cell types hold the pattern, and how far neural and other epigenetic mechanisms contribute, remain unresolved—planaria have a nervous system that adapts to new morphologies (Bischof et al. 2020).

**Instructive voltage.** Earlier, Pai and colleagues showed that a specific hyperpolarization signature marks the endogenous eye field in *Xenopus* embryos, and that imposing that Vmem state on non-eye cells induces *ectopic* eye tissue in a minority of embryos—∼20% partial, lens or pigmented epithelium alone, and 7.5% complete eyes with RPE, retina and lens, some far outside the anterior neural region (Pai et al. 2012). Voltage here is not a byproduct of morphology but an *instructive* signal upstream of the eye-field transcription factors, transduced through a calcium-channel pathway. Levin's cybernetic reading treats bioelectric state as a set-point the tissue works to satisfy.

Together these establish something narrow and secure: a bioelectric control layer, not reducible to gene expression alone, that stores goals (target anatomies) and drives tissue to meet them. Whether that warrants the word "cognition" is the terminology fight below; that it is genuine goal-directed regulation is not seriously disputed.

## Xenobots: Novel Agency From Ordinary Cells

If planaria show competency in an evolved body, xenobots show it in a body evolution never built. Kriegman, Blackiston, Levin, and Bongard used an evolutionary algorithm to design body plans in simulation, then physically assembled the best designs from *Xenopus laevis* skin and cardiac cells (Kriegman et al. 2020). The two xenobot platforms differ in just the respect at issue here. On the 2020 platform the pooled tissue was "manually shaped by subtraction" with microsurgery forceps and a cautery electrode; the organisms "self-maintain their externally imposed configuration" rather than arriving at it unaided. The cells supplied the running of the form rather than the form itself: locomotion driven by "emergent spontaneous coordination among the cardiac muscle cells", spontaneous aggregation of scattered debris, and self-repair that automatically closes lacerations. Cardiomyocytes contract rhythmically in native anatomy too; the novelty is inherited capacities coordinating at organism scale in a body plan they never occupy there.

Self-organised construction belongs to the later platform, where dissociated *Xenopus* cells left to self-assemble form ciliated spheroids that "arise by cellular self-organization and do not require scaffolds or microprinting", navigate aqueous environments, heal after damage, and show emergent group behaviour (Blackiston et al. 2021). Levin's group reads both platforms as evidence for plastic, context-independent morphogenetic competency.

The kinematic-replication follow-up sharpened the case. Xenobots placed in a dish of loose stem cells gather those cells and pile them into new xenobots—**kinematic self-replication**, an assembly-based mode of reproduction distinct from any known animal or plant reproduction (Kriegman et al. 2021). AI-optimized "C"-shaped (Pac-Man-like) morphologies dramatically raise the replication yield. The behaviours themselves—locomotion, self-repair, replication—are not in dispute; only how much theoretical weight the word "agency" can bear absent a nervous system.

Xenobots matter to the Map precisely as a limiting case. Here is novel, self-replicating, goal-directed behaviour with essentially no neural apparatus—a system almost designed to test whether behavioural sophistication tracks phenomenal presence.

## Basal Cognition and the Cognitive Light Cone

Levin frames these results within a general theory. In "The Computational Boundary of a 'Self'" he introduces the **cognitive light cone**: the spatial and temporal scale of the goals an agent can represent and pursue (Levin 2019). This is a schematic of *goal* space, not of sensory or motor range. Bioelectric signalling is proposed as a principal mechanism that binds many small cells, each with tiny goals, into a larger "self" with correspondingly larger goals—scaling the light cone upward from single cells to tissues to whole organisms.

The 2022 **Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere (TAME)** paper consolidates this into a scale-free framework for recognizing and comparing "diverse intelligences" across evolved, engineered, and hybrid substrates (Levin 2022). Cognition, on this account, is a matter of degree defined by measurable competencies—goals, memory, anticipation, error-correction—rather than a binary kind reserved for brains.

The programme name is not Levin's coinage: "basal cognition" was consolidated by Pamela Lyon, whose theme-issue introduction with Keijzer, Arendt, and Levin decomposes the function into a toolkit of separately studiable capacities—"sensing/perception, memory, valence, learning, decision making, communication" (Lyon et al. 2021). Valence is the element the Map's own account of selection treats as phenomenally implicated, so the competency evidence is not one undifferentiated bloc.

The philosophical framing comes with Daniel Dennett. In their Aeon essay "Cognition all the way down," Levin and Dennett argue that cells, tissues, and organs are best treated as agents with goals of varying scale, and reject the dichotomy between "real" goals and merely "as-if" goals (Levin & Dennett 2020). Their wager is methodological: attributing goal-directedness reveals control levers—"pressure points"—that a purely mechanical description misses. This is Dennett's **intentional stance** applied below the level of the whole organism: an instrumentalist licence for agency-talk that pointedly does *not* smuggle in phenomenality. Levin's peer-reviewed successor to that essay, *Cognition all the way down 2.0*, carries the same framing forward into the diverse-intelligence literature (Chis-Ciure & Levin 2025).

Half of that framing holds. The competencies TAME defines are measured by third-person behavioural and physiological criteria, and Dennett's intentional stance is by design an instrumentalist licence for agency-talk rather than a claim about phenomenality. TAME imposes the same restriction on its own exposition, announcing that "the focus of most of the discussion below is on ways to think about cognitive function, not on phenomenal or access consciousness."

The other half does not hold. That restriction is local and methodological—the same sentence promises to "return to this issue at the end"—and when the paper returns to it, it takes a substantive position. Consciousness, on that account, "accompanies specific types of cognitive processes which exert energy toward goals"; there is "no principled way to restrict consciousness to 'human-like, full-blown sophisticated brains'"; and the framework's gradualism "suggests that whatever consciousness is, some variant and degree thereof has to be present very widely across autopoietic systems." TAME "is definitely incompatible with binary views that cut off consciousness at a particular sharp line," leaving open only whether the continuum "contains a true '0' or only infinitesimal levels for very modest agents" (Levin 2022). Levin separates this from panpsychism on the ground that structure matters—"TAME does not claim that mind is inevitably baked in regardless of physical implementation or structure"—but that distinction concerns *which* systems qualify, not whether the phenomenal question is left open. Fields, Glazebrook, and Levin extend the same treatment explicitly, offering a "scale-free characterization of consciousness and cognition" that applies "from the molecular scale upwards" (Fields et al. 2021).

Levin's programme therefore supplies measured competency plus an inference the Map rejects, not a shared silence. Critics who find the vocabulary inflationary—who hold that calling homeostasis a goal and a voltage attractor a memory dresses cybernetic feedback in mentalistic language without earning it—object to the word "cognition" rather than to the phenomenal extension. DiFrisco and Gawne press hardest, judging the agency perspective unsound and unproductive because multiscale feedback evolved by selection explains its phenomena better (DiFrisco & Gawne 2025). Their target is the thesis that agency resists explanation by mechanism or selection, which the Map never asserts—the bioelectric layer above is a physiological mechanism. So the deflation strengthens the decoupling: redundant re-description carries even less about experience. It does cost Levin and Dennett's methodological wager, denied outright; the Map needs that framing's semantics, not its yield. The Map's quarrel is with the extension, on the marker-convergence grounds (P-CS2) set out next.

## Agency Without Experience: The Decoupling

Here is the Map's integration of Levin's programme, kept separate from the exposition above because it is the Map's argument rather than Levin's.

The Map reads Levin's work as a **decoupling test** for the inference "impressive goal-directed behaviour, therefore probably conscious." That inference is the engine of most over-attribution of consciousness. Levin's systems break it. A planarian's bioelectric circuit stores and defends a target anatomy; a xenobot navigates and replicates; each is describable, without strain, in the full vocabulary of goals, memory, and error-correction. And that vocabulary is all the experimental record delivers: nothing in it bears on whether anything is felt, which is why the same results sustain Levin's graded reading and the Map's contrary one, with no measurement able to adjudicate. So agency-, teleology-, and memory-language, however apt, is not by itself informative about experience: it applies with equal ease to systems whose phenomenal status is exactly what is in dispute. It therefore cannot by itself be *positive evidence* for consciousness—which is a claim about what such language fails to show, not a claim that experience has been shown to be decoupled or absent.

This gives the Map's [[valence-and-conscious-selection|account of valence and conscious selection]] a principled **defeater for false positives**. That account leaves a fork explicitly open—whether conscious selection among physically underdetermined outcomes is denominated in valence or runs value-blind—and the Map's lean toward the value-sensitive horn is held at low confidence ([[positions/value-in-selection|P-VS1]]): the value-blind horn remains the rival to beat while the valence-to-physics coupling the value-sensitive horn would need stays unspecified—a debt owed, not evidence against it. The scope question presses on either horn: how far down does conscious selection reach? The [[marginal-organism-scope-of-value-sensitive-selection|marginal-organism ladder]] is tempted downward from one side by behaviour in simple organisms—slime moulds that habituate, plants that count. Levin presses the same ladder from the opposite side: he supplies richly goal-directed systems for which agency is granted and experience is not. The lesson is symmetric with the ladder's own *Physarum* argument. If a Vmem attractor "pursuing" an anatomy need imply nothing felt, then approach/avoidance and goal-pursuit are *defeasible, substrate-sensitive* markers of value-sensitive selection—decisive nowhere on their own, though not therefore worthless. What fixes their weight in a given case is the consideration the Map's [[positions/consciousness-scope|P-CS2]] already makes governing for animal scope: convergence across behavioural and anatomical markers rather than any single capacity taken alone, with the weight a marker carries depending on the substrate it turns up in—the register's own instructive case being linguistic fluency, a strong marker in humans and an apparently decoupled one in large language models. In planaria and xenobots the goal-pursuit marker stands alone: no nervous system, no anatomical homology with any system for which convergent markers have been established, nothing for it to converge with. Its weight comes out low directly, and phenomenal markers earn the larger share because they are the ones that converge in the cases where the attribution is well attested. An unweighted competency criterion would, by contrast, march all the way down to single cells and voltage gradients—which is precisely where Levin's gradualism takes it, and where Fields, Glazebrook, and Levin take it further, to the molecular scale (Fields et al. 2021). That the criterion has a consistent defender makes this a genuine fork rather than an absurdity to be avoided; the reweighting rests on the convergence consideration above, not on where the criterion ends up. Weighting toward phenomenal markers cannot, however, mean discarding behavioural evidence altogether: there are no non-behavioural, third-person markers of consciousness to fall back on—that very absence *is* the explanatory gap. The Map's practice stays graded rather than eliminative, which is why the sibling [[fish-sentience-and-the-teleost-pain-debate|fish rung]] can still treat this marker class as *suggestive* and edge-of-sentience frameworks can grade credence by behavioural criteria instead of ignoring them.

This complements, without duplicating, the plant rung. The two rungs remove different prerequisites and reach the same conclusion: the gap between demonstrable competency and demonstrable experience does not close as behaviour grows more sophisticated.

Two cautions preserve calibration. First, Levin's "cognition" is a functional and intentional-stance category; nothing here shows xenobots or planaria *lack* experience any more than it shows they have it. The point is narrower than strict neutrality: their agency does not entail consciousness and supplies no verdict on its own, and what evidential weight it does carry depends on substrate, anatomical homology, and convergence with other markers—which in these systems leaves that weight very low rather than exactly zero. Second, the reading that extends *experience* along with competency is not a commentator's gloss on Levin but his own: Rouleau and Levin argue that sentience is multiply realizable and that engineered systems are precisely where to look for it (Rouleau & Levin 2023). The Map takes the measured decoupling and rejects that extension, which conflicts with its dualism—but rejects it as a live rival rather than a fringe misreading. The design-side companion [[synthetic-minimal-agents-and-the-engineered-decoupling#the-multiple-realizability-rival|states the rival case in its own voice and answers it]]. [[substrate-independence|Substrate-independence]] of *function* is granted freely; substrate-independence of *experience* is exactly what the Map denies is settled by such evidence.

### What the Decoupling Reaches, and What It Does Not

Four rival criteria go untested against the decoupling, whose evidence is drawn wholly from Levin's laboratory. Two of the four are not competency criteria at all, so the decoupling passes them by rather than defeating them.

**Active inference.** Laukkonen, Friston, and Chandaria (2025) supply what the argument most needs: a stated functional criterion for where consciousness begins. Their third condition, *epistemic depth*, is the recurrent sharing of Bayesian beliefs throughout a system, so that its world model comes to contain the knowledge that it exists. A planarian's Vmem attractor meets nothing of the kind, so the line gets drawn on stated grounds rather than by appeal to what Levin is willing to call felt—better footing, since Levin's own gradualism runs the other way. It also cuts against the Map: on their account recurrence of the right kind *constitutes* experience, which contests the premise that function underdetermines it. The reply is that all three conditions are conditions on representational structure, and the step from structure so organised to anything felt is assumed rather than argued ([[predictive-processing-and-dualism]] engages it at length). Borrowing the criterion while declining the constitution claim leaves a debt, not a refutation.

**Indicator convergence.** Butlin, Long, and colleagues derive "indicator properties" from five theories of consciousness, and their list does include agency—"learning from feedback and selecting outputs so as to pursue goals, especially where this involves flexible responsiveness to competing goals" (Butlin et al. 2023). But it is one indicator among many requiring convergence, and these systems satisfy none of the others; the method returns this article's verdict on grounds Tenet 1 rejects.

**Precaution.** Birch (2024) sets a deliberately inclusive bar—a "sentience candidate" is a system for which sentience is a credible, non-negligible possibility, assessed case by case—and presses exactly where this article stops, asking whether "very low rather than exactly zero" is negligible. On his own bar it is, here: candidature also requires evidence rich enough to identify a welfare risk and design a precaution around it, which the planarian case does not supply. Birch co-authored the Butlin report, so these are not two independent votes.

**Living self-maintenance.** Seth grounds selfhood in predictive control of the body's own viability, and leans—without committing—toward consciousness being possible only in living things (Seth 2021). The decoupling leaves this criterion nearly untouched, because it is not a competency criterion at all: xenobots are living, self-maintaining, self-repairing collectives, and sit on its favourable side. The pressure available is consistency—Seth's viability imperative reaches down to the single cell, so his criterion meets the same gradualism problem this article presses on Levin and cannot obviously halt at the organism. That is pressure rather than refutation; whether life is the relevant boundary sits nearer bedrock than the decoupling can reach.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]**: Levin's functional-versus-phenomenal decoupling is congenial to the Map's core commitment that behaviour and function underdetermine experience. If consciousness were reducible to functional organization, then a system realizing the relevant organization would carry experience answering to it—yet a xenobot's or flatworm's goal-directedness does not itself reveal whether that organization is present. Levin's programme shows measurement and phenomenal verdict coming apart in practice: the competencies are measured and impressive, the experience is not measured at all—Levin's own verdict about it rests on the gradualism of his framework rather than on anything the experiments register. This separation is *consistent with* dualism—indeed it is what dualism leads us to expect—though it does not on its own decide between dualism and a functionalism that locates experience in a specific functional organization rather than in goal-directedness as such.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]**: The competency-versus-experience distinction is precisely the kind of non-simple boundary the tenet protects. A parsimony-driven reading would collapse the two—"call the agency cognition, call the cognition mind, and be done"—and Levin's own framing resists that collapse. The tenet warns equally against the opposite over-simplification, dismissing marginal experience just because the machinery looks simple. Levin's work supplies the discipline for the first error; the ladder's calibration supplies it for the second.

The decoupling also connects to [[biological-teleology-and-the-interface-framework|biological teleology]]: functional teleology can be fully mechanized—a voltage attractor defending a set-point—without touching the interface question of *who, if anyone, is home*. Levin shows how much purpose-like behaviour is available on the near side of that question.
