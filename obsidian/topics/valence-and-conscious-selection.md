The Unfinishable Map commits to two claims that jointly force an unresolved question. First, consciousness causally selects among physically underdetermined outcomes ([[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]). Second, value resides in phenomenal experience—the felt badness of suffering is intrinsic, not projected ([[consciousness-value-connection]]). The question these commitments force: **does the selection mechanism operate guided by valence, or is it value-blind?**

[[akrasia-and-weakness-of-will|Akrasia]]—acting against one's own better judgement—sharpens the stakes: selection there demonstrably diverges from the agent's considered evaluation. The value-blind horn accommodates this readily; the value-sensitive horn accommodates it only if the currency is present felt pull rather than considered judgement, which makes choice *against* present pull its real test (explained under [Why Not Attention Alone?](#why-not-attention-alone)).

If selection is *value-blind*, consciousness steers neural outcomes through attention and intention alone, as neutral pointers; valence is real but causally idle in selection—epiphenomenal to the very mechanism the Map claims consciousness operates through. If selection is *value-sensitive*, the felt goodness or badness of anticipated states shapes which quantum possibilities become actual, making the Map's physics locally teleological: outcomes are selected *because they matter*, not merely *by* something to which things matter. The metaethical stakes are sharpened in [[topics/phenomenal-value-realism#against-epiphenomenal-value|phenomenal value realism's case against epiphenomenal value]]: the metaethics alone secures that value is real and grounded in conscious experience, but unless the *evaluative* dimension does selection work in its own right, value's non-epiphenomenality is weaker than the metaethics delivers.

The [[apex/moral-architecture-of-consciousness#The Open Frontier: Value in the Mechanism|moral architecture apex's Open Frontier section]] reads this question as structurally forced rather than optional and assembles the discriminating tests collated below as the live route by which the fork could be resolved.

## Why the Fork Is Forced

Three commitments intersect to force the fork:

**Selection exists.** The [[psychophysical-laws]] framework specifies that consciousness selects among quantum-mechanically underdetermined neural outcomes. [[control-theoretic-will|Control-theoretic will]] formalises this: consciousness is a low-bandwidth controller (~10 bits/second) that gates, stabilises, vetoes, and steers neural dynamics.

**Value is phenomenal.** The [[consciousness-value-connection]] holds that value requires consciousness. [[topics/phenomenal-value-realism|Phenomenal value realism]] goes further: some phenomenal properties just *are* value properties. The felt badness of pain constitutes badness; it is not a representation of badness.

**Valence is listed as a candidate mental variable.** The [[psychophysical-laws#The Coupling Problem|selection laws article]] lists valence alongside attention, intention, and effort as candidates for the mental side of the coupling law, but notes that valence "lacks a developed mechanism specifying how the mental variable connects to physical selection"—precisely the gap the fork exposes.

If valence is a real phenomenal property that grounds value, and if consciousness causally selects outcomes, then either valence participates in selection or it doesn't. There is no third option within the Map's framework.

## The Value-Blind Horn

On this view, selection operates through attention and intention as *content-neutral mechanisms*: attention directs conscious observation toward specific neural subsystems, intention biases which action patterns reach threshold, effort sustains observation against competing patterns. None requires valence. The controller steers the plant by attentional targeting, not by whether the target state feels good or bad.

### What Value-Blind Selection Preserves

Value-blind selection keeps the *mechanism* of consciousness (how it acts on the brain) separate from its *content* (what it experiences, valence included)—the control-theoretic split between actuator and sensor channels: the sensor carries valenced feedback, the actuator need not encode value. In Stapp's quantum Zeno model, attention raises the observation rate on a neural subsystem and stabilises it (attention intensity → observation frequency → pattern stability); whether the pattern feels good or bad is a fact about the feedback channel, not the control signal.

### The Positive Case for Value-Blindness

The horn deserves its strongest statement: a fork resolved by attrition rather than argument would violate the Map's [[evidential-status-discipline]], and on several counts this is the horn that discipline most favours. Three considerations push toward it.

First, **parsimony and minimal interaction**. Value-blind selection needs exactly one mental-to-physical coupling: attention (with intention and effort as its modes) maps to observation rate. Value-sensitive selection needs a *second* coupling—some law by which felt valence enters the physics—that the [[psychophysical-laws]] framework admits it cannot yet specify. [[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]] does not itself adjudicate between them: its minimality is an empirical corridor ruling out interactions the physical record excludes, not a likelihood ranking over the accounts that clear it, and [[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]] disarms the parsimony-as-truth inference besides. The pull toward value-blindness is methodological, not tenet-derived—an unspecified second coupling owes a specification—and dressing it as tenet-derived would let the Map's preferences outrun its tenets.

Second, **mechanism availability**. The attentional channel has named candidate mechanisms (Stapp's observation-rate model; Eccles's synaptic exocytosis bias); valence has none. Neither is *worked*, though: the register records no formal model of the coupling (P-Q10) and demotes Stapp-Zeno on decoherence-timescale grounds (P-Q4). So the asymmetry is a named candidate against nothing rather than a specified coupling against a promissory note—thinner, but not nothing.

Third, **explanatory sufficiency**. Nothing in the phenomenology of choice *requires* valence to be a selector rather than a sensor. Anticipated valence can be a vivid input to attention—a felt salience that draws observation—without entering the selection law: the felt goodness of an option is real and action-guiding, yet guides by *informing* a value-neutral steering channel rather than by *being* the steering. The value-realist keeps felt value as genuinely mattering and gives up only the claim that mattering must be wired directly into the physics of collapse.

So stated, the value-blind horn is a disciplined position, not a deflationary concession: it honours dualism, satisfies minimal interaction, rests on the only coupling with a candidate mechanism, and leaves valence fully real as content. The Map does not hold it, but would be *forced* to if mechanism-availability and parsimony were the only arbiters—which is why the case against it must be argued, not assumed.

### The Problem: Value Becomes Epiphenomenal to Selection

If valence is real and causally inert in selection, the Map faces an internal tension. It holds that consciousness does genuine causal work ([[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]) and that value is phenomenal ([[topics/phenomenal-value-realism]]); but if the work is done by attention—a value-neutral pointer—while value sits in the feedback channel, the *value* dimension of consciousness is along for the ride. This stops short of full epiphenomenalism, since consciousness remains efficacious through attention. But the specifically *evaluative* dimension—the one the Map claims grounds all value—becomes idle in the one mechanism the Map assigns to consciousness: consciousness matters because it acts, value matters because it is felt, but value doesn't matter *in the way consciousness acts*.

The [[valence#Valence and Causal Work|pain asymbolia evidence]] complicates this: patients with intact sensory pain processing but absent felt badness do not avoid painful stimuli with normal urgency. If valence is inert in selection, this must run through another pathway—perhaps valence modulates attention intensity—but that makes valence causally relevant to selection after all, pushing toward the other horn.

## The Value-Sensitive Horn: Valence as Selection Currency

On this view, valence directly shapes which outcomes consciousness selects. If consciousness selects among physically underdetermined outcomes, it needs a basis for selection; random choice would make conscious causation indistinguishable from noise. The Map proposes that [[valence]]—the felt goodness or badness of experience—serves as that basis: the common metric, or *currency*, by which consciousness evaluates competing neural possibilities and selects which becomes actual. That valence is the *only* alternative to noise is a dichotomy the Map no longer asserts (explained under [Why Not Attention Alone?](#why-not-attention-alone)).

### What "Currency" Means

A currency is a common measure enabling comparison between otherwise incommensurable goods, as money converts bread, labour, and land into a single dimension. Cleeremans and Tallon-Baudry (2022) suggest that phenomenal experience might act as "a mental currency of sorts," letting conscious agents weigh vastly different experiences in a common subject-centred space—the warmth of sunlight against the satisfaction of solving a problem. They answer "Are we defending a dualist position?" with "an emphatic 'no'": their phenomenal efficacy is List's compatibilist, level-of-description efficacy, on which felt valence *is* the neural value, physically constituted. The Map borrows the image, not the ontology; it needs the felt currency to be a variable distinct from the computed one.

Individual experiences differ in every qualitative respect—colour, sound, texture, emotion—but share the valence dimension. As Carruthers (2018) argues, valence is a unitary kind running through all affective states: every conscious state carries some felt goodness or badness. (Carruthers reads valence *representationally*—a nonconceptual representation of value, not an intrinsic property—a reading that if anything favours the value-blind horn; the currency thesis borrows his claim that valence is the common evaluative dimension, not his metaethics.) This shared dimension makes selection *commensurable*: outcomes that differ in kind can be compared on a metric that differs only in degree. Choosing between a visual pattern and a motor plan proceeds not by comparing colours with movements but by each anticipated outcome's valence signature. The brain presents options; valence denominates them; consciousness selects.

### The Anticipatory Valence Mechanism

Selection requires evaluation *before* the outcome is actualised: consciousness cannot experience the result of each option and then choose, since that would require multiple actualisations. It must evaluate anticipated outcomes—an anticipatory valence mechanism assessing the predicted felt quality of each competing neural pattern.

The brain already runs such anticipatory models: anticipatory pleasure and dread are everyday phenomenology, and Panksepp's (1998) work on basic emotional systems indicates that affective anticipation is conserved across mammals. The dopamine system is the tempting correlate, but must be described carefully. Phasic activity in many midbrain dopamine neurons is consistent with one or more varieties of prediction-error signal—Dabney et al. (2020) find a distributional population code in VTA rather than one canonical scalar—and such signals *update* learned value estimates. A prediction error is neither the stored expectation nor the variable consulted at choice, and neither it nor the estimate it updates is felt valence without a separate argument ([[affective-forecasting-gap|the forecasting article]] keeps the three apart).

A formal sketch of the selection law:

**For each set of quantum-mechanically underdetermined neural outcomes {O₁, O₂, ... Oₙ}, consciousness biases selection probability toward the outcome whose anticipated valence V(Oᵢ) is highest, subject to attentional access and bandwidth constraints.**

This claim about what selection is denominated in faces mechanism-sufficient rivals that explain a valence selection-advantage without a felt selector. Common-currency neuroeconomics finds a domain-general subjective-value signal in vmPFC/OFC, computed at choice and shared across dissimilar goods (Levy & Glimcher 2012)—a value-*laden* scalar whose phenomenal status the tradition brackets: phenomenality-neutral rather than value-blind. Sequential-sampling models give valence a parameter-level home: in the attentional drift-diffusion model, evidence accumulates in proportion to subjective value amplified by attention (Krajbich, Armel & Rangel 2010), so value can enter as drift rate, starting point, sampling allocation or, through urgency-gating, as the decision bound. Pavlovian–instrumental transfer and action vigour explain why valenced cues alter response speed and effort; homeostatic reinforcement learning recomputes incentive value from bodily state. Each predicts faster, more reliable or more forceful responses to valenced options with no experiential term, and nothing inside them decides whether the scalar is felt, because their authors bracket the question: the disagreement sits closer to bedrock than a citation contest can reach. What the value-sensitive horn must therefore assert is a *dissociation*: felt valence is a selection variable distinct from the computed one, so that at neural-value indifference choice tracks independently reported anticipatory affect. That is the horn's whole content, and no such datum yet exists.

### Why Not Attention Alone?

The [[attention-as-interface|attention-as-interface]] framework proposes that consciousness acts through attention, stabilising neural patterns via mechanisms like the quantum Zeno effect. This framework is well-developed and connects to Stapp's physics. Why add valence?

Attention specifies *where* consciousness acts—which neural patterns receive observation—but not *why* one target rather than another. "Whatever attention lands on" would be arbitrary, but arbitrariness has more than one alternative: a selector can be structured by intentions, commitments, learned policies or reasons, none of them a felt scalar, and the value-free camp in decision neuroscience holds that choice needs no cardinal common currency at all—heuristics and directly learned action policies suffice (Hayden & Niv 2021). "Without valence, arbitrary" is a false dichotomy, and the Map withdraws it. What remains is the argument the currency tradition itself runs against the same critics: policies and heuristics are themselves value-selected, so the currency reappears at the learning phase or at the level that chooses which policy to follow (Sripada 2026).

Valence, on the value-sensitive horn, is that relocated criterion: consciousness attends to pattern A because the felt valence of anticipating A exceeds that of B—attention the *channel* of selection, valence the *criterion*. The relocation has a cost the Map accepts openly. A currency pushed back into learning, or up to a meta-level, is harder to falsify, not better supported; and if every counter-hedonic choice—sitting the exam, keeping the painful promise—is redescribed as higher-order valence winning, "valence" comes to mean whatever wins and the horn says nothing. The horn therefore predicts, and is refuted by, choice following a commitment while independently measured present affect favours the alternative. The [[consciousness-and-normative-force|normative force]] analysis—the *demand-character* that makes outcomes call for pursuit or avoidance—is what would give felt valence that bite.

### From Currency to Teleology

If valence is the selection currency, the Map's physics becomes locally teleological within conscious brains: outcomes are selected *because of how they would feel*. This is teleology in a precise sense—explanation by what something is *for* rather than by what caused it.

The scope is constrained to where consciousness acts—in brains, at quantum indeterminacies, through the attentional channel; most physical events have no teleological character. But there [[agent-teleology]] is genuine: the agent selects outcomes for reasons, and on this horn felt value is what gives those reasons their weight.

This strengthens [[topics/phenomenal-value-realism]] considerably. If valence both constitutes value (a metaphysical claim) and participates in selection (a causal claim), value is doubly non-epiphenomenal—real as experienced *and* efficacious as selector—and the consciousness-value connection becomes not just that consciousness is necessary for value but that value is necessary for consciousness to function as it does.

### The Problems with Value-Sensitive Selection

**Mechanism specification.** How does anticipated valence enter the selection law? Stapp's model offers attention-as-observation-rate; nothing analogous maps valence to a physical parameter. Valence might modulate which pattern consciousness "observes", or the observation rate itself; both remain speculative.

**The anticipation problem.** If the prediction of an outcome's valence is itself a neural process, the selection-relevant valence is a prediction of phenomenal quality, not phenomenal quality itself. Does predicted valence have the same metaphysical status as experienced valence?

**Scope of teleology.** Does a rat's pain avoidance reflect value-sensitive selection? Where the Map draws the consciousness boundary it draws the teleology boundary, and that boundary is unclear. The [[marginal-organism-scope-of-value-sensitive-selection|marginal-organism scope analysis]] runs this down a ladder of cases (rat, nematode, *Hydra*, slime mould), concluding that the boundary cannot currently be located, that the tenets cannot locate it, and that the two boundaries can come apart: an organism could be minimally conscious yet select value-blindly.

## A Possible Middle Path

The fork may be less stark than it appears. A graduated position is available:

**Valence modulates attention, which drives selection.** Valence is neither a direct selection variable nor causally idle: it shapes *where attention goes and how intensely it is sustained*, and attention drives selection through whatever quantum mechanism operates. Pain captures attention; pleasure sustains it; anticipated suffering redirects it. Valence acts on selection indirectly, through the attentional channel.

This preserves value's causal relevance without a separate valence-to-physics coupling, and is compatible with Stapp's model: valence influences what gets observed, and observation frequency determines which patterns stabilise. In the attentional drift-diffusion model above, the middle path is the attentional weight on value—a classical product term, which is why it and the steelman's concession that valence informs the channel are co-extensive over everything the model measures.

The cost: the selection law itself remains value-blind at the fundamental level, with teleological character emerging from value-sensitive attention feeding value-blind selection rather than from the physics of selection. Whether this satisfies the Map's commitment to value's non-epiphenomenality is debatable—value is a cause of selection via attention rather than a bystander, but the causal chain runs through a value-neutral mechanism.

This stance is worked as its own position in [[graduated-middle-path-valence-modulated-attention]], which specifies the valence→attention coupling through the Stapp observation-rate channel, audits whether it earns genuine non-epiphenomenality, and proposes a discriminating test: an attentional-salience clamp under which a valence selection advantage is *abolished* on the middle path, *persists* on the pure value-sensitive horn, and is *absent* on the pure value-blind horn—a middle-path outcome confirming only the weaker "causally relevant via attention" reading.

## The Bandwidth Consideration

Conscious processing operates at approximately 10 bits per second (Zheng & Meister 2025), limiting selection to coarse-grained choices. A common valence currency is compatible with this: evaluating along one dimension rather than many, ten bits per second suffices to compare a handful of options, and low bandwidth is *adequate* if unconscious processing has already compressed situations into anticipatory valence signals among which consciousness selects. But compatibility is all it is: the figure is a behavioural throughput ceiling measured in conscious tasks, agnostic between the horns by construction ([[topics/grain-mismatch-as-independent-evidence]]), so neither horn predicts a different figure and it does no discriminating work.

## Testability Hooks

### For Value-Blind Selection

- **Attentional deployment should be independent of valence under controlled conditions.** With attention directed equally to positively and negatively valenced targets, selection efficacy should be identical—no "valence bonus."
- **Neural signatures of conscious selection (frontal theta, bidirectional coherence) should correlate with attentional effort, not with the valence of the selected outcome.**

### For Value-Sensitive Selection

- **Valenced content should show a selection advantage.** In tasks requiring conscious selection among competing neural patterns, valenced options should be selected more readily than equally salient neutral options—which every rival above also predicts, so only the operationalised form below discriminates.
- **[[pain-consciousness-and-causal-power|Pain asymbolia]] patients should show degraded selection efficacy, operationalised so that it is not motivation under another name.** Motivation is itself implemented by evidence accumulation, vigour and decision thresholds, so "efficacy, not motivation" separates nothing until it names a parameter. Selection efficacy here means choice *content* at matched value—which option wins when fitted drift is near zero—after drift rate (accuracy, speed), bound (speed–accuracy trade-off) and vigour (force, latency) have been fitted to the same data; a felt selector would show as a residual bias in what is chosen, not in how fast or how hard. The leg discriminates only if that residual survives the attentional-salience clamp, since lost affective gain degrades efficacy on the value-blind horn too, and only so far as asymbolia is the one-variable dissociation [[concepts/pain-asymbolia]] denies it to be.
- **Contemplative practitioners reporting altered valence processing (equanimity) should show different selection profiles from those who only increase attentional control**—mindfulness and loving-kindness traditions train different aspects of the mechanism if value-sensitivity is real.

### Discriminating Evidence

The full battery—these direct tests, the wanting/liking dissociation conditions, and the marginal-organism credence-shifters—is collated with its directions at [[positions/value-in-selection]] (P-VS2). Cases where felt valence and computational reward signals come apart provide the strongest tests:

**Wanting without liking.** Berridge (2009) finds dopaminergic "wanting" and hedonic "liking" neurally dissociable, and reads addiction as intense wanting outlasting enjoyment. If selection is denominated in felt valence, liking—not wanting—should determine which outcome consciousness selects when the two conflict; a chooser consulting incentive salience, or the learned value that prediction errors update, predicts the opposite. The [[wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork|value-in-mechanism fork]] treats this in detail, arguing it constrains but does not settle whether value resides in the mechanism or the experience.

**First-encounter aesthetic preference.** A novel artwork carries no reward history, yet people form immediate evaluative preferences. The valence account predicts these reflect direct phenomenal evaluation—consciousness comparing anticipated felt qualities.

**Moral override.** When felt wrongness overrides expected reward—refusing a bribe despite wanting the money—the valence account predicts that moral valence outweighs hedonic valence in the selection calculus, while reward models must either widen "reward" to absorb moral feelings (weakening specificity) or predict effortful inhibition. The redefinition charge cuts both ways: the case counts for the horn only where felt wrongness is measured before, and independently of, the choice it is supposed to explain.

## Evolutionary Considerations

If valence is the selection currency, natural selection shaped its denominations—pain with tissue damage, pleasure with survival-promoting activities, fear with genuine threats—because organisms whose valence aligned with fitness outcompeted those whose valence was miscalibrated.

This addresses a puzzle epiphenomenalism cannot: if valence were inert, the tight correlation between what feels bad and what threatens survival would be a cosmic coincidence. The epiphenomenalist's best answer is the [[co-optimization-reply-to-the-correlation-problem|co-optimization reply]]—selection co-produces aligned valence and behaviour from one optimised system—which survives the dissociation evidence but pays for its parsimony with an unexplained psychophysical necessity. If valence drives selection, evolution shapes it directly: the felt badness of burning is bad for the organism *and* motivates avoidance *because* valence enters the selection law.

## The Map's Current Lean

The Map's content leans value-sensitive without committing. The [[valence]] article states that "if consciousness biases neural outcomes, valence may provide the criterion for selection"; [[agent-teleology]] affirms that conscious beings act *for reasons*; [[control-theoretic-will]] lists "values" in the controller's setpoint.

But [[psychophysical-laws]] develops even attention and intention only as far as candidate mechanisms (Stapp, Eccles), and valence not at all: leaning value-sensitive without a mechanism is an aspiration, not a position.

The [[graduated-middle-path-valence-modulated-attention|graduated middle path]]—valence modulating attention, attention driving selection—is the most defensible current stance, but even this requires specifying how valence-attention coupling works and whether indirect causal relevance suffices for value's non-epiphenomenality.

## Limits and Open Questions

**Anticipated vs experienced valence.** Selection depends on *anticipated* valence, but Kahneman's experiencing-self/remembering-self distinction and the affective-forecasting literature show that humans systematically mispredict how experiences will feel. If anticipation is unreliable, selection may favour outcomes that feel worse than alternatives: a real but poorly calibrated currency. [[affective-forecasting-gap|The affective-forecasting gap]] develops one candidate resolution—the presently felt valence of anticipating selects, and forecasts the outcome poorly—without closing the question against the graduated position above.

**Negative valence and avoidance.** A complete selection law must specify whether positive and negative valence are symmetric in selection influence or one is primary; asymmetric models (pain avoidance stronger than pleasure seeking) carry different empirical signatures—see [[negative-valence-asymmetry-and-the-selection-weighting-function]].

## Relation to Site Perspective

The fork is forced by the Map's own commitments, and resolving it clarifies what kind of dualism the Map endorses.

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]**: Both horns preserve dualism; on either, consciousness remains irreducible. The value-sensitive horn strengthens it: valence as selection currency requires felt quality to be a real, irreducible property, not a functional state redescribable in physical terms—which is what makes the selection law psychophysical rather than physical.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]**: The value-blind horn risks a partial retreat: if the evaluative dimension is idle in selection, the interaction is narrower than the tenet suggests. The value-sensitive horn gives the tenet specific content—consciousness acts on the physical world by evaluating anticipated outcomes along the valence dimension and selecting accordingly.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]]**: Value-sensitive selection adds a second mental variable to the coupling. The tenet's minimality is an empirical corridor rather than a likelihood ranking (see the positive case for value-blindness above), so it does not adjudicate; whether valence modulating attention enlarges the interaction at all is debatable.

**[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]**: Under Many Worlds the fork dissolves—all outcomes occur regardless. Valence-denominated selection requires genuine collapse: consciousness must choose the better outcome, not merely find itself in the branch where it happened.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]**: Value-sensitive selection is less parsimonious, adding teleological structure to local physics. But if a valence selection-advantage survives the mechanism-sufficient rivals and the clamp, parsimony should yield to adequacy, as this tenet insists.
