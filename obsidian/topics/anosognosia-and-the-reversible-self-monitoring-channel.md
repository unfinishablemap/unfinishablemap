Anosognosia for hemiplegia, the syndrome in which a stroke patient paralysed on one side sincerely denies the paralysis, can be reversed without any change to the damaged tissue. Cold water in the left ear lifted the denial for a while in two of four patients (Cappa et al. 1987). One patient who watched a video of herself trying to move recognised her paralysis at once and kept that recognition (Fotopoulou et al. 2009). The Map's existing treatments, in [[neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture]] and the [[erasure-void|erasure void]], describe anosognosia as a *static* failure: the self-model breaks and consciousness receives a coherent fiction. The reversal literature changes the picture. Whatever fails in anosognosia is not destroyed along with the lesion, because a brief peripheral input can undo it. That rules out the simplest "monitor destroyed" reading. It does not decide between the two live accounts, taken up [[#what-reversal-constrains|below]]: a *misreporting channel* that can be re-routed, and a *belief-updating failure* that new evidence can overcome. Fotopoulou's own explanation is entirely intra-cerebral and fits the data at least as well as an interface reading does. The reversal data are established. The Map's reading of them as a reversible self-monitoring channel is a live hypothesis, consistent with the tenets but adding no evidence for them.

## The Syndrome and Its Anatomy

The most useful clinical anchor is the prospective study by Vocat, Staub, Stroppini and Vuilleumier (2010), who examined 58 right-hemisphere stroke patients with significant left-sided motor deficit at three days, one week and six months after onset. Anosognosia for hemiplegia was present in 32% of patients in the hyperacute phase, 18% at one week, and 5% at six months. Its natural course is therefore mostly transient. That early recovery is not itself a clean case of reversal without tissue change, since swelling and remote functional depression subside over the same days. The reversals below are cleaner: they happen within minutes or at a single viewing, on a lesion that has had no time to change.

Anosognosia tracked other deficits: most strongly proprioceptive loss in the hyperacute phase, and spatial neglect and disorientation in the subacute phase. Patients with both proprioceptive loss and neglect were affected more often than those with either alone, though a few double dissociations appeared. Lesion mapping pointed to the anterior insula and adjacent subcortical structures early, with premotor, cingulate, temporoparietal and medial temporal damage associated with persistence. The authors conclude that the syndrome "is likely to reflect a multi-component disorder due to lesions affecting a distributed set of brain regions" (Vocat et al. 2010). They also found that anosognosia for neglect and anosognosia for hemiplegia were unrelated, "suggesting distinct monitoring mechanisms for each of these domains." Self-monitoring, on this evidence, is several capacities, one for each domain, and not a single faculty.

Berti and colleagues (2005) added a sharper anatomical claim. In right-hemisphere patients who "obstinately denied their motor impairment", denial was associated with lesions in premotor areas 6 and 44, motor area 4 and somatosensory cortex. They proposed that "monitoring systems may be implemented within the same cortical network that is responsible for the primary function that has to be monitored." This is the neural form of the structural point the [[erasure-void]] develops: when the monitor shares substrate with what it monitors, one lesion can take out both. The erasure void's three-mode taxonomy (executive, mnemonic, primary), drawn from the Alzheimer's literature, is not repeated here. This article concerns the stroke syndrome, where reversal has been tested.

## The Reversals

### Vestibular stimulation

Cappa, Sterzi, Vallar and Bisiach (1987) irrigated the left ear with cold water (caloric vestibular stimulation) in four patients with severe neglect and anosognosia. The whole of their reported result is short: "Neglect phenomena improved in all patients, while an effect on anosognosia was found in two cases." The effects were transient, and the authors put the mechanism in the brain, reading the findings as indicating "a possible role of vestibular stimulation on hemispheric activation." Neglect remitted in 4 of 4 patients and anosognosia in only 2 of 4. That asymmetry matters because it shows the stimulation does not act as a general switch restoring every lost access at once. The sample was four patients, and the study was preliminary by its own description.

The same manipulation reaches the related delusion of limb disownership. In a patient with a right fronto-temporo-parietal infarct, "Transitory remission of the patient's delusional belief was consistently observed during unilateral vestibular activation" (Bisiach, Rusconi & Vallar 1991). The pattern is consistent across these reports: a stimulus delivered to the periphery produces a temporary change in what the patient affirms about their own body, on a lesion that does not change. The larger vestibular literature on neglect belongs to a separate subject; [[vestibular-consciousness-and-the-interface]] treats the vestibular system's role in bodily self-location.

### Video self-observation

Fotopoulou, Rudd, Holmes and Kopelman (2009) reported a patient with severe anosognosia for hemiplegia "who recovered instantly and permanently when viewing herself in a video replay." The control condition is the most informative part of the case. The patient's anosognosia "had been unaltered when she observed her plegic arm in her ipsilateral visual field", that is, when she looked at the paralysed arm directly, from her own point of view and in real time. The same information, the arm failing to move, did not correct her when it came in the first person and on-line. It corrected her at once when it came in the third person and off-line.

This is a single case, and the authors call it "preliminary evidence." It is still the most tightly controlled of the reversals discussed here, because it contrasts two ways of delivering the same fact rather than comparing stimulation with no stimulation.

### Emergent awareness

Moro and colleagues (2011) tested twelve patients with anosognosia for hemiplegia and found *emergent awareness*, meaning verbal awareness that increases when the patient attempts the action, in three of them. The lesion correlates differed from those for lack of implicit awareness, and the authors conclude that "the attempt to perform an action may ameliorate body awareness deficits." This reversal happens inside a single session, and the trigger is the patient's own attempt to move.

## The Deficit Information Was Not Absent

Reversal is easier to explain once another finding is in view: in many anosognosic patients the deficit is registered below the level of report. Fotopoulou, Pernigo, Maeda, Rudd and Kopelman (2010) gave fourteen right-hemisphere stroke patients sentences with deficit-related themes. Anosognosic patients were slowed when they had to inhibit automatic completions of those sentences, "despite their explicit denial of the self-relevance" of the same sentences. Six of the seven anosognosic patients showed the dissociation individually. The authors' interpretation is a production-side mechanism: explicit, affectively personalised sensorimotor awareness "requires the re-representation of sensorimotor information in the insular cortex," and "The delusional features of anosognosia for hemiplegia can be explained as a failure of this re-representation."

The two findings fit together. If information about the paralysis is processed and affects behaviour but is not re-represented for explicit report, a manipulation that supplies the same information by another route could plausibly restore report without repairing anything. The implicit-awareness data make the reversals expected rather than surprising, and they do so in neural terms.

## What Reversal Constrains {#what-reversal-constrains}

Three readings of anosognosia need to be kept apart.

**The destroyed-monitor reading.** The lesion removes the capacity to register the deficit. Parts of the Map's corpus lean this way: the ascending self-model channel "delivering fabricated data", or the claim that "the capacity to evaluate one's own cognition is gone" (the latter stated for primary anosognosia in dementia). For the stroke syndrome the reversals count against this reading. A capacity that a video can restore for good, on an unchanged lesion, was not removed. Spontaneous resolution within a week (Vocat et al. 2010) is consistent with this but weaker evidence, because the tissue is recovering at the same time.

**The misreporting-channel reading.** The information reaches some level of processing but the route that normally carries it to explicit self-report is disrupted, and another route can carry it instead. Fotopoulou's own account belongs here, and it is *intra-cerebral*. Right-hemisphere damage impairs updating of the first-person body representation, while "judgements relying on 3rd-person and off-line self-observation may be spared in some patients and may facilitate 1st person awareness" (Fotopoulou et al. 2009). The spared route is a different set of brain processes, those that judge a seen body from outside, and it feeds back into the first-person model. Nothing in this account goes beyond neuroscience.

**The belief-updating reading.** On two-factor theories of delusion, as Davies, Aimola Davies and Coltheart (2005) apply them to anosognosia, a first factor (such as proprioceptive loss or impaired motor monitoring) makes the false belief possible, and a second factor, impaired belief evaluation, explains why it survives contrary evidence. A reversal is then a case where evidence becomes strong enough, or is presented in a form the weakened evaluation system can use. Salient third-person evidence supplies exactly that.

The reversals rule out the first reading for the stroke syndrome. They do not choose between the second and third, and they do not choose between the intra-cerebral version of the second and an interface version. The pattern that makes the video case striking is route specificity: first-person viewing failed and third-person viewing succeeded. An account with two routes inside the brain predicts exactly this. An interface reading, on which a self-model channel delivers the brain's status to consciousness, can accommodate it by saying the brain-side feed was re-routed. But it gets the specificity from the same brain-level distinction Fotopoulou already uses, so it adds nothing she lacks. Vestibular remission is the same. Cappa and colleagues' own mechanism, hemispheric activation, is a physical cause, and imaging in healthy volunteers shows caloric stimulation driving perisylvian cortex, including the insula, and the temporoparietal junction (Bottini et al. 2001).

What reversal does contribute is a change to how the Map should describe the syndrome. The [[curated-mind|curated feed]] framing in the dissociations article ("when the construction mechanism breaks, consciousness gets a coherent fiction") is accurate for the moment of denial. It needs one addition: the construction can often be corrected by input from outside the damaged system. On the Map's own architecture that makes anosognosia a case of a reversible routing failure on the ascending leg, and it is no longer an example of a monitor lost for good.

## Status of the Interface Reading

The data and the readings differ in their evidential grade, and the article keeps them apart as [[evidential-status-discipline]] requires.

**Established, with small samples:** anosognosia for hemiplegia is common early and mostly resolves (Vocat et al., n = 58, prospective). Vestibular stimulation transiently lifts it in some patients (Cappa et al., 2 of 4). Third-person video lifted it permanently in one patient where first-person viewing did not (Fotopoulou et al. 2009, n = 1). Attempting the action raises awareness in a minority (Moro et al., 3 of 12). Deficit information shows implicit effects despite explicit denial (Fotopoulou et al. 2010, 6 of 7).

**Live hypothesis:** that the reversible failure is best described as a self-monitoring *channel* between brain and consciousness, re-routed by the intervention. It is coherent, and it fits the Map's dissociation-by-channel architecture. It is not evidence-elevating, for two reasons. First, the intra-cerebral account explains the same data, including route specificity, with no interface. Second, every measurement here is of verbal report or behaviour, and none of explicit awareness independently of report. The neglect literature has already shown how the two can come apart: Striemer and Danckert (2010) argue that prism adaptation mainly improves visually guided action and does little to the perceptual biases of neglect.

On the three negative states the Map uses for falsifiers, the question is in state 2: **a serious live countermodel stands**. Fotopoulou's re-representation account, and the two-factor account alongside it, are unrefuted rivals that the reversals do not strain. What would move the case off neutral is a reversal whose pattern across patients followed a channel structure the intra-cerebral account had to add case by case. Another would be a restoration of explicit awareness with no measurable change in activity in the spared regions that account names. Neither has been reported in the sources read.

This matters beyond anosognosia. [[clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence]] notes that the clinical catalogue lacks "a targeted, reversible perturbation" of a channel under experimental control. Anosognosia comes nearer than any other case in the wing: a stable lesion, a controlled trigger, a reversal, and in the video case a within-patient contrast between two ways of delivering the same information. It shows what such a test looks like. It also shows that such a test can come back favouring an intra-cerebral account as readily as an interface one. The reversibility signature narrows the options without choosing among the survivors, as with [[conversion-disorder-as-consciousness-side-fault|conversion disorder]] and [[terminal-lucidity-and-filter-transmission-theory|terminal lucidity]].

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]].** Anosognosia gives no argument for Tenet 1 beyond the familiar one. The reversals can be told fully in production terms: a re-represented signal restores explicit report, and a stimulated hemisphere recovers its function for a while. What remains unexplained is the general gap between any such account and what it is like for the patient to realise, at last, that her arm does not move. That gap is the [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard problem]] and is not special to this syndrome. The Map should not present anosognosia as a dualist datum.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]].** Read through the Map's architecture, the reversals concern the *ascending* leg, the brain's report of its own state to the subject. A report corrected by peripheral input, with no tissue change, is *compatible with* the Map's picture of a self-model channel whose brain-side feed can be re-routed. Compatibility is all it offers. As the [[filter-vs-interface-distinction]] and the register's filter/interface partition entry (P-CS6 in [[consciousness-scope]]) insist, evidence about the inbound leg does not establish the outbound leg. Nothing in a corrected report shows consciousness acting on the brain. The patient's belief revision after the video might tempt a reading on which consciousness takes up the new evidence and changes the brain's model. That would be the Map's speculation, not a finding. The data are fully described without it, and Tenet 3 keeps its [[tenets#^tenet-3-standing|available-not-actual]] standing here as elsewhere.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]].** The intra-cerebral account is the more economical reading of each case, and Tenet 5 holds that economy is not decisive when knowledge is incomplete. The tenet cuts both ways, though. It keeps the interface reading on the table despite being the less parsimonious option. It does not turn compatibility into support, and it gives no licence to discount a rival that explains the data well. The honest position is that anosognosia's reversals leave the rivals where they stood, while correcting how the Map describes the syndrome.

[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]] and [[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]] are not invoked: nothing in the reversal data bears on the site or physics of any outbound influence.
