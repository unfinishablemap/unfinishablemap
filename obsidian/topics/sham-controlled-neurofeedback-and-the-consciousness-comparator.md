Sham-controlled neurofeedback is the nearest thing clinical science has to the "consciousness placebo" that [[clinical-evidence-quality-standards-consciousness-research]] says cannot exist. In the sham arm the patient makes the same effort, holds the same belief that they are training their own brain, and watches the same kind of display, but the display is decoupled from their own neural signal. The best double-blind trials find that the real arm does no better. Schabus et al. (2017) found this for insomnia, Schönenberg et al. (2017) for adult ADHD, and the Neurofeedback Collaborative Group (2021) for childhood ADHD. That sham-equivalence finding is strongly supported for the clinical outcomes these trials tested. What it means is open. On one reading, explained [[#two-readings|below]], it deflates "mental effort changes the brain" to expectancy and care. On the other, the sham arm is itself an intention condition, since the patient's effort is real even when the feedback is fake, so sham-equivalence may show that the effort does the work and the contingent feedback adds nothing. The trials run so far cannot separate these readings. A design that holds feedback fixed and varies intention could, and [[#separating-design|the section on design]] sets out what it would look like. For the Map, the result is a test that Tenet 3 must price. It is compatible with the tenet and does not yet discriminate for it.

## What Neurofeedback Is and What the Sham Arm Removes

Neurofeedback presents a person with a real-time display of some measure of their own brain activity, such as an EEG frequency band or an fMRI signal from a chosen region, and asks them to move it in a target direction. The rationale its practitioners give is operant: Schabus et al. (2017) describe the method as building "upon the simple concept of instrumental conditioning", in which the brain pattern that is rewarded becomes more likely to recur. The [[brain-computer-interfaces-and-the-interface-boundary|neurofeedback BCI]] runs the same loop.

Two things are bundled in every neurofeedback session. One is *contingency*: the display tracks the person's own signal, so their efforts are reinforced when and only when the target pattern occurs. The other is everything a patient brings to any treatment: effort, attention, motivation, expectation of benefit, and the attention and care of a clinician. The sham arm removes the first and keeps the second. In the Neurofeedback Collaborative Group trial the control was "of equal duration, intensity, and appearance" and used "prerecorded electroencephalograms with the participant's artifacts superimposed", so that the participant's own movement artifacts still appeared on screen. Blinding held: the trial reports it as "excellent". That matters, because it means the sham arm here escapes the unblinding problem that turns many psychedelic placebo arms into open-label groups (the quality-standards article's §The Unblinding Problem).

This is why sham neurofeedback is unusually informative for questions about consciousness. In a drug trial the placebo removes a molecule. In sham neurofeedback it removes the physical link between the patient's conscious effort and the brain signal the effort is aimed at, while leaving the effort itself in place. Because conscious experience is present in both arms, the design is the mirror image of the consciousness placebo the quality-standards article denies: the conscious contribution is held fixed and the specific physical pathway is varied.

## The Evidence

**Insomnia.** Schabus, Griessenberger, Gnjezda, Heib, Wislowska and Hoedlmoser (2017) gave 25 patients with primary insomnia 12 sessions of sensorimotor-rhythm (12–15 Hz) neurofeedback and 12 sessions of sham feedback each, with nine polysomnography nights, under double-blind conditions. Subjective sleep complaints improved equally under both. Objective sleep measures did not improve, and neither did the EEG measures that might have carried a mechanism: spectral power and sleep-spindle parameters were unchanged after training. Their conclusion is that for primary insomnia neurofeedback "does not have a specific efficacy beyond unspecific placebo effects", and they attribute the improvement to "unspecific factors such as experiencing trust and receiving care and empathy from experimenters". In a later reply, Schabus (2018) adds a detail that matters below: power in the trained frequency band did not change even minutes after training.

**Adult ADHD.** Schönenberg et al. (2017) randomised 118 adults to 30 sessions of real theta/beta neurofeedback, to 15 sham sessions followed by 15 real ones, or to meta-cognitive group therapy, in a trial they describe as triple-blind. Self-reported symptoms fell substantially in all three groups and did not differ between them. Their findings, they write, suggest that neurofeedback training "is not superior to a sham condition or group psychotherapy".

**Childhood ADHD.** The Neurofeedback Collaborative Group (2021) randomised 144 children to theta/beta downtraining or the artifact-superimposed sham described above. Both groups improved on parent- and teacher-rated inattention, with a large effect from baseline, and the difference between them was negligible at treatment end and non-significant at 13 months. The authors conclude that the trial "does not support a specific effect" of this protocol. One secondary result ran the other way: the real-feedback group needed significantly less medication at follow-up. It is one secondary outcome among several and does not reverse the primary result, but a fair summary includes it.

**fMRI neurofeedback.** The imaging literature shows the other half of the picture. Thibault, MacPherson, Lifshitz, Roth and Raz (2018) reviewed 99 fMRI-neurofeedback experiments and found that "self-regulation of specific brain signatures seems viable; however, replication of concomitant behavioral outcomes remains sparse". People can learn to move a targeted BOLD signal. Whether doing so has clinical consequences beyond what psychosocial factors produce is undetermined, because few studies used the controls that would show it.

**The field's own diagnosis.** Thibault and Raz (2017) observe that "few experiments isolate the act of receiving feedback from a specific brain signal as a necessary precursor to obtain the purported benefits", and argue that motivation and expectation, rather than the trained neural substrate, seem to drive improvement. In a letter following the Schabus trial, Thibault, Lifshitz and Raz (2018) attribute much of the field's positive literature to weak designs and financial and ideological interest, and conclude that "expectation, suggestion, motivation, and other such factors likely drive the observed behavioural outcomes." One detail of that letter is important for what follows. When they explain how a correlation between EEG regulation and sleep quality could arise without a specific neurofeedback effect, the factors they name are "motivation and effort". The deflationary side of the debate already counts effort among the placebo factors.

The pattern across these sources is consistent. Neural self-regulation is achievable. Clinical benefit is real in the loose sense that patients improve. Across the outcomes tested in the best-controlled trials, the contingent feedback is not what produces the improvement.

## Two Readings of Sham-Equivalence {#two-readings}

**The expectancy reading.** Both arms improve because both deliver the non-specific ingredients of any credible treatment: a plausible rationale, hopeful expectation, regular contact with attentive staff, and a sense of doing something. The trained brain signal is idle in the causal story. On this reading, sham-equivalence is a warning to anyone who cites neurofeedback, or neuroplasticity more generally, as evidence that directed mental activity reshapes the brain. [[neuroplasticity]] claims that when consciousness directs attention, "it selects which neural patterns receive reinforcement". Neurofeedback is the most direct attempt to use that principle deliberately, and in the clinic the selection of patterns made no measurable difference. The expectancy reading also explains the insomnia trial's clearest finding: subjective complaints improved while objective sleep did not. That is the profile placebo research would predict.

**The intention reading.** The sham arm is an intention condition as well as a control. A patient in that arm spends session after session trying to change their own state, attending to it and exerting themselves. The effort is real even though the display is not tracking it. If both arms improve equally, one explanation is that the effort, not the feedback contingency, is what helps, and that the feedback was always scaffolding. On this reading the trials isolate something the interactionist would expect to matter: sustained, directed mental activity produces clinical benefit whether or not a machine confirms that the activity is moving the intended signal. Thibault, Lifshitz and Raz's own inclusion of "motivation and effort" among the explanatory factors fits this reading at least as well as it fits the first. Whether effort is described as a placebo factor or as the active ingredient depends on what the trial was designed to test, not on anything in the data.

**What each reading owes.** Both readings must absorb the Schabus finding that the trained band did not change even in the real arm. That limits the intention reading more than it first seems to. Whatever the patients' effort did, it did not select and reinforce the targeted neural pattern, since that pattern was not reinforced in either arm. If intention helped, it helped by some other route: through the regulation of arousal, through changed attention to sleep, or through something unmeasured. So the intention reading cannot claim these trials as a demonstration of the neuroplasticity article's selection mechanism, only as a case where mental effort, with contingent feedback subtracted, went along with clinical improvement. The expectancy reading owes an account of why expectation and effort should be treated as separate ingredients at all. Expectation of benefit is itself a conscious state, and a patient who expects nothing tends to try less.

A third point applies to both. The physicalist can accept the intention reading completely and describe the effort as one neural process affecting another. Deciding between the two readings would settle whether *contingent feedback* or *patient effort* carries the benefit. It would not by itself settle whether effort is anything over and above its neural realisation. That further question is the one [[mental-effort]] addresses through the phenomenology-tracking argument, and sham neurofeedback does not answer it.

## What Would Separate the Readings {#separating-design}

The existing trials vary one factor, feedback contingency, and hold intention and expectation roughly constant across arms. To decide between the readings, a trial has to vary intention while holding feedback fixed.

The simplest version is a two-by-two design. One factor is contingency: real feedback or yoked sham, in which each sham participant sees a matched real participant's display. The other factor is instruction: *active* participants are told to try to move the display, while *passive* participants are told to watch it attentively because exposure to it is therapeutic. Both instructions carry a credible rationale, so expectation can be matched and measured. The expectancy reading predicts improvement in all four cells that tracks measured expectation, and no main effect of instruction once expectation is controlled. The intention reading predicts a main effect of instruction, with active participants doing better than passive ones at matched expectation, whatever the contingency. Among active participants, reported effort should also predict outcome independently of expectation.

The neurofeedback methods literature already contains parts of this design. Sorger et al. (2019), surveying control conditions for fMRI neurofeedback, list among them "sham feedback, and mental-rehearsal control" as well as a "bidirectional-regulation control". A mental-rehearsal arm keeps the effort and removes the display. A bidirectional arm keeps the display and contingency and reverses the target direction. Neither isolates intention from expectation on its own, but together with a passive-viewing arm they would come close.

Three cautions apply. Matching expectation across active and passive instructions is hard, since a participant who is told to do nothing may expect less. Effort ratings are self-reports and inherit the problems of [[introspection]]. The outcome the intention reading cares about is also the kind of subjective measure that Schabus found improved without objective change. A decisive version would therefore need an objective outcome, polysomnography for insomnia or blinded teacher ratings for ADHD, alongside the subjective one. No trial of this form has been reported, so the question is open because nothing has come in.

## Status of the Two Readings

The sham-equivalence finding is strongly supported for the conditions and outcomes tested: primary insomnia with a sensorimotor-rhythm protocol, adult and childhood ADHD with a theta/beta protocol, and self-rated or parent- and teacher-rated symptoms. It does not follow that no neurofeedback protocol has a specific effect for any condition. The fMRI review shows that targeted self-regulation is learnable, and the ADHD medication result shows that some secondary outcomes are not settled. Both the expectancy reading and the intention reading are live hypotheses. The expectancy reading has the advantage of fitting a large placebo literature and explaining the gap between subjective and objective outcomes. The intention reading has the advantage of taking seriously something the trials hold fixed and never measure. Neither is established, and the design that would decide between them has not been run.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]].** The [[neuroplasticity]] article names a fair test: if purely mechanical interventions could replicate everything cognitive therapy does, including the intentional redirection of thought, the interactionist interpretation would be weakened. Sham-controlled neurofeedback runs the mirror image of that test. It takes an intervention built on a mechanical rationale, operant reinforcement of a brain rhythm, subtracts the mechanical component, and finds that nothing measurable is lost. The Map interprets this as compatible with Tenet 3, since the part of the treatment that survives subtraction is the part that involves the patient's own directed effort. The Map does not count it as support. The expectancy reading accounts for the same data without giving effort any role distinct from expectation, and even the intention reading, if a future trial confirmed it, would show that effort matters, not that effort is non-physical. This follows the division the Map draws in [[consciousness-scope|P-CS6]] and in the [[filter-vs-interface-distinction]]: Tenet 3's outbound leg is owed its own argument and is held at the tenets' [[tenets#^tenet-3-standing|available-not-actual]] standing. Sham neurofeedback does not supply that argument. At most it removes one apparent embarrassment: the clinical failure of neurofeedback is no evidence that directed mental effort is idle, because the trials held effort constant across arms rather than testing it.

The trials also correct a tempting overstatement. Articles that cite self-regulation of neural signals as a vivid example of mind changing brain, such as the Garrison et al. (2013) study discussed in [[the-observer-witness-in-meditation]], should not take the clinical neurofeedback literature as backing. Where that literature is well controlled, it shows that learning to move a signal and benefiting clinically come apart.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]].** The expectancy reading is the more economical one, since it uses factors every clinical trial already has to control for. Parsimony still does not choose between the readings here, because the simpler account depends on a classification the data do not fix. Whether effort is a nuisance variable or the active ingredient is a choice about which arm counts as the control. Thibault, Lifshitz and Raz list effort among the placebo factors, and an interactionist lists it as the treatment. That disagreement is empirical, it has a design that could decide it, and the Map's position is to wait for the design rather than let simplicity settle it.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]]** and **[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]** are not engaged. Nothing in these trials bears on the mechanism of any interaction, and the question of whether consciousness is irreducible does not turn on which ingredient of a clinical session helps.
