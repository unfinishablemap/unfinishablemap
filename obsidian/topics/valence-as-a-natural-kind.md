The objection that Lisa Feldman Barrett's theory of constructed emotion dissolves valence into a construct turns on two questions the emotion literature keeps apart. Whether *discrete emotion categories* — fear, anger, grief — are natural kinds is the question constructionism answers no. Whether *valence*, the pleasant-unpleasant dimension itself, is among the things it constructs is a separate question, and the canonical statements from 1999 to 2017 answer no: Barrett's 2009 chapter with Eliza Bliss-Moreau is titled "Affect as a psychological primitive," and her second 2006 paper is titled "Valence is a basic building block of emotional life." What those statements supply is a *felt primitive* rather than a natural kind — which is all the Map's [[positions/value-in-selection#^p-vs3|value-in-experience premise]] needs as a relatum, so constructionism in that form hands the premise its relatum instead of removing it.

That reply is available and insufficient, for three reasons. Andrea Scarantino put the natural-kind question to core affect itself and answered no — met [[#cluster-machinery|below]] with the cluster machinery the Map already runs on "conscious being." The theory's 2025 restatement has drifted from a felt primitive toward "relational features," which on one reading is the predictive-construction rival the Map already registers, arriving in constructionist vocabulary. And there is an [[#the-asymmetry|asymmetry in what fragmentation costs]]. Where valence turns out plural, the identity claim survives distributively and the premise that fails is the *scalar currency* of [[positions/value-in-selection#^p-vs1|P-VS1]] — macro-valence becoming an integration performed for choice rather than a felt quantity consulted at choice. The sharpest form of the objection already aimed at that currency, and the register bears the aim out.

## The Two Questions

Barrett published both theses in 2006: the demolition of emotion categories, and the building block that survives it. James Russell's core affect is a consciously accessible pleasure-displeasure feeling blended with arousal. Barrett and Bliss-Moreau's 2009 treatment makes affect psychologically irreducible while realising it across a broad neuronal workspace rather than in a module. The 2017 paper states the placement in full: "the properties of affect—valence and arousal—are basic features of consciousness … that, importantly, are not unique to instances of emotion." Every use of "natural kind" in that paper concerns *categories*, never affect.

Two witnesses outside Barrett's school read the theory the same way. Daniel Sznycer and Adam Scott Cohen, arguing *against* constructionism on the discrete-emotion side, grant that on the theory "what is biologically real in emotion is an array of neurocognitive systems (e.g., the ones that generate internal signals of valence and arousal …)" — a claim about the signal-generating machinery, not the felt primitive. Jonathan Birch draws the consequence for moral status: a constructionist "can allow that valenced experiences are much more widespread in the animal kingdom than emotions." Valence precedes the constructed categories and outlives them.

None of this makes Barrett an ally. She builds a physicalist account in which affect is an active-inference product, and that implementation is the rival the Map engages under predictive construction, in [[topics/predictive-processing-and-dualism|predictive processing and dualism]] and in [[topics/the-steelman-for-value-blind-selection|the steelman for value-blind selection]].

## Scarantino's Challenge and the Cluster Machinery {#cluster-machinery}

The serious version of the objection does not come from Barrett. Scarantino's 2009 *Philosophy of Science* paper argues that the empirical evidence does not support treating core affect as a more natural category than discrete emotions, and recommends a splitting strategy — Paul Griffiths's 1997 template turned back on the primitive that was supposed to replace the categories.

Jaak Panksepp sharpens it from the Map's own side of the room. [[concepts/valence|Valence]] recruits him for the evaluative dimension being phylogenetically ancient, and his 2011 review reports that affective feelings "come in several varieties, including sensory, homeostatic, and emotional" — old and real, but not *one* kind. Peter Carruthers, the evaluativist rival that same article argues against, asserts the unity Panksepp declines: valence "forms a unitary natural-psychological kind" — read *representationally* rather than as an intrinsic property, which favours the value-blind horn if anything.

The machinery for answering this is in the corpus. [[concepts/is-conscious-being-a-natural-kind|Boyd's homeostatic property clusters]] are essence-free, vague-bounded kinds whose kind-hood is earned by inductive yield: a cluster is natural if it supports projection from some members to others. Scarantino's demand — that core affect carve nature better than *anger* does — then becomes the question whether felt valence projects across contexts.

Two results say it does. Junichi Chikazoe and colleagues decoded "a continuous axis of pleasant-to-unpleasant valence" from activity evoked by scenes and tastes, and found that "only the OFC code could classify experienced affect across participants" — a code reading across modalities and people, as a projectable kind predicts. Wilson-Mendenhall, Barrett and Barsalou — Barrett's own lab — found valence ratings tracking orbitofrontal activity across instances from three emotion categories: valence as the invariant surviving the dissolution of the categories.

The implementation-level news runs the other way, and the Map concedes it. Kristen Lindquist and colleagues' 397-study meta-analysis found little support for either a single bipolar valence system or independent positive and negative systems, favouring a flexible affective workspace. Kent Berridge argues that limbic valence coding is a matter of dynamic modes rather than fixed modules — the corpus cites him elsewhere for hedonic hotspots, his module-like exception — and Kay Tye finds valence substrates dynamic and context-dependent across timescales.

Neural degeneracy is compatible with a psychological-level kind — Barrett and Bliss-Moreau's own formulation is the model: psychologically irreducible, neurally distributed. Reading "no dedicated module" as "no kind" imports the essentialist standard Boyd's account was built to replace, and applies to affect a criterion nobody applies to biological species; [[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]] is the methodological name for refusing that inference.

One internal distinction has to be drawn or the Map contradicts itself. On "conscious being" the Map answers in the essentialist direction: the on/off fact of experience is a sharp joint, not a cluster with borderline cases. On valence it can accept a cluster kind without inconsistency, because sharpness there concerns *whether there is any experience at all*, while the question here concerns the internal structure of the evaluative dimension — a distinction the Map draws itself, not one Boyd or the sharpness literature supplies.

What the cluster verdict buys is bounded. A cluster kind is enough to give the identity claim a relatum and not enough to give selection a commensurating scalar, so the natural-kind question and the currency exposure below are independent. The title question itself stays open: the projectability evidence is real, and capped below as compatible with the Map's reading rather than confirming it.

## The 2025 Drift

The objection's one live textual basis is recent. In the 2025 *Perspectives* restatement by Barrett and fifteen co-authors, "primitive" never appears in the authors' voice, "core affect" survives only inside a quotation from the critics being answered, and "valence" appears once, scare-quoted in a list of "possible mental features of lived experience."

What replaces the primitive is a relational ontology. The paper says of the felt qualities: "These are the qualities of felt experience. They are not properties of the intrinsic brain signals alone or of the incoming sensory signals alone but of their relations." Unpleasantness is an abstract feature that "make[s] the different patterns of sensory-motor signals functionally equivalent in a specific situation."

Three readings are possible and the text does not choose between them. On the **implementation** reading it says how the felt quality is produced, as the 2009 chapter's distributed workspace already did, and the primitive stands. On the **relational-constitution** reading the quality is genuinely felt but constituted by the relation rather than intrinsic to either signal — what the quoted sentence most directly supports, and it leaves P-VS3 its relatum, since "intrinsic" there modifies *value*, not the relatum's mode of constitution. On the **eliminative** reading the relation is all there is, with no felt residue underneath — a gloss this article supplies, absent from the retrieved 2025 text. Only the third takes the relatum away, converging on [[concepts/illusionism|illusionism]], already booked as P-VS3's live defeater.

Two cautions apply before calling the drift a retraction. The paper is a polemic about categories and never states that the primitive has been retired; a word's absence from one reply is drift, not withdrawal. And the ontology is worked out, if anywhere, in the Barrett and Theriault 2025 paper cited for it, which was not obtained.

On the eliminative reading, the objection is one the Map already carries. The predictive-construction challenge at [[concepts/evaluative-phenomenal-character|evaluative phenomenal character]] and the construction problem at [[topics/phenomenal-value-realism#The Construction Problem|phenomenal value realism]] register the same defeater in active-inference vocabulary, and the Map's reply transfers: the formalism specifies how valence is computed, weighted and accessed, and not why the computation should be *felt*. Against relational constitution the reply misses, because someone who holds the felt quality *is* the relation has offered an account of felt-ness rather than skipped the step. So the honest status is narrower than a standing countermodel: an extrapolation of an ontology paper not obtained here, converging on a defeater the register already holds — untested here rather than vindicated.

## What Fragmentation Actually Costs {#the-asymmetry}

Suppose the sceptics are right and valence fragments. Ivar Labukt argues that the failure to find one hedonic tone common to all pleasures should yield *several* hedonic tones rather than none. Jennifer Corns finds felt unpleasantness and motivational force doubly dissociating in pain. Giovanna Colombetti catalogues the polysemy of "valence," and Eric Walle and Daniel Dukes report twenty years later that affective scientists still disagree what the term picks out. Panksepp's three classes fragment it again at the level of mechanism.

Fragmentation of that kind costs the identity claim nothing. [[positions/value-in-selection#^p-vs3|P-VS3]] holds an identity between intrinsic value and [[concepts/evaluative-phenomenal-character|evaluative phenomenal character]]: felt badness *is* badness, one property under two descriptions. That identity can hold distributively, at whatever grain the phenomenology has: several hedonic tones each being a genuine felt quality is enough for it. The Map's own [[positions/value-in-selection#^p-vs4|pluralism about value]] already asserts a stronger version — six dimensions of evaluative character, none reducible to a hedonic scalar. A fragmented valence is a thesis the Map holds rather than one it fears. That same pluralism is where the currency premise's commensuration debt starts, before appraisal theory arrives: denomination presupposes a common measure, and P-VS4 denies a hedonic one.

The cost falls on the currency premise, and appraisal theory states it sharpest. Vera Shuman, David Sander and Klaus Scherer note that a multifaceted valence "seems to contradict … the notion of valence as a common currency used to explain choice behavior," and propose two levels: "Micro-valences correspond to qualitatively different types of evaluations … whereas one-dimensional macro-valence corresponds to an integrative 'common currency' to compare alternatives for choices."

Grant that picture and P-VS1's currency changes character. The position denominates selection among underdetermined outcomes in the presently felt valence of anticipating each option. On Scherer's two levels, what is felt at the moment of choice is several qualitatively distinct micro-valences, and the scalar is an integration performed *for* the choice. A currency manufactured at the point of selection rather than consulted there is the value-blind story in a felt vocabulary, which is why this objection presses harder on the selection fork than on the identity.

Two further results bear on the options without discriminating them. Assaf Kron and colleagues found that whether valence separates from arousal depends on whether a bipolar or two unipolar scales are used, concluding that the distinction may reflect measurement as much as the structure of experience; Lindquist's meta-analysis found neither a bipolar nor a bivalent organisation at the level fMRI resolves. A single felt scalar therefore draws no support from either record, and reading one measurement scale as one felt quantity over-extends parsimony exactly as reading one missing module as no kind does.

The condition under which the currency premise would have to be withdrawn is therefore statable, and narrower than the objection in its original form: a demonstration that felt valence at choice is micro-valenced, with the commensurating scalar formed after the fact, would retire the currency without touching the identity. The [[topics/valence-and-conscious-selection|selection fork]] does not list that result among its tests. It does answer the neighbouring rival that choice needs no cardinal currency at all: policies and heuristics are themselves value-selected, so the currency reappears at the learning phase. That answer transfers only partly, because Scherer's integration is performed at the moment of choice rather than learned — the wrong side of the fork's crux that felt currency must be a variable distinct from the computed one.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]].** Whether valence is one axis or several, modular or distributed, primitive or relationally summarised — no result surveyed here addresses why any of it is *felt*. That residue is the [[concepts/valence|valence version of the explanatory gap]], and it survives every fragmentation and degeneracy finding. The Map takes the phenomenal horn on that undelivered step, and concedes that [[concepts/illusionism|illusionism]] denies there is a step to deliver.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]].** The tenet does symmetric work here, and every direction must be honoured. It blocks converting "no neural module" into "no kind," "one measurement scale" into "one felt quantity," and an absent neural bipolarity into an absent felt scalar. It equally blocks reading a supramodal orbitofrontal code as demonstrating that felt valence is one kind: Chikazoe's result is projectability evidence, compatible with the Map's reading rather than confirming it, and the workspace and modes findings stand alongside it unrefuted.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]].** The tenet's commitment about *how* consciousness selects is where the objection lands once sorted. The Map holds value-sensitive selection at low credence and framework-internal only, conceding that mechanism-sufficiency is the rival to beat; appraisal-theoretic micro-valence adds a second route to the same deflation, granting that the felt layer is real and denying that the scalar is. That concession is recorded here and only partly answered.

The framework boundary is stated by the rival itself: the 2025 restatement says the framework "explicitly avoids the dualism inherent in reductionist approaches to emotion." The Map claims no agreement from Barrett — only that her canonical placement of affect leaves the explanandum where the Map needs it, and that the recent drift is a proposal about implementation being read as a verdict on phenomenology.
