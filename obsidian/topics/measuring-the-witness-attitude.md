The Unfinishable Map argues from the witness, awareness that observes thoughts and sensations without identifying with them. The stimulation test proposed in [[brain-stimulation-and-the-witness-mode]] needs that attitude scored for a single session: non-identification, non-reactivity and non-judging, with control retained. Contemplative science's instruments differ in time-scale (trait, session or moment), in which part of decentering they tap, and in method (report, experience sampling or behavioural task). Three limits follow before any metaphysics enters. Every questionnaire score is a score of *reported* attitude. A trait score reports a disposition and cannot certify that a particular episode had the attitude. And the field's own critique of self-report (Grossman 2011; Van Dam et al. 2018) falls hardest on the meditator/non-meditator comparisons that the Map's contemplative evidence also uses.

The instruments can show whether a reported attitude was present in a session, whether it changes with training and whether it covaries with behaviour. They cannot show that the witness is irreducible, and the production, active-inference and interface readings of the witness all predict the same scores. The measured attitude therefore fits the Map's framing without evidencing it. [[#session-profile|The session profile]] (explained below) sets out what the stimulation test can score today, and [[#symmetric-cut|the symmetric cut]] records where the field's critique lands on the Map.

## Three Splits in the Instruments {#three-splits}

| Instrument | Construct, as its authors state it | Time-scale | Decentering process mainly covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire, FFMQ (Baer et al. 2008) | five facets, including non-judging and non-reactivity | dispositional | not classified by Bernstein et al. |
| Experiences Questionnaire, EQ, Decentering (Fresco et al. 2007) | observing thoughts and feelings as temporary events in the mind | trait | disidentification |
| Toronto Mindfulness Scale, TMS (Lau et al. 2006) | two factors, curiosity and decentering | state: the just-finished session | disidentification; one reactivity item |
| State Mindfulness Scale, SMS (Tanay & Bernstein 2013) | mindfulness of bodily sensations and of mental events | state | not classified |
| NADA-T and NADA-S (Hanley et al. 2018) | nondual awareness: self-transcendence and bliss | trait and state | — |
| Equanimity Scale-16, ES-16 (Rogers et al. 2021) | equanimity as "a non-reactive attitude" | validated as trait | not classified |
| MPE-92M (Gamma & Metzinger 2021) | twelve factors of "pure awareness", one named "Witness Consciousness" | one recalled episode | — |

**Time-scale.** Goldberg et al. (2016) call the FFMQ a measure "of dispositional mindfulness"; Lau et al. (2006) offered the TMS as "a promising measure of the mindfulness state", its form asking respondents to rate what they had just experienced.

**Component.** Bernstein et al. (2015) analyse decentering and its relatives, such as reperceiving, into three processes: "meta-awareness, disidentification from internal experience, and reduced reactivity to thought content". Against that model, "no items in the EQ clearly reflect reduced reactivity to thought content, and only one item in the Decentering subscale of the TMS reflects this process", so they treat both subscales as "primarily measures of ... disidentification from internal experience". By 2019 the group concluded that "no single or group of existing self-report scales assesses all three proposed metacognitive processes comprehensively" (Bernstein, Hadash & Fresco 2019).

**Method.** Retrospective questionnaires dominate (see [[#behaviour|Behaviour Instead of Report]]).

## What a Trait Score Cannot Certify {#trait-state}

Bernstein et al. "primarily conceptualize the processes subserving these constructs as mental states", and they "speculate that if activated repeatedly in time and across contexts, expression of these metacognitive processes may grow more stable or trait-like". On that model a trait is the sediment of many states. Inferring a particular episode's attitude back from the disposition needs assumptions about that episode which the trait score does not contain. A disposition raises the probability of a state and certifies none.

The case that matters is Pons et al. (2026), a survey of depersonalisation-like states triggered by meditation or otherwise. Non-judging and non-reactivity were two FFMQ facets rated "from 1 (Never or very rarely true) to 5 (Very often or always true)", compared "to explore whether differences in experience appraisal might reflect trait mindfulness", a legitimate trait question. As [[brain-stimulation-and-the-witness-mode#witness-or-depersonalisation|the brain-stimulation article]] records, the scores describe a standing attitude, and the episodes were rated in a later survey.

A generalizability-theory analysis (Truong et al. 2020) reports the FFMQ's facets as predominantly trait measures, and reports that a state subscale assembled from the least stable items fell short psychometrically. The ES-16 defines equanimity as an attitude and is validated, by test–retest stability over weeks, as a measure of "trait equanimity" (Rogers et al. 2021): sound design for its purpose, the wrong time-scale for an episode.

The split is a special case of one the Map already draws in [[descriptive-experience-sampling#moments|Moments Versus Generalisation]]: self-characterisation and moment-report are not interchangeable evidence. State forms can be validated, though. Hanley et al. (2018) modified the NADA-T's items to make the NADA-S, which registered state effects of a body-scan induction on reported nondual awareness, against an attention control, in a randomised experiment of 53 participants.

## The Session Profile the Stimulation Test Needs {#session-profile}

The [[brain-stimulation-and-the-witness-mode#design|proposed design]] scores a witness profile with four limbs, and the instruments meet them unevenly.

1. **Non-identification for the session.** TMS Decentering fits, measuring mainly disidentification on Bernstein et al.'s analysis; the SMS mental-events factor may partly fit, though that analysis does not classify it. In Lord et al.'s (2024) sham-controlled ultrasound pilot, TMS Decentering scores rose within both arms, active and sham.
2. **Non-reactivity and non-judging for the session.** The search behind this article (Crossref, Europe PMC, OpenAlex and four web searches, 2026-10-23) found no validated session-referenced measure of either. That is a finding of the search, not of the literature. In a daily-life sampling study read here only in abstract (Blanke & Brose 2017), momentary nonjudgmental acceptance, the nearest item set to non-judging, was corroborated as a factor and momentary nonreactivity was not. Ad hoc state rewordings of FFMQ items exist, though Pons et al. flag that adapting "questionnaire instructions and response formats" may affect validity, chiefly for their depersonalisation scale. The alternatives are in-session experience sampling, which one study used to track reduced reactivity during meditation (as described by Bernstein, Hadash & Fresco 2019), or a behavioural reactivity index.
3. **State nondual awareness.** Ehmann et al. (2025, preprint) used the NADA-S in an open-label ultrasound study during a retreat, and their one day-level test found no reliable rise on stimulation days.
4. **Retained control.** Scored behaviourally, as a timed, cued switch of attention, so it escapes self-report.

The test is specifiable today with one limb measured ad hoc. A more decisive version would add state or momentary ratings of all three processes, behavioural indices, instruction-matched controls, invariance checks between practised and naive groups, and blind scoring. Even then it would discriminate between attitude profiles, witness against [[depersonalisation]], not between metaphysical readings, each of which predicts the witness profile once the attitude is adopted ([[brain-stimulation-and-the-witness-mode#what-each-reading-predicts|What Each Reading Predicts]]).

## The Field's Own Critique of Self-Report {#self-report-critique}

The sharpest objections to mindfulness questionnaires come from inside contemplative science. Grossman (2011), writing about trait inventories, lists among other problems "inequivalence of semantic item interpretation among different groups", "response biases related to degree of experience with mindfulness practice" and "conflation of perceived mindfulness competencies with valuations of importance or meaningfulness". His remedy, long practice by researchers themselves, privileges practised understanding, so he is neither a critic of contemplative evidence as such nor an ally of first-person authority.

Van Dam et al. (2018) consolidate the case. Several scales show different factor structures in meditators and non-meditators, and before and after training, which "suggest lack of equivalence on a common underlying latent variable, as well as change in how the items are interpreted". Practitioners may value the items differently, "a potential conflation of desire to be 'mindful' with actually being 'mindful'". The claim that mindfulness improves introspection "has not been well established", and an irony follows: real gains in introspective accuracy "could increase honest responding, thereby exacerbating between group confounds". Their target is hype and weak method; their prescription is multimodal first- and third-person assessment.

In Goldberg et al.'s (2016) trial, FFMQ facets rose, relative to waitlist, in an active control without mindfulness instruction, no differently from mindfulness-based stress reduction, and "the FFMQ failed to show discriminant validity". On equanimity, Desbordes et al. (2015) judge, as a proposal rather than a measured result, that "most individuals will not be able to accurately assess their own levels of equanimity".

The counterweight belongs beside these. In demographically matched meditators and non-meditators, 115 of each, Baer, Samuel and Lykins (2011) found that "evidence for DIF was minimal" for differential item functioning by scoring direction, and concluded it "is not problematic" for such comparisons; Van Dam et al. flag the result themselves. It leaves the semantic and valuation worries standing. The critique is anchored in work from 2011 to 2019, and this search found no later invariance study that settles it.

## The One Factor Named for the Witness {#factor-12}

The only questionnaire factor this search found carrying the witness's name is Factor 12, "Witness Consciousness", of Gamma and Metzinger's (2021) MPE-92M. It rests chiefly on two items asking whether respondents had "the experience of a passive observer being present" or of "an impersonal observer being present". They answered about one recalled episode, chosen where several qualified as one "in which the quality of pure awareness was particularly salient and/or one which you can remember particularly clearly", in a self-selected online sample, and the twelve-factor structure "fit the data moderately well". The authors' limitations name "theory contamination", response drift, reports of experiences from "possibly even decades ago", and "a logical or a performative fallacy" in any participant's claim to have experienced a selfless state.

The factor is retrospective where the stimulation test needs a session, two items where it needs a profile, and a report of how an episode presented where the witness argument concerns structure. Metzinger's self-model theory is naturalist, and the paper glosses the Advaita witness at the level of phenomenology without endorsing its metaphysics. [[minimal-consciousness-void]] uses the twelve-factor model with its own caveat.

## Behaviour Instead of Report {#behaviour}

Bernstein et al. wrote in 2015 that "Laboratory-based behavioral measurement is lacking", and behavioural tasks promise an exit from self-report. Breath counting "differentiated long-term meditators from age-matched controls" (Levinson et al. 2014). An implicit-association task measures "the cognitive association of self with experience" (Hadash et al. 2016). Hadash and Bernstein (2019) judge four of 12 reviewed behavioural measures acceptably reliable, with preliminary construct validity. Van Dam et al. find neurobehavioural results "inconsistent and sometimes contradictory".

Two limits remain. A behavioural index of an experiential attitude is validated against report, reviving the problem the Map's [[descriptive-experience-sampling#fidelity-validity|fidelity/validity distinction]] names: a validated instrument licenses group comparison and correlation, not claims about what experience is like. And covariation between reported attitude and performance is predicted by every reading of the witness.

## The Symmetric Cut {#symmetric-cut}

The field raised its worries against mindfulness-intervention claims, but the Map leans on trained first-person reports in [[contemplative-epistemology]], [[contemplative-practice-as-philosophical-evidence]], [[methodology-of-consciousness-research]] and [[phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence]]. In the Map's own judgement, the response-bias and semantic-shift worries the field raises against mindfulness self-report (Grossman 2011; Van Dam et al. 2018), and the theory contamination Gamma and Metzinger name, cut against the Map's use of meditators' reports exactly as much, because a practised report is still a report. Van Dam et al.'s irony applies as well: if practice sharpened introspection, the confound between practised and naive reporters could deepen.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]].** The measured attitude fits the Map's two-mode reading of the witness without evidencing it. The Map holds weak [[revelation-thesis|Revelation]] only: a report that awareness presents as an observer distinct from its contents is a report of a presentation, real data that does not certify the observer's nature, its irreducibility, or the claim in [[witness-consciousness]] that the witness cannot become an object. No instrument here measures that structure; they measure reported relation to contents, reported reactivity and performance. Nor do the instruments' authors lean the Map's way. Bernstein et al. propose that observing subjective experience "creates a distinction" between "the observing self or consciousness and the observed subjective experience", with neural substrates: a psychological account on which observing makes the distinction rather than finding it, closer to constructivism than to the Map's reading. The self-model reading in [[predictive-self-binding-and-the-naturalist-challenge]] predicts the same scores.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]].** That the attitude is known only through report and performance is a limit on access, not evidence that the attitude or the experience is causally idle, and the [[epiphenomenalism|epiphenomenalist]] gains nothing from it. Covariation of reported attitude with breath counting or implicit identification is what the tenet permits, but every rival predicts it: compatibility, not support.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]].** The tenet constrains the Map's own reasoning. No measurement here refutes the simpler production or active-inference readings; the Map may decline to treat their simplicity as decisive, but may not claim the measurements disconfirm them.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]] and [[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]].** No bearing: attitude scales sit far above any level at which the Map locates the interface.
