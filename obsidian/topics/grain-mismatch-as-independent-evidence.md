The [[grain-mismatch|grain mismatch]] is usually presented as a single philosophical argument: Sellars argued that phenomenal homogeneity and neural discreteness are structurally incompatible, therefore identity physicalism fails for these property pairs. The Unfinishable Map proposes something stronger. The grain mismatch is not one argument but three independent lines of structural evidence — spatial, temporal, and informational — each arising from different empirical domains, relying on different measurements, and generating different testable predictions. The independence claimed is of a specific, limited kind — independence of falsifiers: any one mismatch could be dissolved while the other two stood. All three nonetheless describe the grain of a single phenomenon, the felt character of conscious experience, and that shared source means the convergence carries less than the weight of three fully independent measurements — a discount the programme concedes up front and cannot yet quantify (see [Why Convergence Matters](#why-convergence-matters)). Within that calibration, the convergence constitutes an evidence programme rather than a conceivability exercise. If all three were independently dissolved, dualism would lose one of its most direct structural supports.

## Three Independent Mismatches

### Spatial Grain

Sellars' original argument (1962, 1971) targets spatial properties. Phenomenal experience presents homogeneous fields — uniform pink, smooth warmth, continuous extension. Neural substrates are spatially discrete: individual neurons separated by synaptic gaps, firing or not firing, with no intrinsic colour or temperature. The mismatch is not that we *describe* these at different levels of detail — the granularity diagnosis Bechtel and Mundale brought against multiple-realizability arguments, which a physicalist might turn on this case — but that the properties themselves, homogeneity versus discreteness, are contradictory. A property cannot be both gapless and gapped. The strongest physicalist reply puts homogeneity in what neural states *represent* rather than in the states themselves, as a pixel grid depicts an unbroken sky. It assumes felt homogeneity [[first-order-representationalism|just is represented homogeneity]] without showing why representing a smooth field should feel smooth; the dualist takes the datum to be the character of the experience itself, and there the disagreement reaches the framework boundary.

The argument requires that neural correlates of conscious experience be discrete at some level. A correlate with genuine spatial continuity — a field property, perhaps electromagnetic, both measurably continuous and demonstrably identical to phenomenal experience — would dissolve the spatial mismatch. Electromagnetic field theories of consciousness (McFadden, Pockett) attempt this, but it is not obvious that classical fields, continuous though they are, carry the qualitative character distinguishing one experience from another. A field theorist may answer that field topology encodes that character; the mismatch then turns on whether the continuity is identical to homogeneous experience or merely correlated with it.

### Temporal Grain

The brain samples the world rhythmically. VanRullen's review of perceptual cycles finds a sensory alpha rhythm (~10 Hz) alongside an attentional sampling rhythm near 7 Hz; gamma synchrony (~40 Hz) may bind features; and features integrate within discrete windows lasting up to about 450 milliseconds, depending on the observer (Drissi-Daoudi, Doerig & Herzog 2019). VanRullen states the upshot conditionally — that brain function involves oscillations at various frequencies "could imply that perception and cognition operate periodically, as a succession of cycles mirroring the underlying oscillations" — and whether rhythmic sampling amounts to genuinely discrete perception is still argued. Yet conscious experience presents temporal smoothness — no flickers, no dropped frames, no perceptible gaps between moments.

This temporal mismatch is logically independent of the spatial one. An identity theorist could concede that spatial phenomenal properties are not identical to spatial neural properties while maintaining that temporal experience *is* identical to temporal neural processing. To dissolve the temporal mismatch specifically, one would need to show either that temporal experience is genuinely discrete (matching neural periodicity) or that neural temporal processing is genuinely continuous at the relevant level. As [[neural-refresh-rates-and-the-smoothness-problem|the smoothness problem]] details, the two-stage model of Herzog and colleagues (Herzog, Drissi-Daoudi & Doerig 2020) attempts the latter by locating continuity in unconscious processing — but this relocates the problem rather than solving it, since the question is why *conscious* experience is smooth when the *neural correlate of consciousness* is periodic.

Andrew Lee's work on smooth experience may still be relevant here, though it does not transfer cleanly. Lee concludes that "introspection leaves open whether smooth experiences are continuous or discrete," but he is explicit that his target question and the question about the stream of consciousness are structurally distinct — the first concerns continuous functions, the second continuous spaces — and that his arguments "won't directly adjudicate debates about the stream of consciousness," though he thinks it plausible that a similar analysis could be developed for it. To whatever extent it does transfer, the upshot is that introspective evidence does not settle whether temporal smoothness is genuine continuity or a fine-grained discreteness. The structural puzzle persists either way.

### Informational Grain

Peripheral sensory capacity runs at roughly 10⁹ bits per second. Conscious declarative throughput — the rate at which reportable decisions resolve — runs at approximately 10 bits per second, a compression ratio of 100 million to one. The figure is a Shannon-calibrated behavioural throughput — Zheng and Meister call it a "ballpark number" — that has held at the order-of-magnitude level across roughly seven decades of work and radically different tasks, from 1952 choice-reaction experiments through typing, gaming, and speedcubing (Zheng & Meister 2025; see [[bandwidth-of-consciousness|the bandwidth of consciousness]] for the measurement history). One caveat travels with the number: Zheng and Meister measured *behavioural* throughput in tasks requiring conscious engagement, so the step from "behavioural ceiling in conscious tasks" to a claim about phenomenal content is an interpretation, not a direct measurement. Taken at the level the evidence licenses, the mismatch is informational: conscious output and access throughput is eight orders of magnitude coarser than the sensory intake it draws on — and whether the phenomenal content underlying that output is correspondingly coarse, as Block's access/phenomenal distinction leaves open, is a further question the figure does not settle.

This third mismatch is independent of the first two: even if phenomenal space were discrete and phenomenal time periodic, dissolving both, the compression ratio would remain.

The informational mismatch generates its own prediction. If the compression ratio is a property of the mind-brain *interface* rather than of neural architecture, brain-side optimisation should not change it. Zheng and Meister's survey is consistent with this: well-practised subjects and world-record holders still land near 10 bits per second. Sauerbrei and Pruszynski (2025) add that unconscious processing for real-time motor control substantially exceeds the limit, which appears to hold for high-level cognitive functions — so it is no brain-wide ceiling but sits where consciousness participates. Both teams are physicalists who locate that ceiling in neural architecture, and a fixed neural bottleneck would also resist training; the interface reading is the Map's, and only interventions that alter the architecture itself (Prediction 2 below) could separate the two.

## Why Convergence Matters

Any single grain mismatch might be explained away. The spatial mismatch might reflect our ignorance of the right level of neural description. The temporal mismatch might dissolve under a correct theory of temporal binding. The informational mismatch might turn out to be a contingent feature of human neural architecture rather than a principled interface constraint.

What resists easy dissolution is the convergence. Three domains — space, time, information — each exhibit a structural mismatch between conscious and neural grain. Each mismatch was discovered through different empirical methods (neuroanatomy, oscillation measurement, information-theoretic analysis). Most physicalist responses would reach only one of them. Some would reach two: the vehicle/content reply above, or a demonstration that experience is finely discrete (see [What Would Dissolve the Programme](#what-would-dissolve-the-programme)), would take the spatial and temporal legs together. No such shared response yet touches the informational leg.

When multiple genuinely independent measurements point to the same structural feature, the probability of all being systematically misleading is multiplicatively low. Whether the grain programme earns that arithmetic depends on how independent the three mismatches are — independent in their falsifiers, as the next paragraph argues, but not fully in their source. The continental-drift comparison marks both the aspiration and the gap. Geological, palaeontological, and geomagnetic data were individually contestable but jointly decisive, and they came from independent records — rocks, fossils, magnetised strata — whereas the phenomenal side of every grain mismatch is drawn from one consciousness. The Map does not claim that level of decisiveness, and this disanalogy is one reason the multiplicative arithmetic cannot simply be borrowed.

The Map's standing discipline for convergence claims is the [[project/common-cause-null|common-cause null]]: when N observations converge, ask whether they share an upstream architecture that would produce the convergence by itself, collapsing the apparent triangulation toward one observation read N times. A physicalist could object that homogeneity, smoothness, and informational coarseness are not three separate observations but three descriptions of one feature: the felt unity of consciousness. If so, the three mismatches share a common cause, and their agreement no longer multiplies the way independent measurements do. The programme's reply is that the *explananda* are distinct even if the *explanandum* of consciousness is one. The neural sides are measured by different instruments — discreteness by neuroanatomy, periodicity by oscillation studies, throughput by Shannon-calibrated behaviour; each mismatch could in principle be dissolved while the others stood, and a physicalist mechanism that resolved one (say, a continuous electromagnetic correlate for spatial homogeneity) would leave the informational compression ratio untouched. The coupling is tightest between the spatial and temporal legs, whose phenomenal sides both rest on introspected continuity and which share candidate dissolvers; the informational leg, both of whose terms are measured, is the most independent of the three and, by the caveat above, the least directly phenomenal. Independence at the level of *what could falsify each claim* survives even if all three trace to the unity of experience, and it keeps the mismatches from being redundant; it falls short of the independence of error that full multiplication would need, and the programme cannot yet quantify the shortfall. In the discipline's own terms, the felt-unity objection names real shared machinery but licenses calibration rather than dissolution: one felt unity does not by itself predict the specific pattern the measurements return — an eight-order-of-magnitude compression ratio in one domain, integration windows of up to roughly 450 milliseconds in another. What it removes, and what this article accordingly does not claim, is the right to count the convergence as three fully independent votes.

## Testable Predictions

What distinguishes an evidence programme from a conceivability argument is falsifiability. The grain mismatch programme generates specific predictions:

**Prediction 1: Neural correlates will remain discrete.** As neuroscience discovers the precise neural correlates of specific phenomenal experiences, the spatial grain mismatch predicts these correlates will invariably be discrete at some level. Discovery of a genuinely continuous neural correlate — one that is measurably homogeneous and demonstrably the correlate of a homogeneous experience — would weaken the spatial argument.

**Prediction 2: Conscious throughput will remain invariant.** If the informational mismatch reflects an interface constraint rather than a neural limitation, then no combination of training, pharmacology, or genetic modification should raise conscious throughput by orders of magnitude. A breakthrough that reliably pushed conscious processing to 1,000 bits per second would challenge the interface interpretation (though not necessarily the broader dualist framework).

**Prediction 3: Temporal smoothness will resist neural explanation.** As neural theories of temporal binding improve, the temporal mismatch predicts they will explain *integration* (how discrete samples are combined) without explaining *smoothness* (why the result feels continuous). Any theory that derived phenomenal smoothness from neural periodicity — explaining *why* discrete processing produces continuous experience, not just that it does — would dissolve the temporal argument.

**Prediction 4: Cross-species grain ratios will covary.** This is the most speculative prediction. If [[interface-heterogeneity|different conscious systems]] couple with matter through different mechanisms, grain mismatches may vary across species while keeping a systematic relationship: wherever consciousness is present, some mismatch adapted to the organism's interface architecture but structurally analogous should be detectable. A conscious organism with no grain mismatch in any domain would be significant counter-evidence, though testing this requires first settling which organisms are conscious.

## What Would Dissolve the Programme

The evidence programme would be substantially weakened by:

**Resolution of all three mismatches independently.** If spatial, temporal, and informational mismatches were each explained by domain-specific physicalist mechanisms, the convergence argument would lose its force. The honest test is actual explanations for each specific mismatch, not an argument that grain mismatches can *in principle* be explained.

**Demonstration that phenomenal homogeneity is illusory.** If the question Lee leaves open were closed — if it were shown that phenomenal experience is discrete at a fine grain matching neural architecture — the spatial and temporal mismatches would dissolve at their foundation. The informational mismatch would survive (discrete conscious experience could still be informationally coarser than its sensory intake), but the programme would lose two of its three pillars.

**A physicalist theory that *predicts* the grain ratios.** Currently, physicalist accounts explain that *some* compression exists ([[global-workspace-theory|Global Workspace Theory's]] serial broadcasting, for instance) without explaining why the compression has the specific magnitude it does. A theory that derived the ~10 bits/second figure from neural architecture — and explained why that specific bottleneck occurs at conscious access rather than elsewhere — would provide the kind of explanatory resource the programme claims physicalism lacks.

## Is Compositional Grain a Fourth Mismatch?

The [[the-binding-problem|binding problem]] exhibits its own grain structure: multimodal inputs arrive at different speeds, in different formats, to different cortical regions, yet bind into unified experience. This *compositional grain* — where the parts of experience and the parts of neural processing are carved at incompatible joints — looks at first like a fourth candidate mismatch. The programme should test it by the criteria that establish the other legs: distinct measurements, a distinct physicalist response, a distinct falsifier. All three tests fail, and compositional grain reduces to the spatial and temporal mismatches rather than adding to them.

**Its own measurements?** No. The two joints at which neural processing is "carved" are spatial (distributed feature maps in separate cortical regions) and temporal (features tagged by gamma-band synchrony within shared time windows — see [[the-binding-problem]]). Compositional grain is detected through neuroanatomy and oscillation measurement — the very instruments that operationalise the spatial and temporal legs. It opens no third measurement domain.

**Its own required physicalist response?** No. The response the binding mismatch demands is a binding theory: feature-integration and convergence accounts (spatial) plus binding-by-synchrony (temporal). A physicalist who discharged the spatial and temporal explanatory burdens would thereby discharge the compositional one. It carries no distinct burden.

**Its own falsifier?** No. What would dissolve the compositional mismatch is a successful reductive account of how distributed, asynchronous processing yields unified phenomenology — which just is the conjunction of the spatial falsifier (a genuinely continuous correlate) and the temporal falsifier (a theory deriving continuity from periodicity). No separate falsifier could retire compositional grain while leaving the other two standing.

Compositional grain is therefore a *corollary* of the spatial and temporal legs — those mismatches applied to phenomenal unity, a vivid illustration rather than a new pillar. Where it seems to carry surplus content beyond them — the sheer felt unity of the bound whole — that surplus is precisely the common phenomenal source flagged under [Why Convergence Matters](#why-convergence-matters): not an independent fourth observation but the single unity of which the three legs each measure a facet. Quantum and predictive treatments of binding ([[entanglement-binding-hypothesis]], [[predictive-self-binding-and-the-naturalist-challenge]]), and the panpsychist [[combination-problem]], all bear on *how* that unity arises, not on whether compositional grain is a separate measurement channel.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The grain mismatch evidence programme gives [[tenets#^dualism|the Map's first tenet]] suggestive support, conditional on the phenomenal datum the vehicle/content reply contests. Where other arguments for dualism rest on conceivability intuitions (zombies) or epistemic considerations (Mary's Room), the grain programme anchors one side of each mismatch in measurement: neural discreteness, oscillatory periodicity, and conscious throughput are all measured. In the spatial and temporal legs the other side — homogeneity, smoothness — is reported from within experience, so each mismatch pairs a measurement with a phenomenal datum and calls for explanation in a way a thought experiment does not.

The programme also illustrates [[tenets#^occams-limits|why Occam's Razor has limits]]. The "simpler" identity theory asserts that phenomenal properties just *are* neural properties. The grain programme argues that, on the dualist reading of the phenomenal datum, this assertion is structurally incoherent for property pairs with contradictory characteristics — gapless against gapped, smooth against periodic — and that it leaves the magnitude of the informational ratio unexplained. Simplicity purchased at the cost of coherence is no bargain. This connects to the Map's broader case that [[causal-closure|causal closure]] assumptions cannot override structural incompatibilities — no amount of methodological commitment to physicalism resolves a logical mismatch between property types, if that is what the mismatch is; the vehicle/content reply denies it.

The refusal to pad the programme with a fourth leg (see [Is Compositional Grain a Fourth Mismatch?](#is-compositional-grain-a-fourth-mismatch) above) reflects the same discipline. A dualism that inflated its evidence count would be easier to dismiss; a dualism that counts its structural evidence at exactly the number that survives an independence test earns the calibration it claims — and, under the [[project/common-cause-null|common-cause null]], holds even that number subject to the shared-source discount conceded above.
