If consciousness is real and causally efficacious, it needs a way to reach the physical world. The Unfinishable Map proposes that [[attention-as-interface|attention is that way]]—not identical to consciousness, but the interface through which consciousness selects among the possibilities the brain generates. This claim has specific structure: attention divides into modes with distinct neural signatures, the effort of sustaining it is where the Map looks for causal work, its disorders expose the interface's components, and its limits create cognitive voids that no amount of attentional skill can eliminate. The attention cluster is the Map's most detailed account of how mind acts on matter.

The argument proceeds through three layers. First, the empirical case: attention and consciousness dissociate in ways that constrain what consciousness can be. Second, the mechanistic proposal: attention is a unified selection interface, shared by perception and action, whose conscious behavioural throughput is roughly 10 bits per second (Zheng & Meister 2025)—a measure of what conscious agents *do*, not of the brain's processing rate, which Sauerbrei and Pruszynski (2025) argue is orders of magnitude faster in unconscious motor control. Third, the implications: if agency flows through attention, then freedom scales with attentional skill, and the limits of attention define the limits of conscious influence.

## Attention Is Not Consciousness

The foundation is a dissociation: [[attention-and-the-consciousness-interface|attention and consciousness come apart]]. Koch and Tsuchiya's (2007) framework—neuroscience seeking consciousness's material substrate, not a dualist argument—identifies four combinations: attention with consciousness (focused awareness), attention without consciousness (blindsight, masked priming), consciousness without attention (peripheral phenomenal overflow), and neither (dreamless sleep).

Most cognitive dissociations are theoretically tame, two brain systems operating independently; this one has the [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard problem]] on one side. On Ned Block's contested reading, consciousness without attention is phenomenal experience outrunning cognitive access—subjects see all the letters in a display though they report only 3-4—where Cohen and Dennett read the unreported letters as experienced only generically. Nartker, Firestone, Egeth and Phillips (2025, *eLife*) sharpen the dissociation without settling it: inattentionally blind participants can report the location, colour and shape of stimuli they deny noticing—information that stays accessible, which is not yet experience outrunning access. The 2025 adversarial COGITATE study (Cogitate Consortium; Ferrante, Gorska-Klimowska, et al., 2025) found neither Global Workspace Theory nor Integrated Information Theory clearly vindicated by pre-registered empirical tests—a challenge to particular predictions of both frameworks, not a selection of dualism.

The Map's interpretation: consciousness and attention are ontologically distinct. Consciousness is the non-physical subject; attention is the physical mechanism through which that subject engages with neural processes. One spectral finding is consistent with the separability. In an MEG study of faint gratings at attended and unattended locations, Wyart and Tallon-Baudry (2008) found consciously seen stimuli driving mid-frequency gamma (54–64 Hz) over contralateral visual cortex, while spatial attention modulated high-frequency gamma (76–90 Hz) whether or not the stimulus was seen. The result is paradigm-specific, establishing a dissociation inside that paradigm rather than fixed operating frequencies for consciousness and attention; whether it generalises across modalities remains open. They can come apart because they are different things that normally work together—like a pianist and a piano.

## The Three Modes

[[structure-of-attention|Attention divides into three distinct modes]], each with different triggers, timescales, and neural signatures:

**Exogenous attention** (~100ms): a loud noise, a flash of light. The ventral attention network captures it reflexively; consciousness registers a selection the brain has already made.

**Instructed attention** (~300ms): "look at the red square." An external cue directs attention through the dorsal attention network; consciousness follows an established rule.

**Willed attention** (~300ms): you decide to focus on a difficult passage. In spatial cueing, three markers distinguish it from instructed attention (Rajan et al. 2019)—frontal theta oscillations, bidirectional frontoparietal theta coherence, and additional frontal recruitment—while the salience network switches processing between stimulus-driven and endogenous control.

That frontal localisation is contested. Whether prefrontal activity tracks consciousness or only the machinery of reporting it remains open in the [[neural-correlates-of-consciousness|neural-correlates literature]], and these markers read equally as executive registration of a self-initiated selection rather than as a non-physical contributor entering.

The willed mode is where the Map locates the candidate conscious contribution. Its ~300ms timescale has been read as shared with motor selection, but the numbers are not yet commensurable: Thura and Cisek's (2014) ~280ms commitment point counts back from movement onset in monkeys choosing reaches, while ~300ms is a post-cue deployment latency for voluntary attention in humans (Müller & Rabbitt 1989), and Rajan et al.'s theta marker itself begins only ~500ms post-cue. A common zero-point in one species would make the comparison testable. The architectural case stands regardless: [[attention-and-the-consciousness-interface|Rizzolatti's premotor theory]] gives attending and preparing to act shared substrates, and Fine and Hayden (2022) call all prefrontal cortex premotor. One interface may serve thought and action alike, on the anatomy rather than the clock.

## Why Effort Matters

The [[mental-effort|felt cost of sustaining attention]] is the phenomenological datum most often cited for consciousness doing real causal work. William James saw this clearly: "The essential achievement of the will, when it is most 'voluntary,' is to attend to a difficult object and hold it fast before the mind." Kahneman's *Attention and Effort* (1973) made effort measurable—pupil dilation and heart rate rise with it—and effort is not simply task difficulty: a boring task can cost more to attend to than a gripping one. [[self-control-as-skill-and-scaffolding|The self-control literature]] adds a caution. If felt effort is control's cost signal, it marks where control is hard, not necessarily where conscious contribution is largest.

For materialists, effort phenomenology poses a puzzle: if effort is neural computation, why does it *feel* like work? The illusionist answers that the brain models its own processing as effortful. Asking who experiences the model does not refute this, since a representational system need not instantiate what it represents; the pressure falls on the [[the-relocation-objection|relocation]], because why the model should *seem* to deliver a feeling has not yet proved more tractable than why trying feels like anything.

One proposed mechanism is [[stapp-quantum-mind|Stapp's quantum Zeno effect]]: rapid, repeated observation holds desired neural patterns stable against decoherence. Neural decoherence may be too fast for that, and nothing in the mechanism fixes whether frequent observation stabilises the attended pattern or accelerates its decay; acceleration is the more common outcome, so the stabilising direction has to be argued for ([[sign-problem-for-conscious-observation]]). The phenomenology stands as data regardless of mechanism.

Sjöberg's 2024 essay in *Brain* reflects on his group's surgical cohort: patients with the supplementary motor area resected retained the sense of willing—reporting maximal effort to move—while losing voluntary movement. Sjöberg, no dualist, reads the cases as making Libet's paradigm largely irrelevant to free will; the argument here is the Map's own. If effort were merely what certain neural processes feel like, removing the execution machinery should have altered it, and it did not. That removes a defeater without confirming that consciousness does the work: losing one structure shows only that it is not *necessary* for the phenomenology, whose substrate may sit elsewhere in the brain, and a forward-model account (command issued, execution blocked, prediction error registered as effort) predicts the same datum. [[volitional-control|The phenomenology of volitional control]] treats the cohort in full, including 2025 lesion work showing SMA-complex resections impair executive function, not execution alone.

[[mental-effort|Three converging dissociations]] show the felt sense detaching from the operation it ostensibly reports on. Naccache et al. (2005) describe a patient whose left anterior cingulate lesion extended into the callosal genu and orbitofrontal cortex, and who performed Stroop conflict trials with normal executive control while reporting no conscious feeling of mental effort and showing none of the skin-conductance coupling controls exhibited as effort rose with conflict. Hagger et al.'s (2016) preregistered 23-laboratory replication (N=2,141, d=0.04) and Vohs et al.'s (2021) proponent-led test (N=3,531, d=0.06) failed the standard ego-depletion paradigm; an intensity-dependent effect may survive (Dang et al., 2025), but not the strength-resource model behind the felt-drained sense. Kurzban et al. (2013) and Inzlicht et al. (2021) reframe felt effort as an opportunity-cost computation or adaptive signal rather than a fuel-gauge readout. These results *constrain* rather than *establish* the dualist reading, removing the simplest "felt effort just is what the operation feels like" identity. Naccache and colleagues go further: the feeling of conscious effort "would be a by-product of executive attention, but would not play a causal role in its deployment", and the absent coupling beside intact Stroop control tells against the coupling doing the work. Yet the dissociation held only on simple laboratory tasks, and the authors record executive dysfunction in the same patient on problem-solving and dual-task demands, so the epiphenomenalist generalisation is not earned either. Whether felt effort does genuine causal work is left open, a debt [[tenets#^tenet-3-standing|Tenet 3 records]] rather than discharges; the case is one member of the [[phenomenal-output-causal-machinery-dissociation|phenomenal-output / causal-machinery dissociation cluster]], whose weight is cumulative.

## The Three-Layer Architecture

The clinical and neuroscientific evidence converges on a two-layer architecture; the Map adds a third:

1. **Neural computation** generates competing options—multiple potential actions represented simultaneously in premotor cortex
2. **Dopamine** sets the selection threshold—marking options as salient and modulating how easily they become actions
3. **Consciousness**, on the Map's hypothesis, biases which option crosses threshold—through sustained attentional engagement

Cai et al. (2024, *Nature*) dissociated two dopamine signalling modes. Mice whose spike-evoked dopamine transients were abolished by dopamine-neuron-specific RIM knockout—baseline dopamine persisting—moved normally and still learned reward cues, but initiated fewer trials and pursued reward with less vigour. Baseline dopamine remained necessary for movement itself: depletion or receptor blockade produced bradykinesia, and L-DOPA restored movement without restoring the transients. The paper does not test the third layer: the architecture is consistent with rather than licensed by Cai et al., and stands or falls with the broader interface argument. Desmurget et al. (2009) found that parietal stimulation produces conscious intention to move—even false belief of having moved—without any muscle activity, while premotor stimulation produces movement without awareness. Intention and execution are thereby neurally separable, though the intervention runs stimulation → conscious intention, and the authors read the result as making intention and awareness "emerging consequences" of parietal activity. The Map's policy-level reading is a further claim the dissociation permits rather than supports. Of the five candidate coupling mechanisms the [[psychophysical-laws|psychophysical laws]] article identifies, the architecture instantiates the first two: attention-bounded selection (attention as observation rate) and policy-level selection (intention as policy choice).

## Does Attention Select at All?

The sharpest challenge to this vocabulary comes from Fazekas and Nanay (2021): attention is amplification, not selection. Its core realiser is the not-stimulus-triggered amplification of presynaptic signals feeding the brain's built-in normalisation computations; selective effects arise downstream, from perceptual machinery that winnows whether or not attention is engaged. Attention raises the gain; the circuitry selects. Read as neuroscience, the Map can accept this wholesale: the three-layer architecture already assigns the winnowing to neural competition and casts the conscious contribution as a gain-shaped bias on which option crosses threshold. The account even specifies the lever—whatever sets the gain sets the bias—while characterising the gain's source only negatively, as not stimulus-triggered.

Two cautions temper the fit. Amplification is continuous and graded: winner-take-all competition still issues in discrete outcomes, but a smoothly varying gain names no privileged juncture at which quantum-level influence would enter. The thalamic gate, where [[structure-of-attention|descending goals meet ascending competition]], is a candidate site; turning a candidate into a juncture is a specification the [[interface-specification-programme|interface-specification programme]] owes rather than discharges. And whether consciousness contributes to setting the gain is precisely what the precision-weighting reading below—amplification's close kin—denies; Fazekas and Nanay's sub-personal account is silent between the readings. Their challenge renames the Map's mechanism rather than removing it, at the honest price that "selection" names what consciousness does with the amplified field, not what the attentional machinery itself does.

## The Economics of Agency

If consciousness acts through attention, and conscious behavioural throughput is limited to roughly 10 bits per second, then agency is an [[attentional-economics|economic problem]]. Every act of attention is simultaneously an act of exclusion; freedom becomes a resource to be allocated rather than a binary capacity.

The economic framing [[responsibility-gradient-from-attentional-capacity|relocates moral responsibility]] from the moment of decision—where traditional accounts focus—to the earlier cultivation or neglect of attentional capacity. Contemplative training lets a person sustain selection longer, resist distraction and notice options untrained attention would miss.

The attentional economy has a limit beyond bandwidth: *habituation*. Familiar experiences—the kitchen you navigate without attending to it—drop out of phenomenal awareness while remaining functionally processed, and what has left awareness can no longer be a target of attentional selection. The meditator practising "beginner's mind" is learning to *de-habituate*, restoring the familiar to the phenomenal field and so to the options attention can invest in.

The witness mode, in which observing feels free of selecting, is a distinct economic regime: contemplatives who enter [[witness-consciousness|witness consciousness]] exercise meta-agency, choosing to hold selection back rather than direct it.

## Disorders as Component Failures

Clinical cases show what happens when parts of the interface break: [[attention-and-the-consciousness-interface|ADHD, attention fatigue, depression, and meditation deficits]] each expose a different component.

ADHD primarily impairs willed attention while sparing, sometimes enhancing, captured attention. Stimulants modulate the dopamine-mediated selection threshold, and treated patients report not altered experience but intentions translating more reliably into action: on the interface reading, the interface is compromised rather than consciousness itself.

Attention fatigue shows the interface has running costs: sustained selection degrades and recovers with rest, particularly sleep, while motivation can temporarily override it—rationing rather than exhaustion, which suits the opportunity-cost accounts above better than a draining fuel tank.

Depression brings characteristic attentional biases—capture by negative information, poor disengagement from it. On the interface reading, consciousness works through an interface tilted toward negative content.

An epistemological difficulty lurks here: from inside an attention disorder we cannot tell whether consciousness is unchanged behind a degraded interface or altered in ways the interface hides. That void marks where first-person and third-person investigation both reach their limits.

## The Return Cycle

A single attentional event captures the interface in miniature. [[phenomenology-of-returning-attention|When attention wanders and then returns]]—during meditation, reading, any sustained task—three phases unfold:

**Wandering**: meta-awareness is absent, the default mode network active. You don't know your attention has drifted until it already has.

**Noticing**: a sudden, involuntary recognition—"I was distracted." The salience network activates; noticing happens *to* you.

**Returning**: effortful re-engagement as executive-network regions, including dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, come back online. This phase is something *you do*—selection that costs effort and feels authored.

On the Map's reading the three phases exemplify bidirectional interaction within a single event: the brain wanders on its own (bottom-up), recognition occurs at the interface (salience detection), consciousness re-engages through willed selection (top-down). Rival neural accounts fit the same structure, so this is consistency with the tenet rather than a prediction only it makes. Contemplative training shortens wandering, softens noticing, and eventually makes returning nearly effortless—the interface is plastic.

## Engaging Attention Schema Theory

[[attention-schema-theory|Attention Schema Theory]] offers the most developed materialist alternative. Graziano proposes that consciousness is the brain's simplified model of its own attention—an "attention schema" analogous to the body schema. AST's central move is to identify *being represented as phenomenal* with *being phenomenal*: inside AST's own framework, the question "what experiences the schema?" is the schema generating its own characteristic confusion, not a residue awaiting reply.

The Map's engagement [[attention-and-the-consciousness-interface|identifies a step the framework has not earned]] rather than refuting it. By the mechanistic standard the materialist programme imposes on rival theories, AST owes the bridge from representational content to ontic status. The standard reply—that *recursive* self-modelling makes the difference—names the architectural feature without delivering the bridge: a chess engine running diagnostics on its own search performs recursive self-modelling without generating any apparent phenomenality. AST's sharpest version turns on opacity: the schema has no access to the implementation realising it, so any system built this way models its own states as non-physical and reports an explanatory gap. That is a genuine mechanism for why the gap *seems* unbridgeable; the step from being so represented to being phenomenal stays asserted.

The disagreement runs deeper still. AST treats phenomenal experience as an artefact of representational content; the Map takes it as ontologically primary. Neither refutes the other inside the rival's framework, and the dispute is over which framework to inhabit rather than a victory either can claim on the other's terms.

What AST gets right is that the brain almost certainly models its own attentional processes, which explains access consciousness and reportability. The willed-instructed distinction adds a constraint the schema framework does not address—the schema tracks attention whatever its initiation source—and [[witness-consciousness|witness consciousness]] poses awareness apparently without attentional engagement, though AST can rejoin that practice may deepen the schema's apparent fundamentality. The phenomenal/access gap remains.

## How a Sophisticated Rival Reads the Same Evidence

A sophisticated materialist can accept all of the evidence above and read it differently. The most developed such reading is the **predictive-processing account** (Hohwy, Clark, Friston): attention is the precision-weighting of prediction error, the brain raising the gain on channels it expects to be reliable, and "selection" is what high-precision weighting looks like from the inside. Where the Map sees a non-physical subject biasing which option crosses threshold, predictive processing sees a precision parameter set by the generative model itself.

On this reading the four-way dissociation is precision decoupling from access—high-precision processing without global broadcast, and broadcast of low-precision content—with no ontological distinctness required. The willed-mode markers are the signature of endogenous precision-setting under a high-level prior. The three-layer architecture maps onto the generative-model hierarchy: premotor competition is the option space, dopamine the precision term, and "conscious selection" the settling of the inference. The COGITATE null tells against *both* Global Workspace Theory and IIT without telling for the Map—an unsettled contest among materialist theories is not evidence for dualism.

The readings might come apart in two places, and neither yet delivers a settled discriminator. The first is effort phenomenology. Predictive processing can model effort as the expected cost of reallocating precision, but it inherits the debt pressed against illusionism above: a precision parameter being reset is a functional event, and the account does not say why it should be *felt* as costly. The felt-effort dissociations cut against the simplest dualist identity too, so the residue constrains both readings. The second is a candidate forward claim: that phenomenal effort scales with the entropy of the option set at threshold-crossing, controlling for stimulus discriminability, on the intuition that precision tracks expected reliability, not option-set cardinality. Active inference predicts much the same correlation. Sandved-Smith et al. (2021) model covert attentional control as policy selection over mental actions, and Parr et al. (2023) formalise mental effort as covert mental action in the very Stroop paradigm the effort argument above leans on, where effort scales with how far a task drives beliefs about policies away from their priors—a quantity that rises as the option set grows more contested. The test bites only if the two accounts predict different functional forms, and none has been derived, so the discriminator is a debt on the interface hypothesis rather than a measurement awaiting a laboratory, and the disagreement sits closer to bedrock than the dissociation evidence suggests. Naming the rival is the [[evidential-status-discipline|evidential-status discipline]] at work.

That admits the rival in one register only, as a *reading* of the attention evidence. Active inference and the free-energy principle also come as a *total* generative framework that natively models what the interface hypothesis reserves for a non-physical subject—agency, attention, effort and interoceptive selfhood (Seth's "beast machine"). [[predictive-processing-and-dualism|The Map's treatment of predictive processing and dualism]] carries that framework-level confrontation, steelmanning active inference in its own vocabulary, engaging Laukkonen, Friston and Chandaria's (2025) beautiful-loop theory as the strongest physicalist rival, and concluding that the residue is a framework boundary rather than a refutation in either direction.

## The Void at Attention's Core

Every interface has limits. [[attention-and-consciousness|Attention creates voids]] as a structural necessity: to attend to anything is to render something else unattendable. Gestalt figure-ground, Husserl's thematic field and margin, and James's unfocusable fringes share one structure—the periphery cannot be promoted to the centre without creating a new periphery.

Selectivity is constitutive of engagement rather than a design flaw to be overcome: a consciousness that attended to everything at once would not be attending at all. On this picture agency is bounded not by lack of will but by the structure of the bridge through which will operates. A corollary the [[embodied-interface|embodied-interface synthesis]] draws out: the searchlight illuminates only a small region of the felt body, so most passive felt experience does no selective work at all.

## Synthesis

Five components interlock. The attention–consciousness dissociation—blindsight, contested peripheral overflow, a paradigm-specific frequency-band separation—shows the two functionally distinct and leaves room for the Map's ontological reading. The three-mode architecture places the candidate contribution in willed attention, whose markers are heightened relative to instructed attention. Effort phenomenology is where the Map looks for causal work, though the SMA cases only remove a defeater, the felt-effort dissociations constrain without establishing, and felt effort may mark difficulty rather than contribution. The three-layer architecture—neural competition, dopamine threshold, conscious bias—specifies how the interface would be organised, and the amplification and predictive-processing readings show the same organisation can be read without a non-physical term. The bandwidth and habituation constraints bound where conscious influence can operate. What the pieces achieve together is a narrowing: each constrains where a conscious contribution could sit, and none yet shows that one does.

A candid limitation: the interface hypothesis specifies *where* and *how* consciousness acts but not *why* phenomenal attention exists. The [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard problem]] is [[the-relocation-objection|relocated]] from "why does neural processing produce experience?" to "why does consciousness have this particular interface with neural processing?"—a narrowing that may prove productive but should not be mistaken for a solution.

A second concerns falsifiability. Most conditions that would close the hypothesis—precision weighting explaining all attention phenomena without remainder, many-worlds confirmed, illusionism vindicated—are *borrowed* risks, a rival's vindication rather than a forward prediction of the interface model. The entropy claim was the one model-specific attempt and does not yet discriminate; the framework owes a prediction its strongest rival cannot match before it can claim falsifiability symmetry.

## Evidence and Dependency

The empirical strands (the dissociations, willed-mode markers, dopamine and stimulation studies, SMA cohort and felt-effort dissociations) are externally evidenced, but none discriminates the interface reading from predictive processing, which accepts the same data. That attention and consciousness are *ontologically* distinct is inherited from Tenet 1; that the willed-mode signatures and felt effort register a non-physical contribution is inherited from Tenet 3 and carries its standing debt; the quantum locus comes from Tenet 2 and the single selected outcome from Tenet 4. The case against Attention Schema Theory is independently argued, presupposing only that identifying being-represented with being-phenomenal owes a derivation by the programme's own mechanistic standard; it leaves the choice of framework open. The weight on cumulative convergence is inherited from the [[phenomenal-output-causal-machinery-dissociation|dissociation-cluster synthesis]], and the [[common-cause-null|common-cause null]] forbids counting it again here. The three-layer architecture and the economics of agency are mutually coherent only, organising the evidence without adding to it.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]**: The dissociation between attention and consciousness is consistent with their ontological distinctness, though physicalists read it as distinct neural circuits. The Map's reading gains force only because one side involves the hard problem, and AST's unearned step from representational content to ontic status leaves that irreducibility standing.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]]**: The attention interface is where the Map locates mind-matter interaction, whether through the Zeno mechanism—with its [[decoherence|decoherence objections]] and sign problem noted above—or another. Conservation laws are preserved because the energy for any selected action comes from ordinary metabolic processes. That answers the energy worry, not [[causal-exclusion-argument|Kim's exclusion worry]], which is about causal redundancy given a sufficient physical cause rather than where the joules originate. The Map's reply denies that sufficiency at the quantum joint, where [[causal-closure|closure]] holds only probabilistically—fixing the statistics but not which outcome actualises, so nothing sufficient is there for selection to be redundant to.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]**: The attention cluster elaborates this tenet without discharging [[tenets#^tenet-3-standing|its standing debt]]; the return cycle models it in miniature—the brain wanders (bottom-up), consciousness re-engages (top-down). Schwartz's OCD work is suggestive: patients who learn to redirect attention show reduced caudate activity on PET imaging, though the study was small (n=18), not replicated with the same method outside the UCLA group, and open to a Hebbian-learning account. What remains significant is the *pathway divergence*: cognitive and pharmacological interventions modulate the brain by different causal routes.

**[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]**: The selection model requires that quantum indeterminacies resolve to definite outcomes; if all branches persist equally, there is nothing for consciousness to select. The phenomenology of effort does not decide the matter, since effort felt in *this* branch has a within-branch physical story. The rejection rests on the tenet's indexical objection, and here it is a commitment noted rather than a result won against many-worlds on its own terms.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]**: AST appears simpler—consciousness is "just" a model. Where the phenomenal/access gap is what is in dispute, that simplicity cannot settle the question, since a theory may be lean by leaving the explanandum out. The interface hypothesis pays extra complexity to keep the gap in view, which keeps it on the table without counting against AST: the tenet forbids treating simplicity as a verdict in either direction.
