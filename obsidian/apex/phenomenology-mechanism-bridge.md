The Unfinishable Map holds that consciousness causes physical effects. Other synthesis pieces address what this [[phenomenology-of-consciousness-doing-work|feels like]], how the [[interface-specification-programme|interface is specified]], and what [[consciousness-and-agency|agency requires]]. This article traces the argument chain that connects them — from the first-person experience of willing, through the neural architecture that presents improper-mixture components awaiting actualisation (not already-definite options), through a candidate quantum mechanism for selection, to the metaphysics of the agent who selects. Three levels are independently grounded; the quantum level is a candidate mechanism whose plausibility derives from its fit with the others. The chain's coherence across four domains — phenomenology, neuroscience, physics, metaphysics — is consistent with the framework capturing something real, but coherence alone is not evidence, and in a synthesis this broad the discount is sharp (see [[#The Chain as Evidence|The Chain as Evidence]] below). The register's [[positions/quantum-interface#^mechanism-debt|mechanism-debt convention]] — P-Q3's bias-without-deviation dilemma, P-Q10's missing toy model — sets the citation grade: the causal-selection thesis is a framework-internal coherence result only, never established mental causation, and this chain inherits that debt rather than discharging it.

## Level One: What Willing Feels Like

The argument begins where philosophy of mind should begin: with experience itself.

When you decide to speak rather than stay silent, the [[phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity|phenomenology of agency]] reveals a structured experience with separable components. There is effort — the felt cost of sustaining attention or resisting impulse, scaling with difficulty in ways that track genuine cognitive demand (Kahneman 1973). There is authorship — the sense that "I did that," categorically different from passive reception of sensory input. There is directedness — the future feels open, the present a hinge between genuine alternatives. And there is [[self-opacity|opacity]] — the mechanism connecting intention to action is phenomenologically invisible at every level of contemplative attainment.

These are not a single undifferentiated sensation but separable components that clinical dissociations pull apart. In schizophrenia, patients experience thoughts without authorship — the content is present but the sense of agency is stripped away (Frith 1992). In anarchic hand syndrome, a limb performs purposeful actions the patient disowns — ownership without agency (Della Sala et al. 1991). In Desmurget's neurosurgical studies, parietal stimulation produces vivid intention without movement while premotor stimulation produces movement without intention (Desmurget et al. 2009). The [[volitional-control|phenomenological structure of willing]] carves at joints neuroscience independently confirms. This rules out the simplest deflationary accounts: a unitary post-hoc narrative could not be selectively disrupted at specific neural sites while other components remained intact. The clinical evidence reveals an architecture, not an illusion.

Two further findings constrain the deflationary readings without closing them. The [[empirical-phenomena-mental-causation|choking phenomenon]]: when experts consciously attend to automatised skills, performance degrades, and the disruption tracks the felt mode of attending rather than cognitive load alone. The epiphenomenalist reply is available — *neural* re-engagement of attentional circuitry degrades the skill while the experience of attending merely accompanies it — so the phenomenon supplies a regularity that reply must treat as primitive, not a refutation of it. Contemplative training *sharpens* the agency-passivity distinction rather than dissolving it: experienced meditators discriminate effortful from effortless states more finely (Lutz et al. 2008). That is what a real distinction would look like under scrutiny, and what a sufficiently fine-grained representational one would look like too.

A third line bypasses introspection altogether. Intentional binding — the temporal compression between a voluntary action and its outcome (Haggard, Clark, and Kalogeras 2002) — is an implicit measure of agency: it occurs for voluntary but not involuntary movements, weakens when outcomes are unpredictable, and is altered in schizophrenic patients whose comparator mechanisms malfunction. Mere confabulation should not track the integrity of specific causal circuits. As [[volitional-control]] details, explicit phenomenology, clinical dissociation, and implicit timing triangulate a common architecture rather than a narrative convenience.

A fourth line severs the motor channel entirely. [[covert-consciousness-and-cognitive-motor-dissociation|Cognitive motor dissociation]] — documented by Bodien et al. (2024) in [[covert-consciousness-and-cognitive-motor-dissociation#the-numbers-and-their-denominators|roughly a quarter of patients without observable command-following]] — shows robust task-locked neural responses to commands like "imagine playing tennis" with no overt response. Consciousness sustains attention, parses language, and forms task-relevant patterns when nothing reaches the motor output — the cleanest case of conscious work continuing with the behavioural channel closed, which is hard to square with experience as a downstream report on motor activity. The finding is consistent with the interactionist reading, though what it discriminates is agency architecture rather than causal direction: comparator, higher-order, and predictive-processing physicalisms accommodate preserved covert cognition equally well.

The phenomenological level provides the first anchor: the experience of agency has a specific, structured, clinically dissociable architecture that resists deflationary reduction.

> **Calibrated phenomenology.** Not all introspective reports earn equal weight, and the [[apex/introspection-architecture-void-cluster|introspection-architecture cluster]] sets the ceiling: consciousness receives reconstructed outputs without access to its own production process. A ladder of declining reliability orders what the argument may lean on:
> 1. **Content reports** — *what* is experienced (effort felt, alternative present, outcome singular). High reliability; these are the bounded data the clinical dissociations and intentional-binding measures corroborate from outside introspection.
> 2. **Agency/passivity reports** — *whether* an experience is owned and authored. Mid reliability; the comparator can misfire (schizophrenia, anarchic hand), but the dissociations are clinically structured rather than arbitrary, so the *distinction* survives even where a given report errs.
> 3. **Process reports** — *how* selection is accomplished. Low reliability; consciousness has no readout of its own generating substrate, so the felt mechanism of willing ([[self-opacity|phenomenologically invisible]] in any case) carries little weight. This is exactly where the [[concepts/discrimination-problem|discrimination problem]] bites: a report cannot certify that genuine phenomenal selection rather than a sophisticated functional process produced it.
> 4. **Causal-power reports** — that the experience *did work* in the physical world. Lower still; that an action feels self-caused does not show consciousness contributed the cause, which is why the chain leans on third-person findings (choking, cognitive motor dissociation) rather than the feeling of efficacy — findings that constrain the architecture of agency while leaving causal direction open.
> 5. **Metaphysical inferences** — that the agent is a persisting substance with irreducible powers. Framework-internal only; the irreducibility claim follows from the tenets, while the persisting-substance claim is downstream of the agency cluster's [[agent-causation|agent-causal]] commitment rather than of the Dualism tenet, which stays neutral between substance and property readings ([[where-the-substance-commitment-enters|where the substance commitment enters]]). Neither follows from any report; both are commitments, not data.
>
> The argument leans low on the ladder: Levels One and Two draw on content- and agency-tier reports cross-checked against third-person measures; Level Three's quantum proposal is a *process* claim, a candidate mechanism rather than a deliverance of introspection; Level Four is openly framework-internal. The chain defers to the introspection-architecture constraint rather than refuting it: where consciousness lacks process access, the bridge cannot read its own mechanism off the phenomenology, and does not claim to.

## Level Two: How the Brain Presents Options

The second level moves from what we experience to what the brain does, mapping how phenomenology and neural architecture fit together without claiming one reduces to the other. (Włodzisław Duch's [[concepts/geometric-model-of-mind|geometric programme]], which models effort, deliberation and settling as trajectories between attractor basins, is borrowable tooling on the correlate side; its thesis that mind is a shadow of neurodynamics is not, as the [[research/wlodzislaw-duch-consciousness-2026-05-02|Duch dossier]] maps.)

Paul Cisek's [[motor-selection|affordance competition]] hypothesis describes the motor system's actual organisation: sensory input automatically specifies multiple potential actions in parallel, which compete within neural populations until one reaches threshold. In premotor cortex, 43% of task-related neurons encoded both potential reach targets simultaneously — the brain represented two options before selecting one (Cisek & Kalaska 2005). The basal ganglia implement a brake-release mechanism: tonic inhibition suppresses all motor programmes by default, and selection occurs when the brake is released on one programme while competitors remain inhibited.

This architecture maps directly onto the phenomenology. The felt openness of deliberation corresponds to parallel encoding of competing action plans; the effort of choosing, to biasing the competition; the settling when one alternative becomes actual, to threshold-crossing. Motor neuroscience and philosophy reached this structure independently.

The mapping extends to attention. Rizzolatti's premotor theory shows attention and motor planning share neural substrates: preparing an eye movement to a location improves visual processing there. Willed attention and willed motor action share phenomenology (both feel effortful when chosen) but no common timeline—~280ms motor commitment counts back from movement, and willed attention deploys hundreds of milliseconds later than cued; the frontal-theta and coherence signatures are measured for attention only — suggesting, not establishing, one selection system for perceiving and acting.

A crucial finding: mice whose fast, spike-evoked dopamine transients were abolished by a dopamine-neuron-specific RIM knockout — baseline dopamine persisting — still moved spontaneously, but initiated fewer trials and pursued reward with less vigour (Cai et al. 2024). The paper's own controls fix the limit of the result: depletion or receptor blockade *did* disrupt movement initiation, so baseline dopamine is not dispensable for moving. What the fast dynamics contribute is reward-oriented drive and threshold modulation, not the selection of which option is taken. A three-layer structure emerges: neural computation generates options, dopamine marks salience and sets thresholds, and something selects — on the Map's reading, consciousness, at exactly the point where the phenomenology says selection occurs. The architecture fits that reading without forcing it: basal-ganglia competition is itself a classical selector, and a classical accumulator leaves no open outcome for consciousness to settle unless a sub-classical source supplies one.

Brass and Haggard's (2007, 2008) "what, when, whether" model sharpens the question. The "whether" component — the decision to act or refrain — activates dorsal fronto-median cortex, an active neural process of cancellation with its own circuitry rather than the mere absence of a go signal. The selection gap concerns not only which action wins but whether any action proceeds at all. As [[volitional-control]] argues, the veto need not presuppose a prior unconscious veto because it operates at the interface where conscious selection meets neural preparation — precisely where the Map locates the consciousness-brain boundary.

## Level Three: The Quantum Opening

The third level asks: what fills the selection gap? The Map's answer requires first addressing what appeared to be strong evidence against it. The [[libet-experiments|Libet experiments]] seemed to show the brain decides before consciousness knows — the readiness potential begins ~550ms before movement while awareness of intention appears only ~200ms before — and for decades this anchored the claim that conscious will is illusory. The challenge has been substantially weakened, though not settled. Mele argues the 200ms mark may register an urge to flex that has already arisen rather than a decision already made. Braun, Wessler, and Friese's (2021) meta-analysis finds the overall pattern "largely consistent with the one found by Libet and colleagues", but the crucial brain-activity-to-intention comparison rests on only six studies and carries "a high degree of uncertainty."

Schurger's stochastic accumulator model (2012) reinterprets the readiness potential as an artefact of averaging neural noise: movement occurs when random fluctuations cross a threshold, and averaging many trials produces the RP's gradual ramp. Sjöberg's 2024 essay points the same way from clinical data — patients with the supplementary motor area resected, the very region generating the readiness potential, retain their sense of voluntary action — though Sjöberg is himself no dualist and reads the cases as removing a defeater rather than confirming conscious causation. If the RP were the neural signature of volition, removing its source should impair the experience of willing. It does not.

Weakening the Libet defeater levels the field without supplying support; it leaves the quantum opening free to be considered. On single-outcome indeterministic readings, quantum mechanics specifies probabilities for outcomes but not which outcome actualises. There [[causal-closure|causal closure]] stated as sufficiency fails, since physics supplies no sufficient cause of the particular outcome; closure stated as chance-fixing survives, because exact Born preservation leaves physics fixing every chance. The Map proposes that [[consciousness-and-causal-powers|consciousness settles which outcome actualises]], selecting among energetically equivalent alternatives without injecting energy or violating [[conservation-laws-and-mental-causation|conservation laws]].

Stapp's quantum Zeno framework (Stapp 2007) provides a candidate mechanism specifically for motor control. Competing motor programmes exist as neural states that would naturally evolve and dissipate; by rapidly "observing" the desired programme — attending to the intended action — consciousness prevents it from decaying, giving it a sustained advantage in the threshold-crossing competition. Nothing in the mechanism fixes whether frequent observation stabilises the attended programme or accelerates its decay, and on the general physics acceleration is the more common outcome; the stabilising direction assumed here has to be argued for (see [[sign-problem-for-conscious-observation]]). The continuous effort of holding an intention against competing alternatives maps onto what repeated observation events should feel like.

This closes the loop with Level Two: the brain generates competing plans in parallel, the basal ganglia set thresholds, and consciousness stabilises one plan through attentional observation, biasing the competition — on Stapp's version, through a choice physics leaves open (which question is put, and when) while nature's answer follows the Born rule. The ~10 bits/second bandwidth of conscious processing (Zheng & Meister 2025, sharpening the disparity Nørretranders 1998 popularised at ~16 bits/s) matches policy-level selection — choosing "reach for the glass," not directing individual motor neurons.

Honest gaps remain. Tegmark's calculations placed neural coherence times at femtoseconds, twelve orders of magnitude short of conscious timescales; the most favourable revisions (Hagan et al. 2002), themselves contested, reach 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ seconds in microtubules, still 3,000 to 30,000 times short of a 300ms decision window. Stapp (2000) denies that the hold must outpace decoherence, holding that the Zeno effect survives when the brain state is a mixture; Georgiev (2015) replies that a mind projecting in the basis decoherence has already fixed is redundant with it. As [[motor-control-quantum-zeno|Consciousness and Motor Selection]] details, timing stays in the picture either way: [[zeno-dynamics-on-a-decohered-mixture|repeated observation adds something only before the environment resolves the same alternatives]], and if it must outpace decoherence, each window demands 3,000 to 30,000 observation events, a rate no concrete model supplies.

Here the chain inherits a distinction that limits the damage. As [[consciousness-and-causal-powers]] sets out, the Map's commitment is layered: *phenomenal biasing* — that felt qualities enter the causal story in a way sub-personal computational role does not exhaust — is the architecture-level claim, and quantum Zeno selection is one *candidate implementation* of it. The timing gap constrains the Zeno candidate, not the architecture; if it proves unbridgeable, another implementation would have to carry the load ([[amplification-mechanisms-consciousness-physics]] surveys six proposals). It remains the framework's most serious empirical vulnerability at the implementation tier. The register ranks Stapp-Zeno below [[post-decoherence-selection|post-decoherence selection]] behind exactly these two gates, timing and sign ([[positions/quantum-interface#^p-q4|P-Q4]]), and [[framework-stage-calibration|framework-stage calibration]] files it as a pre-Keplerian *proto-model*; the chain uses it as the motor case's worked illustration, not as the Map's preferred mechanism.

A deeper concern: the quantum Zeno mechanism requires consciousness to perform the observations that hold the attended state — but how consciousness acts on the physical is the very problem the mechanism is meant to address. The chain therefore treats Level Three as a *candidate mechanism*, plausible only through its fit with sustained attentional effort, a neural selection point, and undetermined quantum outcomes. The circularity is real but contained: the mechanism presupposes the kind of entity argued for at the other three levels, and fit is not evidence that it operates.

## Level Four: The Metaphysical Ground

The fourth level asks what kind of entity could do what the previous three describe — select among options, bias quantum outcomes, author actions.

The agency cluster's answer is [[agent-causation|agent causation]]: the conscious agent, understood as a persisting substance rather than a collection of events, directly originates its volitions — the tryings through which actions proceed ([[positions/agency-and-will#^p-a6|P-A6]]). The metaphysics is borrowed from E.J. Lowe's [[substance-property-dualism|substance causation]]: substances have causal powers and exercise them, and events are what substances *do*. The borrowing departs from its source, since Lowe holds free volitions wholly uncaused (Lowe 2008: 7); making them agent-caused is the Map's addition. If all causation is fundamentally substance causation, consciousness causing neural outcomes is the same *type* of causation as a billiard ball causing motion — which removes the "metaphysically exceptional" objection that it *couldn't* happen, without showing that it does.

The framework meets the quantum level where the agent selects among possibilities physics leaves undetermined. Kim's (2005) exclusion argument — that every physical event has a sufficient physical cause, leaving no room for mental causes — loses its grip there, because physics supplies no sufficient cause of the particular outcome for [[mental-causation-and-downward-causation|mental causation]] to overdetermine. The reply is narrower than it looks. Chance-fixing closure is untouched, and if settling which outcome is actual changes no chance, the reading sits close to [[ensemble-level-epiphenomenalism|epiphenomenalism]]. Whether settling counts as causing is the open dilemma [[positions/quantum-interface#^mechanism-debt|P-Q3]] records: a pressure the Map marks rather than dissolves, and does not concede. Bradford Saad's [[delegatory-dualism|delegatory dualism]] offers a complementary account: experiences *preempt* physical causes rather than overdetermining alongside them.

The [[quantum-indeterminacy-free-will|luck objection]] — that undetermined choices are merely random, not authored — is met only in part by the chain's lower levels. The agent [[agent-causation-and-acting-for-reasons|selects on reasons]], with phenomenal engagement that tracks cognitive demand, through mechanisms whose neural signatures separate selection from reception, and the clinical dissociations show agency is structured. These distinguish the *architecture* of agent-causal selection from lucky randomness, anchoring what the metaphysics alone could not. They leave the akratic case open: an agent who selects against their own all-things-considered ranking has no reason for this choice rather than the other, and there the objection [[the-divided-will#luck-objection|stands unanswered]].

## The Chain as Evidence

Each level has its own grounding: clinical dissociations confirm the phenomenological structure of agency whatever one thinks of quantum mechanics; motor neuroscience found competitive selection without reference to philosophy of mind; agent causation is argued independently of any physical mechanism. The quantum opening exists on single-outcome readings too, but the proposal that consciousness fills it is the chain's speculative link. The connections between levels are separately motivated — phenomenology maps onto neural architecture at joints the dissociations mark from both sides, and the architecture's selection point is where the phenomenology locates choosing — but separately motivated links do not make the chain stronger than its weakest level.

A critic might object that this mutual support is circular — that coherence between levels demonstrates internal consistency, not truth. The concern has force: Ptolemaic astronomy was also coherent across its observational, mathematical, and cosmological levels. A second concern is particular to the Map's method: this synthesis is assembled inside a corpus continually pruned for internal consistency by AI-driven adversarial review, so high coherence across the four levels is partly what such a method *produces by construction*. The [[project/coherence-inflation-countermeasures|project's countermeasures]] target this risk; the [[project/common-cause-null|common-cause null discipline]] asks whether the apparent triangulation across four domains might be one upstream construction read four times, a question the [[apex/phenomenal-output-causal-machinery-dissociation|phenomenal-output / causal-machinery cluster]] presses hardest; and the [[project/evidential-status-discipline|evidential-status discipline]] supplies the rule the chain must honour — *a tenet may remove a defeater, but it must not upgrade the evidence level*. What keeps the chain from being mere coherentism is that three anchor points do not depend on the framework:

1. **The hard problem of consciousness.** That consciousness is irreducible to physical processes is argued independently through zombie arguments, the knowledge argument, and the explanatory gap.

2. **The argument from reason.** If all beliefs are fully determined by physical causes, no belief is rationally justified — including physicalism. This self-defeat argument provides a route to mental causation requiring no phenomenological premises and no quantum physics.

3. **The empirical correlations.** The correlation between felt effort and cognitive load, the neural dissociation between intention and execution, the distinct signatures of willed versus instructed attention, the choking phenomenon, and cognitive motor dissociation (conscious cognitive work persisting with the motor channel severed) are findings that require explanation regardless of one's theoretical commitments — though physicalist accounts explain them too, so they fit the Bidirectional Interaction tenet without evidencing it.

## Evidence and Dependency

The phenomenological structure of agency, the motor architecture, intentional binding and cognitive motor dissociation are externally evidenced and reach *strongly supported* at their bounded correlates, but they constrain the architecture of agency, not its causal direction: comparator, predictive-processing and epiphenomenalist accounts fit them too. The Libet reassessment is external as well, and removes a defeater without adding support. The irreducibility of consciousness and the argument from reason are independently argued, each on contested premises of its own. Reading the selection point as a gap consciousness fills, and the quantum Zeno candidate, are inherited from Tenets 2 and 3; the candidate is a demoted *live hypothesis* (P-Q4), and [[positions/quantum-interface#^mechanism-debt|P-Q3]] and P-Q10 hold the causal-selection thesis at external-evidence grade D, framework-internal only. Agent causation, and the agent-caused volition the Map adds to Lowe, come from the agency register (P-A2, P-A6), not from the Dualism tenet. The process-report ceiling is inherited from the introspection-architecture synthesis. The four-level fit is mutually coherent only: it adds no weight beyond its parts.

## What Would Break the Chain?

The chain is falsifiable at each level and at the connections between them.

**At the phenomenological level**: If the felt effort of choosing could be systematically dissociated from cognitive load — effort feeling easy when demands are high, or vice versa — the first anchor would weaken.

**At the neural level**: If voluntary action timing and selection could be fully predicted from prior neural states with near-perfect accuracy, the selection gap would close. Current prediction accuracy for simple binary choices remains around 60% (Soon et al. 2008). Conversely, what would *strengthen* the case is a specific deviation: in Schurger-style self-paced movement tasks, if conscious selection biases threshold-crossing, voluntary actions should show systematic deviations from drift-diffusion statistics between passive and actively willed conditions. No such deviation has yet been demonstrated — and no effect size is honestly attachable to the prediction, because the coupling model that would derive one is exactly what P-Q10 records as missing. The prediction stays qualitative until that debt is paid.

**At the quantum level**: If quantum coherence were proven physically impossible in brain tissue at any relevant timescale, coherence-dependent candidates would fail and the Zeno candidate would rest wholly on Stapp's disputed mixture reading — though, as noted at Level Three, the phenomenal-biasing architecture could in principle be carried by another implementation. If the many-worlds interpretation were empirically confirmed, the deeper account of singular selection would fail outright. A subtler challenge comes from the [[causal-consistency-constraint|causal-consistency constraint]] (Torres Alegre 2025, an unrefereed arXiv preprint): any selection scheme respecting no-signalling must leave each system's marginal statistics per-trial indistinguishable from unbiased Born statistics — multi-agent joint no-signalling and Lorentz-covariant dynamics stay open debts ([[positions/quantum-interface#^p-q10|P-Q10]]). This forbids per-trial discrimination by construction. The framework would inherit the degenerating-research-programme problem only if the surviving ensemble- and correlation-level tests (sequential-dependency, intention-versus-spontaneity contrasts) also returned null at brain-internal precision; those tests have not yet been run, and the verdict is open.

**At the metaphysical level**: If a compatibilist account could deliver genuine sourcehood — explaining not just reasons-responsiveness but why those reasons are *the agent's* — the motivation for agent causation would weaken.

**At the connections**: If the phenomenology-to-neural mapping were shown to be coincidental rather than structural, or if the neural selection gap turned out to have a purely computational explanation, the chain would break at its joints rather than at its nodes.

## Relation to Site Perspective

This synthesis engages all five tenets, chiefly through the connections between levels.

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]** grounds the entire chain. The phenomenological level supplies the structures the Map reads as irreducible. The metaphysical level requires consciousness to be ontologically distinct. Without dualism, there is no bridge to build — phenomenology reduces to neural computation, and the chain collapses into a single level.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]]** constrains the quantum level: consciousness biases outcomes physics already allows, selecting among energetically equivalent alternatives without injecting energy. Its minimality is the empirical-constraint kind, ruling out detectable Born-statistics deviation, which is why the chain inherits the P-Q3 pressure noted at Level Four.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]** is what the chain articulates across the four levels. The chain fits the tenet without evidencing it beyond the citation grade [[positions/quantum-interface#^mechanism-debt|P-Q3 and P-Q10]] set: a framework-internal coherence result carrying the register's mechanism debt, not established mental causation.

**[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]** is required for the chain's account of selection. Under many-worlds, every motor programme executes in some branch and what the chain calls "selection" becomes branching. The chain requires something stronger than branch-weighting under the Born rule (Wallace 2012): that alternatives are genuinely rejected, not merely relegated to lower-weight branches. The phenomenology of willing — its felt cost, its sense that only one outcome became actual — fits singular actualisation more naturally than branching.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]** addresses the chain's complexity cost. A four-level argument spanning phenomenology, neuroscience, physics, and metaphysics is not parsimonious, and the simpler alternatives (agency is illusory, effort is decorative, the selection gap is merely computational, agent causation is confused) each remain live at the level where they claim explanatory power. The tenet blocks parsimony from vetoing the chain; it does not turn the chain's complexity into evidence that reality shares it.
