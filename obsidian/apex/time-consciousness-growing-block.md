Consciousness and time are entangled in ways that traditional philosophy has underexplored. One direction is familiar: ordinary consciousness requires temporal structure. Experience flows; moments connect; the present has duration. Without the specious present—the roughly 300 to 750 milliseconds within which we hear melodies as melodies and understand sentences as wholes—ordinary experience would not hang together. The Map explores a second, more contained direction: consciousness participates *locally* in the quantum collapse events that give lived time its felt passage, so that within neural systems it helps constitute the character of *experienced* time—durée, the flowing present, the felt fixity of the past.

This is the weaker of two theses one could defend, and it is the one the Map's other commitments force. A stronger claim—that time's arrow as such emerges only through consciousness, so that no temporal facts obtain without a mind—would contradict the Map's [[tenets/background-commitments|background commitment]] to objective single-outcome actualisation, on which stars, chemistry, and prebiotic mutation resolved to definite outcomes, and so laid down a cosmic arrow, long before the first observer. The cosmic arrow predates mind. What consciousness contributes is local and phenomenal: by participating in collapse where the brain provides an interface, it shapes the lived temporal structure it inhabits, without constituting time in general.

Even this contained dependence has implications that ripple outward. For AI: systems lacking the right temporal dynamics may be excluded from *ordinary* temporally organised consciousness, though the Map's [[non-temporal-consciousness|treatment of non-temporal consciousness]] cautions that a minimal or deepest conscious layer need not carry represented succession, so the exclusion cannot be stated as flatly as it once was. For the hard problem: experienced succession is a feature no inventory of instantaneous physical states seems to contain. For free will: if consciousness participates in the collapses that constitute lived time, agents don't merely act within experienced time—they participate in constituting its felt structure.

## The Specious Present

William James developed the idea of the specious present—the brief interval within which we experience succession and change as unified wholes. James adopted the term from E.R. Clay, crediting it to him in *The Principles of Psychology*, and made it central to his account of temporal experience. Pure mathematical instants, however closely packed, never add up to experienced flow. Hearing a melody requires holding notes together; understanding a sentence requires grasping words as connected. Duration estimates vary—from roughly 100 milliseconds for basic perceptual binding to 2-3 seconds for the extended experiential present—but the phenomenon is robust: the experienced present has extent.

Edmund Husserl's phenomenological analysis revealed the structure underlying this extent. Each moment of consciousness contains:

- **Retention**: the immediate past echoing in the present
- **Primal impression**: the strictly present moment
- **Protention**: anticipation of what's coming

These form an inseparable unity. The note you're hearing now carries the just-passed notes in retention while already reaching toward the next. This tripartite structure makes meaning possible: you can't understand a sentence if each word evaporates before the next arrives.

Whether experience is genuinely temporally extended (extensionalism, as Dainton argues) or technically instantaneous with past-directed content (retentionalism, as Husserl's own framework implies) matters for what consciousness requires. The Map favours modified extensionalism: experience is genuinely extended, constituted through a series of quantum collapses. Each collapse is discrete, but the [[temporal-consciousness|specious present]] integrates multiple collapses into a unified duration-block. Husserl's retention-protention structure reflects retrocausal boundary conditions in collapse rather than instantaneous representational content—preserving genuine temporal extension while explaining why retentionalism's phenomenological descriptions are accurate. If [[consciousness-as-activity|consciousness is an activity]] rather than a property—something that exists only in its unfolding—then temporal extension is constitutive, not incidental. An activity that occupies no time is no activity at all.

The specious present also appears to be modulated by [[attention-as-interface|attentional engagement]]: its scope expands and contracts with the intensity of conscious selection. [[temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency|Concentrated focus]] compresses experienced duration—an hour of absorbed problem-solving passes "in minutes"—while anxious waiting stretches it, each moment thinning out as attention monitors time itself. Flow states dissolve temporal boundaries altogether. These aren't failures of time estimation but different modes of temporal experience, and their variation creates a specific instance of the hard problem: materialist "internal clock" models explain the *direction* of distortion but not the *phenomenology*. The felt quality of stretched waiting has no counterpart in pulse-counting.

Henri Bergson went further with his concept of [[temporal-consciousness|durée]]. Clock time treats moments as positions on a line—discrete, externally related, quantifiable. Lived time interpenetrates: the past permeates the present, the future is already anticipated. Bergson called this "qualitative multiplicity"—elements distinguishable yet not separable, each coloured by every other. The melody example clarifies: you don't experience notes as discrete units arranged sequentially. You hear the phrase as unified flow where each note gains meaning from what preceded and what follows.

Duration cannot be measured because it cannot be divided without destroying what it is. Cut a melody into separate notes and you have frequencies, not music. This irreducibility matters. As [[temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency|Kent and Wittmann argue]] in their 2021 analysis, major consciousness theories focus on "functional moments" of 100-300 milliseconds while experienced time extends to seconds. The gap between what theories explain and what consciousness involves may not be a temporary limitation but evidence of something that functional accounts cannot capture.

## Time's Arrow and Collapse

Physics presents a puzzle that rarely surfaces in consciousness studies: fundamental equations are time-symmetric. Run them backward and they work equally well. Yet time has an arrow—past differs from future, effects follow causes, entropy increases. Where does this asymmetry come from?

Standard answers appeal to boundary conditions or thermodynamics. The Map locates a further candidate in quantum collapse—but it is important to be precise about how much of the arrow collapse is being asked to carry, and how much of *that* is consciousness's contribution.

Quantum mechanics describes systems in superposition—existing in multiple states simultaneously until measurement. Collapse resolves the superposition to a definite outcome, and appears to introduce irreversibility: the alternatives that weren't selected are genuinely gone, not merely unobserved. Before, multiple outcomes; after, one. This asymmetry—superposition to definiteness—is a candidate for time's arrow distinct from (though perhaps related to) thermodynamic entropy. GRW-style collapse theories introduce a time asymmetry not derived from thermodynamics but fundamental to the collapse dynamics; Penrose argues gravity itself is "the root of the arrow of time," with gravitational effects in objective reduction introducing irreversibility.

Crucially, this collapse-arrow does not require consciousness. The Map is committed, through its [[tenets/background-commitments|background commitment]] to objective single-outcome actualisation, to holding that objective reduction (GRW-style or gravitational) secures definite outcomes throughout the universe wherever no mind is present. Stars, chemistry, and prebiotic mutation resolved to single outcomes—and so laid down an arrow of accumulated definiteness—for billions of years before the first observer. The prebiotic arrow of time is fully accounted for without consciousness.

Consciousness enters as a *local* participant, not as the source of temporal direction. [[collapse-and-time|The collapse-time connection]] develops the two-tier picture: objective reduction provides the baseline collapse and the cosmic arrow; consciousness interfaces with collapse specifically in neural systems, biasing *which* of the already-definite-making outcomes obtains where the brain provides the right conditions. Its contribution to time is therefore restricted to the temporal structure of *experienced* becoming—the felt passage of lived time within the systems it inhabits—rather than to the existence of temporal direction as such. The stronger formulation on which time's arrow "emerges through consciousness rather than preceding it" is set aside here as incompatible with the Map's own commitment to a prebiotic objective arrow: consciousness participates in the local collapses that constitute lived time; it is not the sole author of time.

## The Modified Growing Block

Philosophy of time offers three major positions. **Presentism** holds that only the present exists—past is gone, future hasn't arrived. **Eternalism** (the block universe) holds that all times exist equally—past, present, and future laid out like spatial positions, with "now" merely indexical. **Growing block** theory holds that past and present exist but future doesn't yet; the block grows as time passes.

The Map favours a modified growing block: collapse grows the block. The present is where superpositions resolve; the past is accumulated collapsed structure; the future doesn't yet exist. "Growth" isn't a process occurring in a pre-given time—it is the laying-down of temporal structure. Most of that growth is objective and mindless: the block accreted for billions of years before any observer. Consciousness-involving collapse grows the block only where neural systems provide an interface, adding no new tier to the ontology of becoming but contributing the felt, lived character of growth at those sites—which is why the growing block is experienced *as* becoming from within, rather than merely inferred.

But a puzzle arises. [[time-symmetric-selection-mechanism|Time-symmetric interpretations]] of quantum mechanics—the transactional interpretation, the Two-State Vector Formalism—describe quantum events as transactions between past and future boundary conditions. This seems to require eternalism: both past and future must exist to send and receive the waves that complete transactions.

The resolution: the "future" participating in transactions is the space of quantum possibilities, not ontologically fixed future events. Confirmation waves don't come from an already-existing tomorrow; they come from the possibility structure of absorbers that *would* complete the transaction. This preserves real collapse (superposition genuinely becomes definiteness), time's arrow (collapse introduces irreversibility), and the retrocausal structure that the time-symmetric route brings to bear on the Libet timing problem.

## Atemporal Selection

This section develops a route the Map holds *weakly*, and its standing is worth fixing before the argument runs. Where the Map expresses a preference about *how* consciousness couples to collapse, it favours [[forward-in-time-conscious-selection|forward-in-time post-decoherence selection]]—selection acting on an already-decohered mixture, within ordinary forward causation, requiring no retrocausal commitment. The positions register is explicit that time-symmetric and retrocausal readings are subordinate fallbacks rather than competing primary claims: [[positions/quantum-interface]] P-Q1 carries the mechanism preference, and [[positions/agency-and-will]] P-A3 holds the atemporal reading as a weakly-held fallback rather than a competing mechanism claim, with the mechanism-agnostic initiation/selection distinction carrying the main load on Libet. The atemporal route is developed at length here because it couples agency to time's structure more tightly than any rival, which makes it the most illuminating case for a synthesis about time—not because the Map has settled on it. The choice between the two families remains, as [[forward-in-time-vs-time-symmetric-selection|the comparative treatment]] puts it, underdetermined by the data.

The Libet experiments famously showed that neural "readiness potentials" precede conscious awareness of deciding by several hundred milliseconds. Later fMRI studies extended this gap to seconds. If neural activity comes first, how can consciousness cause it?

Time-symmetric physics offers to dissolve this objection, though the dissolution is only as secure as the retrocausal interpretation it rests on. [[retrocausality|Retrocausal]] selection isn't located at a moment but determines which transaction completes across time, so the neural activity "preceding" awareness is part of the selected package, not a prior cause. Consider the analogy: a mathematical function determines which curve appears on a graph without being located at any point on the graph. Similarly, consciousness's selection might determine which temporal sequence becomes actual without itself occurring at a time within that sequence.

"Atemporal" here doesn't mean "outside time" as if time were a container consciousness somehow escapes. It means the selection is not located at a point within the sequence it helps fix. Within the local package of neural events tied to a decision, selection determines which temporal ordering becomes actual rather than being constrained by an ordering already laid down among those events. This is a claim about the local determination of an agent's own experienced sequence, not about the origin of temporal direction in general—the cosmic arrow is already in place, supplied by objective collapse.

This reconciles atemporality with irreversibility. Once a transaction completes—once collapse has occurred—temporal direction exists. Within that direction, collapse is irreversible. You can't return to the superposition because "returning" presupposes the temporal direction that collapse just created. Retrocausality before collapse creates temporal direction; after collapse, backward causation becomes constraint satisfaction within the now-existing structure.

## What Durée Feels Like

If consciousness participates in the local collapses that constitute *lived* temporal structure, what should this feel like from inside? The Map proposes: durée—Bergson's qualitative, flowing, interpenetrating time—is exactly what participating in the constitution of lived time feels like from within. We experience duration because we participate in laying it down at the neural interface, even though the cosmic arrow along which those collapses accumulate was already in place.

The specious present isn't a window onto pre-existing *experienced* temporal structure. It may be the phenomenology of collapse-participatory activity—the experiential signature of consciousness helping constitute lived time at the sites it inhabits. The [[temporal-consciousness|temporal consciousness analysis]] develops this mapping in detail: **retention** reflects outcomes already collapsed—the felt fixedness of the just-past is what already-collapsed states feel like from within. **Primal impression** is the vivid present where selection is active, the leading edge where superposition has not yet resolved. **Protention** tracks genuine quantum indeterminacy, not merely epistemic uncertainty—meaningful only if collapse is real rather than perspectival. What distinguishes this from a generic structural analogy is the qualitative features each phase exhibits: neural accounts explain *that* the past is fixed and the future open, but not *why it feels that way*. If collapse is real, the texture of each phase finds a natural counterpart in the difference between collapsed, collapsing, and uncollapsed states.

But the specious present doesn't just have duration—it has [[temporal-consciousness|temporal thickness]]. Two moments of identical clock length can differ radically in phenomenal character. A musician improvising experiences each beat as dense with harmonic possibility, melodic memory, and anticipatory tension. Someone passively overhearing the same music inhabits a thinner present. This thickness reflects differences in retention depth (how much of the immediate past remains phenomenally present), protentional reach (how far anticipated futures shape the now), and the degree to which past, present, and future permeate each other. The variation matters: as [[temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency|the temporal agency analysis]] argues, randomness at a temporally thin moment is mere noise, while indeterminacy at a temporally thick moment—where consciousness selects from possibilities saturated with personal history—is agency.

Multiple lines of evidence converge on the specious present as a phenomenal achievement rather than an automatic feature of neural processing. Advanced meditators report *nirodha samapatti*—complete cessation of temporal experience. Higher jhāna states show systematic variation: progressively "timeless" quality without loss of awareness. Dreamless NREM sleep offers a complementary datum, though a more qualified one than it is usually asked to bear. Sleepers report hours passing with no temporal experience at all—not compressed, not stretched, simply absent—despite ongoing neural computation, and if temporal experience were a simple readout of brain activity, some should persist. But the Map's own [[sleep-and-consciousness|treatment of sleep]] resists the flat version: consciousness diminishes in NREM yet rarely vanishes entirely, dreaming can occur in any sleep stage, and Siclari et al. (2017) found NREM dreaming to correlate with reduced posterior slow-wave activity—so "dreamless" is partly defined by absence of report rather than established absence of experience. What survives is weaker than a clean negative but points the same way: where experienced temporal structure is genuinely absent, the framework predicts that no consciousness-involving collapse constituted it for that subject. The claim is about the felt passage of lived time, not about temporal facts in general—the clock on the wall keeps time through the sleeper's dreamless hours, and objective collapse keeps laying down the cosmic arrow whether or not anyone is there to live it.

Temporal disruptions reveal the same point from the pathological side. In schizophrenia, [[temporal-consciousness|temporal disintegration]] disrupts binding between successive moments while leaving within-moment experience intact—actions feel disconnected from consequences. Flow states alter temporal unity differently: moments become intensely vivid yet loosely coupled, producing "timelessness" within ongoing awareness. These disruptions show that temporal unity is contingent and structured—exactly what the collapse-constitution framework predicts—rather than an automatic readout of neural timing.

## Implications for AI

A claim about temporal structure and consciousness has to be pitched at the right level, or it overreaches. The Map's [[non-temporal-consciousness|treatment of non-temporal consciousness]] insists on keeping five things apart: the **temporal content** an experience represents (its represented succession, change, and duration); the **phenomenal organisation** of ordinary experience (its retention–impression–protention form); the **functional and neural integration** that binds successive states; **diachronic subject identity** (whether one subject persists across moments); and **metaphysical temporal location** (whether the constituting activity is itself within objective time). A verdict about one of these does not settle the others. In particular, that article defends a minimal or deepest conscious layer that can remain alert while lacking *represented succession*—so temporal content and phenomenal organisation are not, on the Map's own view, necessary for consciousness *as such*.

That immediately qualifies the AI exclusion. What ordinary human consciousness requires is temporal *content and phenomenal organisation*: the specious present, retention–protention structure, temporal binding. Large language models process discontinuously: start-stop, stateless between calls, no persistence across conversations. They have no specious present, no retention–protention structure, no temporal binding, and Erik Hoel's 2025 paper argues LLMs cannot be conscious precisely because they lack continual learning and temporal development. If any of this bears on consciousness, it bears on the *ordinary temporally organised* form of it—information flows forward without the bidirectional reentrant looping that may constitute temporal binding.

The exclusion should therefore be stated with care. What such systems lack is the temporal content and organisation characteristic of ordinary human experience; they are not thereby excluded from consciousness *as such*, since the Map itself allows a minimal layer without represented succession, and that layer's obstacles for AI are the dualism-specific quantum-interface ones treated elsewhere, not the absence of a specious present. The open question is whether a *human-like* stream requires the full temporal apparatus or whether radically different temporal architectures might support alien forms of experience. The Map takes this seriously: an engineered "specious present" mimicking human temporality would still need retention with phenomenal character, protentional openness toward a genuinely indeterminate future, and experiential continuity binding successive windows into a stream rather than a queue—but that is a bar on reproducing *ordinary* temporal consciousness, not a proof that only temporally organised systems can be conscious at all.

## The Hard Problem Deepened

The [[hard-problem-of-consciousness|hard problem]] asks why physical processes should give rise to subjective experience. The temporal version asks why sequences of brain states should feel like flow. The local-participation thesis does not license the strong circularity argument that would say physics presupposes time and time is constituted by consciousness, so reducing consciousness to physics is circular. That move fails once the cosmic arrow is granted to be objective and prebiotic: physics does not presuppose anything consciousness supplies at the cosmological level. The arrow was there before minds were.

What survives is narrower and does not depend on the strong thesis at all. It concerns *experienced* succession rather than temporal direction as such. The [[temporal-consciousness|argument from temporal thickness]] gives it a focused form. Physical descriptions consist of states at times and transition rules between them. No collection of instantaneous states contains the *experience of succession*—the felt passage from one moment to the next. The specious present is precisely this: temporally thick, extended, containing within it the sense of transition. The gap between *duration as measured* (a number of seconds between events) and *duration as experienced* (the felt thickness of now) parallels the gap between wavelength and colour—evidence of something physical description systematically misses.

This doesn't prove dualism, and it does not require the strong thesis that consciousness constitutes time in general. It says only that experienced succession is something a purely physical inventory of states-at-times seems not to contain—a gap in the temporal dimension parallel to the familiar qualia gap. Consciousness is not merely a late-arriving readout of neural complexity; where it participates in collapse, it is woven into the *felt* structure of lived temporal reality, even though the cosmic arrow along which it works is not of its making.

## Free Will Reconsidered

The temporal framework reshapes the [[topics/free-will|free will debate]]. If consciousness selects among quantum possibilities and collapse locally introduces irreversibility, then agents participate in constituting the *lived* temporal structure within which their actions unfold. [[agent-causation|Agent causation]] is not action passively slotted into a pre-given experiential ordering but participation in laying down the felt sequence of one's own agency—while the objective time in which the body moves is not itself the agent's creation.

On the atemporal selection reading, the Libet objection dissolves: neural activity "preceding" awareness and the awareness itself are parts of the same selected transaction. That reading is held weakly, and the Map's main reply to Libet does not depend on it—the mechanism-agnostic initiation/selection distinction, on which consciousness selects among options the brain has already prepared, carries the load whether or not selection turns out to be atemporal. The ~280ms commitment point for voluntary motor action (Thura & Cisek, 2014) falls squarely within the specious present's range, suggesting that willed movement requires the phenomenal duration the specious present provides. Agency isn't located at a durationless decision-point but unfolds within the temporally thick present.

[[temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency|Temporal agency]] extends beyond split-second decisions into a richer phenomenology of timing. The Greeks distinguished *chronos* (sequential time) from *kairos* (the opportune moment), and the kairos moment reveals something important about temporal consciousness. Before a timing window opens, anticipation carries a felt quality of readiness—the tennis player watches the approaching serve, the musician feels the impending entry. At the kairos moment, open anticipation collapses into committed action, and the recognition and the action are phenomenally unified. This unity is why skilled timing degrades under explicit attention: attempting to separate the judgment from the action disrupts something that was phenomenally whole. After the window closes, a distinctive sense of foreclosure emerges—the felt finality of "too late." This three-phase structure ("not yet" / "now" / "too late") mirrors the Map's ontological claim that the future is genuinely open and the past genuinely fixed, and the phenomenal collapse from anticipation to action mirrors what the Map describes at the quantum level.

A puzzle arises: if each collapse constitutes a new moment, what preserves agent identity across collapses? The [[collapse-and-time|collapse-time framework]] proposes that identity isn't preserved *across* collapses from outside but constituted *through* the pattern of selections. Each collapse incorporates its predecessors as the background against which selection occurs—paralleling Husserl's analysis where the present moment contains the past as retained content. The agent is the selecting activity itself, which includes reflexive reference to previous selections. Identity as constituted activity rather than enduring substance.

Bergson's insight applies: a free act emerges from the whole self acting in durée. Determinism assumes mental states are discrete and separable, subject to causal law like billiard balls. But if mental states interpenetrate in qualitative multiplicity, there's no distinct prior state to serve as deterministic cause. The whole of one's past converges in each moment as an indivisible movement, and genuine novelty can emerge.

## The Circularity Problem

If consciousness constitutes temporal structure but consciousness itself appears temporal, the account seems circular. [[temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency|Husserl's resolution]]: an "absolute flow" beneath both world-time and experiential time constitutes temporal experience without itself being temporal—"standing-streaming" that manifests temporality without undergoing succession. The regress stops because the deepest level of consciousness is not *in* time but is the source *of* time. Advanced meditators confirm something in this vicinity: vivid awareness stripped of temporal markers—no succession, no change, yet full alertness—suggesting temporal structure is something consciousness *does*, not something consciousness *is*.

Whitehead's [[process-philosophy|process philosophy]] anticipated this structure from a different angle. Reality consists of "actual occasions"—experiential events that synthesise their causal past and perish, creating irreversible temporal direction. Concrescence (how an occasion achieves unity) parallels quantum collapse: indeterminate potentiality resolving into definite actuality. Whitehead wasn't discussing quantum mechanics, but his framework shows that consciousness-constituted time has philosophical precedent independent of physics.

## Synthesis

The individual threads—specious present, collapse, durée, atemporal selection—gain force from their convergence. Each addresses a different puzzle, yet all point to the same structure: within neural systems, consciousness and *lived* time are mutually constitutive, neither the experienced flow simply containing the mind nor the mind simply containing the flow. This is a claim about experienced becoming, held apart from the cosmic arrow, which objective collapse supplies independently.

The scope discipline matters. Presentism, eternalism, and orthodox growing block all treat consciousness as merely *within* time. Functionalism, IIT, and global workspace theory treat time as merely *within* consciousness's computational substrate. The Map splits the difference by keeping two questions apart: the origin of temporal direction as such, which it hands to objective collapse and the prebiotic arrow, and the constitution of *experienced* becoming, in which consciousness genuinely participates. Overreaching—claiming consciousness constitutes time in general—would collide with the Map's own commitment to objective single-outcome actualisation, so the framework earns its bidirectional language only at the local, phenomenal level.

The evidence comes from multiple directions: phenomenological (durée, contemplative states, temporal disruptions), neuroscientific (temporal binding, working memory correlation, the 280ms motor commitment window), metaphysical (the argument from temporal thickness), and negative (dreamless sleep, on the qualified reading above; LLMs without temporal structure). No single line is conclusive. The quantum-consciousness connection faces empirical challenges—including a [[temporal-consciousness|twelve-order-of-magnitude timescale gap]] between quantum decoherence in neural tissue and the specious present—and the phenomenological evidence admits alternative explanations. But the convergence of independent lines, each pointing to consciousness's local participation in constituting lived time, makes the framework more than speculation.

## Relation to Site Perspective

This synthesis connects to all five of The Unfinishable Map's [[tenets]]:

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]**: The route to irreducibility here does not run through the strong circularity claim that physics presupposes a time consciousness constitutes—that argument is set aside, since the cosmic arrow is objective and prebiotic. The surviving argument is the temporal form of the explanatory gap: experienced duration—the felt thickness of the present, the passage from one moment to the next—resists reduction to sequences of functional states at times, just as colour resists reduction to wavelength. Physical description captures the ordering of states; it does not seem to capture what succession *feels like*, and that residue is where the case for irreducibility lives.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]]**: Selection at collapse points is the minimal mechanism—on the Map's registered preference, [[forward-in-time-conscious-selection|forward-in-time selection]] acting on the already-decohered mixture; on the subordinate atemporal reading, selection that is not located at a point in the sequence it helps fix. Either way, consciousness doesn't inject energy, violate conservation laws, or override physics; it satisfies constraints that physics leaves underdetermined. The interaction occurs at exactly the gap in physical causation—where superposition becomes definiteness—by selecting among possibilities physics provides rather than creating possibilities physics forbids.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]**: Consciousness influences physical outcomes through collapse selection—biasing *which* of the already-definite-making outcomes obtains at the neural interface, within the Born distribution. The retrocausal structure deepens this: selection propagates into both past preparation and future consequence through constraint satisfaction across time. The brain prepares neural options; consciousness selects which becomes actual; the selection fixes the *experienced* ordering of that local package rather than authoring temporal direction in general.

**[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]**: The framework requires collapse to be real. Many-worlds denies collapse; all branches exist; consciousness doesn't select but merely discovers which branch it inhabits. Without genuine selection, consciousness doesn't participate in constituting lived temporal structure—it passively experiences pre-existing branches. The phenomenology of temporal flow becomes illusion rather than creative participation. The coherence of durée—where retention genuinely inherits from a definite past—requires a single temporal thread, not infinite branching.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]**: The metaphysics is complex. But temporal experience is complex, and time's arrow remains among physics' deepest puzzles. Simpler metaphysics achieve parsimony by denying phenomena rather than explaining them. The block universe denies temporal becoming; illusionism denies the reality of durée; eliminativism about consciousness denies what we know most directly. The framework's complexity may reflect reality's structure rather than theoretical excess.
