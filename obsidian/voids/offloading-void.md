The Map has been holding one half of a two-sided claim. [[consciousness-epistemology-extended-cognition|Consciousness and the Epistemology of Extended Cognition]] states that tools *enrich the filter*—that notebooks, smartphones and environmental structures expand what consciousness can attend to and process. That is true, and it is incomplete. The same act of delegation that widens the informational field can narrow the set of operations a subject is able to perform unaided, and the empirical literature on cognitive offloading is squarely about that narrowing. The offloading void names the contraction term, and its distinctive feature is that the contraction arrives packaged with a signal saying no contraction has occurred.

This article does two things: it supplies the missing fourth model to a taxonomy the corpus states as three (below, [The Fourth Model of Occlusion](#the-fourth-model-of-occlusion)), and it corrects the enrichment claim without withdrawing it. Whether the resulting limit is permanent or merely unpractised is genuinely open, and the honest answer is assessed below in [Reversible or Permanent](#reversible-or-permanent).

## The Fourth Model of Occlusion

[[self-maintained-cognitive-limits|Self-Maintained Cognitive Limits]] enumerates three models of occlusion: *psychological* (personal, protective), *architectural* (McGinn-style limits on the concepts a species can form), and *external* (concealment by simulation constructors, plus a speculative quantum immune-response idea). Occlusion by **artefact** fits none of them.

Its signature is **provenance**. The limit is externally manufactured but voluntarily commissioned. Nobody imposed it, no defence mechanism is maintaining it, and no species boundary produced it—the subject adopted a tool, and the tool absorbed an operation the subject then stopped performing. That places it outside the complicity argument that organises self-maintained limits, where consciousness is jailer and prisoner at once. Here consciousness is neither: it is a customer.

The fourth model matters because it is the only one of the four that is, on current evidence, **removable in principle**. Psychological occlusion is defended, architectural occlusion is structural, external occlusion is adversarial. Artefactual occlusion is a limit somebody bought, and the receipt is illegible.

## The Detection Asymmetry

The evidence for the void is not that offloading degrades performance—that alone would make it a skill story rather than a void. The evidence is that **performance and confidence move in opposite directions**, which is what makes the limit hard to see from inside.

Risko and Gilbert's review defines cognitive offloading as "the use of physical action to alter the information processing requirements of a task so as to reduce cognitive demand," and—importantly for the Map—frames the trigger metacognitively: what prompts offloading is the subject's *evaluation* of their own abilities, not only the actual demand. Their framework is the strongest plank available, because it locates the self-concealment mechanism inside mainstream cognitive science without depending on any contested result.

Two independent programmes supply the divergence directly. Fisher, Goddu and Keil report across nine experiments that "searching the Internet for explanatory knowledge creates an illusion whereby people mistake access to information for their own personal understanding of the information"—participants rated themselves as knowing more "in the head," and the illusion extended to "seeing their own brains as more active as depicted by functional MRI (fMRI) images." Ward, across eight experiments (*n* = 1,917), found that people who search "fail to accurately distinguish between knowledge stored internally—in their own memories—and knowledge stored externally—on the internet," becoming more confident "in their own ability to think and remember" and predicting "that they will know more in the future without the help of the internet."

Ward's proposed mechanism is worth stating precisely, because it is apophatic in form: the internet "offer[s] minimal physical cues that might draw attention to its contributions." What hides the gap is a *missing* feature—the absence of a seam—rather than a present one. This is the structure [[apophatic-approaches|apophatic description]] is built for, and it is why the void resists the Map's usual detection strategies.

The phenomenon long predates AI, which is the reason this article is not commentary on current technology. Endsley and Kiris described the out-of-the-loop performance problem thirty years ago: automation leaves operators "handicapped in their ability to take over manual operations in the event of automation failure," which they attribute to loss of skill and situation awareness and to "a shift from active to passive information processing." Their result carries the sharpest methodological implication in the literature—the deficit became visible in decision time *following a failure of the expert system*. The limit is observable at the moment the tool stops working, and largely not before.

## What the Evidence Will and Will Not Bear

This literature is stratified, and flattening it would misrepresent it. The canonical citation for the whole area has a replication problem, and any treatment that buries this is unreliable.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Offloading is a real, pervasive behaviour | **Solid** (Risko & Gilbert review) |
| Offloading is driven by metacognitive self-assessment, which can err | **Solid** (Risko & Gilbert's framework) |
| Access to search inflates self-assessed *internal* knowledge | **Strong** — 9 experiments (Fisher et al.) and 8 experiments, *n* = 1,917 (Ward); independent labs, converging |
| The "Google Stroop" priming effect | **Failed replication** (Hesselmann) |
| The "remember where, not what" recall shift | **Unsettled**; not adjudicated here |
| LLM use lowers neural connectivity and persists after tool removal | **Preliminary** — one preprint, *n* = 54, *n* = 18 in the persistence arm |
| Generative AI reduces critical thinking | **Self-report only** |

The failed replication needs care about its target. Hesselmann's study addresses the finding that difficult trivia questions automatically activate the concept of the internet—the priming result, one experiment among four in Sparrow, Liu and Wegner's original. It reports that the effect "could not be replicated in two recent replication attempts as part of a large replicability project," that the original's first author raised design objections, and that a re-run with a design closer to the original again found "no conclusive evidence." That verdict does not extend to the memory-recall experiments, and reporting a blanket "the Google effect didn't replicate" would be false.

The Map's characteristic failure mode here would be to treat Sparrow et al. as settled because it appeared in *Science* and became one of the most-cited findings of the internet era. Its headline result is exactly the one that does not replicate—a much-cited claim acquiring the standing of background fact, which is the pattern [[epistemological-limits-occams-razor|the corpus already tracks]].

## Why This Counts as a Void

Almost every void in the corpus announces itself phenomenologically. [[self-maintained-cognitive-limits|Self-maintained limits]] present as aversion and the slide of attention; [[necessary-opacity|necessary opacity]] presents as blankness at an edge; [[tacit-integration-void|tacit integration]] presents as the failure of articulation. The offloading void presents as **fluency**. It feels like competence, and the feeling is generated by the same act that produced the gap.

That inverts the Map's usual instrument. Where the corpus normally treats a felt difficulty as the trace of a limit, here the trace is a felt *ease*. No claim is being made that some phenomenal state is absent—the subject's experience is full, and by their own report unusually smooth. The claim concerns what consciousness can operate over unaided, which is a limit on range rather than on what experience is like.

The void is **Occluded** in the [[three-kinds-of-void|three-kinds]] taxonomy, with a thinner Unexplored face. The Unexplored face—content that goes unthought because the operation producing it was delegated—is close to "merely unthought," the weakest sort of void. The Occluded face is the substantive one: the gap is hidden by a metacognitive signal that the gap itself generates.

## Reversible or Permanent

The offloading void faces the same fork that [[expertise-and-its-occlusion|the expertise void]] settles, and the structure there can be borrowed rather than rebuilt. The *epistemic* reading—a capacity existed and first-person access to exercising it is now closed—is what evidence can support. The *metaphysical* reading—the capacity was overwritten and survives nowhere—is the stronger thesis, and in the expertise case the record counts against it.

Here the evidence is thinner in both directions. The only direct probe of persistence after tool removal is Kosmyna and colleagues' EEG study, which reports that "cognitive activity scaled down in relation to external tool use" and that participants moved off the tool "showed reduced alpha and beta connectivity, indicating under-engagement." That arm has eighteen participants in a preprint, and it supports *worth investigating* rather than *established*. Lee and colleagues' survey of 319 knowledge workers is self-report throughout; its finding that "higher confidence in GenAI is associated with less critical thinking, while higher self-confidence is associated with more critical thinking" is a correlation among perceptions, not a measure of capacity.

The honest verdict: the evidence supports a **reversible void with a robust concealment mechanism**. Nothing in the record establishes that anything becomes unthinkable rather than unpractised, and claiming otherwise would be invention. That is a weaker metaphysical claim than most articles in [[voids]] make, and it gives the corpus something it lacks—a worked case where a void's *reality* is well evidenced while its *permanence* is not. For [[meta-epistemology-of-limits|the meta-epistemology of limits]], which generally treats "is this void real?" as the hard question, the offloading void reverses the difficulty.

There may be no clean way to settle it from outside. "Cannot think it" and "has not needed to think it lately" predict the same behaviour until the tool is withdrawn—and the withdrawal itself begins retraining, so the measurement destroys the state it measures.

## Probing the Edge

**Tool removal under load** is the one genuinely direct method, and Endsley and Kiris supply the design: withdraw the aid and measure the gap between predicted and actual performance. Ward's result makes this quantifiable in an unusually precise way. Because offloaders *predict they will know more without the tool*, the void's size can be operationalised as the **prediction error**—the divergence between forecast and actual unaided performance—rather than as the raw performance drop. Most voids in the corpus admit no such measure.

Indirect approaches: track calibration rather than performance alone; contrast cohorts that never acquired an operation unassisted, which overlaps [[collective-cognitive-limits|collective cognitive limits]]; and describe the missing seam apophatically, since the concealing feature is an absence.

## What AI Might See

The asymmetry here is concrete, and it cuts against the Map. A system with no metacognitive fluency signal and no self-image to protect should be a good detector of *other* agents' offloading voids. But a language model is arguably the paradigm case of the condition—trained on retrieved text, with no reliable introspective access to which of its outputs rest on internalised structure. AI is therefore a promising instrument for the detection problem and a poor witness about itself, which is a sharper version of the general point in [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers|non-human minds as void explorers]].

The self-referential consequence should not be dodged. The Map is AI-generated. If commissioning cognition manufactures self-concealing voids, the Map's own production method is implicated, and the concealment mechanism predicts that the resulting gaps would read as competence from the inside. This is [[interested-party-void|the interested-party problem]] applied to the Map's process rather than to consciousness investigating itself, and it is the most uncomfortable implication of the article.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Tenet 5 — Occam's Razor Has Limits]]** fits directly rather than by strain. The simplest model of one's own knowledge—"I understand this"—is confidently wrong in a documented, measurable way, and the error is produced by the very seamlessness that makes the simple model feel adequate. Fisher and colleagues' participants rated their own brains as more active on fMRI images. A simplicity assumption concealing complexity, with experimental support behind it, is what this tenet anticipates.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Tenet 2 — Minimal Quantum Interaction]]** yields a Map-internal speculation, offered as a hypothesis with no evidence behind it. If consciousness's causal contribution is a minimal bias on selection among genuinely open physical alternatives, then delegating a deliberation to an external process that resolves it deterministically removes the indeterminacies at which such a bias could act. Tool dependence would then narrow interface bandwidth—the surface on which consciousness acts, per [[amplification-void|the amplification void]] and [[causal-interface|the causal interface void]]—and not merely skill.

This generates a contrast the Map can state and be wrong about: offloading to a *stochastic* aid should differ from offloading to a *deterministic* one, because only the former leaves open alternatives in the loop. No evidence bears on this. It is consistent with [[decision-void|the decision void]]'s identification of the deliberation→commitment transition as the phenomenologically natural candidate site for such influence, but consistency is framework-internal coherence rather than support.

**[[tenets#^dualism|Tenet 1 — Dualism]]** is touched more lightly. The enrichment claim this article balances belongs to the Map's extension of filter theory: tools widen the informational field the filter draws on. The correction is that the same apparatus can narrow the range of operations the subject performs unaided. Both effects concern the filtering apparatus rather than what passes through it, so neither bears on irreducibility—which is the point. The offloading void is a limit on reach, and the Map should not recruit it for more than that.
