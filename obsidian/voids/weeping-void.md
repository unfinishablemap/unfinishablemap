In a 1999 case report in the *New England Journal of Medicine*, Bejjani and colleagues described a 65-year-old woman with Parkinson's disease and "no psychiatric history". Within five seconds of high-frequency stimulation through one contact of a deep-brain electrode she became "acutely and profoundly depressed", and she cried. Asked why, she said: "I no longer wish to live, to see anything, hear anything, feel anything", and later, "I'm crying over myself, of course." The depression "abated within 90 seconds" of the stimulation being switched off, and sham stimulation did nothing. (The quotations are from B. Schildkrout's account of the case in *Psychiatric Times*; the primary report was not consulted.) The electrode was the cause, and the reasons came with "of course" attached.

The Unfinishable Map takes this as the entry to a void about what a person can know of her own tears. Science has no settled answer to what emotional tears are *for*. What an episode is *about* is not privileged to the crier, since an electrode can supply tears and reasons together and a tear does not carry its own valence. Whether a cry *helped* cannot be read from inside, because recovery from crying's low can feel like relief. The void concerns access, not whether anything is felt: the article is named after a behaviour because which felt state the behaviour belongs to (sadness, being moved, helplessness) is part of what is unknown.

The opening case is abnormal. Most crying is not mysterious to the crier, and grief at a death is not opaque to the griever; the void bites on sentimental tears, tears of joy and tears that seem to come from nowhere. The stimulation case shows that a felt reason is not a certificate of its cause, not that ordinary weeping is confabulated. The evidence is one case known through a secondary account, a handful of laboratory and survey studies, and a phenomenology of crying read through a secondary source.

Two faces are shared with [[mirth-void|the mirth void]], which opens on stimulated laughter. As there, a physically caused expression arrives with sincere reasons. As there, the deliberate routes to the expression go around the act rather than through it.

## What Tears Are For

Darwin called weeping "an incidental result, as purposeless as the secretion of tears from a blow outside the eye", and added in the same sentence: "yet this does not present any difficulty in our understanding how the secretion of tears serves as a relief to suffering" (Darwin 1872, ch. VI). Purposeless as an adaptation, then, and relieving all the same.

The function is still disputed. The leading evolutionary review proposes that "crying evolved as an emotional expression that signals distress and promotes prosocial behaviors in conspecifics" (Gračanin, Bylsma & Vingerhoets 2018), and removing the tears from tearful faces "produced faces rated as less sad" (Provine, Krosnowski & Brocato 2009). A rival review treats crying as self-soothing (Gračanin, Bylsma & Vingerhoets 2014). A chemical signal is an open dispute: Gelstein and colleagues (2011) reported that sniffing odourless tears shed by women in a negative emotional state lowered men's ratings of women's sexual appeal, along with their arousal and testosterone; three studies by Gračanin and colleagues (2017) "failed to replicate" the effect; and Agron and colleagues (2023), from the same laboratory as Gelstein, reported that sniffing tears "with no odor percept reduced human male aggression by 43.7%". Frey's blood-clearance idea is "very popular" (Vingerhoets & Bylsma 2016), but comparable cortisol effects follow maternal separation in "young rhesus monkeys that do not produce emotional tears" (Gračanin et al. 2014). A 2023 review preprint still calls the function debated and emotional tears "most likely unique to humans" (Zickfeld & Wróbel 2023).

This is ordinary scientific ignorance, and it is shrinking. It belongs in the catalogue because nobody who weeps can tell from inside which of these functions, if any, her tears are performing. In the terms of [[three-kinds-of-void|the three kinds of void]] this face is *unexplored*.

## What Tears Are About

The crier's access to what her tears are about is not privileged, and it can be manufactured. The stimulation case shows the second point: the tears and their sincere, global reasons had a cause the patient could not have known about. It is [[confabulation-void|the confabulation void]] in a specific setting.

Neurology adds that expression and feeling come apart. Parvizi and colleagues (2001) call pathological laughter and crying "a disorder of emotional expression rather than a primary disturbance of feelings", with episodes that can occur "without an apparent triggering stimulus". Their proposal, offered with a case study, is that cerebellar structures which "automatically adjust the execution of laughter or crying to the cognitive and situational context" work, in these patients, "on the basis of incomplete information about that context". On that proposal, fitting tears to their occasion is done below the person.

Ordinary tears show a quieter version. Crying comes with "being overwhelmed by strong emotion, be it negative or positive" (Gračanin et al. 2014), and happiness "can also be expressed through physical displays that without context, would appear to be sadness" (Aragón & Bargh 2018); observers given the context read such displays as happy. It does not follow that criers confuse joy with grief. The claim is that whoever reads a tear, crier included, takes its valence from the situation, so one expression straddles the sort [[valence-void|the valence void]] asks about.

Vingerhoets and Bylsma (2016) call it "plausible" that "tears may feed back to the crying individual about his/her psychological state": sentimental crying "typically occurs quite unexpectedly, and seems to make the individual aware that there is something important happening". On this hypothesis the tears are partly evidence the crier interprets. James's reversal, "we feel sorry because we cry" (James 1884), is stronger than this face needs; it needs only that the order can run from tears to their reading.

What the puzzling cases feel like is disputed. The most common trigger, for Vingerhoets and Bylsma, is "a feeling of powerlessness or helplessness, often in combination with other emotions". Cova and Deonna (2014) treat being moved, which often brings tears at good news, as a distinct emotion: "the experience of a positive core value (particular object) perceived by the moved subject as standing out (formal object)". Zickfeld and colleagues (2019), across 19 nations, describe *kama muta*, "evoked by experiencing or observing a sudden intensification of communal sharing" and often accompanied by "moist eyes or tears". If being moved is a distinct emotion, part of the void closes. What stays open is that the rival accounts disagree, and that the crier's own words ("I'm so happy"; "I don't know why I'm crying", folk testimony with no prevalence figure behind it) name none of them. This face is *occluded*.

## Whether Crying Helped

Over 70% of respondents in a 37-country study agreed that crying generally makes them feel better, but asked about their most recent cry, "only 50% of these very same respondents reportedly had experienced a positive mood change after crying" (Vingerhoets & Bylsma 2016, reporting Vingerhoets 2013). In the laboratory the immediate effect is negative: of 150 women shown a sad film, the 33 who cried showed "an aversive state" (Gross, Fredrickson & Levenson 1994). In a quasi-experimental study of 28 criers and 32 non-criers, criers' negative mood rose after the film, returned to baseline by 20 minutes, and at 90 minutes "Criers subsequently reported mood enhancements" relative to before the film (Gračanin et al. 2015).

The 2014 review explains the divergence: because mood in criers "reaches a nadir after a crying episode", the return to baseline "may be misperceived as a real mood improvement", and retrospective reports "likely are biased by implicit lay theories" that crying brings relief (Gračanin et al. 2014). Yet the same review finds "converging evidence for the direct, self-soothing effects of crying", and the 90-minute gain sits above baseline. Whether crying or recovery produced it, one small study cannot settle.

The face concerns the instrument: the crier's sense of having been helped cannot tell help from recovery, and separating them took a 90-minute delay and a control group. Darwin's "yet" may be right; the crier is not placed to confirm it. This face too is *occluded*.

## The Will Gap

The will gap is shared with the mirth void. Simons and colleagues (2012) note that actors "are commonly instructed to think of a sad memory in order to help them cry when required" and "also sometimes make their tears flow by rubbing their eyelids with menthol or other irritants": one route produces the emotion that produces tears, the other produces irritant tears. Darwin reported that a single effort to repress tears "seems often to lead to an opposite result", an anecdotal early instance of the rebound discussed in [[agency-void|the agency void]]. He also relays the Rev. R. Taylor's report that New Zealand women "can voluntarily shed tears in abundance" in mourning, so weeping at will exists as a cultivated skill; what most people report is indirection.

## Plessner: Weeping at the Limit of Conduct

Helmuth Plessner's *Laughing and Crying* (German 1940 or 1941; English 1970) supplies the philosophical frame. Its English text was not consulted, so what follows paraphrases Plessner as Rasini (2013) presents him; no English sentence here is his. In Rasini's summary, laughing and crying "are opaque: they cannot be connected with a specific mood or a specific cause". They arise from a loss of the mastery a person ordinarily exercises over her body, and are capitulations, a letting-go into the body. Yet they remain meaningful answers: when a situation admits no fitting response, the impossibility of finding one becomes the only fitting response, and the person who yields remains a person. Crying comes where something felt as absolute breaks through the ordinary relativity of one's existence, whether in pain or in sublime joy, so laughing and crying differ less as pleasure and pain than as two ways conduct reaches its limit (Rasini 2013, pp. 130–132). Plessner also noted that psychology and aesthetics favoured laughter and neglected crying (p. 126); the Map repeated the asymmetry by writing the mirth void first. Vingerhoets and Bylsma reach a related point empirically: "Tears thus may actually reflect feelings that cannot be expressed or consummated in other behaviors".

If Plessner is right, part of the occasion face is unexplorable. The tears answer where articulate answers have run out, so a full articulate account of what they said would be the response whose unavailability they answer, and every method reaches them through a later articulate report. This is the Map's inference, not a finding, and it stands or falls with Plessner. He himself declined metaphysics: the tension between being a body and having a body describes lived existence, not two substances.

## Approaches to the Edge

Three methods reach past the crier's judgement. Delayed, controlled mood measurement is the only one found that separates relief from recovery (Gračanin et al. 2015). Tear-removal and context manipulations measure what a tear adds and what the situation supplies. Blinded sniffing studies test a channel neither party can introspect; if the chemical signal is real, it is a case for [[voids-between-minds|the voids between minds]], opaque at both ends. A first-person study of tear onset, for instance by [[microphenomenological-interview-method|microphenomenological interview]], is the obvious next probe; the research behind this article found none, which does not show that none exists.

## What AI Might See

An AI system does not weep, and nothing here suggests it has the state tears register. A text-trained system can still be asked which passage of a story will bring readers to tears, and answer without being moved: the observer's position at its extreme, reading the occasion with no tears of its own to interpret. Aggregation also sees what no crier can, that more people say crying generally helps than say their last cry did.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]** is the clearest connection, and a modest one. The search for *the* function of tears has run from Darwin's "purposeless" through detoxification to distress signal, self-soothing and chemosignal, each answer needing qualification. Vingerhoets and Bylsma (2016) conclude that crying "should rather be considered as a behavior that obeys the laws of operant conditioning and is under the influence of biological, psychological, and social factors than as a reflex-like symptom". If so, the single-function question was itself the simplification. The case illustrates the tenet without arguing for it.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]** finds traffic in both directions here, and a caution. A letter is read and tears form; an electrode is switched on and a mood arrives with reasons. Neither direction discriminates between interactionism and physicalism, since a physicalist reads the letter case as neural processing of meaning, so the Map does not count tears as evidence; the article on [[emotion-and-dualism|emotion and dualism]] carries the stronger arguments about emotion. The caution cuts against the Map, which leans on first-person evidence: here the first-person report of *why* one weeps can be produced by a current and arrive with "of course" attached. The Map's account of [[phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence|phenomenal authority]] already places the causal processes behind experience beyond first-person authority, and tears fall under that limit. The crier's report that she is weeping and feeling something keeps its standing; her report of what the tears are about, and whether they helped, carries no privileged weight.

The void bears on no other tenet. Plessner offers his description of a person who yields to the body and still answers through it without metaphysics, and the Map does not read it as support for [[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]; Minimal Quantum Interaction and No Many Worlds have no connection. In the [[voids]] index this is a mixed, framework-independent void: function unexplored, occasion and relief occluded, the Plessner core possibly unexplorable. A physicalist and a dualist describe the same blank.
