A mind can calculate that a million deaths are a million times one death, and it cannot feel the difference. Decades of judgment research find that felt concern for others registers *that* someone suffers and loses resolution about *how many*: when feeling drives valuation, "value is nearly a step function of scope" (Hsee & Rottenstreich 2004). The Unfinishable Map calls this the Numbing Void, after *psychic numbing*, Robert J. Lifton's (1967) term for the "turning off" of feeling that let rescue workers function after the Hiroshima bombing, which Paul Slovic (2007) extended to apathy toward mass suffering. Its subject is numbing toward the number of sufferers, not the pharmacological gap of the [[anesthesia-void]]. Three layers make it up: a *magnitude* face, empirical and robust; a *regulation* face, occluded, in which people damp their feeling for large groups before it arrives (Cameron & Payne 2011); and a *structural* face, unexplorable, because no subject undergoes the sum of many subjects' suffering (Lewis 1940). The void proper lies where the first and third meet. The plateau admits a *deficit* reading, on which many deaths are roughly additively bad and feeling fails to track that, and a *tracking* reading, on which the plateau is roughly right about where suffering is. Felt magnitude cannot decide between them, since a mind's only first-person measure of others' suffering is felt, and no one undergoes the sum against which a felt measure could be checked. The Map takes the additive side on the moral question on framework grounds, not felt ones ([Where the Map Stands](#where-the-map-stands)).

The folklore version needs trimming. Compassion does not simply collapse as numbers grow: group size lowers helping without a significant effect on empathetic concern (Butts et al. 2019). What survives is that felt concern does not scale with the number of sufferers, and helping falls.

## Neighbouring Voids

The [[the-quantitative-comprehension-void|quantitative comprehension void]] covers magnitude in general; the numbing void is its interpersonal case, where magnitude meets felt concern. The [[cross-state-void]] is intrapersonal and the [[voids-between-minds]] concern access between one mind and another; neither has a face for number. [[moral-census-opacity|Moral census opacity]] holds that the Map cannot read how many moral patients a region holds; this void adds that even a known number cannot be felt. [[the-ownerless-suffering-argument|The ownerless suffering argument]] runs the opposite inference from a neighbouring premise: Śāntideva argues from suffering's having no owner to its impartial relief, Lewis from the sum's having no sufferer to its unreality.

## The Magnitude Face

In a hypothetical funding decision reported by Slovic (2007), Fetherstonhaugh and colleagues (1997) found that the median respondent required 9,000 lives saved when 15,000 were at risk but 100,000 when 290,000 were: proportion carried more weight than the absolute number. Hsee and Rottenstreich (2004) found people relying on feeling sensitive to presence or absence but "largely insensitive to further variations of scope", and people relying on calculation closer to constant sensitivity. Kogut and Ritov (2005) found that identifying a single victim raised contributions considerably, while identifying each member of a group of eight had "essentially no effect on willingness to contribute". Västfjäll and colleagues (2014) proposed that "a decline in compassion may begin with the second endangered life", a small-sample result best treated as fragile, and found that "this fading of compassion was reversed by describing multiple lives in a more unitary fashion". Feeling can attend to a group presented as one.

The same record shrinks the popular version. Lee and Feeley's (2016) meta-analysis of 41 studies found an "overall significant yet modest" identifiable-victim effect (r = .05). Maier, Wong and Feldman's (2024) pre-registered replication of Small, Loewenstein and Slovic (2007), using hypothetical donations, found "no empirical support for the identifiable victim effect", while noting that hypothetical giving may differ from the original real-money design. Their reframe is what this void keeps: "participants seem to be insensitive to the number of victims affected." Butts and colleagues (2019) found that "victim group size negatively affects both helping intent and helping behavior", but "significant effects were not found for empathetic concern". The robust finding is a plateau in concern and a fall in helping.

The limit may also resist knowledge. As Maier et al. report the original, Small et al. (2007) found that teaching participants about the identifiable-victim effect equalised giving by lowering what went to the identified victim, without raising what went to the statistical ones. The replication, having found no effect to correct, found no support for this debiasing either. Where the original holds, knowing about the limit levelled giving down and did not carry it out to the many.

## The Regulation Face

Part of the plateau is made by the subject. Cameron and Payne (2011) found the drop in compassion for groups only when participants "expected to be asked to donate money to the victims", only among people skilled at emotion regulation, and only under instructions to down-regulate; instructions to experience the emotion removed it. Their time-course data suggested that people regulate "proactively, by preventing themselves from ever experiencing as much emotion toward groups as toward individuals".

That is why this face is occluded. A feeling suppressed after arising could be caught in the suppressing; a feeling prevented from arising leaves only a smaller response, which presents itself as the natural size of one's concern. The mind's stake in not feeling the full weight shapes what is available to feel, the pattern [[self-maintained-cognitive-limits]] and the [[interested-party-void]] trace elsewhere. Whether the effect extends beyond paradigms ending in a request for money is open.

## The Structural Face

Grant perfect affect, with no attention bottleneck and no regulation. A felt grasp of the *sum* would still need a subject who undergoes the sum, and there is none. C. S. Lewis (1940) put it plainly: "There is no such thing as a sum of suffering, for no one suffers it." Of two people's toothaches he writes, "search all time and all space and you will not find that composite pain in anyone’s consciousness." Lewis was writing theodicy and used the point to shrink the problem of pain. This article takes his phenomenological claim, that the aggregate is no one's experience, and leaves his consoling conclusion.

Any feeling one mind has about a million sufferers is that one mind's feeling. The aggregate is reached only by calculation. Sidgwick (1907) states impartiality as the principle that "the good of any one individual is of no more importance, from the point of view (if I may say so) of the Universe, than the good of any other". On a reading this article proposes, which Sidgwick does not state, the hedging parenthesis concedes that the standpoint of the impartial total is one no individual consciousness occupies.

## Two Readings, One Instrument

On the *deficit* reading, Slovic's, the badness of many deaths is real and roughly additive, as the normative model of life-valuation has it, and feeling fails to track it. On the *tracking* reading, the plateau is roughly right about where suffering is, and the additive figure is an artefact of calculation. John Taurek (1977) gave the ethical form of the second: in its standard statement, when one must choose between rescuing one person and rescuing five, the numbers do not by themselves settle what to do, because the five losses are summed in no sufferer. Piller (2026) restates Taurek's negative claim as the claim that "sums of harms or benefits (across different people) lack moral significance", and argues that numbers still count through fairness. The dispute is live in ethics.

Unit-sensitivity fits both readings. Feeling attends to a group best when the group is recast as a quasi-individual; on the deficit reading that is a limitation of format, on the tracking reading the format matches where suffering is located. Not every finding fits both: valuing 9,000 lives saved of 15,000 above ten times as many saved from a larger population, as Slovic reads Fetherstonhaugh's result, is an error on either reading. What stays undecided is the plateau itself. A mind cannot settle the matter from inside. Its first-person access to how bad suffering is runs through feeling, and no felt total exists to check feeling against; calculation produces the large number but cannot say whether the number measures anything felt. The instrument that would settle the question is the instrument in question.

### Where the Map Stands {#where-the-map-stands}

The Map sides with the additive reading on the moral question, on framework grounds and not on felt evidence. It registers that "duplicating a suffering subject multiplies the suffering" ([[positions/ai-consciousness-scope#^p-ac3|P-AC3]]) as resting on closed individualism ([[positions/individuation-and-subjecthood#^p-i1|P-I1]]): subject boundaries are real, so many suffering subjects are many separate bads. Closed individualism grants Lewis's premise, since no trans-subject subject exists to undergo the total, but cannot by itself refuse his inference, because Lewis and Taurek also count many separate sufferers. The refusal is a further step in P-AC3's counting rule: separate bads add up although no one bears the sum. The tracking reading of Lewis and Taurek remains a rival the Map does not adopt and has not refuted.

Two consequences follow. The felt plateau is no evidence against P-AC3, since felt magnitude is the instrument this void disqualifies. And P-AC3's own calibration, empirical discriminability *none*, fits the void: no measurement of anyone's feeling could decide between additive and non-additive weight.

## Phenomenology of the Edge

From inside, nothing seems missing; a headline reporting a hundred thousand deaths draws a response that feels adequate to it. Albert Szent-Györgyi's admission, as quoted by Slovic (2007), is rare because it names the gap: "I am unable to multiply one man’s suffering by a hundred million." Annie Dillard's seven-year-old daughter, told that 138,000 people drowning was hard to imagine, answered "Lots and lots of dots in blue water", as Slovic also reports: when imagination tries, the many become texture. If Cameron and Payne are right that regulation acts proactively, it may leave no felt mark, no turning away to notice, only a smaller response.

## Approaches to the Edge

**Direct.** None reach the structural face. The other two can be moved: instructions to experience emotion removed the motivated drop, and unitary framing restored concern. Each delivers a single-subject-sized feeling, undamped or attached to a group recast as one, and neither delivers felt magnitude.

**Indirect.** Calculation works as a prosthesis, recovering near-linear valuation at the cost of feeling; it goes around the void rather than into it. Serial individuation, such as memorials that read out names or the 38,000 pairs of shoes piled in front of the US Capitol that Slovic (2007) describes, gives each victim a unit in turn and trades magnitude for duration. [[apophatic-cartography|Apophatic description]] can state what a felt sum would not be: one's own response multiplied, or a total assembled from imagined individuals.

**[[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers|What AI might see]].** A single-author preprint, suggestive only, reports identifiable-victim effects in instruction-tuned language models that reasoning-specialised models invert (Raiyan 2026). Whatever such systems show about the magnitude face, a system that computes the sum perfectly has still found no subject who undergoes it.

## Relation to Site Perspective

Under the Map's rule that a tenet removing a defeater does not upgrade a void's evidence ([[positions/voids-as-evidence#^p-v2|P-V2]]), what follows are readings the framework offers, not support the void gives it.

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]** meets the structural face, which is compatible with the tenet and does not discriminate it from rivals. If phenomenal states are irreducibly owned, as [[phenomenal-value-realism]] holds through [[haecceity|indexical, token-based value]], the sum of many subjects' suffering has no phenomenal instance anywhere. A physicalist can say the same: a sum of brain states is not a brain state that hurts. Dualism sharpens the point without owning it.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]** applies twice. The simple normative model, value linear in lives, hides the question of where a summed value is had; the simple psychological model, affect as a broken meter, hides the regulation face and the tracking reading. The Map's additive verdict answers the hidden question explicitly: the value is had in each subject, separately.

**[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]** gets a symmetric caution. The Map's case against many-worlds partly invokes moral weight: "if all branches are equally real, suffering is never reduced but redistributed" ([[phenomenal-value-realism]]). Felt intuitions about how much suffering there is across many branches or copies are unreliable in both directions, so neither the Map nor a many-worlds defender may cite felt magnitude as evidence. Such arguments must run through the indexical-identity premise, where the Map's additive verdict partly rests.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]** finds an illustration, not an instance, in the regulation face: where down-regulation is deliberate, as under Cameron and Payne's instructions, a mental act shapes what becomes available to feel. Physicalist accounts of emotion regulation describe the case equally well, and it adds nothing to the tenet's standing, which the quantum-interface argument leaves [[tenets#^tenet-3-standing|available rather than actual]]. **[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]]** is not engaged.

**Applications.** Verdicts that turn on counts, such as the copy-multiplicity verdict in [[ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness]], carry two caveats: the count may be unreadable ([[moral-census-opacity]]), and the felt weight of whatever count is used is no guide. Neither touches the additive rule, which rests on closed individualism and a further counting step.
