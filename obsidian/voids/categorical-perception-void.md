Where the world offers a continuum, experience arrives already carved. Voice-onset time runs smoothly from 0 to 60 milliseconds, but a listener hears /b/ and then, abruptly, /p/; hue, facial expression and musical interval behave the same way. The perceiver reports a few categories, discriminates two stimuli that straddle a boundary more easily than two equally different stimuli inside one, and reports the verdict rather than the gradient. This is categorical perception, and The Unfinishable Map's categorical perception void is the region it hides: the interior of each category, delivered at reduced resolution by an operation the perceiver did not choose and cannot watch.

The strong version of this void has been retired by the field that discovered it. The textbook claim, traceable to Liberman and colleagues in 1957, was that a listener can hear no differences among speech sounds beyond those the phoneme labels reveal. McMurray's 2022 survey of fifty years of behavioural and electrophysiological work concludes that within speech perception this strongest form is widely regarded as an artefact of task demands, and that the underlying representation of the continuum appears linear. What survives is narrower: a limit on *access by default* and a limit on *provenance*. The continuum can be reached when the task is built to let it; by default the perceiver reports and acts on the category. And nothing in the experience of a boundary says whether it tracks a joint in the world, a threshold of the auditory system, or one installed by language, training or the first year of life. An installed boundary feels exactly like a discovered one. In the terms of [[three-kinds-of-void]] it is Occluded, the compression being the perceiver's own operation, with an unexplorable face that deflates under the right instrument.

What this article adds to the neighbouring [[resolution-void]], [[language-thought-boundary]] and [[expertise-and-its-occlusion|expertise void]] is the origin face as a void in its own right, and a contrast the catalogue had not drawn: the same mind that is vague where its concepts should be sharp is sharp where the world is continuous.

## The Warp and Its Deflation

The robust core is a warp. Discrimination is better across a category boundary than within one, controlling for physical difference, the definition in Goldstone and Hendrickson (2010), and it has been reported in five domains. In speech it is the original finding. In colour, Winawer and colleagues (2007) found Russian speakers, whose language obligatorily distinguishes lighter *goluboy* from darker *siniy*, faster to discriminate two blues straddling that border than two from the same side, with the advantage removed by a concurrent verbal task but not a spatial one. In faces, Etcoff and Magee (1992) found expressions morphed in constant physical steps discriminated more poorly within an expression category than across one. In music, Burns and Ward (1978) found trained musicians perceiving melodic intervals categorically under standard procedures. In the laboratory, Pérez-Gay Juárez, Sicotte, Thériault and Harnad (2019) taught subjects to sort textures into two categories by feedback alone and found between-category separation and, to a lesser extent, within-category compression in dissimilarity judgements afterwards, with a correlated decrease in the occipital N1.

The deflation is equally well replicated. Gerrits and Schouten (2004) ran the same vowel continuum through two discrimination tasks: a four-interval task showed no categorical perception at all, while a two-interval forced-choice task on identical stimuli produced the classic pattern; Schouten, Gerrits and van Hessen (2003) attribute the variation to task factors. Burns and Ward's musicians lost most of their categorical signature under an adaptive procedure once performance stabilised, and continuous ratings of how /b/-like a token is track the acoustics. The colour face has deflated in stages. Witzel and Gegenfurtner (2011), with 230 observers, found category effects that were real but not lateralised as Gilbert, Regier, Kay and Ivry (2006) had reported, leaving lateralisation unsettled. Martinovic, Paramei and MacInnes (2020) found the Russian advantage at blue/green but not at *siniy/goluboy*, whose boundary drifted toward lighter shades with a lighter stimulus set. Later work with Witzel reads such effects as post-perceptual: with same- and different-category pairs equally discriminable, He, Witzel, Forder, Clifford and Franklin (2014) found category effects only from 200 milliseconds, in post-perceptual components; and Martinovic and colleagues (2026), re-running the Greek-blue oddball paradigm, found its early mismatch response driven by contrast adaptation and unreliable for contrast-equated category changes. The Whorfian colour effect should be carried as task-dependent, its locus unsettled and often post-perceptual.

So the between face splits, as the [[palette-extension-void]] split, into two questions with opposite fates. Can the perceiver reach the within-category continuum? Yes, by the right task, by continuous rating, by training. Does it reach it by default? No. An occlusion that vanishes when the question is asked differently is an occlusion of the default readout, not of capacity.

## Discovered or Installed

Perceived boundaries have three candidate origins. Some may track discontinuities in the world. Some are built into the senses: chinchillas trained on /d/ and /t/ endpoints place the boundary on the continuum where humans do (Kuhl and Miller 1975), evidence that linguistic knowledge is not necessary for boundary-like responding there. Others are demonstrably installed. Pérez-Gay Juárez et al. (2019) manufactured the texture boundary by feedback in one session, in the roughly half of participants who learned the categories. The *siniy/goluboy* border is one English speakers do not carry. The interval scale belongs to musicians. And the phonemic inventory is pruned early: Werker and Tees (1984) found English-learning infants discriminating Hindi and Salish consonant contrasts at six to eight months and largely losing the ability by ten to twelve, while infants learning those languages kept it, which Werker and Hensch (2015) place inside a critical-period model.

The origin face is the phenomenological point these results make together. Nothing in the experience of a boundary marks it as installed. An adult English speaker hears /r/ and /l/ as two sounds; an adult Japanese speaker hears one; each hears a joint, and neither can inspect its provenance. The [[infant-consciousness|infant consciousness void]] carries the narrowing data as a developmental limit; here the point is what the adult inherits: clean joints that are the residue of a pruning the adult cannot remember or undo by attention.

Burnston (2021) closes the other exit. Proponents of [[cognitive-penetration]] have argued from categorical perception and perceptual learning that beliefs permeate perception as categories are learned. Burnston argues that this diachronic thesis is false: to guide learning, a belief would have to describe contents the subject can already perceive, so it cannot specify the ability actually acquired, and the learning process must operate outside cognitive influence. If that is right, the Map reads the installed boundary as opaque from both directions: the perceiver cannot see whether it was installed, and the perceiver's own intentions during learning could not have specified what was.

The provenance limit has no first-person method. Whether a felt joint is world-given or installed is inferred only by comparing the perceiver to a differently carved perceiver: Russian against English speakers, six-month against twelve-month infants, a subject before and after a laboratory boundary. Boundary drift, as at the *siniy/goluboy* border, is only a weak probe from outside: range effects shift every judged boundary, so drift shows context-dependence, not installation. None of this is available inside a single perceiver's experience, which is what makes the origin face a void rather than an open empirical question.

## The Gradient That Is Kept but Not Delivered

The residue face is fixed by one result. Toscano, McMurray, Dennhardt and Luck (2010) recorded event-related potentials to spoken words varying in voice-onset time. The auditory N1, around 100 milliseconds after onset, tracked voice-onset time linearly and independently of the category the listener assigned, and within-category differences were still present at the later P3. The continuum is in the system a tenth of a second after the sound begins. What the listener reports and acts on by default is the category.

This places the void in the catalogue's operation-void family. The [[fusion-void]] delivers the fused percept and never the component estimates; the [[inference-void]] delivers the conclusion; the [[transit-void]] delivers the arrival. Categorisation quantises one dimension into a few bins and keeps the unquantised copy where the default readout does not look. That the copy is kept separates this void from the [[resolution-void]]'s bit-rate compression, and it is why the between face deflates: the continuum can be reached because it was never thrown away.

Two caveats. Whether the learned warp is perceptual or decisional is contested: the N1 decrease in Pérez-Gay Juárez et al. is read as perceptual, but Fugate (2013) reports the same dispute unresolved for faces, where the paradigm's emotion words may do part of the work. And no study located asks the phenomenological question directly: when a listener reaches within-category detail by continuous rating, is the difference experienced as a difference in the sound, or only judged? The evidence supports *behavioural* access on demand; whether that access is *phenomenal*, and whether the default itself is experienced as categorical or only reported so, are equally untested.

## Sharp Where the World Is Continuous

The [[vagueness-void]] describes a mind that cannot locate where its concepts stop applying: no Sorites series fixes the cut-off for *heap* or *red*, and the borderlines have borderlines. Categorical perception describes the same mind hearing a hard edge between /b/ and /p/ where the acoustics are continuous. The two voids are complementary failures of edge representation in opposite directions: concepts are fuzzy where a decision procedure would want them sharp, percepts are sharp where the input is smooth. The sources consulted did not draw the conjunction, though the [[phenomenal-sorites-problem|phenomenal sorites]] sits beside it; the Map offers the pairing as an observation, not a finding.

The conjunction sharpens the origin face. A perceiver who noticed that an edge was too clean for the input could suspect installation, but the edge presents as the world being simple. Approaching this void feels like nothing: a Japanese-speaking adult listening to *right* and *light* does not experience a blur where a distinction should be; the two words simply sound alike.

## The Training Window

There is one interval in which the operation acquires a texture. Lively and colleagues (1994) trained Japanese listeners to identify English /r/ and /l/ with high-variability natural tokens over three weeks; identification improved and the gain largely survived three months without training. The Map characterises such training, without a reported source, as passing through a period in which the two sounds are known to differ and are not yet heard to differ: the joint is believed before it is perceived. That interval is the only first-person window onto installation, and it closes as the category installs.

Expertise shows the same closure from the other end. Siegel and Siegel (1977) found six musicians with relative pitch identifying in-tune intervals at over 95% accuracy while unable to tell sharp from flat within a category, and afterwards judging that 63% of the stimuli had been in tune when only 23% were. The categories dominate such tasks while fine sensitivity stays recoverable under other procedures, as Burns and Ward found; the musicians' estimate shows the dominance is invisible to the [[expertise-and-its-occlusion|expert]] it governs. Whether retrained listeners regain the continuum phenomenally or only behaviourally, the training literature does not say.

## What AI Might See

For a human perceiver the warp is inferable only through psychophysics and averaged potentials. For an artificial network it is directly measurable: Bonnasse-Gahot and Nadal (2022) show, by information-theoretic analysis and simulation, that category learning induces categorical perception in shallow and deep networks, with expansion near category boundaries and contraction far from them, stronger at deeper layers. The origin face is open for inspection in a way it is not for us.

Cacioli (2026), in a single-author, unreviewed preprint, reports representational-similarity evidence that six language models from five architecture families warp their hidden-state geometry at digit-count boundaries, absent at non-boundary controls. The preprint distinguishes models that both categorise explicitly and warp from models whose geometry warps although they cannot report the category distinction. Until it replicates it is a hypothesis-generator, not evidence; [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers]] treats the general case.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The void bears most directly on **[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]**, in two registers. The first is about the science: categories all the way down was the simpler model, and it held the field for decades on measurement artefact; the lateralised-Whorf story was elegant and is unsettled. The second is about the perceiver: a category is itself a parsimony operation, many stimuli under one label, and the simplicity of a perceived joint is no evidence that the world has a joint there. The colour boundary that drifts with the stimulus set is one illustration.

On **[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]** the Map records two readings and does not pretend the data choose. Toscano et al. (2010) show early encoding, linear and category-independent, coming apart from the categorical content the subject reports; whether default experience is categorical too is untested. If it is, the Map reads the phenomenal deliverable as no transparent readout of the functional encoding, the shape the [[the-surplus-void|surplus]] and [[phenomenal-quality-void|phenomenal-quality]] discussions rely on. On a physicalist reading consciousness is identified with a late, post-N1 categorical stage, and the void is where the readout sits in the pipeline. The evidence constrains both readings by fixing the structure each must explain; it establishes neither.

On **[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]** the connection is modest: the verbal-dual-task result of Winawer et al. (2007) is a modulation of the warp by concurrent cognition, which a physicalist account covers, and is no evidence of non-physical influence.

The void is framework-independent: physicalist and dualist alike owe an account of why the default readout is the category when the gradient is kept, and of why installed and discovered joints are indistinguishable from inside. It belongs in the framework-independent column of the [[voids]] index and the operation-void family of [[apex/taxonomy-of-voids]].
