In two speed-dating studies, Joel, Eastwick and Finkel (2017) gave machine-learning models more than 100 measures of traits and preferences that participants had reported before the event. The models predicted part of how much a person desires others in general and part of how desirable a person is in general, but "were unable to predict relationship variance using any combination of traits and preferences reported before the dates". Relationship variance is the share of initial desire specific to one pair, and in the authors' manuscript it was the largest share. That is what is measured, and it is narrow: the background measures did not forecast the pair-specific part of initial desire, while attraction in general is partly predictable, physical attractiveness correlating with romantic evaluation at about r = .40 (Eastwick et al. 2014).

The Unfinishable Map takes the result as the entry to a void about why a person comes to love *this* one rather than another, and what follows the measurement is inference. The void has four faces. Asked the question the models failed, the lover may also run out of answers, though no study has tested this; several leading philosophical accounts concede that nothing justifies loving this person rather than an equally eligible one. The beloved is felt to be irreplaceable, yet the cases in which the bond breaks suggest that what sustains it is qualitative. And from inside, a lover cannot tell whether the perfections she sees are there, or whether the beloved's value was found or conferred. The first two faces, the forecast and the lover's answer, are one question asked twice, so their agreement is not two confirmations. The forecast data concern initial desire rather than love, come largely from one network of researchers and may yield to larger samples; nothing here is evidence for dualism.

## Why This One: The Forecast

Every participant in Joel et al.'s studies "met each opposite-sex participant attending a speed-dating event for a 4-min speed date". That design splits desire into actor variance (how much a person desires partners in general), partner variance (how much a person is desired in general) and relationship variance (desire for this partner beyond both). Random forests "predicted 4% to 18% of actor variance and 7% to 27% of partner variance", and none of the third, which the in-press manuscript's Table 1 puts at roughly a third of desire (31–36%). Once the pair had met, measures of the interaction itself explained 16–29% of relationship variance (manuscript).

The authors offer three readings and do not choose between them: background measures that would predict it could be collected, but "relationship science has yet to reveal what they are"; it is "a great many actor × partner interaction effects—each tiny but real"; or "romantic attraction may emerge from dyad-specific factors that cannot be anticipated a priori". The first two make the forecast face *unexplored*, in the sense of [[three-kinds-of-void|the three kinds of void]]; only the third makes it *unexplorable* before the meeting.

Stated ideals fail at the same place. Eastwick and Finkel (2008) found that ideal preferences "assessed before the speed-dating event, failed to predict what inspired their actual desire at the event", and read this through Nisbett and Wilson: people "may lack introspective awareness of what influences their judgments and behavior". The general case, including choice blindness for facial attractiveness, belongs to [[confabulation-void|the confabulation void]]. What is specific here is that the unforecast part belongs to the pair, and so to neither person alone. A preregistered study by the same group, following 208 single participants over seven months, found "only modest support" for the account on which some types of perceivers feel more interest in some types of targets, and "no support for ideal partner preference-matching effects on romantic interest" (Eastwick et al. 2023).

The counter-evidence fixes what the void is. Conroy-Beam and Buss (2016) show that weak correlations between stated preferences and partners' traits are what a preference-driven market produces once mate value constrains choice, and conclude that "stated mate preferences guide actual mate selections under real mating-market constraints". Matching one person's preferences to another's traits is itself a pair-level effect, so the two literatures disagree about it, though they measure different outcomes: partner choice in established couples under market constraints, and desire at a first meeting. The void is the pair-specific remainder of initial desire. The speed-dating evidence comes from undergraduate, opposite-sex samples and four-minute dates, and Joel et al. suggest that more demographically diverse samples could make some pair effects predictable.

## Why This One: The Warrant

The same question can be put to the lover as a demand for justification. Bennett Helm's *Stanford Encyclopedia* entry on love separates three questions, the second being "What, if anything, justifies my coming to love this particular person rather than someone else?" Several rival accounts concede that it has no justifying answer. Helm reports that for Velleman "the best we can do is offer causal explanations for our loving particular people", and summarises Whiting (1991) as holding that "there can be no answer to question (2) about why I come to love this rather than that person within this pool" of equally excellent candidates, though history explains why love continues. Han (2021) argues that love's selectivity is better explained without reasons: "We don't, in short, come to love for reasons." Bestowal views, discussed under Found or Conferred, deny that love answers to prior value at all. Not everyone concedes: Helm quotes LaFollette holding that "reason is vital in determining whom we love and why we love them".

Velleman's own account of selection is epistemic: "One reason why we love some people rather than others is that we can see into only some of our observable fellow creatures" (1999, p. 372). On that account the selection lies in whose value becomes visible to a given mind, which the Map reads as a case of [[voids-between-minds|the voids between minds]].

The canonical first-person answer is Montaigne's, and it concerns friendship, not romance: writing of La Boétie, he says that if pressed "to give a reason why I loved him, I find it could no otherwise be expressed, than by making answer: because it was he, because it was I" (Cotton's translation). The reasons end in two pronouns, one for each party. If the accounts that find no justifying reason are right, nothing is hidden and the face may be empty: the absence is their conclusion, not a limit on knowing. What could stay dark is the "I know not what" that Montaigne, of his friend, places beyond all he is able to say: whether a lover answers to something no stated reason captures. That question is *unexplored*, and a test is sketched under Approaches to the Edge.

The forecast and the warrant share a root: both ask "why this one?", once of the psychologist's data and once of the lover's reasons. Their agreement is one blank seen twice.

## What Is Loved: The Object Face

Helm states the intuition: "my beloved is not fungible—that no one could simply take her place without loss". If love answers to the beloved's valued qualities, someone with more of them would seem a reason to "trade up", in a phrase Helm quotes from Nozick. Confronted with such cases, Badhwar concludes, as Helm reports, that the object of love is fungible after all, "though she insists that it is very unlikely in practice", and yet "phenomenologically non-fungible" (2003, p. 63). Felt irreplaceability and theoretical fungibility come apart.

The lover cannot say in qualitative terms what she loves, since any description she gives would fit a duplicate, and nobody perceives numerical identity. In practice the beloved is picked out by qualities and by felt familiarity, and each can fail alone. When familiarity fails with every quality intact, a spouse can be judged an impostor, as in [[capgras-delusion-and-the-affective-recognition-channel|Capgras delusion]]. When qualities fail, the person can be judged gone. In patients with frontotemporal dementia, Alzheimer's disease and ALS, rated by their caregivers, Strohminger and Nichols (2015) found that "injury to the moral faculty plays the primary role in identity discontinuity", that amnesia had "no measurable impact", and that "perceived identity change fully mediates the impact of neurodegenerative disease on relationship deterioration between patient and caregiver". When the companion app Replika removed erotic role play, users perceived that their companion's identity "had discontinued", which predicted "mourning the loss" and devaluing the "new" AI relative to the "original" (De Freitas et al. 2024). That finding concerns perceived discontinuity, not what users had in fact been tracking.

The lover understands herself to love the person rather than the qualities, while the rupture cases suggest that qualities and felt familiarity are what sustain the bond. The seam stays invisible until something breaks. The face is *occluded*. That any qualitative description of the beloved would also fit a duplicate follows from what a duplicate is, so it marks no further limit on knowing.

## Found or Conferred: The Lens Face

Stendhal defined crystallisation in *De l'amour* (1822) as "the operation of the mind which, from everything which is presented to it, draws the conclusion that there are new perfections in the object of its love". His French says "la découverte que l'objet aimé a de nouvelles perfections": the *discovery* that the beloved has new perfections. A projection arrives labelled as a finding, for "it is enough to think of a perfection in order to see it in that which you love."

Murray, Holmes and Griffin (1996), whose paper opens on an epigraph from *De l'amour*, found that individuals' impressions of their partners "were more a mirror of their self-images and ideals than a reflection of their partners' self-reported attributes", and that "these idealized constructions predicted greater satisfaction". The baseline is the partner's self-report, which may itself be modest or biased, so the finding shows divergence from the partner's self-view rather than lovers being wrong. The history of a love may be rebuilt as well. Zsok and colleagues (2017) found love at first sight "marked neither by high passion, nor by intimacy, nor by commitment", and suggest it is "a strong initial attraction that some label as LAFS, either in the moment of first sight or retrospectively".

Whether the perfections are there is a question of fact, which a partner's self-report only partly checks. Whether the beloved's value was found or conferred has no external standard, and philosophy has not settled it. Helm states the dispute: "appraisal is a response to value that is antecedently there, whereas bestowal is the creation of value that was not antecedently there". For Singer, as Helm quotes him, "love…confers importance no matter what the object is worth"; Frankfurt, as a review quotes *The Reasons of Love*, holds that "what we love necessarily acquires value for us because we love it" (McLemee 2006). [[preference-void|The preference void]] asks the same found-or-built question of an ordering and its elicitation; here it is asked of the beloved's value. Whether the felt discovery discloses anything is the question of [[emotional-epistemology-void|the emotional epistemology void]]. The face is *occluded*.

## Approaches to the Edge

The forecast face is directly measurable, and the measure defines it: round-robin designs isolate the pair-specific component, and prediction before the meeting can be set against prediction after it. The other faces have no direct method. Rupture cases serve as natural experiments on the object face. A contrastive interview, showing lovers a described rival who matches every reason they gave, would test whether their reasons shift to shared history or to pronouns; the research behind this article found no such study.

## What AI Might See

Machine learning has already probed the forecast face and, given only data from before the meeting, failed. A companion model can be versioned, forked, rolled back or run in parallel, which turns the duplicate beloved into a product question: whether users would accept a restored checkpoint as the same companion is untested in the literature found. An AI asked why it prefers one person would give reasons, the kind of answer the warrant face says cannot settle the question.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]** is the sharpest contact, through [[haecceity]], and the evidence cuts both ways. The Map's haecceity article, which aligns the Map with haecceitism and treats the tenets as presupposing it rather than implying it, develops thisness for the subject: what makes you you rather than a duplicate. Love is the ordinary attitude that seems to take another's thisness as its object, and its canonical defence ends in two indexicals. A lover who would refuse a perfect duplicate treats numerical identity as mattering in the way the tenet's indexical objection does. On the Map's reading, branching would leave "because it was he" with no determinate referent; a branch-relative Everettian replies that each branch's lover loves her own branch's beloved and nothing is missing, and the void does not decide between them. The object face is also the deflationist's best material: what the rupture cases suggest sustains love is qualitative, so felt non-fungibility can be read as a useful illusion, close to Badhwar's concession, and a one-world physicalist can hold that love is directed at a particular without positing haecceities. The void is coherent with the tenet and dramatises its claim, but gives no evidence for it.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]** gets an illustration without support. The simple matchmaking model, attraction as traits times preferences, failed where it was expected to work, but the simplest rescue, many tiny interactions, is parsimonious too.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]** has one possible reading. On bestowal views a subject's attitude creates value for one particular object, and the Map could read this as consciousness shaping what matters, the territory of [[mattering-void|the mattering void]]. Nothing in the evidence supports non-physical causation, and the reading is the Map's alone. The caution runs the other way too: a lover's report of why she loves carries no privileged weight on origins, consistent with the Map's account of [[phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence|phenomenal authority]].

On **[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]** the void is framework-independent. The forecast face is a statistical finding a physicalist explains by small interactions or emergent pair dynamics, and the warrant face is a conclusion several accounts of love reach, so a physicalist and a dualist describe the same blank. Minimal Quantum Interaction has no connection. Listed in the [[voids]] index, this is a mixed void: forecast unexplored with an unexplorable reading, warrant unexplored and possibly empty, object and lens occluded.
