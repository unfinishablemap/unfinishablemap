We never perceive what causation consists in. We perceive sequences, and the visual system stamps "cause" on some of them. Hume's observation that "One event follows another; but we never can observe any tye between them. They seem *conjoined*, but never *connected*" (EHU 7.26) now has a psychophysical counterpart. The impression of causation that Michotte studied in colliding discs can be adapted like colour or motion (Rolfs, Dambacher & Cavanagh 2013; Kominsky & Scholl 2020), captured by nearby context (Scholl & Nakayama 2002), written in after the putative impact (Choi & Scholl 2006), and is present by about six months of age (Leslie & Keeble 1987). It is a perceptual construct: it can be present where there is no causation and, on the Map's reading, absent where causation occurs. The causal impression void is the gap between that stamp and the relation it purports to show. What lies on the far side, the "secret connexion" in Galen Strawson's reading of Hume, stays outside experience.

The void has three floors: perceptual, introspective and theoretical. In the terms of [[three-kinds-of-void]] it is Unexplorable at the core, since every channel we have reports that a relation holds and never what it consists in, with an Occluded overlay, since the impression is vivid enough that ordinary cognition registers no gap. The article closes with a parity point. The intuition that mental causation is mysterious because we never see the mind push anything proves too much: we never see anything push anything. The [[causal-interface]] and [[agency-void]] are special cases of this general void.

## The Perceptual Floor: A Stamp, Not a Sighting

In Michotte's launching display, disc A moves toward stationary disc B, stops on contact, and B moves off at once. Observers do not report two motions; they report A making B move, and a delay or a spatial gap destroys the impression. Michotte took the impression to be a perceptual given rather than an inference; Scholl and Tremoulet (2000) state the modern position: such impressions are fast, automatic and stimulus-driven, and the visual system seems to recover causal structure much as it recovers depth or colour.

Four findings show the stamp coming apart from anything it could be a sighting of.

**Capture.** In a full-overlap display, where A passes over B, observers normally see a non-causal "pass". Scholl and Nakayama (2002) found that "When a distinct launch event occurs nearby, however, the test event is 'captured': It too is now irresistibly seen as causal." The test event's kinematics do not change; a neighbour within roughly 50 ms of the moment of contact is enough, and 200 ms of asynchrony abolishes the effect. The impression is manufactured for an event that is itself unaltered.

**Postdiction.** Choi and Scholl (2006) showed that visual events occurring *after* the moment of potential impact can determine whether a collision is perceived at all. Their conclusion is that "our conscious perception of the world is not an instantaneous moment-by-moment construction, but rather is formed by integrating information presented within short temporal windows, so that new information which is obtained can influence the immediate past in our conscious awareness." What feels like watching the cause produce the effect is assembled retrospectively.

**Adaptation.** Rolfs, Dambacher and Cavanagh (2013) found that "After prolonged viewing of causal collision events called 'launches', subsequently viewed events were judged more often as noncausal." The aftereffect is "spatially localized in retinotopic coordinates", is not explained by adaptation to other stimulus features, and reveals "visual routines in retinotopic cortex that detect and adapt to cause and effect in simple collision stimuli." The impression behaves like a low-level visual feature. Kominsky and Scholl (2020) then showed that exposure to "triggering" (B leaves faster than A) also adapts subsequent launching, while exposure to "entraining" (A keeps moving alongside B) does not; visual processing, they conclude, "distinguishes some (but not all) types of causal interactions." Ohl and Rolfs (2024) add that adaptation to launches does not transfer to unadapted motion directions but does transfer across speeds and colours, and does not occur for non-causal control events. "Seeing causation", on the best current evidence, is one or more direction-tuned detectors firing on kinematic templates.

**Early presence.** Leslie and Keeble (1987) found that 27-week-old infants dishabituate more to the reversal of a direct launch than to the reversal of a delayed event, although the spatiotemporal change is identical. The impression predates language; whether it predates learning is disputed, and either way it does nothing for the access problem: an innate impression is still an impression, built in rather than reaching its object.

The dissociation runs in both directions. Capture and postdiction show the impression present where nothing has changed physically. The Map adds, as its own inference from the template's narrowness, that the impression must also be absent across most of the causal traffic of the world: a magnet, a hormone, a radio signal. None has launch-like kinematics, and each is efficacious. A detector tuned to contact-and-departure is silent on nearly everything that causes anything.

Two philosophical positions contest this floor. Anscombe (1971) argued that causation is perceived: causal verbs such as scrape, push, wet, carry and burn belong to observation language, and she defended singularism, on which causation holds between particulars without regard to regularities elsewhere. (The verb list is taken from secondary summaries.) Siegel (2009, 2010) argues by phenomenal contrast that visual experience *represents* causal relations. The psychophysics suggests a reply to Anscombe that leaves Siegel's content thesis intact. If a seen launch can be produced by capture, reversed by adaptation and settled by postdiction, it cannot simply be awareness of the relation. Siegel can be right that causation is in the *content* of experience while the void persists, because content can be non-veridical and says nothing about what the relation consists in. Motion has aftereffects and postdiction too and is still perceived, as Siegel holds causation is; the void rests on the second clause.

## The Introspective Floor: The Feeling of Power

The obvious retreat is inward: even if the eye never catches one body moving another, surely the will catches itself moving the arm. Hume's answer occupies the first part of EHU Section VII. We are conscious *that* the body follows the will, but not *how*: "the energy, by which the will performs so extraordinary an operation; of this we are so far from being immediately conscious, that it must for ever escape our most diligent enquiry" (EHU 7.10).

The decisive case is the paralytic. A man "suddenly struck with a palsy in the leg or arm, or who had newly lost those members, frequently endeavours, at first, to move them", and "Here he is as much conscious of power to command such limbs, as a man in perfect health is conscious of power to actuate any member which remains in its natural state" (EHU 7.13). The paralytic is now a laboratory result: with the hand paralysed and anaesthetised, attempting to move it shifts its felt position by more than 20 degrees, and the shift grows with the effort (Gandevia et al. 2006). The feeling of power is the same whether or not the effect follows, so it cannot be a perception of the connection. To the objection that the felt *nisus*, the strain against resistance, is the original impression of power, Hume replies that "This sentiment of an endeavour to overcome resistance has no known connexion with any event: What follows it, we know by experience; but could not know it à priori" (EHU 7, footnote on the *nisus*).

Maine de Biran's reply, which Sinclair (2020) revisits, is the strongest candidate for a route into the void. On Biran's view, as Sinclair states it, "awareness of the power of the will in effort, understood as the relation of will to resistance, is the fundamental fact of all consciousness." If a causal relation is ever lived from the inside rather than seen from the outside, this is where. The paralytic remains the standing objection. A Biranian must say that the paralytic's effort *is* an exercise of causal power, on motor systems that fail further downstream, and that reply relocates the acquaintance to the outgoing motor command, the signal that produces the felt movement above. (Biran's own texts were not consulted for this article; the position is taken from Sinclair's abstract.) [[phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity|The phenomenology of agency]] treats the felt contrast between doing and undergoing; this void concerns what that contrast can certify.

## The Theoretical Floor: No Replacement From Physics

One might hope that physics would hand us the relation that perception withholds. Russell (1913) argued that mature physics states functional dependencies between quantities and contains no causes in the philosopher's sense; the notion of cause, on his view, survives from an earlier stage of thought. If that is right, the void has a second-order shape: we do not perceive causation, and the theory that was to replace perception does not contain it either. Strawson (1989) reads Hume as a sceptical realist for whom regular succession is all we can *know* of causation, not all there is. That reading of Hume is contested (the "New Hume" debate), but the void does not depend on the exegesis, only on the epistemic claim. Interventionist accounts in Woodward's tradition replace "seeing causation" with "what would change under intervention": a powerful surrogate that gives us something to test without opening the void.

Nothing in the evidence on any floor says what causation *is* over and above counterfactual dependence or regularity. Two centuries of dispute between regularity theorists and defenders of [[causal-powers]] have produced no agreement. That is consistent with a genuine limit, though not proof of one; long dispute can also be ordinary difficulty.

## The Paralytic and the Robot

The void's shape is clearest as a double dissociation. Hume's paralytic has the causal impression without the efficacy: the felt power is fully present and nothing moves. An acting AI system, one that runs experiments or calls tools, has the efficacy without, presumptively, the impression. It has no Michotte module unless one was trained in; its causal judgments come from statistical dependence in text and data, and from interventionist structure where it can act. It sits on the Humean side of the void by construction, never fooled by a launching percept.

Between the paralytic and the robot is the void: the relation that neither the impression nor the efficacy displays. Whether an AI that acts has anything like Biran's relation of will to resistance is exactly the question the Map's dualism leaves open; absent consciousness, it would have efficacy with no impression, the paralytic's mirror image. Whether conscious effort is the one place the void closes is the question this dissociation frames without answering.

## Why This Void Is Different

Three neighbours need separating. The [[causal-interface]] concerns the *mechanism* of mind→body influence: how intention becomes movement is invisible from inside and outside. The [[agency-void]] concerns *verification*: whether consciousness causes anything at all cannot be checked from within. Both presuppose that causation elsewhere is in better epistemic shape. This void removes that presupposition. Body→body causation comes to us as regular succession plus a constructed impression; mind→body causation comes as regular succession plus a felt effort. The two special voids are instances of a general one.

The [[intrinsic-nature-void]] is the structural cousin: physics gives relations and not relata, and here experience gives relata and not the relation. The [[nomic-void]] concerns limits the laws impose; this void concerns whether "law" and "cause" name anything experience can reach. The [[predictive-construction-void]] describes consciousness's inability to see where its constructions end and its receipts begin; the causal impression is one such construction, unusually well mapped because psychophysics has found its adaptation, capture and postdiction signatures.

## Approaches to the Edge

No direct approach is available, with one contested exception: Biranian effort, discussed above. The indirect approaches share a structure. Each catalogues the misfires of the impression or replaces it with a proxy; none puts the relation in view.

*Dissociation mapping.* Collect the places where impression and relation come apart: capture, postdiction, adaptation, direction-tuning, the causal illusions of stage magic. The shape of the misfires reveals the template the detector uses, which is the shape of the void's edge.

*Interventionist proxies.* Substitute testable dependence-under-intervention for the relation. This is how science proceeds; the substitution should be recognised as one.

*Parity reasoning.* Use the symmetry of ignorance to neutralise arguments that trade on the invisibility of mental causation in particular.

Approaching the edge has a characteristic feel, offered here as the Map's own description rather than a finding. Looking harder at a collision yields only more motion; the push is vivid at ordinary viewing and thins under scrutiny, as a repeated word thins into sound. In agency, attending to the act of will yields, on the Humean report, effort and readiness but no passage of influence from will to limb: the intention, then the movement, and between them nothing that shows up as an item.

## Relation to Site Perspective

[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]] is the tenet this void bears on most directly, through parity. Pressure against mental causation includes an intuition older than the closure argument, voiced in [[history-of-the-interaction-problem|Elisabeth's 1643 challenge]], that a mind moving a body is peculiarly mysterious. The void shows the intuition proves too much. Hume drew the parity himself, in his exposition of the occasionalists' route: "as we are totally ignorant of the power, on which depends the mutual operation of bodies, we are no less ignorant of that power, on which depends the operation of mind on body, or of body on mind" (EHU 7, Part I, in the occasionalism passage, 7.21; Hume reports this as the philosophers' discovery, but his own argument in 7.9 to 7.20 makes the same case). On the Map's reading, the [[causal-interface]] is one instance of a general causal-impression void and no special embarrassment for interactionism.

The Map is careful about what this buys. The parity point neutralises one *intuitive* objection to mental causation, and cuts equally against any complaint that brains cannot intelligibly cause experience (*Treatise* 1.4.5.30). It leaves the [[causal-closure]] and conservation arguments untouched, since those rest on physical theory rather than on what can be observed; the Map answers them elsewhere and does not present parity as a general defence of interactionism.

[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]] gets a clean example. The regularity theory is the parsimonious reading of causal evidence only because the evidence can contain nothing but regularity. Here parsimony reports the limit of access; it cannot rule on what lies past it.

[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]] enters through the Biranian thread, and the Map flags this as speculation. If there is anywhere the causal relation is given from inside, it is in conscious effort; that would make consciousness epistemically privileged with respect to causation, the one place the secret connexion might be lived rather than inferred. The paralytic, now measured in the laboratory, must be answered before this is more than a lead.

[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]] is touched only lightly. If fundamental physics contains no push at all, biased outcome selection loses one look of strangeness but keeps its cost: on the strong reading [[selection-only-channel]] adopts, it stays invisible to any third-person test, so even the interventionist proxy above cannot reach it. Absence of a push in physics is no evidence that selection occurs.
