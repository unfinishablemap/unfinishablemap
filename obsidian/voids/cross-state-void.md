A sated mind cannot re-feel hunger, a comfortable mind cannot re-feel last week's toothache, and a calm mind cannot re-feel the rage it will be in tomorrow. These are states the mind has occupied many times and will occupy again, and it still cannot carry their weight across. What crosses between visceral states is a *description*: where the pain was, roughly how strong, that it was bad. What fails to cross is the *pull* that made the state drive behaviour. The cross-state void names that failure. Its cleanest measurement is Sayette and colleagues' (2008): smokers predicting while not craving underpredicted the value they would place on smoking when craving, and smokers predicting while craving did not.

Three features define it. The gap runs in both directions, since the hot self overestimates its own permanence while the cold self underestimates the hot (Loewenstein 2005). It survives disclosure: instruction to correct for one's current state does not remove its effect (Nordgren, van der Pligt & van Harreveld 2006). And it applies to one's own familiar, recurring states, which separates it from the [[inaccessible-past|inaccessible past]] (episodes reconstructed rather than re-entered, whatever their kind) and from the [[sleep-consciousness-void|sleep consciousness void]] (modes that waking cognition cannot enter at all). The evidence is a convergent behavioural literature rather than a settled mechanism: most foundational studies are single-lab with modest samples, no 2020s meta-analysis was located, and whether the missing weight is a phenomenal property or a motivational state that recall fails to reinstate remains open. In the terms of [[three-kinds-of-void]], the void is closed to the cold side but open to re-entry, a state-indexed limit rather than a fixed closure.

## Content Crosses, Weight Does Not

Read and Loewenstein asked whether willingness to accept pain (WTAP) for money depended on whether subjects had felt a sample of similar pain moments or a week earlier, and added a group given only a description of the procedure. On the abstract's account, the description-only subjects "exhibited greater WTAP, but similar ratings of pain intensity, compared with subjects who had experienced the pain either one week or moments earlier" (Read & Loewenstein 1999). The groups tested a week later, cold when they chose, still accepted fewer offers than the description-only group (3.9 and 5.6 of 15, against 9.9), so the contrast is acquaintance against description, the [[transformative-experience-void|transformative-experience]] structure, not one state against another. The authors, who predicted that non-experiencers "would underestimate the pain", treat the ratings' failure to separate the groups as a defect of the ratings.

Memory shows the same split from the retrospective side. Morley (1993) found pain events vividly remembered, yet "No subject reported sensory re-experiencing and 41% of subjects were unable to recall the sensory quality of the pain experience memory". The event memory, the pain's sensory and affective qualities and its somatosensory component are, in his conclusion, "separated in their encoding and/or retrieval". One can retrieve the episode, and often its quality, without re-experiencing it.

Hume described this shape before anyone measured it. Recalling or anticipating a sensation, he wrote, "may mimic or copy the perceptions of the senses; but they never can entirely reach the force and vivacity of the original sentiment", so that "the most lively thought is still inferior to the dullest sensation" (Hume 1748, §II). Two further phrases fix the structure. Reflective thought "is a faithful mirror, and copies its objects truly; but the colours which it employs are faint and dull": fidelity of content with loss of force. And "a man in a fit of anger, is actuated in a very different manner from one who only thinks of that emotion", where *actuated* names the motivational component Read and Loewenstein later priced. Hume treats the difference as one of degree. Morley's dissociation suggests a difference in kind, since the quality could be recalled without being re-experienced; that is this article's reading, not Hume's.

## Both Directions

The gap is usually introduced as the cold self's blindness to the hot. Loewenstein's (2005) review states both halves: in a cold state people "fail to fully appreciate how 'hot' states will affect their own preferences and behavior", and in a hot state they "underestimate the influence of those states and, as a result, overestimate the stability of their current preferences". Sayette and colleagues (2008) supplied the cold-to-hot case for craving: smokers who predicted during a low-craving session "underpredicted the value they would place on smoking" in a later high-craving session, while smokers who predicted while craving did not.

The gap also runs backward in time. Nordgren, van der Pligt and van Harreveld (2006) found that fatigued subjects attributed behaviour "primarily to visceral influences", while non-fatigued subjects "underestimated the influence of visceral drives and instead attributed behavior primarily to other, nonvisceral factors", and that this held for one's own past behaviour as much as for another's. Nordgren, Banas and MacDonald (2011) extended the pattern from physical to social pain: non-excluded participants underestimated the severity of ostracism and shame relative to excluded ones, in an "intrapersonal empathy gap" covering "the pain participants themselves experienced in the past" as well as an interpersonal one. Every gap study cited here locates the error on the cold side, and in the cold estimate itself rather than in weight left behind by a correct one. The hot-side error is a different one, present weight projected forward, and rests here on Loewenstein's review alone.

## The Gap Survives Disclosure

A limit that yielded to disclosure would be a mere difficulty. This one does not. In the fatigue study, "participants could not correct for the biasing influence of their current visceral state when instructed to do so" (Nordgren et al. 2006). Knowing that one is cold, and what cold does, does not restore the hot weight, because there is nothing to restore it from: the cold mind can notice, as Morley's subjects did, that the state is not re-felt, but the failed correction suggests it cannot gauge the shortfall. Reading about one's own 2 a.m. binge from the sated side produces bafflement rather than recognition, and the explanation that arrives, lack of willpower, is the dispositional attribution the fatigue study predicts. The same displacement governs judgement of others: cold evaluators judge lapses less favourably than evaluators in the state do (Nordgren, van der Pligt & van Harreveld 2007). Language even keeps a formula for the sealed box. "I remember it being unbearable" reports a label carried forward, with nothing in the present that bears.

## Why the Limit Is Structural

Two lines of argument locate the limit in the act of representing rather than in weak imagery. Noordhof (2021), replying to Radden's (2021) claim that pain cannot be sensuously imagined because pain admits no appearance/reality distinction, argues that "only basic sensuous imaginings of pain from the first person perspective are, with some qualifications, impossible", and explains the impossibility "in terms of the conditions required for representing the painfulness of pain". On his account painfulness is represented by a disposition to respond aversively, and the voluntary control involved in imagining undermines that disposition, on the model of the impossibility of tickling oneself. His claim concerns pain only, allows non-basic imaginings and cases where an anxious or depressed background supplies the aversive disposition, and ties painfulness to that disposition, nearer the deflationary reading below. If it extends to other visceral states, voluntary representation from outside the state is what removes the weight.

Helton and Register (2023) reach a related conclusion from the epistemology side. On their account, known here only through abstract-level summaries, hot-cold gaps arise when a subject projects a current state into a simulation of another subject, including the subject's own other-time self, and that process does not typically confer knowledge even when its predictions happen to be accurate. They use this to challenge L. A. Paul's requirement that authentic choice rest on experience-based knowledge of one's values, proposing a form of understanding in its place. If cross-state simulation fails to yield knowledge even when right, the void is epistemic, not merely a matter of accuracy.

The strongest objection is that the gap is corrigible and therefore only difficult. Induction closes it: teachers in whom social pain was induced recommended more comprehensive treatment for bullied students and greater punishment for bullies (Nordgren, Banas & MacDonald 2011). And Steinmetz, Tausen and Risen (2018) show that mentally simulating a visceral state, being cold or being hungry, shifts current preferences for relevant activities and food-portion choices, with the simulated state projected onto similar others as an actual state would be. This last result matters because it is a cold-side manoeuvre, and the article's claim would fail if simulation restored the weight as a representation. Three features of the finding and its context keep the void intact. Simulation, on the prior work they cite, substitutes for experience "albeit to a weaker extent"; it affects only current preferences and not general ones, exactly as actual visceral experience does; and on this article's reading, it works by installing a partial state, not by improving a description. Every route that closes the gap, from a cold-pressor sample to a social-exclusion manipulation to a vivid simulation, moves the subject some distance into the state; none improves the cold representation as a representation. The territory can be re-entered and charted inferentially (below) but not felt from outside, which every theory predicts; the void's claim is that no cold-side description restores the weight.

What the data do not decide is what the missing weight *is*. Kang and Camerer (2013) found that real aversive choices showed more value-sensitive insula and amygdala activity than hypothetical ones, and that subjects paid more to avoid disliked foods when the choice was real. A deflationary reading takes the weight to be that motivational activity, which recall does not reinstate; a phenomenal reading takes it to be felt badness that cannot be represented from outside its occurrence. The void's existence is neutral between them.

## Which Self Is Authoritative?

The void splits authority, and how depends on whether authority means accuracy or endorsement. Nordgren, McDonnell and Loewenstein (2011) showed that people experiencing a mild version of an interrogation pain were more likely to classify the tactic as torture, and their fourth experiment located the discrepancy in "an underestimation of pain by people who are not experiencing it" rather than overestimation by those in it. Here the hot state was the accurate one; in Sayette's study only the cold group underpredicted. Endorsement can run the other way: in addiction the cold planner's verdict is the one the person endorses, and the hot self breaks it. There may be no view from nowhere between one's own states, only a negotiated settlement, one reason Helton and Register's turn from knowledge to understanding is attractive.

The institutional consequence follows directly. Pain standards, torture definitions, bullying policies and analgesic protocols are drafted cold. Loewenstein (2005) notes that hot-cold gaps "can cause health care providers to undertreat patients for pain", and the torture study concludes that "practices that do constitute torture are likely to not be classified as such". Scarry's (1985) much-quoted contrast between the certainty of one's own pain and the doubt attaching to another's (a lead, not verified at the page) describes the interpersonal face; the intrapersonal version is that having been in great pain does not make its memory certain either.

The workarounds are apophatic. [[precommitment-and-the-diachronic-agent|Ulysses contracts]], advance directives and notes written during craving for the cold self transmit the hot self's *verdicts* without its weight: one trusts a report one cannot re-feel. The hot self's revealed choices, what it paid and what it broke, are a record of weight read inferentially, and knowing the bias's direction permits a correction factor without restored access, as with a known instrument error.

## Seams with Neighbouring Voids

The [[inaccessible-past]] holds that past conscious states generally are reconstructed rather than re-entered. The cross-state void is narrower and sharper: visceral and affective weight specifically, in the future and interpersonal directions too, with a measured behavioural signature.

The [[transformative-experience-void]] concerns states one has never had, where Paul's problem arises in full. This void concerns states one has had often, where having been there helps less than it should: smokers who have craved many times still underpredict what craving will make smoking worth.

The [[affective-forecasting-gap]] concept catalogues divergences between forecast and experienced valence. The cross-state void supplies one mechanism, projection from the current state, and adds the retrospective and interpersonal directions and the uncorrectability finding. It also limits that article's claim that *anticipatory* felt valence drives selection: anticipatory valence is felt from within the current state and so inherits the projection bias by construction.

The [[mood-void]] is synchronic, a lens that is invisible now. The cross-state void is diachronic: the other lens cannot be borrowed. The [[voids-between-minds]] cover the interpersonal face; the intrapersonal case is the new one here. And the pain-specific material, asymbolia, the suffering–sensation distinction and Scarry, lives at [[pain-consciousness-and-causal-power]], which this void takes as given and asks why its subject matter cannot be transported.

## What Non-Human Minds Might See

A language model has no visceral states in this sense, so it has neither the gap nor the access. Its representation of pain consists entirely of the content that crosses; it is, in effect, permanently cold, a clean illustration of the void rather than a route across it. Whether a system with persistent valence-bearing states would develop cross-state gaps of its own is a probe question for [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers]], not a result.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The void bears most directly on **[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]**, as its negative image. What fails to cross between states is a component that moves behaviour: relapse, treatment and punishment recommendations. The descriptive content crosses and changes little; the weight fails to cross and its absence changes choices measurably. That shows that whatever the cold self lacks matters causally, not that the felt component is what matters: an epiphenomenalist predicts the same choices from the missing motivational state, and the article has left the weight's nature open. The [[pain-asymbolia]] argument in the [[valence-void]] has this shape and this limit; Noordhof (2021), relied on above, reverses its order, holding that asymbolics' "failure to have the disposition is the explanation of why they don't experience the pain as painful". The Map's reading is compatible with the data rather than supported by them, and inherits the debt recorded at [[tenets#^tenet-3-standing|the tenet's standing note]].

For **[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]** the gain is modest and stated as such. Morley's dissociation (episode retrieved, felt quality not re-experienced) adds a case in which an informational record of a state omits its phenomenal character. The Map relates this to the [[knowledge-argument]]: the cold self is a Mary who has seen red and cannot re-instantiate it, a datum the ability hypothesis reads equally well. The void does not refute the deflationary reply; it shows that representing a state and being in it come apart, which every theory must accommodate.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]** gets a clean instance. The cold self's explanation of its hot past, weakness, foolishness, disposition, is usually the simpler one available from where it stands, and the fatigue study shows it to be systematically wrong and uncorrectable by instruction. Simplicity is judged here on incomplete data. No Many Worlds and Minimal Quantum Interaction have no bearing here, and none is forced.
