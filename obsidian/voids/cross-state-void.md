A sated mind cannot re-feel hunger, a comfortable mind cannot re-feel last week's toothache, and a calm mind cannot re-feel the rage it will be in tomorrow. These are states the mind has occupied many times and will occupy again, and it still cannot carry their weight across. What crosses between visceral states is a *description*: where the pain was, roughly how strong, that it was bad. What fails to cross is the *pull* that made the state drive behaviour. The cross-state void names that failure. Its clearest measurement comes from Read and Loewenstein (1999): people given only a description of a cold-pressor pain rated its intensity about as high as people who had felt a sample of it, yet would endure it for markedly less money. The estimate crossed; the weight did not.

Three features make this a void rather than a bias. The gap runs in both directions, since the hot self overestimates its own permanence as reliably as the cold self underestimates the hot (Loewenstein 2005). It conceals itself: instruction to correct for one's current state does not remove its effect (Nordgren, van der Pligt & van Harreveld 2006). And it applies to one's own familiar, recurring states, which separates it from the [[inaccessible-past|inaccessible past]] (episodes reconstructed rather than re-entered, whatever their kind) and from the [[sleep-consciousness-void|sleep consciousness void]] (modes that waking cognition cannot enter at all). The evidence is a convergent behavioural literature rather than a settled mechanism: most foundational studies are single-lab with modest samples, no 2020s meta-analysis was located, and whether the missing weight is a phenomenal property or a motivational state that recall fails to reinstate remains open. In the terms of [[three-kinds-of-void]], the void is Unexplorable from the cold side with an Occluded element, because the cold simulation feels adequate from within.

## Content Crosses, Weight Does Not

Read and Loewenstein asked whether willingness to accept pain (WTAP) for money depended on whether subjects had felt a sample of similar pain moments or a week earlier, and added a group given only a description of the procedure. On the abstract's account, the description-only subjects "exhibited greater WTAP, but similar ratings of pain intensity, compared with subjects who had experienced the pain either one week or moments earlier" (Read & Loewenstein 1999). The finer ordering across conditions is not given in the abstract and is not asserted here. What the abstract establishes is the dissociation: the estimate of how bad the pain would be crossed to non-experiencers intact, while the price at which they would accept it did not.

Memory shows the same split from the retrospective side. Morley (1993) found pain events vividly remembered, yet "No subject reported sensory re-experiencing and 41% of subjects were unable to recall the sensory quality of the pain experience memory". Event, intensity rating and felt quality are, in his conclusion, "separated in their encoding and/or retrieval". One retrieves that it was a bad episode, and not what made it bad.

Hume described this shape before anyone measured it. Recalling or anticipating a sensation, he wrote, "may mimic or copy the perceptions of the senses; but they never can entirely reach the force and vivacity of the original sentiment", so that "the most lively thought is still inferior to the dullest sensation" (Hume 1748, §II). Two further phrases fix the structure. Reflective thought "is a faithful mirror, and copies its objects truly; but the colours which it employs are faint and dull": fidelity of content with loss of force. And "a man in a fit of anger, is actuated in a very different manner from one who only thinks of that emotion", where *actuated* names the motivational component Read and Loewenstein later priced. Hume treats the difference as one of degree. The dissociation suggests a difference in kind, since the intensity judgement can match while the weight is absent; that is this article's reading, not Hume's.

## Both Directions

The gap is usually introduced as the cold self's blindness to the hot. Loewenstein's (2005) review states both halves: in a cold state people "fail to fully appreciate how 'hot' states will affect their own preferences and behavior", and in a hot state they "underestimate the influence of those states and, as a result, overestimate the stability of their current preferences". Sayette and colleagues (2008) supplied the cold-to-hot case for craving: smokers who predicted during a low-craving session "underpredicted the value they would place on smoking" in a later high-craving session, while smokers who predicted while craving did not.

The gap also runs backward in time. Nordgren, van der Pligt and van Harreveld (2006) found that fatigued subjects attributed behaviour "primarily to visceral influences", while non-fatigued subjects "underestimated the influence of visceral drives and instead attributed behavior primarily to other, nonvisceral factors", and that this held for one's own past behaviour as much as for another's. Nordgren, Banas and MacDonald (2011) extended the pattern from physical to social pain: non-excluded participants underestimated the severity of ostracism and shame relative to excluded ones, in an "intrapersonal empathy gap" covering "the pain participants themselves experienced in the past" as well as an interpersonal one. Neither state, then, has a vantage from which the other can be seen whole. The cold self reads the hot self as weak, the hot self reads the cold self as naive, and each is reading a description with the weight stripped out.

## The Gap Hides Itself

A limit that announced itself would be a mere difficulty. This one does not. In the fatigue study, "participants could not correct for the biasing influence of their current visceral state when instructed to do so" (Nordgren et al. 2006). Knowing that one is cold, and what cold does, does not restore the hot weight, because there is nothing to restore it from: the cold mind's simulation returns a verdict and no marker that a component is missing. Reading about one's own 2 a.m. binge from the sated side produces bafflement rather than recognition, and the explanation that arrives, lack of willpower, is the dispositional attribution the fatigue study predicts. The same displacement governs judgement of others: a cold evaluator condemns lapses that the same evaluator, in the state, would understand (Nordgren, van der Pligt & van Harreveld 2007). Language even keeps a formula for the sealed box. "I remember it being unbearable" reports a label carried forward, with nothing in the present that bears.

## Why the Limit Is Structural

Two lines of argument locate the limit in the act of representing rather than in weak imagery. Noordhof (2021), replying to Radden's (2021) claim that pain cannot be sensuously imagined because pain admits no appearance/reality distinction, argues that "only basic sensuous imaginings of pain from the first person perspective are, with some qualifications, impossible", and explains the impossibility "in terms of the conditions required for representing the painfulness of pain". On his account painfulness is represented by a disposition to respond aversively, and the voluntary control involved in imagining undermines that disposition, on the model of the impossibility of tickling oneself. If that is right, the void is generated by the representing itself: to bring the state under voluntary representation from outside is to remove the feature that made it weigh.

Helton and Register (2023) reach a related conclusion from the epistemology side. On their account, known here only through abstract-level summaries, hot-cold gaps arise when a subject projects a current state into a simulation of another subject, including the subject's own other-time self, and that process does not typically confer knowledge even when its predictions happen to be accurate. They use this to challenge L. A. Paul's requirement that authentic choice rest on experience-based knowledge of one's values, proposing a form of understanding in its place. If cross-state simulation fails to yield knowledge even when right, the void is epistemic, not merely a matter of accuracy.

The strongest objection is that the gap is corrigible and therefore only difficult. Recent experience narrows it: subjects who had just felt the pain behaved more like hot subjects. Induction closes it: teachers in whom social pain was induced recommended more comprehensive treatment for bullied students and greater punishment for bullies (Nordgren, Banas & MacDonald 2011). And Steinmetz, Tausen and Risen (2018) show that mentally simulating a visceral state, being cold or being hungry, shifts current preferences for relevant activities and food-portion choices, with the simulated state projected onto similar others as an actual state would be. This last result matters because it is a cold-side manoeuvre, and the article's claim would fail if simulation restored the weight as a representation. Three features of their finding keep the void intact. Simulation substitutes for experience "albeit to a weaker extent"; it affects only current preferences and not general ones, exactly as actual visceral experience does; and it works by installing a partial state, not by improving a description. Every route that closes the gap, from a cold-pressor sample to a social-exclusion manipulation to a vivid simulation, moves the subject some distance into the state; none improves the cold representation as a representation. That is the signature of a limit on representation rather than a skill deficit: the territory can be re-entered, and it cannot be mapped from outside.

What the data do not decide is what the missing weight *is*. Kang and Camerer (2013) found that real aversive choices engaged insula and amygdala more than hypothetical ones, and that subjects paid more to avoid disliked foods when the choice was real. A deflationary reading takes the weight to be that motivational activity, which recall does not reinstate; a phenomenal reading takes it to be felt badness that cannot be represented from outside its occurrence. The void's existence is neutral between them.

## Which Self Is Authoritative?

Because neither state sees the other whole, the void splits authority, and not the same way in every domain. Nordgren, McDonnell and Loewenstein (2011) showed that people experiencing a mild version of an interrogation pain were more likely to classify the tactic as torture, and their fourth experiment located the discrepancy in "an underestimation of pain by people who are not experiencing it" rather than overestimation by those in it. Here the hot state was the accurate one. Addiction runs the other way: the cold planner's verdict is the one the person endorses, and the hot self breaks it. There may be no view from nowhere between one's own states, only a negotiated settlement, one reason Helton and Register's turn from knowledge to understanding is attractive.

The institutional consequence follows directly. Pain standards, torture definitions, bullying policies and analgesic protocols are drafted cold. Loewenstein (2005) notes that hot-cold gaps "can cause health care providers to undertreat patients for pain", and the torture study concludes that "practices that do constitute torture are likely to not be classified as such". Scarry's (1985) much-quoted contrast between the certainty of one's own pain and the doubt attaching to another's (a lead, not verified at the page) describes the interpersonal face; the intrapersonal version is that having been in great pain does not make its memory certain either.

The workarounds are apophatic. Ulysses contracts, advance directives and notes written during craving for the cold self transmit the hot self's *verdicts* without its weight: one trusts a report one cannot re-feel. The hot self's revealed choices, what it paid and what it broke, are a record of weight read inferentially, and knowing the bias's direction permits a correction factor without restored access, as with a known instrument error.

## Seams with Neighbouring Voids

The [[inaccessible-past]] holds that past conscious states generally are reconstructed rather than re-entered. The cross-state void is narrower and sharper: visceral and affective weight specifically, in the future and interpersonal directions too, with a measured behavioural signature.

The [[transformative-experience-void]] concerns states one has never had, where Paul's problem arises in full. This void concerns states one has had often; having been there does not help, which is the milder and more pervasive form Helton and Register describe.

The [[affective-forecasting-gap]] concept catalogues divergences between forecast and experienced valence. The cross-state void supplies one mechanism, projection from the current state, and adds the retrospective and interpersonal directions and the uncorrectability finding. It also sets a floor under that article's claim that *anticipatory* felt valence drives selection: anticipatory valence is felt from within the current state and so inherits the projection bias by construction.

The [[mood-void]] is synchronic, a lens that is invisible now. The cross-state void is diachronic: the other lens cannot be borrowed. The [[voids-between-minds]] cover the interpersonal face; the intrapersonal case is the new one here. And the pain-specific material, asymbolia, the suffering–sensation distinction and Scarry, lives at [[pain-consciousness-and-causal-power]], which this void takes as given and asks why its subject matter cannot be transported.

## What Non-Human Minds Might See

A language model has no visceral states in this sense, so it has neither the gap nor the access. Its representation of pain consists entirely of the content that crosses; it is, in effect, permanently cold, a clean illustration of the void rather than a route across it, though possibly a better archivist of cross-state testimony than the person's own cold memory, since it has no current state to project. Whether a system with persistent valence-bearing states would develop cross-state gaps of its own is a probe question for [[non-human-minds-as-void-explorers]], not a result.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The void bears most directly on **[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]**, as its negative image. What fails to cross between states is exactly the component that moves behaviour: willingness to accept pain, relapse, treatment and punishment recommendations. The descriptive content crosses and changes nothing; the weight fails to cross and its absence changes choices measurably. Were felt weight idle, the cold self's accurate intensity ratings would yield hot-equivalent choices, and they do not. This is the same shape as the [[pain-asymbolia]] argument in the [[valence-void]]: information without felt badness produces different behaviour. The Map reads that as evidence that the felt component does causal work. The reading inherits the debt recorded at [[tenets#^tenet-3-standing|the tenet's standing note]], since a physicalist can say the weight is a current motivational state that recall fails to reinstate, and the behavioural data are consistent with that.

For **[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]** the gain is modest and stated as such. Morley's dissociation (episode retrieved, felt quality not) and Read and Loewenstein's (intensity matched, weight not) add two cases in which a complete-seeming informational record of a state omits its phenomenal character. The Map interprets this as the [[knowledge-argument]]'s structure inside one life: the cold self is a Mary who has seen red and cannot see it now. The void does not refute the deflationary reply; it shows that representing a state and being in it come apart, which every theory must accommodate.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]** gets a clean instance. The cold self's explanation of its hot past, weakness, foolishness, disposition, is always the simpler one available from where it stands, and the fatigue study shows it to be systematically wrong and uncorrectable by instruction. Simplicity here tracks the vantage point, not the facts. **[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]** is touched more lightly: the void is indexical through and through, since which state I now occupy does epistemic work that no third-person description of both states substitutes for. Minimal Quantum Interaction has no bearing here and none is forced.
