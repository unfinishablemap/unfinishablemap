Consciousness never catches itself coming to believe. The moment at which a proposition stops being entertained and starts being held true is the most consequential transition in a rational life, since inference, action and testimony all run on it, yet it has no first-person marker. The Unfinishable Map calls this the Assent Void and gives it three faces with three different modal standings: assent cannot be *commanded* (a conceptual limit, on the dominant view); it has *already happened* when deliberate evaluation seems to begin (a contested psychological finding); and any attempt to look *at* the attitude is redirected to the world it is about (a structural feature of the concept of belief). The faces conjoin on one event, and that event is causally central and phenomenally absent.

The epistemic status should be stated at once. The control and transparency faces rest on arguments most of the literature accepts and which would bind any believer whatever its substrate. The timing face rests on an unresolved experimental dispute, Gilbert's Spinozan model against its critics, and claims only what both sides grant: the first truth-assignment happens below deliberate judgment. The Map presents the void as framework-independent, and nothing below is evidence for dualism; what dualism offers is a reading, set out in the Relation to Site Perspective section.

## Assent Is Not Suspension, Decision or Certainty

The [[suspension-void]] concerns *withholding*: consciousness cannot verify, sustain or coherently perform the refusal to judge. Its subject is what happens when assent is refused; this article's subject is what happens when assent occurs. They meet at one point: if the Spinozan model described below is right, "suspension" may always be the retraction of an assent already given, which sharpens the suspension void's verification face; since the evidence is contested, the suspension article should carry the point as a conditional.

The [[decision-void]] concerns practical commitment, the collapse of open deliberation into a choice to *act*. Assent is doxastic commitment, taking a proposition to be true rather than resolving to do something. Hieronymi, discussed below, argues that intention and belief share one non-voluntary structure, so the two voids are cognate; but the decision void's latency face rests on readiness-potential studies with no doxastic analogue, and its closure face concerns the end of weighing options, not the assignment of a truth-value.

Certainty is the *feeling* that accompanies a belief once held, the felt rightness the [[noetic-feelings-void]] catalogues. The assent void is upstream of it: the formation event, not the confidence signal that later reports on its product.

## Three Faces, One Void

The void has the [[apex/conjunction-coalesce|conjunction-coalesce]] shape of the suspension, decision and agency voids: several faces whose informativeness lies in their seam. In the [[three-kinds-of-void|three-kinds]] vocabulary of the [[voids]] index, two faces are unexplorable in principle and one is occluded in fact.

### Control (Unexplorable, Conceptual)

One cannot believe at will. Alston's introspective test makes the point for anyone: try to believe, now, that the United States is still a British colony. The *Stanford Encyclopedia* entry on doxastic voluntarism (Boespflug & Jackson 2024) separates **psychological involuntarism**, on which we happen to lack the capacity, from **conceptual involuntarism**, on which belief's nature excludes it: because belief has a constitutive aim or norm of truth, a state adopted for reasons unconnected to truth would not be a belief at all.

Williams (1973) gave the classic argument for the conceptual reading. In the SEP's rendering, "to believe *p* is to take *p* to be true"; if one could believe at will, one would know one had that power, and could then find oneself holding, as a belief, something one regarded as false. Hieronymi (2006) reaches the same verdict by another route: "although belief is subject to two quite robust forms of agency, 'believing at will' is impossible; one cannot believe in the way one ordinarily acts."

The indirect routes everyone grants, Pascal's holy water or Kant's directing of choice toward the evidence, arrange conditions under which belief will form rather than forming it. Ginet (2001) offers candidate counterexamples where the evidence is non-compelling, such as the juror who decides the defendant is guilty; the SEP presents these as contested. On the dominant reading the control face is unexplorable in the strict sense: no believer, human or artificial, could have a view onto a believing-at-will, because there is no such act to view.

### Timing (Occluded, Psychological)

The second face concerns when assent happens relative to the felt act of judging. Gilbert (1991) contrasted two models. On the **Cartesian** model, named for the Fourth Meditation's picture of judgment as an act of the will, comprehension comes first and an effortful assessment then ends in acceptance or rejection. On the **Spinozan** model, "comprehension includes acceptance of that which is comprehended", and rejection is a later, effortful undoing: "People believe in the ideas they comprehend, as quickly and automatically as they believe in the objects they see."

The Spinozan evidence is a family of load paradigms. Gilbert, Tafarodi & Malone (1993) had participants read statements flagged as false; under cognitive load they later treated the flagged statements as true, and the effect reached consequential judgments. Mandelbaum (2014) gives the strongest philosophical defence: "Evidence is presented demonstrating that we cannot withhold assent from any proposition we happen to consider." If so, what feels like judging is the un-believing of something already assented to.

The model is contested. Hasson, Simmons & Todorov (2005) found that statements informative when false were not represented as true, so "comprehending a statement may not require believing it". Nadarevic & Erdfelder (2013), from two source-memory experiments, concluded that "the results of both experiments clearly contradict the Spinozan model but can be explained in terms of the Cartesian model." Vorms, Harris, Topf & Hahn (2022), in a pre-registered replication with plausibility varied, found that "Gilbert's 'truth bias' does not hold for implausible statements — instead, initial encoding seemingly renders implausible statements 'false'."

What the void claims survives the dispute. Whether the first truth-value is acceptance by default or a prior-weighted tag assigned at encoding, it is assigned below deliberate judgment and is not experienced as a judgment. The two sides disagree about the *content* of an event neither can reach by asking people, which is why it has had to be fought out with dual-task and memory paradigms. This face is occluded rather than unexplorable: the machinery is a third-person object in principle, and the dispute itself is evidence that it is a first-person blank.

### Transparency (Unexplorable, Structural)

The third face concerns what happens when consciousness tries to face the attitude directly. Shah & Velleman (2005) argue that "the deliberative question whether to believe that *p* is transparent to the question whether *p*": to deliberate about whether to believe something just is to deliberate about whether it is so. They explain this by the concept of belief carrying a standard of correctness (correct if and only if true), which sends anyone deliberating under it to the world. They raise, without settling, whether this is the same phenomenon as the transparency of self-ascription following Evans (1982), on which one answers "do I believe *p*?" by attending to whatever would settle whether *p* (paraphrased; not checked against Evans's text).

Either way, the attitude is looked *through* and never *at*. Moore's-paradox sentences ("it is raining, but I don't believe it is raining") register the same structure from the other side, as the [[blindspot-void]] discusses: a first-person report of belief behaves as a report on the world.

## The Seam

Each face is a recognised problem on its own; the void is their conjunction on a single event. The formation cannot be an act, it is over before the felt act of judging begins, and any inward look at it is redirected outward. A believer therefore participates in forming its beliefs, by answering questions about the world, without ever standing in an observer's relation to the forming. The faces are severable (a being for whom the Cartesian model held exactly would lose the timing face and keep the other two), so their co-occurrence in the human case is informative rather than one finding stated three ways.

## Evaluative Control: Agency Without a Handle

The obvious objection to the control face is that it seems to leave believers passive. Hieronymi (2006) distinguishes **managerial control**, in which one acts on an attitude as an object (Pascal's holy water), from **evaluative control**, in which one controls an attitude by changing one's answer to the question it embodies: belief answers "is *p* true?", and to revise the answer is to revise the belief. On her account evaluative control is genuine agency and the ordinary way beliefs and intentions come and go, but it is not voluntary in the way action is and involves no awareness of the attitude as the thing controlled; intention, she argues, has the same structure (a paraphrase).

Evaluative control is agency exercised *through* the transparency face: one acts on one's beliefs by attending to the world, the only direction attention can go. It takes no handle on its object and produces no experience of pushing, which is why trying to believe at will feels, not like effortful failure, but like finding nothing to push on. Contrast [[mental-effort]], where attention *can* be pushed and the pushing is felt; the asymmetry between voluntary attention and involuntary assent is among the most informative features of this region, and the [[phenomenology-of-resistance-across-domains|resistance article]]'s observation that a belief resists being dropped on command has the same source.

## Phenomenology of the Edge

Approaching the edge feels like finding that one already believes. A headline half-read turns out, an hour later, to have been taken on board; a rumour one "does not believe" is shaping what one does. Deliberate judgment is experienced as weighing and then settling, but the settling never presents as a choice between two live doxastic options; it presents as the evidence having come down one way. Where the Spinozan model is right, that experience is a revision of an assent already made. Descartes's picture, on which the will adds affirmation, denial or suspension to what the intellect supplies, is the introspectively natural one.

## Approaches to the Edge

**Direct.** None reaches the formation event. Alston-style introspection establishes the control face and nothing further; contemplative reports of a noticed "grasping" at the moment of perception rest on reports whose accuracy is the thing in question, and were not examined here.

**Indirect.** Load paradigms (Gilbert; Vorms et al.) remove the resources needed for evaluation and inspect what remains; memory-for-falsity paradigms (Hasson et al.; Nadarevic & Erdfelder) infer how a truth-value was first tagged. Pascalian self-manipulation probes the void by working around it, since managerial control succeeds only if the agent loses track of the manipulation, Williams's opacity condition seen from outside. The continued influence of retracted misinformation fits assent without a visible, revocable act.

## Relation to Site Perspective

The void is framework-independent: the control and transparency arguments turn on the concept of belief, the timing dispute is conducted within cognitive psychology, and a physicalist and a dualist would describe the same blank in the same place. Under the Map's rule that a tenet removing a defeater does not upgrade a void's evidence ([[positions/voids-as-evidence#^p-v2|P-V2]]), what follows is a reading dualism offers of this void, not support the void gives to dualism.

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]** faces an objection this void answers rather than raises: if consciousness causes things, why can it not make itself believe? The literature's reply is that the limit is conceptual, a consequence of belief's answerability to truth, and says nothing about causal impotence. Hieronymi's evaluative control also describes what conscious influence on one's own mental life would look like from inside, responsiveness to reasons rather than manipulation of objects, which fits the Map's picture of an influence that would not feel like pushing. Evaluative control is fully compatible with physicalism; its role here is to remove an objection, and it supplies no evidence for interactionism.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]** connects through the Cartesian model. "I understand, then I decide whether to accept" is the simple, introspectively obvious architecture; on both sides of the Spinozan dispute the real process is more complex than it seems from inside. Simplicity as judged by introspection is a poor guide to the architecture of assent, the tenet's claim applied to a single event.

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]** connects indirectly, through the transparency face. Belief shows up in experience as world and never as state, the structure [[self-opacity]] finds in the subject that cannot be its own object. The Map reads this as one more instance of agency without observational access, catalogued in the [[agency-void]] and [[decision-void]]; the pattern is a datum the framework must explain rather than a confirmation of it. Neither [[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]] nor [[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]] bears on the void; placing conscious influence at the assent event would be speculation the Map does not make.
