Stereopsis is the vivid impression of solid form, and of space between things, that most people get from two eyes. The Unfinishable Map gives the name **stereopsis void** to a gap that runs across it in both directions. Susan Barry, a neuroscientist who was stereoblind from infancy, studied the physiology of binocular vision as a student and reports that she could not picture what she lacked until she gained stereopsis at 48. People who have always had it, for their part, disagree about what closing one eye does to the scene, and no agreed method says who is right.

The void has three faces. The [[#forward-face|forward face]] (explained below): descriptive and theoretical knowledge did not give a stereoblind scientist the quality in advance. The [[#backward-face|backward face]]: binocular observers cannot settle, by closing an eye, what one-eyed vision is like. The [[#definition-face|definition face]]: on one theory the quality comes apart from binocular depth information, so what the stereoblind lack depends partly on how "stereopsis" is defined.

Barry's case is a close real-life relative of Mary in the [[knowledge-argument|knowledge argument]], and it illustrates the intuition behind that argument without deciding it. The void is neutral between dualism and physicalism, because both predict what she reports. The evidence from surprised experts is two people, both reporting on themselves.

## The Forward Face: Knowing About It Did Not Supply It {#forward-face}

In the published excerpt of *Fixing My Gaze* (2009), Barry says she was twenty when a college neurobiology lecture suggested she might be stereoblind. She could judge depth by occlusion, relative size and perspective, and her summary is: "I knew the world was in 3D." Of the other way of seeing depth that her professor described she writes: "I couldn’t imagine what he was talking about." She then spent the semester on the visual-cortex literature.

The absence had left no mark. Krulwich (2006) reports that she "never imagined that she saw differently from other kids", and that she later told Oliver Sacks she did not think she was missing very much. Sacks's reply, as Krulwich gives it, was a question: "Do you think you can imagine what it's like to see the world with two eyes?" This is the pattern the Map calls [[inventory-blindness|inventory blindness]], in which a missing capacity produces no felt gap.

In her late forties Barry undertook optometric vision therapy and, in her words to Lehrer (2009), "to my astonishment, began to see in 3D." She describes the change: "For the first time, I could see the volumes of space between different tree branches".

A second vision scientist reports a similar surprise. The Map has not read Bruce Bridgeman's own paper (Bridgeman 2014) and gives his case as two others report it. Barry (2015) writes that Bridgeman, wall-eyed since childhood, paid for 3D glasses at a 2012 screening of *Hugo* and "assumed they would be wasted on him", so that "it came as a complete shock when he immediately saw the movie in 3D". Levi, Knill and Bavelier (2015) cite the paper as "a vivid description of spontaneously recovering stereoscopic depth perception".

The surprise of experts is harder to attribute to ignorance of the theory than a layperson's would be. The evidence is still thin: two self-reports, largely retrospective, and Levi and colleagues note "that neither Barry nor Bridgeman were amblyopic", so the cases may not generalise. Barry's knowledge was also far short of the complete physical knowledge that Mary is stipulated to have. Her case shows that a working scientist's knowledge of the subject did not confer the quality. It leaves open whether complete physical knowledge would.

The comparison with Mary belongs to Maria Popova (2024), who writes that Barry had "been living in Mary’s Room". The Map's research did not find Barry or Sacks making it.

**Which kind of void.** From the stereoblind position this face behaves as unexplorable by imagination, testimony and study, on the evidence of these two cases. Acquisition can cross it, which distinguishes it from bat sonar, but crossing does not map it. Once across, the person can no longer inspect the earlier state, and inherits the backward face instead. The [[transformative-experience-void|transformative experience void]] has the same two sealed stages.

## The Backward Face: What One Eye Shows {#backward-face}

Barry turns the question on the normally sighted. "Many people tell me that the world looks about the same to them whether they look with one eye or with two," she tells Lehrer, and she offers an explanation: "What they don't realize is that their brain is using a lifetime of past visual experiences to fill in the missing stereo information." That is her hypothesis; she cites no measurement for it.

Published reports conflict. Schwitzgebel (2011) quotes Sacks's *The Mind's Eye* (2010) as holding that "The only way to actually perceive depth -- to see it rather than judge it -- is with binocular stereoscopy", and, of Sacks's own later loss of sight in one eye: "With one eye occluded, I have no sensation of distance or depth whatever". Schwitzgebel's own experience is different: "Maybe depth is a little richer and more striking binocularly than monocularly -- but the difference is nothing so radical as the difference between 2-D and 3-D." Most people he interviewed agree with him, he says, and some side with Sacks.

He names the camps "PuffOuters" and "StaySamers" and asks: "Is the difference between PuffOuters and StaySamers a real difference in experience?" The alternative is "a difference in report only". A commenter signing "djc", who says he wrote *The Conscious Mind* (so, on the comment's own text, David Chalmers), thinks both extremes are wrong: "I'm inclined to say that anyone who claims their monocular experience is 2D is misreporting it", and likewise anyone who claims no difference.

Schwitzgebel proposes a test. If the difference is real, PuffOuters should report larger depth effects in stereoscopic pictures and should better detect subtle ones, which would allow catch trials to check the reports. "Yes, this is totally do-able." The test is proposed; the Map's research found no record of its being performed, and one search is not proof that none exists.

**Which kind of void.** This face is occluded in the sense the [[three-kinds-of-void|three kinds of void]] gives for attention disorders: the instrument of inquiry is formed by the thing inquired into. If Barry is right, a visual system with a lifetime of stereopsis does not become a stereoblind one when an eye closes. No claim is made that the opacity was selected for. The face also has an unexplored margin, since Schwitzgebel's test could shrink it to an ordinary finding about individual differences.

## The Definition Face: A Quality and Its Information {#definition-face}

Dhanraj Vishwanath (2014) separates two things the word runs together. Depth can be perceived with one eye or in a picture, he writes, but viewing a real scene with both eyes "is qualitatively different: there is a vivid impression of tangible solid form and immersive negative space." The usual explanation by binocular disparity "is challenged by the observation that the impression of stereopsis can be induced in single pictures under monocular viewing", for instance when a picture is viewed with one eye through an aperture. His proposal is functional: the impression is linked to "the precision with which egocentrically scaled depth (absolute depth) is derived", which could guide the planning of action. This is one theory, and the Map has not assessed later discussion of it.

The consequence for the void is partly a matter of definition. If stereopsis means "the ability to perceive depth from binocular disparity", as the abstract of Chopin, Bavelier and Levi (2019) has it, then the stereoblind lack it by definition and no experiential question is touched. If it means Vishwanath's impression, then whether a stereoblind person could have a version of it through an aperture is an empirical question, and the Map's research found no study of it. Whether Barry would have recognised it before her therapy the sources do not say. That residue is unexplored.

The same abstract, which is all the Map read of that paper, puts prevalence at 7% among adults under 60 and adds that "stereopsis is not a unitary construct".

## Sibling Voids {#siblings}

The [[palette-extension-void|palette extension void]] asks whether an added sensory channel can be certified as new. Here those who cross are in no doubt, and the uncertainty sits with the people who never lacked the quality. The [[acquaintance-void|acquaintance void]] covers knowledge by acquaintance in general; this is an instance where the threshold is sometimes crossed in adult life. The [[imagery-void|imagery void]] shares the backward face's problem of reports that cannot be compared. The [[molyneux-void|Molyneux void]] is likewise neutral on dualism.

## Relation to Site Perspective {#site-perspective}

**[[tenets#^dualism|Dualism]]** gains no support. Barry's report illustrates the intuition behind the knowledge argument, and every standard physicalist reply applies without strain. On the ability reading she gained a capacity. On the [[phenomenal-concepts-strategy|phenomenal concepts strategy]] she gained a new concept of a state she could already describe. On a plain neuroscientific reading she lacked a brain state, and nobody expects reading about a brain state to produce it. If Vishwanath is right, she also lacked information about how precisely distance is known. Dualist and physicalist predict the same report, and under the Map's calibration rule ([[positions/voids-as-evidence#^p-v2|P-V2]]) nothing flows back to the tenet.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Occam's Razor Has Limits]]** receives a small historical illustration. Barry (2015) says it had been thought that the binocular neurons needed for stereovision "must develop during a “critical period” in early childhood or were irretrievably lost", and Levi and colleagues report that amblyopic patients over seven "are often told that they will never be able to recover visual acuity or stereovision". A simple rule was applied clinically and its strong form met adult exceptions. The tenet is illustrated and no more.

The backward face bears on method. First-person reports about a basic feature of everyday vision conflict, and nobody can currently say who is right. That is a reason for caution wherever the Map leans on [[introspection|introspective]] report, its own arguments included.

**[[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|Minimal Quantum Interaction]]**, **[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]** and **[[tenets#^no-many-worlds|No Many Worlds]]** have no bearing.
