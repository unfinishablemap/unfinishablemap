## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`blindspot-void` L97 is the passage Chalmers would keep: a thermostat's inability to represent "I am off" is "a limit of its self-model, not a property of the fact", whereas "I am conscious" is different "because the judgment is itself an instance of the property". That is the explanatory gap restated as an asymmetry of access rather than an intuition, and the article immediately grades it: "The Map advances this as its interpretation and not as a consequence of the logic ... The gain for the tenet is modest and conditional." `cross-state-void` L95 runs the knowledge argument inside one life, "the cold self is a Mary who has seen red and cannot see it now", and then refuses to overclaim: "The void does not refute the deflationary reply; it shows that representing a state and being in it come apart, which every theory must accommodate." Chalmers would also value `categorical-perception-void` L92, where the Toscano dissociation is offered to both readings and "the evidence constrains both readings by fixing the structure each must explain; it establishes neither."

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp's interest is in `causal-impression-void` L107: if fundamental physics contains no push at all, then biased outcome selection "asks for a kind of influence that no causal picture displays anyway, which makes it less exotic than it looks". The article then adds the sentence Stapp's critics would want: "That is a point about strangeness, not support: the absence of a push in physics does not show that selection-style influence is present." The Russell (1913) floor at L69 is also the right frame for a selection-law reading of physics: functional dependencies with no causes in them leave room for a law that reads off something, without asserting that anything does.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Every article in the wing carries a first-person description of approaching its edge, labelled as the Map's own analysis and not as a finding. `blindspot-void` L91 is the finest: "Nothing slips away; the content stays fully graspable from outside. What fails is uptake, instantly and totally", and then "preface vertigo: the conviction that one is wrong somewhere, paired with the inability to locate where". `categorical-perception-void` L72: approaching the void "feels like nothing: a Japanese-speaking adult listening to *right* and *light* does not experience a blur where a distinction should be". `causal-impression-void` L91: the push "thins under scrutiny, as a repeated word thins into sound". `cross-state-void` L57: "I remember it being unbearable" reports a label carried forward, with nothing in the present that bears. Nagel's own contribution, the irreducibility of the first-person point of view, is made precise at `blindspot-void` L69: "the closure marks the location of a perspective rather than a weakness of the mind."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would recognise his own picture of experience as a process of *taking up* in `blindspot-void` L91 (uptake fails) and in `taboo-void` L78, where filling the void changes the agent: "the mind that computes the exchange rate between a child's life and a sum of money no longer stands where the mind that refused stood. Illuminating the region changes the one doing the illuminating." He would also value the wing's refusal of crude substance talk. Not one of the six posits a mental substance; each locates its void in an operation (categorising, weighing, judging, attributing, representing) rather than in a thing. None of this praise cashes out as a tier-upgrade; no article in the wing claims that any organism or system is conscious, and the two AI sections that come closest (`blindspot-void` L75, `cross-state-void` L89) refuse the inference explicitly.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`taboo-void` is Kane's article. Frankfurt's Luther (L72) and Williams's moral incapacity (L74) present the refusal to weigh as "a deliberative conclusion rather than a report of impotence", endorsed at the second order, "so the incapacity is endorsed at the second order and expresses who the agent is". That is self-forming action described from the inside. Kane would also note `mirth-void` L94: consciousness "*causes* laughter but cannot *order* it and cannot *see why* it happened", and `causal-impression-void` L73–77, where the paralytic (impression without efficacy) and the acting AI (efficacy without impression) frame "whether conscious effort is the one place the void closes" as a question rather than an answer. `blindspot-void` L101 handles Sorensen's pre-decisional blindspots with the discipline Kane rarely gets from his allies: "that argument was not retrieved, and a compatibilist could accept it equally."

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

McGinn gets two proved closures. `blindspot-void` L69: "Most voids in the catalogue are inferred from failed attempts or empirical dissociations; this one is a theorem given minimal premises." `causal-impression-void` L69–71 stacks perception, introspection and physics and finds the relation absent from all three, then declines to call that closure: "That is consistent with a genuine limit, though not proof of one; long dispute can also be ordinary difficulty." `taboo-void` L86 is the most McGinn-like sentence in the wing because it is the most careful about *which* closure it has found: "description-relative and person-relative ... the content stays reachable under redescription, and for some minds was never blocked."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This persona's verdict is load-bearing for the wing, and it is favourable on every article.

*Tenet-as-evidence-upgrade praise-worthily not done.* `taboo-void` L110: "The inference from 'not a utility function' to 'not physical' is one the Map should not make, and this article does not." `mirth-void` L96: the stimulation case "is evidence for the occlusion, not for dualism, and the Map does not enlist it." `causal-impression-void` L101: parity "neutralises one *intuitive* objection to mental causation. It leaves the [[causal-closure]] and conservation arguments untouched." `categorical-perception-void` L94: the Winawer dual-task result "is a modulation of the operation by ongoing cognition, which a physicalist account covers; it is no evidence of non-physical influence and is not treated as such here."

*Framework-stage honesty.* No article claims a mechanism. `cross-state-void` L67 ("What the data do not decide is what the missing weight *is*") and `categorical-perception-void` L68 ("The evidence supports *behavioural* access on demand; *phenomenal* access remains open") both leave the phenomenal question open where the evidence does.

## Calibration Concerns

Two, both small and both length-neutral.

1. **`blindspot-void` L97 overreaches its own L39.** The sentence "No physical fact about a system is self-verifying for one observer and self-defeating for another" is contradicted by the article's doxastic layer: under physicalism about belief, "it is raining and she does not believe it" is a physical fact, open to everyone in the room and self-defeating for her (L43). L39 correctly says the doxastic and global layers are "neutral between dualism and physicalism". The distinctive claim at L97 is narrower and is stated in the next sentence: the phenomenal judgment is self-verifying *because it is an instance of the property*. Recasting the first sentence to "No physical fact about a system verifies itself by being an instance of what it reports" keeps the point and removes the false generalisation. Same length.

2. **`categorical-perception-void` L96 cites a family the apex does not name.** "It belongs in the framework-independent column of the [[voids]] index and the operation-void family of [[apex/taxonomy-of-voids]]" is half true: the index has such a column (L102–119) but does not list this void; the apex has no such family. This is an integration gap rather than an error in the article, and it is the subject of the High Priority opportunity below.
