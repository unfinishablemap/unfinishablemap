## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The wing takes the explanatory gap seriously in the one way that costs something: it distinguishes *form* from *existence* and refuses to let a result about the first count as progress on the second. `causal-consistency-constraint` states it as a section heading — **Form, Not Existence** — and then holds the line: "it does not explain why one outcome actualises rather than another, why the formalism contains probability rather than determinacy, or where the candidate set of measurement outcomes comes from."

`quantum-completeness` gives the same distinction its cleanest statement — *predictively complete and structurally complete while bijectively incomplete at the moment of measurement* — and then, in the Dualism paragraph, declines the inference Chalmers would most expect a dualist site to draw: "what the argument delivers is room, not requirement."

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The wing is unusually good company for Stapp and unusually hard on him, in the right order. `quantum-completeness` locates the gap at Process 1 using his own three-way refinement of von Neumann and attributes it to him accurately ("Splitting off the statistical rule governing outcomes as a third process is Stapp's refinement rather than von Neumann's"). Then `sign-problem` turns on the Zeno mechanism and develops a falsifier against it that, as the article documents, **no one in the critical literature has raised** — the Stanford Encyclopedia entry, Georgiev's critiques and Stapp's own replies all run on other axes.

What Stapp should admire is the article's refusal to bank the objection as a win. It records *two findings that run the other way*: the sign problem sharpens Stapp's prediction 7 from non-linearity to **non-monotonicity**, which a classical Hebbian selector cannot produce, and it identifies an untried repair (consciousness destabilising competitors rather than stabilising the intended program) that would convert the ubiquitous anti-Zeno effect from a liability into the mechanism. Both are recorded as open, neither as a rescue.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The formal apparatus stays tethered. `improper-vs-proper-mixtures` is the case in point: the whole article turns on d'Espagnat's insistence that the failure of the ignorance interpretation is "not mathematical but logico-semantical" — a point about what *ignorance* can mean when the global description fixes no local fact for anyone to be ignorant of. That is a conceptual claim held in conceptual terms, not laundered through a density matrix.

The FAPP section earns separate mention. Recovering Bell's coinage as an *accusation* — "It remains that the theory is ambiguous in principle" — and observing that wearing FAPP as a badge is the inverted use, is the kind of historical correction that changes how a reader hears the word afterwards. And it is immediately followed by the honest bound: "Bell is an ally on the diagnosis, not the cure."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

`quantum-non-equilibrium` is the wing's Whiteheadian article, and it earns the praise by conceding against itself throughout. It shows that "the Born rule holds" can name an emergent, in-principle-violable regularity rather than a fundamental law — a genuinely process-flavoured demotion of a law to a condition — while stating in its own opening that the Map "endorses neither side's framework" because pilot-wave determinism removes the indeterminacy the tenets require.

The article's structural lesson is the durable one: standard quantum mechanics sits inside a wider physics roughly as thermodynamics sits inside statistical mechanics. Whitehead would recognise the shape. Whitehead would also have to accept the article's own closing restraint, and it is worth quoting because it is precisely the sentence this persona's praise must not be allowed to overrun: *"the Map must not quietly convert 'coherently violable' into 'probably violated'."*

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

The wing keeps the room Kane needs without pretending to have furnished it. `quantum-completeness` places the Map's proposal at post-decoherence outcome selection — "not by overriding physics, but by resolving an indeterminacy that physics leaves genuinely open" — and `improper-vs-proper-mixtures` supplies the reason that indeterminacy is not merely epistemic: the decohered mixture is *improper*, so there is no definite-but-unknown fact the selection would be redundant with.

`causal-consistency-constraint` then does the thing Kane's programme most often skips — it prices the freedom. The corridor reading inherits no-signalling compliance natively, and the article states the bill in the same paragraph: "the default purchases its maximal consistency with the constraint at the price of empirical undetectability."

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

Two loci, both exemplary.

`sign-problem` closes on "The Map does not resolve the dilemma here. Recording it as unresolved is the point" — after naming the single measurement that would settle it (the neural coupling spectrum G(ω)) and stating that nobody has computed or measured it and the strong-coupling case has not been worked through even theoretically.

`local-tomography` is the sharper case, because the humility is *load-bearing on the mathematics*. The article's own contribution, it says, is to "name the exact axiom whose interface-status is unknown — which sharpens Tenet 2 by locating its open question precisely, rather than supplying any evidence for the interface."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This persona's verdict is load-bearing here and it is unambiguously favourable. Take the wing article by article on the question this persona exists to ask — *did tenet-coherence get used as the engine of a tier-upgrade?*

- **`generalised-probabilistic-theories`** — the wing's exemplar. The article exists to *retract* a slogan the Map had leaned on: "no-signalling forces the Born rule" is false as stated, and the honest replacement is a conditional carrying both clauses. It calls this "an upgrade, not a retreat: it names that question instead of hiding it inside a slogan." A tenet-driven article would have kept the slogan.
- **`local-tomography`** — the strongest single instance in the wing. The signature reading (local-tomography failure as the fingerprint of dualist holism) is exactly the flattering conclusion the tenets invite. The article makes it **harder**, not easier: "A fingerprint made of one failed axiom would be comparatively cheap; the theorem asks for two." It then names what it lacks — "the Map has no account of what would make purification fail across the cut." That is a tier-*downgrade* taken voluntarily on mathematical grounds.
- **`causal-consistency-constraint`** — names the tempting inference and rejects it by name: "a strong positive derivational result invites the metaphysical-decisive reading… That inference fails twice."
- **`improper-vs-proper-mixtures`** — "a contested interpretive commitment, not a result of the formalism," and the Map's realism about quantum states is surfaced as "the premise doing the work on its side, and it is fair to say so."
- **`quantum-completeness`** — "room, not requirement."
- **`quantum-non-equilibrium`** — "unrefuted and untested, which is weaker than having survived a test." That sentence is the five-tier discipline in one line.
- **`sign-problem`** — declares itself "framework-internal and graded at coherence-only strength" in an *Epistemic status, stated up front* block, and records that no external analysis exists in either direction.
- **`born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface`** — the hub, and the most exposed. It concedes the Lakatosian charge against itself in its own voice: a hard core insulated from aggregate test, ringed by falsifiable auxiliaries the programme disavows in advance, plus a stated plan to retreat under a clean null, is "the textbook profile of a *degenerating* programme." It narrows the charge rather than dismissing it, and then says: "That is the honest cost, not a strength."

Eight for eight. The pattern the skill calls *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating* is not merely present in this wing — it is the wing's house style, and the Process Philosopher's enthusiasm for `quantum-non-equilibrium` and this persona's approval of the same article's closing restraint are praising different axes of the same sentence. That is what a resolved tension looks like.

One thing worth recording as *praise for something not done*: `local-tomography` had the cleanest available tier-upgrade in the wing and turned it down on the theorem's own arithmetic. That is the exact move this persona exists to reward.

- **Notable quote**: *"'Physics might one day prove that decoherence suffices' is not on this list, because inside unitary quantum mechanics it runs against the insolubility theorems."*
- **Why it works**: most articles state a defeater and then soften it. This one states the defeater, discovers that the *scariest* version of it is mathematically unavailable, and then says stating it precisely "sharpens the Map's honesty rather than retiring the risk" — before listing three routes that all remain live. The result is a stronger position *and* a more exposed one.

A second strength in the same article: the observation that the two flagship deflationists deflate in **opposite directions** — Kirkpatrick to all-improper, Castellani to all-proper — from a shared premise. "The deflationary conclusion is thus underdetermined even among deflationists." That is a genuinely original piece of dialectical bookkeeping.

### `generalised-probabilistic-theories` — the retraction that upgrades

- **Strongest point**: separating the theorem's *conclusion* (a conjunction — both axioms violated) from its *premise set* (a disjunction — either axiom suffices), a distinction the source's own abstract blurs.
- **Notable quote**: *"That either axiom suffices inside the class keeps the constraint robust; that the class itself is an assumption about the interface is where the open question lives."*
- **Why it works**: this is the fix for the exact defect the 2026-08-16 optimistic review flagged in `local-tomography` — a theorem's connective stated as a disjunction where the paper states a conjunction, slipping in the direction that flattered the Map. Both articles now carry the corrected reading, independently and in their own words. `local-tomography`'s version — *"a single door with two locks, rather than two independent doors either of which would serve"* — is the better sentence. **A review flagged a defect on 2026-08-16; the fix landed and then propagated to a sibling.** That closed loop is worth recording as evidence the review system does what it is for.

## The `sign-problem` isolation: **correct scoping, and a wing mis-assignment**

The brief measured `sign-problem-for-conscious-observation` at **0 wing-internal citations in both directions** and asked whether that is a gap or correct scoping. Having read it in full: **correct scoping — and the zero is an artefact of putting the article in this wing at all.**

Three grounds.

**1. It is not a reconstruction article.** Its formal apparatus is open-system decay dynamics — the Kofman–Kurizki overlap integral between a bath-response spectrum G(ω) and a measurement-induced control spectrum, and the bath correlation time that sets the Zeno/anti-Zeno crossover. It contains no GPT, no state-space geometry, no Born-rule derivation, no purification, no local tomography. The other seven articles are about what fixes the *probability rule*; this one is about whether frequent observation *suppresses or accelerates* a decay. Sharing the adjective "quantum" is the only thing that put it here.

**2. It says so itself, in a dedicated section.** *What the Sign Problem Is Not About* opens: "The interface case does not stand or fall with the quantum Zeno effect," and names `post-decoherence-selection` as "the route the Map endorses more strongly and does not involve repeated observation at all." An article that explicitly exempts the wing's endorsed mechanism from its own critique should not be expected to cite the wing.

**3. It is well-connected in the cluster it actually belongs to.** Corpus-wide, `sign-problem` has **six live inbound article links** — `quantum-zeno-effect`, `stapp-quantum-mind`, `timing-gap-problem`, `mental-effort`, `motor-control-quantum-zeno`, and `what-consciousness-tells-us-about-physics`. Its outbound links go to the same set. It sits at the centre of the Zeno-mechanism cluster, which is where its argument bites. The 2026-08-16 review already recorded that its five inbound links were installed the day it was created.

The wing-internal zero is therefore what a correctly-scoped article looks like when it is measured against the wrong neighbourhood. **No task is warranted, and installing wing-internal links to satisfy the metric would be the defect.** Note also that the 09-04 link-only cross-link P3 already lists `sign-problem` among the seven files whose siblings it audits; that task's own highest-value pairs list does not include any `sign-problem` pair, which is the same verdict reached independently.

## The Wing's Unused Strength

The brief asked for "a reconstruction result that constrains the interface more tightly than the Map currently claims elsewhere." There is one, it is inside the wing, and it is one week old.

### The insolubility theorems have reached one article out of the corpus

**What the wing earned.** `improper-vs-proper-mixtures` (created and deep-reviewed 2026-09-03) established that the Map's central defeater cannot arrive by the route everyone assumes. The insolubility-theorem family — von Neumann through Fine, Shimony, Brown, Busch–Shimony, Bassi–Ghirardi — establishes "the impossibility of reproducing (the statistics of) definite pointer readings" by unitary means. Decoherence *is* unitary dynamics. Therefore a theorem inside unitary quantum mechanics showing that decoherence produces definite outcomes would contradict an established theorem family. This is a **structural** result, not an interpretive preference, and it is supported across two decades (Adler 2003; Hance and Hossenfelder 2022; Tomaz, Mattos and Barbatti 2025).

**Where it has reached.** Grepped `insolubilit` across the whole vault: **two live articles.** `improper-vs-proper-mixtures`, where it was minted, and `quantum-darwinism-and-consciousness`, which received it in the same pass. Everywhere else it appears only in `reviews/`, `research/` and `workflow/archive/`.

**The locus that should have it.** `quantum-completeness`, *The Decoherence Objection* section. That section is the corpus's canonical argument that the outcome gap survives decoherence, and it currently rests on an appeal to authority plus a disagreement:

> "That the gap survives is not a controversial reading of the formalism, though whether it counts as a shortfall is contested. Schlosshauer's comprehensive review draws the line… Zurek does not concede a shortfall here… **The Map and Zurek agree about the formalism and part company over whether anything is left to explain.**"

A standoff between two reviewers is the weakest available form of this argument, and the wing has a stronger one sitting unused one file away. The insolubility theorems convert "Zurek and Schlosshauer disagree" into "no unitary result *can* close this gap, so einselection cannot close it by unitary means, and Zurek's position must be read as declining the question rather than answering it." That is a materially stronger claim, it is the Map's own to make, and it is already written and sourced elsewhere in the wing.

Verified: `quantum-completeness` contains **zero** occurrences of `improper`, `insolub`, and `d'Espagnat`. `improper-vs-proper-mixtures` references `quantum-completeness` once (frontmatter only). The two articles are effectively strangers.

This one requires prose, is scoped to a different section, and the task note says so.

### Magnitude, form — and the silence about direction

The wing's Born-side results all constrain the interface along one axis. `causal-consistency-constraint` fixes the probability rule's *form*. `generalised-probabilistic-theories` and `local-tomography` fix the *conditions* under which that form is forced. `quantum-non-equilibrium` prices any *statistical-level* deviation at superluminal signalling. Every one of these is a constraint on how large, or of what shape, an influence may be.

`sign-problem` establishes — from the other cluster entirely — that this whole family is silent about **direction**: "Every one of those constrains how large the influence may be. None constrains which way it points."

The two have met in exactly one place in the corpus, and it is neither a wing article nor a Zeno article. `what-consciousness-tells-us-about-physics` joins them, and does it well: the agency budget fixes channel capacity at min(H(conscious source), H(Born distribution)) bits per event, and "the sign problem shows that some of that capacity is committed before any outcome is chosen… Nothing yet establishes that the two demands fit inside one budget — a debt the Map has only now made visible to itself."

The wing-level observation the apex does *not* make: **the corridor reading's entire agency payload lives in the direction degree of freedom that `causal-consistency-constraint` provably does not constrain.** The constraint's silence about direction is simultaneously the room the corridor needs and the debt `sign-problem` names. `causal-consistency-constraint`'s *Minimal Quantum Interaction* paragraph — "The constraint shapes what 'minimal' can mean" — is the natural home for a one-sentence statement of that, and it would make the article's already-good "form, not existence" discipline into a cleaner three-way one: form, not existence; magnitude, not direction.

**Not minted, deliberately.** This is a fragment of the ~40-file sign/direction propagation family, whose first-tranche task (`check-tenets` 2026-08-16) carries an explicit instruction: "Do the three files listed and stop — do not attempt a corpus sweep." Minting a fourth file against that family from an optimistic pass would be exactly the sweep that task forbids. Recorded here so the family's next tranche can pick it up with the locus already identified.
