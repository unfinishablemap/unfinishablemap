## Executive Summary

These three articles are the same argumentative move performed on three different materials — the constructed self (predictive self-binding), constructed memory (simulation theory), and constructed percept (chemosensory coding). In each case the Map *adopts the constructive mechanism whole* — REBUS precision-relaxation, Schacter–Addis episodic simulation, the combinatorial receptor code — and refuses only the eliminative gloss that construction *exhausts* the phenomenon, locating an irreducible residue at the explanatory gap: for-me-ness that survives ego dissolution, the autonoetic *this-happened-to-me* marker, the felt mintiness the activation pattern leaves unexplained. The calibration discipline is exemplary and uniform: "accommodation, not proof"; "illustration, not demonstration"; "empirically equivalent on altered-state data." This is the cluster where the Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist *converge* rather than conflict — all three lean on process-friendly, brain-side constructive theses (Deane's active-inference valence, Barwich's processual olfaction, Michaelian's generative memory) yet none is cashed out as a tier-upgrade. The dominant opportunity is not new content — topics sit at 321/320 and concepts at 320/320, both at cap — but a single conspicuous wiring gap: **the three articles share a load-bearing structural signature and do not cross-link at all**, and the constructive-processing hub (`predictive-processing-and-dualism`) that two of them already cite is missing from the third.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
Every article isolates the hard problem in its purest local form and refuses to let the mechanism disguise it. Predictive self-binding names "the hard-problem debt" explicitly: the formalism reaches self-*prioritisation* (faster processing, tighter binding, heavier weighting) but "does not explain why there is something it is like to be the system running that model." Chemosensory does the same with combinatorial coding — "combinatorial coding *explains the discrimination*… The residue is not a mechanism gap but the explanatory gap — why the pattern of activation is *like anything at all*." Simulation-theory routes it through the discrimination problem. Chalmers would applaud that in all three cases the gap is *earned* by first conceding the mechanism its full explanatory reach, not asserted over its head.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
The reconsolidation opening in simulation-theory is the Stapp-shaped moment: "the reconsolidation window, when a reactivated trace destabilises and the physical system is briefly underdetermined" is precisely the locus of physical indeterminacy where a minimal mental contribution could bias an outcome without violating dynamics — and the article correctly holds the interface reading *separate* from the empirical signature (deferring to `reconsolidation-as-selection-window`). Stapp would recognise the discipline of naming the underdetermined seam without over-claiming a mechanism at it.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
The cluster centres first-person experience without leaning on it naively. Predictive self-binding's "persistent minimal self" prong is Nagel-exact: the bare pre-reflective for-me-ness is the *subject-pole* any content is given to, distinguished from the narrative character that psychedelics dismantle. Chemosensory's Proust-phenomenon and constitutive-valence sections keep "what it is like" load-bearing exactly where smell resists the "dispassionate snapshot" model perception theory inherited from vision. Crucially, each article *declines* to make first-person report load-bearing where it would be self-undermining — predictive self-binding notes the "dismantled metacognitive instrumentation" makes phenomenological adjudication of dissolution fraught.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
This is a good-use cluster for the persona. All three articles reach for brain-side, *processual* rather than substance-based theses and treat them as defeater-removers: simulation-theory reframes memory as active "goal-directed selection among candidate reconstructions" rather than archival readout; chemosensory borrows Barwich's thesis that "the brain acts not only like a map but also as a measuring device"; predictive self-binding cites Deane's active-inference valence as "the most fundamental layer of experience." Each of these is process-philosophically congenial — experience as event and construction, not stored substance. Note (per the persona constraint) that none of the three cashes this resonance out as a tier-upgrade for anything: the process-friendly theses *remove defeaters* for the metaphysical possibility of an irreducible contribution, and are never presented as positive evidence that settles the case. See the Hardline Empiricist below, where the two personas converge.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
The bidirectional-interaction threads give Kane his opening. Simulation-theory's "the *setting* of that goal is where consciousness contributes directional input rather than passively reading out the strongest trace" and chemosensory's active-sampling reading (we *sniff* to sample, learn to taste structure "that was always arriving but not previously perceived") locate a place for genuine directional agency in construction. The articles resist over-selling it — each flags the interpretation as "consonant with," not "uniquely predicted by," bidirectional interaction, and concedes the epiphenomenalist can redescribe the same sampling as wholly neural.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)
Strong epistemic humility throughout. Predictive self-binding's central concession is McGinn-grade: on altered-state data *alone* the two accounts are "empirically equivalent," and the discriminating work "is done not by psychedelic data but by broader theoretical commitments." Simulation-theory registers the discrimination problem as "a live problem inside the simulationist literature, not merely an objection from outside it." Chemosensory repeats "this is accommodation, not proof" at every section boundary. None of the three pretends to more knowledge than it has.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)
This is the load-bearing verdict for this cluster, and it is strongly positive. All three articles are textbook cases of **tenet-as-evidence-upgrade being praise-worthily *not* done**. Each had the structural opportunity to slide its residue-claim up the evidential scale on tenet-coherence — predictive self-binding could have declared the minimal self's survival established because Tenet 4 needs a determinate indexical subject; simulation-theory could have upgraded autonoesis-as-irreducible because the discrimination problem is convenient; chemosensory could have promoted "qualia are non-physical" from illustration to demonstration on the strength of the carvone case. **All three explicitly decline.** Predictive self-binding registers its minimal-self prediction as "defeasible" and notes the indexical commitment "is motivated on independent grounds, not read off the psychedelic data" — the honest *"tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating"* pattern named in situ. Chemosensory's whole spine is "the physicalist has a credible account of each… and those accounts are taken seriously here and, where relevant, adopted," reserving only the explanatory-gap residue. Simulation-theory frames its verdict as "adopting a mechanism while refusing its eliminative gloss" and grounds the Occam move properly ("parsimony favours the simpler theory only where the rivals have equal explanatory power"), not on tenet-preference. The five-tier scale is honoured implicitly by keeping the "mechanism adopted" register cleanly separate from the "residue claimed" register in every article. This is the counterweight persona finding nothing to flag and much to praise — and, notably, converging with the Process Philosopher rather than opposing him: the process-friendly theses are used to *remove defeaters*, never to upgrade status, which is exactly the discipline that keeps the two personas in agreement.

## Content Strengths

### predictive-self-binding-and-the-naturalist-challenge.md
- **Strongest point**: The symmetric-debt honesty. The article grants that filter theory "inherits a parallel debt… neither account discharges the hard problem; both inherit it" — then turns the symmetry into the actual argument (Tenet 5: parsimony can't break a tie where conceptual tools are inadequate). It wins by refusing to pretend it wins.
- **Notable quote**: "The self-binding account does not *win* on the hard problem; it postpones it while presenting the postponement as a solution."
- **Why it works**: It presents the strongest naturalist rival "at full strength" (its own words) before replying, and concedes empirical equivalence on the shared data — the reader trusts every subsequent move because the concessions came first.

### simulation-theory-of-memory.md
- **Strongest point**: Using the opponent's *internal* dissent as the entry point. The discrimination problem (Rivadulla-Duró 2024) is "a live problem inside the simulationist literature," not an external dualist objection — so the residue the Map claims is one simulationism itself has flagged and not absorbed.
- **Notable quote**: "The discrimination problem the simulationist literature raises against itself is, on the Map's reading, the signature of that missing contribution."
- **Why it works**: The shortest article in the cluster (~1600w) and the most economical — it shares STM's anti-archival deflation "wholesale" and disagrees on exactly one thing (the autonoetic residue), so the disagreement is surgically located rather than diffuse.

### chemosensory-consciousness-and-the-interface.md
- **Strongest point**: Choosing the modality that removes the physicalist's most natural foothold. Where colour and pitch offer a tidy stimulus-space geometry an identity theorist can anchor to, odour space is "high-dimensional, categorical, and best modelled as non-Euclidean" — so "the chemical senses remove the convenience that makes identity theory feel natural in the visual case."
- **Notable quote**: "There is no place where the smell is, no axis along which it sits."
- **Why it works**: It converts a long-standing loose end — the sommelier and "Barolo" examples that recur across the corpus were "always chemosensory" — into this article's payoff, giving those examples "their modality home."
