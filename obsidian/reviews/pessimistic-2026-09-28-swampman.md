## Executive Summary

The weaknesses are structural. (1) The article's central verdict column, "narrow content: yes", rests on a notion its own sibling page declines: `content-externalism` L46 says the Map's internalist half "rests on phenomenal character, not on a constructed narrow-content notion" and draws a deliberately narrower perimeter than the one this page uses. (2) The article hands the discrimination against Papineau 2001 to the zombie, but the Map's zombie and metaproblem pages hold zombies nomologically impossible under interactionism, and the article itself grants Papineau's point that merely possible cases do not bear on an actual-world reduction, so the fallback discriminator is a case the Map deems impossible and its opponent deems irrelevant. (3) The article treats "is Swampman conscious?" as merely unverifiable, omitting the published naturalist argument that he is *not*: Dretske's "Absent Qualia" in the same 1996 *Mind & Language* forum, whose phenomenal externalism the Map's hybrid page already names as its opponent. Of the eight Swampman pieces in that forum the article cites two.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would say the article elevates a folk intuition ("surely a creature indistinguishable from you thinks") into a metaphysical datum and then builds a "split verdict" on it. Millikan's own section 1 asks whether the whole dispute, including "whether to label the relevant aspects of his inner arrangements 'narrow contents'", is merely verbal, and argues it is not; the article never engages that argument, it simply reports her conclusion. Worse for the Map, Millikan's phrase names precisely the Map's move: labelling Swampman's inner arrangements "narrow content". Churchland would add that "the cognitive phenomenology of meaning something" (L54) is folk psychology of exactly the kind teleosemantics was built to replace, and that the article's only reply to the naturalist is that PIT "carries" the opposite commitment (L78), which concedes the point.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett wrote for the same 1996 forum ("Cow-sharks, Magnets, and Swampman", *Mind & Language* 11(1): 76–77) that Swampman is an illicit intuition pump, and the article does not mention it. Dennett would press that the table at L62–66 assigns Swampman "determinate narrow content" on the strength of a stipulation ("if he is conscious") the article admits no test can discharge, so the Map's row differs from Papineau's 2001 row only in a word the Map cannot cash. He would also note that Papineau's non-rigid route, which the article concedes "reaches a verdict close to the Map's without any appeal to consciousness", is the deflationary result: content goes with role, and the Map's addition of phenomenal grounding does no discriminating work here by the article's own admission (L68, L86).

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark would ask what, concretely, the lightning fails to copy (L76: "something the lightning does not copy"). On the Map's own tenets the interface acts at quantum indeterminacies in a living brain; a lightning-made brain has the same indeterminacies. The Map's register in fact answers him: P-AC2 (`positions/ai-consciousness-scope`) concedes that once consciousness "acts through *whatever* live indeterminacy arises", no-cloning "on its own no longer disqualifies" a copy, so on the Map's weakened reading a molecular duplicate with a live brain *should* be conscious, and the residual question is the subject–brain pairing law P-SC1 (`positions/subject-census`) records as an unpaid debt. The article cites none of this. Its "the Map does not know which" is under-informed by the Map's own positions, and the placeholder "something the lightning does not copy" reads, to a physicist, as a haecceity dressed as physics.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch would observe that the article leans on "physical implausibility" (L86) borrowed from Millikan, whose own paper concedes the time-reversal point that nothing makes a Swampman formation physically impossible, only improbable. Under Tenet 4 the Map holds one world, so improbable events are merely rare, not distributed across branches; the Map cannot use improbability as a proxy for impossibility without saying so. Minor, but the article's third "honest limitation" runs the two together.

### The Empiricist

Popper would say Dretske's account has more empirical content than the Map's: Dretske delivers a determinate verdict (no history, no representational content, no qualia) that could in principle be wrong, while the Map delivers a conditional whose antecedent it declares unverifiable. The one falsifiable-looking consequence the article offers, behavioural divergence of a non-conscious Swampman under Tenet 3 (L84), is immediately flagged as speculation and not linked to the tenet's registered standing (`tenets#^tenet-3-standing`), which says such claims "inherit that debt rather than discharge it". The article should link that anchor, since the paragraph is exactly the class of claim the standing note governs.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would say the interesting Map-internal question is not "is the replica conscious?" but "which subject?", and the article never poses it. Under the Map's closed individualism (P-I1; P-AC3 "conscious copies are morally additive") the replica is a numerically distinct subject from Davidson; under the deflationary route the tenets register at `tenets#^no-many-worlds` the question dissolves. Either way the article's framing, "Swampman is conscious *if* consciousness attaches to brains of this kind by psychophysical law", quietly assumes a subject waiting to be paired, which is the substance-leaning sub-reading the Dualism tenet says is downstream, not tenet-level. The article should say which sub-reading generates "he may not be".

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: "Narrow content" contradicts the perimeter the sibling hybrid page deliberately draws
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/swampman.md`
- **Location**: L26 ("narrow, phenomenally constituted intentionality"), L56 ("determinate narrow content", "phenomenal intentionality ... internally constituted (developed at [[content-externalism]])"), table L62–66 ("Narrow content? Yes, if conscious")
- **Problem**: `concepts/content-externalism.md` L20 states the hybrid as "externalist about wide *intentional* content (aboutness, reference) while internalist about *phenomenal* content (felt character)", and L46 says the Map "can concede wide intentional content and hold only that phenomenal character is internally constituted. That is a more defensible perimeter", explicitly to sidestep Yli-Vakkuri and Hawthorne (2018) on constructed narrow content: "the Map's internalist claim rests on phenomenal character, not on a constructed narrow-content notion". The Swampman page attributes to that same page a stronger view (narrow phenomenal *intentionality*, determinate narrow *content*) that it does not develop and in one sentence declines. `concepts/intentionality.md` also never says PIT content is narrow; the narrowness claim is imported from Horgan, Tienson and Graham 2004, not from the Map.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Either (a) re-express the verdict in the vocabulary the hybrid page licenses (Swampman's states have internally constituted phenomenal character, and whatever aboutness the Map's phenomenal-constitution stance grants that character, with wide reference open) and relabel the table column, or (b) supply the bridging argument from "phenomenal character is internal" to "determinate narrow content", and say why Yli-Vakkuri and Hawthorne's critique does not bite. Do not leave the two pages asserting different perimeters.

### Issue 2: The zombie fallback is a case the Map holds impossible and Papineau holds irrelevant
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/swampman.md`
- **Location**: L68 ("The discriminating case for that further question is the zombie"), L86 ("the zombie case does that work")
- **Problem**: `concepts/philosophical-zombies.md` L163–167 and `topics/metaproblem-of-consciousness-under-dualism.md` L88 hold that under Bidirectional Interaction a physical duplicate cannot produce identical reports without consciousness, so the zombie is nomologically impossible on the Map. And L86 of this article accepts Papineau's point that "a merely possible case bears on a conceptual claim about content in a way it does not bear on a claim of actual-world reduction". So the discrimination against Papineau 2001 is handed to a case that (i) the Map says cannot exist and (ii) Papineau's actual-world reduction says cannot count. The article's L84 even half-notices this by predicting behavioural divergence for a non-conscious Swampman, which makes Swampman-minus-consciousness a zombie the Map has already ruled out.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: State plainly that no duplicate case discriminates the Map from a non-rigid actual-world teleosemanticist; the disagreement is over the *ground* of content, and the Map's live replies are elsewhere: the two-normativities argument at `concepts/teleosemantics.md` L86, and Kim's (2020) argument that Swampman intuitions challenge the conceptual foundations (success semantics, etiological function) on which the reduction's explanatory power rests, which is an in-framework argument the article cites but does not use. Cross-link the zombie page's Interactionist Escape section rather than treating the zombie as an available discriminator.

### Millikan: the "narrow content" dispute is verbal
- **Current content says**: The Map's verdict is "the direct opposite of the Millikan verdict, on the same case" (L54).
- **A critic would argue**: Millikan's section 1 asks whether calling Swampman's inner arrangements "narrow contents" is a substantive claim at all, and argues the dispute is real only because content is a historical kind. The Map has not shown its "narrow content" label is more than relabelling.
- **Suggested response**: Engage her verbal-dispute section directly; the Map's substantive claim is that phenomenal character is present-tense and content-conferring, which is a claim about what content *is*, not a label.

### Papineau: no duplicate case touches an actual-world reduction
