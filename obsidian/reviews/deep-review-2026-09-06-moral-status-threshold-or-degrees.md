## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. Dropped qualifier in the internal-quote channel — the deciding-fact check.** The article read: the degrees-of-consciousness page *"opens "Consciousness is not binary" and invokes Tenet 5 against treating consciousness as on or off."* That page's lead was reordered earlier the same day and now opens **"Consciousness is not binary *in what it contains*"** — and the same sentence explicitly defers presence to *this* article ("whether a subject is present at all is a further question, left open here, on which the moral-status threshold rests"). (−9 words.)

**2. Register-fidelity: a term of art populated with the wrong roster.** The lead asserted *"The rivals of record are Kagan's limited hierarchy, DeGrazia's sliding scale, and McMahan's two tiers."* "Rival of record" is a **defined register term** (`positions/methodology-and-calibration` L47: *"a named, published author, current to the live debate"*), and that definition's own review clause covers *"an article arguing one"* — so this check is in contract. The register's actual P-MS2 roster is **Kagan 2019, DeGrazia 2008, Sebo 2025**, with Sebo expressly *"read as a credence over a threshold rather than a rival on this point."* **McMahan appears nowhere in the register** (grepped across `obsidian/positions/`). The article had swapped Sebo for McMahan while keeping the register's vocabulary. **Resolution**: split the claim — the register's gradualist rivals named as the register names them, McMahan's two tiers attributed to *this article*. Register-side question (should McMahan join P-MS2's roster?) deferred to the open P3, not decided here.

**3. Buchanan: a word he never uses (raw-text proven).** The article said a unitarian must defend the threshold *"by conceding a fuzzy lower boundary and calling it vague."* "Fuzzy" is Buchanan's own — verbatim, p. 361: *"Proponents of the respect-based view can admit that there may be a **fuzzy lower boundary** for this threshold."* But a full vagueness-vocabulary sweep of the normalised PDF (`vague`, `indeterminac`, `imprecis`, `borderline`, `sharp line`, `penumbr`, `sorites`, …) returns **zero** hits applied to the threshold; his gloss is *"difficult to judge."* His single "vague" is p. 352, about biological species. The label is in fact **Floris & Timmer's** — their abstract, verified verbatim: *"we reject common responses to this arbitrariness objection, such as that moral status thresholds are not arbitrary but **merely vague**."* The article had borrowed their word and pinned it on Buchanan. **Resolution**: Buchanan's actual move restored ("conceding a "fuzzy lower boundary" and falling back on uncontroversial cases"), and the vagueness label returned to Floris & Timmer, whose rejection of it the same clause already named.

**4. Vallentyne: a *reductio* presented as a thesis.** The article said *"The distributive form — Vallentyne (2005) … — says a unitarian who accepts a priority principle must favour the worse-off mouse."* Two errors. (i) Vallentyne's target is **egalitarianism**, not priority principles; prioritarianism appears in one narrow aside (leximin and strongly weighted total prioritarianism), after which he writes *"For simplicity, however, we shall focus on moderate egalitarianism."* (ii) More seriously, he **does not hold the conclusion** — he names it *"The Problematic Conclusion,"* assumes it wildly implausible, and the paper exists to block it. Citing him for the claim without saying so states his reductio as his view. **Resolution**: reordered to lead with Kagan (whose ch. 3 *does* press it, and whose "mice" is exact), Vallentyne marked as stating it in order to block it, and "priority" corrected to "egalitarian."

**5. Jaworska & Tannenbaum: a label the authors explicitly disown — in a source the article already cites.** The article called theirs a *"relational person-rearing account."* In SEP §5.6 — an entry **these same authors wrote**, and which the article cites as reference 10 — they write that the account grounds *"impartial rather than relationship-based"* reasons and that *"the feature grounding status is **not a relationship** but rather is vested in the individual."* SEP files the paper under "Incompletely Realized Sophisticated Cognitive Capacities," not "Special Relationships," and escaping the relational charge is the paper's point. **Resolution**: "relational" removed; "person-rearing account" kept, which is the standard shorthand Timmerman & Fischer themselves use.

**6. Korsgaard: a dropped hedge on a claim the Map uses as a ground, plus wording attributed to the wrong author.** The article read *"importance is a tethered property — to be important is to be important to someone — so there is **no** perspectiveless standpoint from which humans could be found to matter more than animals."* Two problems, both from the book's own full text.

(i) **Dropped qualifier.** Korsgaard hedges exactly here. Her section heading is *"Why Tethered Values and Superior Importance Are **(Almost)** Incompatible"*, and she writes *"I have not exactly been arguing that animals are just as important as people. I have been arguing that **the comparison is nearly incoherent**."* She deliberately keeps logical room for "absolute importance" read as importance-to-everyone (judging it minimally plausible, but not incoherent). The article had the flat version — and then **leaned on the flat version** two clauses later, adopting it "as the ground beneath the translation argument." A defeater Korsgaard leaves open was silently closed.

(ii) **Wording attributed to the wrong author.** "Tethered" *is* unambiguously Korsgaard's own term (29 occurrences; two section headings; an index entry) — but *"tethered **property**"* and *"to be important is to be important to someone"* are **Bernstein's** formulations from the review, not hers. Korsgaard writes "tethered values", "all importance is tethered", and *"nothing can be important without being important to someone."* The article was citing Bernstein, so this was defensible rather than false — but it read as though the phrasing were Korsgaard's.

**Resolution**: rewritten to Korsgaard's own phrasing throughout and with her hedge carried — "all importance is tethered — nothing can be important without being important to someone — so ranking humans above animals is *nearly* incoherent: there is no perspectiveless standpoint for the comparison." The perspectiveless claim stays flat, because *that* half **is** flat in both Korsgaard and Bernstein; only the ranking conclusion is hedged. Net **−2 words**, so the fix paid for itself. Bernstein 2019 and Korsgaard 2018 both remain cited inline — no orphan reference created.
