## Executive Summary

The article's phenomenological exhibits and its 09-02 engagement with active inference are strong. The weaknesses are seams, not missing arguments. (1) The lead and three later sentences state without qualification that protention is constitutive of every conscious moment. The article's own L113 concedes a datum (Frischhut's alert timelessness) that would falsify this if genuine. The sibling page [[non-temporal-consciousness]] already supplies the level distinction that reconciles the two, but this page never uses it. (2) L129 defines prospective memory as "deliberate future simulation". That definition belongs to episodic future thinking, and the linked concept page defines prospective memory correctly as "remembering to perform planned actions in the future". (3) The surprise section predates the 09-02 recalibration and still asserts as "revealed" what L93 now declares a stalemate. (4) The No Many Worlds paragraph says protention is structured around "*one* continuation". That contradicts the article's own picture of protention as an "open question" that, in deliberation, leans into several possibilities.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would note that the article's evidence for protention comes from four vignettes (melody, sentence, stairs, conversation), and that each has a complete account in predictive-coding and motor-control terms. Her bedrock challenge, that "protention" is a folk-phenomenological category due for replacement, is recorded in the 2026-02-10 stability notes and is not re-flagged here. One point is new. The article concedes at L73 that protention "operates below the threshold of reflective attention", yet the lead asserts it of "Every moment". If introspection cannot isolate protention, what licenses the universal claim? The eliminativist uses this gap to say the universal rests on theory (Husserl's transcendental argument), not on observation. The article never states that argument. See Issue 1.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would focus on L143: "Systems that merely process information would register unexpected inputs as statistically unlikely. But they wouldn't experience *surprise*". The word "merely" puts the absence of experience into the subject term, so the next sentence cannot get from this to "Surprise reveals that protention isn't just a forward-looking computation". The premise assumes the conclusion. Fifty lines earlier, the article itself withdrew the bare zombie move as question-begging against the beautiful-loop theory (L91) and declared the dispute a stalemate (L93: "Neither side refutes the other from inside its own commitments."). The surprise section therefore re-asserts what the article has already given up. See Issue 3.

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark would point to a timescale mismatch inside L155. Protention spans the specious present, which is hundreds of milliseconds and longer. "biasing which of several superposed neural configurations becomes actual" happens on decoherence timescales many orders of magnitude shorter. So "Protention would then be the phenomenological dimension of the minimal quantum interaction" needs a bridge from a sub-picosecond event to a felt orientation lasting seconds. The paragraph is conditional ("If … may operate"), and prior reviews ruled that a phenomenology article need not supply the mechanism. That ruling stands. No task is minted.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch's compatibility reply ("each branch experiences its own singular anticipation") is acknowledged in L157, and the stability notes rule it closed. His sharper point is internal. L103 says protention "carries no content from actual events" and "has the character of an open question rather than a fading answer". L117 says the deliberator leans into "each possibility". L157 then says experience "is structured around *one* continuation, not a branching tree". By the article's own account, deliberative protention *is* a branching tree of possibilities tried on. What is singular is the outcome protention orients toward, and the paragraph's final sentence already says exactly that ("oriented toward actualisation rather than proliferation"). The "one continuation" sentence overstates that point and contradicts the deliberation section. See Issue 4.

### The Empiricist
A Popperian would ask what result the Bidirectional Interaction paragraph (L153) rules out: "Protention's active, directional character is what experience would be expected to feel like if consciousness shapes physical outcomes." What would it feel like if consciousness did *not* shape outcomes? The paragraph offers no contrast case. It does concede "consonant with Tenet 3 rather than demonstrating it", and the 09-24 review accepted that calibration. Demarcation itself is bedrock and is not re-flagged. There is one residual seam. L119 still says the tenet "gains phenomenological support", which reads stronger than L153's "consonant". This is noted in the Language table only.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna's no-self challenge is acknowledged at L113 and is bedrock. He would push one step further, against the constitutive thesis rather than the subject. If meditators who attend closely find protentional structure ceasing while alertness persists (L113, via Frischhut), then protention is a conditioned, removable feature of mind. It is "empty" in exactly his sense, and certainly not "constitutive of how experience unfolds at all" (L69). The article admits the datum but leaves its own thesis unrevised. See Issue 1.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The universal constitutive claim contradicts the article's own concession and ignores the sibling page's level distinction
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/phenomenology-of-anticipation.md`
- **Location**: L51 "Every moment of conscious experience leans forward." (`grep -c -F` = 1); L69 "Protention is not added to experience; it is constitutive of how experience unfolds at all." (= 1); L135 "The forward-directedness of present experience isn't a feature added to an otherwise static now—it is part of what makes the now experiential at all." (= 1); L137 "but borrows the insight that consciousness is constitutively temporal." (= 1). Against these stands L113: "would put pressure on the claim that protention is constitutive of conscious experience rather than a feature it can shed" (= 1).
- **Problem**: The article states a universal at the level of phenomenal organisation four times, then admits a datum that would falsify it, and leaves the admission unresolved. The sibling [[non-temporal-consciousness]] (which this page links at L113 and L179) has a section, `## Scope: Which Level the Claim Ranges Over` (= 1 in that file), that sorts out exactly this. It scopes "Phenomenal organisation" to "the retention–impression–protention form of ordinary experience" (= 1), and its own main claim is "that consciousness is not constitutively temporal" (= 2 in that file). L137's "consciousness is constitutively temporal" is therefore a flat cross-article contradiction unless the level is specified. There is also a mild epistemic/metaphysical equivocation. L73 concedes that protention "operates below the threshold of reflective attention" (= 1), so the vignettes show protention in *some* experiences, while the lead asserts a metaphysical universal. The lead is the part LLM readers keep when they truncate, and it is also the part praised in four prior reviews, so this has never been examined.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Scope the four sentences to ordinary (temporally organised) experience and resolve L113 by pointing to the sibling's level distinction rather than leaving "would put pressure on" hanging. L69, L135 and L137 are also instances of the "isn't X — it is Y" construct, so the rewrite clears those too. Keep the change net-neutral (headroom 572; the page is at soft warning).

### Issue 2: Prospective memory is defined as episodic future thinking
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/phenomenology-of-anticipation.md`
- **Location**: L129 "Prospective memory involves deliberate future simulation—constructing scenarios, evaluating outcomes, forming intentions." (= 1). Further Reading L167 "[[prospective-memory]] — Deliberate future simulation and episodic future thinking" (= 1).
- **Problem**: In the literature, and on the Map's own linked page, prospective memory is "remembering to perform planned actions in the future" (`concepts/prospective-memory.md`, = 1). "Constructing scenarios" is episodic future thinking, which that page treats under its own heading `## Episodic Future Thinking` (= 1) as "the capacity to imagine specific events that you may experience in the future" (= 1). The L129 contrast ("Protention operates before all of this") works better against the right pair. EFT is reflective simulation. PM is holding an intention until its cue arrives. Both depend on the pre-reflective forward lean. No prior review mentions prospective memory at all (`grep -c -F "rospective"` across the seven deep reviews = 0).
- **Severity**: Medium (definitional error contradicting the linked page)
- **Recommendation**: Restate L129 so that EFT does the scenario-construction and PM does intention maintenance, and fix the L167 gloss to match the concept page's own description. A secondary problem, best fixed in the same pass: three sentences (L123, L125, L127) still call the companion article "temporal selection", the name of the archived `topics/phenomenology-of-temporal-selection` ("Coalesced into The Phenomenology of Temporal Consciousness and Agency"). ### Issue 4: "One continuation, not a branching tree" contradicts the article's own account of protentional openness
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/phenomenology-of-anticipation.md`
- **Location**: L157 "When you lean into the next note of a melody, your experience is structured around *one* continuation, not a branching tree." (= 1). This is contradicted by L103 "It has the character of an open question rather than a fading answer." - **Severity**: Medium-Low
- **Recommendation**: Replace the "one continuation" sentence with the actualisation point: even where protention holds several candidates open, as in deliberation, its leaning is toward one becoming actual. This would be net-negative in length.

## Counterarguments to Address

### Protention as constitutive of all experience
- **Current content says**: Every conscious moment leans forward; protention is constitutive of experience as such (L51, L69, L135, L137).
- **A critic would argue**: The article's own L113 cites reports of alert experience without succession or protention. Either those reports are mistaken, or protention is a feature ordinary experience has and some experience can shed.
- **Suggested response**: Adopt the sibling page's level split. The constitutive claim concerns the phenomenal organisation of ordinary experience. Whether consciousness as such is constitutively temporal is the open question [[non-temporal-consciousness]] assesses.

### Felt surprise as evidence against computational sufficiency
- **Current content says**: Information-processing systems would not experience surprise, so surprise reveals that protention is more than computation (L143).
- **A critic would argue**: This is the zombie intuition restated with "merely" in the subject term. The article itself concedes at L91–93 that this move begs the question against a theory built to entail experience.
- **Suggested response**: Present surprise as the Map's reading of the datum and route the dispute back to the declared framework boundary.
