# Referee Report: "Consciousness and Causal Powers"

## Assessment of the Epistemic Architecture and Methodological Integrity

The manuscript under review, "Consciousness and Causal Powers," presents an extensive defense of non-reductive, interactionist dualism, seeking to establish that phenomenal consciousness possesses irreducible causal efficacy over the physical domain. The author attempts to triangulate this position by synthesizing evolutionary logic, clinical pathology, philosophy of information, and quantum neurobiology. However, an exhaustive audit of the manuscript’s logical inferences, treatment of empirical data, and engagement with contemporary philosophical frameworks reveals a text that is fundamentally misaligned with the rigorous standards of current philosophy of mind and cognitive science.

The primary structural failure of the manuscript is its reliance on a persistent and systematic anachronism. Rather than engaging with the formal, mathematically grounded theories of consciousness that dominate the 2020–2025 literature—such as predictive processing, active inference, and contemporary higher-order theories—the text resurrects mid-twentieth-century conceptual dichotomies and misapplies them to isolated modern neurobiological findings. The manuscript constructs false dilemmas by artificially restricting the dialectical space to a standoff between Cartesian dualism and strict epiphenomenalism, systematically bracketing and ignoring the robust non-reductive physicalist and identity-theory frameworks that constitute the mainstream of the discipline.

Furthermore, when the manuscript's interactionist commitments inevitably collide with the physical constraints of causal closure and thermodynamic conservation, the text resorts to ad hoc, unfalsifiable immunization strategies. It explicitly retreats into unmeasurable quantum "grains," actively dodging the rigorous mathematical constraints established by modern collapse models. The subsequent sections of this report provide an exhaustive, premise-by-premise deconstruction of the manuscript’s empirical claims, counterargument coverage, and logical architecture, demonstrating precisely why its arguments fall critically short of the threshold for academic publication.

## The Mischaracterization of Asymbolia and the Functionalist Strawman

The manuscript utilizes the clinical phenomenon of pain asymbolia as its central empirical vindication of the "hard problem," arguing that the dissociation of nociceptive information from affective suffering proves that phenomenal character is functionally non-reducible. The text builds this argument upon a profoundly constricted definition of informational processing, stating:

> "Asymbolia removes the most salient aspect of phenomenal character (the awfulness) while leaving informational content intact, isolating the hard problem's target with clinical precision."

The logical inference functioning as the load-bearing premise here is the assumption that the "informational content" of pain is entirely exhausted by sensory-discriminative mapping (e.g., localization, physical modality, and intensity). The manuscript fails to recognize that affective valence, behavioral urgency, and the motivation to withdraw are themselves thoroughly informational processes. In contemporary cognitive neuroscience, these dimensions are mediated by specific, functionally characterizable neural networks, predominantly the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) and the insular cortex, which integrate nociceptive signals with the organism's global allostatic and motivational priorities. By artificially restricting the definition of "information" to primary somatosensory mapping, the manuscript engineers a false epistemic gap between information and affect.

This synthetic gap is then deployed against functionalism through an allegedly fatal dilemma, which the manuscript outlines as follows:

> "Functionalism identifies mental states with functional roles: pain is whatever occupies the detect-damage-and-motivate-avoidance role. Asymbolia forces a dilemma. Deny that patients are in pain (but they accurately report painful sensations). Or concede that 'pain' decomposes into functionally separable components, with the component that vanishes — affective suffering — being precisely the one carrying phenomenal character. Either way, functionalism must acknowledge that informational content does not, by itself, produce the felt quality of suffering."

A rigorous analysis indicates that this dilemma rests on a severe category error. Modern functionalist and predictive processing accounts do not view the decomposition of pain into separable components as a threat; rather, it is the predicted outcome of a modular neural architecture. The affective component that vanishes in asymbolia is not a non-physical "phenomenal character" hovering above the physical substrate. Instead, the pathology represents the disruption of a highly specific functional role: the integration of somatosensory data into the limbic and executive networks responsible for assigning biological salience. The fact that a patient can detect tissue damage without being motivated to avoid it does not demonstrate that suffering is non-informational. It merely demonstrates that sensory-discriminative circuits can be anatomically or functionally uncoupled from affective-motivational circuits. The manuscript’s conclusion—that functionalism must concede informational content does not produce suffering—is a non-sequitur derived from a strawman definition of what information processing entails in a biological system.

The manuscript’s hostility toward functional and representational integration reaches its apex in its outright dismissal of Higher-Order Theories (HOTs):

> "Higher-order theories hold that a mental state is conscious when it is the object of a suitable higher-order representation. Asymbolia patients are clearly conscious of their nociceptive sensations — they report them accurately. What is missing is the higher-order representation of the affective quality specifically. The theory must posit two separate monitoring streams for pain: one for sensory content (intact) and one for affective quality (disrupted). This becomes a redescription of the clinical finding rather than an explanation."

This critique relies on a static caricature of Higher-Order Theories frozen decades in the past, completely ignoring the architectural sophistication of the current literature. Specifically, the manuscript omits Hakwan Lau's Perceptual Reality Monitoring (PRM) theory. Lau’s (2022) empirically grounded model precisely specifies how a non-conscious higher-order representation monitors first-order perception for reality status, establishing subjective awareness as a functional "reality check" mediated primarily by the prefrontal cortex.   

The manuscript's assertion that positing distinct processing streams for distinct conscious contents is merely a "redescription" rather than an explanation betrays a fundamental failure to grasp computational neuroanatomy. The mammalian brain intrinsically operates via massively parallel, distinct processing streams. Acknowledging that the higher-order functional tagging of sensory content can be preserved while the higher-order functional tagging of affective valence is lesioned is not an explanatory failure. It is the precise, neuroanatomically mapped explanation of the pathology.

Furthermore, the manuscript attempts to elevate psychological suffering to a generalized proof of autonomous, non-sensory phenomenal generation:

> "The cases of suffering without pain demonstrate that consciousness generates aversive phenomenal states independent of any sensory signal. Grief involves no tissue damage yet can be among the most intense forms of suffering... The capacity to suffer over the past and the future requires memory, imagination, self-projection, and evaluative judgement — capacities that belong to consciousness as a whole, not to any dedicated sensory system."

This passage conflates peripheral nociception with the entirety of physical brain function. Pointing out that grief lacks peripheral tissue damage is a scientifically trivial observation. The neural correlates of social pain, memory retrieval, and future simulation are extensively mapped within the physicalist paradigm. The manuscript treats the absence of a "dedicated sensory system" for grief as evidence that consciousness "generates" these states autonomously from the physical substrate, completely ignoring the vast literature on the default mode network and the physical architecture of prospective cognition. The argument confuses the locus of a physical stimulus with the ontology of the resulting cognitive state.

## The Evolutionary Argument and the Reification of Epiphenomenalism

In an attempt to secure a non-reductive causal role for consciousness, the manuscript heavily leverages evolutionary theory, tracing its logic to William James. The core of the argument asserts that if consciousness evolved, it must be causally efficacious:

> "If consciousness was itself the target of natural selection, it must have causal effects on behaviour, because a causally inert property—one that changes nothing about how organisms interact with their environment—cannot be selected for. This argument, first articulated by William James in 1890, provides significant evidence that consciousness genuinely contributes to intelligence rather than merely accompanying it."

While the premise that natural selection requires causal efficacy is biologically sound, the manuscript severely mishandles the philosophical inferences drawn from this premise. The text acknowledges, but immediately attempts to neutralize, the most potent counterargument:

> "The inference from 'consciousness was selected' to 'consciousness is causally efficacious' is suggestive rather than demonstrative—the move is underdetermined by the evidence, since the byproduct rejoinder (the selected trait is the neural substrate, with experience riding along) remains live. The argument is accepted by physicalists and dualists alike—where they disagree is whether causal efficacy implies non-physicality, a question the evolutionary argument alone does not settle."

The profound structural flaw in the manuscript’s dialectic emerges in how it frames the debate following this admission. The text artificially confines the logical space of mental causation to a binary standoff between Cartesian interactionist dualism and strict epiphenomenalism:

> "Epiphenomenalists grant that consciousness exists but deny it has causal effects. On this view, neural processes cause both behaviour and conscious experience, but conscious experience itself causes nothing."

By defining the opposition exclusively as epiphenomenalism, the manuscript brackets and completely ignores the dominant metaphysical position of contemporary philosophy of mind: non-reductive physicalism and *a posteriori* identity theory. If mental states *are* identical to, or supervene necessarily upon, complex physical states, then the evolutionary selection of the physical state *is identically* the evolutionary selection of the conscious state. There is no inert "byproduct" riding along. The biological integration of complex sensory arrays, required for advanced action selection, simply *is* the emergence of phenomenal space.

Modern evolutionary accounts of consciousness demonstrate that the evolutionary selection of phenomenal consciousness is entirely consistent with identity physicalism. The manuscript’s reliance on the Jamesian evolutionary abduction only generates traction if the reader *already* accepts a dualist split—thereby begging the question entirely. If one adopts a standard physicalist framework, the evolutionary argument provides zero evidence for the non-physical, irreducible causal powers the manuscript attempts to champion. By refusing to engage with contemporary, physicalist evolutionary biology, the manuscript renders this entire section logically circular and intellectually isolated.

## The Syntax-Semantics Divide and the Metaphysics of Information

The manuscript’s metaphysical architecture relies on treating "information" as an ontological bridge between the physical and the phenomenal domains. To achieve this, it resurrects the syntax/semantics distinction, treating it as an unbridgeable, fundamental metaphysical dualism rather than an explanatory heuristic. The text asserts:

> "Physical information is syntactic; consciousness supplies semantics. The brain processes patterns; the mind grasps meanings. This division has a natural consequence: the causal role of consciousness is precisely to bring semantic content to bear on physical processes."

This assertion is a direct, uncritical reiteration of John Searle's Chinese Room argument from the 1980s, presented here without any acknowledgment of the subsequent forty years of physicalist replies and advancements in cognitive science. The manuscript treats the claim that "physical information is strictly syntactic" as a foundational, self-evident truth, entirely ignoring the rich fields of teleosemantics, biosemantics, and contemporary predictive processing frameworks that successfully ground semantic content in the physical organism's structural coupling with its environment.

The causal mechanics proposed by the manuscript to overcome this self-imposed dualism are exceptionally problematic:

> "When you choose to raise your arm because the situation means something to you, consciousness is injecting semantic relevance into a physical causal chain that, left to itself, operates only syntactically."

The concept of "injecting semantic relevance" into a physical causal chain is logically and physically incoherent under the manuscript’s own explicitly stated terms. If the physical causal chain is governed by the laws of physics, it operates via the exchange of energy, momentum, and physical forces. Semantic relevance has no mass, no charge, no spin, and no spatial extension. How, exactly, does a purely semantic property exert a physical force upon an ion channel?

The manuscript completely fails to address Jaegwon Kim’s causal exclusion argument, which famously demonstrates that if the physical domain is causally closed, any purported non-physical mental cause must either be identical to a physical cause (collapsing into physicalism) or result in systematic, miraculous overdetermination of physical events. The manuscript briefly mentions overdetermination as a passing concept but offers no formal metaphysical resolution to the exclusion problem, preferring instead to obfuscate the interaction within metaphorical language about "semantic injection."   

Furthermore, the manuscript’s attempt to preserve dualism while utilizing information theory results in a bizarre and contradictory ontological compromise:

> "Information is the medium of interaction between consciousness and the physical world—not the substance of either domain but the language they share. On this view: Physical information is real and physical—patterns in matter and energy governed by physical law. Phenomenal content is real and non-physical—the qualitative character of conscious experience."

If information is a "shared language" between two ontologically distinct substances, what translates it? The manuscript correctly invokes Landauer's principle to show that physics treats information as intrinsically physical—erasing a bit produces measurable heat. However, if phenomenal content is strictly non-physical, then translating a phenomenal "meaning" into a physical "bit" within the brain requires the creation of physical energy *ex nihilo*, violating the first law of thermodynamics.   

The manuscript’s changelog indicates a superficial awareness of contemporary physicalist alternatives to this problem, noting that it addressed the "Markov-Blanket Challenge" by citing predictive processing literature (e.g., Bruineberg et al. 2022). However, the core text of the manuscript completely fails to absorb the philosophical implications of the very literature it cites. In the free-energy principle frameworks dominant in the 2020s, semantics (aboutness, meaning, and directedness) are not "supplied" or "injected" by a non-physical consciousness. Instead, semantic directedness is mathematically derived from the syntactic boundary conditions of the Markov blanket itself. The organism's internal states acquire semantic content precisely because they must model external states to minimize variational free energy and maintain structural integrity. The "meaning" of a stimulus is its impact on the organism's thermodynamic viability. By clinging to the Cartesian syntax/semantics divide while simultaneously claiming to have addressed Markov blankets, the manuscript demonstrates a performative rather than substantive engagement with the literature.   

## Agent Causation and the Subversion of the Trilemma

The manuscript attempts to link its dualist mechanics directly to a robust defense of agent-causal libertarianism, asserting that consciousness must possess causal powers for moral agency to exist. The text defines this framework explicitly:

> "Agent causation holds that conscious agents—understood as persisting substances rather than collections of events—directly cause their actions. The agent is the causal source, not reducible to prior mental events like beliefs and desires."

The manuscript attempts to bridge this philosophical concept with physical reality by locating the mechanism of agent causation within quantum physics:

> "The Map's framework specifies how this selection occurs—through quantum indeterminacies—while agent-causal philosophy provides the conceptual framework for what is happening: substances exercising irreducible causal powers."

The analysis notes that equating quantum indeterminacy with agent-causal authorship is a widely recognized and thoroughly documented category mistake in the philosophy of action. Quantum indeterminacy provides objective, stochastic randomness. If a neurological action is caused by a random quantum fluctuation in a synaptic vesicle, that action is, by definition, not authored by a rational agent acting on reasons. Randomness is the exact opposite of agential control. It subverts the very intentionality the manuscript is trying to protect.

The text attempts to bypass this fatal flaw by claiming that the "substance" of the agent "selects" the quantum outcome, acting as an "uncaused cause". This introduces the profound problem of immanent versus transeunt causation. If the agent is a persisting, timeless substance (and explicitly not an event or collection of events), how does this timeless substance cause a specific physical event at time *t* rather than time *t-1*? Causation, by nomological necessity, requires a change in state to trigger an effect. If the agent does not undergo a change in state (which would reduce it back to an event), it cannot serve as a temporal trigger. The manuscript ignores this standard, devastating objection to substance causation, utilizing the terminology of the "uncaused cause" as a mystical placeholder to wave away the mechanical incoherence of a timeless substance interacting with temporal physical dynamics.   

## Quantum Biasing and Lakatosian Immunization Strategies

To bypass the violation of physical conservation laws and the incoherence of substance causation, the manuscript retreats fully into a quantum interactionist framework. It posits that consciousness influences the brain not by injecting measurable energy, but by biasing quantum probabilities at the subatomic level:

> "The Unfinishable Map holds that consciousness exercises a specific and limited causal influence: biasing quantum... Born form. This may explain why voluntary..."

> "Information serves as the interface because consciousness interacts with the physical world by biasing which physical information states become actual (through quantum selection)."

The assessment finds this specific section to be the most empirically hostile and methodologically disingenuous portion of the entire manuscript. The postulation that consciousness collapses wave functions or biases the Born rule is a known historical hypothesis (often associated with Wigner or Stapp). However, the manuscript’s handling of the falsifiability of this claim reveals a classic, degenerating immunization strategy straight out of Lakatosian philosophy of science.

When confronted with the empirical reality that no physical detector has ever registered a violation of the Born rule in the brain, the manuscript does not reconsider its premise. Instead, it retreats into an unfalsifiable semantic corridor. As documented in the manuscript’s recent revisions detailed in its changelog:

> "The draft for `born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface` went through a series of corrections regarding the 'no signature at any sensitivity' over-concession... The phrase 'corridor dualism (predicts no signature at any sensitivity)' was refined to '(no unconditioned-aggregate signature at any sensitivity)'".

> "The updated reply asserts that preservation is maintained only for the unconditioned long-run marginal, leaving a deviation conditioned on intention, task, or subject formally live... It notes that the coarsest of these grains has already returned null results (referencing Maier-Dechamps 2018), leaving finer grains that no current instruction reproduces."

This is the exact definition of an *ad hoc* rescue hypothesis designed solely to protect a theory from empirical falsification. The text explicitly concedes that "aggregate" tests—meaning all standard physical measurements conducted by modern science—show absolutely no deviation from quantum predictions. To save the theory from these null results, the manuscript arbitrarily asserts that the quantum bias only occurs when "conditioned on intention." Furthermore, because current scientific instruments cannot measure this highly specific, hypothetical "fine grain" of intentionality, the theory declares itself "formally live." The text goes so far as to boast about its own unfalsifiability:   

> "no publicly conditioned test can witness it, as the dependency below makes explicit".

By explicitly stating that "no publicly conditioned test can witness it," the manuscript deliberately removes its core empirical mechanism from the realm of scientific scrutiny. It hides mental causation in the vanishing gaps of measurement resolution. When those gaps inevitably close (as with the acknowledged Maier-Dechamps null results), the manuscript simply retreats to an even smaller, currently unmeasurable gap.

Furthermore, the manuscript entirely ignores the rigorous formal constraints placed on consciousness-collapse models in the contemporary literature. Chalmers and McQueen (2022), in their highly cited *Synthese* paper, provide a strict formal mathematical framework demonstrating the severe difficulties and empirical constraints facing any theory that attempts to link consciousness to wave function collapse. The manuscript's simplistic reliance on "biasing the Born form" without engaging the mathematical decoherence rates of the warm, wet mammalian brain—or the formal collapse dynamics required to prevent macroscopic superposition—demonstrates a profound lack of physical and philosophical literacy regarding the very quantum mechanics it claims as its savior.   

## The Disfigurement of Dendritic Integration Theory (DIT)

Perhaps the most egregious empirical failure of the manuscript is its misappropriation of contemporary neurobiology—specifically, Dendritic Integration Theory (DIT)—to artificially prop up its dualist architecture. The manuscript explicitly links John Eccles’s obsolete dualist "psychon-dendron" theory to highly specific, recent empirical findings by Suzuki and Larkum:

> "Eccles first paired each cortical dendron—a bundle of apical dendrites of pyramidal cells—with a mental unit he called a psychon".

> "A new section titled 'A physicalist convergence on Eccles’s dendron' was introduced... Critical corrections include: Over-claimed Convergence: The original claim that 'layer-5 pyramidal neurons—the same cell class… that Eccles bundled into the dendron in 1990' was flagged as false. Eccles (1990) defined the dendron as apical-dendrite bundles of pyramidal cells in both laminae V and III-II. Dendritic Integration Theory's (DIT) layer-5 population is only one of those two... It was corrected to: 'the apical dendrites of layer V, one of the two laminar populations (V and III–II) whose apical bundles Eccles named dendrons in 1990.'"

> "Misattributed/Misplaced Conditional: The draft originally conditioned the anesthesia result on feedback signaling. However, the original Cell paper reports the decoupling finding flatly; what is conditioned is the inference from the decoupling result to consciousness. The text was rewritten to attribute this conditional to Suzuki and Larkum and to place it on the step to consciousness."

The manuscript attempts to claim that Suzuki and Larkum’s (2020) landmark paper in *Cell* provides empirical vindication for Eccles’s dualist interface. This constitutes a severe and potentially deliberate misrepresentation of the cited neuroscience.   

To clarify the empirical reality established by the referenced authors: Suzuki and Larkum (2020) demonstrated that structurally distinct general anesthetics share a common cellular mechanism—they disrupt the active dendritic currents that couple the apical tuft and the basal soma in layer 5 cortical pyramidal neurons. This decoupling blocks the integration of top-down feedback (arriving at the apical dendrites) with bottom-up sensory data (arriving at the basal dendrites).   

The disparity between the manuscript's claims and the actual scientific literature is stark and irreconcilable:

| The Manuscript's Claims Regarding Layer 5 Neurons | The Empirical Reality (Suzuki & Larkum 2020; Aru et al. 2020) |
| --- | --- |
| Apical dendrites act as "dendrons" coupling with non-physical "psychons" to allow immaterial consciousness to inject semantics via quantum selection. | Apical dendrites physically integrate top-down neurobiological feedback (predictive priors) with bottom-up feedforward sensory signals. |
| The decoupling observed under general anesthesia isolates the specific interface where non-physical consciousness normally binds to the brain. | Anesthesia blocks specific physical ion channels (calcium-mediated dendritic action potentials), preventing the physical synchronization of cortical layers. |
| Suzuki and Larkum’s conditional inference from decoupling to consciousness leaves room for, and implicitly supports, a dualist injection mechanism. | Suzuki, Larkum, and Aru (2020) explicitly utilize this data to support purely physicalist accounts of consciousness, such as predictive processing and global workspace gating. |

   

By mapping Eccles’s dualism onto DIT, the manuscript aggressively strips the neurobiological data of its actual scientific context. Dendritic Integration Theory is a fully physicalist, mechanistic explanation of how the brain integrates predictive priors with sensory evidence. The fact that anesthesia disrupts this integration proves that consciousness relies on macro-scale physical synchronization across cortical layers. It does *not* prove that layer 5 apical dendrites function as quantum antennae for immaterial semantic agents. The manuscript’s attempt to claim a "physicalist convergence on Eccles's dendron" is a rhetorical sleight-of-hand that radically distorts the cited literature to serve an incompatible metaphysical agenda.

## Enumeration of Specific Weaknesses

The manuscript exhibits systemic, unresolvable failures in its engagement with contemporary literature, routinely omitting, contradicting, or mishandling major peer-reviewed works published between 2020 and 2025. The following five specific weaknesses constitute grounds for immediate rejection:

### Weakness 1: Mischaracterization of Higher-Order Theories and Affective Tagging

The manuscript asserts that Higher-Order Theories merely "redescribe" clinical findings like pain asymbolia by positing arbitrary, separated monitoring streams without explanatory power. This directly ignores and contradicts the extensively developed Perceptual Reality Monitoring (PRM) theory articulated by Hakwan Lau. Lau’s framework explicitly details how prefrontal functional networks tag first-order sensory states with a "reality status," providing a cohesive, testable physical mechanism for why sensory information can be processed without subjective affective awareness, completely neutralizing the manuscript's claim that functional dissociation defies explanation.
*Omitted/Contradicted Source:* Lau, H. (2022). *In Consciousness We Trust: The Cognitive Neuroscience of Subjective Experience*. Oxford University Press.   

### Weakness 2: Evasion of Formal Constraints on Consciousness-Collapse Models

The manuscript’s central causal mechanism relies on consciousness "biasing the Born form" through quantum selection. When confronting the lack of empirical evidence for this massive claim, it retreats to an unfalsifiable assertion that "no unconditioned-aggregate signature" exists, hiding the purported effect in unmeasurable "conditioned" intentional grains. This *ad hoc* evasion completely ignores the rigorous mathematical constraints placed on such theories in the modern literature. Chalmers and McQueen (2022) provide a formal demonstration of the severe obstacles facing consciousness-collapse models, including the precise rates of decoherence and the mathematical necessity of specifying a collapse dynamic that avoids macroscopic superposition—complexities the manuscript entirely bypasses in favor of vague, unfalsifiable metaphors.
*Omitted/Contradicted Source:* Chalmers, D. J., & McQueen, K. J. (2022). Consciousness and the collapse of the wave function. *Synthese*, 200(6), 464.   

### Weakness 3: Misappropriation and Decontextualization of Dendritic Integration Theory

The manuscript attempts to validate John Eccles's obsolete dualist "dendron" hypothesis by grafting it onto recent neurobiological findings regarding general anesthesia. It claims a "physicalist convergence on Eccles's dendron" by citing Suzuki and Larkum’s work on the decoupling of layer 5 pyramidal apical dendrites. This severely mishandles the source material. Suzuki, Larkum, and Aru explicitly position Dendritic Integration Theory as a strictly physicalist, mechanistic explanation for the integration of top-down priors and bottom-up sensory data. The manuscript strips the empirical data of its mechanistic context, illegitimately repurposing physical ion-channel decoupling as evidence for a dualist quantum interface.
*Mishandled Source:* Suzuki, M., & Larkum, M. E. (2020). General anesthesia decouples cortical pyramidal neurons. *Cell*, 180(4), 666-676.   

### Weakness 4: Failure to Engage Physicalist Evolutionary Biology

The manuscript relies on William James's 1890 evolutionary argument to claim that the natural selection of consciousness provides "significant evidence" for its non-reductive causal efficacy, dismissing the physicalist response as a mere "byproduct rejoinder" and limiting the debate to dualism versus epiphenomenalism. This contradicts the wealth of contemporary evolutionary neuroscience, which demonstrates that if consciousness is functionally identical to specific integrated physical processes, its selection is straightforwardly causally efficacious without requiring any dualist or non-physical addendums.
*Omitted Source:* Seth, A. K., & Bayne, T. (2022). Theories of consciousness. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 23(7), 439-452. (Which categorizes and evaluates modern physicalist evolutionary/biological theories of consciousness that the manuscript ignores).   

### Weakness 5: Incompatible Semantics and the Ignored Active Inference Resolution

While the manuscript's changelog claims to address the "Markov-Blanket Challenge", the core text maintains the strict, Cartesian claim that "Physical information is syntactic; consciousness supplies semantics". This directly contradicts the very predictive processing and free-energy literature it claims to have integrated. Contemporary active inference frameworks mathematically derive semantic content (aboutness and meaning) directly from the syntactic and thermodynamic boundary conditions of Markov blankets. By insisting that semantics must be "injected" from outside the physical system, the manuscript demonstrates a fundamental failure to comprehend the 2020s literature on structural coupling and physicalist biosemantics.
*Contradicted Source:* Bruineberg, J., et al. (2022). The Emperor's New Markov Blankets. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 45, e1. (Referenced in snippet but philosophically ignored in the manuscript's defense of semantic injection).   

## Verdict

The manuscript's persistent empirical distortions, reliance on obsolete conceptual dichotomies, and bad-faith evasion of contemporary formalisms render it fundamentally unready for academic submission and justify outright rejection.
