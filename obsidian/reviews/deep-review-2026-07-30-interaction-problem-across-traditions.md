## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. Independence over-claim at "What the Typology Reveals" (was L115).** The article asserted that "Sāṃkhya, Nyāya, Avicenna, Descartes, and Buddhist Abhidharma **independently** identify the same difficulty." Three of those five — Sāṃkhya, Nyāya, Abhidharma — share a single Indian philosophical milieu, and Avicenna inherits Greek philosophy directly, as does Descartes. The claim therefore triple-counted one lineage and double-counted another, presenting roughly two near-independent lineages as five.

This contradicts the Map's own register. [[positions/arguments-for-dualism|P-D3]] holds that cross-traditional convergence is *near-independence, not a third confirmation*, citing the shared Indian milieu and Indo-Greek exchange through Gandhara as possible influence channels. Per the driver's instruction the article was fixed, not the register.

The direction matters: this over-claim ran **in the Map's favour**, inflating a convergence count that supports its argument. That is the class of error a sympathetic reviewer waves through, and six prior reviews did.

*Resolution applied.* Rewritten to state the honest count. Confirmed first that the existing hedges do **not** already cover it: L117 ("The convergence is on the *problem*, not the *answer*") and L133 ("broadens the warrant for the *problem*... does not widen the evidential base for the Map's *solution*") both calibrate **what the convergence licenses**, and both are excellent. Neither says anything about **how many independent data points there are**. The two hedges are orthogonal to the defect, so the fix was genuinely needed and was confined to the one paragraph.

Before → after:

> When Sāṃkhya, Nyāya, Avicenna, Descartes, and Buddhist Abhidharma independently identify the same difficulty in mind-body relations, the difficulty cannot be dismissed as an artifact of Cartesian metaphysics.

> Sāṃkhya, Nyāya, Buddhist Abhidharma, Avicenna, and Descartes all identify the same difficulty in mind-body relations—though not as five independent identifications. The first three share an Indian philosophical milieu and count as one lineage rather than three, and Avicenna and Descartes both stand downstream of Greek philosophy, so the recurrence spans something closer to two lineages than five. Even those two are near-independent rather than sealed off, since Indo-Greek exchange left possible channels of influence open. What survives that deflation is still substantial: the difficulty cannot be dismissed as an artifact of Cartesian metaphysics, because it was posed in India centuries before Descartes and inside ontologies sharply unlike his.

The functionalist counter-argument that follows was preserved and strengthened rather than weakened: the reply now notes the Indian schools differ in ontology "as much from one another as from Descartes," which is the *stronger* form of the anti-bias argument and does not depend on the inflated count.

**2. Reference 4 pointed at a non-existent SEP entry.** `Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Nyaya": https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nyaya/` returns **HTTP 404**. SEP has no standalone Nyāya or Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika entry; its Nyāya material is distributed across *Epistemology in Classical Indian Philosophy*, *Analytic Philosophy in Early Modern India*, *Gaṅgeśa*, and others (confirmed against the SEP search index). This reference had survived six reviews.

*Resolution applied.* Repointed to the **IEP "Nyāya" entry** (https://iep.utm.edu/nyaya/), verified live and confirmed to support the exact body claims: the four-link perceptual chain ("a self and its *manas*, the *manas* and a sense organ, and the sense organ and an object"), the selective-attention inference ("the absence of simultaneous cognition from all of the senses indicates the presence of a faculty which governs selective attention"), and the *saṃyoga* / *samavāya* distinction.

**3. Reference 19 conflated two distinct Tempels publications.** The entry read `Tempels, P. (1945). *Bantu Philosophy*. Paris: Présence Africaine.` That merges the 1945 French first edition with the 1959 English translation. Verified publication history: *La philosophie bantoue* first appeared in **1945** from **Lovania** (Élisabethville); the Dutch *Bantoe-filosofie* followed in 1946 (De Sikkel); Présence Africaine issued the French edition in 1949 and the **English *Bantu Philosophy* in 1959**. So neither the English title nor the Présence Africaine imprint belongs to the 1945 date.

*Resolution applied.* Corrected to `Tempels, P. (1945). *La philosophie bantoue*. Élisabethville: Lovania. English translation: *Bantu Philosophy*. Paris: Présence Africaine, 1959.` Notably this is the *same reference* whose *-ntu* attribution failed a check on 2026-07-29 — the paragraph's source apparatus was wrong in a second, independent way.

### Empirical-Claim Fidelity Check

The body states Hagan et al. (2002) "found estimates **eight to nine orders of magnitude** longer than Tegmark's." The 2026-06-03 review of this same file recorded the figure as **seven** orders — a discrepancy worth resolving at source.

Verified verbatim at the arXiv abstract (quant-ph/0005025): Tegmark's calculation is **10⁻¹³ s**; Hagan et al.'s corrected recalculation yields **10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ s** (and 10⁻² to 10⁻¹ s once actin gelation is included). 10⁻¹³ → 10⁻⁵/10⁻⁴ is exactly **eight to nine orders of magnitude**.

**The article's current figure is correct**; the "seven" in the 06-03 review was the pre-correction wording, since fixed by an intervening refine. No change needed. Recorded here so a future review does not re-open it.

### Doctrine-Attribution Check (non-Western material)

Prompted by the 2026-07-29 Tempels/*-ntu* misassignment. Term-genealogy errors are invisible to metadata checking, so each doctrine was checked against a source independent of the corpus.

- **Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika *manas*** — sound. IEP confirms the four-link chain (self–manas–sense organ–object) and the serial-attention inference from *manas*. Atomicity (*aṇu*) of manas is Vaiśeṣika doctrine and the article correctly attributes to "Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika" rather than to Nyāya alone. *Minor note*: IEP glosses manas as "insentient" where the article says "material"; in Vaiśeṣika manas is a distinct *dravya*, not composed of the four elements, so "material" is a defensible comparative shorthand but not a technical match. Left as-is — changing it risks importing a different imprecision.
- ***saṃyoga* / *samavāya*** — sound. IEP confirms both terms and the conjunction/inherence glosses as sense-object connection types.
- **Sāṃkhya reflection model, Vācaspati Miśra and Vijñānabhikṣu** — sound. IEP explicitly attributes the mirror/reflection theory to "later commentators (Vācaspati Miśra and Vijñāna Bhikṣu)," not to Īśvarakṛṣṇa — matching the article, which is careful to place the reflection model with the commentators and the lame-and-blind analogy with the *Kārikā*. Dates (c. 9th c. / c. 16th c.) are standard.
- **Lame-and-blind analogy** — confirmed in the *Sāṃkhyakārikā* per IEP, quoted directly there.
- **Magnet analogy** — **unconfirmed.** IEP's Sankhya entry does not contain it, and the search budget was exhausted before an alternative source could be consulted. The analogy (*ayaskānta*, the lodestone moving iron by mere proximity) is well attested in Sāṃkhya-Yoga commentarial literature, so per verification discipline **no action was taken** on a failed lookup. Flagged in Remaining Items.
- **Avicenna's Flying Man** — sound. SEP *Arabic and Islamic Psychology* confirms the argument shows "the soul is an independent intelligent substance," matching the article's "argues for the soul's independence from the body."
- **Avicenna's practical / theoretical intellect** — sound as doctrine. SEP's entries do not spell the distinction out for Avicenna in those terms (the *arabic-islamic-mind* entry attributes an explicit version to Fārābī), but the two-faces-of-the-rational-soul account is standard Avicennan doctrine from *Kitāb al-Nafs*, and the article attributes the specific quoted phrases ("bottom-up preparing influence" / "top-down actualizing effect") to Jamali et al., not to SEP. No cross-wiring.
- **Mulla Ṣadrā, substantial motion** — sound. SEP confirms *al-ḥaraka al-jawhariyya* and the "corporeal in its origination and spiritual in its survival" progression, matching the article's body-as-lower-grade-of-soul framing. Only the dates were wrong.
- **Theravāda *Paṭṭhāna*, twenty-four conditions** — sound. SEP *Abhidharma* states verbatim: "The Theravāda theory of causal conditioning, as set out in the *Paṭṭhāna*, proposes a set of twenty-four conditional relations (*paccaya*)," and lists root cause (*hetupaccaya*), object support, predominance, and proximity — precisely the four the article names, in order.
- **Yogācāra *ālaya-vijñāna* and karmic seeds** — sound. SEP confirms the seed theory feeding into "the Yogācāra's concepts of 'store consciousness' (*ālayavijñāna*)."
- **Wang Yangming, *zhīxíng héyī*** — sound. SEP gives the same romanisation and the doctrine. The article's framing of *lǐ*/*qì* meeting in *xīn* as the general Neo-Confucian background that Wang "radicalised" is fair: SEP records Wang's radicalisation as "the mind *is* Pattern," which is precisely a radicalisation of the Cheng-Zhu position.
- **Yoruba *ara* / *emi* / *ori*** — **unconfirmed.** Both candidate reference-work URLs 404'd and the search budget was exhausted. The tripartite division is standard in the Gbadegesin/Makinde/Abimbola literature; the one point that would repay checking is whether "inner head" is the correct gloss for *orí* simpliciter or for *orí inú* specifically. **No action taken.** Flagged in Remaining Items.

**No doctrine was found cross-wired to the wrong school.** The 2026-07-29 *-ntu* defect appears to have been an isolated instance rather than a pattern — but note that the *same paragraph* carried a second, independent apparatus defect (Critical 3), so the paragraph was doubly wrong even after last week's fix.

- *Process monist*: Type 3 dissolves the problem more economically than the Map's Type 4. The article concedes this is "the typology's deepest challenge" and answers at the axiom level rather than claiming a win. Correctly calibrated; framework-boundary disagreement.
- *Historian of philosophy*: even two lineages may not be independent. This is now stated in the article itself rather than left for the reader, which is the improvement.
