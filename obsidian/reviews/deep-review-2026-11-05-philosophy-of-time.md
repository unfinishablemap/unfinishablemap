## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Nirodha-samāpatti reversed (L193; factual error, and a fit claimed as confirmation).** The page said cessation reports describe "not unconsciousness but awareness without temporal flow" and that "this is exactly what cessation reports describe". The source says the opposite. Laukkonen et al. (2023, *Prog Brain Res* 280:61–87; PubMed 37714573 abstract, verified verbatim) report that practitioners describe "a total absence of consciousness during meditation" and associate cessations "with the absence of any time experience". The sibling repairs at `topics/temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency` L220 and `apex/time-consciousness-growing-block` L117 already carry the corrected reading. **Fix**: the page now says practitioners report a total absence of consciousness, not awareness without flow. Because all experience stops, the case cannot isolate temporal experience. Linked to [[cessation-versus-plenitude]].
2. **Fox et al. 2012 drew an inference the study does not support (L199).** The page said: "The logarithmic improvement in introspective accuracy with meditation experience suggests temporal continuity is constructed from discrete constitutive events." In the study (PMC3458044 full text), accuracy means reports of *tactile sensitivity across 20 body regions*. Nothing in it is temporal. The design is cross-sectional, so "improvement" is causal language the authors avoid ("further work could experimentally examine possible training effects"). The authors read the logarithmic shape as "suggestive of diminishing returns … strongly reminiscent of many forms of skill learning". **Fix**: the page now states the finding and the authors' reading, and says it bears on trusting trained reports, not on discrete constitutive events. Fox 2012, previously an unnamed reference, is now cited inline.
3. **A quotation with no source (L199).** The page claimed neurophenomenology "calls" this "fine-grained temporal dynamics". The phrase is absent from Fox 2012 (full-text grep: 0 hits for "fine-grained" or "fine grained"). It has no other attributed source. **Fix**: the quotation marks and the naming claim are gone. The page now says it is "the grain neurophenomenology tries to match against neural dynamics", which matches the field's own description in the opening paragraph of `concepts/neurophenomenology-and-contemplative-neuroscience`.
4. **Stapp's Zeno model was misdescribed (L147).** The page said attention holds "desired neural patterns against decoherence". In Schwartz, Stapp & Beauregard (2005, *Phil Trans R Soc B* 360; PMC1569494 HTML, verified verbatim), decoherence effects "are automatically taken into account in the von Neumann formulation employed here". The holding effect "can override very strong mechanical forces arising from process 2". This is the same defect fixed today at `topics/clinical-neuroplasticity-evidence-for-bidirectional-causation` L108. The earlier corpus regex missed this page because the wording here is "against decoherence", not "prevent… decoher". **Fix**: "holding desired neural patterns in place; the model includes decoherence rather than preventing it."
5. **The A-theorist reply used a B-theoretic move (L51).** The page credited A-theorists with the reply that "the indexical nature of tense (like 'here' for space)… just marks perspective". That indexical analysis is the B-theory's. The page itself gives it to the eternalist at L67 ("'now' is merely indexical (like 'here')") and to the B-theorist at L53, so L51 contradicted it. **Fix**: L51 now gives the tense-logic reply: an event *is* present, *was* future and *will be* past, and the properties conflict only if the tenses are flattened into a tenseless "is". Cited to Prior 1967. Secondary sources confirm that Prior's ch. I answers McTaggart this way (the inference is valid only if the copulas are tenseless).
