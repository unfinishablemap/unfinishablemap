   - **Old (L47)**: "Michael Zimmerman (1997) and Gideon Rosen (2004) argue that blame for acting in ignorance must trace back to an earlier act done in awareness of its wrongness, and that the trace often runs out, so blameworthiness is far rarer than ordinary practice assumes."
   - **Why it fails.** The SEP separates the two authors.
     - On Zimmerman it says "it can be speculated that ignorant wrongdoing very rarely has such non-ignorant (or akratic) origins (Zimmerman 1997: 418; Levy 2011: 131)".
     - On Rosen: "Moreover, it has been argued that it would be very difficult, or even impossible, for us to ascertain whether the akrasia requirement has been met on any particular occasion (Rosen 2004: 308)."
