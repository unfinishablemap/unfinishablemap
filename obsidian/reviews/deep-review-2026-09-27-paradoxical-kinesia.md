## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Schlesinger 2007 count overstated (factual)**: the article said two of fifty patients reported paradoxical kinesia "under missile attack", and the table read "2 of 50 under missile attack". The abstract says "one war related and the other historical". Fixed in the body and the table: two cases in total, only one during the war.
- **Schlesinger 2007 selective reporting (source fidelity)**: the article used the study only against the bypass story. It left out the authors' own conclusion, "Visual, but not auditory, triggers appear to be needed to prompt its occurrence", which favours an external-cue account. That conclusion is now quoted, with a note that it rests on two patients.
- **Redgrave 2010 misstated as silent (attribution)**: the article said applying Redgrave et al. to the cue benefit was "this article's extrapolation; their abstract does not discuss it". The full text (PMC3124757) does make this move itself. Replying to the objection that cue benefits show sensorimotor circuits still work, Redgrave et al. write "sensory input is equally important for goal-directed control". The sentence now credits them, and the extrapolation claim is narrowed to urgency and threat, since the paper never names paradoxical kinesia.
- **Dual-task test not credited**: the "What Would Discriminate" section offered dual-task costs as its own test. Redgrave et al. predict that reliance on goal-directed control makes patients' actions "increasingly subject to interference from other goal-directed tasks". They are now credited.
