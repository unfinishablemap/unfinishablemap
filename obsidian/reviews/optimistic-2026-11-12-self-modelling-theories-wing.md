**Why these four belong together.** Each theory explains consciousness, or the appearance of it, by a system representing something about itself: a separate thought about a state (HOT), a state that represents itself (SRT), a model of the system's own attention (AST), a transparent model of the organism as a whole (SMT). The Map answers all four with one move, the relocation objection, and the link counts under [Cross-Linking Suggestions](#cross-linking-suggestions) show that the pages share that argument far more than they share links.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would recognise the same structural question asked four times without being stretched. HOT L180: "why one representation taking another as its object should be accompanied by anything at all. Every refinement surveyed here sharpens the specification without reaching that question." SRT L31 asks why the reflexive structure "is *felt* rather than processed in the dark". He would particularly approve of AST L66, which credits his own meta-problem as the question AST is best placed to answer, and then asks what the answer leaves over.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp gets little here, and the pages say so. HOT L172 reports that the quantum interface "is sometimes said to predict anomalies classical HOT cannot accommodate, but no anomaly class has been named and the prediction is not yet operationalisable", and names the missing interface-eligibility law as "a debt the Map does not possess". AST L207 says only that the Map's alternative "remains viable if decoherence objections can be met". What Stapp would value is AST L173–175: attention as the place where effort is exerted, with the phenomenology of effort offered as the datum a control model has to redescribe.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

SRT is the page Nagel would read twice. It separates qualitative character from subjective character (L37–38), credits Kriegel with seeing that the second is "the real heart of the mystery" (L40), and ends on the sentence that best states the wing's verdict: "the best structural map yet drawn of the fact *that* experience is for-a-subject, and it fails, informatively, to explain *why*" (L97). SMT L59 makes the matching point about transparency: it "is a claim about the introspective *availability* of representational vehicles; it does not by itself manufacture experience from non-experience."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

AST L153–163 gives Whitehead a section and keeps him in his place: process philosophy is "a rival order of explanation the Map finds congenial without adopting its panexperientialism", and L157 adds that "the Map's own objection (below) needs no Whitehead." That is the right use of this persona. The section removes nothing from the evidential ledger and upgrades no system's standing on the five-tier scale; it shows that AST's direction of derivation is not the only one available.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane's interest is in whether conscious states do anything. HOT L174 draws the consequence for higher-order thoughts that are "just neural computations": "the computation does the work while consciousness watches." AST L209 puts the same pressure on the schema, and SRT L95 on self-representation ("the felt for-me-ness rides along on processes that would run identically without it"). The three passages make one argument about agency from three theories and do not cite each other.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

McGinn would single out SMT L57, which quotes the Map's own tenet text against itself: the indexical objection is "as strong as the non-deflationary 'I' it presupposes, and no stronger." He would also approve of HOT L140, which declines to treat the dreaming and contemplative cases as refutations and concludes instead that "HOT operates more as a philosophical framework than an empirical theory", a verdict about what can be settled, not about who is right.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This persona's verdict matters most for the wing, and it is favourable on four counts.

1. **SRT L85–87 declines a tenet-as-evidence upgrade by name.** The drift from representation to acquaintance among Kriegel's defenders is the most tempting piece of support in the wing. The page says it "points less far than the Map would like", that Giustina and Williford "are acquaintance **primitivists**" whose view "is standardly offered as a broadly naturalistic monism, not a dualism", and that "reading the drift for more than that would be [[possibility-probability-slippage|possibility/probability slippage]]: a defeater has been removed ... and defeater-removal keeps the Map's reading live without supplying positive evidence for it." This is the "tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating" pattern stated in the article's own words.
2. **HOT L109–115 reports the evidence against its own section.** Blind insight "involves implicit learning rather than perception"; the aPFC stimulation result "did not replicate: Bor et al. (2017)"; and "the same group supplies both the evidence this section leans on and the evidence weakening it." The table at L126 carries "(contested)" and "not replicated" in the row itself.
3. **AST L80 and L86 keep the engineering results at their weight.** The ASTOUND project "tests, rather than establishes" the functional gains, and the neural-agent results show a "*useful* designed resource ... the cautious, better-supported reading—not something that emerges spontaneously in any sufficiently complex system."
4. **AST L141 weighs its own contemplative evidence before using it:** "first-person phenomenological pressure on AST, not independent empirical evidence against it".

The Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist do not conflict anywhere in the wing: the Whitehead section in AST is marked as a rival framework and carries no evidential claim. No calibration task is generated.

One reservation belongs here, and it is a matter of evidence, not tone. AST L42 (the lead) still says the appearance "must appear *to something*, and that something cannot be another model without infinite regress", while L103 says "the bare regress is not decisive, and the Map grants this" and L205 says the Map's "primary response is not the bare regress". A reader who stops at the lead takes away the argument the page withdrew. See Priority 1.

- **Notable quote**: "The fork therefore refutes self-representationalism *given* phenomenal realism rather than compelling a committed reductionist to abandon the reduction on its own terms." (L91)
- **Why it works**: the page states the condition under which its own argument has force, which neither HOT L97 nor AST L169 does for the same fork.

### AST: `concepts/attention-schema-theory`
- **Strongest point**: L103–105 concedes the bare regress to Graziano and Frankish and relocates the Map's objection to the question that survives the concession.
- **Notable quote**: *Relocating the demand from "what observes the model" to "what makes its operation felt" does not dissolve it.* (L105)
- **Why it works**: the "What Would Challenge This View?" list (L187–199) gives five conditions under which the Map's critique would fail, the first of which is an answer to this very question.

### SMT: `concepts/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity`

- **HOT, Block (2011).** Defect. L81 opens "Ned Block argues 'the higher-order approach to consciousness is defunct'" and goes on to the phenomenal/access distinction. The quotation is the paper's title (so recorded by deep review 2026-07-11). The paper's argument is a different one. Its abstract (OpenAlex, DOI 10.1093/analys/anr037, fetched 05:53Z) says: "There is a well-known objection to the higher order approach, a version of which is fatal", and that Rosenthal's "retreat ... fails to solve the problem." Block's own restatement in "Response to Rosenthal and Weisberg" (DOI 10.1093/analys/anr036, abstract fetched 05:57Z) gives the objection: the theory holds "both that an appropriate higher order thought is sufficient for a conscious state and that being the object of an appropriate higher order thought is necessary for a conscious state", and a lone, unconscious, "empty" higher-order thought "determines a conscious state at t, but – contrary to the necessary condition – there is no thought at t about that conscious state – unless the lone thought is about itself. But such self-reference would collapse the HOT theory into the rival same-order theory." HOT L69 reports this objection and attributes it to "Critics". The reply is missing too: Rosenthal, "Exaggerated reports: reply to Block", *Analysis* 71(3), 431–437, DOI 10.1093/analys/anr039, whose abstract says "Ned Block sees the theory as harbouring an incoherence" built on "the much discussed possibility of one's having a HOT without actually being in the state the HOT represents one as being in", and answers that "one will still be subjectively aware of oneself as being in whatever state the HOT describes". The body of Block's paper is closed-access and was not read; the abstract does confirm that he also contests the what-it-is-like-ness claim, so L81 is right about his direction and wrong about what the "defunct" verdict rests on.
