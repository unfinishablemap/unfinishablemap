### Critical Issues Found

- **Quotation 1, commentator's gloss presented as the author's words.** Marks removed; the sentence now gives Michaelian's own formulation in paraphrase: "to remember is to imagine the past, reliably".
- **A view reversed (same sentence).** The page had Simulationism say the "feeling of remembering" cannot distinguish memory from confabulation. The note (L61) said the opposite: that we distinguish them by phenomenology. Michaelian's 2016 paper is a taxonomy that separates remembering from confabulation by reliability. Replaced with what Perrin et al. 2020 argue: the feeling of pastness is a metacognitive signal, not contact with the past. This also gives reference 4 its first inline citation.
- **Quotation 2, absent from its source and stated as field consensus.** Now "Cognitive science treats episodic memory as construction, not playback", no quotation marks.
- **Quotation 3, words changed inside the marks.** Restored to Bartlett's wording with the page number.
- **Quotation 4, cited to the wrong translation.** Replaced with Bodhi's wording, sutta number added.
- **Snell misreported twice.** (a) "there is no word in ancient Greek corresponding to 'consciousness' as we understand it (Snell 1953)": Snell's claim is about Homer, and about mind and soul: "Homer has no one word to characterize the mind or the soul" (archive.org full text of the 1953 edition, 1 hit). Now quoted. (b) "Homeric heroes do not deliberate internally — their decisions arrive as external commands from gods" was stated flat and as fact. Snell's sentence concedes the pondering: "Homer does not know genuine personal decisions; even where a hero is shown pondering two alternatives the intervention of the gods plays the key role" (1 hit). Now attributed to Snell, with the concession, and marked as disputed (Williams 1993, *Shame and Necessity*, whose text takes up "what … Snell thought was lacking").
- **Taylor mis-summarised.** "the Reformation's conscience, Enlightenment rationalism, Romantic expressivism" is the note's gloss of a Wikipedia summary. The book's own parts are Inwardness (Augustine to Descartes and Locke), The Affirmation of Ordinary Life, and the expressivist turn. Corrected. The trailing clause that dated the modern concept of consciousness to Locke read as Taylor's claim; it is now a separate statement resting on SEP "Seventeenth-Century Theories of Consciousness" ("In the seventeenth century, 'consciousness' began to take on a uniquely modern sense").
- **Logomorphism cited to the wrong book and glossed as something Barfield did not say.** "logomorphism" has 0 hits in *Saving the Appearances* (1957), the only Barfield work in the reference list, and is coined in *Poetic Diction* (1928, ch. 4): "projecting post-logical thoughts back into a pre-logical age—would perhaps be 'Logomorphism'" (primary hit, two scans; Loftin 2024 confirms where the term is coined and warns that the projection *leads to* logomorphism and does not define it). The page said the term "names the mechanism by which the historical void conceals itself", which is the Map's use. Now: Barfield's coinage quoted and dated, the void-mechanism reading marked as the Map's. *Poetic Diction* added to the references.
- **Husserl enlisted for preservationism.** The note (L159) invented "Husserl's sedimented retentions" as a hope that past experience "can genuinely be re-accessed". SEP "Memory" names no Husserl in its preservationism discussion (0 hits), and Husserl's lectures treat recollection as re-presentation, not original givenness. Name and reference removed. Not verified against Husserl's text this session; removed as unsupported, not as proven false.
- **Conway overstated.** "memory serves identity maintenance, not accurate recording" is the note's summary line (L63) and contradicts the quotation two lines above it in the same note (the working self mediates between "coherence and correspondence to reality"). The 2000 abstract supports only that current goals of the working self shape access. Now stated at that grade.
- **Infant line out of step with its repaired target.** "synaptic pruning reorganises the very circuits that supported infant experience beyond re-entry" now reads "reshapes the circuits that supported infant cognitive modes, plausibly beyond re-entry", matching [[infant-consciousness]] as repaired today.

- **Description stated the historical thesis flat** while the lead and body make it conditional. Now conditional.
