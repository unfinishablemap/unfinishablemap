## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would recognise SV's zombie generalisation (SV:68) as his own move stated cleanly: Wallace picked out particular capacities, while the zombie argument makes "*all* phenomenal experience" surplus to function. He would be more impressed by what SV then declines to do with it. SV:118 says the surplus's identification as a void "was carried out under dualism-friendly methodology, and the fact that the dualism tenet makes the surplus expected cannot then serve as independent evidence for the tenet itself." A dualist who refuses a free confirmation is more persuasive than one who takes it.

PV:122 does the same for overflow: "The move 'something that feels full must have crossed into phenomenal territory' is not an independent argument for dualism — it presupposes the very distinction between physical sparsity and phenomenal fullness that materialism denies." The passage works because it names the tempting inference in the opponent's hearing before rejecting it, and then says what survives: the void has "a different *shape* inside each framework."

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp gets little here, and the pages are right to give him little. TV:108 is the strongest sentence for him because it turns his model against itself: "The selection model presupposes a selector, leaving its beginning and individuation unexplained, a debt beside the [[positions/quantum-interface#^mechanism-debt|mechanism debt]]." SV:100 asks the question a selection theorist should want asked: if the causal job is biasing indeterminacies, "a much 'thinner' consciousness could do the job." LWM:54 prices the practical promise at the register's own figure (P-VS1, credence low, grade D; confirmed against `positions/value-in-selection` L51) and states the compensation consequence in the lead paragraph instead of a footnote. MMS:110 and MMS:112 ("gains context", "gains a natural geometry") are the weakest material for this persona: both stack a filter hypothesis on a higher-dimensional hypothesis and report the stack as something the tenet gains.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

TV is the wing's phenomenological centre. TV:82 notices that the void "is not typically experienced as a limit", and TV:84 says why: consciousness presents "like a programme with no visible boot sequence." TV:86 then separates three moods the tradition is usually allowed to blur — Heidegger's *Angst*, Sartrean nausea, and Levinas's horror of the *il y a*, set "to the fear of nothingness". PV:40 gives the wing's sharpest single sentence on first-person method: "The verification procedure manufactures the evidence it seeks." MMS:49 uses Nagel correctly and then marks the border of his authority: "the inference that physical methods cannot in principle survey mind-space is the Map's, drawn from the dualism of its tenets rather than from Nagel's own naturalism."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

LWM:108 uses Whitehead for what he can supply: value as "constitutive of what experience *is*", with each occasion "steering its own subjective aim". LWM:110 draws the consequence that makes the section worth keeping: "Meaning is concentrated by transience, not diluted by it." Nothing in the wing uses process-philosophical resonance to move an organism or system up the five-tier scale, so the constraint on this persona is not engaged. WV:85 is quietly process-friendly too: identification as "partly enacted rather than held as an attitude", so that asking about it "converts it into a represented identification."

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane would value LWM:164 because it concedes the most and keeps the instruction: "decline the posit and you still have every branch-local reason to deliberate with care." He would also value LWM:94, which admits that "the libertarian framing's distinguishing work here is tenet-coherence rather than unique moral explanatory power." WV is the wing's real gift to him. Its claim at WV:87 that "The conjunction is a closure, not a sum of inconveniences" gives the self-forming-action theorist a problem he has to answer: a will that sets itself cannot check, at the time, that the setting is its own. LWM never mentions it (see Cross-Linking).

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

TV:70 handles McGinn better than most pages in the corpus: his thesis "is a separate, naturalist thesis", he "leaves open whether that closure is absolute", and his argument "concerns an explanatory problem rather than a protective function". MMS:66 does the same and quotes his self-description ("naturalistic but not constructive"). PV:116's distinction between *evidential* and *definitional* unfalsifiability is a contribution McGinn would want: it says which kind of closure PV is, and concedes it "is not offered as a positive metaphysical finding."

**D1 (lens a, confirmed). PV:66 credits Tye with an argument about richness, and cites the wrong work.** The article: "Michael Tye presses further: if consciousness were merely an illusion of richness, then nothing would *appear* any way at all." The reference list gives Tye (2009), *Consciousness Revisited*. The research note (`research/voids-plenitude-void-2026-02-22` L113, L246) took the argument from Tye's essay "The Grand Illusion" in *Inference* and gave it as "Vol. 6, No. 3". The fetched essay is Vol. 4, No. 1 (May 2018), a review of Dennett's *From Bacteria to Bach and Back*, and its sentence is: "But if consciousness is an illusion, then nothing can appear any way at all." Tye's target is the claim that consciousness itself is an illusion. The words "of richness" are the Map's, and they move the argument onto a thesis it does not touch: a sparse-experience theorist grants appearances and disputes only how much detail they carry. The essay contains no instance of "change blind". So the paragraph misstates the target, the reference entry is to a different work, and the research note's volume number is also wrong. The sentence occurs on no other page (obsidian/ and archive/ grepped).
