## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical issues found and fixed

**1. L-creativity / C-creativity attributed to the wrong authors (named idea, wrong coiner).**
Body read: *"**L-creativity** (Hausman) holds that true creativity produces metaphysical novelty …
requiring 'underived origination.' **C-creativity** (Kronfeldner) holds that psychological novelty
suffices"*. The labels are **Gregg Caruso's**, from "Free Will Skepticism and the Question of
Creativity: Creativity, Desert, and Self-Creation", *Ergo* 3(23), 591–607 (2016), §2: "The first,
which I will call L-creativity, employs a libertarian conception of free will and is rooted in the
notion of underived origination. Kane defines underived origination as follows (1996: 79)".
"Underived origination" is therefore **Kane's** term, and the "L" and "C" stand for libertarian and
compatibilist. A grep of the full text (soft hyphens stripped) returns **zero** hits for both
"Hausman" and "Kronfeldner". Caruso also defends neither pole: he introduces a third,
"S-creativity", consistent with free-will scepticism.

Origin of the error: the research note
`research/consciousness-creativity-novelty-generation-2026-01-19` lists the Caruso URL as its
source and places two bullets about Hausman and Kronfeldner under it. The article then moved the
labels onto those two names and dropped Caruso and Kane entirely. Five reviews certified Hausman
1984 and Kronfeldner 2009 as `real-correct` (true of the metadata) without asking whose labels
these were.

**Resolution**: paragraph rewritten to credit the labels to Caruso (2016) and the quoted phrase to
Kane (1996), with Hausman and Kronfeldner kept as kindred views on each side, and Caruso's own
sceptical stance stated in a clause. "The Map's framework offers a middle path" was also wrong on
the page's own terms (quantum openness for consciousness to exploit is the libertarian side, as
the research note itself records) and now reads "The Map sides with the first by a specific
route". Caruso 2016 and Kane 1996 added to References.

**2. Jordanous finding cited to the wrong work.** The FACE result ("ranked musical improvisation
systems in the opposite order to other evaluation methods") is cited by Lamb et al. to
**Jordanous 2012b**, the Sussex PhD dissertation, and the article's References carried
**Jordanous 2012a**, the *Cognitive Computation* paper, which Lamb et al. cite in the next sentence
for SPECS. References entry replaced with the dissertation; inline year added.

**3. Unsupported universal in the machine-creativity section.** *"two decades of work has not
produced one [operationalisation] that a random-search-and-retrieval system fails"*. The source
supports far less: RASTER was aimed at Ritchie's criteria and described as meeting the Tripod;
nothing in Lamb et al. says it passes SPECS, FACE or anything else, and Lamb et al. say RASTER
partly works by rendering the inspiring-set criteria *inapplicable*. Re-scoped to "the two criterion
sets tested against a random-search-and-retrieval system both failed to exclude it, while the
field's own survey reports no agreed replacement". A tenet-accepting reviewer would have flagged
the original, so this is calibration and not bedrock.

### Medium issues

- **Bowden et al. (2005) and the four "experiential markers".** The article credited suddenness,
  certainty, positive affect and altered agency to Bowden et al. The full text is paywalled and was
  not retrieved; the abstract supports only the sudden "Aha!" framing. Attribution narrowed to
  suddenness and immediate confidence, with the four-marker profile left as the article's own.
  **This is a lead, not a confirmed defect** (see Remaining Items).
- **Hausman's publication year.** The SUNY Press catalogue gives a June 1985 release for the SUNY
  edition (first edition Nijhoff 1975); the corpus cites 1984 throughout. Not changed: a 1984
  copyright with a 1985 release is ordinary, and the catalogue page alone does not settle it.
- **Bergson quote subject.** The body writes that durée "is 'invention or it is nothing at all'";
  Bergson's subject in *Creative Evolution* is "time". The splice sits outside the quotation marks
  and durée is Bergson's real time, so it stands.
