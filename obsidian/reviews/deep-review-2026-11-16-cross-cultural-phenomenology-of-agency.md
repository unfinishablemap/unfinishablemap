## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Thompson (2007) cited for a claim the book places outside its scope.** The page said the Eastern positions "shape how practitioners actually experience their own actions (Thompson, 2007)". *Mind in Life* (archive.org full text, whitespace collapsed) has zero hits for wu wei and says of the Buddhist and Hindu analyses based on contemplative mental training: "The relationship of these schools to Western phenomenology and mind science is beyond the scope of this book, and will be the subject of future work." Resolution: citation removed; the sentence now says the traditions pair their positions with practices meant to change how action is experienced, and the following sentence cites Nave et al. (2021) for a measured change in the sense of agency during meditation. Thompson (2007) was cited nowhere else in the body, so its References entry was dropped.

2. **Markus & Kitayama (1991) misdescribed, and its most relevant sentence omitted.** The page called it "studies of agency attribution" finding that collectivist cultures "distribute agency across relationships, social roles, and situational contexts", with "internal locus of agency". The paper is a theoretical review of independent and interdependent self-construals; "locus" occurs only in a reference-list title. What it says about agency (full text, MIT-hosted PDF via pdftotext): "for those with independent selves, agency will be experienced as an effort to express one's internal needs, rights, and capacities and to withstand undue social pressure, whereas among those with interdependent selves, agency will be experienced as an effort to be receptive to others, to adjust to their needs and demands, and to restrain one's own inner needs or desires." It also says an interdependent view does not imply "that people do not have a sense of themselves as agents who are the origins of their own actions." Resolution: sentence rewritten to report the paper's own proposal with two verbatim spans, plus the origins-of-action sentence, which bears directly on the page's invariant.

3. **The central invariant was asserted as exceptionless against published counter-evidence.** Three passages said Buddhist practitioners "still report" the felt difference between acting and being acted upon, and the Tenet 3 paragraph said of cultures that dissolve the agent reporting its phenomenal absence: "They do not." No source was cited. Lindahl et al. (2017, PLoS ONE full text) report from interviews with 60 Western Buddhist meditators: "Practitioners also reported a loss of sense of agency—or the loss of a 'doer' of actions—in relation to automatic actions such as crying, to habitual actions such as walking, and to typically intentional actions such as speaking." A reviewer who accepts every tenet would still flag the universal, so this is a calibration error and not a framework-boundary disagreement. Resolution: the first-invariant paragraph now separates the doctrinal denial of a self from a denial of the felt contrast, reports the Lindahl finding with two verbatim spans, and scopes the invariant to ordinary experience; the irreducibility paragraph and the Tenet 3 paragraph carry the same scope. The Tenet 3 inference is narrowed from "tells against cultural confabulation" to "tells against confabulation by doctrine alone while leaving open that training reshapes the experience". The existing epiphenomenalist-parity clause is untouched.

4. **Meta-description contradicted the body's calibration.** It promised what the invariants "reveal about consciousness as genuinely causal"; the Tenet 3 paragraph says the persistence is not evidence of causal efficacy. Resolution: description now reads "what cross-cultural invariants in that experience can and cannot show about consciousness".
