## Executive Summary

This is one of the best-calibrated clusters in the corpus. It engages its strongest physicalist rival by name, concedes empirical co-extensiveness rather than claiming a discriminator, and propagates its central concession cleanly across five articles without dilution. The opportunity is almost entirely connective: the cluster **independently re-derives the Map's own `evidential-status-discipline` five times over and cites it once in eight articles**, so the corpus cannot see that its flagship dualism-mechanism wing is its best worked exhibit of the discipline's hardest branch. Three concrete, cap-free follow-ups are named below; no new articles are proposed.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This persona's verdict is the load-bearing one for this cluster, and it is strongly positive.** The `evidential-status-discipline` installs a Strongest-Physicalist-Rival Gate (L140–142): every empirically-loaded article must name the single strongest physicalist rival that natively explains its core evidence and either confront it with a named discriminator *or honestly concede co-extensiveness*. The second branch is the harder one to take, because it costs the article its evidence. This cluster takes it, repeatedly and unprompted:

- `skill-delegation` L76 names the rival mechanism (explicit-monitoring reinvestment, over-arousal), cites its substrate (Smoulder et al. 2024), and then **refuses the obvious rescue**: the interventions that alter choking "were *designed from* the explicit-monitoring and distraction models, and Gröpel and Mesagno (2019) catalogue their effects across 47 studies as those models' own successes." An article looking for evidence would have counted those 47 studies as support. This one hands them to the opponent.
- `interface-friction` L78 generalises the concession to article scope: "As with every claim in this article, this is an interpretive fit rather than an empirically distinguishable prediction... the interface-friction reading redescribes that established result under a dualist ontology rather than discriminating against it."
- `delegatory-dualism` L158 declines to bank its own falsifier: observational closure "is a *constitutive* constraint of the theory... So this is not an operative falsifier." L162 then nets out that the report-tracking and function-tracking advantages "presuppose rather than support the choice of dualism."
- `delegatory-causation` L130 turns the discipline on the Map itself with a *tu quoque*: "appealing to theoretical virtues when empirical evidence is unavailable is a move the Map elsewhere criticises physicalists for making... If that move is suspect when physicalists make it, it looks suspect when dualists make it too."
- `philosophy-of-habit-under-dualism` L85 marks its central interpretive move as exactly that: Ravaisson's "descent" gloss is "held as a live position calibrated to the Map's tenets rather than a finding the data compel."

This is the *"tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating"* pattern executed five times without the vocabulary. **Tenet-as-evidence-upgrade is praise-worthily not done here**: the cluster had every structural opportunity to convert delegation's coherence into evidential support and explicitly declined each time.

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`delegatory-dualism` reconstructs Saad's five constraints faithfully and, crucially, keeps Part I (Saad's theory) and Part II (Map integration) behind an explicit firewall at L194: "These connections are not claims made by Saad. The integration is exploratory — each framework must stand independently before the combination has force, and combining two speculative frameworks multiplies assumptions rather than reducing them." That last clause is the one most integration-minded articles omit.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The Born-rule identification (`delegatory-causation` L140–150) is the cluster's most ambitious move and is correctly flagged as Map-specific, not Saad's. Stapp would applaud the ambition; the article's own L172 supplies the caution he would resist — that quantum biasing is "difference-making and gives up strong causal closure, whereas delegatory trumping is difference-making-free and preserves it," so the two are "distinct and potentially competing mechanisms," not one process. An article eager for convergence would have suppressed that tension.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`philosophy-of-habit-under-dualism` centres the first-person register properly: the felt shift between absorbed and self-aware execution, the "second nature" that Proust says "prevents us from knowing our first."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Habit's transactional treatment (Dewey, Malafouris, L47–53) avoids crude substance dualism by locating habit's traces in worn tools and grooved paths, not only neurons. Note per this skill's constraint that this praise **must not cash out as a tier-upgrade**, and the article does not invite one: the Peirce section (L73–77) cites "the shape of habit-taking and explicitly declines the metaphysics," refusing the panpsychist reading that would have been the easy tenet-friendly move. Process Philosopher and Hardline Empiricist converge here rather than conflicting, which is the sign the article resolved the tension honestly.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`skill-delegation`'s retained-override phase (L58–62) gives agency a concrete, observable form.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`delegatory-dualism` L96 is the cluster's finest moment of humility: the default causal profile "is the load-bearing primitive of the whole theory, yet its only access route — the zombie functional-duplicate — depends on a contested metaphysical possibility... It earns its keep by the coherence it buys, not by independent observation of the entity it quantifies over." An article naming its own load-bearing primitive as a postulate is doing what McGinn asks.

#### 2. `delegatory-causation` never adjudicates whether its own inverse supplies a fourth access route

The article's "The Counterfactual Character" (L79) asks "how can a counterfactual serve as the foundation for a causal theory?" and answers with exactly three routes — **functional specification** (L81), **zombie thought experiment** (L83), **physical law** (L85). Each is a way of characterising retained-but-unexercised causal capacity that is never observed.

Eighty lines later, "The Inverse: Delegation From Consciousness" (L161) asserts that skill delegation shares "the crucial feature of preemption — one causal source does the work while the other retains capacity without exercising it." And `skill-delegation`'s comparison table (L104) makes the asymmetry explicit:

> | Evidence of retention | Theoretical (preemption structure) | Empirical (choking, override) |

So the article states, in one place, that retained-but-unexercised capacity is in principle unobservable, and in another that in the mirror direction it is *empirically* observed. **It never says whether that transfers.** The honest answer is most likely no — the relata differ (agent-level executive control versus a sub-personal physical causal profile), and an expert re-engaging conscious control is not a physical state exhibiting its pre-delegation profile. But that disanalogy is informative and currently unstated, and the alternative — that the inverse genuinely supplies a fourth, empirically-grounded route into the theory's most-conceded primitive — would be a significant result. Either way the article should say which.

This is a genuine unexploited asset: the cluster owns both halves and has never put them in the same paragraph.

The flagship exposition of the theory is thus missing the two articles that supply its only empirically-accessible instances — precisely the material a reader arriving at the abstract mechanism most needs.
