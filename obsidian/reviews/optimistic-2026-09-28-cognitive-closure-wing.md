## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`consciousness-defeats-explanation` L84-90 makes the reflexive gap precise. Explanation produces understanding; understanding is a phenomenal state; so any explanation of why explanations work must itself be understood. The heterophenomenology reply is met on its own terms (L90): the investigator must *understand* the reports, so "the method presupposes, in the investigator, precisely the phenomenal capacity it denies in the subject." Chalmers would also value L112, where three rival theories each score as "simplest" on their own preferred dimension. That is the parsimony problem stated without special pleading.

`cross-domain-void-comparison` L128 is the passage Chalmers would single out, and for its restraint. The phenomenal core stands outside the Lawvere fixed-point structure that unifies the deepest physics and mathematics voids. The page then refuses the inference: "that a class of voids is not captured by *one* formal mechanism does not show it is not formal at *all*, and not-formal-at-all would not show it is non-physical." The resistance is "*compatible with*, not *evidence for*" dualism.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The wing is not about mechanism, so Stapp's praise is for one methodological point. `naturally-occluded` L139 says what the second tenet predicts about its own detectability: if consciousness biases outcomes below the threshold of introspection, the absence of a felt mechanism is the expected absence, not an anomaly. Stapp would recognise this as the right shape of claim. He would also note, with the page (L87, L111), that it is filed at *live hypothesis* and that the page names the risk of "turning an apparent absence-of-evidence into the expected absence the framework requires." The claim is stated, its unfalsifiability edge is named, and the tier is held.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`phenomenology-of-cognitive-limit-types` L43-49 is the wing's best first-person work. Frustration announces a wall; satisfaction disguises one as completion; illusory comprehension "signals *nothing at all*." Then L49: "Frustration is loud. Satisfaction is quiet. Illusory comprehension is silent." The fine-grained signatures that follow (L55-81) are each given a felt texture rather than a definition: the ladder that dissolves beneath you (L57), being "trapped by one's own understanding" (L61), oscillation rather than paralysis (L65), *knowing* you are wrong while *experiencing* that you are right (L69).

`necessary-opacity` L98-104 does the same for its own void. The snap-back (L100), in which naive realism reasserts itself almost immediately, is read as evidence *for* the transparency mechanism rather than against the worry. And L88 refuses the easy reading of psychedelic noesis: "Did they encounter destabilising truth the system then sealed away, or did the destabilised state merely *feel* like insight? The phenomenology cannot distinguish these possibilities."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

`epistemology-of-limit-knowledge` L99-111 proposes that limit-knowledge may be *phenomenally constituted*: the felt encounter with closure partly constitutes the recognition that a limit exists, rather than merely reporting it. Whitehead would welcome the idea that a limit is "known *by being felt*" (L99). The page keeps three options open (constituted and reliable; constituted but unreliable; accompanied but not constituted) and calls the thesis "a live hypothesis, not a settled Map commitment" (L103).

`naturally-occluded` L91 supplies a process-friendly criterion for the whole category: a void is Naturally Occluded only where "the void's *opacity is itself the function the void serves*." The opacity is an activity the system performs, not a gap in it.

This persona's praise **does not** extend to the "grounds" clause at `epistemology-of-limit-knowledge` L111 (see Calibration concern 5).

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

The wing's contribution to agency is negative and honest. `naturally-occluded` L87 and `biological-cognitive-closure` L139 both say that if consciousness has a causal role, that role is a candidate for adaptive concealment, so the agent's inability to see its own interface is expected rather than embarrassing. Kane would accept the point and add that L87 keeps the claim where it belongs: the category "predict[s] the structural invisibility a sub-threshold mental contribution would have," and does not "claim that visibility has been measured and found absent." `necessary-opacity` L114 also separates the *representational* opacity this wing studies from the *causal* opacities (amplification, agency) that bear on whether the agent caused anything. Keeping the two apart stops the agency question from being answered by the perception question.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

This is McGinn's wing, and the pages treat him better than most of the literature does. `biological-cognitive-closure` L137 says plainly that "McGinn's own conclusion is naturalist, not dualist," that the Map "borrows the epistemic claim about closure, then argues separately," and that "the metaphysical step is the Map's; McGinn would resist it." `closure-types-void` L40 quotes his 1989 definition verbatim and then does what his critics rarely do: it separates the representational reading (what can be *framed*) from the psychological reading (what can be *assembled*), shows that Kriegel's incoherence argument bites only the second (L48-50), and that Vlerick and Boudry's historical argument bites only the inference from the second to the first (L52). Both collapses fail; the distinction survives (L54). `epistemology-of-limit-knowledge` L47-49 makes the same cut from the side of the question, with Chomsky's problems/mysteries and Demircioglu's child who can understand "where is x?" without the concepts some answers require.

McGinn's deepest point in the wing is the second-order void (`closure-types-void` L68-76). Representational closure "is, by its own definition, undetectable" (L72). The history of apparent representational closures that turned out to be psychological "does not prove there are no genuine representational closures. It shows that minds are unreliable judges of which is which" (L74). The catalogue is then reframed (L92) as "a catalogue of *suspected* closures, most of which are probably psychological with pockets of genuine representational limit." That is mysterianism with its confidence calibrated.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

The wing makes no claims about minimal organisms, so the persona's main test does not apply. Its praise goes to four pieces of restraint, which together make this the best-calibrated wing I have reviewed:

- **`epistemology-of-limit-knowledge` L129.** "Closure undercuts over-confident reduction; it does not positively establish dualism. The possibility that we are closed to a solution is not evidence that the solution is dualist—defeater-removal is not the same as positive evidence, and the possibility of closure is not the probability that any particular metaphysics is correct." This is the pattern *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating*, stated in the Map's own words at the point where the temptation is strongest. The 2026-08-19 deep review marks this language as not to be trimmed; I concur.
- **`cross-domain-void-comparison`, whole page.** The article designed a test, ran it, and reported the result as *coherence, not discrimination* four times (L37, L100, L122, L124). It admits the bar was applied asymmetrically and that "the asymmetry inflates any apparent difference" (L43). It reports that its second axis rests on "an inventory built from the very cases it then finds special" (L100). And it reports a difference that cuts the other way (L104-114): the Map's own Lawvere work makes physics and mathematics voids *less* independent than the apex claimed, which "shrinks the abductive base" (L110). Reporting that the catalogue's distinctive part is "the smaller set of phenomenal voids—precisely the voids where dualist and physicalist or illusionist readings are evidentially tied" is exactly the restraint this persona values.
- **`naturally-occluded` L107-121.** Every claim in the page carries a tier: FBT at the perceptual site *strongly supported* with the model-to-world transfer marked contested; the extensions beyond perception *realistic possibility, contested*; every specific catalogue assignment *live hypothesis*. L113 names the slippage ("from *the category permits the adaptive reading* to *the adaptive reading is supported by evidence*"), and L115-119 name the three evidence classes that would earn an upgrade. L111 adds an external test that could go *against* the page: convergent recurrence of the introspection-architecture signatures in silicon systems never under selection would favour generic architectural opacity over selection-maintained occlusion. The 2026-09-26 outer review asked the page to state its prediction for non-evolved systems; it now does.
- **`apophatic-cartography-four-criteria` L104 and L118.** The page reports that the one exhibit scored against a pre-registered rubric landed *moderate / moderate / weak / weak* despite a surface that invited a strong reading, and then withholds the conclusion it could have drawn: the non-flatness claim is "*provisional with a rubric, pending independent grading*," because "one author wrote the rubric and applied it." L118 gives the cluster-level translation of the evidential-status rule: "the criteria may identify a cluster's eligibility for evidential weight but must not upgrade the cluster's independence score beyond what its anchors can supply."

Also worth naming: `biological-cognitive-closure` L127-129 lists the WEIRD-sampling caveat and four falsifiers, and L61 says convergence "provides evidence" and limits are "likely" built in (the 2026-07-30 pessimistic review of `mysterianism` found that page firming this to "shows ... universally"; the source is still calibrated).

Against this, the same persona flags `phenomenology-of-cognitive-limit-types` L123 and `necessary-opacity` L152 (Calibration concerns 2 and 3).

### High Priority

#### A third closure type: adaptive occlusion
- **Builds on**: `closure-types-void` L34 and L38-44; `naturally-occluded` L57
- **Would address**: `closure-types-void` opens with "Every voids article makes one of two claims" (representational or psychological). `naturally-occluded` L57 describes a third: "The cognitive system *could* in principle access the territory — there is no architectural impossibility — but was shaped not to." The second-order void applies to it with extra force, since a limit that feels like completion is the hardest kind to diagnose from inside. One section in `closure-types-void` (headroom 1013), one sentence in `naturally-occluded` L57 (headroom 338; keep it to a clause), reciprocal links.

- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits (three kinds are less simple than two, and the simpler story is wrong in a way that matters), with no tier change anywhere.
