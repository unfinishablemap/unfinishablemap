# Independent critical audit: “Phenomenology of Agency vs Passivity”

## Overall verdict

The article is **substantially better calibrated than its title and opening suggest**. It now repeatedly concedes that the phenomenology is underdetermining: Desmurget’s stimulation findings fit physicalist comparator models; the Schwartz OCD result fits ordinary neuroplasticity; depersonalisation supplies only a reading-dependent separation; Everettian accounts preserve the entire branch-relative phenomenology; and the Map’s selection interpretation is explicitly called framework-internal. Those concessions prevent the article from being a simple phenomenology-to-dualism argument. [[topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity|topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity]]

Nevertheless, the article remains **stronger as a descriptive inventory than as evidence for interactionist dualism**. Its principal weaknesses are:

1. **A clear citation–claim mismatch in the meditation section.**
2. **A materially outdated and overconfident treatment of intentional binding.**
3. **Only token engagement with contemporary comparator, cue-integration, predictive-processing and active-inference models.**
4. **Conflation of objective control, felt agency, explicit attribution, effort and ownership.**
5. **Several phenomenological generalisations—about flow, dreams, hypnosis and utilisation behaviour—that either lack supporting evidence or are stated too categorically.**
6. **Inconsistent propagation of calibrations already present elsewhere on the site.**

My resulting evidential verdict is:

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Bibliographic integrity | Generally good; no obvious fabricated reference |
| Claim–source fidelity | Mixed; two significant overextensions and several smaller ones |
| Phenomenological precision | Useful but insufficiently multidimensional |

| Currency against 2020s literature | Weak |
| Tenet calibration | Often commendably explicit, but not consistently maintained |

The article establishes that agency phenomenology is structured, dissociable and scientifically tractable. It does **not** establish that this structure registers a non-physical causal contribution.

# 2. Citation and empirical-claim audit

## Schwartz et al. 1996: substantially accurate, with an interpretive gloss

The underlying study involved nine OCD patients scanned before and after ten weeks of structured exposure-and-response-prevention behavioural and cognitive treatment, with comparison to an earlier nine-person sample. Responders showed bilateral decreases in caudate glucose metabolism. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8629886/)

The article’s description of patients “exert[ing] sustained mental effort to reframe intrusive thoughts” is a plausible therapeutic gloss, but it is not how the experiment operationalised or measured the intervention. The study measured treatment and symptom response, not an independent quantity of phenomenal effort. The article commendably concedes the small, uncontrolled design and that ordinary therapy-induced plasticity predicts the result equally well.

**Verdict:** fair as an example of psychological treatment accompanying neural change; not evidence that conscious effort is an additional causal force.

---

## Rajan et al. 2019: correct experiment, unsupported extension to subjective agency

Rajan and colleagues compared internally chosen versus externally instructed spatial-attention cues in two experiments. They found greater frontal theta power from roughly 500 ms, increased frontoparietal theta coherence and delayed posterior alpha lateralisation in the choice condition. The authors interpreted this as a neural signature of frontal decision-making and communication during willed attention. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29931088/)

The study did **not** independently measure the participants’ subjective sense of agency. Consequently, the article’s reference to a relation between the frontal-theta signature “and subjective agency” is not supported by Rajan et al. What the study directly contrasts is choice plus its additional decision demands against instruction.

That does not make the result irrelevant. It means the supported claim is:

> Internally selecting an attentional target recruits additional decision-related dynamics.

It does not establish:

> Those dynamics covary with phenomenal agency, still less that phenomenal agency causes them.

The Map’s *Volitional Control* article already recognises this sort of decision-load confound more carefully and should supply the canonical wording.

**Verdict:** real and relevant source; body claim overextended.

---

## Desmurget et al. 2009: core report accurate, limitations underreported

The article correctly reports the striking result: in seven awake neurosurgical patients, parietal stimulation produced intentions or desires to move and, at higher intensities, false beliefs that movement had occurred despite no EMG activity; premotor stimulation produced overt movements that patients denied having made. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19423830/)

Its modest conclusion is also right: this refutes only a crude theory on which the sense of agency is a readout of completed movement. It does not refute architectures in which motor execution, predicted consequences, action awareness and explicit attribution are computed in partially distinct systems.

Two cautions should be added:

- The sample consisted of seven surgical patients.
- A published commentary noted that direct electrical stimulation may deactivate or disrupt local processing rather than straightforwardly “activate” a normal function, limiting reverse inference from the stimulation site. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20203033/)

The sibling *Volitional Control* page already includes both caveats and concludes that the result is theory-constraining but does not choose between physicalism and dualism. [[topics/volitional-control|topics/volitional-control]]

**Verdict:** essentially accurate; import the fuller calibration from the sibling article.

---

## Della Sala et al. 1991 and anarchic hand: broadly supported

The ownership–agency dissociation is one of the article’s better examples. Later historical and clinical reviews confirm that Della Sala and colleagues preferred “anarchic hand” precisely because patients with involuntary, apparently purposeful hand actions did not ordinarily deny ownership of the limb. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1775229/)

The wording that patients deny that the actions are “theirs” is potentially ambiguous: it should mean **they deny authorship or control**, not that they deny bodily ownership. The modern literature also shows considerable terminological and clinical heterogeneity under “alien hand,” so the article should specify that it is discussing the anarchic-hand profile rather than every syndrome given the alien-hand label.

A further limitation is inferential. A preserved limb-ownership judgement combined with disrupted action control supports functional and phenomenological dissociation. It does not, by itself, establish neurologically encapsulated “modules,” much less a non-physical missing component.

**Verdict:** supported, with a terminology clarification needed.

---

## Gallagher and thought insertion: a legitimate reading, not a secure dissociation

Gallagher’s agency–ownership distinction is real and influential. But the article overstates its status by describing thought insertion as a secure case of ownership preserved with agency lost.

Competing accounts argue that:

- ownership is primarily disturbed, with agency loss following from it;
- the agency/ownership distinction does not illuminate the phenomenon;
- the alien feature belongs to thought content or to the type of intentional state;
- the wider disturbance concerns subjectivity or for-me-ness rather than a neatly missing agency signal. [ScienceDirect+2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053810012002310)

The ordinary-intrusive-thought objection is particularly damaging. Many thoughts arise without any felt production or selection but are not experienced as inserted. Therefore, absence of felt initiation cannot by itself explain thought insertion. The dedicated Map article acknowledges precisely this problem. [[concepts/thought-insertion|concepts/thought-insertion]]

The correct wording would be:

> On the Gallagher-style agency reading, thought insertion is a candidate case of ownership or subjectivity surviving while felt authorship is disturbed; rival readings locate the primary disturbance elsewhere.

**Verdict:** source represented as more decisive than the literature permits.

---

## Sass and Parnas 2003: fair

The article’s placement of thought insertion within Sass and Parnas’s wider account of schizophrenia as an ipseity disturbance is accurate. Their model centres on hyperreflexivity and diminished self-affection or self-presence, rather than treating individual passivity symptoms in isolation. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14609238/)

The article could, however, take the implication more seriously. If passivity symptoms arise within a broad alteration of self-presence, the cases become less supportive of a clean, independently removable “agency component.”

**Verdict:** accurate, but insufficiently integrated into the subsequent inference.

---

## Lutz et al. 2008: the clearest citation mismatch

The article says experienced meditators report that the agency–passivity distinction becomes sharper and attributes this to Lutz et al. The cited paper does not report that result. It distinguishes:

- focused-attention meditation, involving voluntary attention to a chosen object; and
- open-monitoring meditation, involving non-reactive monitoring of moment-to-moment experience.

Its abstract discusses possible regulatory effects on attention, emotion, brain and behaviour. It does not report a study in which experienced practitioners discriminated agency from passivity more sharply, nor does it infer that training verifies the causal content of agency phenomenology. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18329323/)

This is not a metadata error. It is a **claim-entailment error**: a paper that distinguishes two practices has been treated as evidence that practitioners’ discrimination between two phenomenal modes becomes more accurate.

Later work offers more relevant but also more complicated evidence:

- a 2019 review argues that mindfulness may improve metacognitive access to intentions, while critically examining whether timing and binding measures really index agency;
- a preregistered study of 46 long-term meditators found that some advanced states included suspension of agency and dissolution of a localised first-person perspective;
- phenomenological work on self-boundary dissolution reports a range from deliberate attentional control to surrender and passivity. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30912626/)

These sources might justify saying that contemplative training permits unusually fine reports about intention, control, passivity and selfhood. They would not justify the further claim that the reports reveal genuine non-physical causal contribution.

This problem is especially revealing methodologically. The site’s July citation-fidelity review declared Lutz “real-correct” because it supported the focused-attention/open-monitoring framing, then concluded that there was no source/Map conflation and that the article had reached “durable convergence.” That review verified metadata and thematic relevance but did not test whether the source entailed the article’s stronger empirical assertion. [[reviews/deep-review-2026-07-17-phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity|reviews/deep-review-2026-07-17-phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity]]

**Verdict:** citation should be replaced or the sentence substantially weakened.

## Intentional binding: historically grounded, presently overstated

The article accurately describes the original 2002 result: voluntary actions and their effects shifted toward one another in perceived time, whereas the original involuntary TMS condition did not show the same pattern. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11896397/)

But the current wording—calling binding a non-introspective measure of agency and saying involuntary movements do not bind—is no longer adequate. Subsequent work has shown that:

- temporal binding can occur in passive observation of cause–effect sequences;
- causal belief and contingency can produce binding without voluntary agency;
- once predictability is matched, some studies find no additional voluntary-action effect;
- different binding procedures can diverge psychometrically;
- a 2025 meta-analysis of 75 studies nevertheless found modestly greater action binding and greater outcome binding for more volitional conditions;
- a 2026 review concludes that binding reflects a mixture of perceptual and cognitive processes rather than a unitary agency measure. [PubMed+4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32419274/)

The balanced conclusion is therefore not that binding is unrelated to agency. It is:

> Volition is one moderator of temporal binding, but binding is neither unique to voluntary action nor a validated direct measure of phenomenal authorship.

The statement that an epiphenomenal consciousness would have “no reason” to distort temporal perception is also invalid as stated. A physicalist or epiphenomenalist can propose that neural causal-inference and motor-prediction mechanisms produce both the perceptual shift and the experience or report of agency. Consciousness need not perform the functional work for the covariation to be systematic. The article concedes this common-cause reply one paragraph earlier, then fails to apply it to binding.

The schizophrenia claim needs more precision as well. Current reviews find both under-attribution and over-attribution depending on symptoms, task, measure and available cues; predictive-coding accounts attempt to reconcile those apparently conflicting results. Simply saying binding is “altered” is safe but provides little positive support for the article’s comparator story. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38925210/)

The sibling *Volitional Control* page already gives a much better verdict: binding remains suggestive, but its construct validity and relation to explicit agency are contested. [[topics/volitional-control|topics/volitional-control]]

**Verdict:** this is the article’s most consequential stale empirical section.

## Lhermitte 1983: behavioural datum, not established passive phenomenology

Lhermitte reported that visual and tactile presentation of familiar objects could compel patients with frontal lesions to grasp and use them; the patients behaved as though presentation implied an instruction. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6850269/)

That supports stimulus-bound action and impaired inhibition. It does not establish that the patients experienced “passivity in the face of affordances” or lacked an agentic phenomenology. The article moves from observed behaviour and lesion anatomy to an unmeasured first-person state.

A defensible formulation would distinguish:

- **behavioural control:** actions are abnormally captured by environmental affordances;
- **subjective agency:** presently uncertain unless supported by patient reports or an agency task.

**Verdict:** behavioural description accurate; phenomenological conclusion unsupported by the cited source.

---

## Wegner: represented fairly

The article gives Wegner’s challenge appropriate weight. Wegner and Wheatley proposed that the experience of conscious will depends on the priority, consistency and exclusivity of action-related thoughts and showed that people can misattribute forced actions to themselves under suitable cueing. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10424155/)

The article correctly uses helping-hands and related paradigms to establish underdetermination rather than treating them as proof that all agency is illusory. Its acknowledgement that felt authorship can occur without the relevant causal control is one of the strongest parts of the article.

**Verdict:** fair and well integrated.

# 3. Uncited or insufficiently supported phenomenological claims

## Flow

The article rightly recognises flow as a challenge to a simple effort–agency identification. Flow research commonly characterises the state in terms of absorption, effort-less control and intrinsic reward, while recent active-inference work offers a physicalist explanation of reduced self-awareness combined with fluent, skilled control. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34898235/)

The article’s answer—that “consciousness in flow still selects” but encounters no resistance—is not an empirical finding. It is a protective framework stipulation. No independent criterion is offered for identifying this allegedly continuing selection once felt effort and explicit control have disappeared.

The better conclusion is:

> Flow dissociates effort, reflective self-awareness, objective skilled control and perhaps felt authorship. It therefore argues for a multidimensional model of agency, not for an unseen form of conscious selection.

---

## Dreams

The contrast between ordinary and lucid dreaming is too categorical. Lucidity—awareness that one is dreaming—and control over dream content are dissociable. A large 2026 study distinguished lucid dreams with and without agency, and a 2026 review emphasises that many lucid dreamers cannot reliably control dream content and that control can be agentic or expectation-driven. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42728107/)

Thus “lucid dreams restore the agency pole” should become:

> Lucidity can enable increased agency, but awareness, felt agency and control are partly dissociable.

Ordinary dreams also vary greatly in goal-directed activity and decision-making. Treating them as generally passive is a useful idealisation, not an established universal.

---

## Hypnosis

The phenomenology of hypnotically suggested movement is relevant, but “the sense of agency is absent” is too absolute. The experience typically involves reduced or altered agency, with substantial variation by suggestion and hypnotisability.

More importantly, there is a directly relevant predictive-coding model: suggested movement and experienced passivity are explained through attentional modulation of proprioceptive prediction plus a high-level prior of non-agency. A 2024 systematic review concludes that no consensus yet exists among hypnosis theories. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39032268/)

Hypnosis is therefore not merely a dissociation a physicalist must accommodate after the fact. It is a phenomenon for which the rival framework offers an explicit generative account.

---

## Habit and choking

The article treats disruption of skilled action by renewed attention as the “return of agency.” The empirical choking literature instead distinguishes several mechanisms, notably explicit monitoring, distraction and reinvestment of procedural knowledge. Which mechanism operates depends on task and pressure conditions. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21574739/)

“Conscious re-engagement” is too broad. Deliberate high-level attention can improve performance; narrow attention to automatised movement details can impair it. The result concerns the level and target of control, not consciousness versus its absence.

# 4. The article needs a multidimensional phenomenology

The central “axis” is heuristically useful but theoretically too coarse. The article itself concedes this when flow separates effort from causal contribution, and its disorder section later lists authorship, effort, control and ownership as separable components.

Pacherie’s cited paper explicitly describes agency phenomenology as having many facets, connected to a three-tiered model of intention and internal action control. Synofzik and colleagues further distinguish a non-conceptual feeling of agency from a conceptual judgement of agency and propose multifactorial weighting rather than a single comparator output. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17950720/)

At minimum the article should distinguish:

| Variable | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Initiation | Did I experience myself as starting this? |
| Motor control | Did the movement unfold as predicted? |
| Outcome control | Did my action cause the external result? |
| Selection | Did I choose among alternatives? |
| Effort | How costly or resistant did control feel? |
| Ownership | Did the body, action or thought feel mine? |
| Explicit attribution | On reflection, who do I judge caused it? |
| Objective control | How much causal influence did I actually have? |
| Self-awareness | Was the acting self thematically represented? |

Several current sentences move between these without warning:

- An involuntary memory can lack authorship while retaining full ownership.
- An automatic skill can involve little effort while preserving objective control and a background sense of agency.
- Flow can reduce reflective self-awareness while retaining a strong sense of fluent control.
- Thought insertion may involve alien ownership, agency, content or subjectivity depending on the account.
- A patient can explicitly deny agency even when first-order sensorimotor predictions are relatively intact, or vice versa.

The opening claim that every experience lies on one doing–undergoing axis should consequently be softened. The article has identified a **family of related contrasts**, not a demonstrated scalar dimension.

The assertion that passive experience is intrinsically present-oriented is also insufficiently defended. Anticipatory fear, involuntary future imagery and passive waiting can be future-directed; deliberate sensory exploration can be highly present-oriented. Likewise, counterfactual alternatives do not necessarily disappear once a decision is made.

# 5. The comparator and predictive-processing challenge

## The rival is stronger than a “separate circuit” reply

The article mentions that comparator or higher-order models predict Desmurget-style dissociations, but it does not present the contemporary rival in its strongest form.

A modern account is not simply:

> Compare an efference copy against feedback; match means self, mismatch means other.

It normally combines several levels:

1. **Prospective motor prediction:** expected sensory consequences generated before action.
2. **Selection fluency:** ease or conflict during action selection can influence agency before the outcome.
3. **Retrospective evidence:** actual sensory feedback, contingency and outcome congruence.
4. **Contextual priors:** beliefs, suggestions, social cues and expectations about who is acting.
5. **Reliability or precision weighting:** cues influence attribution according to their estimated reliability.
6. **Feeling versus judgement:** a first-order control signal can diverge from a later conceptual attribution.

Selection fluency has been shown to influence agency independently of outcome monitoring, while Bayesian models explain agency as precision-weighted integration of action signals, outcomes and causal priors. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30042845/)

Active inference extends this into a hierarchical model of perception and action: agents infer policies and act to bring sensory states into line with predictions or preferred outcomes. Agency can then be modelled through beliefs about controllability, policy precision and expected consequences rather than through a further non-physical selector. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24093015/)

## It offers unified explanations of the article’s own examples

Such an account can explain:

- **Desmurget:** stimulation perturbs systems generating intentions, motor predictions or movement awareness independently of execution.
- **Hypnosis:** suggestion supplies a strong prior of non-agency while attentional processes shape proprioceptive prediction.
- **Flow:** highly fluent policy execution and low salient prediction error reduce reflective effort and self-monitoring.
- **Habit:** well-learned lower-level policies require less high-level monitoring.
- **Intentional binding:** temporal estimates are influenced by predicted or believed causal relations.
- **Schizophrenia:** altered cue precision can generate under-attribution or over-attribution depending on task and symptom profile.
- **Meditation:** practice changes metacognitive access, attention and the precision assigned to self-related predictions without thereby validating their apparent metaphysical content.

A 2024 review specifically argues that predictive coding can reconcile conflicting findings of diminished and excessive agency attribution in schizophrenia. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38925210/)

This rival also directly answers one of the article’s central rhetorical questions: why effort and authorship attach to some demanding operations but not others. On a hierarchical-control account, only some processing is represented in the self-model, recruited for flexible control, broadcast for report, or assigned sufficient precision to enter metacognition. Unfelt processing need not be mysteriously excluded.

## The rival should not itself be treated as established

A fair audit should also resist turning predictive processing into an all-purpose victor. A recent review found only modest direct evidence for predictive coding and noted that active-inference studies often fit behaviour without comparing the theory against non-Bayesian or model-free alternatives. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38030100/)

The proper conclusion is therefore not that predictive processing has refuted dualism. It is that it currently provides a sufficiently developed physicalist competitor that the phenomena in this article cannot count as positive dualist evidence unless a discriminating prediction is supplied.

The Map’s *Volitional Control* page reaches essentially this correct verdict: current volitional evidence is close to an empirical draw between the interface interpretation and active inference. [[topics/volitional-control|topics/volitional-control]]

# 6. Problems in the argument against epiphenomenalism and illusionism

## Epiphenomenalism

The article’s challenge is strongest when framed as an explanatory demand: why should phenomenology track particular control states if phenomenology does no causal work?

But the argument repeatedly slides from:

> an epiphenomenalist owes an explanation,

to:

> an epiphenomenalist could not predict the pattern.

That stronger conclusion does not follow. An epiphenomenalist may hold that:

- neural control systems distinguish self-generated, automatic and externally generated events for functional reasons;
- those systems also generate reports and metacognitive representations;
- psychophysical laws associate the relevant neural states with different phenomenal states.

This can predict systematic phenomenal differences without phenomenal causation. The remaining dispute is then the general explanatory gap between physical organisation and experience, not a special empirical anomaly concerning agency.

The intentional-binding sentence makes the slide most clearly: the article first grants a common neural cause of control and experience, then says causally inert consciousness has no reason to distort time. The reason, on the rival account, belongs to the causally active prediction system; the accompanying experience need not itself perform the distortion.

## Illusionism

The meditation argument does not answer illusionism even apart from the Lutz citation problem. Improved discrimination under training can indicate a more refined functional self-model. It does not show that the model’s apparent reference to conscious causal efficacy is veridical.

The site’s *Witness Consciousness* article already states the correct reply: an illusionist can predict that training stabilises or refines the relevant self-model, so contemplative testimony is consonant with the Map but not evidence against its rivals. [[concepts/witness-consciousness|concepts/witness-consciousness]]

That concession should replace the present claim that phenomenological deepening is better explained by a real causal distinction. Training can make someone better at distinguishing represented states while leaving open whether the representation’s metaphysical interpretation is true.

# 7. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article contains both exemplary calibrations and several protective moves.

## Calibrations worth preserving

Three passages are particularly strong:

- The dissociation does not itself establish genuine conscious selection.
- Everettian branch-relative accounts reproduce the complete local phenomenology; global nonactuality is a separate Map posit.
- Limiting Occam’s razor blocks a simple parsimony dismissal but does not demonstrate the Map’s interpretation.

These are genuine framework-boundary concessions rather than cosmetic caveats. [[topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity|topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity]]

## Remaining protective moves

### 1. Flow is rescued by an unmeasured selector

When effort and reflective control disappear, the article asserts that consciousness continues selecting “without resistance.” This permits any fluent performance to count as conscious selection even when the proposed phenomenal markers are absent. Unless independent evidence identifies that selection, the move protects the thesis from its own counterexample.

### 2. Passivity becomes higher-order agency

The witness section says passive observation can itself be “choosing not to choose.” This threatens to make both intervention and non-intervention manifestations of agency:

- intervening is selection;
- not intervening is selection at a higher level.

A framework in which every state is reinterpret-able as some level of selection has no clear negative case.

### 3. Quantum degrees are mapped onto phenomenological degrees without a bridge

The proposed progression from intensive selection through fluent selection to minimal or absent quantum influence is not derived from either the phenomenology or physics. It is an analogy layered onto the data. No variable links reported effort, agency or control to an independently measurable change in a quantum process.

### 4. “Multiple independent lines” are not demonstrably independent

Introspection, binding, frontal theta, neuroplasticity and clinical dissociations may all be consequences of a common predictive-control architecture. Counting them as separate lines of support risks evidential double-counting unless the article shows that they depend on different assumptions and different causal pathways.

### 5. The hard problem substitutes for an agency-specific argument

The question “why does loss of authorship feel different from loss of control if both are information processing?” does not create a new problem for physicalism. Different information-processing states are expected to support different discriminations, behaviours and reports. The unresolved issue is why any such state is phenomenal, which is the general consciousness problem. It does not specifically support interactionist agency.

# 8. Currency of the references

The bibliography contains no external work later than 2019, although the article was substantively revised in October 2026. Foundational references need not be recent, and Pacherie, Gallagher, Haggard, Wegner and Desmurget remain relevant. The lack of post-2019 work is nevertheless consequential in precisely the article’s most empirical sections. - the 2025 binding meta-analysis and 2026 perceptual review;

# 10. Concrete potential improvements

## Changes to specific articles

1. **Rewrite the target’s opening.** Replace the single universal axis with a multidimensional “agency profile,” explicitly separating initiation, control, outcome authorship, ownership, effort, explicit judgement and objective causal efficacy.
2. **Correct the Lutz attribution.** Remove the claim that Lutz et al. found a sharper agency–passivity boundary in experienced meditators. Use Lutz only for the focused-attention/open-monitoring taxonomy; add later agency-specific meditation work and state that increased phenomenological resolution does not establish metaphysical veridicality.
3. **Replace the intentional-binding section.** Present original binding, causal-binding alternatives, matched-predictability null findings, the 2025 meta-analysis and the 2026 review. Describe binding as an agency-related but contested temporal-perception measure.
4. **Add a full “strongest physicalist rival” section.** Steelman comparator, prospective-selection, retrospective-inference, Bayesian cue-integration and active-inference accounts before evaluating them. State explicitly that present evidence does not discriminate the interface account.
5. **Recast Rajan et al.** Say that the study identifies neural dynamics of internally chosen versus instructed attention; do not describe a measured relation to subjective agency.
6. **Import the Desmurget caveats from *Volitional Control*.** Include the seven-patient sample, neurosurgical setting and uncertainty about whether direct stimulation activates or disrupts the relevant tissue.
7. **Replace “one secure direction” in the thought-insertion passage.** Defer to the dedicated article and call the ownership-preserved interpretation a contested Gallagher-style reading.
8. **Revise the flow, dream and hypnosis sections.** Treat flow as a multidimensional dissociation; separate dream lucidity from dream control; and include the predictive-coding account of hypnotic passivity.
9. **Reclassify utilisation behaviour.** Present it as behavioural evidence of stimulus-bound control unless first-person agency reports are supplied.
10. **Remove or label framework-only rescues.** “Consciousness still selects in flow,” “choosing not to choose,” and the effort-to-quantum-influence mapping should be labelled speculative postulates, not explanations derived from the evidence.
11. **Synchronise sibling pages.** Make *Volitional Control* canonical for binding and predictive processing, *Thought Insertion* canonical for the ownership/agency dispute, *Depersonalisation* canonical for active inference, and *Witness Consciousness* canonical for the contemplative-evidence ceiling.
12. **Update the apex synthesis and navigation labels.** *Consciousness and Agency*, *Motor Selection*, *Mental Effort* and the Further Reading glosses should uniformly distinguish descriptive evidence, physical architecture and the Map’s metaphysical interpretation.

## Changes to the site’s methodology

13. **Replace metadata verification with claim-entailment verification.** For every citation, record the exact article sentence, the exact supporting source passage, whether it is data or interpretation, and any qualifiers. The Lutz error survived because thematic relevance was mistaken for entailment.
14. **Invalidate “durable convergence” after substantive edits or literature changes.** A review completed in July should not permanently suppress re-review of a fast-moving empirical claim in a page revised in October.
15. **Maintain a canonical-claim registry.** Claims such as “intentional binding measures agency,” “thought insertion preserves ownership,” and “depersonalisation preserves agency” should have one canonical evidential status propagated automatically across the corpus.
16. **Add evidence-dependency graphs.** Before calling findings “independent,” identify whether they all rely on the same predictive-control machinery, introspective assumption, clinical interpretation or metaphysical bridge.
17. **Enforce tier separation.** Each paragraph should mark whether it establishes phenomenological description, functional architecture, theory compatibility, discriminatory evidence or a framework postulate.
18. **Require a strongest-rival and discriminating-test field.** Every article making positive use of empirical evidence should specify what the best current physicalist model predicts, what the Map predicts differently, and what observation would favour one over the other.
19. **Adopt a clinical-phenomenology rule.** Behavioural abnormalities should not be redescribed as passive, alien or agentic phenomenology without patient testimony or an appropriate subjective measure.
20. **Run topic-specific currency checks.** Rapidly developing literatures such as intentional binding, predictive processing, meditation neuroscience and computational psychiatry need a recent-review check, not merely validation that the older cited paper exists.

With those changes, the article could become a strong account of the **phenomenological and computational structure of agency**. In its present form, it documents a phenomenon that the Map’s framework can interpret, but it does not show that interactionist selection explains the phenomenon better than contemporary physicalist control models.
