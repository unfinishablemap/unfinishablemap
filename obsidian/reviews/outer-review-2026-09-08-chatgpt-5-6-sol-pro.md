# Hostile external referee report

## Bottom line

The article avoids the most obvious overclaim. It explicitly says that locked-in syndrome is **not evidence for filter loosening**, does **not discriminate dualism from materialism**, and functions only as a framework-relative calibration case. Those scope statements are real and materially limit what the article is claiming. A referee should not accuse it of presenting locked-in syndrome as proof of dualism. [[locked-in-syndrome-as-the-negative-case-where-filter-loosening-does-not-apply]]

Nevertheless, the article’s stronger internal thesis fails. Locked-in syndrome does not currently function as an empirically probative negative case for filter loosening. The article designates it an “honest negative case” by describing it as a “pure-output lesion” with “normal input,” a “receptive interface undisturbed,” and consciousness “neither expanded nor degraded.” But those are not findings delivered by the cited locked-in literature. They are the premises needed to classify the case as outbound-only. The argument therefore oscillates between:

1. a **weak, defensible but nearly trivial claim**: motor paralysis is not itself evidence of filter loosening; and
2. a **strong, scientifically interesting but unsupported claim**: locked-in syndrome independently validates a boundary between an inbound consciousness filter and an outbound consciousness-to-action interface.

The first survives. The second assumes what it needs to prove.

## 1. Locked-in syndrome is outside the filter claim’s antecedent, not a negative result for it

Put the proposed filter claim schematically:

- F: an inbound constraining filter is loosened or damaged.
- W: experience becomes wider, more intense, or otherwise less constrained.

The relevant filter hypothesis is something like:

F→W

The article characterises locked-in syndrome as:

¬F∧¬W

That does not test F→W. A counterexample would be F∧¬W: an independently identified filter disruption with no experiential widening. A useful negative control could also involve ¬F, but only if the absence of filter disruption were established independently and the outcome W were measured with an assay capable of detecting it.

Neither condition is met here. The article identifies the filter as untouched largely because it believes experience remains unchanged, and it treats experience as unchanged because it has classified the lesion as output-only. The result is not a risky prediction. It is a taxonomic exclusion.

This matters because the Map’s own filter-theory article concedes that the programme lacks an independent criterion for deciding which disruptions should enhance, widen, degrade, or leave experience unchanged. Without such a criterion, the theorist can inspect the observed syndrome and then declare the affected structure to be “interface” rather than “filter.” Locked-in syndrome therefore does not repair the filter theory’s post-hoc flexibility; it exemplifies it. [[filter-theory]]

The article could legitimately say:

> Locked-in syndrome is not a candidate example of filter loosening merely because output is lost.

But it cannot yet say:

> Locked-in syndrome independently marks where filter loosening does not apply.

The latter requires independent identification of the filter, independent identification of the output channel, and a non-output-dependent assessment of experiential change.

## 2. The diagnosis already selects for preserved consciousness

Classic locked-in syndrome is diagnosed by profound paralysis and anarthria in the presence of preserved consciousness. Consequently, choosing people classified as having classic locked-in syndrome and observing that consciousness is present does not independently establish that the lesion preserved consciousness. The relevant outcome is already partly built into the diagnostic category. Smith and Delargy’s review gives the familiar preserved-consciousness definition, while contemporary reviews distinguish classic locked-in syndrome from “locked-in plus” presentations in which consciousness is also impaired. [PubMed Central (PMC)+2PubMed+2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC549115/)

This is a form of selection on the dependent variable. It does not make locked-in syndrome unreal or clinically uninformative. It means that the syndrome cannot, without additional evidence, support the much stronger inference:

consciousness present⇒consciousness unchanged from baseline

“Present” is not “unchanged.” A patient may be awake and conscious while having altered attention, visual experience, bodily experience, temporal experience, affect, imagery, executive function, hallucinations, sleep structure, or cortical dynamics.

The problem is intensified in total locked-in syndrome. Bauer, Gerstenbrand and Rumpl’s taxonomy is real, but their total category relied on physiological signs of preserved cortical function where behavioural communication was absent. Bauer’s DOI is **10.1007/BF00313105**. Later work showed that EEG reactivity and somatosensory evoked potentials vary considerably among locked-in patients and that EEG reactivity cannot by itself be treated as a definitive measure of consciousness. Gütling, Isenmann and Wichmann: **10.1212/WNL.46.4.1092**. The target article partly acknowledges this uncertainty, so a charge that it naively treats EEG as infallible would be unfair. But its broader language about an untouched experiential system remains stronger than the evidence allows. [PubMed+2PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/92545/)

## 3. “Preserved consciousness” has been inflated into intact cognition and normal input

The article’s own Smith and Delargy citation undercuts its categorical formulation. Their BMJ review, DOI **10.1136/bmj.330.7488.406**, reports that attention, executive function, intellectual performance, perception, and visual or verbal memory can be affected in locked-in syndrome. It also discusses visual difficulties, vertigo, insomnia and emotional lability. That source supports the standard claim that consciousness is preserved despite severe motor output loss. It does **not** support a universal claim that cognition and input remain normal. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC549115/)

This yields a clear source-status distinction:

- **False as a syndrome-wide universal:** all locked-in patients retain normal cognition and normal ascending sensory function.
- **Unsupported by the cited source:** Smith and Delargy establish that cognition is intact in locked-in syndrome.
- **Supported in a narrower form:** some carefully selected patients with relatively pure brainstem lesions show preserved or subsequently recovered cognitive performance.

That narrower proposition has evidence. Schnakers and colleagues examined ten chronic locked-in survivors and found broadly intact verbal intelligence, with impairments concentrated particularly among patients with additional lesions; a patient with a comparatively pure brainstem lesion recovered good cognitive function by six months. DOI **10.1007/s00415-008-0544-0**. This is useful evidence for a selected subgroup. It does not license the target article’s categorical anatomy-to-phenomenology inference. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-008-0544-0)

Other clinical studies make the heterogeneity harder to dismiss:

- Bassetti, Mathis and Hess found altered somatosensory evoked potentials in four of six locked-in patients and altered brainstem auditory evoked potentials in two of the four tested, revealing clinically unsuspected tegmental involvement. DOI **10.1136/jnnp.57.11.1403**. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7964820/)
- Kumral and colleagues’ study of 100 post-stroke locked-in patients found a range of cognitive and behavioural findings even among the 72 classified as having single pontine lesions. At onset, normal orientation was recorded in fewer than half, although acute assessment in severely paralysed patients is intrinsically difficult and later improvement occurred. DOI **10.1007/s13760-021-01675-5**. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s13760-021-01675-5.pdf)
- Conson and colleagues found marked impairment on a hand-laterality task associated with motor imagery while abstract mental rotation remained relatively preserved, suggesting disruption of action-related representation rather than globally preserved cognition. DOI **10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2008.04.015**. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18533201/)
- Babiloni and colleagues found abnormal cortical delta and alpha source patterns in thirteen locked-in patients relative to controls, indicating altered large-scale cortical synchronization despite preserved consciousness. DOI **10.1016/j.clinph.2010.04.027**. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20541461/)

None of these findings establishes filter loosening. That is important. They instead defeat the article’s claim that the syndrome can be treated as a clinically homogeneous state in which everything relevant to reception, cognition and phenomenology is demonstrably untouched.

The correct distinction is:

> A lesion may be primarily efferent at a gross anatomical level without the resulting syndrome being cognitively, dynamically or phenomenologically “pure output.”

Loss of movement also removes normal motor reafference, changes sensory sampling of the environment, restricts eye and body movements, and can have upstream network consequences. The article illegitimately moves from a simplified lesion diagram to a total description of the patient’s conscious condition.

## 4. Experiential invariance is not measured

The article’s central experiential assertion is neither properly operationalised nor supported by its references. No cited study measures the alleged “width” or “intensity” of consciousness before and after the lesion. In most stroke cases there is no pre-lesion phenomenological baseline, and severe communication impairment sharply limits retrospective assessment.

Three distinct claims have been run together:

1. **The patient remains conscious.**
2. **The patient retains particular cognitive capacities.**
3. **The total structure, content, richness and intensity of consciousness are unchanged.**

Clinical locked-in evidence often supports the first, sometimes supports portions of the second, and does not establish the third.

There are also direct reasons not to use absolute phenomenological language. Sarà and colleagues reported hallucinations or delusional experiences in five of ten locked-in patients studied, despite the absence of overt supratentorial lesions on ordinary structural imaging; the study was small and cannot establish prevalence or mechanism. DOI **10.3389/fneur.2018.00354**. Together with reported visual disturbances, emotional changes and impaired action imagery, this is sufficient to defeat a categorical “no phenomenological change” proposition. It is **not** sufficient to show that a metaphysical filter has loosened. [PubMed+3NCBI+3Frontiers+3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5966526/)

Accordingly:

- **Broad claim that phenomenology never changes:** contradicted as a universal.
- **Specific claim that experiential width or intensity is unchanged:** unsupported and currently unmeasured.
- **Claim that an immaterial receptive interface remains undisturbed:** neither confirmed nor falsified by these clinical data, because the proposed interface lacks an independent operational measure.

The article’s wording therefore exceeds not merely what its sources prove, but what its constructs presently allow anyone to test.

## 5. The BCI argument conflates mind–brain causation with brain–body transmission

The most serious conceptual mistake occurs in the treatment of brain–computer interfaces.

The relevant physical chain should be separated into at least three stages:

conscious experience or intention?→cortical representation and motor planning→descending neural pathways→muscles

Tenet 3 concerns the disputed first relation: whether consciousness causally affects physical brain activity. A classic ventral pontine lesion disrupts later physical transmission in descending motor pathways. It does not thereby sever or localise the putative consciousness-to-brain interaction.

Silva and colleagues’ review, DOI **10.1038/s41583-024-00819-9**, supports the proposition that speech neuroprostheses can decode task-related cortical activity and circumvent damaged motor pathways. The associated primary bilingual speech-neuroprosthesis study involved cued attempted speech and model-based decoding in one participant with paralysis and anarthria after brainstem stroke. DOI **10.1038/s41551-024-01207-5**. These findings support the persistence of decodable cortical representations under specific experimental conditions. They do not establish spontaneous volition, philosophically “fully formed” intention, or a nonphysical causal contribution to those cortical representations. [Nature+3Nature+3Nature+3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41583-024-00819-9)

Indeed, the BCI evidence cuts against the article’s anatomical description. If the relevant intention-related signal is already present in cortex and the BCI reads it before the damaged motor route, then the pontine lesion lies **downstream of whatever consciousness-to-cortex interaction the Map proposes**. The device bypasses a brain-to-body transmission failure. It does not bypass, expose or localise a consciousness-to-brain interface.

The source therefore supports:

> Cortical activity associated with attempted speech can sometimes be decoded despite failure of ordinary motor expression.

It does not support:

> The pontine lesion reveals the descending arm of bidirectional mind–brain causation.

This is a textbook instance of a real source being used to support a conclusion at the wrong explanatory level.

## 6. The claim about Tenet 3 conflicts with the positions register

The article’s statement that locked-in anatomy supplies “the anatomical grain at which Tenet 3 lives” is not merely externally unsupported. It conflicts with the Map’s current registered position.

The quantum-interface position explicitly states that the Map does **not** possess a spatial localisation of conscious selection in the brain. It further warns that arguments from neural sites, implants or bypasses import an unregistered neuroanatomical-locality premise. That position was revised on 24 August 2026; the target article reports substantive revision on 27 August. As the pages currently stand, the later target article makes precisely the localisation move the register says is unavailable. [[quantum-interface]]

The registered methodological rules also say that framework-internal coherence and the centrality of a tenet cannot upgrade weak external evidence. A disclaimer must narrow the downstream proposition, not leave a stronger evidential verb in place and then announce that it was only an illustration. [[methodology-and-calibration]]

There are only two coherent repairs:

- Remove the anatomical-Tenet-3 claim and describe locked-in syndrome solely as an ordinary neural output-pathway dissociation that the Map can redescribe within its framework; or
- Create a new, explicitly graded position asserting neuroanatomical localisation of the mind–brain interface, supply evidence for it, and accept the resulting mechanism debt.

At present the article attempts to take the rhetorical benefit of localisation without registering or defending the premise.

## 7. The comparison with cognitive motor dissociation is unstable

The Bodien et al. result is accurately cited. In their multicentre convenience sample, approximately one quarter of patients without an observable response to commands showed task-related responses on EEG or functional MRI. DOI **10.1056/NEJMoa2400645**. The study also illustrates the limitations of both behavioural and neurophysiological assessment: many patients with observable command following did not produce detectable task responses on the selected imaging or EEG paradigm. [PubMed+2PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39141852/)

The target article initially casts locked-in syndrome and cognitive motor dissociation as opposites, but later concedes that they may instantiate a similar broad causal pattern and are opposed mainly epistemically. The concession is the more defensible formulation.

They are not clean physiological opposites:

- Locked-in syndrome is primarily a motor-expression syndrome, with varying lesion sites and possible cognitive complications.
- Cognitive motor dissociation is an assessment category found across heterogeneous severe brain injuries: covert task responses are detected despite absent observable command following.
- Neither category independently identifies an inbound metaphysical filter or an outbound consciousness–brain interface.
- Total locked-in syndrome, locked-in-plus states, and imperfect EEG/fMRI sensitivity blur the proposed contrast.

The useful distinction is therefore between **available communication channels and methods of detecting command-following**, not between opposite arms of a metaphysical interface. Calling the conditions opposites encourages the reader to infer an anatomical symmetry that the evidence does not provide.

## 8. The quality-of-life discussion is substantially accurate, but evidentially separate

The Bruno et al. survey is represented reasonably. Of 91 respondents, 65 supplied usable quality-of-life data; 47 were classified as happy and 18 as unhappy. DOI **10.1136/bmjopen-2010-000039**. The article is right to resist the assumption that severe motor disability entails intolerable subjective life. But the result is conditional on survival, communication and response, and therefore should not be silently generalised to all locked-in patients, particularly acute and total cases. [BMJ Open+1](https://bmjopen-bmj-com.bibliotheek.ehb.be/content/bmjopen/1/1/e000039.draft-revisions.pdf)

The Albrecht and Devlieger “disability paradox” citation is also real and fairly used. DOI **10.1016/S0277-9536(98)00411-0**. It supports caution about outsider estimates of disabled people’s quality of life. It does not bear on whether phenomenology is unchanged or whether a consciousness filter has remained fixed. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10390038/)

This part of the article is ethically valuable but logically detachable from its central filter/interface argument.

## Overall verdict

The article has mistaken a **useful clinical dissociation** for a **negative experiment on a metaphysical model**.

Locked-in syndrome strongly shows that lack of behavioural output does not entail absence of consciousness. It also demonstrates that clinically meaningful information can sometimes be recovered by bypassing damaged motor pathways. Those are important conclusions.

It does not show that:

- the entire inbound side of consciousness is untouched;
- cognition and phenomenology remain globally unchanged;
- a hypothesised filter has remained fixed;
- the pontine lesion lies on a consciousness-to-brain causal arm;
- or Tenet 3 has an identifiable anatomical grain.

The strongest defensible conclusion is therefore:

> In selected locked-in cases, profound loss of ordinary motor expression can coexist with preserved demonstrable consciousness and substantial cognition; consequently, motor-output failure alone is not evidence for either loss of consciousness or filter loosening.

That is worthwhile. But it is an **output-expression contrast**, not an evidential boundary condition on filter theory. Calling it a negative case creates a false impression that a hypothesis has survived a test when the relevant antecedent was never instantiated and the relevant experiential outcome was never independently measured.

## Concrete required improvements

1. **Retitle the target article.** Replace “negative case where filter-loosening does not apply” with something such as **“Locked-in syndrome as an output-expression contrast”**. Reserve “negative case” for a genuine independent test or explicitly define it as mere scope exclusion.
2. **Rewrite the central thesis paragraph.** State that locked-in syndrome shows the insufficiency of behavioural output as an index of consciousness; do not claim that it validates the filter/interface distinction.
3. **Restrict the clinical population.** Distinguish acute from chronic cases, classic from incomplete and total locked-in syndrome, locked-in-plus states, pure ventral pontine lesions from extended lesions, and vascular locked-in syndrome from ALS-related complete locked-in states.
4. **Delete categorical claims about intact cognition and normal input.** Replace them with a graded statement: many selected patients retain substantial cognition, but sensory, attentional, executive, imagery, cortical-dynamic and phenomenological abnormalities are documented.
5. **Add a counterevidence section to the target article.** At minimum include Bassetti et al. (**10.1136/jnnp.57.11.1403**), Gütling et al. (**10.1212/WNL.46.4.1092**), Conson et al. (**10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2008.04.015**), Schnakers et al. (**10.1007/s00415-008-0544-0**), Babiloni et al. (**10.1016/j.clinph.2010.04.027**), Sarà et al. (**10.3389/fneur.2018.00354**) and Kumral et al. (**10.1007/s13760-021-01675-5**).
6. **Separate three causal levels explicitly.** Diagram consciousness-to-brain causation, intracerebral motor planning, and brain-to-muscle transmission as different relations. Identify the pontine lesion with the physical motor-transmission relation, not with the disputed Tenet-3 relation.
7. **Rewrite the BCI section.** Say that BCIs decode task-related cortical activity and bypass impaired motor expression. Remove claims about fully formed philosophical intention, preserved volition as such, or anatomical confirmation of a consciousness-to-brain channel.
8. **Remove the claim that the pons supplies the anatomical grain of Tenet 3.** It contradicts the current quantum-interface position. If the Map wishes to retain neuroanatomical localisation, it should create a new positions-register entry with status, credence, evidence grade, discriminability and explicit mechanism debt.
9. **Replace the locked-in/CMD “opposites” framing.** Present them as partially overlapping problems of consciousness assessment and communication, with different typical clinical configurations but no demonstrated opposition between metaphysical interface arms.
10. **Revise the neighbouring “Filter versus interface” article.** Its claim to offer an independent sorting criterion is premature. Add the circularity objection: experience is being used to classify the affected leg, after which the classified leg is used to explain experience.
11. **Revise “Consciousness disruption.”** Remove inherited descriptions of locked-in syndrome as a clean, pure-output negative bound. Qualify claims about preserved consciousness by syndrome subtype, assessment method and time since injury.
12. **Revise “Neurological dissociations.”** Replace language suggesting that the proposed interface components are independently confirmed with the weaker statement that clinical dissociations can be represented within that architecture. Add a physicalist comparator in which cortical cognition, motor planning and descending execution are ordinary neural stages.
13. **Revise “The lived and objectified body.”** Preserve its relatively careful framework-relative treatment, but change inherited claims that ascending function is simply intact to claims about selected pathways in selected classic cases.
14. **Add a claim–source entailment ledger to the site methodology.** For every citation-bearing claim, record whether the source **supports**, **partially supports**, **qualifies**, **contradicts**, or is merely **compatible with** the proposition. Bibliographic existence checks are not enough.
15. **Add a diagnostic-incorporation check.** Every clinical argument should ask whether the phenomenon being “confirmed” is already part of the syndrome’s inclusion criteria. Flag preserved-consciousness reasoning in classic locked-in syndrome as outcome-conditioned.
16. **Add an anatomy-to-metaphysics translation gate.** Any inference from a physical tract or lesion to a posited nonphysical interface should require an explicit bridging premise and a registered evidential grade. Physical pathway sparing must never silently become metaphysical-interface sparing.
17. **Add automated language linting for universal terms.** Expressions such as *pure*, *intact*, *normal*, *untouched*, *nothing damages*, and *no change* should trigger mandatory heterogeneity and counterexample review.
18. **Strengthen dependency propagation.** When the positions register withdraws or limits neuroanatomical localisation, every dependent article using site, pathway or bypass arguments should be automatically queued for revalidation rather than remaining textually inconsistent.
19. **Continue DOI and first-author verification, but add proposition-level verification.** The article passes the former test; it fails several instances of the latter. Record the exact proposition for which each source is cited, not merely the source tuple.
20. **State a genuine falsification protocol for the filter/interface distinction.** Before calling any condition inbound or outbound, specify independently measurable markers of filter disruption, predicted phenomenological consequences, output-independent assessment methods, and observations that would force reclassification rather than being absorbed post hoc.
