## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would keep `time-bias` L85, which transposes Prior's argument "from language to experience": the felt pastness of a finished pain "is a first-person feature that tenseless description, dated or token-reflexive, does not capture", and the sentence that follows refuses to cash the parallel as evidence: "the Map notes the parallel without treating it as independent evidence: B-theorists who are also physicalists accept the datum and locate it in the representational content of tensed states". That is the explanatory-gap structure applied to time and then correctly fenced. He would also value `correlationism` L84, where the Map denies itself the privileged access to consciousness that a dualist is usually accused of assuming: "The Map asserts that both terms exist apart from the correlation; it asserts transparent access to neither." And in `pudgalavada` L81 the reply to Vasubandhu's ownership argument is the one Chalmers would give: the argument "dissolves ownership understood as mastery", and "What the argument leaves untouched is ownership as phenomenal for-me-ness".

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp is the subject of one article and would find it fair. `process-1-specification-problem` reads his 2012 reply at source and grants him the point he actually made: the mind "does not slow decoherence but 'rolls back the brain-based process of evolution'" (L67). It then does what his critics rarely do, which is to show that his own 2000 consent wording and the Laskey fix de Barros credits "are the same move" (L77), so that the narrowing of Process 1 to timing and assent is Stapp's, not an opponent's. Stapp would also notice that the page treats Georgiev's theorem with the scope discipline he asked for: "The theorem's scope is narrower than its title" (L63), conditional on brain-local, Born-consistent projections. What he would resist is the verdict at L91 that "once the basis is handed to decoherence the mode is timing control under another name"; the page states the grain-versus-strip comparison as "the quantitative crux, and this page states it rather than settling it" (L69), which is where an honest account of the dispute currently ends.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Nagel would take `time-bias` as written for him. Prior's argument "works by pointing at an attitude and asking what its object is. The relief is the evidence; the paraphrases fail because they change what one would be relieved about" (L43), and the article's own ranking puts the phenomenal datum first: "The relief is the most secure item on the list and the one theories of time most often explain away" (L81). The standing complaint against the evolutionary story is stated in his terms: the B-theory "explains the attitudes' usefulness without explaining their apparent correctness" (L51). In `correlationism`, Zahavi's defence of phenomenology is reported "as theirs, not as the Map's" (L94), and the article then says plainly why the Map cannot take the phenomenologist's exit: "That escape is closed to the Map, and the closure is the cleanest statement of why the Map is not a correlationism" (L96). Nagel would respect a dualism that refuses anti-realism about unobservables in order to stay honest about physics.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would find the wing's refusal of crude substance talk most explicit in `pudgalavada`. The article places the Map's subject "between" the pudgala and the *ātman*, "real and particular like the pudgala, non-physical and agentive like the *ātman*, unlike both in persisting through change" (L73), and then charges itself the full price: "The Map takes as a horn what the Personalist took as a refutation, and so inherits the arguments aimed at the eternal soul as well." That is a subject defined by what it does through change, which is Whitehead's own instinct. He would also value `paradoxical-kinesia` L88, where the Map's interest is located in a *mode of control* rather than a thing: "A patient walking by attending to each step is arguably a case of *more* reliance on the kind of control the Map cares about." None of this praise bears on any evidential tier: no article in the wing claims that any organism or system is conscious, and the one that comes nearest (`paradoxical-kinesia`) grades its own reading down.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane's article is `diverging-worlds-everettianism`, because it says exactly what libertarian agency needs and what a rival that lacks it must do instead: "there is no indeterminacy for consciousness to bias, and the Map's minimal quantum interaction has nothing to act on ... A diverging-worlds dualist would have to locate mental causation elsewhere" (L66). The retained disagreement is stated as a requirement, not a result: the Map's bidirectional interaction "in its outcome-selecting form, needs genuine indeterminacy, and diverging Everettianism has none" (L75). He would also find `pudgalavada` L79 honest about the cost of agent causation: Vasubandhu's definition of the agent as "the principal cause" is one "the Map can meet only if the subject's selection, physically minimal under Tenet 2, is what settles which open alternative becomes actual; whether that makes it the principal cause or one cause among many is unsettled."
