## Executive Summary

The memory-and-self cluster is the most calibration-disciplined body of work the Map currently
holds, and two articles in it — `phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self` and
`diachronic-agency-and-personal-narrative` — have done something rare: they conceded a
tenet-supporting inference *away* under pressure and rewrote the article around the weaker claim
rather than adding a confession and moving on. The topic tier has invented, in the process, a
methodological move that appears nowhere else in 203 articles carrying a falsifier section: an
audit of whether the article's own defensive strategy already absorbs the falsifiers it names.
The cluster's weakness is inheritance, not calibration — the concept-tier siblings one click away
still run the argument the topic tier retired, and the invented discipline has not been written
down where the rest of the corpus can use it.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The cluster puts the explanatory gap in its temporal form better than most of the literature does.
`phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self` L112 states the residue precisely: functional,
source-monitoring, and metacognitive accounts "identify mechanisms correlated with rememberedness
— hippocampal pattern completion, prefrontal monitoring, source-attribution, emotional tagging —
but none has yet *derived* why mechanism-output is experienced *as* re-encountering one's own past
rather than merely activating stored information." That is the hard problem stated as an
outstanding derivation rather than as an intuition pump, which is the form Chalmers would want.

The remember/know contrast is the cluster's strongest gap exhibit and it is used correctly across
three articles: the *same* propositional content, accessed with or without the phenomenal
accompaniment. `anoetic-noetic-autonoetic-consciousness` L102 draws the right conclusion — that
phenomenal character varies independently of informational content — without claiming this settles
anything metaphysical.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

`episodic-memory`'s reconsolidation reading is the cluster's most disciplined interface proposal.
L169 does what quantum-mind proposals usually fail to do: it treats the *gating* of the window as
strengthening the proposal rather than weakening it — "That the window is gated rather than opened
by every retrieval makes the proposal more tractable, not less: it names specific occasions on
which the substrate is underdetermined rather than asserting a permanent openness." A permanently
open interface is unfalsifiable; an occasion-specific one is a research target. And L135 keeps the
mechanism severable from the argument: "This mechanism is speculative. The dual-domain argument
rests on the explanatory gap ... an argument that holds regardless of where the interface is
located."

`simulation-theory-of-memory` L74 holds the same line — the interface reading is explicitly "held
separate from the empirical signature."

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The double-presence analysis is the cluster's best original phenomenological work.
`phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self` L60-70 gets the Husserl relation right where most
secondary treatments get it wrong: retention is named as an *analogue* of autonoesis "rather than
the same phenomenon", with recollection identified as the nearer counterpart. That is a correction
against the easy conflation, made in the article's own voice.

L62 states the "what is it like" question in its memorial form without needing Nagel's phrasing:
"Perception gives you a world; imagination gives you a scenario; episodic memory gives you
*yourself* as having been someone—the same someone now remembering."

`memory-anomalies` L44 turns the same lens on the failure cases and reaches the sharper claim:
"The felt certainty is the explanandum, not the evidence."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

`memory-anomalies`'s Bergson section is the model of how to raise a congenial metaphysics without
cashing its congeniality as support. L90: "The Map raises Bergson as one candidate explanation,
not a load-bearing commitment. The recognition-memory evidence and the gestalt-match story
*constrain* identity readings ... they do not by themselves *establish* either Bergson's
surfacing-of-the-virtual reading or the standard retrieval-failure reading, a distinction the Map
keeps explicit so neither is over-credited."

Bergson is the most Whitehead-adjacent figure in the cluster, and the article declines to let his
congeniality do evidential work. That restraint is exactly the axis-separation the two personas
below are designed to police, and here they do not conflict.

The dual-domain framing in `episodic-memory` also avoids crude substance dualism: memory is a
*capability* whose operation is divided across domains, not a faculty relocated wholesale into an
immaterial mind. The Descartes and Bergson precedents at L129 are cited as historical company, not
as authority.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`diachronic-agency-and-personal-narrative` is the cluster's most improved article and the reason
is Kane-shaped: it distinguishes the capacity to *reorganise* commitments from the capacity to
carry a disposition forward, and rests the anti-reductionist claim on the former alone. L134:
"What parsimony still cannot absorb is the capacity to *reorganise*: to reinterpret past choices,
redirect current efforts, and revise future plans in a single unified act, which needs the
synoptic grasp described earlier rather than a mechanism that carries a disposition forward."

The Minimal Quantum Interaction paragraph at L130 is the best statement of the scale argument in
the corpus: "The impressive temporal coherence of diachronic agency emerges not from massive
interventions but from sustained sequences of minimal selections, organised by narrative or some
other coordinating structure into patterns no single selection contains." That sentence makes the
minimality constraint do explanatory work rather than merely absorbing an objection.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`memory-anomalies` L116 is the single most epistemically humble passage in the cluster, and it is
humble about *itself*: "This article both *uses* the cluster ... and *contributes to* it ... The
article is therefore not an independent observer of the convergence; it is part of what the
convergence counts." It then downgrades its own evidential tier conditionally — the cluster-level
move "reaches the strongly-supported tier only conditional on the common-cause-null surviving the
discrimination tests catalogued at the apex; absent that survival, the cluster-level move sits at
*live hypothesis*."

Self-referential evidential honesty of that specificity is rare anywhere. An article that notices
it is inside the evidence base it is appealing to, and prices that in, has done the mysterian's
work without the mysterian's despair.

`parfit-reductionism` L95 shows the same restraint on a live dispute: it *retires* the Tallis
regress as a reply ("the bare regress assumes the seeming is itself phenomenal, precisely what
illusionists deny, and it proves nothing"), keeps only the narrower performative point, and states
that "neither side closes the case against the other inside the rival's framework."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This persona's verdict is load-bearing here, and it splits cleanly by tier.**

**The topic tier earns unqualified praise.** `phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self` had every
structural opportunity to slide memory phenomenology up the scale on tenet-load and explicitly
declined, twice, in the section where such slides normally happen:

- **Dualism (L150)**: "*Dualism* is the reading the Map places on this material, not a conclusion
  the material delivers on its own ... felt ownership does not belong on any list of things
  dualism draws support from."
- **No Many Worlds (L154)**: "records conditional consistency here rather than support drawn from
  memory. Nothing in memory phenomenology discriminates a single-world physics from an Everettian
  one; both ontologies predict identical first-person reports ... Memory phenomenology is
  compatible with the tenet; it does not argue for it."

That is *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating* stated in the Map's own words, in the position
where the inflation would otherwise sit. **Declining a tier-upgrade you were architecturally
positioned to take is itself the strength**, and this article does it in the hardest place —
the tenet section, where the pull is strongest.

**The falsifier-survival audit (L116-118) is the cluster's most valuable single contribution and
it is unique in the corpus.** The article notices that its own relocation reply, applied without
limit, "disqualifies every mechanistic result in advance—and an argument shielded that thoroughly
earns nothing distinctive from memory science." It then audits its own candidate falsifiers
against that reply, discards two as absorbed ("Naming them as falsifiers while holding the
relocation reply would be decorative"), and keeps the two that survive — decomposition of felt
pastness into separately manipulable cues, and a smooth noetic/autonoetic gradient. A grep of
`topics/`, `concepts/`, `apex/`, `voids/` and `project/` finds **203 articles carrying a
falsifier section and exactly one performing this audit.**

**The calibration concern is inheritance, and it is verified.** The concept-tier siblings still
run the inference the topic tier retired:

- `concepts/episodic-memory` **L165**: "Episodic memory supports all five foundational
  commitments" — and **L173** makes the retired argument in full: "If identity bifurcated at every
  quantum event, there would be no fact about which memories are *truly* mine." The sibling one
  click away says memory phenomenology "does not argue for" this tenet. Same inference, opposite
  calibration.
- `concepts/semantic-memory` **L172 / L180**: same "supports all five" header, and a No-Many-Worlds
  paragraph asserting that many-worlds "would make each fact indexed to a branch-history,
  fragmenting the unity of semantic knowledge" with no acknowledgement that both ontologies
  predict identical first-person reports.
- `concepts/episodic-memory` **L161**: five named falsifiers, of which three are neutralised in the
  same sentence that names them — "(1) phenomenology dissolving under introspective analysis—
  *instead, closer attention intensifies it*; (2) retrieval phenomenology persisting despite
  lesions—*hippocampal amnesia shows the opposite*; (3) pastness being fully inferential—
  *phenomenological investigation suggests it arrives already tagged*." These are the decorative
  falsifiers the sibling article's L116 audit exists to catch.

**`concepts/prospective-memory` L174 is a deliberate exclusion from that list and should not be
swept with it.** It names the MWI reply first and states exactly what the reply leaves untouched
("on every branch, a counterpart deliberated and did the opposite") — it is calibrated, and only
its section header inherits the "supports all five" framing.

**The Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist converge, not conflict, on
`memory-anomalies` and the topic tier.** They diverge only on the concept tier, and the divergence
is a lag rather than a disagreement: the concept pages were last deep-reviewed 2026-07-11 and
2026-06-24, before the topic-tier de-inflation landed.

## Content Strengths

### `obsidian/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self.md`
- **Strongest point**: The falsifier-survival audit at L116-118 — the only instance in the corpus
  of an article checking its own named falsifiers against its own defensive strategy and
  discarding the ones the strategy already absorbs.
- **Notable quote**: "Naming them as falsifiers while holding the relocation reply would be
  decorative, and the evidential-status discipline rules that out."
- **Why it works**: It converts an unfalsifiability objection into a positive result. The article
  ends up with *fewer* but *real* falsifiers, and can then say what memory contributes "beyond the
  general premise" — which is a stronger position than the longer list it started with.

### `obsidian/topics/diachronic-agency-and-personal-narrative.md`
- **Strongest point**: The Strawson concession is structural, not cosmetic. Title, description,
  lead sentence and the Breakdowns section were all rewritten to the weaker claim.
- **Notable quote**: "Against a claim that narrative is *constitutive* of diachronic agency, the
  descriptive prong is decisive, and the Map concedes it."
- **Why it works**: L112 goes back and re-reads the article's *own prior evidence* under the
  concession — "Each pathology disrupts a coordinating capacity rather than establishing that the
  capacity had to be narrative: the amnesic patient loses the plans along with the story." Most
  concessions are appended; this one propagates backwards through the argument. It also separates
  the surviving disagreement (Strawson's thin subject) and marks it framework-boundary, so the
  concession is not confused with capitulation.

### `obsidian/topics/memory-anomalies.md`
- **Strongest point**: L70's dual-process contingency plan. The article states its architecture on
  the dual-process reading, flags that reading as contested by Wixted and Mickes, and then shows
  the load-bearing claim re-deriving from what the *contesting* accounts also grant.
- **Notable quote**: "Continuous-signal-detection relocates the boundary ... but the gap between
  phenomenal output and truth-tracking machinery survives the relocation intact. The trio is a
  vivid exhibit of that gap; it does not need the channels to be metaphysically two for the
  exhibit to hold."
- **Why it works**: It insulates the philosophical claim from an unsettled empirical dispute
  without pretending the dispute is settled. This is the correct handling of a live scientific
  contest and is directly reusable.

### `obsidian/concepts/episodic-memory.md`
- **Strongest point**: The reconsolidation repair at L85 — the cleanest claim-narrowing in the
  cluster, gated by three separately cited sources (Kida 2020, Sinclair & Barense 2018, Elsey et
  al. 2018) rather than by a hedge.
- **Notable quote**: "The accurate formulation is therefore conditional: *under identifiable
  conditions*, retrieval renders a trace labile and the act of remembering alters it."
- **Why it works**: It separates two claims that the field routinely runs together
  (reconstructive-at-retrieval vs. destabilising-the-trace), keeps the general one, conditionalises
  the narrow one, and then shows at L169 that the tenet argument is *better* on the conditional
  version. The article's SDAM handling at L61/L79 is equally good: it names a finding that checks
  its own universal claim and shows the finding delivering the article's central dissociation from
  an undamaged population.

### `obsidian/concepts/anoetic-noetic-autonoetic-consciousness.md`
- **Strongest point**: L137's explicit exposure-propagation to the sibling article — a rare
  instance of an article stating that another article inherits its evidential liabilities.
- **Notable quote**: "The two articles depend on the same architectural commitments and face the
  standing challenges jointly rather than separately."
- **Why it works**: It is the correct antidote to cluster-level double-counting, and it is written
  from the *upstream* article, which is where it is cheapest to maintain. It also engages De
  Brigard 2024 accurately and at the right strength (L70).

### `obsidian/concepts/simulation-theory-of-memory.md`
- **Strongest point**: The split verdict, announced as the article's thesis rather than as a
  hedge. L36: "The Unfinishable Map's verdict is split, and the split is the point."
- **Why it works**: It sources the key objection *inside* the rival literature (Rivadulla-Duró
  2024), then says so explicitly at L66 — "a live problem inside the simulationist literature, not
  merely an objection from outside it." An objection the rival raises against itself is worth more
  than one imported from a hostile framework, and the article knows it.

### `obsidian/concepts/parfit-reductionism.md`
- **Strongest point**: The Lockwood convergence at L67 — evidence that the Map's opponents
  themselves see the Parfit/MWI alignment the Map claims, sourced to a named quantum-foundations
  programme rather than asserted as a parallel.
- **Notable quote**: "That a leading quantum-foundations program reaches for Parfit by name is the
  clearest articulation in the literature of the view the Map argues against on both fronts at
  once."
- **Why it works**: It converts an internal coherence claim (our two objections are the same
  objection) into an externally checkable one.

## Expansion Opportunities

### High Priority

#### Name the falsifier-survival audit in the methodology layer

- **Builds on**: `topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self` L116-118; `project/coherence-inflation-countermeasures` (16 countermeasures, none covering this); `project/evidential-status-discipline`.
- **Would address**: 203 articles carry a "What Would Challenge This View?" section. Exactly one
  audits its named falsifiers against its own defensive strategy. `concepts/episodic-memory` L161
  shows what the un-audited version looks like — three of five falsifiers neutralised in the
  sentence that names them. The Map has invented a discipline in one article and left it there.
- **The move, stated generally**: before listing falsifiers, an article must check each against
  its own standing replies. A falsifier the article's own defensive strategy already absorbs is
  decorative and must be struck; what remains is what the article genuinely risks. An article that
  strikes *all* of its falsifiers has discovered that its argument earns nothing from the local
  evidence and rests entirely on its framework premise — which is itself a finding worth recording.
- **Estimated scope**: Medium — a Countermeasure 17 entry, not a new article. Direct precedent:
  apex #30 was admitted on exactly this basis (a reusable methodological pattern the Map performed
  but had not named), per the `apex/apex-articles` entry.
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits) — it is the instrument that stops the
  Map's own coherence from being mistaken for evidence.
- **Note**: `project/coherence-inflation-countermeasures` has a P1 task open and a NEEDS-HUMAN
  methodology-ratification reservation on its governance half. This entry is a *lens*, not a gate,
  and should be minted as such — see the task note.

#### Propagate the topic tier's tenet-section calibration to the memory concept pages

- **Builds on**: `topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self` L150/L154 (the retired inferences).
- **Would address**: `concepts/episodic-memory` L165/L173 and `concepts/semantic-memory` L172/L180

### Medium Priority

#### Import the dual-process contingency pattern into the cluster's other contested-empirics articles

`memory-anomalies` L70 shows how to state a philosophical claim on a contested empirical scaffold
and then re-derive it from what the contesting accounts also grant. `concepts/episodic-memory`
leans on the remember/know paradigm throughout without noting that Wixted and Mickes's continuous
account — already cited in the sibling — reads remember/know as one signal at two criterion
points. The claim almost certainly survives the relocation, as it does in `memory-anomalies`; the
article should show that it does.

#### Give `simulation-theory-of-memory` the predictive-processing steelman

The article's Dualism section (L72) rests on autonoesis being what "physical construction cannot
supply." The self-model reply — that the *this-happened-to-me* marker is itself a generative-model
output rather than a residue construction leaves behind — is the strongest live physicalist rival
to precisely that move and is absent. `obsidian/research/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity-2026-07-11.md`
already holds the material. This is a smaller sibling of the P1 task already open on
`topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self`; it is listed here as a *later* item deliberately,
so the two are not worked in parallel and the topic-tier reply can be reused rather than re-derived.
