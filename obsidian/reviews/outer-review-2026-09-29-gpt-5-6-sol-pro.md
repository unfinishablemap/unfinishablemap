# External audit: “The Constitutive Exclusion”

## Overall judgment

**Major revision is still required, although the September revision materially improved the article.**

The article now makes an unusually valuable concession: Kant, Nagel and Wheeler do **not** derive the Map’s metaphysical thesis that consciousness partly constitutes physical reality. It identifies the transition from an epistemic limit to a metaphysical constitution claim as the Map’s own, tenet-dependent inference. It also restores Wheeler’s crucial qualifiers—*elementary* and *registered*—and distinguishes apparatus registration from consciousness. The September 21 changelog confirms that the latest substantive addition was a discount for LLM-mediated agreement, accompanied by the Kim and Buyl references; an earlier commit that evening changed only model-attribution metadata.  

Those repairs do not, however, make the headline argument hold. The article still moves among four importantly different theses:

| Thesis | Audit judgment |
|---|---|
| Human cognition and measurement condition how reality is encountered | Well supported, although almost too weak to be controversial |
| No completely perspective-free standpoint is available | Plausible and supported by several cited traditions |
| We therefore cannot have fallible knowledge of mind-independent reality | **Does not follow** |
| Consciousness helps constitute actual physical reality or selects outcomes | **Not supported by Kant, Nagel, Wheeler or the delayed-choice experiment; conditional on the Map’s tenets** |

The strongest defensible result is therefore a thesis of **perspectival humility**: our knowledge is conditioned, selective and possibly incomplete. The article has not established its more ambitious “constitutive exclusion”—that reality independent of the conscious contribution is inaccessible in a sense that defeats scientific realism—or its further metaphysical claim that consciousness helps make physical reality what it is. The live article largely acknowledges this in its convergence section, but periodically resumes the stronger language in its lead, void hierarchy and tenet discussion. [[constitutive-exclusion]]

## What the revision fixed—and what remains

The September 10 internal review correctly identified several serious earlier errors:

- Wheeler’s saying had lost both *elementary* and *registered*, making a quantum-registration claim look like a universal consciousness claim.
- Wheeler had been represented as directly supporting the Map’s metaphysics.
- The article had said consciousness was present in “all measurement,” conflicting with the site’s narrower local-neural account.
- It had treated the void as independent support for dualism, contrary to the site’s own position that a tenet’s explanatory fit does not upgrade the evidence.
- Its Occam’s Razor section had asserted a metaphysical conclusion that the article had not established.

Those were genuine and worthwhile corrections. 

The September 21 model-mediation paragraph is also intellectually responsible. Kim et al. do report correlated errors across a very large model comparison, and Buyl et al. report systematic ideological differences associated with model provenance and language. But these findings justify only a qualitative caution about the Map’s own selection, synthesis and mutual self-citation. They do not quantify how much to “discount” a particular philosophical interpretation, and Buyl et al. explicitly face entangled causal factors rather than identifying creator region as a clean causal variable. The article mostly observes that limitation, but the passage is methodological hygiene rather than evidence for constitutive exclusion. [Proceedings of Machine Learning Research+2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kim25e.html)

## Citation and quotation audit

I found no obviously nonexistent reference entry. The major problems concern **what the sources support**, quotation provenance and the absence of page-level citations.

### Kant: substantially recognizable, but rendered through a misleading “filter” model

The article is broadly right that, for Kant, space and time are forms of intuition and the categories condition possible experience. It is also right that theoretical cognition does not give us things as they are independently of those conditions.

Its wording nevertheless risks turning transcendental idealism into a psychological picture in which a preformed noumenal world enters a “cognitive apparatus” and emerges distorted. Kant’s appearances are not merely corrupted images of things in themselves: space and time are empirically real conditions of objects of experience, while things in themselves are not possible objects of theoretical cognition. Moreover, causality and substance are categories of understanding, not “forms” in precisely the same sense as space and time. [Project Gutenberg+1](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/4280/4280-h/4280-h.htm)

The article’s later statement that Kant does **not** say the mind constitutes the noumenon is correct. But that qualification needs to govern the opening Kant paragraph as well. “We cannot know things in themselves through theoretical cognition” is much safer than “reality has passed through a filter and is permanently inaccessible.”

### Merleau-Ponty: the clearest source failure

I could not locate either quoted Merleau-Ponty formulation in the cited 2012 edition of *Phenomenology of Perception*, including the longer sentence:

> “We are both a part of the world and coextensive with it, constituting but also constituted.”

Nor could I locate it in the older English translation. The formulation may be a composite paraphrase, a translation from another work, or a secondary-source gloss, but it should not remain in quotation marks without a page image or exact bibliographic provenance. [Archive.org+1](https://ia600600.us.archive.org/17/items/G.BachelardThePoeticsOfSpace/Phenomenology%20of%20Perception.pdf)

More importantly, the cited book contains language running against the article’s use of it. In the Preface, Merleau-Ponty says that the real is to be described, “neither constructed nor constituted”; he emphasizes a world prior to analysis and consciousness already engaged in that world rather than mentally manufacturing it. [Archive.org+2](https://ia600600.us.archive.org/17/items/G.BachelardThePoeticsOfSpace/Phenomenology%20of%20Perception.pdf)

That does not make Merleau-Ponty a naïve realist. His account of embodiment, intentionality and the intertwining of subject and world certainly challenges a Cartesian spectator model. But it supports **direct, embodied involvement in a pre-existing world** at least as readily as it supports constitutive exclusion. The article currently converts an anti-representational phenomenology into an idealist or quasi-idealist constitution thesis.

This is particularly concerning because the September internal review says the Merleau-Ponty quotations had previously been “publisher-verified.” The absence of page locators and the failure to recover the wording indicate that the verification record itself needs correction. 

### Nagel: materially misread

“The view from nowhere is impossible” is a misleading condensation of Nagel’s position. Nagel regards complete escape from all perspective as unattainable, but the book defends the possibility and importance of **objective ascent**: progressively abstracting from one’s particular standpoint in order to understand a mind-independent world.

Nagel expressly affirms realism, describes the world as existing independently of our minds, and argues that our limited standpoint does not warrant denying that we know anything about things in themselves. He regards objective theories as partial rather than worthless. [Arnes+2](https://www2.arnes.si/~surdsust/Nagel1986.pdf)

This makes the article’s claim that increasingly abstract and relational physics takes us “farther from reality as it is” especially problematic. Nagel’s position is closer to the reverse: abstraction may remove us from immediate subjective appearance while improving our grasp of objective structure.

Nagel can support the proposition that no objective account exhausts subjective character. He cannot straightforwardly support the proposition that objective inquiry is trapped behind an impenetrable constitutive lens.

### Putnam: genuine quotation, selectively framed

The familiar sentence about mind and world “jointly” making up mind and world is genuine. But the surrounding passage says that the mind does **not** simply make up the world and labels the joint-making formulation metaphorical. The article reproduces the phrase most congenial to its thesis while omitting both restraints. [PagePlace+1](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139927086_A23869341/preview-9781139927086_A23869341.pdf)

The source should also be identified as **Putnam’s 1981 internal realism**, not “Putnam” without qualification. Putnam’s later movement toward natural or direct realism makes a single 1981 quotation an incomplete representation of his position. The later work is highly relevant because it directly challenges the interface or veil model implicit in “constitutive exclusion.” [Google Books+1](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Threefold_Cord.html)

### Heidegger and Husserl: real references, weak evidential role

The bibliographic references exist, and it is reasonable to place Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty in a broadly post-Kantian genealogy. But Heideggerian thrownness is not simply another argument that cognition filters an otherwise inaccessible world. It concerns Dasein’s always-already situated being-in-the-world.

Likewise, Husserl’s epoché is a methodological suspension of the natural attitude, not an empirical discovery that a mental “lens” blocks reality. Husserlian constitution is a technical account of intentional sense and objectivity; it should not be allowed to slide without argument into the physical claim that consciousness selects quantum outcomes. The current lack of page or section locators makes those transitions difficult to police. [[constitutive-exclusion]]

### Wheeler: much improved, but still presented too much as established physics

The corrected 1983 wording is source-faithful: Wheeler’s claim concerns an **elementary** phenomenon becoming a **registered** phenomenon, with registration exemplified through irreversible apparatus amplification. The original source does not put a conscious mind at the point where the result becomes registered. [Physics GG](https://physicsgg.me/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/wheeler_law_without_law.pdf)

The 1990 essay is more philosophically expansive. Wheeler’s observer-participant selects questions, operates apparatus and participates in communal meaning-making. Nevertheless, Wheeler explicitly says he is steering clear of the consciousness problem. His “it from bit” proposal is a speculative research programme or metaphysical agenda, not a result forced by quantum mechanics. [Cryptography and Quantum Information+1](https://cqi.inf.usi.ch/qic/wheeler.pdf)

The article’s new sentence that substituting consciousness for registration is “the Map’s move rather than his” is therefore correct. Two further refinements are needed:

1. Replace “Wheeler’s physics establishes” language with “Wheeler’s interpretive and cosmological proposal suggests.”
2. Avoid reducing Wheeler’s broader participatory universe entirely to a photodetector. Apparatus registration and human participation are distinguishable strands in Wheeler, but neither supplies the Map’s local neural dualism.

### Jacques et al.: accurate citation, interpretation presented as result

The 2007 delayed-choice experiment exists and the metadata are correct. It shows that the observed interference or which-path statistics depend on the measurement arrangement, including when the choice is made after the photon enters the apparatus. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0610241)

What it does **not** uniquely demonstrate is that there was literally no determinate pre-measurement fact, still less that consciousness constitutes that fact. That conclusion belongs to a Bohr–Wheeler-style interpretation. Bohmian and other realist accounts reproduce delayed-choice statistics while providing different accounts of what was physically occurring. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.06100)

A defensible sentence would be:

> The statistics depend on the later measurement context; on the Bohr–Wheeler interpretation, no definite prior wave-or-particle history should be attributed.

That separates observation from ontology.

### Kim and Buyl: current and substantially accurate, but tangential

Both recent references exist and broadly support the model-mediation caution. Kim et al.’s reported model count and error-correlation conclusion are accurate. Buyl et al. do compare 19 models and find systematic ideological variation associated with provenance and language. [Proceedings of Machine Learning Research+1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kim25e.html)

Their relevance is confined to the Map’s **selection and synthesis process**. They do not discount the historical existence of similar themes in Kant, phenomenology, Nagel and Wheeler, and they do not establish that the article’s particular philosophical errors were caused by model correlation. The live article now says this reasonably clearly.

## The central inference does not hold

The article’s central phrase—“we cannot access reality independent of our contribution”—has two readings:

1. **Trivial reading:** any act of access necessarily involves a perceiver, conceptual scheme or instrument.
2. **Substantive reading:** because access has conditions, we cannot acquire knowledge of anything that exists independently of those conditions.

The first is close to true by definition. The second does not follow from it.

A map necessarily uses a projection; that does not prevent it from tracking geographical relations. A telescope alters and processes incoming signals; that does not imply that the stars are constituted by the telescope. Scientific knowledge ordinarily depends on models, instruments and inferential reconstruction rather than unmediated acquaintance. “No access without mediation” is therefore not equivalent to “no knowledge of the mind-independent.”

The article sometimes changes the target again, treating four different things as though they were the same:

- the world absent this individual observer;
- the world absent any cognitive scheme;
- the categorical or intrinsic nature underlying physical structure;
- quantum values before a specified measurement context.

A Kantian limit concerning noumena, Nagel’s concern about subjective character and a delayed-choice result concerning incompatible observables do not converge on one exclusion unless these targets are first shown to be equivalent.

## Missing counterarguments

### Structural realism

This is the most conspicuous omission. Worrall’s epistemic structural realism was designed precisely to explain how science can retain realist knowledge through changing theories: what survives may be relational or mathematical structure rather than an inventory of intrinsic substances. [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/pdf/42970613.pdf)

That directly reverses one of the article’s key assumptions. Increasing abstraction and relationality need not move science farther from reality. They may isolate precisely the observer-independent invariants that warrant realism.

Two variants need engagement:

- **Epistemic structural realism:** intrinsic nature may remain unknown, while objective structure is knowable.
- **Ontic structural realism:** structure may be ontologically fundamental, making the demand for hidden categorical “stuff” questionable.

Unless the article refutes those possibilities, it cannot treat the inability to recover non-structural intrinsic nature as exclusion from “reality” simpliciter. At most it establishes a possible limit on one kind of metaphysical knowledge.

The article’s own “structural inference” method comes close to this reply but treats stable structure merely as evidence of the lens rather than as possible knowledge of the world. It needs to explain why cross-perspectival invariance, intervention and predictive robustness do not count as objective access.

### Naturalised epistemology and externalism

Quine rejects the demand that knowledge be justified from a standpoint outside science. Epistemology becomes the scientific study of how sensory input, cognition and theory are causally related. That does not provide Cartesian certainty, but it makes world-tracking a natural phenomenon rather than an impossible comparison with uninterpreted reality. [California State University Long Beach+1](https://home.csulb.edu/~cwallis/382/readings/quineepistemology%20nat.pdf)

Davidson’s criticism is even more directly relevant: the distinction between a conceptual scheme and wholly uninterpreted content is itself unstable. Abandoning an uninterpreted “given” does not require abandoning objective truth or ordinary contact with familiar objects. [Projekt+1](https://projekt.ht.lu.se/fileadmin/user_upload/sol/ovrigt/projekt_ccs/Davidson.pdf)

Externalist and reliabilist approaches add a further point: a subject need not possess an internal, perspective-free certificate proving that a process tracks reality. A belief can count as knowledge because it is reliably caused, calibrated and corrected by the world. The article currently assumes an internalist standard under which knowledge is legitimate only if the knower can step outside the entire knower–world relation and compare representation with reality directly.

That standard would make nearly all empirical knowledge impossible by stipulation.

### Direct realism and enactivism

The article assumes that cognitive contribution functions like an intervening screen or lens. Direct-realist and enactivist views deny that picture. Perception can be a bodily form of openness to and engagement with the environment, even though it is selective and organism-dependent.

Merleau-Ponty is one of the strongest resources for this reply. His situatedness need not imply imprisonment within constitution; it can describe how a subject is directly embedded in a world that precedes analysis. The article therefore omits a major interpretation of one of its own principal authorities.

### Calibration, intervention and error

The world’s resistance to expectation is itself epistemically important. Independent instruments, observers, sensory modalities and interventions can converge; failed predictions can expose errors; manipulations can discriminate between rival causal models.

None provides a God’s-eye view. But scientific realism has never generally required one. The article needs to explain why these fallible checks do not provide progressively better knowledge of observer-independent structures. Its current statement that “nothing available to us can check” an independent reality is much too strong.

### Quantum-interpretive underdetermination

The article acknowledges that Wheeler does not establish consciousness-dependent collapse, but its No-Many-Worlds paragraph partially reverses that concession. Rejecting Many-Worlds does not entail that consciousness constitutes outcomes. Alternatives include objective-collapse theories, Bohmian mechanics and interpretations that understand quantum states or outcomes relationally or agent-relatively without interactionist dualism.

Nor is Many-Worlds adequately summarized as an observer merely discovering which already-complete branch they occupy. The roles of decoherence, branching, self-location and probability are more complicated. The tenet can exclude Many-Worlds as a site commitment, but it cannot use that exclusion as positive evidence for consciousness-driven selection.

## Tenet-protective bracketing

The revised article is commendably candid about some of its circularity. Nevertheless, five protective moves remain.

### 1. Causal influence is silently upgraded to constitution

The Bidirectional Interaction section effectively reasons:

> Consciousness causally influences physical outcomes; therefore it partly constitutes reality; therefore reality independent of that contribution is excluded.

The middle step needs argument. Causes routinely influence outcomes without constituting the ontology or epistemic conditions of the affected system. A magnet alters an experiment but does not thereby create an unknowable “reality independent of magnetism.” The article must distinguish:

- ordinary causal contribution;
- transcendental constitution of objects of experience;
- semantic or conceptual constitution;
- physical selection of quantum outcomes.

At present, “constitutes” equivocates among all four.

### 2. The nonphysical contribution is defined as undetectable

The Dualism section says that if the contribution is nonphysical, physical methods will be categorically unable to isolate it. That gives the tenet explanatory immunity: failure to detect the contribution becomes exactly what dualism predicts.

The article now correctly says this fit is not independent evidence for dualism. But it still needs a discriminator between:

- a real but physically undetectable influence;
- no additional influence at all;
- an entirely physical mechanism not yet understood.

Without different observable expectations, “ontological depth” is interpretive decoration rather than explanation.

### 3. Wheeler is narrowed when objections arise but retained rhetorically

When Wheeler’s cosmological participancy threatens to imply universal observer-dependence, the Map narrows its own claim to local neural modulation. When the article wants historical or scientific resonance, it retains Wheeler’s participatory vocabulary.

That is not formally contradictory, but it permits an advantageous scope-switch. The article should say plainly that Wheeler supplies an analogy, not independent support for either dualism or the local mechanism.

### 4. Rejection of Many-Worlds is treated as a positive route to consciousness

The article’s own source audit establishes only that Wheeler’s registration claim is compatible with several metaphysical readings. The No-Many-Worlds tenet removes one reading; it does not establish the Map’s preferred one. Treating “not Many-Worlds” as “therefore genuinely consciousness-constituted outcomes” is a false binary.

### 5. The thesis is close to self-sealing

If every successful observation, intervention or cross-check remains “inside the lens,” then no evidence can count against the exclusion. That makes the claim difficult to distinguish from the trivial statement that all knowledge is acquired by knowers.

A structural void should have conditions under which its scope would be reduced. For example: would successful convergence among radically different perceptual architectures count against the thesis? Would an empirically successful structural realism? Would a complete causal explanation of observer effects? The article currently identifies no such defeaters.

The AI section is better disciplined: it explicitly notes that treating artificial systems as lacking consciousness would merely apply the dualist premise rather than independently confirm it. That calibration should be extended to the rest of the article. One further correction is needed, however: dualism by itself does not imply that artificial systems lack phenomenal consciousness.

## Integration with neighbouring articles

### Observation and Measurement Void

The article distinguishes the two topics clearly at the level of navigation, but its claim that the observation void is a consequence of constitutive exclusion is too strong. Measurement disturbance can occur in a wholly mind-independent world; a probe may physically alter its target without consciousness constituting either one. Conversely, perspectival or conceptual conditioning can occur without any significant physical disturbance. [[observation-and-measurement-void]]

The two articles should therefore be related through a matrix rather than a hierarchy:

- physical intervention by apparatus;
- selection of an observable or question;
- conceptual interpretation;
- phenomenal access;
- alleged nonphysical outcome selection.

At present, those mechanisms are too easily grouped under “observation.”

### Self-Opacity

The revision improved this relationship by replacing total subsumption with the claim that only **much** of self-opacity may be a special case. That is correct. Self-opacity can arise through computational limits, confabulation, divided cognitive architecture, self-reference and relational properties visible only through comparison with other agents. Those mechanisms do not require a constitutive limit on access to reality. [[self-opacity]]

The *Self-Opacity* article is also better calibrated regarding dualism: it says dualism offers a reading rather than receiving evidential support. That formulation should be the template for the present article and the other void pages.

### Intrinsic Nature Void

The present article treats the intrinsic-nature void as a downstream expression of constitutive exclusion. That relation is conditional on rejecting structural realism. An epistemic structural realist may agree that categorical intrinsic nature is unknown while maintaining that objective relational structure is genuine knowledge of reality. An ontic structural realist may deny that a further intrinsic substrate is required at all. [[intrinsic-nature-void]]

There is also a site-wide calibration inconsistency: the intrinsic-nature article still speaks of dualism receiving independent support, whereas *Self-Opacity* and the present article’s revised P‑V2 language say a tenet merely supplies a compatible interpretation.

### Subject–Object Distinction

This is the most important cross-article tension. The subject–object article treats the distinction as a genuine structural feature of reality and links it to dualism. The present article invokes phenomenology to emphasize the inseparability and mutual implication of subject and object. [[the-subject-object-distinction-as-philosophical-discovery]]

These propositions can coexist, but only if the site distinguishes:

- **phenomenal polarity:** experience presents a subject–object orientation;
- **epistemic asymmetry:** the subject knows objects and itself differently;
- **phenomenological inseparability:** neither pole is intelligible in lived experience without the other;
- **ontological dualism:** subject and physical object belong to irreducibly different kinds;
- **causal interaction:** those kinds influence one another.

Phenomenological correlation establishes neither substance dualism nor consciousness-dependent physical constitution. The current articles move among these levels too freely. The subject–object page also inherits the over-simple Nagel reading and needs the same correction.

## Reference currency

The bibliography is not stale simply because Kant, Husserl or Wheeler are old; these are appropriate primary sources for historical claims. Its **argumentative coverage**, however, is stale and one-sided.

The core philosophy and physics bibliography effectively ends with the 2007 delayed-choice experiment. The 2025–26 papers concern LLM methodology rather than the subject matter. Missing are:

- structural realism and its critics;
- Quinean naturalisation, Davidson’s scheme/content critique and externalist epistemology;
- Putnam’s later natural realism;
- direct-realist and enactivist interpretations of phenomenology;
- modern quantum-foundations work separating experimental results from interpretation;
- contemporary scholarship on whether Kantian and phenomenological “constitution” is epistemic, semantic, transcendental or ontological.

The article consequently cites classic authorities but does not cite the literature most capable of resisting its synthesis.

## Concrete potential improvements

1. **Rewrite the lead of “The Constitutive Exclusion” as a four-level claim ladder:** conditioned access; absence of a total perspective-free view; possible limits on intrinsic knowledge; alleged consciousness-dependent physical constitution. Mark explicitly which levels are argued, which are merely possible and which are tenet-conditional.

2. **Remove both Merleau-Ponty quotations until page-level provenance is produced.** Replace them with verified passages from the 2012 edition, including the anti-construction language in the Preface, and present direct-realist and constitutive interpretations side by side.

3. **Rewrite the Nagel paragraph.** Say that complete detachment is unattainable but objective ascent and partial knowledge of a mind-independent world remain possible. Delete the suggestion that relational abstraction necessarily moves science farther from reality.

4. **Contextualise Putnam’s 1981 quotation.** Include his warning that the formulation is metaphorical and that the mind does not simply make up the world; add a paragraph on his later natural/direct realism.

5. **Replace the “filter” metaphor in the Kant section.** Distinguish forms of intuition, categories, appearances and things in themselves, and use A/B section locators rather than a whole-book citation.

6. **Split every quantum claim into “experimental result” and “interpretation.”** For Jacques et al., state the observed dependence on measurement context first, then separately identify Wheeler–Bohr, Bohmian, objective-collapse and other readings.

7. **Change “Wheeler’s physics” to “Wheeler’s interpretive programme” where appropriate.** Preserve the distinction among apparatus registration, choice of experimental question, intersubjective communication and consciousness.

8. **Add a substantial structural-realism objection section.** Address both epistemic and ontic structural realism and explain why observer-independent invariants would—or would not—count as access to reality.

9. **Add a naturalised-epistemology section.** Engage Quine, Davidson, reliabilism and causal/externalist theories of knowledge rather than assuming that knowledge requires an outside-the-system comparison.

10. **Add a direct-realism/enactivism objection.** Use Merleau-Ponty as a source for the possibility that embodiment is a mode of contact with reality rather than an intervening veil.

11. **Rewrite the tenet section so that causal contribution is not equated with transcendental or ontological constitution.** For each tenet, specify whether it supplies evidence, removes a defeater, offers an interpretation or merely states a framework commitment.

12. **Delete the implication that rejecting Many-Worlds positively supports consciousness-driven collapse.** List the remaining non-MWI alternatives and state what additional evidence would distinguish the Map’s view from them.

13. **Revise “Observation and Measurement Void” to separate physical disturbance, contextual observable selection, conceptual interpretation and phenomenal access.** Do not treat every form of observer-dependence as evidence of a nonphysical contribution.

14. **Use “Self-Opacity” as the calibration model for all void articles.** Its formulation—dualism offers a reading but does not receive support—should replace stronger evidential claims in “Intrinsic Nature Void” and related pages.

15. **Revise “Intrinsic Nature Void” to engage structural realism directly.** Ask whether unknown intrinsic nature is a genuine missing fact, merely underdetermined, or unnecessary on an ontic-structural account.

16. **Revise “Subject–Object Distinction” with an explicit taxonomy of phenomenal polarity, epistemic asymmetry, phenomenological inseparability, ontological dualism and causal interaction.** Correct its Nagel discussion and prevent phenomenological correlation from being counted as evidence for substance dualism.

17. **Revise the dedicated Wheeler article to distinguish quantum registration from the broader observer-participant rhetoric.** Label the connection to local neural dualism as a Map analogy rather than historical or physical support.

18. **Introduce sentence-level citation provenance across the site.** Every quotation should record edition, translator, page and a retained scan or excerpt; automated verification should fail when a direct quotation has only a whole-book reference.

19. **Correct the changelog or verification ledger concerning the Merleau-Ponty quotations.** A prior “publisher-verified” designation should not survive once the quoted wording cannot be recovered from the cited edition.

20. **Add three labels to the site’s methodology and, where useful, to article prose:** “source claim,” “Map inference” and “tenet-conditional consequence.” This would prevent historical authorities from silently underwriting conclusions they did not hold.

21. **Require every structural-void article to name potential defeaters.** The methodology should ask what observation, convergence, theoretical success or rival explanation would narrow or remove the alleged void; a claim with no possible reducer should be marked framework-definitional rather than evidential.

22. **Build a cross-article consistency check for repeated propositions.** Claims such as “dualism receives support,” “Wheeler makes consciousness constitutive,” “the view from nowhere is impossible” and “all measurement involves consciousness” should be tracked centrally so a correction in one article cannot remain unrepaired in its neighbours.
