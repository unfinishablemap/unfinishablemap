## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would accept every datum and reject the name. Suppressed representations plus inhibitory control is a complete neural story: Shtulman and Valcarcel, Kelemen, and Leroux, which the page cites for persistence, are findings about competing representations and frontal inhibition. The reply at "A materialist might object" answers only the pruning version of materialism. The inhibition version, which the page's own lead paragraph supplies, predicts the same access failure, and the page does not say what "the gap between what the brain retains and what consciousness can re-enter" adds to "inhibited representations do not drive report".

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would press the heterophenomenology point. The evidence for a lost "novice phenomenology" is that experts mispredict novices (curse of knowledge, the tapper study, expert blind spot). Those are failures of third-person prediction about other people. The page converts them into a first-person claim that the expert "cannot hear the bare tapping as others heard it". The inference from "cannot predict the uninformed" to "cannot re-occupy one's own earlier experience" is not argued. Camerer et al. themselves treat hindsight, the own-past case, as a separate "analogue".

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark has little to attack, since the page makes no quantum claim, and he would say so of the Minimal Quantum Interaction paragraph: "consciousness already economising its involvement" in skill is a fact about attention and practice at the scale of seconds and years. It is an analogy to the tenet's word "minimal" and carries no evidence about quantum outcomes. The page says "suggestive parallel", which is honest, but the paragraph does no work.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch would object that the No Many Worlds paragraph misdescribes the rival. On Many Worlds the expert in every branch has the same history of learning and the same closed access; no branch offers "the novice perspective" to this expert. The felt loss is predicted identically. The 2026-07-07 review lists "MWI indexical" as a framework-boundary disagreement not to be re-flagged, and this review does not re-raise the tenet disagreement. The narrower point stands apart from it: the sentence "The phenomenology of the expertise void resists this framing" reads a datum as counting against a rival that predicts the same datum. It is not on the Priority List.

### The Empiricist
A Popperian would ask what observation would count against the void. The lead paragraph helps: persistence findings refuted the "overwritten" reading and the page withdrew it. The remaining claim, closed phenomenal access with sub-personal persistence, is harder to test. "Deliberate de-skilling" is cited both as showing the regime "is still there to be re-engaged" and as showing it can be "temporarily disrupted, though not fully reversed". If blind contour drawing re-engages the pre-expert regime, access is partly open, and the page should say which outcome of a de-skilling study would count as re-entry.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would question the "novice regime" as a thing that persists and could be re-occupied. The novice's seeing was itself conditioned and changing; there was no stable prior standpoint to return to. The page's contrast between "persists sub-personally" and "cannot be occupied" treats the earlier state as a place. The 2026-07-07 review lists this as a framework-boundary disagreement, and it is noted here only for completeness.

- **Current content says**: "On identity theories, consciousness *is* the relevant computational process, so improved computation should entail richer conscious access, not less; the expertise void shows the opposite."
