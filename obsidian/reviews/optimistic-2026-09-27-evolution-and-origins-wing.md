## Executive Summary

The wing's newer and more recently reviewed pages hold their calibration very well. `biological-teleology-and-the-interface-framework` separates four senses of "goal" and places genuine purpose only at the fourth. `cetacean-and-corvid-consciousness` keeps both lineages at *realistic possibility, contested* and lets Gutfreund's agnostic challenge limit every species claim. `emergent-dualism` states the Map's closest dualist rival fairly and admits that its own preference for coupling moves a debt without paying it. On Many Worlds, `consciousness-evolution-and-biology` L148 and `biological-teleology…` L118 are the best treatments in the wing. Each grants the Everettian a branch-local story and puts the dispute on global exclusion, a posit.

The weak points are **drift between pages**, in two directions:

1. **Generation versus coupling is settled silently, and in opposite ways.** `emergent-dualism` L80-84 calls "emerges from the brain" versus "independent and merely coupled" a *substantive open commitment*, resolved provisionally. Yet `evolution-under-dualism` L48/L62 presents the receiver/interface picture as simply what dualism says ("Brains are not consciousness generators"). `consciousness-evolution-and-biology` L126 does the same. `agent-teleology` L79 says consciousness is something "evolution produced but does not fully explain". Its next paragraph (L81) says evolution produced interfaces, "not generators of experience". `voids/origin-of-consciousness` L93 lists "Not emergence" as a settled negative, although the Map's own concept page treats Hasker's emergent dualism as "a coherent and well-defended horn" (L84).
2. **The quick Many Worlds argument survives in three places the hubs have moved past.** These are `evolution-under-dualism` L160 ("the unbiased branch also exists, making consciousness's biasing irrelevant") and `agent-teleology` L114 and L126 ("conscious 'selection' selects nothing"). `consciousness-evolution-and-biology` L148 names this exact move as "closer to a restatement of the tenet" than an argument.

Two smaller calibration items follow the same pattern. `agent-teleology` L93 missed today's Rajan/frontal-theta sweep. `evolution-under-dualism` L106 calls corvid and primate forebrains "structurally similar", while `cetacean-and-corvid-consciousness` L49/L81 builds its whole case on their being radically different. Nothing in the wing moves a minimal organism up the evidential scale. One exemplar in `biological-teleology…` L86 cuts against the page's own four-senses discipline (Calibration concern 4).

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`consciousness-evolution-and-biology` L74-78 turns the zombie argument into an evolutionary diagnostic without overselling it. The puzzle is read as a "reductio against the view that biological function exhausts fitness" (L74). The physicalist's denial of zombie possibility is stated fairly (L76). Then comes the interactionist's own move: if consciousness biases quantum outcomes, "a behaviourally-matched zombie is therefore not a physical duplicate, and selection *can* reach the difference consciousness makes" (L78). The same paragraph states the cost "explicit and symmetrical": the Map owes an account of the interface. Chalmers would see the hard problem used as a constraint on theories, not as a trump card. The closing triage (L132-136) says theories that ignore the problem fail, those that dissolve it are unstable, and those that embrace it are incomplete. The last clause includes the Map's own view.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

`evolution-under-dualism` L78 answers the obvious objection to its own "fixed domain" model: a timeless domain sounds like the passive radio the page had just rejected. The reply is precise: "The fixity is about the menu, not about whether anything is ordered from it." Stapp would recognise the shape of his own separation between the Process-1 choice and the state space it chooses over. `cetacean-and-corvid-consciousness` L120 draws the right conclusion for the physics: any interface mechanism predicts architecture independence, "including those that do not rely on sustained coherence at all". That frees the comparative argument from the microtubule bet.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`agent-teleology` L91 describes purpose from the inside with three features: *directedness* (leaning toward a future not yet actual), *ownership*, and *normativity* (what *ought* to result). Nagel would welcome a concept page that treats first-person purposiveness as data to describe before any theory handles it. `voids/origin-of-consciousness` L101-107 does similar work for the origin question. "The asymmetry of certainty and opacity", total presence combined with total mystery about origin, is a good phenomenological account of a void.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

`evolution-under-dualism` L94-98 gives the "unfolding" model, Whitehead's concrescence as the physical trace of richer integration, a real place among the options. Then it says plainly that the model is "philosophically rich but empirically elusive". `agent-teleology` L75 uses subjective aim as "a model for how directed selection works in conscious agents" and explicitly declines Whitehead's panpsychism. Both are the right use of process resonance: a way of modelling, not a source of evidence. Nothing in the wing uses Whitehead-friendliness to move a boundary organism up the scale.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`biological-teleology-and-the-interface-framework` L48-60 is the most useful addition to the agency cluster in some time. It separates attractor stability, control-theoretic set-points, represented goals and phenomenally valued goals, and places genuine purpose only at the fourth. Its key sentence is: "competence documented at the first two licenses nothing about the fourth in either direction." Kane's view needs exactly this: agency tied to valued reasons, not to goal-shaped dynamics. The comparison table at L70-76 is one of the clearest in the wing. The "Counterfactuals" row ("If the agent had chosen differently, different action would follow") states the libertarian point in one line.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`voids/origin-of-consciousness` L111-119 is restrained about its own evidence. Persistence is not proof, "the nature of stars persisted as a mystery"-style caveats appear in the sibling `emergence-void` L62, and the regress argument "applies to all fundamental questions, not uniquely to consciousness" (L117). McGinn would approve of a void page that keeps "possibly impossible" separate from "impossible" (L62).

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This persona's verdict matters most for the wing, because the wing touches animal consciousness, minimal organisms, and evolutionary arguments built on the tenets.

- **`cetacean-and-corvid-consciousness` is the exemplar.** L41 places both lineages at *realistic possibility, contested* and the amplification hypothesis at *live hypothesis*. It then says: "The Map's tenets remove a defeater … they do not, by themselves, raise evidence-grade." L114 repeats the discipline at the point where the convergent-evolution argument is most tempting. L57 reports that corvid mirror self-recognition failed replication (Soler et al. 2020; Vanhooland et al. 2023) rather than keeping the older headline result. L108 concedes that the preference for consciousness vocabulary "is tenet-driven, not forced by the comparative evidence". This is *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating* done correctly.
- **`evolution-under-dualism` L74** could have treated the nematode's 302 neurons as a crude but real interface. Instead it adds "or perhaps failing to interface at all". That restraint deserves praise.
- **`fitness-beats-truth` L69** handles the symmetry objection honestly: "The FBT theorem does not *support* dualism—it removes one class of objection to it." L75 anchors the theorem as *strongly supported* at one bounded site (perception) and gives other cognitive sites only *live hypothesis*.
- **Where the persona dissents**, see Calibration concern 4 (the moth exemplar) and concern 5 (the formal statement in `fitness-beats-truth`).

## Calibration Concerns (refine-draft, not expansion)

**1. Generation versus coupling is settled silently in four pages, in opposite directions.** `emergent-dualism` L80-84 shows that "does consciousness emerge from neural complexity, or is it independent and coupled?" is open, and resolved only *provisionally*. Other pages treat it as settled:
- `evolution-under-dualism` L48 ("systems capable of interfacing with something beyond matter") and L62 ("Brains are not consciousness generators; they are consciousness receivers") present coupling as what *dualism* implies. They never mention that Hasker's emergent dualism is a dualism in which evolution *does* produce the subject. All three "What Evolves?" models (L68-106) assume independence.
- `consciousness-evolution-and-biology` L126: "Brains didn't produce consciousness".
- `agent-teleology` L79 goes the other way ("consciousness, which evolution produced but does not fully explain"), then L81 reverts ("not generators of experience"). That contradiction sits within one section.
- `voids/origin-of-consciousness` L93 lists "Not emergence" among settled negatives. Its reason, "presupposes consciousness in the explanation", misdescribes the problem: emergence from non-experience does not presuppose experience. It is disputed on intelligibility grounds.

Fix: a length-neutral qualifier in each of the four, naming the choice as the Map's *provisional* coupling preference, with a piped link to `[[emergent-dualism]]`. `agent-teleology` L79 needs the contradiction removed.

**2. Many Worlds: the quick argument survives in `evolution-under-dualism` L160 and `agent-teleology` L114 and L126.** "Under Many Worlds, the unbiased branch also exists, making consciousness's biasing irrelevant to the total outcome" (EUD L160). "Conscious 'selection' selects nothing—all alternatives are equally real" (AT L126). `consciousness-evolution-and-biology` L148, `biological-teleology…` L118 and `cetacean-and-corvid-consciousness` L150 all concede that an Everettian gets branch-local selection and causal efficacy, and that the Map's objection rests on global exclusion, a posit (`tenets/background-commitments`). The template is BT L118. AT L114 lists "Confirming Many Worlds" under "What would challenge this view?". That is roughly right, but the reason ("vacuous") should become "the unchosen would not be globally nonactual".

**3. `agent-teleology` L93 missed today's Rajan/frontal-theta sweep.** Commit `3e228ba788` rescoped the willed-versus-instructed evidence in 17 obsidian files. `agent-causation` now reads "a decision-load signature physicalism also predicts, untested for movement" (L146). `agent-teleology` was not touched. L93 still lists "frontal theta, bidirectional coherence, the 300ms voluntary deployment window" as correlates of purposive phenomenology, and calls consciousness-as-cause "the parsimonious interpretation". That is a parsimony appeal on a page whose own L128 says parsimony is unreliable here. A related remnant is `agent-causation` L131, which still says motor selection "shows a parallel signature (… frontal theta, bidirectional coherence …)". That contradicts the same page's L146 ("untested for movement"). Both are sibling misses of that sweep. Verify with `git log -S "bidirectional coherence"`.

**4. `biological-teleology…` L86: the moth exemplar contradicts the page's own four senses.** Nothing is upgraded, since the sentence is conditional ("may have … But if"). However, the example chosen to show "the fourth sense in its barest form" is moth flight toward light, a textbook taxis. By the page's L54 logic (chemotaxis is a set-point, sense two), a taxis is exactly the competence that "licenses nothing about the fourth". The illustration undercuts the discipline the page sets up, and it sits awkwardly beside `marginal-organism-scope-of-value-sensitive-selection` L90, which says tenet-coherence must not be converted into valence-denominated selection. Fix: keep the conditional point, but use a case that is not a taxis, or add one clause saying the moth's phototaxis as observed is sense-two behaviour and the fourth sense is hypothesised on top of it.

**5. `fitness-beats-truth` L29 against L37: the theorem is stated two different ways.** L29: "for all fitness functions and all probability distributions … fitness-tuned perception *strictly dominates* veridical perception". L37: fitness-tuned strategies "achieve *equal or higher* expected fitness". Both cannot be the theorem, and the published statement (Prakash et al. 2021) is usually given probabilistically or generically, not universally. **Verify at the publisher before editing.** I could not retrieve the abstract: Springer redirects to login, the OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar abstracts are elided, and I did not read it. L39 ("does not depend on the specific structure of the environment") and L85 ("these results *establish*") read as stronger than the page's own Contestation section allows. A missing counter-literature item is Berke, Walter-Terrill, Jara-Ettinger & Scholl (2022), "Flexible Goals Require that Inflexible Perceptual Systems Produce Veridical Representations", *Cognitive Science* 46, doi:10.1111/cogs.13195. Its metadata is verified on Crossref. Its argument is that organisms with several changing fitness functions favour veridical perception. Treat that argument as a **lead**; I did not read the abstract. It bears directly on the *strongly supported* anchor at L75.

**6. `evolution-under-dualism` L106 against `cetacean-and-corvid-consciousness` L49/L81.** EUD proposes a test using "convergently evolved but structurally similar interfaces (such as corvid and primate forebrains)". The cetacean page's case rests on corvid pallium being "nuclear clusters rather than cortical layers", "radically different substrates". One of the two framings has to give way. (Lead, unverified here: Stacho et al. 2020 *Science* reported cortex-like columnar and laminar circuitry in bird pallium, which would support EUD's framing and date the cetacean page's L49. `grep -rlF Stacho` finds no content page citing it.)

**7. `cetacean-and-corvid-consciousness` L47: "cortical-neuron counts approaching the human range (Roth & Dicke 2005)" and "Cortical-neuron parity".** As I remember that paper's table, it gives the bottlenose dolphin roughly half the human cortical-neuron count. Parity was reported for the false killer whale, and a later study reports a much higher count for pilot whales. This is from memory and is a **lead, not a finding**: check the table at the publisher before changing the text. The page is 137 words below hard, so any fix must be length-neutral.

**8. `voids/emergence-void`: the "weak emergence gap" contains a phenomenal term.** L52 and L76 ask why vibrating particles "constitute *felt warmth*" / "*that* thermal experience". That is the consciousness gap placed inside the temperature case, so the "universality across domains" evidence (L60) partly assumes what it sets out to show. Separately, L94 calls large language models "strongly emergent". In the Map's own `[[emergence]]` usage, strong emergence means not deducible even in principle. Scaling-emergent capabilities are the textbook weak case. L106 then draws dualist support from the gap being *deeper* at consciousness, while L110 calls the hard problem "consciousness's particular expression of a universal cognitive limit". If the gap is the same kind everywhere, depth alone does not favour dualism. The page should say that it needs a difference in *kind*.
