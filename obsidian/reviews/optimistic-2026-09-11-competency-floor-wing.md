## Executive Summary

This wing is the strongest-calibrated cluster this reviewer has measured. Across nine independently written articles the same discipline holds without a single lapse: every verdict about what a sponge, a bacterium, a plant, an immune system or an organoid does *not* feel is marked **framework-relative** rather than demonstrated, and Tenet 2's minimality is repeatedly scoped to the *magnitude* of non-physical influence rather than allowed to become a parsimony heuristic about how experience is distributed. The Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist — personas this skill deliberately sets in tension — **converge** here rather than conflict, which on the skill's own criterion means the wing has resolved the possibility/probability problem honestly. There is no calibration concern to report.

The findings are therefore **structural, not argumentative**. The wing's articles talk to each other well (38 body-link edges among 11 nodes) but the three artefacts that carry the wing's calibration apparatus — the concept page, the five-tier scale, and the discipline document — are barely wired in, and the cluster's canonical tenet-scoping has no row in the matrix built to hold exactly that. Everything below costs link-work, not article-work.

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The wing keeps the explanatory gap exactly where it belongs and refuses to let mechanism close it. [[plant-cognition-and-the-plant-neurobiology-debate]] names the structure of the whole plant-neurobiology dispute in one stroke — "everyone broadly agrees on the behaviour, and disagrees about which words it earns" — and then identifies the terminology war as "that open question wearing empirical clothes." That is the explanatory gap diagnosed in the wild, in a live scientific controversy, rather than asserted from the armchair.

[[minimal-consciousness]] presses the same point at the threshold: "The explanatory gap applies to the smallest degree: why is there *any* experience rather than none?" And its treatment of the levels debate is better than most of the literature — if minimality turns out to be plural (Bayne, Hohwy & Owen; Birch, Schnell & Clayton), "the threshold problem does not dissolve; it multiplies," leaving "not one unexplained edge but several."

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Tenet 2 is handled with unusual care here, and the care runs *against* the framework's convenience. [[neuron-less-animals-sponges-placozoans-and-the-lower-bound-of-cognition]] could trivially have run "minimal interaction implies no interface in a sponge, therefore nothing is felt." It declines, and constructs the counterexample that defuses its own argument: "A sponge experience that made no difference to any physical outcome would add no non-physical footprint at all, and minimality would have nothing to weigh against it."

[[the-enteric-nervous-system-and-the-gut-brain-distributed-interface-question]] is the wing's best piece of interface reasoning. It enumerates three readings, gives the distributed-interface reading (b) its own section flagged "raised but least supported," and says plainly that it is flagged "precisely so as not to assume it away." It then names the cost of its own preferred reading: (a) "must explain why a system with complete CNS-like reflex architecture is nonetheless *outside* the interface, without lapsing into ad-hoc brain-chauvinism — privileging the brain simply because it is the brain."

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The wing never loses the first-person question behind third-person competence. [[brain-organoids-and-the-organoid-intelligence-question]] locates the real asymmetry precisely: where the same neural facts are read as indicating experience in humans, "there the inference is anchored by first-person report, which the dish cannot give." That is the honest reason the cases differ, and it is a phenomenological reason, not a neuroanatomical one.

[[the-enteric-nervous-system-and-the-gut-brain-distributed-interface-question]] supplies the phenomenological datum on its own side of the ledger: "There is no felt agency of enteric control — we do not consciously select peristalsis, secretion, or the timing of a migrating motor complex."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

The wing is genuinely hospitable to chemistry-first and continuity-based accounts, and — crucially — states the opposing case at full strength rather than knocking it down. [[neuron-less-animals-sponges-placozoans-and-the-lower-bound-of-cognition]] says of Bechtel and Bich's "eating is a cognitive activity" thesis that it "deserves to be stated at full strength rather than caricatured," and then concedes that new results "strengthen rather than weaken their hand." [[bacterial-chemotaxis-and-minimal-biogenic-cognition]] grants the Cellular Basis of Consciousness programme its continuity claim outright: the Map "grants CBC's continuity of *biology* — excitable membranes really do run from bacteria to neurons — while denying that the continuity of biology carries a continuity of *phenomenality*."

Per this skill's constraint, none of that praise is permitted to cash out as a tier-upgrade. It does not, anywhere in this wing. See the next section.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

[[single-cell-proto-agency-and-the-evidence-problem]] contains the wing's sharpest piece of epistemology, and it bears directly on whether indeterminacy can ever be read as agency. Having shown that the stochastic "noise" in bacterial chemotaxis is functionally integrated into the search rather than error riding on top of it, the article refuses to convert that into a proto-agency result: the two readings "can be two descriptions of the *same* mechanism, converging on identical predicted behaviour and diverging only in what they are willing to *call* it." The article then does something rarer still — it flags its own synthesis as a synthesis: "This convergence is the Map's own reading of the two literatures rather than a published position on either side."

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

[[single-cell-proto-agency-and-the-evidence-problem]] ends where a mysterian would want it to: the question at the prokaryotic floor is "a conceptual question, not one an experiment resolves," and the article treats that as "a result about the limits of parsimony, not a failure to find one." The Sourjik observation is the finest detail in the wing — the researcher who introduced in vivo FRET to bacterial chemotaxis, and so built the means of *seeing* the noise, is a co-author on the paper calling it mere noise. "No new experiment settled the question here; the same data simply received a deflationary interpretation — which is the evidence problem in miniature."

[[minimal-consciousness]] is equally candid about its own falsifiability: after splitting its challenge conditions into in-practice and in-principle, it concludes "The framework's net falsifiability is genuinely modest."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This persona's verdict is load-bearing for this wing, and it is strongly positive.** Every structural opportunity to slide a boundary case up the evidential scale on tenet-load is declined, explicitly, in the article's own voice.

Measured, not impressionistic: the phrase *realistic possibility* occurs exactly twice in the whole wing, and **both occurrences are quotations that the article then refuses to read as a tier verdict**.

1. [[minimal-consciousness]] quotes the 2024 New York Declaration and immediately scopes it: "The final clause carries the weight. The Declaration states a rule for acting under uncertainty, scoped to decisions affecting the animal; it does not claim consciousness has been established in the creatures it covers. Reading it as a verdict on the evidence would overstate what its signatories asserted."
2. [[brain-organoids-and-the-organoid-intelligence-question]] quotes Birch's own sentience-candidate threshold and separates policy from ontology: "Candidature is a policy threshold… But the rule fixes when ignoring the possibility of an experiencer becomes reckless; it does not locate the interface."

Four further things this persona wants named as praise-worthy things *not* done:

- **The MPE-to-organism analogy is refused as evidence.** [[minimal-consciousness]] takes the tempting bridge from a meditator's content-stripped state to a nematode's inner life and blocks it three ways, concluding: "The analogy therefore neither raises nor lowers the probability that simple organisms are conscious; it is a methodological bridge to be inspected, not evidence, and per [[evidential-status-discipline]] tenet-coherence does not upgrade where the empirical case stands." This is the *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating* pattern stated almost verbatim.
- **A free ally is refused.** [[minimal-consciousness]] places IIT accurately rather than conveniently: "it is a physicalist identity theory… Its verdict that consciousness is widespread therefore lends the Map no support against physicalism." An article wanting allies would have banked IIT's permissiveness.
- **The toolkit discipline upgrades itself against a stronger defeater.** [[neuron-less-animals-sponges-placozoans-and-the-lower-bound-of-cognition]] states the rule — "toolkit presence — genomic or cell-typological — is not functional presence, and functional presence is not experience" — and then tests it against Najle et al. 2023, which moved the parts-list from genome to cell type: "The discipline below must therefore hold against this stronger datum, not just the 2010 genome — and it does."
- **A researcher's restraint is treated as evidence rather than as a gap.** [[the-cognitive-immune-system-and-immunological-selfhood]] notes that Irun Cohen, who coined the immunological homunculus, declines the phenomenal step himself, and adds: "That restraint, like Michael Levin's parallel restraint about xenobots in [[basal-and-bioelectric-cognition]], is evidence the Map uses rather than a gap it must fill."

Two more calibration habits worth commending. [[the-enteric-nervous-system-and-the-gut-brain-distributed-interface-question]] refuses a precise figure it could have asserted: "~500 million… estimates in the literature range roughly 200-600 million, and older popular sources tied to Gershon's book sometimes quote 'over 100 million,' so the figure should be read as an order-of-magnitude consensus rather than a precise count." And [[plant-cognition-and-the-plant-neurobiology-debate]] concedes a live objection it has not answered — Mallatt et al.'s IIT-internal reductio — with "It stands as a live countermodel the Map has not answered — not an absence of skeptic argument."

**Verdict: no possibility/probability slippage found in this wing. Process Philosopher and Hardline Empiricist converge.** Per this skill's own criterion, that means the wing has resolved the tension honestly, and no `refine-draft` calibration task is warranted.

## Content Strengths

### neuron-less-animals-sponges-placozoans-and-the-lower-bound-of-cognition

- **Strongest point**: the Tenet 2 scoping paragraph, which distinguishes an allocation rule about causal footprint from a claim about distribution of experience, then supplies the counterexample that limits its own reach.
- **Notable quote**: "That is a framework-relative rule about where influence is exerted, not a demonstration that the sneeze or the feeding pause is unfelt."
- **Why it works**: the article's headline claim (behaviour is no marker of the interface) is *strengthened* by refusing the stronger claim, because the weaker one is the one the evidence actually supports.

### plant-cognition-and-the-plant-neurobiology-debate

- **Strongest point**: the split verdict — agreeing with the deflationists' conclusion while rejecting their argument, and keeping the two apart explicitly.
- **Notable quote**: "Taiz et al.'s conclusion may well be right; their argument for it is more confident than its evidence base supports, and rests besides on a foundation the Map does not accept."
- **Why it works**: the first objection to neural necessity "needs no dualism at all" — it is a methodological point about Feinberg and Mallatt's sampling. Leading with the framework-independent objection and only then reaching for dualism is the right order, and rare.

### single-cell-proto-agency-and-the-evidence-problem

- **Strongest point**: reframing "do cells choose?" as "what observation could tell proto-choice apart from complete mechanism?" — converting an unanswerable question into a tractable result about evidence.
- **Notable quote**: "The person who developed the means of seeing the noise is on record calling it noise."
- **Why it works**: it also disposes of the interventionist criterion honestly — if agency is graded and scale-relative by construction, "is the cell *really* a proto-agent, yes or no?" is mis-posed. "This dissolves the question rather than answering it, which is itself a result the article can bank."

### synthetic-minimal-agents-and-the-engineered-decoupling

- **Strongest point**: it is the only wing member that consults the [[tenets]] dependency matrix and splits the bare-phenomenality question from the interface-location question accordingly.
- **Notable quote**: "Design certifies function; it neither installs nor excludes an experiencer."
- **Why it works**: because designers specify what goes in, the engineered class gives the decoupling "a sharper instrument than evolved organisms provide." The article is also the wing's newest (created 2026-09-01) and its best-scoped — the treatment other members should inherit. See Finding 2.

### minimal-consciousness

- **Strongest point**: the Ginsburg & Jablonka qualifier, where the precision *is* the content: not associative learning but **unlimited** associative learning, with the consequence of getting it wrong spelled out — "Cited loosely, as though any associative learning indicated experience, the criterion would sweep in most of the animal kingdom — the opposite of what it was built to do."
- **Why it works**: it protects a marker from becoming a rubber stamp, which is precisely how marker criteria fail in practice.
