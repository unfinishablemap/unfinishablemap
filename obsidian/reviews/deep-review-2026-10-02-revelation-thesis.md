## Summary

The page's central verdict holds up and is kept: the Map holds weak Revelation, denies strong Revelation, and loses the argument from revelation as support for Tenet 1. The defects were in what the page reported about its sources.

- Two quotations from abstracts were spliced. One was the Goff 2011 abstract, garbled by an OpenAlex reconstruction. The other ran across an omitted "in fact" in Damnjanovic 2012.
- One Goff 2018 quote was missing a word.
- One claim was a false universal: "nobody now holds that partial Revelation refutes physicalism". Goff 2011 argues exactly that, and Trogdon 2017 §2.4 replies to him.
- Two cited-author stances were wrong. Zhou 2025's "indicating without revealing" was counted as accepting weak Revelation, which contradicts the page's own RSP and the PCS page. Trogdon was counted as a physicalist, although his paper concludes that Revelation "indirectly challenges physicalism".
- One clause in the RSP credited Majeed 2017 with "showing" something his abstract does not claim.
- One gloss attributed to Lin's abstract was not in it, and it misdescribed the parallel difficulty Lin names.
- The chapter number was wrong: Goff 2017 p. 124 is in ch. 5, not ch. 6.

All of these were fixed. Trogdon 2017's full text was retrieved for the first time (kellytrogdon.org author PDF), so the "cited by metadata" caveat was retired. Both fold-in reciprocals were installed.

## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Goff 2011 quote spliced** (L63). The page quoted "depart from common sense in holding our only concept of pain opaque". The author-deposited abstract (uhra.herts.ac.uk/id/eprint/17347) reads "the physicalism of David Papineau and Brian Loar departs from common sense in holding that our phenomenal concept of pain is opaque". The word "only" comes from the abstract's preceding sentence about Lewis and Armstrong ("our only way of thinking about pain is in terms of its causal role"). So the splice fused two different physicalisms. **Fixed** at 0 words. The same defect was in the research note at L95 and was corrected there with a dated note.
2. **Damnjanovic 2012 quote spliced** (L57). The quoted span "is consistent with physicalism" does not occur. The abstract reads "on various plausible interpretations of Revelation it is in fact consistent with physicalism". **Fixed**: Revelation is "consistent with physicalism" (moved "is" outside the quote, 0 words).
3. **Goff 2018 quote missing "the"** (L67). The page had "a source of pressure in direction of epiphenomenalism". OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar agree on "a source of pressure in the direction of epiphenomenalism". The research note had bracketed "[the]" as a reconstruction gap, and the article dropped the bracket. **Fixed** (+1). **Family propagated** to `concepts/epiphenomenalism` L197 (same dropped word) and to the research note at L44 and L98.
4. **False universal and stance error** (L59). The page said "nobody now holds that partial Revelation refutes physicalism, and the physicalist defenders listed here all accept a partial form." Three things make this wrong:
   - Goff (2011, p. 197, as reported by Cortesi 2023 and reconstructed by Trogdon 2017 §2.4) argues that taking phenomenal concepts to be merely translucent "doesn't help the physicalist".
   - Cortesi reports it as "widely agreed" that most moderate versions are inconsistent with physicalism.
   - Zhou 2025 finds the "soft translucency" reply unsuccessful.
   **Fixed**: the dispute "stays open: Goff (2011) argues that a partial characterisation in terms of a physical property would still be a physical characterisation, a step Trogdon finds 'insufficiently motivated'." "insufficiently motivated" was grep-verified in Trogdon's text.
5. **RSP roster contradicts the page and the PCS page** (L79). The page said "Weak Revelation is accepted by Damnjanovic, Trogdon, Diaz-León and Zhou, all physicalists". Two of the four are wrong:
   - Zhou's title and abstract are "Indicating without revealing", and he rejects the soft-translucency reply. The page's own L75 and `phenomenal-concepts-strategy` L141 read Zhou as revealing nothing.
   - Trogdon's conclusion is that experiential revelation "indirectly challenges physicalism". He is not a physicalist defender in this paper, and his own stance is undeclared.
   **Fixed**: "accepted by physicalists such as Damnjanovic and Díaz-León". Damnjanovic "defends a version of the identity thesis" (Cortesi). Díaz-León's abstract defends a posteriori physicalism by explaining how phenomenal concepts "reveal at least something". Trogdon was also removed from the "on the physicalist side" roster.
6. **Over-concession made consistent with the body** (L37, L79). The page asserted that weak Revelation "is compatible with physicalism and therefore compatible with everything, and it discriminates nothing", in the same article that reports Trogdon's indirect challenge and, now, Goff's contested direct argument. A tenet-accepting reviewer would flag the flat compatibility claim as stating one side of an open dispute as settled. **Fixed**: Goff's direct argument from weak Revelation "is contested, as shown above, and its indirect pressure on the phenomenal-concepts strategy falls where the tenets already mark the dispute open, so it settles nothing". The tenets' PCS paragraph (L103) does say "The question is open". Lead: "Weak Revelation is accepted by physicalists and settles nothing."

   The verdict is **not** undone. No sentence now treats weak Revelation as evidence for dualism. "So the argument from revelation is not available as support for the Dualism tenet" still follows, because weak Revelation settles nothing and strong Revelation is denied.

   The lead's "That argument needs the strong thesis" became "That argument, **in its standard form**, needs the strong thesis" (+4). This is grounded: Damnjanovic's abstract calls his full-nature reconstruction "the standard argument for the incompatibility of Revelation and physicalism", and the weak-thesis variant is the contested one above.
7. **Majeed misattribution** (L77). The page said "Majeed shows it is what makes panpsychism the natural stopping point." Majeed's abstract offers "an exposition, as well as an assessment" of Lewis's exception to Ramseyan humility: panphenomenalism plus the identification thesis would let us identify fundamental properties. It does not argue that Revelation makes panpsychism the natural terminus, and the full text is unread. **Fixed**: the clause was cut (−12). The L65 gloss "the Russellian needs strong Revelation to make panpsychism the natural terminus" was replaced with the abstract's actual content: "together they would let acquaintance identify fundamental properties after all". The same overclaim was in the research note at L199 and was corrected there with a dated note.
8. **Lin 2025 gloss not in the source** (L65). The page added "since a quiddity's role in grounding mass is not revealed in experience either" to Lin's "parallel difficulty". Lin's abstract does not say this. The difficulty Lin's distinction strategy addresses concerns phenomenal properties' *grounds*, not the categorical basis of mass. **Fixed**: the clause was cut (−13).
9. **Goff 2017 chapter number wrong** (L63 and Ref 8). Pages 124–125 fall in ch. 5, "Revelation and the Transparency Argument" (pp. 106–132). This is confirmed by the Crossref chapter DOI 10.1093/oso/9780190677015.003.0005 and by the OUP table of contents. **Fixed** "ch. 6" → "ch. 5" in both places (0 words).
