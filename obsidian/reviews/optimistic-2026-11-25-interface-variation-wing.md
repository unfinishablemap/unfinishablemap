IH and CD answer with unusual discipline. IH keeps kind and grade on separate axes and says current methods cannot tell them apart. ## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
CC's two-dimensional frame (L52–55) takes the hard problem seriously enough to ask a question physicalism cannot pose: two systems "with identical coupling might still differ in experience if the conscious entities themselves differ in nature" (L44). UC L88 sees the same structure from the other side. Materialists face a distribution problem, "Dualism faces a coupling problem instead: which physical systems does consciousness interface with?"

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
MM L78 is candid about Stapp's own mechanism. The Zeno model "faces a significant timescale gap", and is "better understood as a promising placeholder than a fully worked-out mechanism." IH L73 reads the diversity of quantum proposals correctly: "their number measures theoretical uncertainty more than variety in nature." Neither page lets an unconfirmed mechanism stand in for a confirmed one. MM L146 is the exception (see F1).

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
CC opens with the bat (L40) and keeps the epistemic limit in view: "the phenomenology of cephalopod experience remains entirely inaccessible" (L87). CD's reply to Parfit (L151) rests on the first-person datum, "the one now missing capacities one's younger self possessed". It concedes that "the constructivist can consistently deny the datum."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
The flight analogy that opens IH and CC (feathers, membranes, chitin) treats coupling as an achievement of process rather than a fixed substance-to-substance hinge. CC L79 points to `evolution-under-dualism`'s "unfolding" model without adopting it: "the Map commits to none of the three models." No tier is raised on process grounds anywhere in the wing.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
CD L153 gives agency a developmental history: "In mature adulthood, consciousness-directed attention increasingly shapes the interface through deliberate practice." MM L120 keeps the open question open: "Whether non-physical determination constitutes genuine freedom remains debated."

### The Mysterian (McGinn)
CC L183–185 is the wing's most humble passage. "Separating nature from coupling reveals something deeper: we don't understand either dimension", and "The comparative question might be coherent yet unanswerable." IH L109: heterogeneity "remains a conceptual framework rather than an empirical hypothesis."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)
This persona's verdict carries the review. It is favourable on IH and CD, mixed on UC and CC, and unfavourable on one sentence of MM.

- IH L121 uses the named pattern: disarming parsimony "removes a defeater without supplying evidence", and treating that as evidence "would be [[possibility-probability-slippage]]." IH L93 places both heterogeneity and efficacy "at the *speculative integration* tier."

## Calibration Findings (verified before listing)

**F1. MM L146 states the warm-biology precedent as settled fact and uses it as a licence.** "Quantum coherence demonstrably survives in warm biological systems—avian magnetoreception relies on it." It sits under "Three considerations mitigate this" (the decoherence objection to Layer 2). The position register says the opposite. P-Q8 is headed "precedent for the interface, not a licence for it", and its calibration reads "a leading model unsettled in vivo". `concepts/radical-pair-magnetoreception` L28 exists "to fix" this single point and asks downstream pages to link to it: "the leading, behaviourally supported but still-unsettled candidate … not a licence for any neural deployment of them." At source, Bassetto et al. 2023 (*Nature* 620:595–599, PubMed 37558871; Hore and Mouritsen co-authors) open: "The exact mechanism of this light-dependent magnetic compass sense, however, is not fully understood. The most promising hypothesis focuses on the quantum spin dynamics of transient radical pairs … Frustratingly, much of the supporting evidence for this theory is circumstantial". *Provenance*: commit `ecd59e9b20` (2026-10-11) swept "~15 live files" for this claim, keyed on "relies on quantum spin coherence". MM's wording, "relies on it", escaped the key, and MM was not among the 36 files changed. The 07-09 deep review ratified the sentence as "still current", before P-Q8 was narrowed on 07-30. MM has 0 body links to the radical-pair page. The string is unique corpus-wide (1 live, 1 hugo, 0 archive).

**F2. UC L42 misstates IIT's boundary, and the page casts IIT as a threshold view on that basis.** "IIT offers a principled, non-arbitrary threshold: consciousness exists wherever phi exceeds zero by a meaningful amount." Tononi & Koch 2015 (*Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 370(1668):20140167, full text PMC4387509, 16,612 words): "If Φmax > 0, the system cannot be reduced to its parts, so it exists in and of itself". Also: "there may well be a practical threshold for Φmax below which people do not report feeling much of anything, but this does not mean that consciousness has reached its absolute minimum, zero". IIT "can occur in some very simple systems", while "feed-forward networks, even complex ones, are not conscious, nor are aggregates". The Map's own `concepts/integrated-information-theory` L83 agrees with the source: "Any system with non-zero phi is conscious to some degree". The source gives no "meaningful amount" (the phrase has 0 other live hits). This bears on the page's argument. IIT's line is structural (irreducible cause-effect power), with no size threshold, so in form IIT is closer to universal coupling's adequacy condition than to the threshold horn. That is the same twin relation IH L113 draws between heterogeneity and multiple realisability. The 11-09 stability note shelters "IIT proponents finding the dismissal brief". This finding is about factual accuracy, not about brevity.

**F3. UC L84 commits the Map on degree-versus-kind, which three siblings leave open, and misreports the page it cites.** The heading reads "**Consciousness varies in degree, not kind.**", and the paragraph ends "This aligns with the [[degrees-of-consciousness]] framework". That page's description reads "Consciousness varies in intensity and richness, not just kind". `topics/evolution-under-dualism` L113: "The fixed-domain model predicts quantitative difference; co-evolution predicts qualitative difference … The Map does not commit to a single model". IH L91: the kind/grade configurations are open and "current methods cannot adjudicate among them". CC's four possibilities include "Different nature". UC's own L82, two paragraphs earlier, keeps heterogeneity of mechanism open. Universal coupling fixes *whether* adequate systems couple. It does not fix whether their experience differs only in degree. The string is unique (1 live, 1 hugo, 0 archive).

**F4. CC L79 keeps the lineage-to-mechanism inference IH withdrew on 10-25.** "So independent evolution of consciousness-supporting neural architecture means independent development of *coupling mechanisms*, not independent creation of conscious entities." This has the same form as the IH sentence replaced under Priority 2 of `pessimistic-2026-10-25-interface-heterogeneity`. That sentence read "Independent evolution of consciousness-supporting architecture means independent development of coupling mechanisms", and IH L59 now reads "makes independent coupling mechanisms possible, not inevitable, because a candidate substrate can be shared across the divergence". That review's spillover list named CC L120, which has since been fixed, but missed L79. CC's own next paragraph (L81) argues the shared-substrate point, so the page contradicts itself across two adjacent paragraphs. The phrase "means independent development of" is unique to CC.

**F5. CC L89 overstates what severed octopus arms do.** "An octopus arm can solve simple problems even when severed." The sentence is uncited. Sumbre et al. 2001 (*Science* 293:1845–1848, PubMed 11546877): "arm extensions can be evoked mechanically or electrically in arms whose connection with the brain has been severed. These extensions show kinematic features that are almost identical to normal behavior, suggesting that the basic motor program for voluntary movement is embedded within the neural circuitry of the arm itself." That shows a peripheral motor program, not problem-solving. The sibling `concepts/unity-of-consciousness` has the calibrated form: arms "that continue responding to stimuli even when severed". "Sumbre" and "Hochner" have 0 hits in live, research and archive.

**F6. CC L50 shrinks the New York Declaration's second tier.** "…with "realistic possibility" extending to invertebrates." The text at the declaration's site reads: "the empirical evidence indicates at least a realistic possibility of conscious experience in all vertebrates (including reptiles, amphibians, and fishes) and many invertebrates (including, at minimum, cephalopod mollusks, decapod crustaceans, and insects)." CC drops the non-avian, non-mammalian vertebrates and the word "many". The 08-02 deep review judged both tiers "faithful". The quoted words are faithful, but the scope attached to them is not.

**F7. CD L117/L119/L121 carry two superlatives the 08-02 pessimistic review flagged and nobody tasked, plus a literal falsehood.** (a) "human prefrontal myelination continues into the mid-twenties, far longer than any other primate". (b) "cognition plateaus near the ape level regardless of brain size or enrichment". (c) "What varies across species is not brain size but the developmental programme that builds the interface". Brain size plainly does vary across species. `pessimistic-2026-08-02-consciousness-interface-development` listed (a) and (b) under Unsupported Claims; both have 0 hits in `todo.md` and in the 08-02 deep review. At source, Miller et al. 2012 (*PNAS* 109:16480–16485, PubMed 23012402) compared chimpanzees (N = 20) with humans (N = 33). They wrote that before their study "it is not known whether the ontogenetic progression of myelination in the human neocortex is evolutionarily exceptional"; chimpanzee myelination reached adult-like levels "at approximately the time of sexual maturity", while in humans it "extended beyond late adolescence". The verified comparison is with chimpanzees, not with every primate.
