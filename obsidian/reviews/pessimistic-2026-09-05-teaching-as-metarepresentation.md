## Executive Summary

The article's thesis is about *metarepresentational* teaching (a knower representing a learner's ignorance as a state to be changed), but its evidence is *functional*-definition evidence (teaching identified by its effects — costly to the donor, in the learner's presence, improves the recipient) — and the article itself concedes at L81 that functional teaching does not evidence metarepresentation. It then uses the *rarity* of functional teaching in apes to infer the *absence* of metarepresentation, which the concession disallows; and it never mentions that functional teaching is documented in tandem-running ants and meerkats, the literature its own ledger entries (Thornton & Raihani 2008; Kline 2015) review but the body never cites. The support for the tenet-bearing premise — that metarepresentation requires *phenomenal* rather than access consciousness — is entirely access-level (working memory, GWT, theory of mind) plus one undischarged conditional, which is the exact substitution [[project/evidential-status-discipline]] names. Two further defects are cluster-level: the ape "Level 1 only / teaching needs Level 2" claim (L97–99) contradicts the cluster's own source page [[concepts/theory-of-mind]], which recalibrated Level 2 to "contested, and it has moved" after Krupenye et al. 2016 and locates the unambiguous metarepresentation requirement at Level 3; and the "metarepresentation → teaching → cumulative culture" chain (L122) states as structural a middle link that Zwirner & Thornton 2015 experimentally found "useful but not essential." The No Many Worlds section is a non-sequitur, and the Illusionist Challenge section is framework-boundary marking dressed as in-framework refutation. The article contains its own corrective (L81) and a real falsifier section; the fix is calibration and rival engagement, not demolition.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland: "Metarepresentation" is folk-psychological bookkeeping — "knowing that you know" — and the Map's own [[concepts/metacognition]] page already gives it a neural address: frontopolar cortex, Brodmann area 10, whose grey-matter structure predicts metacognitive accuracy and whose stimulation causally alters it. So the substrate of declarative metacognition is a prefrontal monitoring circuit that apes have less of. The article's "phenomenal standing-back" (L34) adds nothing to that circuit story except a word. And the comparative record this page ignores is fatal to the "consciousness marks the boundary" framing: the category *teaching*, on the only operational definition biology has, cuts straight across neural sophistication — *Temnothorax* ants with brains of a few hundred thousand neurons teach with bidirectional feedback (Franks & Richardson 2006). If a capacity appears in ants and meerkats but not in chimpanzees, the variable that predicts it is ecology and the value of information, which is what those authors concluded — not a phenomenal threshold that ants would then have to have crossed.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett: this is the January critique, verbatim, unabsorbed. The article's only argument that metarepresentation needs *phenomenal* consciousness runs through working memory and Global Workspace Theory (L91) — but GWT is a functional theory of *access*; citing it concedes that "conscious" here means "globally available," which any suitably organised system has. The "felt gap" that "generates the motivation to bridge it" (L132) is a user-illusion of a gap-representation doing its job; heterophenomenology takes the "aha, they don't get it" report (L105) as data about what the teacher's system represents, not as evidence of a non-physical ingredient. And the "Illusionist Challenge" section never meets illusionism: it equates "functional processing" with "processing power" (L130) and then asks why apes with "comparable neural resources" don't teach — as if illusionism claimed that any amount of computation yields pedagogy. It claims the *right organisation* does, and Csibra and Gergely's natural pedagogy — a species-specific communicative adaptation with ostensive cues, sitting uncited in this article's own reference list — is a ready-made functional answer to the article's "why not apes?" that the illusionist can adopt wholesale.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark: the tenets require consciousness's causal contribution to enter at the quantum level, minimally. Teaching is a slow, warm, classical, multi-second behaviour — repositioning a stone, splitting a fishing probe, choosing a word. Nothing in this article connects a femtosecond-scale minimal interaction to a pedagogical adjustment, and nothing distinguishes the human–ape working-memory difference (roughly 4 versus 2±1 items) from an ordinary classical difference in prefrontal capacity. The January review's Issue 10 — the quantum mechanism has no resources to explain species differences, since both species have the same neurons — is exactly as unanswered here as it was then. The article's causal story, if it has one, is classical throughout; it is the *Bidirectional Interaction* section (L199) that asserts a quantum-tenet payoff from a classical exhibit.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch: the No Many Worlds section (L203) is not an argument. "If personal identity branched across quantum splits ... the asymmetry between knower and ignorant learner would dissolve into symmetrical ignorance across branches." In any decohered branch a definite teacher knows and a definite learner does not; Everettian indexical facts are perfectly determinate *within* a branch, which is where classrooms are. No branch shares a classroom with another. Teaching requires within-branch determinacy about who knows what — which the Everettian grants — and nothing about cross-branch determinacy, which is the only thing the tenet denies. The section is a forced tenet-connection of the family the 2026-09-03 review flagged in `cognitive-integration-and-the-self`, and the discipline records the same family being "formally withdrawn" at the topic tier of the memory cluster while lingering on concept pages. Its sibling [[concepts/cumulative-culture]] L198–200 makes the parallel "single history" move.

### The Empiricist

Popper: the argument has two premises — (P1) teaching requires metarepresentation; (P2) metarepresentation requires *phenomenal* consciousness — and every one of the four listed falsifiers (L183–189) tests P1 or an *access* claim ("lacking conscious *access* to their own knowledge states," L187). P2, the only premise the tenets depend on, has no stated falsifier. That is the mark of a metaphysical rather than empirical claim, and the article should say so. Separately, the falsifier section reports no state for any falsifier, against the writing-style rule adopted 2026-08-18 (after this article's last edit): falsifier 1 has evidence in (Musgrave 2016, 2020 — thin, so state 3); falsifier 2 has Strauss, Ziv & Stein 2002 reporting three-year-olds teaching by demonstration before typical false-belief passing (state 2/3, and the developmental synchrony at L142 is cleaner than the literature); falsifiers 3 and 4 are state 1 — nothing has come in, which is untested, not survived.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna: "The teacher must be a definite someone who knows, addressing a definite someone who doesn't" (L203) reifies precisely the knower the contemplative traditions the article then invokes (L173–177) work to dissolve. Zen's mind-to-mind transmission is the tradition's own denial that a determinate content passes from a determinate knower to a determinate learner; a koan is administered to exhaust, not to fill, the student's knowledge state. The article conscripts this against its own grain, and it does so with a terminological slip — *shikantaza* is Sōtō "just sitting," not "direct pointing," which is the Chan transmission formula (L173). The Jourdain framing has the same shape as the reification: "apes have skills without knowing they have skills" (L54) presupposes a self that either possesses the second-order knowing or lacks it, when the tradition's point is that the "knowing that one knows" is itself a construction the evidence can only ever show to be *reported*, never *had*.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Functional-definition evidence is recruited for a metarepresentational thesis
- **File**: `concepts/teaching-as-metarepresentation.md`
- **Location**: L34, L40–48 and L152–155 (the thesis, stipulated representationally); L71–81 (the evidence, functional); L81 (the article's own concession); L120 ("suggests great apes cannot make this move"); L195 (Dualism section's inference from the pattern)
- **Problem**: The article's thesis concerns teaching defined by the donor's *representing* the learner's ignorance as a state to be changed. Its ape evidence is teaching identified by Caro–Hauser *functional* criteria (costly to the donor, in the learner's presence, improves the recipient). L81 states correctly that the functional cases "satisfy a functional definition of teaching ... without showing that the donor represents the learner's ignorance." The concession runs both ways: if functional presence does not evidence representation, functional *rarity* does not evidence representational *absence* — yet L120 and L195 infer exactly that. The omitted comparative literature makes this concrete. Functional teaching is documented in *Temnothorax* ants (Franks & Richardson 2006, *Nature* 439:153 — tandem running with bidirectional feedback, the authors concluding "it could be the value of information, rather than the constraint of brain size, that has influenced the evolution of teaching") and meerkats (Thornton & McAuliffe 2006); the article's own uncited ledger entries Thornton & Raihani 2008 and Kline 2015 are the reviews of that literature, and Kline 2015 supplies a five-type taxonomy (social tolerance, opportunity provisioning, stimulus/local enhancement, evaluative feedback, direct active teaching) that dissolves the article's binary. Two consequences: (a) the ape rarity is at most *consistent with* metarepresentational absence and is better predicted by ecology; (b) the Dualism section's straw prediction — "we might expect gradations ... increasing with brain size" (L195) — is one no rival makes, since the actual distribution of teaching across taxa is non-monotonic in brain size on everybody's account. This is the horizontal substitution [[project/evidential-status-discipline]] names under *Epistemic Reading ≠ Metaphysical Reading*: the same words ("apes don't teach") carry a weak reading the evidence supports and a strong reading the conclusion needs.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Split the readings explicitly. State that ape functional-teaching rarity does not bear on metarepresentation either way, cite the ant/meerkat cases and Kline's taxonomy inline (the ledger already holds two of the sources), and relocate the evidential weight onto the one datum that *does* address representation — the Goualougo sensitivity-to-need result (Musgrave et al. 2020), currently filed under "Rare Exceptions?" rather than treated as the relevant test. Drop the L195 gradation prediction.

### Issue 2: Access-level support for a phenomenal-level premise, then a phenomenal-causation payoff
- **File**: `concepts/teaching-as-metarepresentation.md`
- **Location**: L85 ("depends on phenomenal consciousness rather than mere functional processing"); L87–91 (working memory → GWT → "conscious access"); L93–99 (theory of mind); L101–110 (phenomenology, ending in an undischarged conditional); L199 (Bidirectional Interaction)
- **Problem**: Each of the three "converging considerations" is access-level. Working memory and GWT establish that teaching draws on globally available representations — a functional claim physicalism grants. The theory-of-mind requirement is functional. The phenomenology section ends "*if* teaching's phenomenology is constitutive rather than incidental ... *then* teaching requires phenomenal consciousness" — the antecedent is never argued, and given the antecedent the consequent is close to tautological. L199 then cashes the tenet: "consciousness causally influences the physical world ... teaching produces physical changes in learners' brains." What the article has shown is that a cognitively individuated state (the representation of the learner's ignorance) makes a causal difference — the weak thesis under the discipline's *Access/Functional Mental Causation ≠ Phenomenal Mental Causation* rule — and no independent discriminator (a feature of teaching only phenomenal-level causation predicts) is named. The 2026-01-21 review made the GWT point in these words; it was never absorbed.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Either supply the bridge (a discriminator that natural-pedagogy or predictive-processing accounts of teaching cannot model) or adopt the honest register: teaching depends on declarative metarepresentation, which in humans is conscious; the claim that the *phenomenal* character does the causal work is conditional on the tenets, and the Bidirectional Interaction section should say so.

### Issue 6: The teaching → cumulative-culture link is asserted as structural against experimental evidence
- **File**: `concepts/teaching-as-metarepresentation.md`

- **Problem**: Zwirner & Thornton 2015 (*Scientific Reports* 5:16781, verified at nature.com this run) ran transmission chains for a basket-building task and found that "neither teaching nor imitation were strictly necessary for cumulative improvements; emulation chains generated equivalent increases in efficacy despite exhibiting relatively low copying fidelity" — teaching "useful but not essential." ## Counterarguments to Address

### Teaching without metarepresentation is common in nature
- **Current content says**: apes "rarely if ever" teach (L71); non-ape cases are a footnote (L81); the ape–human contrast "reveals something fundamental about consciousness" (L34).
- **A critic would argue**: on the only operational definition available, teaching is found in ants, meerkats and pied babblers and absent in most primates; the predictor is the cost of individual learning versus the value of transmitted information, not cognition. The ape–human contrast in *functional* teaching is therefore not the contrast the thesis needs.
- **Suggested response**: concede the functional point; move the argument to the declarative/procedural boundary and to the Goualougo sensitivity-to-need data as the one place where ape *representation* of learner state is actually tested.

### Natural pedagogy is a functional explanation of the human specificity
- **Current content says**: "The simplest explanation of teaching—complex information processing—fails to explain why great apes don't do it" (L207).
- **A critic would argue**: Csibra and Gergely's natural pedagogy is a species-specific communicative adaptation (ostensive cues, generalisation bias in infants) that explains human ubiquity and ape rarity without any phenomenal premise. It is in the article's own reference list.
- **Suggested response**: engage it by name; the Map's claim then has to be that natural pedagogy presupposes declarative metarepresentation on the teacher's side — which is arguable — rather than that no functional account exists.

### Great apes may track false beliefs
- **Current content says**: apes stop at Level 1 (L97).
- **A critic would argue**: Krupenye et al. 2016 and Buttelmann et al. 2017, which the Map's own theory-of-mind page accepts as having moved the question.
- **Suggested response**: inherit the theory-of-mind page's two-systems reading; relocate the claimed deficit from belief-tracking to its declarative use.
