# Pre-Publication Referee Report: "Personal Identity"

## Introduction and Scope of the Audit

This report constitutes a formal, exhaustive pre-publication referee assessment of the manuscript "Personal Identity," alongside its heavily integrated sibling texts (e.g., "The Self: Minimal, Narrative, and Substantial," "Haecceity and Indexical Identity," and "Indexical Knowledge and Identity"). The remit of this audit is strictly confined to the philosophical rigor, empirical accuracy, logical coherence, and scholarly adequacy of the manuscript's claims regarding what constitutes the persistence of a conscious subject over time. The assessment entirely brackets the host platform's internal architecture, focusing purely on the manuscript's philosophical mechanics.

The manuscript attempts to mount a comprehensive defense of a non-reductive, dualist account of personal identity. It explicitly grounds diachronic identity in a substantial, primitive "thisness" or *haecceity*, arguing that psychological continuity theories—such as Derek Parfit's reductionism—fatally fail to capture the phenomenal indexicality of subjective experience[1]. To sustain this framework, the text posits several foundational commitments: a rejection of the Many-Worlds Interpretation (MWI) of quantum mechanics[1], a dismissal of illusionist and eliminativist paradigms[1], and an appeal to empirical neuroplasticity—specifically recent split-brain research—to defend a unified conscious agent[4].

However, a rigorous examination of the manuscript against the philosophical and empirical literature of the 2020s reveals fatal structural deficiencies. The text systematically engineers false dichotomies, structurally ignores the most formidable contemporary counter-frameworks, fundamentally misappropriates distinct philosophical traditions, and relies on a circular architecture wherein highly contested internal tenets are treated as bedrock axioms to beg the question against deflationary rivals. The manuscript consistently exhibits a pattern of citing outdated literature to dismiss competing frameworks, while entirely evading the sophisticated defenses of those same frameworks published between 2020 and 2025. The following sections exhaustively detail these severe weaknesses across the domains of metaphysics, the semantics of indexicality, empirical neurophilosophy, the philosophy of physics, and comparative cross-cultural philosophy.

## The Metaphysics of Identity and the Strawmanning of Reductionism

A central pillar of the manuscript’s argument is its outright rejection of Derek Parfit’s psychological reductionism. The text argues that because psychological continuity (Parfit's "Relation R") can be hypothetically duplicated, branched, or severed without preserving the intuitive, indexical "thisness" of the subject, personal identity must consist in a "further fact"—specifically, an irreducible, conscious *haecceity* or substantial self[1]. The manuscript asserts that Parfitian reductionism dissolves the indexical fact of identity and must, therefore, explain away the stubborn phenomenology of being a particular subject. In contrast, the manuscript claims its own agent-causal framework takes this phenomenology seriously, asserting that agents cause actions as persisting substances, not as psychological patterns[1].

This argumentative structure commits a textbook false dichotomy that fundamentally misrepresents the contemporary landscape of personal identity metaphysics. The manuscript frames the debate as a forced choice: either one accepts Parfitian psychological reductionism (where identity is merely a pattern that can dissolve or undergo fission), or one must accept the manuscript's dualist, haecceity-based framework[1]. This framing only works by systematically ignoring the most prominent non-psychological, non-dualist theory of personal identity in contemporary philosophy: animalism.

Animalism, historically championed by Eric Olson and Paul Snowdon, and heavily refined in the 2020-2025 literature, posits that human persons are fundamentally biological organisms. On this view, numerical identity over time consists not in psychological continuity, nor in the persistence of a dualist soul or primitive indexical "thisness," but strictly in the continuation of the biological and metabolic life processes of the human animal[7]. The manuscript contains absolutely no mention of animalism, biological persistence, or the hylomorphic alternatives that dominate the current metaphysical literature[7]. By omitting this vast swath of contemporary scholarship, the author conveniently evades the most devastating physicalist counter-argument to their thesis. Animalism perfectly accounts for the "further fact" that Parfit’s fission and teleportation thought experiments highlight—explaining why *I* am not the teleported replica—without needing to invent a non-physical haecceity. The replica is not me because the replica is a numerically distinct biological organism, causally and metabolically disconnected from the continuous life of my current animal body.

The severity of this omission is drastically compounded by the manuscript's reliance on psychological thought experiments, such as uploading, philosophical zombies, and perfect copying, to derive its dualist conclusions[1]. The author leverages the conceivability of these scenarios to demonstrate that identity must involve a conscious *haecceity* over and above physical continuity. However, the 2020s literature on personal identity rigorously tests the boundaries of animalism precisely through edge-cases of biological and psychological disunity, proving that one does not need dualism to anchor numerical identity in odd situations. For instance, Hugo Luzio and Ricardo Santos (2026) in the journal *Philosophia* provide a highly sophisticated defense of animalism against the "dicephalus objection" (the case of conjoined twins with two psychological centers but one shared organism)[8]. They utilize a "psychological disunity solution" to demonstrate how biological essentialism can accommodate severe psychological fracturing without surrendering numerical identity or multiplying ontological entities[8].

Because the manuscript totally ignores the metaphysics of biology, it fails to recognize that physicalists possess robust, non-reductionist (in the Parfitian sense) accounts of numerical identity. The author demands that the reader accept a primitive, dualist "thisness" strictly because psychological patterns are insufficient for identity, completely ignoring that the biological organism provides exactly the numerical, non-replicable grounding the argument requires. The manuscript's treatment of personal identity is effectively trapped in a 1980s paradigm, sparring with a narrow reading of Parfit while remaining entirely blind to the biological and hylomorphic turns that define the contemporary field.

## Epistemic vs. Metaphysical Indexicality and the *De Se* Fallacy

The manuscript's semantic and metaphysical treatment of indexicality represents a second layer of profound structural weakness. In its sibling article on "Indexical Knowledge and Identity," the text demarcates three grades of indexical knowledge: perspectival knowledge, self-locating knowledge, and a bespoke category it terms "phenomenal indexical knowledge"[10]. The manuscript explicitly acknowledges the "epistemic thesis"—the widely accepted view that indexical knowledge (*de se* knowledge) is cognitively irreducible to objective, third-person facts. The text correctly points to David Lewis's "Two Gods" thought experiment and John Perry's case of the essential indexical to establish this epistemic irreducibility[10].

However, the manuscript then executes an unearned, colossal logical leap to the "metaphysical thesis." It declares that this cognitive and epistemic irreducibility must track "ontologically real indexical facts"—features of reality grounded in a primitive *haecceity* that impersonal description cannot capture[1]. The text admits that Lewis and Perry themselves treat *de se* knowledge merely as the self-ascription of properties or a difference in cognitive belief states, rather than a missing metaphysical fact in the universe[10]. Yet, it provides no substantive, independent argument for *why* the Lewisian or Perry framework is insufficient, other than nakedly asserting that an impersonal ontology violates the site's own internal tenet of "indexical identity"[1].

This is a textbook instance of begging the question. To justify the leap from epistemic distinctness to ontological primitive, the manuscript waves away deflationist and physicalist accounts by fiat. The 2020-2025 philosophical literature is replete with highly sophisticated, naturalistic accounts of *de se* content that successfully explain the unique motivational, cognitive, and phenomenal features of indexical thought without bloating the ontology with primitive haecceities. Manuel García-Carpintero's recent work on *de se* thought and action generalization (2020, 2021) rigorously demonstrates how indexical concepts operate functionally and communicatively[12]. García-Carpintero provides a robust framework for understanding how subjects navigate self-location and objective reality without requiring the metaphysical reification of the subject itself[12]. The manuscript completely ignores this contemporary semantics of self-location, pretending that the only way to validate the unique cognitive role of the word "I" is to posit an invisible, dualist substance that corresponds to it.

Furthermore, the manuscript attempts to dispatch the threat of illusionism by invoking a so-called "Tallis regress," leaning heavily on Raymond Tallis's 2024 critique of Keith Frankish[1]. The manuscript argues that Frankish's illusionism—the view that phenomenal consciousness is a representational illusion generated by the brain's self-monitoring systems—must be rejected because it fails to explain away the "stubborn phenomenology of being a particular subject." This demonstrates a severe misunderstanding of the contemporary illusionist research program. Illusionism, as defended by Frankish in the 2020s, does not claim that we do not possess strong, compelling beliefs about our own phenomenal properties; it claims that the *properties themselves* (the primitive haecceity, the ineffable "what it is like") are representational artifacts[15].

The manuscript argues that illusionism is false because the phenomenology of the "substantial self" is too intense and stubborn to be an illusion[1]. This is entirely circular. The illusionist explicitly argues that this "stubborn phenomenology" is exactly what a deeply entrenched representational illusion would feel like from the inside. One cannot refute the claim that *X* is an illusion by merely pointing out that *X* feels overwhelmingly real to the subject experiencing it. By treating its own phenomenological intuition as an unassailable metaphysical axiom, the manuscript immunizes itself against critique but removes itself from serious philosophical discourse. The failure to engage with the actual mechanics of modern *de se* deflationism and illusionist representationalism leaves the manuscript's core metaphysical leap completely unsupported.

## Empirical Overreach and the Misappropriation of Split-Brain Neuroscience

The manuscript attempts to ground its metaphysical dualism in empirical science by appealing to recent neurological studies, a maneuver that results in a profound failure of both empirical literature review and conceptual disambiguation. In its attempt to defend a unified, non-reductive conscious subject, the text heavily relies on a highly publicized 2025 PNAS study by Santander, Miller, et al., which demonstrates that full interhemispheric integration can be sustained by a fraction of posterior callosal fibers following split-brain surgery[4]. The manuscript interprets this finding of structural neuroplasticity as an empirical vindication of a single, undivided conscious agent, effectively arguing that the physical resilience of these networks maps directly onto the indivisibility of the dualist subject[6].

The critical error here is not in the citation of the 2025 empirical data, but in the philosophically illiterate conclusions drawn from it. The manuscript assumes a direct, isomorphic mapping between *behavioral/agentic integration* and *phenomenal unity*. By asserting that the preservation of minimal physical pathways ensures a unified "subject," the text completely bypasses the most consequential neurophilosophical debate of the current decade regarding split-brain patients: the dissociation between agency and phenomenal experience.

A competent 2020s philosophical review of consciousness and personal identity cannot rely solely on a structural neuroimaging paper to settle deep metaphysical questions of phenomenal unity. It must engage with the dominant conceptual interpretations of that data. The manuscript entirely omits Schechter and Bayne’s landmark 2021 critique in *Neuropsychologia*, titled "Consciousness after split-brain surgery: The recent challenge to the classical picture"[18].

Schechter and Bayne systematically dismantle the exact false equivalence the manuscript relies upon. They meticulously argue that even if recent behavioral and neuroimaging data indicate that a split-brain patient operates with a unified consciousness in the *agency-based* sense of the term (facilitated by subcortical cross-cueing or minimal intact posterior fibers), this behavioral unity absolutely cannot be reconciled with the claim that the subject's consciousness is fully unified in the *experience-based* (phenomenal) sense[18]. Schechter and Bayne point out that split-brain subjects consistently fail cross-matching tasks that require the co-accessibility of phenomenality[19]. The visual field and the explicit cognitive access to that field are demonstrably fractured, even if the subject manages to coordinate gross motor actions through subcortical channels.

The manuscript utilizes the split-brain "resilience" narrative to prop up a speculative dualist interface theory—the idea that the non-physical mind interacts with the brain as a holistic system, and thus cannot be fractured simply by severing the corpus callosum[6]. However, by ignoring the agency/phenomenality distinction mapped out by Schechter and Bayne, the manuscript begs the question. It points to unified motor output and agentic behavior as definitive proof of an undivided "witnessing subject," willfully ignoring the fact that standard physicalist theories perfectly predict subcortically mediated unified agency existing alongside fractured cortical phenomenality. The failure to address Schechter and Bayne (2021) leaves the manuscript's empirical defense of the "substantial self" resting on an elementary category mistake. It treats neurobiological redundancy as proof of a non-physical soul, rendering its neurophilosophical section obsolete upon arrival.

## Quantum Mechanics and the Misrepresentation of Everettian Modal Realism

The manuscript's intersection with the philosophy of physics is perhaps its most overtly hostile and under-researched component. The text asserts a "No Many Worlds" tenet, arguing that the Many-Worlds Interpretation (MWI) of quantum mechanics is fundamentally incompatible with the existence of a determinate, persisting subject[1]. The author argues that because MWI treats identity as pattern-instantiation across equally real, divergent branches of the wavefunction, the indexical question "which branch am I in?" is rendered senseless. Consequently, the manuscript concludes that MWI "dissolves the indexical question as empty," claiming that this requires the outright rejection of MWI in favor of a framework that preserves primitive indexical identity through single-outcome wave collapse[1].

To support this sweeping rejection of one of the dominant interpretations of modern physics, the manuscript relies entirely on Michael Lockwood’s *Mind, Brain and the Quantum* from 1989. The text aligns Lockwood’s many-minds view with Parfit's reductionism, essentially arguing that because Lockwood in 1989 thought the indexical question was meaningless, MWI is metaphysically bankrupt today[1]. Relying on a 1989 text to dismiss the ontological implications of Everettian Quantum Mechanics (EQM) in 2026 is an egregious display of scholarly malpractice. It completely ignores the Oxford Everettian school's structural and decision-theoretic developments over the last two decades (e.g., the extensive work of Saunders, Wallace, and Greaves), and crucially, it entirely ignores the 2020-2025 literature that explicitly solves the exact modal and indexical problems the manuscript claims are fatal to the theory.

The most glaring and fatal omission in this section is Alastair Wilson’s 2020 monograph, *The Nature of Contingency: Quantum Physics as Modal Realism* (Oxford University Press)[23]. Wilson provides a comprehensive, rigorous metaphysical framework for EQM that directly refutes the manuscript's claim that Many-Worlds dissolves identity and contingency. Wilson demonstrates that the branching structure of EQM does not destroy the subject; rather, it provides an actual, physical realization of David Lewis's modal realism.

In Wilson's Quantum Modal Realism (QMR), contingency is analyzed precisely as indexicality. When an observer in a post-measurement Everett world asks, "Why did I experience spin-up rather than spin-down?", the question is not senseless, as the manuscript falsely claims. The question is perfectly meaningful, and the answer is inherently *indexical*: "Because 'I' (this specific observer counterpart) am located in the branch where the spin-up outcome occurred"[25].

The manuscript claims that MWI cannot answer "why am I this branch rather than another?" and treats this presumed failure as a definitive refutation[3]. Wilson (2020) explicitly shows that this question functions exactly like asking "why am I here rather than there?" or "why is it now rather than yesterday?"[25]. The uncertainty of self-location in a branching multiverse (*de se* uncertainty) fully explains the phenomenology of objective probability, alternative possibilities, and indexical subjective experience without needing a primitive, non-physical haecceity to "collapse" the wave function into a single outcome.

By completely failing to engage with Wilson (2020) and the contemporary philosophy of probability in EQM, the manuscript is arguing against a naive, 1980s caricature of Many-Worlds. It falsely frames primitive dualist identity as the *only* coherent way to make sense of quantum contingency and indexical experience, entirely ignoring the rigorous physicalist alternatives published in the very academic spheres this manuscript attempts to critique. This assumes the manuscript's own conclusion—that identity must be an unbranching primitive—to rule out physics that models identity as branching, an intolerable circularity.

## Cross-Cultural Malpractice: The Bastardization of Buddhist *Anatta*

In an attempt to project a veneer of broad, cross-cultural synthesis, the manuscript integrates Eastern philosophical concepts into its defense of the self. Specifically, it targets the foundational Buddhist doctrine of *anatta* (no-self). The text recognizes that *anatta* poses a direct, existential threat to its assertion of a substantial, persisting subject. To neutralize this threat, the manuscript invents a highly idiosyncratic concept termed "process haecceitism"—suggesting that *haecceity* (primitive thisness) can apply to a stream of experiential processes without requiring a permanent Cartesian soul-substance[10]. The author then claims that this maneuver successfully reconciles the site's dualist commitments with Buddhist phenomenology, citing distinctions in Vedantic and Buddhist interpretations of dreamless sleep states[14].

This synthesis is not merely inaccurate; it is a fundamental violation and bastardization of Buddhist metaphysics. The manuscript attempts to smuggle an ontologically real, irreducible indexical "thisness" into a contemplative tradition whose core philosophical and salvific imperative is the eradication of precisely that illusion.

Contemporary comparative philosophy forcefully contradicts the manuscript’s framing. Jay L. Garfield’s highly influential 2022 monograph, *Losing Ourselves: Learning to Live without a Self* (Princeton University Press), represents the scholarly consensus on this issue[30]. Garfield rigorously demonstrates that *anatta* does not merely reject a "permanent soul-substance" while leaving room for the manuscript's "process haecceitism." Rather, the tradition categorically rejects *any* fundamentally real locus of subjectivity, indexicality, or unified "thisness."

Garfield explicitly argues against the four conceptual pillars that the manuscript relies upon to build its self: priority, unity, subject-object duality, and agency[32]. For the Madhyamaka Buddhist tradition—a tradition the manuscript elsewhere attempts to selectively co-opt to bolster its contemplative credentials[29]—the self is purely a conventional fiction. It is a "persona" or social role constructed entirely through linguistic and conceptual imputation[32]. There is no foundational "witness," no unobservable observer, and absolutely no bare awareness possessing a primitive haecceity.

The manuscript claims that its "witness consciousness" is a bedrock reality that survives deep meditative states[1]. Garfield (2022), alongside scholars like Mark Siderits (whose recent works on personal identity and Buddhist reductionism are equally and conspicuously ignored by the manuscript[36]), demonstrates that taking this phenomenal feeling of "mineness" or "thisness" as an ontological primitive is exactly the cognitive delusion (*avidya*) that Buddhist practice aims to diagnose and uproot[30].

The manuscript’s "process haecceitism" is simply Cartesian dualism cloaked in process vocabulary. By claiming compatibility with *anatta* while vehemently insisting on the metaphysical reality of indexical particularity, the manuscript exhibits a profound failure of cross-cultural scholarship. It selectively harvests contemplative phenomenology to support a conclusion that the actual progenitors of that phenomenology explicitly diagnose as the root of metaphysical error.

## Required Weaknesses List and Verdict

The manuscript contains fatal structural, empirical, and scholarly weaknesses that render its central thesis intellectually indefensible in its current state. The following specific deficiencies demand major revision or outright rejection:

1. **Conflation of Agentic and Phenomenal Unity in Split-Brain Patients:** The manuscript relies on recent structural neuroplasticity data to claim a unified conscious subject, completely ignoring the crucial neurological distinction that while split-brain subjects maintain unified *agency* via subcortical channels, they demonstrate fractured *phenomenality*, thus invalidating the empirical defense of a unified dualist interface.

- **Citation:** Schechter, E., & Bayne, T. (2021). "Consciousness after split-brain surgery: The recent challenge to the classical picture." *Neuropsychologia*, 160, 107987.
2. **Evasion of Animalism and Biological Persistence:** The manuscript constructs a false dichotomy between Parfitian psychological reductionism and dualist haecceity, failing to address the dominant physicalist alternative of biological numerical identity (animalism), which smoothly resolves identity thought-experiments (such as dicephalus or teleportation) without positing primitive thisness.

- **Citation:** Luzio, H., & Santos, R. (2026). "Animalism and Dicephalus: The Psychological Disunity Solution." *Philosophia*.
3. **Strawmanning Everettian Quantum Mechanics:** The text dismisses the Many-Worlds Interpretation as incompatible with personal identity based on outdated 1980s critiques (Lockwood 1989), willfully ignoring contemporary Quantum Modal Realism which successfully reconstructs contingency, indexicality, and *de se* self-location within a deterministic branching multiverse.

- **Citation:** Wilson, A. (2020). *The Nature of Contingency: Quantum Physics as Modal Realism*. Oxford University Press.
4. **Scholarly Misappropriation of Buddhist *Anatta*:** The text attempts to reconcile its commitment to indexical "thisness" with Buddhist phenomenology via the invented concept of "process haecceitism," contradicting the absolute consensus of modern Buddhist scholarship that *anatta* rejects the ontological reality of *any* persisting indexical subject or foundational witness.

- **Citation:** Garfield, J. L. (2022). *Losing Ourselves: Learning to Live without a Self*. Princeton University Press.
5. **Question-Begging Jump from Epistemic to Metaphysical Indexicality:** The manuscript acknowledges the deflationary, epistemic nature of *de se* thought (as treated by Lewis and Perry) but asserts a jump to ontological *haecceity* by flat fiat, ignoring sophisticated 2020s semantic frameworks and updated illusionist arguments that robustly explain indexical phenomena without metaphysical reification.

- **Citation:** García-Carpintero, M. (2020). "Indexical Thought: De Se Thought and Communication," in *The Routledge Handbook of Propositions*.

**Verdict on Academic-Submission Readiness:** The manuscript is utterly unready for academic submission and warrants outright rejection due to its structural reliance on false dichotomies, circular reasoning, and the systematic omission of the most formidable empirical and philosophical counter-frameworks of the current decade.
