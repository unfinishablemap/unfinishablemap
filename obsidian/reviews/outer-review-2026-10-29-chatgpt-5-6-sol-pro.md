## Reply

# Hostile referee report: *Where the Substance Commitment Enters*

**Recommendation: major revision; the article should not remain the canonical statement in its present form.**

The page gets one important claim right: **Tenet 1, considered in isolation, is neutral between substance dualism, property dualism and the site’s aspectual option.** But its advertised answer to where the stronger commitment “enters” is backwards. By the article’s own later account, agent causation and the anti-many-worlds indexical argument do not independently generate a persisting subject. Both inherit one from the previously adopted “Posit One.” The page therefore identifies two **uses** of the commitment, not its point of entry or its justification. The commitment actually enters when the Map posits a primitive, diachronically persisting subject. [concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

Its central claims divide accordingly:

- Tenet 1 is propositionally neutral: **correct**.
- Standard Chisholmian or Lowean agent causation employs a persisting causal agent: **correct by definition of those theories**.
- Authorship or free agency therefore requires such an agent: **not established**.
- Such an agent supports a move from property to substance dualism: **not established and substantially undermined by the article’s own Steward concession**.
- The indexical argument identifies a conditional incompatibility between primitive-subject metaphysics and branch-egalitarian Everettianism: **correct**.
- It refutes many-worlds or independently supports a primitive subject: **no**.
- The proposed “single-question” diagnostic reliably tracks these distinctions: **no; the page itself supplies a counterexample to it**.

## 2. Tenet neutrality: technically honest, architecturally misleading

At the narrowest level the neutrality claim is honest. “Consciousness is irreducible to physics” does not determine whether irreducible consciousness is a property of physical organisms, an aspect of some underlying reality, or a distinct mental substance. The Tenets page correctly calls the five tenets chosen starting points rather than proofs and explicitly says that Tenet 1 does not decide between substance and property dualism. [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

The problem is the repeated claim that the substance commitment is merely “downstream,” “one layer down,” and local to the agency cluster. The Map’s own background-commitments page says something considerably stronger: the agency, No-Many-Worlds, personal-identity, copying and survival treatments all presuppose a numerically determinate, diachronically persisting subject, and several stated tenets jointly rest on that shared posit. It tells readers to understand the tenets as a package sharing hidden foundations rather than as independent pillars. [tenets/background-commitments](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/background-commitments/)

That reverses the target article’s apparent dependency:

1. The article initially says agent causation and indexical identity are the two routes through which the substance lean enters.
2. It later says those routes are not independent and descend from a common thick-indexical subject.
3. It then says that subject is posited rather than inferred.

On that structure, the subject is not downstream of agent causation. **Agent causation is downstream of the subject posit.** Likewise, the indexical argument does not deliver the subject; it becomes available only after the subject has been stipulated. The title should therefore be *Where the Persisting-Subject Posit Is Used*, not *Where the Substance Commitment Enters*. [concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

This makes the neutrality claim both honest and tenet-protective:

- It is honest about the literal content of Tenet 1.
- It is protective insofar as it treats a framework-wide foundational posit as a local downstream choice, thereby preserving the appearance that the framework remains ontologically neutral after its actual operating commitments are included.

The right formulation is: **Tenet 1 is neutral; the Map’s operational framework is not.** The background-commitments page nearly says exactly this. [tenets/background-commitments](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/background-commitments/)

The role assigned to Tenet 5 compounds the problem. Tenet 5 can legitimately prevent parsimony from being treated as a decisive objection to a substantial subject. It cannot positively support that subject. The target nevertheless says the Map adopts the heavier ontology “where agency requires it” and should do so when the argument has “earned it.” But its own position register classifies the relevant claims as having no independent evidence. Blocking one objection is not earning the ontology. [concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

## 3. Agent causation does not establish the claimed substance commitment

### 3.1 The historical attributions are now mostly accurate

The account of Chisholm’s terminology is correct. Chisholm contrasts event-to-event “transeunt” causation with “immanent” causation in which an agent causes an event. But his formulation is deliberately noncommittal about the agent’s ultimate nature: the event is caused by the agent, “whatever he may be.” Chisholm’s passage therefore supports an agent/event distinction, not a distinct nonphysical substance. [Real Seeker Ministries+1](https://realseekerministries.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/chisholm-roderick.-human-freedom-and-the-self..pdf)

The treatment of Lowe is also substantially accurate after the October correction. Lowe’s official book description presents all causation as fundamentally causation by substances, with rational agents occupying the role of initiators of new causal chains. Reviews confirm that volitions are uncaused on Lowe’s account and that agents cause bodily results by willing rather than causing the volition itself. [OUP Academic+2](https://academic.oup.com/book/6201)

But Lowe’s conclusion follows from his general neo-Aristotelian metaphysics of causation. It is not a result forced on anyone who believes in agency or libertarian freedom. Citing Lowe establishes that one systematic substance-causal account exists; it does not establish that rivals collapse into incoherence.

O’Connor is similarly relevant as an exponent of irreducible agent causation, but not as evidence that human agency actually has that structure. In his classic treatment he explicitly distinguishes offering a coherent descriptive metaphysics from adducing evidence that the theory applies to human action. The target includes O’Connor in its bibliography but does not use him to supply the missing necessity argument. [toconnor.org+1](https://toconnor.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/OConnor_Agent-Causation95.pdf)

### 3.2 The article equivocates between four different notions

The page repeatedly slides among:

1. a **persisting concrete object**;
2. a **subject of experience**;
3. a **primitive, non-bundle substantial self**;
4. a **nonphysical mental substance**.

These are not interchangeable.

The decisive error occurs in the description of the supposedly inadequate property-dualist alternative. The article characterises it as mental properties supervening on a physical substrate “without any substance bearing them.” But the site’s own substance/property article correctly defines standard property dualism as physical objects bearing both physical and irreducible phenomenal properties. There is already a bearer: the organism, brain or person. Property dualism denies a **second mental substance**, not all substances or concrete bearers. [concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

Consequently, an interactionist property dualist can propose:

- the physical animal is the persisting causal agent;
- it bears irreducible phenomenal and rational powers;
- an exercise of one of those powers causes the action.

That may face exclusion, realization or top-down-causation problems. But it does not lack a referent for “the agent caused the action.” The target article simply defines this position out of consideration by treating “no distinct mental substance” as “no thing doing the causing.”

The Steward concession demonstrates the point rather than merely qualifying it. Steward provides a physical animal as the persisting causal whole, and the Map’s dedicated article concedes that a nonphysical settler adds nothing on sourcehood or luck. It says explicitly that the leverage for a nonphysical subject would have to come from the hard problem of consciousness, not the free-will debate. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/4661)

Once that is conceded, the route has the following form:

> Agent causation requires a concrete causal agent.  
> The agent may be a physical animal.  
> Consciousness is irreducible.  
> Therefore, perhaps the agent is a nonphysical mental substance.

The conclusion does not follow. Irreducible phenomenal properties of the physical animal remain an available interactionist-property-dualist countermodel. A further argument is required to show that irreducible phenomenal causal powers cannot be powers of a physical substance. The target provides none.

### 3.3 Event-causal alternatives are dismissed rather than defeated

There is a harmless definitional truth here: **standard agent causation**, as distinguished from event causation, says that an agent or substance is an irreducible causal relatum. If that is what “agent causation” means, it requires an agent-cause.

But the important question is not whether event-causal libertarianism secretly counts as agent causation. It is whether authorship, sourcehood and responsibility require the additional agent-causal relatum. The target answers this only by saying that otherwise the result “looks like event causation under another name.” That identifies the rival; it does not refute it.

Contemporary work makes the omitted dialectic especially conspicuous. Martinez argues that a strict event-cause/substance-cause dichotomy is false and develops causal pluralism; his later survey shows active disagreement over how agent causation should integrate motivating reasons. Morales develops an emergent-substance account rather than assuming a primitive subject. Steward develops process resources intended to steer between standard agent causalism and event-causal theories. A 2025 analysis argues that agent-causalists can themselves face a disappearing-agent problem once causation by an agent is compared with causation by its parts. [Springer+4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-023-04117-2)

The neighbouring Agent Causation page now at least mentions Kane’s 2024 hybrid as a naturalistic rival. The canonical target article, last modified before that page’s 25 October update, does not engage it. [concepts/agent-causation](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/agent-causation/)

### 3.4 Bundle and process alternatives are not answered

The treatment of bundle theory is effectively absent. The article assumes that without a primitive substantial subject there are merely properties “co-occurring” with neural events and no thing that can act. That is precisely what a bundle theorist denies. Bundle theories attempt to constitute a mind or person from appropriately related experiences, tropes, stages or processes without adding a separately existing substratum. Defences of bundle views explicitly address the objection that they imply experiences without experiencers. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/68211B833A2BD880EF8C0CB89FC87298/S0045509100007931a.pdf/mental-particulars-mental-events-and-the-bundle-theory.pdf)

There are then two possibilities:

- If any sufficiently unified concrete bundle or process counts as a “substance,” the target’s claim becomes easy but philosophically thin. It establishes only that action has a concrete causal bearer.
- If “substance” means a primitive bearer whose identity is not constituted by its parts, properties or continuity relations, then the target has not argued for it.

Nor does causing an action at a time entail primitive identity across an extended life. An agent may have to endure throughout the temporally extended action, but that is not yet the Map’s much stronger claim that numerical identity across times is a real further fact. The article moves without argument from **synchronic causal unity** to **primitive diachronic persistence**.

**Verdict on the agency route:** Standard substance-causal theories require a persisting concrete agent because they are defined that way. The article does not show that free agency requires that theory, that a bundle or process cannot be an agent, or that the relevant agent must be a distinct nonphysical substance.

## 4. The indexical-identity argument against many-worlds is not sound as a refutation

The argument is best reconstructed as follows:

1. There is a primitive further fact about which numerically determinate subject I am and which future subject remains me.
2. Standard branch-egalitarian Everettianism supplies no such primitive one-to-one identity fact.
3. Therefore standard Everettianism is false.

This is a valid conditional incompatibility argument if both premises are granted. It is not a sound neutral argument against many-worlds because premise 1 is the Map’s disputed background posit. The page itself concedes that the subject is posited rather than inferred and that the two supposed “homes” provide no independent mutual support. [concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

The site’s indexical article also correctly distinguishes an epistemic thesis from a metaphysical one. Perry and Lewis show that de se knowledge is cognitively distinctive; they do not show that reality contains primitive indexical facts or haecceities. Indeed, their own analyses are deflationary competitors to the Map’s metaphysical conclusion, and the site admits it has not answered them. [concepts/indexical-knowledge-and-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/indexical-knowledge-and-identity/)

The target therefore cannot move from:

> “I do not know which successor I will be”

to:

> “there must be a primitive fact assigning this pre-branch subject to exactly one successor.”

That is precisely what the Everettian rejects. Treating the rejection as an explanatory omission rather than a rival metaphysics begs the question.

### 4.1 The sentence about successor properties is defective

The article says the subject must be individuated by something branch-relative properties do not exhaust, “since every successor shares those properties.” As written, this is false or self-defeating.

If the properties are genuinely branch-relative, the successors do not share all of them: they occupy different branches, encounter different outcomes and possess different relational locations in the branching structure. If the intended claim is that they initially share all qualitative intrinsic properties, an Everettian can still locate them by relational branch facts without introducing primitive thisness. The site’s later haecceity repair now concedes exactly this possibility through Lewis-style self-ascription. [concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

### 4.2 Everettian self-location is a substantive rival, not an unanswered question

Saunders and Wallace provide a semantics on which informed speakers in branching worlds can coherently be uncertain about which branch is theirs. Sebens and Carroll locate self-locating uncertainty more specifically in the post-decoherence, pre-observation interval. These accounts are contested, but their existence defeats the claim that Everettianism simply has no fact or coherent content for uncertainty. [RCNi Company Limited+1](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1093/bjps/axn029)

Dawid and Friederich argue that Sebens and Carroll’s epistemic-separability principle cannot non-circularly derive the Born rule. That is a serious objection to a probability derivation. It does **not** establish a primitive persisting subject or show that all branch-relative self-location is incoherent. The target would need to keep those disputes separate. [RCNi Company Limited](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1093/bjps/axaa002)

The current Many-Worlds page is more sophisticated than the target article. It concedes that branch-relative indexicality accommodates felt singularity within each branch and says the residual dispute concerns whether MWI can combine branch-local self-location with the cross-branch identity relations used in certain decision-theoretic derivations. It also admits that felt singularity is consistent with a one-world interpretation rather than a knockdown of the Everettian reply. [concepts/many-worlds](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/many-worlds/)

Even that stronger cross-branch objection does not support the target’s unrestricted conclusion. At most it challenges particular decision-theoretic treatments of probability. It does not show that branching ontology is impossible, that a primitive subject exists, or that every Everettian account must use one disputed cross-branch identity relation.

### 4.3 Diachronic persistence is not “equally required” without begging the question

The target asserts that diachronic persistence is equally required because the question is which future subject is genuinely me. But fission theories deny that there must be one uniquely identical future subject. They can replace strict one-to-one identity with psychological continuity, causal continuity, counterpart relations or branch-relative identity.

The Map may reject those relations as insufficient. It cannot count their refusal to posit primitive identity as a failure to answer the question unless it first establishes that the question demands a unique identity fact. Its Kant article recognizes the vulnerability: neither memory nor causal continuity by itself distinguishes a persisting subject from a relay or transfer structure. [topics/kants-paralogisms-and-the-maps-subject](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/kants-paralogisms-and-the-maps-subject/)

Christian List’s first-personally centred worlds provide an additional counterexample to the article’s breadth. They build first-personal privilege into the ontology rather than leaving all branches uncentred. The Map rejects that view on a separate modal-realist or global-uniqueness commitment, not through the basic indexical objection. [Wiley Online Library+1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/nous.12408)

**Verdict on the indexical route:** the argument establishes that the Map’s primitive-subject posit is incompatible with standard branch-egalitarian Everettianism. It does not independently support that posit and does not refute many-worlds from premises an Everettian has reason to accept.

## 5. The diagnostic fails its own test

The article advertises a “single question”: does the argument treat the agent as an ontological source, or does it need only irreducible consciousness? It then immediately admits that this question returns “no” for the indexical case even though the article still assigns a substance-leaning verdict. That is not a minor exception. It disproves the claim that there is a single diagnostic question. [concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)

The diagnostic collapses at least three independent dimensions:

| Dimension | Alternatives |
| --- | --- |
| Causal relata | events, processes, substances, or plural causation |
| Ontology of the bearer | physical animal, emergent physical whole, nonphysical mental substance |
| Personal identity | reductionist continuity, branch-relative identity, primitive diachronic identity or haecceity |

An article can be substance-causal on the first dimension, physicalist on the second, and reductionist on the third. Steward is close to such a counterexample. An article can also be event-causal while retaining a robust physical person as the agent. Indexical realism concerns the third dimension and need not settle either of the first two.

The page’s central label, “substance-leaning,” therefore hides the distinctions it is supposed to police. Sometimes it means only that a concrete object exercises a power. Sometimes it means a primitive subject. Sometimes it means a distinct nonphysical mind. Sometimes it means haecceitistic identity. Until these are separately recorded, the diagnostic will manufacture apparent agreement between positions that share little beyond the word “substance.”

## Concrete potential improvements

1. **Rewrite the opening and title of the target article.** State that the primitive-subject commitment enters at Posit One and is subsequently deployed by agent causation and the anti-MWI argument. Rename the page *Where the Persisting-Subject Posit Is Used* unless an actual derivation of the subject is supplied.
2. **Replace “substance-leaning” with a three-axis vocabulary.** Record separately whether an argument requires a concrete continuant, a primitive substantial self, a nonphysical mental substance, or haecceitistic diachronic identity.
3. **Delete the misdescription of property dualism.** Acknowledge that ordinary property dualism already has physical objects as bearers. Present interactionist property dualism with a physical animal bearing irreducible phenomenal causal powers as the principal countermodel.
4. **Rewrite the agency conclusion.** It should say that the Map’s chosen Chisholmian/Lowean theory requires a persisting causal agent—not that agency as such requires one, and not that this yields a nonphysical substance.
5. **Add a genuine rival-theories subsection.** Engage event-causal libertarianism, Kane-style hybrids, causal pluralism, emergent-agent accounts, process theories and bundle theories. State what each allegedly fails to explain rather than dismissing it as event causation “under another name.”
6. **Separate synchronic agency from diachronic identity.** Explain why causing an action requires primitive persistence across times rather than merely an agent existing throughout a temporally extended causal process.
7. **Replace the “single question” diagnostic.** Use three independent questions: what are the causal relata; what kind of entity bears the powers; and what account of personal identity is presupposed.
8. **Rewrite the many-worlds section as a conditional incompatibility argument.** Say explicitly: given the Map’s primitive one-to-one subject and global-uniqueness posits, branch-egalitarian Everettianism is excluded. Do not present the posit as a datum that Everettianism has failed to explain.
