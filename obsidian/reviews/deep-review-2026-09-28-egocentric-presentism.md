## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **False dichotomy and a cross-article contradiction in the quadrilemma section.** The article presented Hare (drop non-solipsism) and List (drop one world) as "the two principled rivals". At L61 it said Hare's cost "is paid in the one place the Map can most easily afford it ... because the alternative horn is the one the Map's tenets forbid", which implies that dropping non-solipsism is the Map's only option under Tenet 4. List's paper names four exits, one per claim: deflationism drops first-person realism, Hare drops non-solipsism, Kit Fine–style fragmentalism drops non-fragmentation, and a many-worlds theory drops one world. [[vertiginous-question#the-quadrilemma]] (L106) states that on the strong first-person-realist reading, the Map, keeping non-solipsism and one world, must accept **fragmentation**. This article implied the Map's horn was Hare's weak solipsism, and it never mentioned fragmentalism. **Fix:** added List's four-exit mapping after the quadrilemma statement; replaced the L61 sentence with a neutral comparison of the two one-world exits that keep first-person realism; changed "the two principled rivals" to "the sharpest contrast ... though not the only live options"; added to the Tenet 4 paragraph that the Map declines Hare's weak solipsism and, on the strong reading, pays in fragmentation, with a link to the vertiginous-question section.

### Medium Issues Found

- **List's preference overstated slightly.** The article said "List's own preferred resolution". List writes that he has a "tentative preference for the fourth route" and "I have not sought to defend this route here" (OUP, *Phil. Quarterly* 75(3)). Changed to "which he says he tentatively prefers but does not defend in the quadrilemma paper".
