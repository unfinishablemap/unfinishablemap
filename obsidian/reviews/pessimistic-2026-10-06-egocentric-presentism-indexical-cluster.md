## Executive Summary

The two overnight repair passes on `egocentric-presentism` fixed the verified errors they were briefed on but installed two new misattributions of Hare's own position, both of which the raw sources contradict. (1) The temporal-analogue section (02:01Z) states that egocentric presentism is the analogue of the *moving-spotlight* A-theory and that "Hare's 2010 survey pairs the views in just this way"; Hare 2007 §IV.C explicitly draws the opposite analogy — only one subject world exists, and the egocentric presentist's attitude to other points of view is "very like a temporal presentist's attitude toward the standard four-dimensionalist semantics for tense logic" (p. 369), with modal fictionalism as the second comparison — and Hare 2010 never mentions egocentric presentism at all (it pairs *perspectival realism* with tense realism). List 2023 and 2025 both read Hare the same presentist/fictionalist way. (2) The non-solipsism gloss (00:53Z, written in the Map's voice because Builes was unreachable) says other subjects are "conscious *relative to their own standpoints* — each can truly say, from where it stands, that its experiences are present"; Hare 2007 p. 372 repudiates exactly that relativised reading ("It is not that my pain is present to me and his present to him. Mine is present and his is absent"), and List's actual rationale for filing Hare under the solipsist horn is that others are conscious only "in some third-personal and functionalist sense" while only one *subject world* is real. The gloss also contradicts the same article's L48 ("false simpliciter"). An open P2 (`todo.md` L1987) instructs two further pages to adopt this wording, so the correction should land before that task runs. Two smaller defects: the lede's rewritten conditional is logically garbled (its antecedent is the conjunction of all four claims), and "weak version of metaphysical solipsism" is a Wikipedia sentence in quotation marks attributed to Hare. On `indexical-knowledge-and-identity`, the Perry rebuild and Lewis concession are sound, but three pre-existing passages still claim what the page now concedes it cannot show — most sharply the Bidirectional Interaction paragraph, which tells the functionalist "that concedes the point" while the point is precisely what the functionalist denies.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would note that `indexical-knowledge-and-identity` now admits (L84) that Lewis and Perry are "serious deflationary competitors ... the Map has not answered", and then spends four sections (L90–L108, L114, L156) talking as though the metaphysical thesis were established: "the indexical fact that *this* brain is mine adds something it lacks", "there would be a fact about which one is me", "whatever produces these reports must track the indexical fact". A theory that concedes the competitor is unanswered in one paragraph and presupposes its defeat in the next is not hedging; it is equivocating between a concession and a conclusion.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would go for the "explanatory gap argument" at L92: "we need to specify *which* system—and 'which' is itself an indexical notion." The theorist specifies the system by description or coordinates; the only person for whom "which" is indexical is the system itself, and Lewis's self-ascription story explains exactly why a system of that architecture self-ascribes. The argument conflates the theorist's "which" with the subject's, and the page's own L84 concession already grants that the subject's "which" can be redescribed as self-ascription.

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark has little purchase here except the Tenet 3 paragraph of `egocentric-presentism` (L83), which the 02:01Z pass wrote down honestly: if presence has no third-personal footprint it does no work for bidirectional interaction; if it does, "immeasurable" collapses to "not yet measured". He would ask why the Map lists a view under its tenets at all when the view's defining property is stipulated to be causally inert. The article's answer — exploratory fit, not evidence — is adequate, and no change is recommended.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch would say the Map has quietly moved the goalposts on List. `egocentric-presentism` L67 now says List's primitive first-personal fact "encodes the vertiginous datum without saying why this subject-world is the one being lived"; `vertiginous-question` L163 still says List's picture "answers it by construction", and L189 that it "*answers* the question". List's own words (2023 p. 657, read): the many-worlds picture "can make recourse to a richer set of facts in answering it ... There is a first-personal fact that holds at my first-personally centred world to the effect that I am having my conscious experiences rather than anyone else's." So "answers" is List's claim and "encodes" is the Map's verdict on it; the two pages should say the same thing, and only `vertiginous-question` was left unpropagated.

### The Empiricist
Popper's ghost would accept the Tenet 3 paragraph as the one place where the article states a disconfirmation condition for its own reading. He would then observe that the quadrilemma lede (L32) has been "repaired" into a tautology: "*if* [all four claims hold], the four conflict and at most three can hold" asserts nothing beyond the inconsistency, and the following sentence — "the forced choice binds only those who grant" irreducibility — is false on List's own statement of the result ("these four claims are mutually inconsistent. Any theory can retain at most three of them at once and must give up at least one", 2025). Denying irreducibility *is* the first horn, not an exemption from the choice. `vertiginous-question` L129 (fixed 10:37Z) states this correctly: "his quadrilemma itself leaves the deflationist exit open."

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would observe that both pages treat a *subject world* with exactly one present experiencer as the unit of analysis and never ask whether the "one and only one" is itself a construction. He would also notice that `infant-consciousness` L73 already concedes "experience without a self experiencing it" for the infant case — which is the position the indexical pages treat as the unanswered deflationist rival. ## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Temporal analogue attributes to Hare the opposite of Hare's own analogy
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/egocentric-presentism.md`
- **Location**: L30 lede ("though the cousin is the moving-spotlight A-theory rather than strict presentism") and L42 ("Egocentric presentism is the analogue of the second ... Hare's 2010 survey pairs the views in just this way")
- **Problem**: Hare 2007 §IV.C (p. 369, read): "I believe that all that exists is an S-world, S_ME, in which the experiences of one and only one person ... are present. That is it." and "An egocentric presentist's attitude towards the S-world semantics, then, is very like a temporal presentist's attitude toward the standard four-dimensionalist semantics for tense logic ... It is also very like a modal fictionalist's attitude." List 2023 (§1 of the author PDF, read): Hare's egocentric presentism "treat[s] only a single subject's world as real". List 2025 (read via fetch): Hare combines subject worlds "with fictionalism about the subject worlds of others" and "believes that only one subject world exists. There are no other subject worlds." Hare 2010 (read): zero occurrences of "egocentric"; the pairing with tense realism is for *perspectival realism*. The article's claim that EP is the analogue of the eternalist A-theory is defensible only as a reading at the level of creatures-within-my-world (other functionally sentient creatures exist and lack presence), and the article presents it as Hare's pairing.
- **Severity**: High (misattribution of a primary author's self-description, installed 02:01Z on the reviewer's suggestion rather than the source)
- **Recommendation**: see Priority 1, item (b).

### Issue 2: Non-solipsism gloss misstates List's rationale and contradicts Hare 2007 and the article's own L48
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/egocentric-presentism.md`
- **Location**: L57 ("other subjects are conscious *relative to their own standpoints* — each can truly say, from where it stands, that its experiences are present ... conscious in the second, standpoint-independent sense")
- **Problem**: Hare 2007 p. 372 (read): "For an egocentric presentist, the situations are not symmetrical. It is not that my pain is present to me and his present to him.
