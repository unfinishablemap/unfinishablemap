## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would accept every word of the first three sections and then point at the fourth. "Phenomenal unity" is introduced as a problem distinct from feature binding, but the page gives no criterion for telling a solved unity problem from an unsolved one, so its "residual gap" is defined by what the neuroscience has not yet said rather than by anything it could not say. Worse, the page claims neuroscience "has not addressed" the question (L73). Tononi's programme has addressed it for two decades; the correct complaint is that it has not resolved it, and that complaint needs an argument the page does not give.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett's objection is to premise (a), the one premise the page waves through. "Given together in a single subjective field, from a single point of view, at once" (L71) is the Cartesian theatre stated as a datum. Multiple Drafts denies that there is any moment at which contents are "given together"; unity is a retrospective narrative achievement, and the split-brain literature shows it fractionating under disconnection. If (a) is not a datum, the page's residual is not a gap in neuroscience but an artefact of introspection, and the relocation of the Map's interest to phenomenal unity moves it from refuted ground to unexamined ground.

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark would say the page is too generous to Baum in one respect: it treats premise (d) as the conclusion that fails because (c) fails, when (d) fails on its own physics. Entanglement cannot coordinate the timing of classical firing events without a classical channel (no-signalling), so even a brain that did violate the classical delay bound could not have done so by entanglement. The page leaves that standing argument on the table and spends its force on the empirical premise. Separately, the physicist would flinch at "lagging the hub" (L45): in a periodic regime a fixed phase offset has no privileged direction, and the paper does not say which way it runs.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch has little purchase on this page, which makes no interpretive commitment; the one indexical hook is premise (a). He would note that if "a single subjective field" is doing work, the Map owes an account of why that field is one in a branching ontology, and that `unity-of-consciousness.md:146` already concedes branch-relative accounts count each branch as unified. Not a defect on this page, but a reason not to assert (a) as flat fact.

### The Empiricist
A Popperian would commend the page for stating a falsifiable premise and reporting its falsification. He would then ask what falsifies the relocated claim. "Any non-classical claim strictly at phenomenal unity, where the field is silent" (L73) is positioned precisely where no observation reaches, and the page's own fifth-tenet paragraph admits the economical move and the honest move "coincide here". The Map's register permits a stated residual; it does not permit calling the field "silent" when a named research programme is publishing on it.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would say the page has deconstructed the wrong unity. It dissolves the inference from synchrony to a quantum binder and then reifies "one consciousness" as the thing left over to be explained. The Map's own split-brain and plurality pages treat the oneness of the field as partially divisible; this page should not present as indivisible what the corpus elsewhere presents as a question.

## Counterarguments to Address

### Integrated information theory as a physicalist answer to phenomenal unity
- **Current content says**: neuroscience "has not addressed" phenomenal unity; "the field is silent" (L73).
- **A critic would argue**: IIT's integration axiom and the Φ measure are an explicit, formal attempt to say why experience is unified rather than a sum of parts (Tononi 2004, *BMC Neuroscience* 5:42, DOI 10.1186/1471-2202-5-42; Oizumi, Albantakis & Tononi 2014, *PLoS Computational Biology* 10(5): e1003588, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003588; both Crossref-verified). The Map may hold it fails (the sibling page says it "redefines unity as integration"), but failure is not silence.
- **Suggested response**: Issue 1's repair, plus one clause borrowing the sibling's verdict if headroom permits.

### The unity datum itself (strongest unanswered objection)
- **Current content says**: experience is "given together in a single subjective field, from a single point of view, at once" (L71); premise (a) passes unexamined (L55).
- **A critic would argue**: Nagel (1971, *Synthese* 22(3–4): 396–413, DOI 10.1007/BF00413435) argued that split-brain cases defeat any determinate count of subjects; Schechter (2018, *Self-Consciousness and "Split" Brains*, OUP, DOI 10.1093/oso/9780198809654.001.0001) defends a duality reading; Dennett (1991) denies the synchronic "given together" picture. If (a) is open, the Map's residual is open on both ends.
- **Suggested response**: Issue 2's repair; the page need not adjudicate, only stop presenting (a) as the one premise beyond dispute.

### No-signalling undercuts premise (d) independently of (c) — an argument the page leaves unused
- **Current content says**: the argument fails because (c) is refuted.
- **A critic would argue** (Tegmark): even granting (c), entanglement cannot coordinate the timing of classical spikes without a classical channel, so a quantum-entangled substrate could not *produce* zero-lag firing. Baum's conclusion does not follow from his premises on standard quantum mechanics. (Standard no-communication result; no new citation required, or cite a textbook treatment as a lead.)
- **Suggested response**: optional one-sentence strengthening at L59; it is a gain for the page's thesis, not a defect.

### Is the relay motif real in brains, not only in models?
- **Current content says**: "the classical account is a sufficiency result, not a finished mechanism" (L51).
- **A critic would argue**: this understates the classical case. Gollo, Mirasso, Sporns & Breakspear (2014, *PLoS Computational Biology* 10(4): e1003548, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003548) generalise the mechanism across cortical motifs, and Saalmann, Pinsk, Wang, Li & Kastner (2012, *Science* 337(6095): 753–756, DOI 10.1126/science.1223082) show the pulvinar synchronising cortical areas in vivo. Vicente's own text also reports that *broad* delay distributions keep the outer lag "almost zero even when the mean delay values of the branches differed in several milliseconds", which the page's Viriyopase-only caveat ("zero-lag is lost when the two delays differ") omits.
- **Suggested response**: optional; it would move the page from "sufficiency" toward "sufficiency with in-vivo support". Noted as an opportunity rather than a defect.
