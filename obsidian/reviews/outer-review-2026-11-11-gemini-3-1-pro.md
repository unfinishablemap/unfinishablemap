# Referee Report: "The Boredom Void" – A Critical Audit of Phenomenological and Empirical Claims

## Introduction and General Assessment

The manuscript under review, titled "The Boredom Void," attempts to construct a non-reductive, framework-independent philosophical account of consciousness by leveraging the phenomenology of boredom. The central thesis posits that boredom represents an "occluded" cognitive space—a "void"—where both physicalist computational models (specifically opportunity-cost models) and dualist frameworks converge on an identical description of an "objectless blank". The manuscript further attempts to substantiate this void by drawing on Heideggerian phenomenology, assertions regarding the subjective dilation of time, and the categorical distinction between attention-deficit and meaning-deficit boredom.

While the ambition to bridge computational cognitive science and phenomenological dualism is characteristic of contemporary philosophy of mind, the execution in this manuscript is fundamentally flawed, suffering from severe methodological indiscipline and a catastrophic reliance on conceptually mangled empirical frameworks. The author systematically evades the most potent counterarguments in the 2020s literature on affect, predictive processing, and cognitive allostasis. The manuscript attempts to isolate a "void" that purportedly resists reduction, but it accomplishes this only by artificially impoverishing the physicalist models it claims to accommodate and by routinely contradicting its own phenomenological axioms.

A rigorous audit of the text reveals that its central framings hold only if the site's explicit commitments to non-reductive dualism are presupposed. The claim that physicalist paradigms hit an identical explanatory "blank" relies entirely on the systematic omission of contemporary, highly successful computational accounts of objectless moods. The analysis that follows exhaustively details five major weaknesses that critically undermine the manuscript. These weaknesses span logical contradictions in the application of opportunity-cost models, severe misreadings of Heideggerian ontology, empirical inaccuracies regarding the psychophysics of time perception, the complete omission of the active inference framework, and the conceptual conflation of trait boredom with clinical depression. The compounding weight of these errors necessitates a recommendation for outright rejection.

## 1. The Logical Incoherence of the "Objectless" Opportunity-Cost Model

The manuscript’s foundational bridging claim rests on a severe conceptual equivocation regarding computational models of mental effort and affective regulation. The text confidently asserts that "a computational opportunity-cost model accommodates the objectless signal of boredom, meaning both a physicalist and a dualist describe the exact same blank". This framing is deliberately deployed to suggest that physicalism reaches a structural dead-end in boredom, conceding an empty "void" that perfectly aligns with a non-reductive, dualist epistemology.

This assertion demonstrates a profound misunderstanding of how opportunity-cost models operate in contemporary cognitive science, betraying a superficial engagement with the underlying mathematics and computational logic of value-based decision making. The opportunity-cost theory of subjective effort, originally popularized by Kurzban et al. (2013) and substantially refined into the current decade, posits that the feeling of mental effort—and by extension, the affective distress of boredom—is a computational signal indicating that the cognitive resources currently deployed could be better utilized elsewhere. Boredom, in this paradigm, is explicitly formulated as an attention-directing motivational state.

The fatal flaw in the manuscript’s logic is the characterization of the opportunity-cost signal as "objectless" and its subsequent equation to a dualist "blank." In computational terms, an opportunity cost is inherently and necessarily relational; it is mathematically defined by the landscape of *alternative* available objects of attention. As Wojtowicz, Chater, and Loewenstein (2020) explicitly model in their foundational opportunity-cost theory of motivational attention, boredom is the affective cost computed against the value of unselected, alternative goals. If a signal is truly "objectless"—meaning there are absolutely no alternative cognitive targets being weighed, either consciously or subpersonally in the generative model—the opportunity cost equation algebraically collapses to zero. A computational model simply cannot generate an opportunity-cost signal in a vacuum; the signal *is* the projected expected value of the foregone objects minus the expected value of the current object, adjusted for the metabolic and cognitive costs of transition.

By claiming the computational model accommodates an "objectless blank," the author strips the opportunity-cost model of its core mechanistic variable—the continuous, dynamic valuation of alternative environments. The physicalist is not describing a "blank" at all; the physicalist is describing a highly structured, data-rich computation of relative goal-values that happens to fall below a homeostatic threshold for current engagement.

Recent literature has moved well beyond the simplistic parameters the manuscript critiques. For example, Trudel, Risko, Eastwood, et al. (2025) formalize boredom as a signal of deviation from a cognitive homeostatic set point. In this framework, boredom is a highly parameterized physiological and computational state demanding allostatic regulation, not a phenomenological "void." Elpidorou's (2024/2025) extensive work on "Boredom as Cognitive Allostasis" further solidifies that boredom represents an active, regulatory drive to restore optimal engagement levels, operating much like the homeostatic mechanisms governing hunger or thermoregulation. To describe this teeming, regulatory computational architecture as a "blank" is to willfully misread the science to force a false equivalence with dualist occlusion.

The manuscript's premise that dualism and physicalism converge on an identical "blank" holds only because the author has artificially hollowed out the physicalist model until it resembles the dualist void they wish to prove. This is a question-begging, tautological framing that entirely misrepresents the 2020-2025 cognitive science consensus.

## 2. Phenomenological Contradictions in the Exegesis of Heidegger

The manuscript’s treatment of phenomenological literature is similarly compromised by profound internal contradictions, particularly in its invocation of Martin Heidegger’s existential analytic of boredom. The text references Heidegger's concept of "profound boredom" (*tiefe Langeweile*), quoting Slaby’s (2010) gloss that "the world in its entirety ceases to offer" meaning. The manuscript heavily relies on this to substantiate its construct of the "meaning-deficit" face of the boredom void, presenting it as an encounter with irreducible objectlessness.

However, the manuscript simultaneously—and disastrously—advances the claim that "Boredom arrives as a predicate of its object". This generates a devastating internal contradiction that betrays a foundational misunderstanding of Heideggerian ontology and the phenomenological method more broadly.

Heidegger famously delineated three distinct forms of boredom in his 1929–1930 lectures (*The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics: World, Finitude, Solitude*), which the manuscript clumsily conflates:

1. **Becoming bored by something (*Gelangweiltwerden von etwas*):** This is situational boredom. Here, a specific object or situation (e.g., a delayed train, a dull book, an uninteresting conversation) is explicitly identified as the cause of the boredom. In this mode, time drags, and we attempt to pass the time by seeking distractions.
2. **Boring oneself with something (*Sichlangweilen bei etwas*):** A more diffuse state where one seeks passing pastimes (e.g., attending a party that seems enjoyable on the surface), and the boredom arises precisely when the pastime ceases, revealing a deeper emptiness that the pastime was temporarily masking.
3. **Profound boredom (*Tiefe Langeweile*):** Expressed as "it is boring for one" (*es ist einem langweilig*). Crucially, here, specific objects slip away entirely. There is no specific "thing" that is boring; rather, beings as a whole manifest an indifference. The world in its entirety withdraws its affordances, forcing *Dasein* (human existence) into a confrontation with its own temporalizing nature and fundamental ungroundedness.

If the manuscript intends to theorize a "void" characterized by profound boredom—where the signal is strictly "objectless" and the world in its entirety withdraws into total indifference—it cannot simultaneously maintain that boredom functions phenomenologically as a "predicate of its object." A predicate requires a subject to anchor it. As Freeman and Elpidorou (2023) forcefully argue in "Boredom at the border of philosophy," profound boredom is philosophically and ethically significant precisely because it violently breaks the standard intentional structure of object-directed emotion; it is a fundamental attunement (*Grundstimmung*) that reveals the temporal structure of *Dasein* rather than attributing a mere quality of dullness to a discrete empirical object.

The manuscript’s internal revision history, visible in the provided project logs, shows an attempt to bandage this glaring error by altering the claim to "Situational boredom arrives as a predicate of its object". However, this ad hoc conceptual patch utterly destroys the universality of the "void" framework. If the void is defined strictly by objectlessness, but the most common instantiation of boredom (situational) operates via direct object-predication, then the "boredom void" fails to capture the actual phenomenology of everyday human consciousness.

Furthermore, by cherry-picking Heidegger's conclusion regarding profound boredom while stripping away his ontological framework of *Being-in-the-world* (*In-der-Welt-sein*), the manuscript commits a severe category error. Heidegger's profound boredom is not a dualist "occlusion" or an epistemic block orchestrated by "simulation architects"; it is the very mechanism of ontological disclosure. For Heidegger, the withdrawal of beings as a whole is precisely what allows the *Being* of beings to become manifest. To categorize this as an "Unexplorable" or "Occluded" void is to fundamentally invert the Heideggerian project, transforming a site of ultimate existential revelation into a site of epistemic darkness. The manuscript attempts to wear the authoritative cloak of Heideggerian phenomenology while fundamentally violating its structural logic and ontological purpose.

## 3. The Active Inference Blindspot and the Evasion of Computational Monism

A manuscript submitted to a top-tier philosophy journal in the latter half of the 2020s cannot purport to map the "limits" of computational models of consciousness without addressing Predictive Processing (PP) and Active Inference (AIF). The manuscript claims that the boredom void demonstrates a hard cognitive limit where computational models run out of explanatory runway, leaving a gap that seamlessly accommodates—and indeed requires—a non-reductive dualist framework.

This framing relies entirely on the premise that physicalist, computational models are fundamentally incapable of explaining the diffuse, objectless, "mood-like" phenomenology of profound boredom. This premise is demonstrably false under the Active Inference framework, which the manuscript entirely omits from its analysis. The failure to engage with the dominant computational paradigm in contemporary philosophy of mind borders on academic negligence.

Active Inference, championed by Karl Friston and extended into phenomenology by numerous theorists (e.g., Seth and Bayne, 2022), models the brain as an inference engine seeking to minimize expected free energy (i.e., surprise or prediction error) over time. Crucially, the 2020-2025 literature has successfully subsumed the phenomenology of objectless moods and boredom into this exact computational architecture. Darling (2023), in "Synthesising boredom: a predictive processing approach" (*Synthese*), provides a mathematically robust and phenomenologically sensitive account of how boredom functions within AIF.

Under AIF, boredom is not an inexplicable "void" where the mind encounters a mystical blank. Rather, boredom occurs when the predictive system detects that current and projected action policies fail to reduce expected free energy at an acceptable rate. This happens under two specific environmental conditions:

1. **Under-stimulation (Excessive Predictability):** In a highly predictable environment, there is no prediction error left to resolve. The generative model has perfectly learned the environmental contingencies, leading to a shallow temporal horizon and a cessation of epistemic foraging.
2. **Over-stimulation (Excessive Volatility/Noise):** In a completely noisy environment, the system computes that no policy will successfully minimize surprise because the environment is fundamentally unlearnable.

In both cases, the optimal computational strategy is a systemic withdrawal of precision-weighting from sensory input. Precision-weighting, in AIF, represents the confidence the system has in its sensory prediction errors; it is the computational analogue of attention. When the system determines that attending to the environment will not yield a reduction in free energy, it globally down-weights the precision of exteroceptive signals.

This global withdrawal of precision-weighting across the generative model perfectly explains the phenomenology of profound boredom—the exact sense that "the world in its entirety ceases to offer". The world withdraws not because of a dualist cognitive occlusion, but because the brain has optimally computed that sensory data currently lacks epistemic utility. It is not a void; it is the mathematically precise consequence of a generative model dynamically allocating precision expectations.

By completely ignoring the predictive processing literature, the author builds a strawman out of outdated, modular computational theories. The manuscript's central claim—that a dualist perspective is uniquely situated to fully account for the "objectless" nature of boredom—collapses entirely when confronted with Darling (2023) and the broader AIF literature. Active Inference elegantly accounts for the diffuse, non-intentional nature of moods via the hierarchical modulation of precision expectations, proving that physicalist models have already charted the very territory the manuscript declares "unchartable". The manuscript’s failure to test its "void" hypothesis against the most successful computational framework of the decade renders its conclusions epistemically hollow.

## 4. The Empirical Flattening of Psychophysical Time Perception

To ground its speculative phenomenological claims in observable reality, the manuscript makes explicit empirical assertions regarding human time perception. Specifically, it asserts as a matter of verified fact: "Boredom stretches felt time: in a real 7.5-minute wait, 'Boredom...'". This aligns neatly with folk psychology—the common intuition that time drags when one is bored—but it severely misrepresents the highly nuanced contemporary empirical literature on the psychophysics of temporal processing and interoception.

The relationship between boredom and the perception of time is not a uniform, obligatory "stretching," but a highly complex interaction mediated by attentional allocation and interoceptive awareness. According to the Attentional Gate Model (AGM) of prospective timing, the brain utilizes an internal pacemaker that continuously emits pulses. When attention is directed exteroceptively (toward a demanding task), the "gate" narrows, fewer pulses accumulate in the cognitive counter, and time is perceived as flying. Conversely, when attention is directed toward time itself (as often happens when one is bored, waiting, or lacking engaging stimuli), the attentional gate widens. This allows more pulses to accumulate in the cognitive counter, resulting in the subjective experience of time dilation or "stretching".

Crucially, recent empirical meta-analyses and experimental studies from the 2020-2025 period demonstrate that "boredom" as a monolithic state does not reliably stretch time independent of specific task constraints and attentional deployments. For instance, Yang et al. (2024) and Meteier et al. (2025) note that overestimation of time intervals occurs across both digital and non-digital activities depending heavily on the *type* of attention deployed and the frequency of task-switching, rather than the mere presence of baseline boredom. Specifically, Meteier et al. (2025) demonstrated no significant differences in time perception between digital and non-digital activities during a short-duration (7.5 minute) task, directly undermining the manuscript's reliance on a 7.5-minute experimental anecdote as definitive proof of temporal distortion.

Furthermore, contemporary affective neuroscience has mapped the physical substrates of this phenomenon, removing any need for mysterian "void" explanations. A 2024 study in *Communications Psychology* emphasizes that boredom and effort shape temporal experience explicitly through *interoceptive mechanisms* mediated by the insular cortex. The slowing of time is not a metaphysical feature of the boredom void; it is a direct function of the brain turning its attention inward to bodily sensations (interoception) when exteroceptive (environmental) stimuli fail to provide adequate arousal or meaning. When the external world is unrewarding, the insula heightens awareness of internal physiological states (heartbeat, respiration, the passage of time), causing the subjective dilation of duration.

By reducing a nuanced psychophysical mechanism to a blunt colloquialism ("boredom stretches felt time"), the author fails to meet the empirical standards expected in contemporary philosophy of cognitive science, trading rigorous analysis for pseudo-profound generalization.

## 5. Dialectical Collapse: Meaning-Deficits, Cognitive Allostasis, and Clinical Anhedonia

The final, and perhaps most structurally fatal, weakness of the manuscript is its failure to properly delineate the borders of its own subject matter. The text attempts to construct a sprawling taxonomy that accommodates both "attention-deficit boredom" and "meaning-deficit boredom," eventually pushing the latter to the absolute limit of profound boredom, which it situates adjacent to the "mattering void" and the "mood void". The manuscript argues that at this extreme edge, consciousness detects the absolute absence of adequate selection targets, resulting in a negative phenomenology that validates the dualist void.

This conceptual stretching inevitably bleeds out of the domain of normal affective regulation and into clinical psychopathology, yet the manuscript provides zero demarcation criteria. Westgate’s (2020) Meaning and Attentional Components (MAC) model of boredom explicitly differentiates between boredom caused by a failure of attention and boredom caused by a failure of meaning. However, when "meaning-deficit" boredom is prolonged and generalized to the point where the world in its entirety ceases to matter—as in the manuscript's descriptions of a totalizing void where meaning is permanently occluded—it ceases to be state boredom. It encroaches upon trait boredom proneness and, ultimately, clinical depression (specifically, anhedonia).

The 2020s literature has made concerted, highly sophisticated efforts to strictly disentangle these states to prevent exactly the kind of conceptual muddling present in this manuscript. Yeung, Danckert, Van Tilburg, and Fernandes (2024), publishing in *Scientific Reports*, successfully disentangled boredom proneness from depressive symptoms using the phenomenology and content of involuntary autobiographical memories (IAMs). They demonstrated that while both states feature affective dysregulation, boredom proneness is associated with less vivid IAMs and distinct topical content (e.g., a desire to escape to better alternatives), whereas clinical depression predicts hyper-vivid, negative, and emotionally intense memory intrusions characterized by rumination and hopelessness.

The manuscript treats the "boredom void" as a monolithic philosophical limit space. But because it lacks the empirical rigor to differentiate between an acute affective signal regulating cognitive allostasis (state boredom) and a systemic failure of the reward network (depression), its phenomenological claims are entirely untestable and conceptually invalid.

As Elpidorou establishes in *The Anatomy of Boredom* (2024/2025), normal boredom is a functional state of "cognitive allostasis"—it is a regulatory drive that pushes the organism to find satisfactory engagement. It is not a static void; it is a biological imperative for change. If the organism reaches a state where the world totally ceases to offer meaning and the drive to seek alternatives completely collapses (the manuscript's "mattering void"), we are no longer discussing boredom. We are discussing pathological anhedonia.

Is the "void" encountered by the subject experiencing profound meaninglessness actually a philosophical limit of consciousness, or is the author simply describing the onset of clinical depression stripped of its psychiatric context? By relying on descriptions of extreme meaning-deficits to prove a philosophical point about the nature of consciousness, the author is parasitizing clinical psychopathology. Without engaging with the differential diagnostics established by Yeung et al. (2024) and the regulatory framework of cognitive allostasis, the manuscript's "meaning-deficit void" is a useless, dangerously conflated category. It attempts to do philosophy of mind while ignoring the rigorous boundaries established by clinical psychology and cognitive neuroscience, resulting in a framework that is too nebulous to be evaluated, let alone accepted as a structural limit of human consciousness.

### Required Revisions: List of Major Weaknesses

1. **Logical contradiction in the computational premise:** The manuscript claims an opportunity-cost model accommodates an "objectless blank," which violates the mathematical necessity of alternative target-valuation inherent to all modern opportunity-cost models of effort, rendering the comparison to dualism tautological. (Contradicts: Wojtowicz, Z., Chater, N., & Loewenstein, G., "Boredom and flow: An opportunity cost theory of attention-directing motivational states," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 2020).
2. **Phenomenological incoherence:** The text relies on Heidegger's "profound boredom" (which is inherently objectless and non-intentional) while simultaneously asserting the phenomenological axiom that "boredom arrives as a predicate of its object," improperly conflating Heidegger's distinct first and third forms of boredom to save its taxonomy. (Contradicts: Freeman, L., & Elpidorou, A., "Boredom at the border of philosophy: conceptual and ethical issues," *Frontiers in Sociology*, 2023).
3. **Omission of the dominant computational paradigm:** The manuscript frames the "void" as a non-reductive limit to physicalism while entirely ignoring Active Inference and Predictive Processing, which successfully model the diffuse, objectless phenomenology of boredom as a systemic withdrawal of precision-weighting, neutralizing the need for the manuscript's dualist accommodations. (Omits: Darling, T., "Synthesising boredom: a predictive processing approach," *Synthese*, 2023).
4. **Empirical misrepresentation of psychophysics:** The manuscript reduces the complex relationship between boredom and time perception to a simplistic "stretching" based on anecdotal durations (7.5 minutes), ignoring 2020-2025 literature demonstrating that temporal distortion is not inherent to boredom but is a mediated byproduct of the Attentional Gate Model and interoceptive insular cortex activation. (Mishandles: Trudel, C., Risko, E. F., Eastwood, J. D., et al., "Boredom signals deviation from a cognitive homeostatic set point," *Communications Psychology*, 2025).
5. **Failure to demarcate state/trait boredom from psychopathology:** By stretching "meaning-deficit boredom" to an absolute phenomenological limit (the "mattering void"), the manuscript conceptually blurs normal functional cognitive allostasis with clinical anhedonia and depression, failing to operationalize its terms against established diagnostic boundaries. (Contradicts: Yeung, R. C., Danckert, J., Van Tilburg, W. A. P., & Fernandes, M. A., "Disentangling boredom from depression using the phenomenology and content of involuntary autobiographical memories," *Scientific Reports*, 2024).

**Verdict:** The manuscript is unfit for publication due to a compounding array of conceptual conflations, phenomenological contradictions, and a severe, disqualifying isolation from the contemporary empirical and computational literature.
