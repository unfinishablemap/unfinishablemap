## Executive Summary

The page is careful and well sourced. Its hinge, the split between ontological and epistemic primitiveness, is new to the Map and correctly stated. Its weak point is the one the driver suspected: it says Type-B "concedes most of" the cost, and the support for that comes almost wholly from the anti-materialists. Two quotes do the work. One is a Block & Stalnaker parenthetical that, in context, belongs to their argument that the identity *can* help close the gap. The other is Chalmers's own inference about what type-B is committed to, reported as a record of what type-B theorists accept. Meanwhile Papineau's 2011 epistemic answer, printed on the page the article cites, is left out. Separately, both glosses of "strong necessity" drop "metaphysically possible". Read naturally, they make a strong necessity a priori, which erases the conceivable/possible gap that is the crux of the two-dimensional argument, the very content the routing table's "Two-dimensional argument" row now sends readers here for. The analogy premise is asserted in the Map's voice without Chalmers and Jackson's own qualifiers ("objective (non-indexical) … among natural phenomena"), two paragraphs after the page reports Schaffer and Block & Stalnaker denying it. The "does not show" list misses that interactionism carries a second primitive bridge, its selection laws, which Type-B and epiphenomenalism do without. The tier verdict (*compatible*) and the P-D1 filing are correct and should be kept.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

"Epistemic primitiveness is relative to the concepts you have now. Vitalists found 'life is physicochemical' just as underivable." The page's opponent is Type-B, which itself holds the gap to be permanent, so this objection belongs to Type-C, and the routing page already handles it. No finding.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

"Chalmers and Jackson derive water only 'modulo phenomenology'. They put every phenomenal truth into the base and then report that nothing is left over except the phenomenal. The uniqueness is built into the base." This is Type-A's complaint and is routed elsewhere. It does bear on the page in one place: L49 states in the Map's voice, as fact, that "An ordinary scientific identity is still epistemically derivable". That is Chalmers and Jackson's thesis, and Block & Stalnaker, Papineau (2011, p. 9: "I strongly doubt that even such paradigm scientific identities as water = H2O can in fact be derived") and Levine (p. 91) all deny it. Folded into Issue 1.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

"You say denying closure answers the causal half of Block and Stalnaker's case. Fine. But your own Tenet 3 needs *selection laws* as well as correlation laws. That is a second fundamental psychophysical bridge, which the identity theorist and the epiphenomenalist never posit. By the ledger this page keeps, epistemically primitive bridges, you pay twice." The Map's own [[psychophysical-laws]] page draws exactly this distinction ("Supervenience Laws vs. Causal Laws"; selection laws are "Compatible with epiphenomenalism? No"). Issue 4.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

Not engaged. The page's modal discussion concerns the space of metaphysically possible worlds, not Everettian branching, and it makes no claim under Tenet 4. No finding.

### The Empiricist (Popper's Ghost)

"Your 'does not show' list ends with 'Anything empirical', and the tier stays *compatible*. Good. But your extra primitive, the selection law, does not even buy testability on your default reading. Your own register says the Born-preserving corridor is empirically indistinguishable by construction." This sharpens Issue 4. It argues for stating the extra primitive plainly, and against any reply that it earns its keep through an empirical channel.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

"'Identities are ontologically primitive: a thing just is itself.' That is the svabhāva claim, the claim of self-nature, in a lab coat." The page is about property identity, and it disambiguates subject identity at L43. Out of scope. No finding.

### The Type-B physicalist (Papineau), added for this page

"You quote my 1993 line and my 2011 line together, then let Chalmers and Jackson's 2001 'conflation' charge answer both. My 2011 paper keeps the two questions apart. Under 'Explaining Identities?' I say identities need no explanation. Under 'Explaining Belief in Identities?' I say identities 'can be evidenced more directly', for instance when 'the two kinds at issue co-occur' (p. 10). "You cite me only for a reading of Kripke. My half of the paper answers your surviving argument. Chalmers's '37 worlds' worry targets a *primitive* metaphysical modality, and 'stress-free modal dualists can happily accept the existence of radically opaque terms without needing to commit to a primitive notion of metaphysical modality which can't be analysed in terms of conceivability'. Conceivability stays the guide. The question then becomes whether phenomenal concepts are transparent, which is my route against physicalism." Issue 3.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Type-B's concessions are overstated, and its best epistemic reply is missing

- **File**: concepts/primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities.md
- **Location**: L41 ("the cost is real and that Type-B concedes most of it on the side of explanation"); L49 ("An ordinary scientific identity is still epistemically derivable"; "The slogan … leaves the epistemic one untouched"); L59 ("Type-B largely grants the parity half"; the Block & Stalnaker parenthetical; "Chalmers records that type-B theorists"); L91 ("The parity result removes a defeater"); L114 ("The cost shows that Type-B pays property dualism's explanatory price").
- **Problem**: Four things.
  - (a) **Block & Stalnaker's parenthetical is recruited against its context.** On p. 29 the sentence before it reads that the thought experiments "don't show that an identity claim connecting consciousness with a kind of neurophysiological process cannot be true, and cannot play a role in closing an explanatory gap. (Note that we are not saying that the identity closes the gap all by itself.)" The parenthetical limits their *positive* claim, that the identity helps close the gap. Read in context, it denies parity with property dualism rather than conceding it.
  - (b) **Chalmers's inference is reported as a record.** The 1999 sentence is "Although a type-B materialism will reject my conclusions about ontology, it will (in effect) accept my conclusions about explanation" (§3.6). Its subject is the *position*, and "in effect" marks Chalmers's inference about what the position commits to. "Chalmers records that type-B theorists …" changes the subject to the theorists and the verb to a report. The predicate is verbatim and the subject is spliced.
  - (c) **Papineau 2011 is answered with a charge it does not commit.** Chalmers and Jackson's "conflate" (2001, §6) names Papineau 1993 and Block & Stalnaker. Papineau 2011, on the page the article cites (p. 9) and the next one, separates "Explaining Identities?" from "Explaining Belief in Identities?" and answers the epistemic question directly. He gives co-occurrence evidence, and he argues that names and indexicals are underivable without any felt gap. Chalmers & Jackson's base includes indexical truths, and Chalmers 2010's audit takes up names (case 1) and demonstratives (case 4), so the reply has answers. But the page never states it.
  - (d) **Contested premises in the Map's voice.** "An ordinary scientific identity is still epistemically derivable" (L49) is Chalmers & Jackson's PQTI thesis. Block & Stalnaker (p. 29), Papineau (2011, p. 9) and Levine (p. 91) deny it. The parity result's power to remove a defeater (L91) depends on it.

  Of the type-B authors, only Levine concedes in his own words. The page's lede and its Relation to Site Perspective (L114, "The cost shows") are stronger than the body can support.
- **Severity**: High. This is a fairness defect against the live opponent, carried by two quotes the reader cannot check in context, and it sits in the lede.
- **Recommendation**: Scope the lede to what Type-B actually grants (non-derivability), give Papineau's 2011 epistemic reply in his words, drop the parenthetical, change "records" to "argues", and attribute the derivability thesis. Priority list, item 1.

### Issue 2: Both glosses of "strong necessity" drop "metaphysically possible", which makes a strong necessity look a priori

- **File**: concepts/primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities.md
- **Location**: L39 ("it comes out true however the actual world turns out"); L67 ("so no world, however considered, verifies its negation").
- **Problem**: Chalmers's definition quantifies over *metaphysically possible* worlds: strong necessities "are a posteriori necessities that are verified by all centered metaphysically possible worlds" (2010, §7). And: "The primary intension of a strong necessity … will be false in no metaphysically possible world. Of course the primary intension will be false in some conceivable world, because the statement is a posteriori. Hence the crucial feature of strong necessities, which is that they require a distinction between conceivability and possibility at the level of worlds" (1999, §3). L39's "however the actual world turns out" reads most naturally as epistemic: whatever we discover. On that reading the sentence is a priori, contradicting "a posteriori" earlier in the same sentence. L67's "no world, however considered" says the same and removes the conceivable-but-impossible world, which is the whole content of a strong necessity and the point thesis (ii) of the two-dimensional argument denies. This is an epistemic/metaphysical equivocation in a definition, not in a conclusion. Hedging does not touch it.
- **Severity**: High. It is a definitional error in the lede, and in the section the routing table's "Two-dimensional argument" row now targets (`#Strong Necessities`, L90 of the routing page).
- **Recommendation**: Restore the qualifier at both loci and quote Chalmers's "at the level of worlds" sentence, which carries the two-dimensional crux in 13 words. Priority list, item 2.

### Issue 3: The identity-or-law considerations are stated more strongly, or more narrowly, than the sources allow

- **File**: concepts/primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities.md
- **Location**: L75 ("every other epistemically primitive regularity is a law"); L95 ("**Analogy.** Every other epistemically primitive regularity is a law."); L97 ("It is a priori, and it meets Papineau's counter."); L99 ("is a separate, causal consideration that rests on the closure of the physical"); L116 ("Only his argument from the epistemology of modality survives").
- **Problem**: Four things.
  - (a) **The analogy has lost its qualifiers.** Chalmers & Jackson's premise is restricted: "The point where one finds objective (non-indexical) epistemically primitive regularities among natural phenomena is precisely the point at which one finds fundamental natural laws" (2001, §7), and they introduce it with "one can argue". Indexical truths are epistemically primitive and sit in their own base, and mathematics lies outside "natural phenomena". So "every other epistemically primitive regularity is a law" is false by their own text. The qualified version is also contested: L77, two paragraphs earlier, reports Schaffer (gaps "are pervasive", bridged by grounding principles) and Block & Stalnaker (water is not a priori entailed) denying it. L95 then asserts it in the Map's voice.
  - (b) **The epistemology strand is half-answered and ambiguously stated.** "It is a priori, and it meets Papineau's counter" reads as two credits ("meets" = answers). The research note has "It is a priori, though, and it meets…", where the point is a limitation: a priori arguments bear on plausibility rather than tier, as the routing page says. The page also omits Goff's half of the same joint paper. Goff argues that stress-free modal dualists accept strong necessities "without needing to commit to a primitive notion of metaphysical modality which can't be analysed in terms of conceivability" (§2.3), which disarms the "37 worlds" worry for strong necessities as such and moves the question to whether phenomenal concepts are transparent. The one consideration the page says survives Tenet 5 is answered from both sides, and Goff's answer leads back to the phenomenal-concepts dispute the analogy already depends on.
  - (c) **Block & Stalnaker's transfer argument is filed as purely causal.** Their p. 24 passage is about "explanatory *and* causal force", and its explanatory half is the dissolving of the why-question: "once we realize that heat is molecular kinetic energy, questions like this will be seen as wrongheaded". That half rests on no closure premise. It is the no-explanation reply, which the page's first section answers.
  - (d) **"Only … survives" (L116) reads as covering the analogy too**, which the body calls "the Map's strongest consideration". Scope it to Chalmers's case against modal dualism.
- **Severity**: Medium–High. The analogy is the page's "strongest consideration", and it is stated in a form its authors do not hold.
- **Recommendation**: Restore Chalmers & Jackson's qualifiers with attribution, add Goff, mark the a priori limitation, split the transfer argument, and scope L116. Priority list, item 3.

### Issue 4: The "does not show" list misses interactionism's second primitive

- **File**: concepts/primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities.md
- **Location**: L107 ("**Anything specific to interactionism.** … The cost argument favours D, E and F alike."); L118 ("because the dualist's psychophysical law is as primitive as Type-B's identity").
- **Problem**: The page treats the dualist's bridge as one law. The Map's own [[psychophysical-laws]] distinguishes *supervenience laws* (upward, "Compatible with epiphenomenalism? Yes") from *selection laws* (downward, "No"), and Tenet 3 "requires causal laws". On the cost argument's own currency, epistemically primitive bridging principles, the Map's interactionism therefore carries a family of primitives that Type-B (mental causation is physical causation) and type-E do without. The bullet's verdict ("favours D, E and F alike") holds for the identity-or-law question, but the bullet is headed "Anything specific to interactionism", and the one specific thing the cost argument implies about interactionism is a debit. Popper's point stands too: on the Map's default Born-preserving reading the extra primitive buys no empirical channel ([[positions/quantum-interface]] P-Q2, discriminability "none-by-construction").
- **Severity**: Medium. It is an omission in the list whose purpose is to stop the page being over-read in the Map's favour.
- **Recommendation**: One sentence in the bullet and one clause in the Relation to Site Perspective. Priority list, item 4.

### "Stress-free modal dualism keeps conceivability as the guide"
- **Current content says**: the epistemology-of-modality strand survives Tenet 5 and "meets Papineau's counter" (L97).

- **Suggested response**: Name Goff's reply, and note that it turns the question into whether phenomenal concepts are transparent, which is the phenomenal-concepts dispute again. Item 3.

### "The analogy is itself an induction from uniformity"
- **Current content says**: the analogy survives Tenet 5, conditional on the master argument (L95).

- **Suggested response**: The master-argument condition already carries most of this. Not minted. Note it if the page is ever expanded.
