## 1. Citation and quotation audit

### Direct quotations

The six direct quotations in the live article all check out.

1. Russell and Barrett’s statement that “a person is always in some state of core affect, even if neutral” is verbatim from the cited page. The quotation is accurate, although the underlying proposition is part of their theoretical model rather than an independently demonstrated universal of consciousness. [Affective Science Lab](https://www.affective-science.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/RussellFB1999.pdf)
2. Barlassina and Hayward’s “Get less of me!” is an exact excerpt. Their fuller paired slogan is “Get more of me! Get less of me!”, corresponding to positive and negative affective imperatives. [DOI+1](https://doi.org/10.1093/MIND/FZZ035)
3. Bain’s formulation that the “missing component consists in evaluative content” is verbatim and used in the right theoretical context. [Enlighten Publications+1](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/82688/)
4. Martínez’s reference to “the familiar asymbolic pattern of sensory pain without painfulness” is verbatim and accurately attributed. [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/archive/MARGIG.pdf)
5. Jacobson’s “shooting the messenger” wording is exact and accurately represents his objection to removing unpleasantness while leaving the represented bodily problem untouched. [Wiley Online Library+1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9213.12029)

## 2. Claim-by-claim substantive audit

### 2.1 Valence as a common dimension

The article begins with a reasonable working definition: valence is the positive–negative or pleasant–unpleasant aspect of experience. Its stronger assertion is that this “generality” makes valence fundamental by supplying a “common evaluative dimension” across otherwise incommensurable experiences. [[valence]]

That passage runs together at least four different theses:

1. Experiences can be described as pleasant, unpleasant or neutral.
2. The nervous system contains signals that distinguish beneficial from harmful conditions.
3. decision systems compute a common comparison variable across options;
4. all felt valence is one phenomenally homogeneous scalar magnitude.

The first thesis is relatively uncontroversial. The other three do not follow from it.

Current affective science still lacks a stable, universally accepted operational definition of valence. Walle and Dukes report disagreement over whether valence denotes feeling, appraisal, approach–avoidance disposition, stimulus property or some combination, and warn that one-dimensional uses can oversimplify emotional functions and measurement. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37744985/)

Mixed-valence work also makes a simple bipolar scale difficult to treat as the whole phenomenon. A 2024 fMRI study found neurally consistent representations of simultaneous positive and negative feeling, with mixed and univalent states showing different patterns across the insula, anterior cingulate and ventromedial prefrontal cortex. This does not disprove valence, but it weighs against treating each experience as occupying a single point on one positive–negative line. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38566509/)

Recent human imaging provides evidence for cross-modal neural representation of valence: a 2024 study of sustained pleasure and pain found distinguishable valence- and intensity-predictive voxel populations across prefrontal, orbitofrontal, insular and amygdala regions, with successful out-of-sample decoding. But this supports distributed neural representation of valence, not an intrinsic phenomenal scalar or a single quantity consulted by consciousness. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38857402/)

The Map’s own *Valence as a Natural Kind* page is more careful than the host article. It explicitly says that fragmentation threatens the scalar-currency premise while leaving open a plurality of felt hedonic properties. It also notes that the site’s wider value pluralism denies that every evaluative dimension reduces to one hedonic measure. [[valence-as-a-natural-kind]]

**Verdict:** the reality of positive and negative feeling is adequately motivated. The stronger claim that felt valence is a single scalar common currency is not. The article should stop moving between “shared descriptive dimension,” “neural value representation,” “decision currency” and “phenomenal magnitude” without marking the transitions.

### 2.2 Core affect is “always present”

The Russell–Barrett quotation is accurate, but the prose subtly upgrades a theoretical posit into a general fact about human consciousness. “Some hedonic state is always present, though it may be neutral” is defensible only if “hedonic state” includes a zero or indeterminate point. Otherwise, calling a neutral condition hedonic risks defining the claim into truth. [[valence]]

The article also leaves open whether non-human or artificial consciousness might lack valence. That is an appropriate qualification. But it creates tension with *The Valence Void*, which says that every moment of experience demonstrates valence and that all cognition used to investigate it is already valenced. The host page allows a genuinely neutral or valence-free mind; the void page repeatedly writes as though valence pervades consciousness necessarily. [[valence]]

A cleaner formulation would distinguish:

- the empirical core-affect hypothesis about ordinary waking humans;
- the phenomenological question whether apparent neutrality is low-intensity valence, a midpoint, mixed affect or absence;
- the metaphysical question whether consciousness without valence is possible.

The current wording partly distinguishes them, but neighbouring pages erase the distinction.

### 2.3 Panksepp and the evolutionary antiquity of valence

The article says that Panksepp’s work shows subcortical circuits generating positive and negative affect across species, “suggesting valence is a foundational feature of conscious experience rather than a cognitive overlay.” [[valence]]

That fairly reports Panksepp’s interpretation, but it is not a balanced statement of what the neuroscience establishes. It moves too quickly from:

- conserved subcortical approach, avoidance and homeostatic circuitry,

to:

- conserved conscious positive and negative feeling.

LeDoux and Brown defend the opposite interpretation: subcortical survival circuits provide nonconscious first-order inputs, while conscious emotional feelings arise through cortical higher-order or general cognitive systems. Their paper explicitly contrasts this with Panksepp’s subcortical affect-system view. [PNAS+2](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/114/10/E2016.full.pdf)

Constructionist and higher-order approaches therefore do not deny ancient affect-regulating circuits; they deny that activity in those circuits by itself demonstrates conscious valence. A recent review of cognitive versus precognitive emotion theories concluded that the issue remains unresolved, with methodological constraints preventing either camp from claiming a decisive empirical victory. Contemporary model-testing has also found patterns more compatible with distributed, context-sensitive construction than with a small set of dedicated basic-emotion circuits. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2023/1/niad005/7109548)

Panksepp remains historically and theoretically important. The staleness is not that a 1998 book is unusable; it is that it is presented without a current adversarial companion. The article should say:

> Panksepp interprets conserved subcortical systems as generators of conscious affect; higher-order and constructionist theorists interpret much of the same circuitry as nonconscious input into subsequently constructed feeling.

That would accurately identify the evidential underdetermination.

### 2.4 The three philosophical accounts

The treatments of the hedonic, evaluativist and imperativist accounts are mostly accurate and substantially improved. The article properly notes that pain asymbolia does not discriminate among them because each can decompose the sensory and affective dimensions in a different way. It also correctly separates representationalism from reductive physicalism and concedes that the Map’s dualism would survive the defeat of the intrinsic-hedonic account. [[valence]]

Two qualifications are needed.

First, these are not exhaustive alternatives. At least four other families need acknowledgement:

- functional or computational-role accounts;
- higher-order and self-representational accounts;
- relational or constructionist accounts;
- illusionist or eliminativist accounts.

Some can be combined with evaluativism or imperativism, but they answer different questions. The current organisation makes active inference appear later as one especially developed functionalist objection, while illusionism is omitted entirely.

Second, the hedonic account receives the most phenomenologically sympathetic presentation. The painkiller and objectless-mood arguments are legitimate considerations, but neither settles the issue. Representationalists can represent the bodily condition, the experience itself or the world under a global evaluative aspect; the article acknowledges part of this. It does not engage Carruthers’s stronger concern that, if motivation requires detecting an intrinsic property of one’s experience, the intrinsic-quale account appears to require a form of awareness of one’s own pain state that is implausibly demanding for infants and many animals. [Paperzz+1](https://paperzz.com/doc/7650404/valence-and-value---university-of-maryland)

Nor does it discuss evidence and theory concerning unconscious affective valuation, subliminal conditioning and nonconscious motivational states. Such phenomena do not show that conscious valence is unreal, but they undermine any simple identification of evaluative or motivational force with conscious feeling. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/pq/article/72/4/795/6510180)

**Verdict:** the taxonomy is accurate as far as it goes, but it is arranged around accounts hospitable to phenomenal realism and omits the family most directly challenging the host article’s explanandum.

### 2.5 The hard-problem argument

The article writes:

> “A complete functional description of nociception … leaves the badness unexplained.”

It then invokes a functionally identical pain zombie. [[valence]]

For a dualist, this is the article’s central intuition. It is not, however, a neutral consequence of completing a functional description. A functionalist holds that the relevant pattern of representation, self-model, appraisal, attention, learning, motivation and global availability constitutes the badness. An illusionist holds that introspection represents such states as possessing an additional intrinsic phenomenal property when no such property exists. On those views, the alleged residue is not an agreed datum awaiting an extra cause.

The article partly recognises this at the end, where it calls the gap an “unclosed seam” rather than a demonstration that reduction must fail. That calibration is good. [[valence]]

But the body does not engage the most direct recent rival. François Kammerer’s 2026 *House of Mirrors* contains an explicit treatment of apparent phenomenal valence. His proposal is that affective systems represent states and outcomes as primitively good or bad, while introspective mechanisms misrepresent those functional–representational states as possessing irreducible phenomenal goodness or badness. The theory may ultimately fail, but it directly addresses why valence appears introspectively intrinsic; it does not merely explain avoidance behaviour while ignoring the appearance. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/62981/chapter/565378730)

The Map’s predictive-processing neighbour mentions meta-problem approaches but dismisses them chiefly on the ground that explaining consciousness-talk does not explain consciousness. That is too compressed. It restates the Map’s ontology instead of assessing whether the rival successfully explains the evidence that led us to posit that ontology. [[predictive-processing-and-dualism]]

The host article therefore needs to divide the disagreement into three questions:

1. What functional and representational processes produce aversion and valuation?
2. What produces introspective judgments that badness is intrinsic and irreducible?
3. Is there an additional phenomenal property beyond the answers to the first two?

The article treats question 3 as already answered affirmatively and then faults its rivals for answering only questions 1 and 2. That is the principal tenet-protective feature of the argument.

### 2.6 Active inference: the material source-characterisation failure

This is the clearest technical defect.

The article combines Joffily and Coricelli’s proposal with Hesp and colleagues’ model, then concludes that valence is:

> “a prior over the nociceptive stream … on which asymbolia is disrupted valence-prior assignment … predicting exactly this dissociation.” [[valence]]

The cited sources do not jointly establish that account.

Joffily and Coricelli model emotional valence in terms of the negative rate of change of free energy. Hesp and colleagues instead construct a hierarchical proof-of-principle model in which an agent infers higher-level valence states from patterns of changes in expected precision or “affective charge.” [PLOS+2](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article)

Crucially, Hesp and colleagues explicitly say that they **do not equate expected precision directly with valence**. Changes in expected precision supply one source of evidence from which a higher-level valence state is inferred. Their simulated agent is a synthetic rat in a T-maze, and the authors describe the work as the first step in a larger programme that still needs fitting to behavioural and neural responses. [pure.uva.nl+2](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/60962725/neco_a_01341.pdf)

The paper contains no discussion of nociceptive processing or pain asymbolia. [pure.uva.nl+1](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/60962725/neco_a_01341.pdf)

The live article has therefore added four claims not supplied by the cited papers:

- that the relevant state is straightforwardly a “signal of expected value”;
- that it is a prior specifically over the nociceptive stream;
- that pain asymbolia consists in disruption of that prior;
- that the model “predicts exactly” the clinical dissociation.

Those may form a defensible Map-authored synthesis, perhaps with support from Gerrans or other predictive-processing accounts of pain and depersonalisation. But they must be labelled as a synthesis and separately sourced. At present the paragraph attributes a pain-specific lesion model to two papers that do not present it.

The phrase “expected value” is also technically loose. In active inference, expected free energy contains both preference-sensitive or pragmatic and epistemic terms; expected precision modulates reliance on expected free energy in policy selection. It is not simply a scalar expected-value signal. [pure.uva.nl+1](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/60962725/neco_a_01341.pdf)

This matters dialectically. The article first makes the functional rival more unified, specific and clinically predictive than its sources warrant, then concedes that mechanism and locates the surviving dispute at phenomenal constitution. The concession is intellectually welcome, but it must be made to a correctly reconstructed rival.

### 2.7 Pain asymbolia

The article is right to have retreated from treating asymbolia as decisive. It says that the dissociation does not determine whether felt badness causes avoidance or merely accompanies the neural state that does. That is an important and appropriate concession. [[valence]]

Its remaining shorthand is still too clean:

> patients have “intact sensory pain processing but absent felt badness,” and normal avoidance disappears when the badness disappears.

The live *Pain Asymbolia* article, updated on 24 November, is more cautious. It notes that the lesions and associated deficits are not cleanly localised, that Griffith and Kind argue the retained state no longer qualifies as pain, that Duval and Klein dispute that conclusion, and that the condition should not be described as a pristine one-variable subtraction. It further concedes that a predictive-processing decomposition can generate the same broad dissociation and that the clinical evidence does not by itself favour the Map’s causal interpretation. [[pain-asymbolia]]

There is also an internal tension within the neighbour: its opening still categorically says patients do not withdraw, guard injured limbs or show distress, whereas its body now emphasises classificatory uncertainty, heterogeneous damage and the limits of the clinical record. [[pain-asymbolia]]

The valence article’s 17 November wording should be updated to match the 24 November source-fidelity repair. A defensible version would say:

> Some reported asymbolia cases preserve substantial sensory-discriminative responsiveness while losing much of the ordinary affective, defensive and motivational profile. How much “pain” remains, and whether the dissociation isolates phenomenal badness rather than a broader integrative or self-model function, remains contested.

That would preserve the philosophical importance without presenting a neurologically clean subtraction experiment.

### 2.8 Valence and moral status

The article’s weakest philosophical conclusion is:

> “valence provides the necessary condition: without felt badness, there is nothing for moral concern to be about.” [[valence]]

Three different claims are being compressed:

1. Negative valence grounds suffering and welfare loss.
2. A capacity for positive or negative valence is sufficient for at least some moral standing.
3. Valence is necessary for every kind of moral standing.

The first is highly plausible. The second is a major form of sentientism. The third is controversial and is not established by Rawlette, Bentham or the preceding neuroscience.

Taken literally, “without felt badness” would deny moral concern to a being capable only of positive experiences. If the intended claim is “without any positive or negative valence,” it still excludes philosophical Vulcans that are conscious, intelligent, autonomous, socially related or capable of projects but lack hedonic feeling.

Smithies’s now-published chapter defends hedonic sentientism, but its framing explicitly recognises Chalmers’s opposing inclusive view, according to which conscious Vulcans retain moral status. The cited literature therefore presents a live dispute, not an established necessary condition. [DOI+2](https://doi.org/10.1093/9780198994121.003.0011)

The claim also conflicts with the Map’s own *Phenomenal Value Realism*. That article treats meaning, aesthetic character, understanding, connection and agency as potentially intrinsic dimensions of experienced value that do not reduce to pleasure or pain. It specifically says that authorship of action may carry significance independently of hedonic payoff. A valence-free subject possessing those forms of experienced significance would appear to matter on the neighbour’s pluralist account but not on the valence article’s necessary-condition account. [[phenomenal-value-realism]]

The host page acknowledges an is–ought gap but then crosses it too quickly. Bentham’s famous question is a powerful moral orientation; it is not an argument that suffering is the only possible basis of moral status. Rawlette’s phenomenal-value realism can support the intrinsic disvalue of suffering without proving that autonomy, agency, relationships, conscious projects or non-hedonic phenomenal value are morally irrelevant.

**Verdict:** valence is very plausibly sufficient for welfare interests and moral patienthood. The article has not shown that it is necessary for all moral status. The wording should be changed from “the necessary condition” to “one especially strong and widely accepted basis of moral patienthood.”

### 2.9 Animal sentience and invertebrates

The host article deserves credit for saying that its expectation of valence wherever coupling occurs is “a framework commitment, not a probability the evidence supplies.” [[valence]]

But the discussion is much too compressed for the moral burden placed on it. It jumps from valence as a moral ground to the possibility that trillions of invertebrates in agricultural systems possess it, without presenting a taxon-specific evidence structure or directly sourcing the numerical scale claim.

Current animal-sentience methodology is deliberately multi-criterion and graded. The decapod review applies eight neural and cognitive-behavioural criteria and reports strong evidence for true crabs, substantial evidence for anomuran crabs and astacid lobsters or crayfish, and weaker evidence for several other infraorders. It does not infer one uniform answer for “crustaceans.” [wellbeingintlstudiesrepository.org](https://www.wellbeingintlstudiesrepository.org/animsent/vol7/iss32/1/)

The 2026 cephalopod update similarly finds strong evidence for octopuses and cuttlefish, substantial evidence for squid, and far greater uncertainty for nautiluses. [Wiley Online Library+1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/brv.70125)

Behavioural markers need equally careful interpretation. Motivational trade-offs can increase the probability of sentience when they involve memory, flexible integration and representation of absent risks and opportunities. But simple trade-offs also occur in systems such as *C. elegans*, so trade-off behaviour alone is not sufficient. Brown and Birch’s 2025 analysis explicitly stresses that the relevant mechanistic and representational structure must be examined case by case. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41229290/)

The Map also needs to distinguish:

- nociception;
- affective valuation;
- conscious positive or negative feeling;
- a persisting subject of experience;
- welfare;
- moral status.

Evidence for one raises but does not settle the others.

There is an important methodological tension here. The Map denies that behavioural and computational roles constitute feeling, yet it must use those same roles as evidence when attributing feeling to animals. This is not a contradiction if the site clearly distinguishes **epistemic indicators** from **metaphysical constitution**. At present that distinction is insufficiently explicit.

The linked invertebrate article introduces a further overstatement by saying that under dualism consciousness simply either couples or does not, making subjecthood binary rather than graded. That does not follow from dualism as such. It follows, if at all, from the Map’s particular coupling architecture plus a substantive anti-vagueness or threshold premise. Moreover, binary subjecthood does not imply binary welfare or establish that every subject is valenced. [[ethics-of-consciousness-invertebrate-question]]

### 2.10 Valence as a quantum selection criterion

This is one of the better-calibrated parts of the page. The article labels value-sensitive quantum selection as speculative, low-credence, framework-internal and unsupported by independent evidence. It also acknowledges mechanism debt and an unresolved distinction between direct valence-sensitive selection and attention-mediated effects. [[valence]]

The remaining problem is the return of “currency” language. The article proposes that valence makes qualitatively different outcomes commensurable, while its own natural-kind and value-pluralism pages deny that all evaluative dimensions share a hedonic measure. [[valence-as-a-natural-kind]]

The selection article itself is more disciplined. It distinguishes felt valence from dopamine prediction errors, stored value estimates and domain-general choice signals, and concedes that neuroeconomic common-currency models are phenomenally neutral: their existence does not establish a felt selector. [[valence-and-conscious-selection]]

The host should therefore formulate the quantum proposal conditionally:

> If a unified phenomenal comparison variable exists, it could in principle supply a selection metric.

It should not use the framework’s need for a metric as evidence that phenomenology actually supplies one.

## 3. Missing serious counterarguments

### Affective neuroscience

The article should engage at least the following rival considerations:

**Higher-order emotional consciousness.** LeDoux and Brown argue that subcortical survival circuits contribute nonconscious inputs while conscious emotional feeling is assembled in cortical higher-order systems. This directly challenges the inference from ancient affect circuits to ancient conscious valence. [PNAS+1](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/114/10/E2016.full.pdf)

**Psychological construction.** Contemporary constructionist models treat valence as relational, context-sensitive or constructed through distributed systems rather than a fixed intrinsic ingredient generated by dedicated affect modules. The host mentions Barrett but uses the canonical core-affect formulation more than it engages the later constructionist challenge.

**Distributed and dynamic coding.** Current evidence favours overlapping, flexible and context-dependent valence systems rather than a simple positive-versus-negative circuit dichotomy. Recent work on nucleus accumbens circuitry, for example, warns against the older picture in which D1 pathways simply encode reward and D2 pathways simply encode aversion. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763425001253)

**Mixed valence.** Simultaneously positive and negative experience challenges the claim that every state can be denominated in one scalar without loss. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38566509/)

**Reward versus punishment asymmetry.** The common-currency literature is much better developed for reward than for punishment, and recent reviews identify substantial conceptual and methodological problems in extending one currency across positive and aversive outcomes. [Annual Reviews+2](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/psych/76/1)

### Functionalist accounts

The generic functionalist introduced at line 66 is too weak. Serious functionalists do not merely rename “whatever is missing when the feeling disappears.” They propose interacting roles involving:

- bodily-damage representation;
- global availability;
- attention capture;
- action prioritisation;
- precision weighting;
- reinforcement and counterfactual learning;
- self-attribution;
- long-term protection of bodily integrity;
- metacognitive confidence and policy control.

The active-inference paragraph begins to correct this, but the article should present the full role before criticising it. Its best objection is then not that functionalism specifies no role, but that the Map denies that even the complete role constitutes phenomenal badness. That is a metaphysical disagreement, not an empirical omission.

The article should also confront the functionalist response to asymbolia more directly: the lesion may remove an integrated appraisal, self-model or action-prioritisation subsystem, with the changed experience and changed behaviour both resulting from that loss. The current article acknowledges this possibility but still repeatedly describes behaviour as “tracking” the absent phenomenal property. [[valence]]

### Illusionism

The complete absence of a named illusionist treatment is the largest philosophical omission.

Kammerer’s 2026 account is especially relevant because it addresses apparent valenced phenomenality directly rather than only generic consciousness. It attempts to explain why affective states are introspectively represented as primitively good or bad and why that property appears irreducible. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/62981/chapter/565378730)

A fair engagement would ask:

- What does intrinsic phenomenal badness explain that an evaluative representation plus introspective misrepresentation does not?
- What observations would differ between the Map’s account and the illusionist account?
- Does the Map’s argument rely on introspection being metaphysically transparent?
- Why should the apparent impossibility of mistake about painfulness survive known errors about the structure, cause and object of affective experience?
- Can illusionism preserve suffering-related ethics through aversive interests, preferences or representational harm, or does it collapse moral concern?

Merely responding that an illusion requires someone to be deceived, or that illusionists still take painkillers, would not answer the theory. The former assumes the disputed subject and phenomenality; the latter is fully predicted by an account built around aversive control and self-protection.

### Animal sentience

The article and its neighbour need engagement with:

- the eight-criterion precautionary framework;
- taxon-specific differences within decapods and cephalopods;
- criticisms of motivational trade-offs as standalone evidence;
- distinctions between nociception, pain-like valuation and conscious pain;
- uncertainty about whether the same neural or computational indicators transfer across radically different architectures;
- the possibility of conscious but non-valenced animals;
- the possibility of valence-like control without consciousness.

The New York Declaration’s “realistic possibility” language is appropriately precautionary, not a declaration that all named taxa have been shown to be conscious. The Map should retain that evidential calibration when translating the literature into its own coupling framework. [Google Sites+1](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration)

## 4. Does the article protect the site’s tenets?

### Dualism

The page is less protective than many neighbouring articles. It explicitly says that defeat of the hedonic theory would not defeat dualism, and it limits its conclusion to an open explanatory seam rather than claiming a proof of irreducibility. [[valence]]

The protection occurs at a deeper level: the article treats intrinsic felt badness as an explanatorily independent datum and requires rivals to bridge to it, while not seriously considering accounts on which that appearance is constructed, relational or illusory. The dualist conclusion is therefore partly secured through choice of explanandum.

### Bidirectional interaction

Calibration here is comparatively good. The article says that asymbolia pressures simple epiphenomenalism but does not distinguish phenomenal causation from a common neural cause. It also acknowledges that a phenomenal-concept version of epiphenomenalism remains available. [[valence]]

The remaining slippage is verbal: phrases such as “what the behavior tracks” and “when the felt badness goes, normal avoidance goes” encourage a causal reading immediately after conceding that the data do not establish it.

### Minimal quantum interaction

The article appropriately marks this as speculative, peripheral and lacking independent evidence. There is no serious tenet-protective overstatement in its explicit confidence labelling. The problem is instead that the proposed selection criterion inherits unexamined scalar-currency and teleological assumptions. [[valence]]

### Rejection of Many-Worlds

The article supplies no substantive argument for the no-Many-Worlds tenet. Valence might have implications for branch-relative value, identity or selection elsewhere on the site, but nothing in this article’s evidence discriminates collapse, hidden-variable, Many-Worlds or other quantum interpretations.

The introductory claim that valence supports the Map “at multiple points” should not be allowed to imply support for all five tenets.

### Limits of Occam’s razor

The article’s symmetry rule is methodologically sound: parsimony should not be used as an automatic physicalist trump, but neither should the Map immunise its own additional ontology from evidential cost. [[valence]]

What remains missing is a positive model-comparison standard. Once simplicity is denied decisive force, the article needs to say what would count in favour of intrinsic phenomenal valence over a complete functional, representational or illusionist account: novel prediction, improved unification, intervention sensitivity, independent evidence for coupling, or some other discriminator.

**Overall tenet verdict:** the host page is moderately self-critical, but the surrounding cluster repeatedly restores stronger conclusions than the host earns. The result is **partial self-correction combined with asymmetric adversarial pressure**.

## 5. Integration with neighbouring articles

### *Pain Asymbolia*

This is the clearest propagation failure. The neighbour’s 24 November revision now says that the condition is classificatorily contested, neurologically impure and compatible with predictive-processing decomposition. The 17 November valence page still uses the cleaner “intact processing, absent badness” formulation as a load-bearing example. [[pain-asymbolia]]

### *The Valence Void*

The host page says the explanatory gap is open but does not demonstrate that reduction must fail. The void page says valence “resists explanation from every direction,” calls introspection structurally circular and suggests that the problem may be intrinsically irresolvable. It also says that every moment of experience and every attempt at understanding is already valenced. [[valence-void]]

Those are materially stronger claims. They risk self-sealing: any successful explanation can be rejected because the cognition evaluating it is itself said to contain valence. The page should be presented as a speculative epistemic-boundary hypothesis, not as an established void.

### *Emotion and Dualism*

Its opening declares emotional consciousness “the strongest empirical case for interactionist dualism” and says intrinsic affective properties “resist every attempt at functional reduction.” Its next sentences then concede that asymbolia constrains identity claims without evidencing bidirectional interaction. [[emotion-and-dualism]]

The opening outruns the body. “No currently accepted reduction commands consensus” would be defensible; “resist every attempt” suggests failed demonstration rather than continuing controversy.

### *Valence as a Natural Kind*

This page is better integrated with current objections than the host. It recognises polysemy, fragmentation, dynamic coding and the possibility that macro-valence is an integration for choice rather than one consciously consulted quantity. It explicitly says fragmentation threatens scalar currency but not the existence of felt qualities. [[valence-as-a-natural-kind]]

The host links to it but does not absorb its main constraint. It continues to call valence the common dimension that enables commensuration and later uses it as the proposed selection currency.

### *Phenomenal Value Realism*

The neighbour’s value pluralism recognises agency, meaning, understanding and aesthetic character as independent dimensions of phenomenal value. That conflicts with the host’s assertion that without valence there is nothing for moral concern to be about. [[phenomenal-value-realism]]

The site must choose among:

- hedonic exclusivism;
- affective sentientism as a sufficient but not necessary account of status;
- wider phenomenal-value pluralism.

It currently states all three in different places.

### *Valence and the Mechanism of Conscious Selection*

This article is admirably cautious about dopamine, prediction error, common-currency neural signals and phenomenality. It says that ordinary decision models can explain valence-sensitive behaviour without deciding whether the relevant scalar is felt. [[valence-and-conscious-selection]]

That caution should flow back into the concept page. At present, the host treats scalar valence as more phenomenologically unified than its specialist neighbour allows.

### *Predictive Processing and Dualism*

The linked article recognises active inference as a direct rival and discusses a contemporary recursive or “beautiful loop” account of consciousness. But the valence page’s specific nociceptive/asymbolia model is not supplied by that article or by Hesp. The cross-link therefore gives the appearance of support without identifying the actual source of the pain-specific synthesis. [[predictive-processing-and-dualism]]

### *Ethics of Consciousness and the Invertebrate Question*

The neighbour usefully distinguishes moral agents from moral patients and separates threshold status from graded stakes. But it overstates what follows from dualism, treats the Map’s coupling threshold as though it were a generic dualist consequence, and assumes that a conscious but valence-free being has no welfare before fully addressing alternative accounts of interest or status. [[ethics-of-consciousness-invertebrate-question]]

## 6. Staleness against the 2020s literature

The references are not generally obsolete, but the **empirical balance is stale**.

Panksepp 1998, Russell and Barrett 1999, Klein 2007 and the 2011–2014 pain debate remain legitimate foundational sources. The problem is that several carry conclusions that now require current companion sources.

The most important missing or under-integrated work includes:

- Walle and Dukes’s 2023 critique of valence as an ill-defined and poorly operationalised construct; [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37744985/)

- the 2024 cross-modal decoding work distinguishing valence from intensity; [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38857402/)
- recent reviews distinguishing reward common-currency evidence from the weaker and methodologically harder punishment literature; [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39418537/)
- current work complicating binary positive/negative circuit pictures; [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763425001253)
- Kammerer’s 2026 illusionist treatment of apparent valenced phenomenology; [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/62981/chapter/565378730)
- the final 2026 Smithies publication and the continuing Vulcan dispute; [DOI+1](https://doi.org/10.1093/9780198994121.003.0011)

The article is therefore current in some philosophy-of-pain references but not current enough in affective neuroscience, illusionism or comparative sentience.

# Concrete potential improvements

## Changes to concepts/valence

3. **Distinguish four uses of “valence.”** Define separately phenomenal tone, affective appraisal, neural value coding and decision-theoretic currency. Do not infer one from another without argument.
4. **Downgrade the scalar claim.** Replace “the common evaluative dimension” with language allowing bipolar, bivariate, mixed and multidimensional models. Make scalar phenomenal currency a disputed hypothesis rather than part of the introductory definition.

## Changes to site methodology

19. **Introduce a claim ledger.** For each consequential sentence, record: exact source claim, Map inference, confidence, empirical or philosophical status, and date last checked. This would have exposed the unsupported jump from Hesp’s general model to a nociceptive asymbolia prediction.
20. **Build dependency-aware propagation.** Canonical pages such as *Pain Asymbolia* should declare dependent pages. A material correction should automatically create review tasks for every page using the corrected proposition. The changelog already recognises non-propagation as a recurrent failure.
21. **Separate three layers in every argument.** Visually label:

    - empirical evidence;
    - metaphysical interpretation;
    - framework-internal consequence.
        The present prose frequently moves from conserved circuitry to conscious valence, and from conscious valence to quantum selection, without making those transitions sufficiently visible.
22. **Adopt a rival-parity rule.** A tenet-supporting section should include the strongest identifiable contemporary rival in its own vocabulary, not merely a generic objection. For this page, that means higher-order affective neuroscience, constructionism, full functional role accounts and 2026 illusionism.
23. **Require a discriminator for “explanatory gap” claims.** When the Map says a functional account leaves something unexplained, it should state whether the disagreement is empirical, conceptual, introspective or metaphysical, and what could count against the Map’s interpretation.
24. **Add recency companions for foundational sources.** Any load-bearing empirical source more than roughly ten years old should be paired with a recent review or adversarial update. Foundational works should remain, but not carry the current state of a contested field alone.
25. **Automate quotation and metadata checks.** Exact-string or normalised-string matching could verify quotations; Crossref and publisher metadata could detect “forthcoming” items that have appeared and fill missing DOI, pages and article numbers.
26. **Version external-review inputs.** Record the page timestamp or commit hash supplied to every reviewer. The changelog’s recent stale-copy incident shows that live-versus-indexed provenance must be part of the audit record.
27. **Audit summary text separately from article bodies.** Several current problems occur in leads, cards and short descriptions that state stronger conclusions than the qualified body. Treat those summaries as independent claims requiring source and calibration checks.
28. **Maintain a cross-page contradiction register.** The present register should include at least: valence necessity versus phenomenal-value pluralism; scalar currency versus fragmented valence; cautious host-page agnosticism versus the valence void’s structural impossibility; and dualism generally versus the Map’s specific binary-coupling model.
