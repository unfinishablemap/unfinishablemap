## Executive Summary

The article's second thesis is in its description ("a rival the Map rejects, a cost it avoids."). It says the Map *structurally* avoids the de-combination problem because "there is no single cosmic conscious field". The Map's own two-layer interface model is "intentionally agnostic" on exactly that question. It leaves Huxley's Mind at Large open as the "Source/Field" that brains filter, and `concepts/altered-states-of-consciousness` uses Mind at Large in the Map's own voice. On that branch the Map holds the filtering view the article lists as a cosmopsychist reply and judges at L87 to explain nothing. So the avoidance is conditional, and the article credits it to the Dualism tenet, which says nothing about a cosmic subject. There are two further seams. L117 and L85 were never brought into line with L87's concession and verdict. And the article calls the Map closed-individualist on a synchronic definition while never engaging empty individualism, the third Kolak position, which the Map's own tenets page concedes threatens the indexical argument.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would say the whole closed / empty / open taxonomy is a dispute over how to extend the folk word "I" to cases (fission, cosmic minds) that the folk never met. The article's three-difference section (L95-99) treats "subject" as a kind that both rivals share. That is bedrock and is not tasked. One sharper point does land. L113 already concedes that neural theories (IIT exclusion, workspace broadcast) supply boundary accounts where the Map "has a void". After that concession, the claim in L111 that the Map wins on de-combination carries the article's comparative weight, and Issue 1 shows that claim is conditional.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would say L107's own concession ("the Map has a posit rather than an argument") settles the identity dispute in his favour by default. That is bedrock (stability note 5). The new point is about the sibling page. `topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-individuation` L67 glosses the Parfit-associated empty individualism as "Subject boundaries are conventional, not metaphysical. The self is a useful fiction." That is nearer a narrative-centre account of selves than anything the target's own corrected Parfit bullet (L56) allows. Two Map pages now define one Kolak category in different ways (Issue 4).

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark would object to L117: "The interaction problem is at least *empirically locatable*—it concerns a locus (quantum indeterminacy) where physics leaves outcomes undetermined". Indeterminacy of outcomes depends on the interpretation: Bohmian mechanics is deterministic, and the article does not say the Map rejects it. No neural locus has been located. One has been hypothesised, under a decoherence budget the article never mentions. "Locatable" claims more than "posited". The L117 scoping sentence was swept on 2026-09-08 (cc77b347af), so this goes to the Language table, not to a task.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch would point out that the natural Everettian account of personal identity across branching is close to *empty* individualism: momentary subjects, no further fact about which successor "I" become. The article treats open individualism as the only identity thesis that conflicts with Tenet 4. Yet tenets.md L123 concedes: "Were the subject deflated, fragmented, or treated as a mere process, the indexical objection would weaken correspondingly". The question "why am I *this* branch?" needs a pre-branch subject that *persists into* one branch. L107's synchronic definition of the Map's closed individualism does not supply one (Issue 3).

### The Empiricist
A Popperian would note that the article honestly calls the identity question tenet-driven. Its single claim to an *empirical* advantage is L117's contrast: the interaction problem is "locatable", while de-combination is "conceptual … about whether subject-subject parthood is even intelligible". That contrast is drawn against the form of the rival problem that L87 has already conceded away. Once parthood is dropped, both sides owe an unexplained relation, and no observation separates them (Issue 2).

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would say that the haecceity the Map needs is the reified self. The Madhyamaka view sits nearest empty individualism, which this article (whose subject *is* the identity taxonomy) never engages after the taxonomy bullet. `concepts/buddhism-and-dualism` L163 already records this as bedrock, and the article could cite that for free.
