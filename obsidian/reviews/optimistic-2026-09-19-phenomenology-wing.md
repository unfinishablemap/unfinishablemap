## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

[[phenomenal-depth]] converts depth into an explanandum-multiplier rather than a new mystery: "The gap is not merely between neural activity and the *existence* of experience but between neural activity and experience's *internal structure*." That is the explanatory gap widened without being restated. [[phenomenal-overflow]] gives the gap a capacity argument alongside the conceivability ones and is careful to say what each does: zombies show "function without phenomenology is conceivable", inversion that "phenomenology can vary while function stays constant", overflow that "phenomenology can exceed function in capacity". Three distinct shapes, not three restatements — Chalmers would take the taxonomy.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

[[phenomenal-overflow]]'s Bidirectional Interaction section is the wing's only quantum contact and it is correctly sequenced: the mechanism is offered, then explicitly made dispensable. "[[stapp-quantum-mind|Stapp's quantum Zeno model]] proposes a specific mechanism: sustained attention (rapid repeated observations) could bias quantum indeterminacies toward particular neural patterns ... Whether or not this specific mechanism is correct, the structural point remains." Stapp gets his model named and the Map's argument does not become hostage to it. The stage/selection/access picture — "the rich phenomenal field is the stage; attention is conscious selection; access is the downstream result" — is the cleanest statement on the Map of where MQI would have to act.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

[[phenomenal-transparency-opacity-spectrum]] closes with Nagel and earns him: "attempts to objectify one's own representational process generate new transparent representations rather than revealing the process. The bat's sonar is transparent to the bat; our vision is transparent to us; neither can access the other's transparency because access would require the very medium it seeks to examine." That turns *What Is It Like to Be a Bat?* from a thought experiment into an architectural claim about why the perspective cannot be stepped out of. [[phenomenal-depth]]'s three-modality tour (perceptual / affective / cognitive), with pain asymbolia as the dissociation showing the affective layer is separable, keeps first-person structure as the thing being described rather than the thing being explained away.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Depth's gradient — "depth is not an all-or-nothing property but something that varies with the complexity and integration of conscious states" — is process-friendly in exactly the permitted way, and **the article keeps it there**. Its panpsychism sentence is conditional and says so: "*if* even very simple organisms have some phenomenal depth, this lends partial support to [[panpsychism|panpsychist]] intuitions, though the Map does not commit to panpsychism as a general thesis." The spectrum's opacity cases (blur, depersonalisation, lucidity, the painter's squint) treat experience as an ongoing construction rather than a substance inspecting itself — prehension rather than Cartesian survey. **Constraint honoured; nothing to flag.** See Birch below for the one place where the two personas converge on the same passage, which is this review's test for honest calibration.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

The spectrum's Bidirectional Interaction paragraph gives Kane something better than an assertion of agency: a case where consciousness demonstrably acts on its own apparatus. "If sustained contemplative attention can shift representations from transparent toward opaque ... then consciousness is doing causal work on its own representational apparatus — suggesting something beyond mere feedback loops within a physical system." And the "Attentional restructuring" case — the painter squinting at a white wall under candlelight "until it yields its actual orange cast" — is voluntary, repeatable, and available to anyone, which is more than most agency arguments on the Map can offer. Kane would note the hedge ("suggesting", not "showing") and approve of it.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

Depth supplies mysterianism with a mechanism rather than a mood: "consciousness may have more internal structure than any mind can fully articulate about itself." The spectrum's Epistemic Limits of Opacity section is the wing's most disciplined piece of humility, and it is humility about the Map's *own* preferred route: "What opacity reveals is *that* a representation is a representation. It does not reveal *how* the representation is generated ... Opacity grants awareness of the representational status without access to the representational process." Contemplative opacity is the Map's most-invoked evidential channel, and here the article caps what that channel can deliver before anyone asks it to deliver more. [[cognitive-phenomenology]]'s "Three faces of irresolvability" does the same for its own dialectic and names the limitation as structural rather than temporary.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This persona's verdict is favourable, and in two places it is the review's headline.** No article in this wing misuses the five-tier vocabulary, and no tenet-load is converted into evidential weight.

**Praised restraint 1 — [[phenomenal-overflow]] declines a favourable finding.** Nartker et al. (2025) found that inattentionally blind participants report location, colour and shape of stimuli they deny noticing. A weaker article would have banked this as overflow confirmed. This one does not: "This result is double-edged for overflow ... because those features can be reported when cued, the finding arguably shows degraded *access* rather than the phenomenology *outside* access that Block's thesis needs. The authors, skeptical of rich unconscious perception, present it as constraining the overflow debate more than settling it." **Verified at source this run** (OpenAlex, eLife 13:RP100337, DOI 10.7554/eLife.100337): authors Makaela Nartker, Chaz Firestone, Howard E. Egeth, Ian Phillips — surnames, order, venue and volume all correct; the abstract states participants "can successfully report the location, color, and shape of stimuli they deny noticing" and that the results "challenge the use of inattentional blindness to argue that awareness requires attention." The article's gloss of the authors' own reading as *residual conscious vision* is an interpretation rather than a quotation and is presented as such. This is the wing's best single moment: a result that looks like a win, correctly declined as a constraint.

The same section closes the loop honestly on unfalsifiability, both ways: "This unfalsifiability is generally a mark against a hypothesis—yet it's also exactly what we'd expect if phenomenology genuinely transcends function. The situation is frustrating rather than decisive: unfalsifiability doesn't confirm overflow, but neither does it refute it." And the article states its own tier without being asked: overflow is presented "as evidence for the richness of phenomenology beyond function, not as established fact", with a secure/contested split immediately below it.

**Praised restraint 2 — [[phenomenal-transparency-opacity-spectrum]] retracts its own argument mid-paragraph.** The Dualism tenet paragraph begins with the invariance argument (phenomenal character persists while representational status changes, so they are not identical), then dismantles it in its own voice: "The opacity transition is itself a functional change ... And the feel itself changes across the transition — blur is not sharp sight, lucid is not non-lucid dreaming. So this is not an invariance of phenomenal character under a transformation that leaves it untouched. The defensible claim is narrower: representational status changes while *some* phenomenal character persists, in altered form, across the change." It then prices what survives: "The Map reads the persistence as evidence for dualism, but this is a burden-shift to the functionalist, not a deduction from the data: it is consistent with dualism and costly for functionalism, not a proof that closes the question." A tenet section that argues itself down from a deduction to a burden-shift is rare anywhere and I have not seen a cleaner instance on the Map. **Whitehead and Birch converge here**: the process-friendly reading of opacity is praised, and it is explicitly not allowed to do evidential work.

**Praised restraint 3 — [[phenomenal-transparency-opacity-spectrum]] demands a discriminating marker for its own contemplative evidence.** "The claim risks unfalsifiability — absence of reported opacity can always be ascribed to insufficient training, and its presence read as confirmation — so it needs a discriminating marker: genuine opacity, unlike a mere trained belief that perception is constructed, should predict measurable changes in perceptual processing or report (for instance, altered susceptibility to constancy illusions or to the constructive corrections that are normally undetectable), and the honest position is that whether trained reports track such a marker rather than expectation remains an open empirical question." This is the Map's best-formulated contemplative-evidence claim. It is also the thing an open task is about to reinvent — see Addendum 2.

**Praised restraint 4 — [[cognitive-phenomenology]] lets contemplative evidence cut against it.** The Buddhist formless-attainments passage is the wing's most self-critical moment: the higher jhānas are "a *progressive deconstruction* of phenomenal furniture ... The trajectory is dismantlability, not constitutive necessity. This is genuine pressure on the constitutive form of liberalism." The article then narrows its own thesis in response rather than explaining the pressure away: "Qualified liberalism is consistent with this narrower reading: scalar phenomenal character in ordinary cognition, dismantlable under contemplative discipline, with the constitutive claim restricted to ordinary range." Every liberal argument in the article carries its conservative re-description in the same breath (recursion's "click" becomes "a high-confidence transition in a precision-weighted predictive coder"); PCT is labelled "an abductive bet" with downstream applications marked "conditional on PCT"; the Methodological Asymmetry section concedes the functionalist reply outright — "conceded" — and then claims only consistency, "a defence of consistency, not a positive case for phenomenal trust on its own." **Verified at source this run**: the Fürst quotation in the "dialectical face" — "introspective judgments about phenomenology are regimented by the view initially held" — is verbatim from the abstract of Fürst, M. (2017), *On the Limits of the Method of Phenomenal Contrast*, Journal of the American Philosophical Association 3(2) (OpenAlex abstract reconstruction), and the article's framing of the method as "dialectically ineffective" matches the paper's own "dialectical ineffectiveness". No over-attribution.
