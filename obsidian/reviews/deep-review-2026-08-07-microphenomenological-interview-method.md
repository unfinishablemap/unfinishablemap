## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found and Fixed

Six defects, all verified at primary text. Five are source-fidelity errors that four "verified" reviews passed because they lived inside cites whose *metadata* was correct.

- **1. Direction-inverted source verdict — "Can we trust the phenomenological interview?" (CRITICAL — fixed).** The article placed this paper in §Documented Limits and reported that it "catalogues the objections in detail and finds the method's responses partial." The paper is a **defence**. Høffding, Martiny & Roepstorff (2022) conclude that phenomenological interviews are "in no principled way less reliable or less valid than quantitative or experimental methods," and that a critic must either grant the method its grounds or adopt a scepticism corrosive to all consciousness science. The article conscripted a friendly source as a hostile one. Fixed: the authors are now named, their actual verdict reported, and the Map's use of them narrowed explicitly to the catalogue rather than the verdict. The reference was also **anonymous** (no authors, wrong year/volume) — corrected to Høffding, S., Martiny, K. M., & Roepstorff, A. (2022), *Phen. Cog. Sci.* 21(1), 33–51.

- **2. "15-stage procedure" attributed to the wrong paper (CRITICAL — fixed).** The article credited Petitmengin, Remillieux & Valenzuela-Moguillansky (2019), *Phen. Cog. Sci.* 18(4):691–730, with a "15-stage procedure." Primary text (`pdftotext` of the author's own PDF) shows **no stage count anywhere**: six top-level sections, and the only "15"s in the file are Fig. 15, a page cite and a footnote marker. The fifteen stages belong to **Valenzuela-Moguillansky & Vásquez-Rosati (2019)**, *Constructivist Foundations* 14(2):123–145 — abstract: "It has fifteen stages organized into five sections." A partial-author-overlap conflation: Valenzuela-Moguillansky is on both papers, both 2019, both about micro-phenomenological analysis. Fixed by splitting the attribution; the Constructivist Foundations paper added as ref 11a.

- **3. "Three reliability levels" — wrong count and wrong content (CRITICAL — fixed).** The article claimed convergence "at three reliability levels: intra-subject consistency, inter-subject structural recurrence, inter-analyst agreement." §5.4 of the source specifies **four modes** of assessing *performative consistency*: confirmation/invalidation by **iteration**, by **partition**, by **neurophenomenological triangulation** with neural measures (Lutz 2002), and by **inter-researcher** confirmation. The article's "intra-subject consistency (the same subject re-interviewed gives the same structure)" is not in the source, and the omitted neurophenomenological mode is the one most relevant to the Map — the article's own Confabulation-Resistance Discipline section already relies on exactly that Lutz-2002 co-variation test, so the article had the right material and mis-summarised the taxonomy. Fixed to the source's four modes.

- **4. Fabricated clinical outcome in the epilepsy case (CRITICAL — fixed; the most serious).** The article stated that patients "who learned to recognise their own prodromes could take counter-measures (relaxation, anti-convulsant adjustments, withdrawal from triggers) and, **in the clinical follow-up, reduced seizure frequency non-pharmacologically**," and rested its headline claim on the prodromes being "**clinically actionable**." Primary text of Petitmengin, Baulac & Navarro (2006):
  - There is **no clinical follow-up and no seizure-frequency outcome**. The authors write: "we cannot give a precise evaluation of the success of these countermeasures. Nevertheless, we gathered some testimonials that reflect the **beliefs of the patients** about the effectiveness of their countermeasures." They further endorse Spatt et al.'s warning that "self-reporting of seizure abortion does not necessarily demonstrate the efficiency of these countermeasures, as auras frequently stop spontaneously," and raise placebo/indirect-effect caveats.
  - The abstract's own verdict is that "**Prodromes are not specific enough for clinical use**, but could refine the behavioral strategies used in the treatment of epilepsy" — a direct contradiction of "clinically actionable."
  - Counter-measures were **spontaneously developed by patients** (n=6 avoid facilitating factors, n=1 prevent, n=5 interrupt), not taught by the interview. "**Anti-convulsant adjustments**" appears nowhere; the documented measures are neutralising/avoiding a facilitating factor, motor activity, rubbing, regular breathing, focusing, internal monologue, conversation.
  - "Working in Varela's Salpêtrière team" was loose: Petitmengin's affiliations were GET/INT and CREA; Baulac and Navarro were the Pitié-Salpêtrière clinicians; the paper is dedicated to Varela's memory (d. 2001).
  Fixed: the paragraph now reports the study's actual design (9 aura patients, 6 with prodromes), the spontaneous counter-measures, the authors' own refusal to evaluate efficacy, and their "not specific enough for clinical use" verdict — and re-bases the demonstration on *ampliative access to otherwise-inaccessible content* rather than on a clinical benefit that was never measured.

- **5. Attributed concession Sparby et al. did not make (CRITICAL — fixed).** The article had Sparby "concede that interviewer-mediation provides a reliability contribution that self-inquiry forgoes." The paper reports the training as workable and concludes self-inquiry "can potentially be employed successfully in a clinical setting with initially untrained subjects"; its stated trade-off is that bracketing interpretation buys depth at the cost of overlooking overarching dimensions of meaning. Fixed to the paper's actual limitation, with the interviewer question marked contested rather than conceded. Ref 14 also had **three missing co-authors** — corrected to Sparby, T., Leass, M., Weger, U. W., & Edelhäuser, F. (2023), *Scand. J. Psychol.* 64(1), 60–70.

- **6. Reference 15 — entirely wrong authors (CRITICAL — fixed).** "The lived experience of remembering a 'good' interview: Micro-phenomenology applied to itself" (2022) was credited to Valenzuela-Moguillansky & Vásquez-Rosati. It is by **Heimann, K., Boelsbjerg, H. B., Allen, C. T., van Beek, M., Suhr, C., Lübbert, A., & Petitmengin, C.**, *Phen. Cog. Sci.* 22(1), 217–245. Same root cause as defect 2: Valenzuela-Moguillansky & Vásquez-Rosati's real joint paper is the 2019 Constructivist Foundations procedure paper, and their identity had been smeared across two separate reference entries.
