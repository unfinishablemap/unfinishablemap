## Executive Summary

The page is carefully sourced. Its creation-day deep review re-checked 28 quotations and figures at source, and I found no quotation defect. The weaknesses are in how the page connects evidence to claim, and they sit in the lead. (1) The lead promises "a plain way round" (test yourself later), but the page's own Persistence section shows that a later test, on its own, did not correct the misjudgement. What worked for most participants was results kept apart by method, plus an explanation. The next sentence, "Delay also improves the judgement itself", attaches an item-level result to the method-level judgement the page is about. (2) The page gives felt ease as the only cause. Its main persistence source, Yan, Bjork and Bjork (2016), says in its abstract that three influences combine, one of them "pre-existing beliefs learners bring to new tasks", and the page never mentions it. (3) Two sentences that frame the evidence overstate it. The section frame says the judgement follows "the performance side", which is false for the classroom study by the page's own definition. The lead's "three studied arrangements (spacing, interleaving and self-testing)" counts one painting manipulation twice. (4) One outbound crosslink misapplies the definition of the cross-state void. All four fit one refine-draft pass at about +80 words, with 554 words of headroom.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would accept every datum and question the label. A judgement of learning that tracks processing fluency is a cue-validity problem in a predictive system. Calling it a "void" sets an ordinary calibration error beside the hard problem. The page half concedes this. It calls itself "an occluded limit of a practical sort" and "unexplorable only in the time-bound sense". But the canonical [[three-kinds-of-void]] defines the Occluded as thought "blocked *on purpose*" (L69), or as access blocked by "the inescapable conditioning of the only available channel" (L91). The learning void has a second channel, the later test, which the page itself names. On the taxonomy's own terms it is neither occluded nor unexplorable. It is a known bias with a known fix. That is a classification question, not a defect in the evidence (see Counterarguments).

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett would welcome the page and then press it. It grants that "Cue-utilisation accounts of metamemory, on which such judgements are inferences from available cues such as fluency, predict this". That is a local case of the inferential model of self-knowledge, the model illusionists generalise. A confident first-person verdict ("I can tell when I am learning") turns out to be built from cues and not read off. The Dualism paragraph says the datum "does not discriminate between dualism and physicalism". Dennett would say it does lean one way. Each such case lowers the prior that any introspective verdict is a direct reading, and the page does not say why its local concession stops short of the self-reports the Map relies on elsewhere. He would also note that the page's own account of the cue is incomplete. Yan et al. found that a belief brought to the task, a folk theory, feeds the judgement, which is more grist for the inferential model (Issue 2).

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark has nothing to attack. The page says "Minimal Quantum Interaction and No Many Worlds are not engaged", and it is right.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch likewise. Nothing on the page depends on branch indexicality.

### The Empiricist

A Popperian would praise the page's exposure. Its claims are falsifiable group-level comparisons, with samples, n's and limits stated. The attack falls on the lead's remedy claim. "The limit is narrow and has a plain way round: test yourself later" makes a testable prediction: learners who test themselves later stop misjudging. The page's own evidence does not bear that out. Kornell and Bjork's participants rated massing better "even after their own test performance had demonstrated the opposite". Yan et al. report that drawing attention to the link between schedule and performance "had only modest effects" (abstract). Only separated results plus an explanation moved a majority, 75% against 46% without the explanation. The prediction that fits the page's evidence is narrower than the one the lead states (Issue 1).

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would recognise the finding as a textbook *vipallāsa*: taking the passing ease of a practice session as a sign of something lasting. He would say the remedy works at the conventional level only, since it swaps one sign (fluency) for another (a test score) and still keeps a learner-self who "has learned". The page claims no metaphysics of the self, so the critique has little purchase. It would apply to the self-description "I can tell when I am learning" that the Occam's Razor paragraph uses, but that paragraph already treats the phrase as a simplification.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The remedy is stated without the condition the page's evidence requires
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/learning-void.md`
- **Location**: lead, second paragraph (about L35): "The limit is narrow and has a plain way round: test yourself later, with the answer out of sight. Delay also improves the judgement itself." Also the description ("Human+AI mapping of a narrow limit with a known way round: a later test.") and `voids/voids` about L231 ("with a known way round in a later test").
- **Problem**: (a) The page splits the limit into three parts and makes persistence the third (L39-45). Persistence is the failure of exactly this way round. Kornell and Bjork's participants rated massing better "even after their own test performance had demonstrated the opposite" (L51, quoted from their abstract). Yan et al.'s abstract (PubMed 27227415, read this run) says theory-based information and "explicitly drawing attention to the link between study schedule and subsequent performance" both "had only modest effects". Only when test performance on the two schedules was separated (Experiment 6), and an explanation added, did a majority come round. The page's own Remedy section states the needed condition ("Keeping predictions and later results side by side, sorted by study method"), but the lead and the two propagated summaries leave it out. (b) "Delay also improves the judgement itself": the antecedent is the judgement the paragraph is about, the judgement of which method works better. The support is Rhodes and Tauber's delayed-JOL effect, which the page itself scopes to item-by-item sorting. The page's own headline case runs against that reading for method judgements: Kornell and Bjork's ratings came after the test and still favoured massing. The deep review of 2026-11-17 separated the three claims (delayed judgement, test experience, later self-test) and asked that later reviews keep them apart (Stability Notes). This sentence still joins two of them through "the judgement itself". Today's phenomenology-of-forgetting deep review fixed the same slide in a host sentence.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Lead (a draft): "The limit is narrow and has a known way round: test yourself later, with the answer out of sight, and keep each method's results apart. Delay also sharpens item-by-item judgements: a meta-analysis found … missed." Name the scope ("a result about items, not about which method works better") in a clause. Description and `voids/voids` line: add "scored method by method" or similar. `topics/consciousness-and-skill-acquisition` L96 ("which later self-testing can check") is accurate, since checking is not correcting. Leave it.

### Issue 2: Yan et al.'s pre-existing-belief factor is absent
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/learning-void.md`
- **Location**: lead first paragraph ("The judgement people make during practice follows how easy the practice feels."); Persistence and Its Exit (L61-63).
- **Problem**: The page's only causal account of the judgement is felt ease, together with the substituted cue in "What the Learner Has to Go On". Yan, Bjork and Bjork (2016), the page's source for persistence and its exit, hypothesise in their abstract that "learners come to the experimental task with a pre-existing belief that blocking instruction by topic is superior to intermixing topics". They conclude that "3 influences combine to make altering learners' misconceptions difficult: the sense of fluency that can accompany nonoptimal modes of instruction; pre-existing beliefs learners bring to new tasks; and the willingness, even eagerness, to believe that 1 is unique as a learner" (PubMed efetch, PMID 27227415, read this run). The page reports the third influence (the 78% who placed themselves in the 10%) and never mentions the second. Searches of the body: "a priori" 1 hit, "belief" 2 hits, "theor" 1 hit, and every one is in the reference titles (L101, L102, L110). Positive control: "fluen" has 7 hits. The omission matters for a voids entry. A misjudgement partly driven by a folk theory brought to the task is partly about belief, and the page offers the void as a limit on what *experience* during practice can witness. The Map's reading in the Dualism paragraph ("experience during practice is a poor witness to change in one's own capacities") survives, because the fluency part stands, but the factual account must name both.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: One sentence in Persistence and Its Exit giving the authors' three influences, attributed to them. Optionally, qualify the lead sentence ("… follows how easy the practice feels and, in the interleaving studies, a belief brought to the task that blocking is better"). Leave the tenet section unchanged; its grade is settled. A wider literature disputes the experience-based reading. Kornell et al. (2011) credit the font-size effect to an ease-of-processing heuristic, and Mueller, Dunlosky, Tauber and Rhodes (2014, *JML* 70:1-12, DOI 10.1016/j.jml.2013.09.007), titled "Does it exemplify fluency effects or reflect people's beliefs about memory?", argue for beliefs. I could not obtain that paper's abstract or text, so it is a **LEAD**: do not cite it unless it is read.

### Issue 3: Two sentences that frame the evidence claim more than the cases show
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/learning-void.md`
- **Location**: (a) Where Feeling and Outcome Part, L49: "Four results show the learner's judgement following the performance side." (b) Lead, L33: "Under three studied arrangements (spacing, interleaving and self-testing) that judgement has been shown to favour the method that served the learner worse." Also `voids/voids` L231 "(spacing, interleaving, self-testing)".
- **Problem**: (a) The page's own definition (L33) separates "*performance*, which can be watched during practice" from "*learning*, the change that shows later in retention and transfer". In the classroom study, "The test came at the end of the same class" and "shows nothing about retention" (L57), so both measures sit on the performance side. Feeling of learning went *against* the immediate score. The judgement followed ease (the polished lecture) against measured performance, which supports the page's actual thesis ("follows how easy the practice feels") and contradicts the frame sentence. Soderstrom and Bjork's abstract (PubMed 25910388, read this run) defines learning as "relatively permanent changes … that will support long-term retention and transfer", so the classroom score does not measure learning on the source's terms either. (b) The body's only case for "spacing" is Kornell and Bjork (2008), which the page itself describes as "massed for some artists and interleaved for others" (L51). Yan et al. call the same manipulation interleaving against blocking. The lead therefore counts one manipulation under two names, and the body's fourth case (active against lecture instruction) is outside the list of three. The voids index repeats the count. A study of spacing alone with judgements exists in the literature. Kornell (2009), "Optimising learning using flashcards: Spacing is more effective than cramming", *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 23:1297-1317, DOI 10.1002/acp.1537, checked at Crossref for metadata only. Its findings are a **LEAD**, not read.
- **Severity**: Low-Medium
- **Recommendation**: (a) "Four results show the learner's judgement following ease rather than the outcome measured" (net zero to +2). (b) Either name what the body shows ("Under interleaving (called spacing in the painting studies) and self-testing, and in one classroom comparison of active and lecture teaching, …"), or read and cite a study of spacing alone, at source, before keeping "spacing". Propagate to the `voids/voids` line.

## Counterarguments to Address

### Is it a void at all?
- **Current content says**: "an occluded limit of a practical sort, with a known mechanism and a known way round, and it is unexplorable only in the time-bound sense that later retention is not yet there to inspect."
- **A critic would argue**: By [[three-kinds-of-void]] (Occluded: "blocked *on purpose*", L69, or blocked by "the inescapable conditioning of the only available channel", L91; Unexplorable: cognitive closure, L53), a limit with a second channel and a known fix is neither. Admitting it opens the catalogue to the whole heuristics-and-biases literature.
- **Suggested response**: The qualifiers already do most of the work. A refine pass could add that the entry is kept as a *control case*, a limit the catalogue can show to be ordinary, which is how the tenet section already treats it. Not tasked; report only.

### The inferential model of self-knowledge
- **Current content says**: the datum "does not discriminate between dualism and physicalism".
- **A critic would argue**: It is one more case where a confident self-verdict is built from cues, which is the inferential model illusionists generalise, so it lowers the Map's priors on introspective evidence elsewhere.
- **Suggested response**: The page could say in one clause why the local concession does not travel: it concerns predictions about future capacity, not reports of present character. Only if room allows. Other tasks cite the tenet section as a model, so any addition must keep its grade. Not tasked.
