## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Balcerowicz 2020: wrong title, installed by the 2026-07-16 review
  (real-wrong-metadata; corrected).** The 07-16 review replaced a nonexistent
  book title with the chapter title "Anekāntavāda." Balcerowicz's own
  publication list (indologia.uw.edu.pl) shows three entries in *Brill's
  Encyclopedia of Jainism* (2020): "Jainism and Ājīvikas" (277–294), "Jain
  Epistemology" (837–856), "Siddhasena" (905–911). None is titled
  "Anekāntavāda." The research note's source URL (balcerowicz2020c.pdf) is
  "Jain Epistemology." The only web hit confirming the "Anekāntavāda" title was
  this site's own 07-16 review page (self-contamination). Reference corrected to
  "Jain Epistemology," 837–856.
- **Balcerowicz's position inverted (attribution error; corrected).** The
  article said he "renders *anekantavada* as a controlled 'relativity' of
  standpoints." The entry renders it "multiplexity of reality" throughout and
  says, in its section on relativism, that a sentence with its perspective
  supplied "necessarily assumes a definite truth value (i.e. is either true or
  false). Consequently, no relativism of our knowledge is implied in the
  theory." The scare-quoted "relativity" was the research note's gloss, carried
  into the article and ratified by the 07-16 review as "faithful to the real
  chapter" without the chapter being read. Replaced with his rendering and two
  verbatim fragments (both grep-verified in the PDF text).
- **Dharmakirti credited with the wrong objection (attribution error;
  corrected).** The article had "Buddhist logicians such as Dharmakirti" charge
  that the doctrine is self-undermining because it applies to itself.
  Dharmakirti's objection (Pramāṇavārttika-svavṛtti) is the curd-and-camel
  argument: if everything has every nature, distinctions collapse. The
  self-application and indeterminacy charge is Śaṅkara's (Brahmasūtrabhāṣya
  2.2.33). Paragraph rewritten to give each critic his own objection and to
  report Balcerowicz's summary of the traditional charges (contradiction;
  scepticism and relativism) with his verdict and his concession that the theory
  "is not entirely unproblematic" (verbatim, grep-verified).
- **Johnson 1995 flattened (dropped qualifier; corrected).** The article had
  Johnson read Jaina karma theory as making passions "the operative mechanism
  rather than action as such." His thesis is historical: in the earliest texts
  bondage is the inevitable result of activity and volition is not a relevant
  factor; the passions enter later as a systematisation (Umasvati). The SEP
  entry agrees ("in the early Jaina conceptions, in which the concrete acts, not
  the intention, are foremost"). Sentence rewritten to state the change.
- **"*radicalized partial perspectives*" attributed to Gorisse (paraphrase
  dressed as a term; corrected).** "radicalized" does not occur in the SEP
  entry. Her phrase is "the erroneous radicalization of a perspective." Replaced
  with the verbatim fragment and her own account of the error (each school
  refutes the aspects it does not address).

### Medium Issues Found

- **"seven truth-values" → "seven modes of predication."** Gorisse writes "seven
  modes of predication"; Balcerowicz says each completed sentence is bivalent.
  The seven-truth-value reading is one modern reconstruction among several, not
  the doctrine. Also changed "seven-valued scheme" in the Tenet 5 paragraph.
- **"a *fourth* independent classical-Indian tradition" (lead).** "Independent"
  sat against the article's own common-cause paragraph, which says the shared
  method prevents independent confirmation, and against the SEP remark that the
  Jaina dualism is "close to that of Sāṃkhya." Word dropped.
- **Tenet 5 reading overstated the parity of standpoints.** The article said
  partial standpoints are each truth-tracking "without any one being complete."
  Classical Jaina authors held the Jina's standpoint complete and used the
  scheme to subordinate rival systems; the tolerance reading is a
  twentieth-century reinterpretation that, per Balcerowicz, "find[s] no support
  in historical sources." One sentence added to the exposition and one to the
  Tenet 5 paragraph so the precedent claimed is many-sidedness of the object,
  not parity among frameworks. A tenet-accepting reviewer would have flagged the
  earlier wording.
- **"oldest extant systematic Jaina treatise" → "oldest extant Jaina treatise
  in Sanskrit"** (Gorisse's wording), in the lead and the reference entry.
- **Further Reading label** said the interaction survey was "now including Jaina
  karmic matter"; that article only links here. Label reworded.
