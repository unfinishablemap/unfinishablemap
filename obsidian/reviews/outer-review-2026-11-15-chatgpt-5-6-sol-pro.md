# Critical audit: “Personal Identity”

## Overall assessment

The live page is the requested **6 November 2026** version. The changelog confirms that earlier defects were repaired before that date: an invalid inference from imperfect memory preservation was removed, “pastness” was recast as representing rather than detecting an original indexical position, and the site has separately recognised over-reading of the 2025 Santander split-brain study as an outstanding problem. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

**Verdict:** the revised article is markedly more philosophically responsible than its earlier architecture suggests. It repeatedly concedes that:

- phenomenological seeming does not establish haecceity;
- zombie conceivability only opens logical space;
- causal history individuates an already-posited subject rather than producing one;
- Madhyamaka is not defeated on its own terms;
- the fission/Many-Worlds asymmetry is framework-relative;
- the survival implications are conditional.

Those are substantive improvements, not cosmetic hedges. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

Nevertheless, the central positive case still establishes at most that **primitive indexical identity is compatible with the Map’s tenets and phenomenology**. It does not independently establish that such identity exists, persists, or has the specific quantum-causal role assigned to it. The article frequently acknowledges this in its argumentative sections, then quietly promotes the posit into a result in “What Personal Identity Requires,” “The Meaning Connection,” and “Relation to Site Perspective.” Its rival coverage is uneven: Parfit receives a recognisable account, while animalism and narrative identity are compressed almost to caricature, and no-self theories are encountered mainly after the Map’s preferred ontology is already in place.

The bibliography is mostly genuine and correctly formatted. The greater problem is **citation fit**: several sources exist but do not support the precise claim for which they are being used.

## 1. The article needs to distinguish five different identity questions

The opening presents psychological continuity, animalism and narrative identity as three competing answers to one question: what makes a person at one time numerically identical with a person at another. That taxonomy is too coarse.

Contemporary treatments distinguish at least:

1. **Persistence:** what makes a later being numerically identical with an earlier one?
2. **Nature:** what sort of entity are we—organism, person, mind, subject or process?
3. **Characterisation:** which traits, commitments and experiences define who someone is?
4. **Individuation or population:** what makes there be one subject here rather than two?
5. **Practical importance:** what grounds prudential concern, responsibility or survival-related value?

The current SEP treatment stresses that these questions are loosely connected and often confused; it specifically identifies Schechtman’s narrative project with the characterisation question rather than straightforwardly with persistence. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy +1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/Identity-personal/))

This matters because much of the Map’s apparent victory is achieved by switching questions:

- Narrative accounts are criticised for failing to secure numerical persistence, although that is not always their principal target.
- Zahavian minimal selfhood concerns the synchronic first-personal character of experience, not by itself identity across years.
- Parfit’s claim about what prudentially matters is treated as if it denied numerical distinctness between an original and a replica.
- Split-brain evidence about the number or unity of subjects is recruited into a theory of diachronic persistence.

A preliminary taxonomy would prevent several subsequent overclaims.

## 2. Citation and attribution audit

### What is accurate

The central bibliographic entries are real. Parfit, Olson, Locke, Schechtman, Gallagher and Zahavi, Tulving, Whitehead, Dennett, Frankish, Tallis, Albahari, Nagel and Chalmers are all identifiable works with substantially correct publication details. Frankish’s article is correctly given as *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 23(11–12), 11–39; Nagel’s paper appeared in *Synthese* 22, 396–413; and the Montupil and Santander DOIs and publication metadata are correct. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

Montupil et al. do distinguish total absence of mental content from internally conscious but environmentally disconnected states and connected consciousness under anaesthesia. The article’s three-state summary is therefore well grounded. ([DOI +1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjao.2023.100224))

The treatment of Nagel is also broadly fair: his position was that split-brain cases manifest too much unity for a simple two-mind verdict and too much separation for an unqualified one-mind verdict. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy +1](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2019/entries/consciousness-unity/))

### Sources that are real but overstretched

#### Schechtman

“**You are your story**” is too crude as a representation of Schechtman. Her 1996 narrative self-constitution view was formulated primarily as an answer to the **characterisation question**: which actions, experiences and traits are attributable to a person in the identity-defining sense. The book later reconnects this to survival and responsibility, but it is not simply a rival numerical persistence criterion alongside animalism and neo-Lockean continuity. ([JSTOR +2](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7591/j.ctv75d3xw))

The objection that narratives may exclude fragmented people is legitimate, but incomplete. Schechtman’s account includes reality and articulation constraints and does not require a polished, continuously narrated autobiography. The Map’s own later article on diachronic agency now provides the fairer treatment: narrative is one coordinating structure among plans, standing values, semantic self-knowledge, habits, embodiment and social scaffolding. That November 2026 treatment should replace the three-sentence summary in the personal-identity page. ([topics/diachronic-agency-and-personal-narrative](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/diachronic-agency-and-personal-narrative/))

#### Zahavi and the minimal self

Gallagher and Zahavi support the existence of a pre-reflective “for-me-ness” or minimal experiential self. They do not thereby establish a numerically identical subject persisting throughout a life. The Map’s own specialist page now says explicitly that Zahavi’s account is synchronic and that whether for-me-ness grounds diachronic identity is a further question. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

Thus the sentence that personal identity has “a minimal layer that persists throughout” runs ahead of the source. It should say something such as:

> A minimal first-personal structure may recur throughout conscious life; whether its successive instances belong to one numerically identical subject is the disputed persistence question.

The 2012 second edition of *The Phenomenological Mind* is also needlessly dated, since the site’s specialist page already cites the 2020 third edition and Zahavi’s later treatment of diachronic unity. ([concepts/self-and-self-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/))

#### Olson and animalism

Olson is correctly associated with animalism, but the article’s claim that animalism handles fission by saying “neither is you because the organism ceased” is not a general consequence of animalism. The verdict depends on exactly what happens to the original organism and its life processes. Animalism’s core thesis is that we are human animals; its usual persistence condition is continuity of biological life. Different fission constructions may kill the animal, divide its organs, leave one organism alive, or generate an indeterminate biological description. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

More importantly, the article omits animalism’s strongest positive arguments: that each of us appears to be the thinking animal located where we are; that psychological-person theories risk positing both a thinking organism and a coincident thinking person; and that animalism straightforwardly explains our existence as fetuses and possibly through profound psychological loss. These are not peripheral objections. They are the principal reasons animalists accept the counterintuitive transplant verdict. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy +1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/Identity-personal/))

#### Santander et al.

The 2025 PNAS study is accurately identified, and the article correctly reports that about one centimetre of spared splenial tissue was associated with widespread interhemispheric coupling, whereas complete callosotomy broadly disrupted normal network organisation. ([PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12582319/))

But the study involved **six patients**, was explicitly limited by its very small sample, and investigated structural and functional integration—not numerical identity, primitive subjecthood, or first-person reports of being one subject. Its authors state that the results were not suited to endorse a particular theory of consciousness. ([PubMed Central (PMC) +1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12582319/))

Consequently, the next sentence—“patients report a single first-person perspective”—is not supported by Santander. That claim belongs, if anywhere, to the disputed Pinto line of research, and even there Schechter and Bayne argue that the findings establish unity of behavioural agency more readily than unity of phenomenal experience. The Map’s dedicated split-brain page now acknowledges exactly that distinction. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

This is a **verified citation-fit defect**, not merely a difference of interpretation.

#### Albahari

Albahari’s *Analytical Buddhism* is a philosophical reconstruction defending a two-tiered account in which the ordinary owner-self is illusory but a form of ownerless or witness consciousness remains. It is not an empirical compilation of “contemplative reports.” ([Springer Link +1](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1057/9780230800540))

The article’s challenge condition therefore changes evidential category mid-sentence: it asks for contemplative reports, then cites a philosophical account. Albahari can support the proposition that one sophisticated no-self theory retains awareness or a perspectival structure; she cannot establish what contemplatives generally report.

### Material claims without adequate references

The page lacks direct references for:

- the Mandukya-based deep-sleep interpretation;
- the Many-Minds/Lockwood attribution;
- the specific Deutsch-style fission objection;
- the Madhyamaka/svabhāva analysis;
- consciousness operating by a quantum-Zeno mechanism;
- the claim that split-brain patients generally report a single perspective;
- the strong “pastness quale” inference;
- Tallis’s supposed standard regress argument, including a page locator.

Some of these are developed on linked pages, but core premises should not be supported only by an internal link to another AI-generated synthesis. The external source should either appear in this page’s references or the sentence should be explicitly labelled as the Map’s reconstruction.

### Are the references stale?

The canonical works are not obsolete simply because they are old. The problem is **coverage staleness** rather than bibliographic invalidity.

- Olson 1997 is foundational but should be supplemented by later animalist work and the substantially revised 2026 SEP animalism survey, which documents multiple formulations, new-animalist variants, recent defenders and recent critics. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/animalism/))
- Schechtman 1996 should be supplemented by the later narrative debate, including Strawson’s anti-narrativity objection and more recent accounts that distinguish narrative selfhood from numerical persistence. The Map already has much of this material elsewhere. ([topics/diachronic-agency-and-personal-narrative](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/diachronic-agency-and-personal-narrative/))
- Gallagher and Zahavi 2012 should be updated to the 2020 third edition and paired with Zahavi’s explicit work on diachronic unity.
- Albahari 2006 should not stand alone for contemporary Buddhist/no-self discussion.
- Tallis 2011 is an idiosyncratic critic, not the natural primary source for the strongest illusionist response; Frankish and contemporary self-model accounts should do more of the dialectical work.

## 3. Are the rival views stated and answered fairly?

### Psychological continuity and Parfit

The main article’s basic statement is recognisable: psychological continuity involves overlapping chains of memories, intentions, beliefs and other psychological connections, and fission creates trouble because two successors may stand in the relevant relation to one predecessor. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

But Parfit’s actual position is more exact. His psychological criterion includes:

- overlapping chains of psychological connectedness;
- the right kind of cause;
- a **non-branching** condition.

In fission, both successors may be psychologically continuous with the predecessor, but numerical identity cannot branch. Parfit’s preferred description is that neither successor is the original, while Relation R may nevertheless preserve what prudentially matters. ([eClass UOA +2](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PHS688/Metaphysics_Bibliography/Parfit%20Reasons%20and%20Persons.pdf))

The main page eventually states this correctly. Its fission section says that neither successor is you and explicitly notes that this aligns with Parfit’s conclusion that identity cannot branch. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

However, three distortions remain:

1. **“Parfit says teletransportation has no determinate answer” is too unrestricted.** His treatment distinguishes different versions of the psychological criterion and different cases. Branching and spectrum cases motivate indeterminacy or emptiness; not every teletransportation case receives the same verdict. ([eClass UOA +1](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PHS688/Metaphysics_Bibliography/Parfit%20Reasons%20and%20Persons.pdf))
2. **The Meaning Connection misstates Parfit.** The article says that, if Parfit is right, “you’re interchangeable with any replica sharing your psychology.” Parfit explicitly describes the original and Branch-Line replica as two different people. His claim is not numerical interchangeability; it is that the relation to a replica may contain what matters even where identity is absent. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))
3. **Linked pages contain a direct attribution error.** The haecceity page says that, for Parfit, a perfect replica “would be you” and that personal identity “can branch or fuse.” Parfit says the opposite: Relation R may branch, but personal identity cannot. The identity apex similarly says without qualification that “on the psychological view” the teletransported person is you. ([concepts/haecceity](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/haecceity/))

The article also does not answer reductionism on its strongest terms. “Patterns don’t choose; consciousnesses do” assumes that an agent must be an entity over and above the organised events constituting deliberation. A Parfitian, functionalist or process theorist will deny that assumption rather than struggle to identify an additional chooser. The article acknowledges reductionism’s internal coherence but does not supply an argument against process agency beyond the phenomenology that deliberation seems to belong to someone.

The site already contains a detailed quasi-memory article explaining the standard answer to Butler’s circularity objection and conceding that memory cannot certify which subject had an experience. That material should appear directly in the psychological-continuity section rather than being available only behind a link embedded in the “pastness quale” discussion. ([concepts/quasi-memory-and-the-circularity-objection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/quasi-memory-and-the-circularity-objection/))

### Animalism

Animalism receives only one substantive objection: it conflicts with brain-transplant intuitions. This is inadequate. A fair presentation should include:

- the thinking-animal argument;
- the too-many-thinkers problem for coincident-person theories;
- fetal and vegetative-state continuity;
- animalism’s fit with all ordinary, actual survival judgments;
- the animalist response that transplant intuitions may be less secure than they initially appear.

Current reference treatments explicitly present these as the considerations animalists regard as outweighing the transplant case. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy +1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/Identity-personal/))

The Map’s dualism does not by itself refute animalism. A property dualist could hold that human animals instantiate irreducible phenomenal properties. Even an interactionist could maintain that a nonphysical contribution belongs to or couples with a persisting organism. To reject animalism, the article must argue that the conscious subject has persistence conditions different from the animal’s—not simply restate that the subject is nonphysical.

### Narrative accounts

The narrative view is the least fairly presented. “You are your story” suppresses the distinction between:

- narratives constituting character, practical identity and ownership of actions;
- narratives supporting diachronic agency;
- narratives constituting numerical persistence.

The fragmented-narrative objection is important, but the article does not consider replies involving implicit narratives, locally coherent narratives, social scaffolding, plans, standing values or non-narrative forms of temporal coordination. The Map’s own revised diachronic-agency article now makes all those distinctions and concedes the force of Strawson’s episodic-person objection. ([topics/diachronic-agency-and-personal-narrative](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/diachronic-agency-and-personal-narrative/))

The personal-identity page should not merely link to that article in Further Reading; it should import its corrected formulation into “The Standard Positions.”

### No-self accounts

No-self is not presented as a standard rival at all. It appears later through Buddhism, process haecceitism and illusionism. This structurally disadvantages it: the reader first receives the Map’s haecceitistic answer and only then encounters views that deny the question’s presuppositions.

The page also presents too sharp a choice between primitive particularity and “no fact at all about which stream is undergone.” Buddhist accounts can accept:

- causally connected streams of psycho-physical events;
- conventional persons;
- action, responsibility and practical continuity;
- first-personal or perspectival phenomena;

while denying an independently existing owner or bearer. Classical Buddhist traditions disagree about the exact status of persons and consciousness, but causal continuity without a substantial or haecceitistic self is a central, developed alternative—not simply indexical eliminativism. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy +2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mind-indian-buddhism/))

The strongest contemporary naturalist no-self rival is Metzinger’s self-model theory. The Map’s dedicated page treats it responsibly as a coherent framework-relative challenge that the tenets do not refute. It should be promoted from a Further Reading bullet to a fourth standard position in the main article. ([concepts/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity/))

## 4. Does the positive haecceity argument succeed?

### The zombie argument

The article now correctly admits that zombie conceivability does not entail haecceity. At most, if accepted, it supports the claim that phenomenal facts are not fixed by physical-functional facts. The additional move from nonphysical consciousness to nonqualitative individual thisness is a separate inference. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

That inference remains weak. Even if two physically identical beings could differ in whether consciousness exists, it does not follow that two conscious beings with the same qualitative phenomenal character differ through primitive haecceities rather than through location, causal relations, embodiment, phenomenal fields or other relational facts.

The linked Parfit article makes a further questionable move by saying that a zombie would satisfy Parfit’s psychological-continuity criterion. This equivocates between **functional duplication** and **psychological continuity**. Parfit formulates his view in terms of psychological connections and their causal derivation; whether a phenomenally unconscious zombie literally has such psychological states cannot simply be assumed. ([concepts/parfit-reductionism](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/parfit-reductionism/))

More fundamentally, zombie conceivability concerns whether consciousness supervenes on physical facts. Personal identity is a relation between stages or continuers over time. Establishing the former would not specify the latter’s persistence conditions.

### The causal-history argument

The revision recognises the central problem: a distinct causal history trivially individuates tokens but does not generate primitive thisness. The article now says causal history supplies content to an already-posited particular. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

That concession leaves the argument circular as an account of diachronic identity. To describe one history as the series of selections made by “this consciousness” already assumes that a single consciousness persists across the history. The history cannot then explain why those temporally distributed selections belong to one subject.

The site’s quasi-memory article reaches the correct methodological verdict: neither memory nor a represented causal history establishes that one persisting selector made all the relevant selections; what makes one subject identical through time remains open. ([concepts/quasi-memory-and-the-circularity-objection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/quasi-memory-and-the-circularity-objection/))

### The explanatory-gap argument

Persistent puzzlement is some evidence that a conceptual or explanatory problem remains. It is not evidence selecting haecceity over competing diagnoses. The article candidly admits that conceptual confusion could generate the same puzzlement. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

The Occam section nevertheless says the haecceitistic framework “honors” the puzzlement while reductionism explains it away. That is rhetorically asymmetric. A theory can honour an intuition by explaining why agents have it without treating its represented object as fundamental. The illusionist and self-model alternatives do not ignore the datum; they disagree about what would explain it.

### The past-self argument

The difference between representing a past experience and literally re-undergoing it is real. But it does not establish a persisting haecceitistic bearer. A Parfitian can say that the current state is appropriately causally and psychologically related to a numerically distinct past experience; a Buddhist can describe causal succession; a narrative theorist can describe present appropriation of a past event.

Indeed, quasi-memory was designed precisely to show that a state can present a past experience from the inside without presupposing that the current subject originally underwent it. The article’s own linked treatment concedes that felt pastness and ownership cannot certify the source subject. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

The “past-self” argument therefore supports **non-recurrence of token experiences**, which almost every rival can accept. It does not establish numerical persistence of one subject between them.

### Process haecceitism

Process haecceitism is a potentially interesting Map-specific position: primitive particularity without an unchanging Cartesian substance. The article is admirably explicit that this is not a Buddhist doctrine and may merely reintroduce svabhāva in thinner form. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

But it presently names rather than solves the persistence problem. Saying that “this series” of moments possesses particularity leaves unanswered:

- what makes one moment part of the same series as another;
- whether the unifying relation is primitive, causal, psychological or phenomenal;
- how that relation crosses unconscious intervals;
- why it cannot branch;
- what determines when a stream begins or ends.

If causal or psychological relations constitute series-membership, the account approaches the reductionism it rejects. If a primitive diachronic relation does the work, a second primitive has been added alongside synchronic haecceity. If one enduring bearer unifies the moments, process haecceitism has returned to a substance-like commitment.

The site’s self-consciousness page now states the situation more accurately: the Map treats diachronic identity as primitive and cannot fully explain what constitutes it. ([concepts/self-and-self-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/))

## 5. Where posits become “results”

The article contains unusually good local calibration, but four passages reverse it.

### “What Personal Identity Requires”

After saying that the arguments merely open room for a posit and shift no burden, the article states that identity requires consciousness persistence, causal continuity and indexical uniqueness. It also calls indexical knowledge irreducible to impersonal description and extends this “thisness” into physics. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

This is acceptable only if headed **“What the Map Posits Personal Identity to Require.”** As written, it reads as the conclusion of the preceding argument. The linked indexical-knowledge article is more cautious: Perry and Lewis establish cognitive or epistemic irreducibility, not ontological indexical facts, and the Map admits it has not answered the deflationary interpretation. ([concepts/indexical-knowledge-and-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/indexical-knowledge-and-identity/))

The main page should not state the stronger metaphysical thesis without carrying over that concession.

### Dualism and identity

The tenet section says that, if consciousness is irreducible, personal identity cannot be purely physical or functional. That does not follow. Irreducible phenomenal properties could belong to a biologically persisting animal; the identity of the bearer could remain physical even if some of its properties are not reducible. Likewise, a psychological criterion could include phenomenal psychological continuity.

Dualism is **hospitable** to a nonphysical identity criterion but does not entail one. The site’s haecceity page actually states this more accurately. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

### Causal contribution and individuation

The claim that any nonphysical causal contribution would individuate consciousness through its unique history again moves from token distinctness to diachronic identity. Two causally efficacious events can be numerically distinct without belonging to one persisting subject. A rule assigning temporally separated contributions to one subject is still required.

### Meaning

The claim that Parfit makes particular existence less significant because “patterns are repeatable, and so would you be” is a substantive misrepresentation. Parfit denies that a replica is numerically the original in a branching case. His controversial claim is that Relation R may preserve much of what rationally matters despite the absence of identity. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

A fair criticism would ask whether Relation R captures all the value of unique first-person survival. It should not attribute numerical repeatability to Parfit.

## 6. Fission and split-brain evidence

The conclusion that neither fission successor is numerically identical with the original is defensible and agrees with Parfit’s best-description verdict. What is not established is the Map’s explanation: that an indivisible conscious particular necessarily ends whenever the physical substrate divides. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

That is another framework commitment. The article acknowledges this regarding the fission/Many-Worlds asymmetry, but it should also acknowledge it regarding the alleged indivisibility of perspective. Nothing in the preceding haecceity argument shows that a process particular cannot branch, transform, continue in one branch, or stand in a non-identity survival relation to both.

The empirical section has three problems:

1. **“If consciousness were merely brain processes, complete disconnection should produce complete division” is too strong.** Physicalist and functionalist theories can predict partial, graded or multidimensional dissociation, continued integration through subcortical routes, or unity of agency without full phenomenal co-consciousness.
2. **Santander does not support the first-person claim.** It supports a physical explanation of extensive integration where posterior fibres remain and extensive network disruption after complete transection. It does not establish that complete-callosotomy patients retain one phenomenally unified perspective. ([PubMed Central (PMC) +1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12582319/))
3. **The site’s specialist page is more cautious than the personal-identity page.** It says Pinto-type findings may show one responding organism or unified agency rather than one phenomenal field, and ultimately concedes that the evidence is compatible with both views. ([topics/split-brain-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/split-brain-consciousness/))

The personal-identity page should adopt that wording. At present it converts contested unity of report into evidence for an identity-bearing subject distinct from computational binding.

## 7. Sleep, anaesthesia and persistence gaps

The article correctly identifies a real pressure on its account: if consciousness constitutes identity and genuinely ceases, what connects the pre-gap and post-gap subjects?

The Montupil taxonomy is accurately cited, but it does not favour the interface-disruption interpretation. It distinguishes possible states under anaesthesia; it does not show that apparent unconsciousness is usually hidden consciousness or that an independently existing subject persists through the interval. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

The Vedantic witness proposal establishes a metaphysical possibility within that tradition, not evidence that consciousness continues through ordinary dreamless sleep. “A memory gap need not imply a consciousness gap” is logically correct; it leaves equally open actual cessation.

This section exposes a central incompleteness in process haecceitism:

- If the subject continues during the gap, what constitutes that continuation without experiences or selections?
- If it ceases and a later subject begins, what makes resurrection of the same particular possible?
- If a primitive identity fact bridges the interval, what constrains that fact?

The article raises these questions but does not incorporate the resulting uncertainty into its positive persistence criteria.

## 8. Illusionism and no-self: much improved, but still asymmetric

The illusionism section is one of the strongest parts of the revision. It admits that the “a seeming requires someone to whom it seems” regress begs the question against functional seeming, accurately presents the Dennett/Frankish reply, and explicitly says the anti-error-theory considerations are non-decisive. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

The remaining asymmetry is that the illusionist is required to make the self-model explanation “tractable,” while haecceity is permitted to remain primitive and empirically indistinguishable. That may be a defensible methodological policy under Tenet 5, but it is not neutral adjudication. The article should compare explanatory costs symmetrically:

- reductionism must explain the intuition of particularity;
- haecceitism must explain persistence, stream boundaries, gaps and why the posit has no discriminating consequences.

The claim that an illusionist must “in practice assume their own identity” is also dialectically weak. A deflationist can use ordinary first-person discourse, reasoning and temporally extended cognitive organisation without accepting primitive identity—just as one can speak of sunrise without accepting geocentrism. The article does acknowledge this answer, which prevents the passage from becoming a straightforward error. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

## 9. The proposed challenges are not yet genuine falsifiers

The four items under “What Would Challenge This View?” mostly describe philosophical defeats rather than operational evidence.

- A “successful” illusionist account has no specified threshold of success.
- A decision-theoretic Born-rule derivation concerns quantum probability and does not by itself decide personal identity.
- Contemplative loss-of-particularity reports cannot refute a primitive that the theory can always say persisted unnoticed.
- A “complete neural explanation” sets a much higher evidential bar for the rival than the Map imposes on its own interface account.

The article itself recognises that teletransportation cannot discriminate because every replica reports continuity. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

A stronger test section would identify observations that alter comparative likelihoods rather than demanding total rival success. For example:

- reproducible double dissociation between phenomenal unity and all candidate neural integration measures;
- reliable evidence of two independent autobiographical centres after complete callosotomy;
- evidence that subject-count changes continuously with neural connectivity;
- a well-defined quantum-conditioned selection effect attributable to a continuing individual subject;
- loss and restoration profiles that distinguish an enduring interface from reconstituted cognition.

Even those would not directly measure haecceity, but they would make the surrounding interface theory more or less credible.

## 10. Cross-site consistency

### Main article versus indexical-knowledge article

The personal-identity page says indexical knowledge is irreducible to impersonal description and uses it as part of what identity “requires.” The specialist article distinguishes epistemic from ontological irreducibility and says explicitly that Perry/Lewis-style deflationists have not been answered. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

The specialist page is better calibrated. Its wording should govern the main page.

### Main article versus the vertiginous-question page

The personal-identity page treats the vertiginous question as a genuine cognitive boundary and positive motivation for haecceity. The specialist page says the deflationist exit remains open, the Map has accepted none of the proposed solutions and refuted none, and List’s quadrilemma does not establish that irreducible first-person facts exist. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

Again, the specialist page’s caveats need propagating into the main article.

### Main article versus self-and-self-consciousness

The personal-identity page suggests a minimal self that persists throughout. The specialist page explicitly says Zahavi’s minimal self is synchronic and that diachronic identity is an additional Map commitment not supplied by Zahavi. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

This is a direct cross-page inconsistency.

### Main article versus haecceity and the identity apex

The main article correctly says Parfit denies branching identity and that neither fission successor is numerically the original. The haecceity page says Parfitian identity can branch or fuse, while the identity apex says the psychological view necessarily identifies the teletransported person with the original. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

Those linked pages should be corrected; otherwise the main article links readers to less accurate versions of its own rival.

### Process haecceitism versus persisting substances

The main article says haecceity need not involve a permanent soul-substance and presents the Map’s view as process haecceitism. The Parfit page says agents cause actions “as persisting substances.” ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

This may be reconcilable if “substance” means merely a non-reducible persisting bearer rather than an unchanging Cartesian substance, but the terminology currently creates a real contradiction. The site needs to decide whether its subject is:

- a substance;
- a process;
- a bare particular instantiated by a process;
- an aspect of an organism;
- or a primitive diachronic relation among experiential occasions.

### Personal identity versus the quasi-memory article

The main article presents consciousness persistence and causal history as its answer. The quasi-memory page says what makes one subject the same subject over time remains open, and that the Map’s causal-history proposal assumes the persisting selector it needs to establish. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

The latter is the more accurate statement of the current research position.

### Personal identity versus the split-brain page

The main article says the surviving evidence suggests identity is distinct from computational binding. The dedicated page says the evidence does not settle unity of experience and explicitly concludes that its own narrower challenge to functionalism fails. ([topics/personal-identity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/personal-identity/))

That is a substantive conflict, not merely a difference in emphasis.
