## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Wrong-work attribution plus axiom overstatement (interval rung, level one).** The article said "Reisenzein and Junge (2024) run the Krantz et al. difference-structure axioms — weak ordering plus the sextuple condition, or its stronger quadruple variant — … retaining the quadruple axiom for 71–97%". Checked against the raw full text of the 2024 paper, three things were wrong: (a) the axiom test and the 71–97% figures come from **Junge and Reisenzein (2016)**, which the 2024 paper reports ("For the six emotions investigated by Junge and Reisenzein (2016)…"); (b) only the quadruple axiom was tested, and the 2024 paper says weak ordering "could not" be tested because it "is necessarily fulfilled if difference comparisons are derived from GPCs"; (c) the quadruple axiom comes from "alternative axiomatizations" (Debreu 1958; Luce and Suppes 1965), not from Krantz et al., whose standard axiom is the sextuple condition. The article's own research note had already recorded the 2016 provenance, but the article compressed it away. **Fixed**: the sentence now credits the test to Junge and Reisenzein (2016), says only the quadruple axiom ("stronger than Krantz et al.'s sextuple condition") was tested, and keeps the "ultimately based on introspection" quote attributed to the 2024 review. Junge and Reisenzein 2016 was added to References.
- **Knock-on effect on the ordinal rung.** The rung cited "introspective tests of weak ordering … (level one below)". Level one no longer claims a weak-order test, so the pointer would have dangled. The claim itself is supported by a different passage: the 2024 paper reports a transitivity analysis "conducted for the present article" showing judgments "nearly perfectly transitive for practically all participants". **Fixed**: the ordinal rung now cites that finding directly (Reisenzein and Junge 2024).
