## Reply

# External referee report: *Anaesthesia and the Consciousness Interface*

**Recommendation: major revision; reject-and-resubmit if the article is intended as empirical evidence for interactionist dualism.**

## Executive verdict

The article identifies a real and important empirical pattern: anaesthesia is not a unitary on/off switch. Arousal, environmental connectedness, internal experience, responsiveness, memory, and report can dissociate; different drugs produce different molecular, circuit-level, electrophysiological, and phenomenological profiles; and emergence is an active, state-dependent process rather than simple passive drug clearance.

The central metaphysical inference does not follow. Anaesthetic interventions support a **structured, multidimensional neural architecture of conscious states**. They do not establish that the dimensions are channels through which an independently existing consciousness interfaces with the brain. The interface reading remains compatible with the evidence, but it is not better supported than sophisticated physicalist realizational, functionalist, thalamocortical, dendritic-integration, predictive-processing, workspace, or dynamical-systems accounts.

The distinction matters because the article’s description and opening call the pharmacology a “molecular map” of the mind–brain connection and say that interactionist dualism “predicts exactly” the observed architecture, while a late section correctly concedes that anaesthesia cannot distinguish the interface interpretation from sophisticated functionalism. The late concession is the defensible position; the headline framing is not.

# 1. What the evidence warrants

The anaesthetic evidence supports three increasingly strong propositions:

1. **Unresponsiveness, disconnectedness, amnesia, and absence of experience are not equivalent.**
2. **Different molecular interventions affect distinguishable neural and cognitive functions.**
3. **Conscious state depends causally and systematically on neural receptor, circuit, and network dynamics.**

The third proposition is especially important. Because anaesthetic studies involve interventions rather than passive correlations, the article should not rely too heavily on the generic reply that correlation does not prove production. Manipulating GABAergic, glutamatergic, thalamic, cortical, and dendritic mechanisms causally changes consciousness-related capacities. This still does not establish metaphysical identity or production, but it places a positive explanatory burden on the interface theorist: why should changing physical mechanisms have exactly the effects expected if those mechanisms realize the relevant functions?

To favour an interface hypothesis over a physicalist realization hypothesis, the article would need to show:

P(E∣Hinterface​)>P(E∣Hphysicalist​)

But the interface theory currently supplies no prior mapping from drug, dose, receptor, circuit, or network state to a predicted interface-channel failure. The drug-to-channel assignments are inferred after the phenomenology is known. Meanwhile, several physicalist models independently predict selective failure of feedback, integration, access, connectedness, memory, or arousal.

The evidence therefore discriminates against a **naive single-switch model**, not against contemporary physicalism.

## Explicit answer on the central inference

**The inference from anaesthetic action to a dualist interface reading is not warranted as an empirical inference.** It is warranted only as a framework-relative reinterpretation: given dualism and filter theory already, one may describe the affected neural systems as an interface. The anaesthetic findings do not independently increase the probability of that ontology over sophisticated physicalist rivals.

The defensible conclusion is:

> Anaesthesia maps neural mechanisms governing dimensions of conscious state. Whether those mechanisms constitute consciousness or mediate an independently existing consciousness remains underdetermined.

That conclusion would preserve the article’s useful empirical synthesis without presenting a relabelling of neural functions as a discovery of non-physical channels.

- one reference is nonexistent as printed;

# 3. Unsupported or insufficiently supported claims

## 3.1 The proposed “temporal-binding channel” lacks an identifiable source

The article reports that patients under light sevoflurane experience events “floating” without sequence and that volatile agents disrupt the lowest levels of temporal binding first. No study in the reference list clearly reports this finding, and I could not identify the primary experiment corresponding to that description.

This is not a minor gap. The four-channel taxonomy depends on temporal binding being independently pharmacologically dissociable. Without a source, the article has evidence for dimensions involving connectedness, arousal, report and memory, but not for its claimed four-part interface map.

## 3.2 Absence of report is repeatedly treated as absence of experience

Sarasso et al. obtained reports after emergence. Anaesthesia can disrupt encoding, consolidation, retrieval, report access, or all four. The article itself recognises memory as an independently vulnerable component, making its inference from “nothing reported” to “phenomenal presence abolished” internally unstable.

The primary isolated-forearm study found responsiveness in 12 of 260 patients immediately after intubation, including five who signalled pain. Postoperative interviews did not recover corresponding explicit memories. The article’s own changelog later highlighted this latency problem: real-time probing detected connected consciousness at approximately 4.6%, while none of the subsequently interviewed patients recalled the detected episode. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27984262/)

The correct wording is therefore:

> Propofol and xenon were associated with no subsequently reportable experience in this protocol.

It should not be:

> Propofol and xenon abolished phenomenal experience.

## 3.3 The “same receptor, opposite phenomenology” argument oversimplifies pharmacology

Calling xenon and ketamine instances of the same primary-receptor intervention invites a false controlled comparison. General anaesthetics are polypharmacological; receptor affinity, dose, distribution, kinetics, effects on potassium channels, cortical feedback and subcortical arousal systems differ. Molecular overlap does not mean the system-level interventions are otherwise matched. Contemporary pharmacological reviews already treat anaesthesia as an interaction among molecular targets, neural pathways and network dynamics. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn2372)

The contrast is scientifically valuable, but it shows that **NMDA antagonism alone does not determine phenomenology**. It does not isolate one phenomenal-interface channel.

## 3.4 The ketamine self-stultification argument targets only a narrow rival

Later ketamine reports show that whatever neural process encoded the experience remained causally connected to later memory and report. They do not show that a non-physical phenomenal property, as distinct from its neural realization or correlate, caused the memory.

A physicalist common-cause account is immediate:

ketamine neural state→⎩⎨⎧​experiencememory tracelater report​

No epiphenomenal non-physical property is required. The argument may pressure a particular form of property-dualist epiphenomenalism, but it does not pressure identity theory, functionalism, realization physicalism, GNW, recurrent processing, predictive processing or IIT.

## 3.5 Neural inertia is not puzzling for production models

The article asks why a generator should resist switching back on at the same threshold at which it switched off. But physical generators and controllers with feedback, attractors or mutually inhibitory populations routinely display hysteresis. A Schmitt trigger, magnetic material or bistable neural circuit need not invoke an independently persisting output to explain different forward and reverse thresholds.

Friedman and Sepúlveda’s evidence therefore supports **state-dependent nonlinear neural dynamics**. It is not an anomaly that specifically favours a filter.

## 3.6 Active recovery does not imply re-entry

Hu et al. show that molecular and thalamic mechanisms actively create conditions associated with emergence. This is entirely expected if consciousness is realized by a system that must be restored to an appropriate dynamical regime.

The sequence

recovery mechanism→wake-like dynamics→behavioural responsiveness

does not imply:

brain prepares empty channel→non-physical consciousness re-enters

A physical precondition normally precedes the state it enables. Ignition precedes engine operation without implying that engine operation exists elsewhere waiting to enter.

## 3.7 The recovery-order inference is underdetermined

Executive performance recovering before reaction time is interesting, but “simple before complex” is not a general physicalist prediction. Recovery order depends on circuit sensitivity, task demands, ceiling effects, neuromodulation and network topology, not an abstract ladder from simple to complex. Mashour et al.’s own interpretation in terms of workspace recovery is a direct rival to the interface interpretation. [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/59525)

## 3.8 Anaesthetic resistance supports pharmacology, not interface individuality

Specific genetic or acquired differences in drug sensitivity are exactly what a physicalist pharmacological account predicts. Receptor expression, metabolism, tolerance and network adaptation should matter more than brain size alone. The article constructs a straw production model when it suggests that physicalism would expect sensitivity mainly to follow brain size or metabolic rate.

The red-hair evidence is particularly too weak and inconsistent to bear metaphysical weight. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15277908/)

# 4. The death-boundary section should not be treated as discriminating evidence

Calling cardiac-arrest and dying-brain findings the article’s “strongest discriminating evidence” is the least defensible part of the piece.

First, these are not anaesthesia studies. They introduce different pathophysiology, measurement problems, selection effects and report delays.

Second, neither Xu nor Parnia establishes conscious experience during the relevant recorded activity. Xu reports gamma changes in two of four dying patients without reports. Parnia’s 39% figure concerns a small interviewed survivor subset, not 40% of all cardiac-arrest patients showing organised activity. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37126719/)

Third, the filter prediction is post hoc and bidirectional:

- neural disruption plus reduced experience is called filter closure;
- neural disruption plus vivid experience is called filter loosening;
- no report is called memory-channel failure;
- a report is called persistence of consciousness.

Unless the theory specifies in advance which intervention should close which channel and with what measurable consequences, almost any result can be absorbed.

The death material currently launders speculative evidence from one domain into an ostensibly controlled pharmacological argument. It should be removed or placed in a separate, explicitly low-confidence convergence section.

# 5. Physicalist and deflationary rivals left unengaged

## 5.1 Dendritic Integration Theory

Dendritic Integration Theory proposes that conscious processing depends on coupling between feedforward somatic inputs and feedback arriving at apical dendrites. Anaesthesia can decouple these streams, preserving substantial sensory processing while preventing the recurrent integration associated with conscious access. This directly explains why word-level or sensory cortical responses can persist while higher-order integration, connectedness or report fails. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32855048/)

This is arguably the most direct physicalist rival to the article’s “separable interface channels,” because it provides a cellular mechanism for the same dissociation.

## 5.2 Predictive processing and predictive routing

Intracranial macaque work under propofol found disruption of alpha/beta predictive modulation and fronto-sensory coherence alongside preserved or enhanced lower-level responses to unexpected stimuli. This offers a mechanistic account of sensory processing without global contextual integration or connected experience. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39374396/)

Recent human work also treats thalamic and cortical predictive processing as a basis for disconnection under anaesthesia. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41314941/)

These models explain the Kallionpää-type result without positing a sensory channel connected to an extraneural consciousness.

## 5.3 Global Neuronal Workspace Theory

The article briefly says early frontoparietal restoration “fits” GNW and then immediately redescribes the workspace as a channel that must be inhabited. That is not engagement. Mashour et al. explicitly regarded their findings as consistent with GNW: restoration of the broadcasting architecture precedes the reportable state it enables. [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/59525)

The article needs to state what additional observation the interface interpretation predicts that GNW does not.

## 5.4 Integrated Information Theory

High PCI or complexity during ketamine does not obviously challenge IIT. IIT distinguishes the presence or richness of experience from environmental connectedness and behavioural responsiveness. A highly integrated but disconnected internally generated state is therefore not an evident counterexample.

More seriously, the Onoda paper cited by the article treats its Φ-related findings as broadly supportive of IIT-style expectations and does not study ketamine. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40901488/)

## 5.5 Thalamocortical mesocircuit accounts

Redinbaugh and Hu provide unusually concrete causal evidence for thalamocortical control of state transitions. A physicalist can say that thalamic disinhibition and cortical coupling constitute or enable the relevant conscious state. The Map calls the same machinery a channel, but supplies no observation differentiating those descriptions. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32053769/)

## 5.6 Nonlinear dynamical-systems accounts

Hysteresis, stochastic first-passage times, metastability and attractor transitions are standard properties of complex physical networks. Stone’s broad emergence-time distribution and Friedman’s neural inertia are positive evidence for this family of models, not residual anomalies left after physicalism has failed. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20689589/)

Recent cross-agent work is particularly important because it complicates the article’s inference from molecular difference to distinct interface components. Studies report common changes in cortical stability, low-frequency phase organisation and dynamical recovery across anaesthetics with different proximal molecular targets. [PubMed+2PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40349347/)

## 5.7 Higher-order and metacognitive-access theories

Failure to report or later remember an experience may result from failure of higher-order representation, metacognitive access or report preparation rather than disappearance of first-order content. These accounts are particularly relevant to the article’s repeated movement from no report to no phenomenal presence.

## 5.8 Recurrent-processing and posterior-cortical accounts

The article often treats frontoparietal activity as the interface, but recurrent and posterior-hot-zone theories locate conscious content differently from executive access. Agent-specific effects could therefore dissociate content, access and report within an entirely neural architecture.

## 5.9 Illusionism and deflationary operationalism

The article assumes without argument that phenomenology is an independently identifiable component and then asks which channel carries it. An illusionist would instead analyse the mechanisms that generate reports, introspective judgments and dispositions to classify a state as vivid or absent.

A less ambitious operationalist response is even simpler: “connected,” “disconnected,” and “unconscious” are clinically useful state categories. They do not themselves answer whether consciousness is produced, realized, transmitted or merely attributed on the basis of reports and neural markers.

# 6. Selective rather than merely stale literature

The article contains several appropriate recent references from 2024–25, so its weakness is not simply age. It is **selective updating**.

It includes recent work congenial to complexity, disconnected consciousness and quantum microtubule speculation, but does not engage recent findings that supply ordinary neural mechanisms or convergence across molecularly different drugs:

- cortical dendritic decoupling under anaesthesia; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32084339/)
- predictive-routing failure under propofol; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39374396/)
- common dynamical destabilisation across propofol, ketamine and dexmedetomidine; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41850281/)
- cross-agent low-frequency phase organisation; [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40349347/)
- human thalamic predictive-coding accounts of disconnected consciousness; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41314941/)
- high-density EEG evidence for posterior, parietal and subcortical connectivity markers during sedation and surgery. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41616765/)

The newer convergence literature matters dialectically. If drugs with different receptor profiles converge on common cortical stability or phase-dynamical changes, the article cannot move directly from molecular diversity to separate ontological channels. Molecular heterogeneity may converge on a shared neural state-transition mechanism.

Foundational sources such as Friedman, Casali and Sarasso are not obsolete. The problem is that their interpretation has not been revised in light of newer mechanistic alternatives.

# 7. Tenet-protective bracketing

## 7.1 The caveat arrives after the conclusion has already been advertised

The article ultimately admits that anaesthetic evidence cannot distinguish the Map’s interpretation from sophisticated functionalism. But the title, description and lead have already represented the work as a molecular map of how consciousness connects to matter.

This is not merely rhetorical imbalance. It reverses the evidential hierarchy:

- the conditional, underdetermined claim should control the article;
- the dualist interpretation should appear as one proposed reading;
- the current article instead treats the interpretation as the result and underdetermination as a late limitation.

## 7.2 “Production theory” is defined as its weakest possible version

The article repeatedly contrasts filter theory with a generator that should:

- lose consciousness uniformly;
- scale with total activity or tissue quantity;
- recover simple functions before complex functions;
- switch at the same threshold in both directions;
- react similarly to different drugs producing similar behaviour.

No major current physicalist theory makes all of these predictions. The target is a scalar power-supply metaphor, not physicalism.

## 7.3 Functional distinctions are converted into ontological channels

Evidence that memory, connectedness and responsiveness dissociate establishes distinguishable functions. The further claim that they are channels between two substances requires an argument. Naming each function an “interface component” does not supply one.

The same evidence can be described as:

- separate interface channels;
- separate neural mechanisms;
- separate workspace access conditions;
- separate levels of recurrent integration;
- separate predictive hierarchies;
- separate metacognitive and mnemonic capacities.

The article never identifies a result that selects the first description.

## 7.4 The no-report problem can immunise the theory

The article uses reports to establish ketamine consciousness, but discounts absent reports under propofol by treating them as genuine phenomenal extinction. If challenged, it can instead invoke memory-channel failure. This gives it incompatible evidential rules:

- report present → consciousness persisted;
- report absent → consciousness was extinguished;
- alternatively, report absent → consciousness persisted but memory failed.

A principled report model must be fixed before the result is interpreted.

## 7.5 “Prepared before arrival” is inferred from ordinary causal precedence

Neural recovery preceding behavioural recovery is redescribed as the brain preparing for consciousness to enter. Yet any realizational theory predicts that the enabling physical state precedes its behavioural manifestations. The article treats ordinary causal ordering as evidence for a second entity because the second entity is already assumed.

## 7.6 Convergence is doing work the article says it cannot do locally

The article concedes that anaesthesia alone does not discriminate, then appeals to convergence with contemplative pathology, lesions, psychogenic dissociation, near-death reports and terminal lucidity. But those cases share report, interpretation and framework dependencies. Unless their evidential independence is demonstrated, adding them yields framework coherence rather than a cumulative likelihood advantage.

The Map’s own P-M2 says precisely that convergence must remain compatibility-and-cumulative-fit until a distinguishing test is passed.

# 8. Relation to the five tenets

## Dualism

Anaesthesia does not supply independent support for dualism. It is compatible with dualism and constrains simplistic behaviourist or global-switch accounts. The article’s stronger language turns a prior metaphysical commitment into an empirical conclusion.

## Minimal Quantum Interaction

No cited anaesthesia study detects consciousness-dependent quantum selection. Craddock offers modelling; Wiest offers a theoretical synthesis; Stone explicitly does not distinguish classical noise from genuine indeterminacy. The quantum material should therefore be classified as speculative integration, not as an implication of anaesthetic pharmacology. [PubMed+2OUP Academic+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25714379/)

## Bidirectional Interaction

Hu et al. demonstrate physical causation within a mouse thalamic recovery pathway. They provide no evidence of mental-to-physical traffic. Internally generated ketamine imagery likewise does not show consciousness acting back upon matter; every physicalist rival represents it as neural activity generating both experience and memory.

This section is the clearest instance of tenet-protective recruitment: a purely neural mechanism is redescribed as evidence for bidirectionality because bidirectionality is already a tenet.

## Rejection of Many Worlds

The anaesthesia paragraph has no evidential force against Everettian quantum mechanics. An Everettian expects branch-relative physical brain states to depend on drug-specific molecular and neural dynamics. Nothing in Many Worlds predicts that propofol, ketamine and xenon should produce phenomenologically identical states.

The paragraph neither cites Everettian literature nor identifies a probability, branching or personal-identity result specific to anaesthesia. It should be removed.

## Limits to Occam’s razor

The finding that emergence is complex defeats only the simple claim that recovery is exhausted by declining drug concentration. Complexity does not favour a non-physical interface. Physical biological systems commonly have active recovery, feedback, hysteresis and stochastic transitions.

# 9. Integration with neighbouring Map articles

## The positions register is better calibrated than the subject article

P-CS4 says that the interface reading is:

- compatible with, but not forced by, anaesthesia and related evidence;
- held against a live production-model rival;
- externally supported only at Grade C;
- dependent on the bare-dualism/filter interpretation, **not on the quantum apparatus**.

The subject article conflicts with this in two ways:

1. its lead presents interface architecture as something pharmacology has revealed and the Map predicted exactly;
2. its tenet section connects the result to microtubule quantum processes, stochastic quantum emergence and bidirectional interaction.

The article’s late underdetermination paragraph aligns with P-CS4. Its opening and tenet synthesis do not.

## *The Interface Problem* is more epistemically disciplined

That neighbouring article explicitly states that identity theory, functionalism and emergentism can absorb the same observations, and that the interface interpretation is selected by the Map’s prior commitments rather than by a rival-discriminating measurement. It even acknowledges the risk of a Lakatosian degenerating shift when new evidence is repeatedly absorbed into a flexible hierarchy.

The anaesthesia article should import that paragraph’s epistemic stance almost verbatim in substance.

## *Filter Theory* partly corrects and partly propagates the problem

The filter-theory article appropriately concedes that sophisticated physicalism can accommodate coherent or enhanced experience and that filter “predictions” are partly post hoc because the theory lacks independent criteria for when disruption should enhance rather than degrade experience. But it then repeats the anaesthesia article’s unsupported four-component taxonomy, including the uncited temporal-binding channel.

Corrections therefore need to cascade into both articles.

## *Consciousness Disruption and the Mind–Brain Interface* is better framed globally but still overclaims locally

Its introduction says production accounts can accommodate the same cases and that the interface reading is favoured by coherence rather than forced by the data. That is appropriately calibrated. Yet its anaesthesia section again describes propofol as near-total phenomenal extinction and argues that physicalism should expect similar neural disruption to yield similar phenomenology.

The article therefore contains the right methodological conclusion and a recurring local straw model.

# 10. Overall assessment

The article should be retained, but its intellectual role needs to change.

As presently framed, it purports to move from pharmacology to a structured dualist interface. It does not succeed. Its strongest empirical material supports a multidimensional, intervention-sensitive, state-dependent neural architecture. Its strongest philosophical contribution could be to show that these findings **underdetermine realization versus mediation**, while developing concrete tests that might eventually separate them.

The article is strongest where it:

- distinguishes connectedness from experience;
- acknowledges that emergence is active;
- recognises classical and quantum interpretations of stochasticity as empirically unresolved;
- states that sophisticated functionalism remains viable.

It is weakest where it:

- converts no report into no experience;
- treats modular neural function as a non-physical channel map;
- cites physicalist studies as though their results supported transmission;
- elevates dying-brain evidence into a discriminator;
- uses Hu for bidirectional mental causation;
- treats ketamine as an IIT counterexample;
- attributes experiments to Wiest that he did not conduct;
- misreports Parnia;
- recruits anaesthesia against Many Worlds.

The appropriate evidential classification is:

- **Multidimensional anaesthetic state architecture:** strongly supported.
- **Specific neural mechanisms governing state transitions:** strongly supported.
- **Filter/interface interpretation:** live, framework-relative interpretation.
- **Non-physical interface favoured over physicalist realization:** not established.
- **Quantum-interface inference:** speculative.
- **Bidirectional-interaction and anti-Everett implications:** unsupported.
