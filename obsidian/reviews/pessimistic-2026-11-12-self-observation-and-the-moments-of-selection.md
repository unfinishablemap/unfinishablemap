## Findings Against the Driver's Six Questions

### 1. Does the weak premise survive the article's own three blocking results?

It survives two of the three and survives the register only conditionally. Four separate problems.

**1a. The best-evidenced kind is not a first-person kind.** L109: "the first-person kinds are candidate classifications: arbitrary against deliberate (which Maoz shows has a neural signature), effortful against effortless, completed against withheld." Maoz and colleagues assigned arbitrary and deliberate by task design (the research note, L389: "a $1000-donation task"). No self-report produced the classification. So the one contrast the article can show to be well defined needs no self-observation, and "task" is the first item in P-Q2's own list of public labels ("task, subject, recorded intention", calibration history, 2026-10-14). What self-observation uniquely supplies is the effortful/effortless contrast, which the article itself calls "unevidenced as a site of indeterminacy" (L95). *Defect, high.*

**1b. The test has lost its conditioning clause.** P-Q9 states the test as "residual mutual information between outcome and a preregistered intention covariate surviving conditionalisation on the measured physical state". The research note kept this (note L421: "given the measured physical state"). The article does not: a search of the article for "physical state" or "screen" returns nothing. L37 speaks of "statistics conditioned on the conscious state" and L109 of conditioning on a classified state. Without the screening clause a difference between kinds is trivially expected and shows nothing, as the Maoz signature the article cites in the same sentence demonstrates: kinds differ physically. The article compressed a distinction its research note got right. *Defect, high.*

**1c. The weak premise quietly needs what the strong premise needed.** The strong premise failed partly because "nothing shows that selections coincide with felt choice" (L37). A kind-conditioned test needs an outcome variable at the grain of a decision episode and needs selection to couple differently to differently felt episodes. That is a statistical form of the coincidence claim, available only on the second horn of the article's own fork (L93: "Only on the second horn could a selection have a decision-sized grain at all"), which the article declines to choose. The reconstructed-timing result bites too: a retrospective label has to be aligned to an outcome window, and L57 shows the report is not a reliable timestamp. The article never connects L93 to L109. *Logical gap, medium.*

**1d. "Raises the Map's exposure to a null result" (L37) is true on one reading and false on the other.** See question 2. No experiment is named. L111 says only "A preregistered null at a well-defined kind would count against the Map". The outcome variable, its Born weights, the physical state to be conditioned on and the grain are all unspecified, and P-Q10 ("The Map does not currently possess a worked formal model") means the Born prediction against which a deviation would be measured cannot presently be computed. An exposure with no specifiable experiment is a promissory note. *Overclaim in the lead, high.*

A smaller point in the same sentence: L37 says the hypothesis "leaves exactly one place where a difference could show". P-Q9 says the residue "runs on three channels" (conditional-statistical, mechanism, psychophysical). Applying P-Q9's own wording, "exactly one" is wrong unless restricted to the statistical channel. *Low; five-word fix ("exactly one statistical place").*
