## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
PLUD names the gap at the right level: "This is the **semantic gap**: the analogue, within philosophy of language, of the explanatory gap in philosophy of mind" (L43). The reply to the phenomenal concept strategy (L49–L51) concedes the strategy's best move before answering it: "A physicalist might reply that neural categorisation mechanisms explain this systematicity". It then presses the residue: categorisation "does not explain why those states have qualitative character at all".

Chalmers would also praise PLUD L72–L74 for declining the easy LLM inference. The page says LLMs make the two layers vivid, and calls them "an illustration of the distinction between the layers, not evidence that they come apart." This is the strongest paragraph in the hub, and item 1(c) asks the rest of the page to keep to it.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
The wing does not engage the quantum interface and says so: ST L116, VI L113 and CoT L109 each mark Minimal Quantum Interaction as not engaged. Stapp would welcome the restraint and one result in VI. The case for selection without awareness rests on cueing effects (L70, "These are cueing effects, not tracking"), and the 2015 study is labelled "exogenous object-based attention without awareness". Stapp places mental effort in *voluntarily* sustained attention. Evidence that stimulus-driven object selection runs unconsciously therefore leaves his locus untouched, and it narrows where an attention-based interface could sit (lead below, Ideas for Later).

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
ISA centres the first person without trusting it naively. Its best sentence is L105: "What the framework licenses is *withholding* — declining to convert an absence of reported words into a claim about the presence or absence of experience." It also uses Hurlburt's sampling with its limits stated: "it has narrowed the question being asked rather than escaped the first-person predicament" (L67). Nagel's view that the subjective resists objective capture is applied to the page's own evidence, as well as to the opponents'.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
PLUD's Wittgenstein section (L84–L90) avoids crude substance dualism in the philosophy of language. It accepts that "our *language about* consciousness is socially constituted" while holding that what the language refers to is not. Then: "The public dimension makes communication possible. The private dimension makes the communication *about something*" (L86). This is process-friendly praise of a relational picture of meaning, and it asks for no upgrade in evidential status, so the evidential-status constraint is satisfied.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
There is little here on agency, and the pages do not pretend otherwise. Kane would note VI L109: the sea-of-faces case "supposes that pointing can proceed without conscious attention to the target", and the page stops there: "That would not show conscious attention idle where present". The guard keeps an action-guidance result from becoming an argument that consciousness is idle, which matters to anyone defending conscious agency.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)
PLUD's McGinn section (L112–L116) is the hub's most humble passage: "Other minds—alien or artificial—might have the conceptual resources to articulate what we cannot." It is undercut two paragraphs earlier, where L108 says the qualitative core "resists all possible linguistic expression". McGinn's closure is a limit on *our* concepts. Item 1(b) brings L108 into line with L116 and with `language-thought-boundary`, which says "The boundary likely contains all three" (L129). That page also says the metaphysical reading "is the Map's interpretation, not something this evidence delivers" (L145).

**PLUD L110, Musacchio and the word-anchoring problem. CONTRADICTED in the inference drawn; the framing and citation survive.**
- **What the page says**: "The word-anchoring problem, as Musacchio frames it, asks how phenomenal terms get their reference... Under dualism, the anchoring crosses an ontological boundary... The persistent difficulty of the word-anchoring problem is diagnostic of the ontological gap dualism asserts."
- **What survives**:
  - The citation metadata. Reference 8 is correct; the author was fixed 06-24 and confirmed 08-03.
  - The framing of the problem as how phenomenal terms get their reference.
  - The page does not call Musacchio a dualist.
- **What the author holds** (Musacchio 2012, his own later restatement, which cites the 2005 *Language Sciences* paper as its ref. 33, PDF L931):
  - p. 59 (summary): qualia "are easily understood when we realize that they are neural processes"; they are "ineffable because they cannot be transmitted through explanations".
  - p. 62 (Table 5.1): "Phenomenal-red is both, a neural process and an ineffable qualitative experience that serves to anchor 'red' in the verbal-phenomenal lexicon", and "The word red is anchored in the verbal-phenomenal lexicon by giving a name to the experience".
  - p. 64: the imperceptibility of neural realizers "creates the highly misleading illusion that experiences and the mind are nonphysical entities"; "science indicates that the presumed immateriality of the mind is an illusion"; and "the anti-physicalist intuitions of popular psychology are derived from the imperceptibility of the brain mechanisms".
  - All five strings were grep-verified in the `pdftotext` output. In the p. 64 window the last one breaks across a line as "psychol- ogy".
  - His companion 2005 paper is titled "Why do Qualia and the Mind Seem Nonphysical?" (*Synthese* 147(3), 425–460; OpenAlex-verified title).
- **The defect**: the page frames the problem with an author who answers it physicalistically and explains away the dualist intuition. It then reads the problem's "persistent difficulty" as diagnostic of the gap, and never tells the reader the framer's verdict. This is the `named-idea-cited-to-its-populariser-not-its-coiner` family's sibling: the work is real and the quote is not at issue, but the conclusion is the opposite of the source's.
- **Not checked**: the 2005 *Language Sciences* text itself (ScienceDirect 403). The fix below therefore cites the verified 2012 chapter for Musacchio's verdict and leaves the 2005 entry as the framing citation.
