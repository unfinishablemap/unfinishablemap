# External audit: “Consciousness as Activity Rather Than Property”

## Overall verdict: **major revision**

The September 26 revision is materially stronger than earlier versions. It now acknowledges process-identity physicalism, admits that the whirlpool and dancing analogies do not establish dualism, accurately records James’s and Whitehead’s departures from a persisting-subject ontology, and concedes that enactivism does not settle the dispute in the Map’s favour. Its 13 listed references are real, and I found no fabricated work, nonexistent quotation, or seriously incorrect bibliographic entry. The site’s own September review also correctly verified the new James quotations and the Place, Smart, Clark, Dehaene–Changeux and Nagel entries. [topics/consciousness-as-activity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/consciousness-as-activity/)

The article’s main remaining problem is not citation fraud or even routine citation error. It is that **“activity” performs too many different argumentative jobs**:

- metaphysical process or becoming;
- phenomenological temporal flow;
- embodied sensorimotor engagement;
- neural or computational dynamics;
- intentional agency;
- causal efficacy;
- nonphysical selection of quantum outcomes.

The cited sources support some of these individually. They do not establish their equivalence, and none establishes the chain the article needs:

> consciousness is dynamic → consciousness is an activity → activity requires a persisting performer → that performer is nonphysical → its activity selects quantum outcomes.

Almost every arrow in that chain is contestable. The article often acknowledges the contest locally, but still writes as though the overall convergence favours the Map. Its best-supported conclusion is considerably more modest:

> Consciousness is temporally structured and should not be modelled solely as an instantaneous, static configuration.

That conclusion is philosophically and scientifically defensible. It is also compatible with process physicalism, enactivism, neutral monism, panexperientialism, nonreductive physicalism and interactionist dualism. The article has not shown why it favours the last of these.

## 1. What the article now gets right

Several revisions deserve explicit credit.

First, the article now says that the activity/property contrast does not defeat physicalism by itself. It recognizes that Place, Smart and later token-identity theorists identify mental events or processes with physical events or processes, not with abstract physical properties. That concession is historically correct and removes an elementary straw man. [topics/consciousness-as-activity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/consciousness-as-activity/)

Second, it clearly marks its departures from James, Whitehead and Thompson. James allows the passing thought itself to serve as the thinker; Whitehead’s occasion becomes its own subject-superject and then perishes; and Thompson’s enactive approach does not posit a separate nonphysical agent. Those are no longer being silently recruited into interactionist dualism. [topics/consciousness-as-activity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/consciousness-as-activity/)

Third, it now states that causal engagement is only “defeater-removal,” not a positive mechanism. That is the correct calibration. Merely describing consciousness as an activity does not solve causal exclusion. [topics/consciousness-as-activity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/consciousness-as-activity/)

Fourth, the article is commendably candid that the evidence underdetermines the noun and verb framings. That admission should become the controlling thesis of the page rather than a local disclaimer surrounded by stronger claims.

### The James quotation

The quoted stream passage is authentic, and the ellipsis does not distort it. The later “passing Thought” formulation is also genuine. The significant issue is interpretive: James’s dynamic phenomenology does not deliver the article’s persisting selector. Indeed, the very material cited supplies a prominent alternative—subjecthood constituted by the stream rather than a subject standing behind it. [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/57628/57628-h/57628-h.htm)

### The Whitehead claim

The article says Whitehead avoids the transition from wholly nonexperiential matter to experience. That is a reasonable description of his panexperientialism. But it should not say simply that, for Whitehead, “consciousness” is what actuality does all the way down. What is pervasive in Whitehead is experience or prehension, not reflective human consciousness. That distinction matters because otherwise the page makes Whitehead sound like a supporter of the very macro-conscious subject he replaces with perishing occasions and societies of occasions. [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/processp/)

The further suggestion that “actual-occasion machinery may do work at the quantum interface” is unsupported by the Whitehead citation and not established by the linked Map article. It is a speculative analogy between concrescence and definite outcome formation. The page needs either a worked mapping or an explicit label such as **Map-specific conjecture, not a result drawn from Whitehead**.

## 3. The central conceptual error: activity and property are not exclusive categories

The page’s opening contrast is too coarse. A property need not be:

- static;
- intrinsic;
- spatially attached;
- causally inert;
- momentary;
- separable from the process that instantiates it.

There are dispositional properties, relational properties, temporally extended properties and dynamically realized properties. A physicalist can say that an organism is conscious in virtue of undergoing a certain integrated process, while still using “being conscious” as a property predication. Conversely, an activity has properties: dancing can be graceful, synchronized, energetic or badly timed.

Thus the distinction between the grammar of “having” and “doing” does not map cleanly onto an ontological distinction between properties and processes.

The whirlpool example actually illustrates this. A whirlpool is a dynamically maintained process with numerous properties. Calling whirlpooling an activity does not remove the need to explain why it occurs, what constitutes it or why some fluid configurations count as whirlpools. It merely replaces one legitimate descriptive level with another.

The dancing analogy makes the same mistake more visibly. A physicalist does not need to identify dancing with “a physical property.” The relevant proposal is that a particular dance is a temporally extended physical and social activity constituted by bodily movements, environmental relations, intentions and conventions. The article eventually acknowledges this by admitting process identity, but that concession arrives only after the opening has framed the alternatives as though physicalism must treat consciousness like colour attached to a surface. [topics/consciousness-as-activity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/consciousness-as-activity/)

### The “exactly two families” taxonomy is false

The opening says the standard question invites exactly two families: identity/functionalism or nonphysical property dualism. This omits, among others:

- substance dualism;
- neutral monism and Russellian monism;
- idealism;
- panpsychism and panprotopsychism;
- process monism;
- emergent and nonreductive physicalisms;
- illusionism and eliminativism;
- constitution and realization theories that reject simple identity.

It also conflicts with the Map’s own tenets page, which distinguishes tenet-level neutrality between substance and property interpretations from a downstream, substance-leaning agency cluster. [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

This is not a peripheral taxonomic complaint. The omitted positions include the article’s strongest competitors: theories that make consciousness dynamic or processual without accepting interactionist dualism.

## 4. The hard problem has not been materially reframed

The article says that the property question—

> Why does this physical system have consciousness?

—becomes the activity question—

> What makes certain activities experiential?

But Chalmers’s original formulation already asks why physical **processing**, information processing, neural mechanisms and the performance of cognitive functions are accompanied by experience. He even argues that substituting nonlinear dynamics or quantum processes leaves the explanatory question intact: more dynamics still does not entail experience. [Consciousness Net](https://consc.net/papers/facing.html)

The reformulation is therefore not a new version of the hard problem. It is close to a restatement of it.

This produces an internal site inconsistency. The Map’s own *Hard Problem of Consciousness* page correctly frames the problem as why cascades of neural activity and physical processes are accompanied by experience. That page does not rely on a static-property conception. [topics/hard-problem-of-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/hard-problem-of-consciousness/)

The activity page could still make a valuable narrower claim: static snapshots and purely instantaneous descriptions may be explanatorily inadequate because the relevant physical or phenomenal organization is temporally extended. But that is a claim about the **proper explanans**, not a dissolution or substantial reframing of the explanatory gap.

The assertion that the new question has “more conceptual traction” is unsupported. The page supplies no:

- solved subproblem;
- new discriminating prediction;
- principled measure of experiential activity;
- case in which the property formulation fails but the activity formulation succeeds;
- account of why one dynamic process is conscious while another is not.

Until it does, “more tractable” is a promissory judgement.

## 5. “Activity” conflates five distinct theses

The article presents its historical and scientific traditions as converging, but they support different claims:

| Tradition | What its activity language establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Whiteheadian process metaphysics** | Reality may be fundamentally event-like or processual. | A persisting nonphysical selector; the Map’s quantum mechanism. |
| **Bergsonian and Jamesian phenomenology** | Experience presents as flowing, temporally structured and selective. | That its subject is ontologically separate from neural processes. |
| **Enactivism** | Cognition and perception depend on embodied organism–environment activity. | A performer distinct from the organismic process. |
| **Predictive processing and GNW** | Cognition and conscious access involve dynamic neural/computational organization. | Nonphysical causation or irreducible agency. |
| **The Map’s agency theory** | Consciousness is hypothesized to select quantum outcomes. | That this follows from process, temporality, embodiment or phenomenology. |

The article’s repeated movement between these senses creates an appearance of convergence without a common conclusion. Saying that Whitehead, Bergson, James, enactivism, predictive processing and GNW are all “activity-first” is not yet philosophically informative unless the page specifies what proposition they jointly support.

The plausible shared proposition is only:

> Consciousness, cognition or experience cannot be adequately characterized without temporal and dynamic relations.

That proposition has little discriminatory power between the Map and its rivals.

## 6. Claims resting on insufficient evidence

### 6.1 “Properties don’t strain. Activities do.”

This is rhetorically effective but philosophically weak. Effort can be construed as:

- a phenomenal quality;
- a dispositional or functional state;
- conflict between control systems;
- sustained allocation of cognitive resources;
- an organism-level regulatory process.

Calling effort an activity does not make it inexplicable under physicalism, nor does calling consciousness a property require that effort itself be a static property. James’s phenomenology establishes that voluntary attention can feel effortful; it does not establish that a nonphysical agent supplies the effort. James even discusses whether effortful attention can be understood as a resultant rather than an independent force. [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/57628/57628-h/57628-h.htm)

### 6.2 Temporal extension is asserted too strongly

The page says consciousness cannot exist at an instant and compares an instantaneous brain state to one film frame. This assumes an extensional model of temporal consciousness. Live alternatives include:

- cinematic models, on which experiences are individually nonextended;
- retentional models, on which a momentary experience represents a temporal interval;
- extensional models, on which the experience itself extends.

Contemporary work explicitly treats all three as serious models and explores multiscale combinations. The page can defend extensionalism, but it cannot simply build it into the definition of activity. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2021/2/niab020/6348789)

Moreover, a time-slice could contain structures encoding retention, anticipation and dynamical tendency. Whether that suffices for experience is precisely the disputed issue. The frozen-runner analogy does not decide it.

### 6.3 Agent-dependence does not follow from activity

“Dancing requires a dancer” is grammatically true but ontologically underdetermining. The dancer could be:

- the living organism;
- a temporally extended bodily process;
- an embodied control system;
- a stream that constitutes rather than presupposes its subject;
- a persisting nonphysical substance.

James, Whitehead and enactivism instantiate the alternatives. The Map selects the final answer because its agent-causal theory needs a selector that persists. That explains why the Map adopts the position; it is not evidence that the position is true.

The appeal to unity is similarly underargued. Saying unity “reads more naturally” as one subject acting records an intuition. It does not compare that intuition with integration-based, self-model, higher-order, recurrent-processing or organismic accounts.

### 6.4 Causal efficacy does not follow from activity

The claim that activities are not “naturally read as inert” exploits ordinary action grammar. But:

- an activity can occur without affecting the variable under discussion;
- a causally idle phenomenal process is conceptually coherent;
- a property may be a causal power or disposition;
- higher-level activity descriptions can be causally explanatory without introducing an additional ontological cause.

The article eventually acknowledges that an epiphenomenalist can call experiencing a causally idle process. Once that is conceded, the alleged advantage over a property ontology largely disappears.

### 6.5 The proposed candidate features are neither shown necessary nor sufficient

The page proposes self-referential integration, selective engagement, temporal thickness and creative synthesis. Each needs control cases.

**Self-referential integration:** Basic pain, colour experience or sudden noise awareness may not require recursive self-awareness. Conversely, nonconscious systems can integrate their prior states.

**Selective engagement:** Attention and consciousness dissociate. Stimuli can guide attention and top-down control without awareness, while experiences can occur involuntarily without agent selection. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-15439-x)

**Temporal thickness:** Many nonconscious biological and computational processes are temporally extended.

**Creative synthesis:** Unless “genuinely novel” is operationalized, almost any nonlinear state transition can qualify.

These features may characterize important forms of conscious cognition. The page offers no evidence that they distinguish consciousness from sophisticated nonconscious activity.

### 6.6 The skill-acquisition paragraph lacks direct support

The claim that consciousness “trains procedural systems, delegates to them, and painfully re-engages when skills break down” is an interesting model, but the target article cites only another Map page. It needs direct literature on:

- explicit versus implicit learning;
- automaticity;
- reinvestment and choking;
- error monitoring;
- conscious access during skill breakdown;
- the role of working memory in training and performance.

Even then, the evidence would concern functions of access consciousness and cognitive control, not necessarily phenomenal causation.

## 7. Scientific alignment is real but nondiscriminating

### Predictive processing

Predictive processing is indeed dynamic, recurrent and action-oriented. It is also usually formulated as a naturalistic account employing hierarchical generative models. The article’s “brains don’t have models, they do modelling” slogan imposes a contrast Clark does not accept: on his account, modelling is performed through a generative model. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/whatever-next-predictive-brains-situated-agents-and-the-future-of-cognitive-science/33542C736E17E3D1D44E8D03BE5F4CD9)

More importantly, predictive processing does not imply that consciousness is nonphysical. The activity page correctly admits that Clark’s treatment of consciousness is limited, but then predictive processing can only support a general dynamic orientation—not the page’s ontology.

### Enactivism

The page’s current discussion is comparatively fair. It correctly says that enactivism’s “someone” is the embodied organism, not a nonphysical agent. Thompson’s body–body formulation is especially significant: it seeks continuity between living and lived embodiment and regards a speculative extra ingredient as lacking scientific motivation. [Evan Thompson](https://evanthompson.me/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/thompson_precis_jcs_author_proof.pdf)

The article should therefore describe enactivism as a **direct rival activity ontology**, not as a neighbouring view that nearly reaches the Map’s conclusion. Its existence shows that the move from property to activity does not naturally favour dualism.

### Global neuronal workspace

Dehaene and Changeux’s ignition language supports the limited proposition that conscious access involves temporally evolving, distributed neural coordination. It does not establish that phenomenal consciousness is constituted by “activity” in the Map’s stronger sense.

The empirical presentation is also stale. The 2025 COGITATE adversarial collaboration, involving 256 participants and multiple imaging modalities, found results compatible with some GNWT and IIT predictions but substantially challenged key tenets of both. In particular, GNWT was challenged by the general lack of offset ignition and limited prefrontal representation of some conscious dimensions. This does not falsify GNWT, but a 2026 article should not present the 2011 ignition picture without the update. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40307561/)

A 2024 integrative review also argues that major consciousness theories often address different aspects or mechanistic levels and may be partly compatible. That reinforces the point that generic appeals to dynamics or integration do not uniquely support one metaphysics. [KNAW](https://pure.knaw.nl/ws/files/1314823078/Storm2024.pdf)

## 8. Quantum selection is imported rather than derived

The article’s distinctive proposal appears when “activity” becomes selecting, directing or biasing quantum outcomes. Nothing in the preceding historical or scientific discussion establishes this transition.

To turn activity language into a mind–matter mechanism, the site still needs:

1. a defined physical state space in which the relevant alternatives arise;
2. a criterion identifying the relevant neural quantum events;
3. a law pairing a particular subject with those events;
4. an account of how reasons or intentions determine the bias;
5. an explanation of how the bias remains Born-compatible;
6. a model connecting token selections to stable macroscopic action;
7. a test capable of distinguishing selection from chance.

The Map’s tenets page now candidly says that post-decoherence selection is an interpretive proposal, that no consciousness-conditioned neural test has returned positive, that the interface establishes availability rather than actuality, and that no worked coupling model exists. It also says that articles claiming “real causal work” inherit rather than discharge this debt. [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

Against that background, the activity article’s statement that the exclusion problem is “answered” at the quantum interface is too strong. At most:

> The Map locates a hypothesized opening where a nonduplicative mental contribution might occur, conditional on unresolved assumptions about quantum actualization, neural implementation and subject–event pairing.

Calling selection an activity contributes no new physics. It relabels the proposed influence.

Chalmers’s original warning is directly relevant: replacing ordinary mechanisms with nonlinear or quantum dynamics still leaves the question of why there is experience, unless psychophysical principles are independently supplied. [Consciousness Net](https://consc.net/papers/facing.html)

## 9. The Many-Worlds argument does not gain strength from activity language

The article argues that an activity copied across branches becomes a different activity, reinforcing indexical identity. An Everettian can readily accept that post-branch activities are numerically distinct tokens. The disagreement is whether all branch-relative tokens are real, not whether they are the same activity.

Thus:

- one pre-branch act can have multiple branch-relative continuations;
- those continuations can be distinct acts;
- nothing in that fact implies that only one continuation is actual.

The activity claim therefore does not answer the Everettian. It presupposes the Map’s further commitment to a globally unique persisting subject.

The tenets page now states this dependency more carefully: the indexical objection is only as strong as the non-deflationary “I” it presupposes, weakens if subjecthood is treated as processual, and does not by itself address centred Everettian variants. [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

The target article should inherit that calibration. At present, it risks circularity:

1. activity is said to require a unique performer;
2. the unique performer is used against branching;
3. the rejection of branching is then treated as support for unique performance.

That is mutual framework coherence, not independent support.

## 10. Strongest counterarguments still missing or under-represented

### 10.1 Process physicalism

This is the decisive immediate reply. Consciousness can be an activity identical with, realized by or constituted by neural and organismic activity. The article now mentions this, but only after most of the rhetorical work has been done.

The reply that process identity “helps itself” to the experiential bridge is fair as an explanatory-gap objection. But it is not an objection specifically generated by the activity framing. It is the ordinary hard problem again.

### 10.2 Dynamic and dispositional properties

The article needs a serious account of why “property” must mean static qualitative attachment. Contemporary metaphysics routinely recognizes dynamic, historical, dispositional and process-dependent properties. Until these are considered, the property trap is largely manufactured by definition.

### 10.3 Neutral monism and process monism

James and Whitehead offer especially awkward alternatives for the Map. They reject or bypass the physical/nonphysical split rather than placing a persisting nonphysical performer behind activity. Since these are two of the article’s principal historical roots, their ontologies deserve treatment as live rivals, not merely recorded divergences.

### 10.4 No-subject and constituted-subject accounts

The article considers James’s and Whitehead’s alternatives but does not answer them. It says a persisting selector is needed by the Map’s agency theory and that unity “reads more naturally” that way. A hostile referee will say this is precisely the disputed point.

The page must explain why:

- a stream cannot constitute diachronic subjecthood;
- organismic continuity cannot ground agency;
- memory, character and causal continuity cannot unify action;
- a persisting substance does explanatory work beyond renaming the unity.

### 10.5 Passive consciousness and unconscious activity

The page emphasizes selecting, attending and effort, but much consciousness is apparently passive or involuntary:

- pain;
- intrusive thought;
- afterimages;
- spontaneous emotion;
- dream imagery;
- sensory experience that captures attention.

Conversely, substantial selection, integration, control and temporal processing can occur without awareness. This double dissociation directly challenges the proposed identification of consciousness with selective activity. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-15439-x)

### 10.6 Levels of description without ontological duality

A dance is not exhausted by a joint-angle list, but this does not make it nonphysical. Timing, expression and responsiveness may be relational and organism-level physical facts. The article acknowledges this briefly but does not confront its implication: failure of a lower-level vocabulary to capture a higher-level pattern does not establish ontological irreducibility.

### 10.7 Causal powers of properties

The red-surface example unfairly chooses a property that is plausibly causally inert as such. Physical properties such as charge, mass and elasticity are routinely treated as causally relevant; dispositions are defined through their manifestations. A defender of mental causation can likewise treat mental properties as realized causal powers. The activity/property contrast therefore does not align with the causal/epiphenomenal contrast.

### 10.8 Randomness versus reasons-responsive control

Even if quantum indeterminacy creates causal room, it does not create agency. The mechanism must explain why selection tracks reasons, intentions and long-term identity rather than merely adding stochastic variation. The linked *Consciousness and Agency* page recognizes determinism, randomness, compatibilism and event-causal libertarianism as serious alternatives, but the target article does not inherit those qualifications. [apex/consciousness-and-agency](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/consciousness-and-agency/)

## 11. Tenet-protective bracketing

The page is more transparent than before, but several passages still allow commitments to enter as though they were discoveries.

### 11.1 Agent-causal selection is built into the description of consciousness

The whirlpool caveat says conscious activity includes subjective character, intentionality and agent-causal selection that physical description cannot capture. But agent-causal selection is not an uncontested datum alongside subjective character. It is one of the Map’s downstream theoretical commitments. Putting it inside the initial characterization of conscious activity loads the conclusion into the premise. [topics/consciousness-as-activity](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/consciousness-as-activity/)

### 11.2 The performer is justified by a mechanism that already assumes a performer

The page’s explicit reason for positing a persisting subject is that the Map’s agent-causal selection theory needs a selector persisting across selections. This establishes conditional coherence:

> If agent-causal selection is true in the Map’s preferred form, then a persisting selector may be needed.

It does not establish:

> Conscious activity itself provides evidence for such a selector.

The distinction between framework requirement and independent evidence should be marked.

### 11.3 “Answered” outruns “available”

The target says the exclusion problem is answered by quantum-indeterminate selection. The tenets page says the interface shows that mental causation may be physically available but does not show it to be actual, records no worked toy model and acknowledges empirical indistinguishability problems. [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

This should be treated as a direct calibration conflict.

### 11.4 Under-determination is conceded and then effectively ignored

The article explicitly says that the phenomena underdetermine noun and verb readings. It then says the activity view earns its place through the puzzles it avoids. But the activity view adds or inherits substantial puzzles:

- what performs the activity;
- how the performer is paired with a brain;
- how it persists;
- how its reasons alter quantum probabilities;
- why its contribution is statistically hidden;
- how branch uniqueness is established.

The article counts the property view’s debts without giving an equivalent account of its own.

### 11.5 The review pipeline freezes disputed premises too early

The site’s September deep review did useful citation work and made genuine improvements. But it ended with “Remaining Items: None” and marked the causal wording, the MWI indexical argument and predictive-processing/enactivism issues as “settled” or “bedrock.” [reviews/deep-review-2026-09-26-consciousness-as-activity](https://unfinishablemap.org/reviews/deep-review-2026-09-26-consciousness-as-activity/)

That conflicts with the site’s newer methodological register, which says:

- a tenet can remove a defeater but cannot upgrade evidence;
- convergence remains framework-internal without a distinguishing test;
- the project is an open programme;
- disclosure alone is not self-correction;
- post-decoherence selection has no independent evidence;
- no worked toy model exists. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

“Bedrock” can legitimately mean a disagreement that argument has not resolved. It should not mean exempt from hostile re-examination, especially when the premise is load-bearing.

## 12. Integration with surrounding Map articles

### *The Hard Problem of Consciousness*

This page correctly states the hard problem in terms of physical processes and neural activity. The activity article’s claim that the problem presupposes property framing should be harmonized with it. At present, the two pages offer incompatible diagnoses. [topics/hard-problem-of-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/hard-problem-of-consciousness/)

### *Process Philosophy* and *Process and Consciousness*

These pages say process philosophy replaces enduring substances with events or occasions and can dissolve the mind–matter dichotomy by treating experiential and physical aspects as modes of becoming. The apex then says the Map retains interactionist dualism while borrowing process resources. [concepts/process-philosophy](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/process-philosophy/)

The missing bridge is exactly what the activity article needs: why should an ontology built to replace substance/property dualism support a substance-leaning, mind–body interaction theory? Selective borrowing is permissible, but the compatibility cannot be assumed.

### *William James and Consciousness*

The James page notes his radical empiricism, pure experience and rejection of consciousness as a separate entity or substance. It also recognizes tension between those commitments and the Map’s filter and quantum-selection uses of James. [topics/william-james-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/william-james-consciousness/)

The activity page should make this tension central. James is evidence for processual phenomenology, but not straightforward evidence for the Map’s ontology.

### *Bergson and Duration*

The Bergson page says durée gives the Map’s dualism “independent philosophical support.” That is too strong. Lived temporal flow may challenge simplistic snapshot reduction, but moving from phenomenological nonspatiality to ontological nonphysicality requires a separate argument. [topics/bergson-and-duration](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/bergson-and-duration/)

The activity article currently inherits the same slide.

### *Temporal Consciousness: Structure and Agency*

This page treats temporal structure as required for consciousness and links it to quantum selection. It should engage cinematic and retentional alternatives rather than treating extensional duration as settled. It also needs to separate temporal integration from agent initiation: a process can integrate across time without anyone deciding when its integration occurs. [topics/temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/temporal-consciousness-structure-and-agency/)

The target article is more calibrated than its further-reading gloss. It admits that enactivism’s phenomenology does not settle the ontology in the Map’s favour. But the linked page still says the evidence suggests enactivism’s dissolution fails, and the activity page describes that challenge as “ultimately” reinforcing dualism. [topics/enactivism-challenge-to-interactionist-dualism](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/enactivism-challenge-to-interactionist-dualism/)

The stronger conclusion is not earned. At most, enactivism has not compelled all parties to accept dissolution of the explanatory gap.

This concept is explicitly a diagnostic for theories that make an agent load-bearing while declining to theorize it. The activity article is itself a prime candidate: its selector performs the decisive work, yet its nature, persistence, pairing and causal law are not supplied. [concepts/the-agent-shaped-hole](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/the-agent-shaped-hole/)

Those caveats should propagate automatically into the activity article. A cross-link should not allow the target page to use “agent-causal selection” as though the linked page had established it.

### *Quantum Measurement and Subjective Probability*

This page diagnoses agent-dependence in QBism but does not demonstrate that Whiteheadian concrescence supplies a physical outcome-selection mechanism. [topics/quantum-measurement-and-subjective-probability](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/quantum-measurement-and-subjective-probability/)

The activity article’s statement that quantum measurement is “closing on precisely this point” is therefore too suggestive. The convergence is thematic: both involve transition to determinacy. It is not yet theoretical or mathematical.

## 13. Reference currency

The historical references are not stale merely because they are old. Bergson, James, Whitehead, Nagel, Place and Smart are primary historical sources and remain appropriate.

The scientific support is another matter.

1. **GNW:** Dehaene and Changeux 2011 remains foundational, but the article needs the 2025 adversarial test and a cautious distinction between conscious access and phenomenal consciousness. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40307561/)

2. **Theory comparison:** A current review such as Storm and colleagues’ 2024 integrative treatment would prevent the misleading impression that “dynamic” findings uniformly converge on one activity ontology. [KNAW](https://pure.knaw.nl/ws/files/1314823078/Storm2024.pdf)

3. **Temporal consciousness:** The page needs modern work comparing cinematic, retentional and extensional models and multiscale temporal frameworks. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2021/2/niab020/6348789)

4. **Attention and selection:** The proposed selective-engagement criterion must be tested against literature on unconscious attention, control and semantic processing. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-15439-x)

5. **Predictive processing:** Clark 2013/2016 remains foundational, but newer work specifically addressing whether predictive-processing architectures explain phenomenal consciousness is needed. Otherwise predictive processing is merely evidence that cognition is dynamic.

6. **Skilled activity:** The expertise claims need direct contemporary empirical references rather than only a Map cross-link.

## 14. Final assessment

The article contains a worthwhile corrective to static, snapshot-oriented thinking about consciousness. Its strongest contributions are:

- emphasizing temporal organization;
- bringing process philosophy into contact with contemporary consciousness research;
- recognizing that conscious cognition involves unfolding patterns rather than isolated states;
- showing how activity language cuts across several traditions;
- honestly recording that those traditions do not agree with the Map’s ontology.

But the article currently overstates what follows from those observations. The verb shift does not substantially reframe Chalmers’s problem, because the problem was already posed in terms of physical processing and functioning. It does not establish a performer over and above the organismic or neural process. It does not confer causal efficacy. It does not supply a quantum selection law. And it does not favour dualism over process physicalism, enactivism or neutral/process monism.

A defensible revised thesis would be:

> Consciousness is best investigated as a temporally extended, dynamically organized phenomenon rather than solely through instantaneous state descriptions. This methodological process orientation is metaphysically underdetermining. Interactionist dualism is one possible interpretation, but it requires independent arguments for an irreducible subject, mental causation and a mind–matter interface.

That thesis would be less rhetorically dramatic but considerably more robust.

## Concrete potential improvements

1. **“Consciousness as Activity” — replace the opening taxonomy.** Remove “exactly two families” and provide a compact map including process physicalism, substance and property dualism, neutral/Russellian monism, idealism, panpsychism, enactivism and illusionism.

2. **Define the central terms.** Add a box distinguishing property, event, process, activity, action, disposition and performance. State explicitly that processes can have properties and properties can be dynamic.

3. **Retract the Nagel–Chalmers presupposition claim.** Replace it with the accurate point that their formulations leave open whether the relevant physical basis is a state, process or activity.

4. **Acknowledge that Chalmers already asks the activity question.** Explain that “why is this activity experiential?” is the hard problem restated, not yet made more tractable.

5. **Move the Place–Smart objection to the beginning.** Present process-identity physicalism as the first and strongest reply, not as a late qualification after the framing has done its rhetorical work.

6. **Disaggregate “activity.”** Give separate subsections for process metaphysics, phenomenal temporality, embodied engagement, neural dynamics, intentional action and quantum selection.

7. **Replace the convergence claim with a comparison matrix.** For each cited tradition, identify the exact shared proposition and the points at which it diverges from the Map.

8. **Downgrade “supports dualism.”** Say that activity language is compatible with dualism but does not independently raise its probability over process physicalism or enactivism.

9. **Reframe the whirlpool analogy.** Use it to show that some entities are dynamically constituted, while explicitly noting that it supplies no argument for irreducibility or nonphysicality.

10. **Rewrite the dancing analogy.** Distinguish irreducibility of description, multiple realization, higher-level explanation and ontological nonreducibility.

11. **Delete or defend “Properties don’t strain.”** Replace the slogan with a discussion of phenomenal effort, cognitive control and the causal-powers response.

12. **Expand the James section.** Make “the passing thought as thinker” and James’s later neutral-monist position full rival accounts, rather than brief disclaimers.

13. **Correct the Whitehead section.** Distinguish experience from consciousness, emphasize the perishing of actual occasions, and label the quantum-interface application as Map-specific speculation.

14. **Operationalize the candidate features.** For self-reference, selection, temporal thickness and creative synthesis, specify predicted conscious cases, unconscious controls, necessity conditions and possible falsifiers.

15. **Add passive-consciousness counterexamples.** Test the activity account against pain, involuntary emotion, intrusive thoughts, dreams, afterimages and passive perception.

16. **Add unconscious-activity counterexamples.** Address unconscious attention, integration, control, prediction, sequence processing and skilled automaticity.

17. **Revise the temporal-constitution section.** Engage cinematic, retentional and extensional models rather than assuming extensionalism.

18. **Correct the predictive-processing slogan.** Replace “brains don’t have models” with language faithful to Clark’s hierarchical generative-model account.

19. **Update the GNW discussion.** Add the 2025 COGITATE results, distinguish ignition at stimulus onset and offset, and separate conscious access from phenomenal consciousness.

20. **Add contemporary theory-comparison literature.** Use integrative reviews to show that recurrent dynamics, global access, integration and prediction may concern different explanatory levels.

21. **Source the expertise paragraph directly.** Add empirical work on implicit learning, automaticity, error monitoring, choking, reinvestment and conscious cognitive control—or remove the paragraph.

22. **Rewrite the causal-engagement claim.** Say activity language makes causal interpretation intuitive but does not itself defeat epiphenomenalism, causal exclusion or realization-based physicalism.

23. **Change “answered by the selection mechanism” to “assigned to a hypothesized interface.”** Import the tenets page’s concessions about availability versus actuality, statistical indistinguishability and absence of a worked coupling model.

24. **Give the quantum proposal a mechanism-debt box.** List the missing coupling law, neural locus, subject-pairing rule, reasons-to-bias mapping, amplification path and distinguishing test.

25. **Remove the suggestion that Whitehead supplies quantum machinery** unless a separate article produces an explicit mapping from actual occasions to quantum states, basis selection, probabilities and actualization.

26. **Rewrite the Many-Worlds paragraph.** Address the branch-relative reply: duplicated activities can be distinct while all remain real. Make the dependence on a non-deflationary unique subject explicit.

27. **Harmonize *The Hard Problem of Consciousness*.** Cross-link a statement that the hard problem already concerns physical processes; define the activity article as proposing a better explanandum, not a new problem.

28. **Revise *Process Philosophy* and *Process and Consciousness*.** Explain how resources from process monism or panexperientialism can be imported into a substance-leaning interactionist framework without importing their rejection of enduring substances.

29. **Revise *Bergson and Duration*.** Replace “independent philosophical support” for dualism with the weaker claim that durée challenges some reductive and snapshot-based explanatory strategies.

30. **Revise *William James and Consciousness*.** Foreground radical empiricism and thought-as-thinker whenever James is cited in support of agent selection.

31. **Revise *Temporal Consciousness: Structure and Agency*.** Separate temporal integration from agency and incorporate cinematic, retentional, extensional and multiscale models.

32. **Revise *Enactivism’s Challenge*.** Remove the suggestion that enactivism “ultimately reinforces” dualism. State that it preserves the phenomenological explanandum while offering a competing organismic ontology.

33. **Self-apply *The Agent-Shaped Hole*.** Add a section testing whether the Map’s persisting selector is itself a load-bearing, under-theorized agent primitive.

34. **Make *Consciousness and Agency* caveats inheritable.** Pages invoking agent causation should automatically reproduce its warnings about compatibilism, luck, random indeterminacy and the absence of a worked mechanism.

35. **Revise *Quantum Measurement and Subjective Probability*.** Distinguish a thematic analogy between actualization and experience from a demonstrated physical or metaphysical bridge.

36. **Create a claim–source entailment ledger.** Grade each important citation as “supports,” “is compatible with,” “provides an analogy,” “is appropriated against the author’s ontology,” or “contradicts.”

37. **Separate evidence labels visibly.** Mark claims as empirical result, historical interpretation, phenomenological datum, tenet assumption, framework-internal inference or speculative mechanism.

38. **Require a strongest-rival section.** Every synthesis article should reconstruct the best process-physicalist or enactivist version before advancing a Map-specific conclusion.

39. **Institute a literature-currency trigger.** Empirical sections whose newest primary source is more than roughly five years old should receive a mandatory update review.

40. **Do not treat “bedrock” as review-exempt.** Bedrock should mean that disagreement remains unresolved, not that load-bearing premises are settled or need no further hostile review.

41. **Add automatic cross-page contradiction checks.** Flag cases where one article calls an issue “answered” while a tenet or position entry calls it merely available, untested or model-free.

42. **Audit convergence for shared roots and equivocation.** James, Bergson, Whitehead, enactivism and predictive processing should not count as independent convergence unless they support the same proposition for independent reasons.

43. **Require a discriminatory output.** Either state what consciousness-as-activity predicts that process physicalism does not, or label the proposal explicitly as a heuristic redescription rather than an explanatory theory.
