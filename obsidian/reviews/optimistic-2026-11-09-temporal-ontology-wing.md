## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The wing's best statement of the temporal explanatory gap is now on the apex, and it is the honest version. `time-consciousness-growing-block` L179 routes the dualist case through "the temporal form of the explanatory gap, not the circularity claim set aside above", after L131 has retired the strong circularity argument because "the cosmic arrow is objective and prebiotic: physics presupposes nothing consciousness supplies at the cosmological level." `temporal-consciousness` L106 keeps the focused form of the argument ("no collection of instantaneous states contains the experience of succession") and L178 is candid about its evidential standing: "It is presently empirically inert—its value is philosophical." `philosophy-of-time` L185 states the constraint any deflationary metaphysics must meet without overclaiming: "A metaphysics that dismisses temporal phenomenology as mere confusion must explain why the confusion is so systematic and universal."

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

What Stapp would praise here is that the wing no longer leans on his mechanism harder than its numbers allow. `time-collapse-and-agency` L135 puts Tegmark's decoherence figures (10⁻¹³–10⁻²⁰ s) against the Zeno re-measurement requirement and concludes the question "remains formally open but, on Tegmark's numbers, currently looks unfavourable at the neural scale." The apex L99 records the Map's registered preference for forward-in-time post-decoherence selection and labels the atemporal route "subordinate", pointing to P-Q1 and P-A3 by name. `collapse-and-time` L61 keeps the one point that survives any decoherence estimate (decoherence "doesn't solve the measurement problem … consciousness remains a candidate selector") without inflating it.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Husserl's retention–impression–protention structure is presented three times in this wing and each presentation keeps the phenomenology distinct from the quantum gloss placed on it. The apex L113 is exemplary: after the mapping onto collapsed/collapsing/uncollapsed states, "The felt texture of each phase fits this mapping without evidencing it." `consciousness-and-the-ontology-of-temporal-becoming` L83–85 takes Prosser and Hoerl seriously as "a standing rival, not a view convicted of error", and locates the Map's disagreement narrowly: the refined B-theorist "concedes that the felt specialness of the present tracks nothing in temporal ontology, and the Map judges that concession, not a demonstrated mistake, too high a price." `temporal-consciousness` L130 keeps protention apart from prediction ("Predictive processing models … capture the computational dimension of anticipation but leave untouched the phenomenological one").

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead's concrescence appears on five of the seven pages, and the wing uses it the right way: as a precedent showing the picture "does not depend on quantum mechanics for its coherence" (apex L153), not as evidence. The decisive sentence is `consciousness-and-the-ontology-of-temporal-becoming` L101: "For Whitehead the leading edge is experiential through and through; the Map takes the narrower view that it is experiential only where minds are." That is process-philosophical compatibility declined as a licence for panpsychist widening — exactly the restraint this persona's constraint asks for. (The driver addendum on task L2231 asks `philosophy-of-time` to make the same point where it uses the Whitehead parallel; that page's L165–169 still read concrescence as consciousness-specific. Already queued.)

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

The agency material is where the wing has most visibly matured. The apex L141 says the Map's "main reply does not depend on" the atemporal reading: "the mechanism-agnostic initiation/selection distinction … carries the argument either way." `time-collapse-and-agency` L107 is careful that "the felt asymmetry is evidence that the asymmetry is *experienced*; it does not by itself select between consciousness *constituting* the asymmetry and consciousness *registering* an asymmetry grounded elsewhere." The apex L107 ("Why only the seed?") is a genuine argumentative advance: a readiness-potential ramp "comprises many separately completed transactions … and no single transaction spans it", so "Fixing the whole ramp would need those neural states to stay uncollapsed throughout" — and it concludes, against the Map's own earlier framing, that "The difference from the forward route is therefore smaller than the Map once claimed". Kane would welcome a libertarian account that shrinks its own retrocausal dependency.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`non-temporal-consciousness` is the wing's model of marking framework boundaries rather than claiming victories. L89: the two readings of meditative timelessness "are observationally equivalent" at the level of report. L91: "What tilts toward the non-temporal reading is the independent regress argument, not the contemplative evidence." L136: the eternalist dispute "is bedrock: both sides agree on the formalism and disagree on its ontological reading, and the dispute cannot be settled from inside physics." `consciousness-and-the-ontology-of-temporal-becoming` L149 declines to dissolve the non-rate form of the rate-of-passage objection: "The Map does not claim to dissolve this version … The non-rate form remains a live cost." `temporal-consciousness` L182 on the seeming-regress: "It marks where the Map and illusionism part on foundations rather than refuting the position inside its own framework."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

No minimal-organism boundary case appears in this wing, so the five-tier scale is not directly exercised, but the register discipline the scale protects is everywhere. The apex has a dedicated "Evidence and Dependency" section (L163–173) opening with "their convergence should not be read as several independent confirmations of one conclusion", and L185 states the No-Many-Worlds relation correctly: "Lived time does not decide between the views." `non-temporal-consciousness` counts its evidence honestly twice over — L95 "the cross-tradition cluster carries the evidential weight of *one* recurring pattern, not five independent confirmations", and L140 "the regress is Husserl's own transcendental argument *for* the absolute flow, counting once" — and L83 classifies Frischhut's Extended Now Interpretation as "a deflationary rival to this article's stronger atemporal-ground reading, not a corroboration of it." `time-collapse-and-agency` L185 brings the disruptive states (anaesthesia, slow-wave sleep, dementia) alongside the contemplative ones and concedes "The 'suspended-process → suspended-flow' inference is available to both sides." `philosophy-of-time` L211 marks its contemplative support as post-hoc: "Current reports fit, but were known before these predictions were drawn." `consciousness-and-the-ontology-of-temporal-becoming` L111 says of the consciousness-collapse tradition that it "is a minority interpretation of the measurement problem", and L121 downgrades the "removing consciousness removes the phenomenological arrow" consideration to "a consistency check on the first two considerations, not an independent argument." Where the Process Philosopher praised L101, this persona praises the same sentence: the wing resolves the tension between the two personas honestly.
