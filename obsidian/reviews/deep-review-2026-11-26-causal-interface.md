## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found (all resolved)

1. **Historical conflation (L62).** "Descartes gestured toward the pineal gland" was set out as his answer to Elisabeth. His reply of 21 May 1643 appeals to the soul–body union as a basic (Shapiro: "primitive") notion. "Pineal", "gland", "conarion" and "brain" occur nowhere in the Bennett text of the correspondence. (The only "gland" hits were "England".) The pineal seat comes from *The Passions of the Soul* (1649), arts. 31–32. The paraphrase of Elisabeth's question ("lacking size, shape, or location, make contact with a material brain") was also off. She asks how a soul "only a thinking substance" can affect "the bodily spirits", and she grounds the difficulty in contact and extension. **Fix**: rewrote the question and the reply faithfully, put the pineal gland in the 1649 *Passions*, and linked [[history-of-the-interaction-problem]].
2. **Wrong venue (Ref 7, Horgan 2011).** The page cited Horgan 2011 to *The Oxford Handbook of Free Will*. The paper is "The Phenomenology of Agency and Freedom: Lessons from Introspection and Lessons from Its Limits", *Humana.Mente* 15 (2011): 77–97. The issue PDF's table of contents lists it at p. 77. Prior reviews called this entry "real-correct" on 02-08, 06-13 and 07-19.
3. **Thin-phenomenology claim set against its own cited author (L86).** The page said "Phenomenological analysis reveals that agency has thin phenomenology" and then cited Horgan, who in the cited paper calls agentive phenomenology "much more pervasive" than effort of will. **Fix**: the page now reports Horgan's pervasive view with his verbatim phrase ("the what-it's-like of self as source of the motion"). It also states his limits-of-introspection thesis: introspection cannot settle whether that phenomenology has libertarian or compatibilist satisfaction conditions. And it gives his stance: he defends package-deal compatibilism ("good news for compatibilists, myself included"). The void claim now rests on what Horgan actually argues.
4. **Dropped qualifier and contested interpretation stated as definition (Libet, L78–80).** "Readiness potential (brain activity preparing for movement)" took the contested reading as a definition. The 550 ms figure also lost its scope ("spontaneous acts involving no preplanning", per the 1985 abstract). The line "the robust finding is that we cannot introspect the *moment* of causation … aware of having willed, not of willing" was the Map's reconstruction presented as an experimental finding. **Fix**: restored the qualifier, marked the readiness potential as Libet's reading, and added Schurger, Sitt & Dehaene (2012), which models the build-up as averaged spontaneous fluctuations. The unsupported "robust finding" was replaced with a real one: Banks & Isham (2009) showed that the reported decision time is "largely inferred from the apparent time of response". The quotes around "veto window" were removed: Libet's abstract has "veto" but not "veto window". The 150 ms figure comes from the abstract.
5. **Unsourced empirical claim contradicted by a primary source (L100).** "Meditators across traditions report the same finding. Sustained attention on the moment of decision reveals less and less." This had no source, and the seeding note's own "Gaps in Research" section lists contemplative reports on willing as a gap. Mahasi Sayadaw's progressive-practice instructions report the reverse for intention: it becomes *more* distinct, and the meditator "experiences directly that a bodily process takes place after a preceding intention". **Fix**: rewrote the paragraph around what that text actually says. Attention sharpens intention, movement and their order, and finds succession between them rather than a connection. The no-self reflection is given verbatim. The "millennia of contemplative practice have not resolved this problem" line (L150) was recast to match.
6. **Banned construct and an internal overclaim (L102).** "it is not that the observation fades. It is that there was nothing to observe." This is the "This is not X. It is Y." form. "Nothing to observe" also sat badly with the Map's view that the interface exists. **Fix**: "no observation of the connecting was ever made."
7. **Physics framing contradicting the tenet text (L110–112).** "Scales where observation is inherently limited", "minimal precisely to … remain below experimental detection thresholds" and "A small interaction leaves small traces" all treat the opacity as a sensitivity limit. `tenets.md` says the indistinguishability holds "by construction, not by any sensitivity limit" (Born-conformity), and that a conditioned test remains possible and has not been run. `voids/amplification-void` calls the "too small to detect" argument "analytic" and says it "predicts nothing". **Fix**: the page now gives the Born-conformity account, says the opacity explains only the outside half of the void and adds nothing for the tenet, and notes the conditioned-test opening. L158 was aligned to match: MQI "entails the outside half of this void", where it had said the tenet entailed the whole void and called it "proportionally difficult to observe".
8. **Non-discriminating comparison presented as suggestive (What AI Might See).** The section compared AI's third-person inspectability with human first-person opacity and inferred "the opacity is consciousness-specific". It held neither access route fixed. Brains are also inspectable from outside in principle. And from inside, Claude 3.5 Haiku described the carry algorithm for 36+59 while its attribution graphs show parallel approximate and exact pathways (Lindsey et al. 2025, quoted verbatim). The first-person process/product gap therefore appears without presumed experience, which tells *against* the page's inference. **Fix**: rewrote the section, linked [[chain-of-thought-as-verbal-protocol]] (which uses the same case and the same scope limit, "for this capability"), and stated what a discriminating comparison would need.
9. **Necessity claim (L156).** "The causal interface void shows why that commitment must be chosen rather than discovered" conflicts with the page's own challenge conditions 2 and 4 and with the conditioned-test opening in the tenet. **Fix**: "together, the two voids explain why, for now, that commitment is adopted rather than discovered."

### Medium Issues Found (resolved)

- **Nisbett & Wilson overstated (L70, L72).** "Established that people cannot reliably identify…" overstated the paper, and "…but never the processes" contradicted it. The abstract says "suggests that there may be little or no direct introspective access" and that reports are accurate when the influential stimuli are salient and plausible. Both now follow the abstract. The page keeps the content/process confusion, which matches the paper's section heading "Confusion Between Content and Process".
- **Physicalist reply asserted the contested premise (L94).** "We have maximal epistemic access to consciousness" sat badly with the page's own Nisbett–Wilson section. **Fix**: removed "maximal" and the duplicated closing sentence. The reply is now marked as holding only if willing is a conscious act. It states the physicalist's answer (subpersonal opacity, plus the LLM self-report case) and places the dispute at the framework boundary.
- **Map-internal misdescription (L124).** The page said `self-maintained-cognitive-limits` explores limits "imposed by design—by evolution, by the structure of reality, or … constructors". That page's thesis is "limits where consciousness itself is the jailer": self-deception and motivated avoidance, with external occlusion as one of four models. Now described accurately.
- **"Testably speculative" followed by "may be impossible" (L128).** Self-undercutting; now "speculation, perhaps untestable".
- **Interoceptive paragraph (L114).** "The most plausible region" and "the substrate consciousness is in closest contact with" were unsupported. Now "a natural region to look", which matches the wording on [[interoceptive-void]] ("the natural place to look").
- **Wegner's stance was missing (cited-author leg).** The page now says Wegner himself left open whether intentions are causes ("may or may not be causes", BBS précis 2004), so his model gives the Map's causal claim no support.
- **Orphan references.** SEP *Mental Causation* and SEP *Quantum Approaches* were uncited inline, and so was Descartes 1641 *Meditations*. All three were removed or replaced.
- **L44.** "the causal-impression-void *shows*" became "*argues*", matching that page's own "On the Map's reading".
- **L54.** "The more precisely you look, the less you find" became "Closer looking sharpens the two ends and still finds nothing between them", to stay consistent with the Mahasi evidence.
