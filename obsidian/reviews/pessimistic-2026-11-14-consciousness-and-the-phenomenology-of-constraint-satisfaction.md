## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

"Look at what your own reply section concedes. At L74 you grant that neural valuation circuits explain *which* constraints feel heavy. At L76 you grant that different subsystems explain why the constraint types feel different. At L104 your own speculation says grammar feels rail-like *because* it engages structured linguistic systems. That is my programme working, three times over. All that is left is the generic hard problem, which you bring to every topic, and the constraint domain adds nothing to it. You call the domain a 'microcosm' of the hard problem. A microcosm is a smaller copy, so it is not extra evidence. Yet L46 and L108 still present those very distribution facts as trouble for computation." (The general "just another computation" reply is bedrock. The point here is narrower: the article's own concessions are inconsistent with two of its conclusions. See Issues 2 and 3.)

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

"'These are not metaphors for computational states; they are descriptions of what constraint navigation actually feels like from the inside' (L48). Nobody said they were metaphors. A type-identity theorist says the felt precariousness *is* the computational state, described from the inside, so the denial does no work. Your account of resolution claims more than the evidence gives: 'the recognition and the feeling are aspects of a single conscious event' (L64). Danek & Wiley (2017) found that false insights exist. Wrong answers arrive with the same pleasure, suddenness and certainty, if weaker on average. So the 'click' is a separable signal that can misfire, and it does not mark constraint satisfaction. Your own Map says so: 'Insight signals do not select for truth' (`voids/noetic-feelings-void` L137). Then L74: 'no such account has been provided.' Global-workspace and higher-order theories are exactly accounts of why some valuation is conscious and cerebellar processing is not. You may find them inadequate, but they exist."

### The Quantum Skeptic

"L102 maps the felt narrowing onto 'what the Map proposes physically', which is quantum selection at the attentional interface. Constraint satisfaction unfolds over seconds or minutes, as in writing a sentence or working a crossword. Any selection event at a decohered neural site is many orders of magnitude shorter. A felt 'click' spanning hundreds of milliseconds is not obviously the signature of one event. It would need the amplification story, and the page does not give it." The paragraph already hedges ("a suggestive convergence ... not a demonstration that the phenomenology proves the metaphysics"), so this is a missing pointer, not an overclaim. A link to `topics/amplification-mechanisms-consciousness-physics` would answer it at small cost. Not ranked.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

"The 2026-09-14 rewrite of the Tenet 4 paragraph (L106) is honest: it concedes that within-branch resolution is consistent with branching. But the paragraph now says nothing about constraint satisfaction that bears on Tenet 4. It just points to the tenets." Bedrock is recorded, and the paragraph is not defective. ### The Empiricist

"L94 says the three-phase pattern 'matches the control-theoretic prediction'. What result would have contradicted it? If attending to an expert skill had *improved* it, the Map would say consciousness helps. Standard physicalist skill-acquisition models also predict engagement during learning, delegation in mastery, and interference on re-engagement. Anderson's (1982) proceduralisation account of skill acquisition (*Psychological Review* 89(4), 369–406) is one. A pattern that every rival predicts cannot count as 'support' (L100). Worse, the cited lab found the pattern does *not* generalise to cognitive problem solving. In mathematics, pressure harmed only unpracticed, working-memory-heavy problems, and practice *eliminated* choking (Beilock et al. 2004)." See Issues 1 and 4. The introspection-methodology objection is bedrock and is not repeated.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

"Your criterion for phenomenal weight is 'how the constraints bear on what matters to the agent' (L70). Your leftover incompleteness after a trade-off (L86) is 'a specific shade of dissatisfaction'. In my terms, the heavy constraints are the ones bound up with self-view, and the leftover ache is *dukkha*. The phenomenology fits dependent arising at least as well as a selector." Selector-reification is bedrock. The new point is only that "personal significance" assumes a self for whom things matter. The page could say in one clause that this is the Map's reading of the data and not something the data show. Not ranked.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The automatisation pattern is presented as refuting epiphenomenalism; the Map's own empirical page and the sibling's 2026-11-04 rescope say it does not
- **File**: `topics/consciousness-and-the-phenomenology-of-constraint-satisfaction.md`
- **Location**: L100 (Relation to Site Perspective) and the L94 premise "watching doesn't interfere"
- **Problem**: L100 reads: "The Map's commitment to [[interactionist-dualism]] gains support from the asymmetric automatisation pattern. If consciousness were [[concepts/epiphenomenalism|epiphenomenal]], it could neither usefully engage with novel constraints nor disruptively re-engage with automatised ones. Both directions of causal influence — helpful engagement and harmful choking — require consciousness to be doing genuine work." This is a refutation claimed inside the opponent's own framework, and that framework absorbs the data. The page L92 links as "choking phenomenon", `topics/empirical-phenomena-mental-causation`, says epiphenomenalism "absorbs content-specificity comfortably" (L147). It calls the dispute "a framework-boundary disagreement rather than an in-framework refutation" and says "The phenomena are compatible with both" (L153). It also says "The two cases establish less than the dualist reading needs" (L165). On 2026-11-04 both outer reviewers flagged the weaker claim "must treat as primitive" on `topics/phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity`, and that page was rescoped (synthesis C16). Its L101 now reads: "an account on which consciousness is causally inert can say that the *neural* re-engagement of attentional circuitry degrades the skill while the experience of attending merely accompanies that change. What the case supplies is a fit, not an anomaly". This page makes the stronger claim and was not swept. P-MC1 also records that the Map's strongest anti-epiphenomenalism argument is self-stultification, and that even it binds only the bare-correlation version. The L94 premise repeats the slide: "If consciousness were merely observing constraint satisfaction, re-engagement should be neutral — watching doesn't interfere." The epiphenomenalist places the interference in the neural monitoring that the watching accompanies.
- **Verification**: Beilock & Carr (2001), PubMed 11757876, abstract: "In putting, choking was unchanged by dual-task training but eliminated by self-consciousness training. These findings support explicit monitoring theories of choking". Explicit monitoring is a mechanism stated in terms of attention, and a physicalist can adopt it.
- **Severity**: High. This is the page's main Tenet 3 claim, and it outruns the Map's own grade.
- **Recommendation**: Regrade to "fit, not proof" and match the sibling's wording. See Priority item 1.

### Issue 2: L108 says the salience-computation exchange "shows" the reverse of what that exchange concedes
- **Location**: L108 (closing sentence of Relation to Site Perspective), against L74
- **Problem**: L74 concedes: "The materialist can explain *which* constraints feel heavy and which feel light by pointing to neural valuation circuits. What remains unexplained is why this valuation has a felt quality in the first place." L108, as rewritten on 2026-09-14 (commit 8b88ebe03c), reads: "The disconnect between computational complexity and phenomenal intensity is unexplained by any view identifying consciousness with computation, as the salience-computation exchange above shows, and hospitable to the Map's dualism". The disconnect is exactly the fact that intensity tracks significance rather than complexity, and L74 grants that salience computation explains it. The exchange leaves only the existence question open. The trailing clause, "a consciousness evaluating constraints through its own non-physical perspective would weight personal significance over logical complexity", also discriminates nothing, because the salience account predicts the same weighting. The 2026-09-14 refine fixed the earlier "explains this naturally" and introduced this misreport while doing so.
- **Severity**: High. It is an internal contradiction, and it sits in the section an LLM reader is most likely to quote as the Map's position.
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 2.

### Issue 3: The differentiation argument (L46) is withdrawn by L76 and L104, and it rests on a false claim about constraint solvers
- **Location**: L46
- **Problem**: "If constraint processing were purely computational, there would be no reason for different constraint types to feel different. A constraint-satisfaction algorithm treats all constraints identically — as conditions to be satisfied or weighted." The page itself supplies the reason that this says does not exist. L76 grants that different subsystems "explain[] why they feel different" ("it explains the *differentiation* of phenomenal character while deepening the mystery of why there is phenomenal character to differentiate"). L104 says "Grammatical constraints feel rail-like because they engage the brain's highly structured linguistic systems." The conclusion, "not through a single uniform processing mechanism", targets a view no materialist holds. The claim about algorithms is also false as stated. Soft-constraint and weighted frameworks treat hard and soft constraints differently, and global constraints get dedicated propagators. The "Soft Constraints" chapter of the *Handbook of Constraint Programming* (2006; Crossref DOI 10.1016/S1574-6526(06)80013-1) surveys this. The phrase "or weighted" in the sentence itself admits non-uniform treatment.
- **Severity**: Medium-High. This is the first argument the page makes, and the reader meets it before the concession at L76.
- **Recommendation**: Merged into Priority item 2 (same pass, same fix logic).

### Issue 4: The Zheng & Meister figure is used for a claim the paper does not make, and the paper's own picture runs against the "high-N" reading
- **Location**: L54 (the candidate-generation clause) and L56's "high-N"
- **Problem**: L54 reads: "the brain's unconscious processing generates candidate solutions at enormous bandwidth — the sensory periphery alone has a raw capacity of ~10⁹ bits/second". The page's own References gloss says the figure "is the channel capacity of one eye's cone population (~1.6 Gbit/s) rather than any measure of total neural processing". So it measures sensory input, not candidate generation. The paper puts its parallel, high-rate processing in the sensory and motor "outer brain". It describes the decision stage as serial. Verified in the arXiv v2 raw text: "central processing appears to be strictly serial"; "Even in tasks that do not require any motor output, such as thinking, we can pursue only one strand at a time"; and, of chess planning, "even grandmasters contemplate possible moves serially, one at a time" (§7.2). The inner brain "operates on a dramatically reduced data stream, filtered to the essential few bits that matter for behavior at any one moment. The challenge for the inner brain is to combine the animal's goals with current inputs from the world and previous memories to make decisions" (§8). §5.3 ("But what about unconscious processing?") rejects the high-rate unconscious objection it considers. So the cited source gives no figure for how fast candidates are generated, and on its account deliberative options are weighed one at a time. That sits badly with L56: "The felt narrowing of constraint satisfaction is what a high-N candidate set collapsing toward a single actualised solution feels like from the inside."
- **Scope note**: The open P3 on L54 covers only "conscious throughput". This defect is in the clause before it. Its verify-first step already fetches this paper.
- **Severity**: Medium-High (source fidelity).
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 3. Fold it into the open P3.

### Issue 5: Sensorimotor choking is generalised to "the constraint domain", but the cited lab found the reverse in cognitive problem solving
- **Location**: L94 (final sentence) and the Beilock & Carr References gloss
- **Problem**: "the constraint domain makes the causal contribution particularly vivid because the quality of solutions degrades measurably when conscious engagement is either absent (novel problems handled by rote) or excessive (expert performance disrupted by attention)". The page centres on cognitive constraint satisfaction: composing sentences, crosswords, schedules, honesty against kindness. The only measured "excessive engagement" evidence it cites is golf putting. Beilock & Carr (2001) themselves report "Choking occurred in putting but not alphabet arithmetic". Beilock, Kulp, Holt & Carr (2004), *JEP: General* 133(4), 584–600, DOI 10.1037/0096-3445.133.4.584 (PubMed 15584808, abstract), found that "pressure harmed performance on only unpracticed problems with heavy working memory demands". Practice to direct retrieval "eliminated choking under pressure". These findings "support distraction theories of choking in math, which contrasts with considerable evidence for explicit monitoring theories of choking in sensorimotor skills." In cognitive constraint solving, then, the "excessive engagement" part of the pattern is not shown, and automatised problems are the ones that do *not* choke. The gloss "[Empirical evidence for attentional interference with automatised constraint processing.]" overstates the same way.
- **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 4.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The "click" is a fallible signal
- **Current content says**: Resolution brings felt rightness, "immediate and non-inferential". Recognition and feeling are "aspects of a single conscious event" (L64), and L102 calls the click a possible "experiential signature of this selection event".
- **A critic would argue**: False insights share the pleasure, suddenness and certainty of true ones (Danek & Wiley 2017, *Frontiers in Psychology* 7, 2077, PubMed 28163687: "First, false insights exist"). So the click and the solution's fit to its constraints come apart.
- **Suggested response**: Add one clause at L64 linking [[noetic-feelings-void]], which already carries this finding: "the same click can accompany a wrong answer". About +12 words. This is consistent with L102's hedge, since a selection event need not pick the right candidate. Lower priority than items 1–4.

### The materialist has offered accounts; the Map rejects them
- **Current content says**: "The materialist owes an account of why neural valuation is different, and no such account has been provided." (L74)
- **A critic would argue**: Global-workspace and higher-order theories are such accounts. They explain why valuation, unlike cerebellar processing, is conscious.
- **Suggested response**: "and no account yet offered closes the gap" (net +2). This keeps the hard-problem point without denying the rivals exist.

### Learning-function surplus (L86)
- **Current content says**: The residual dissatisfaction "varies with the type of constraint left unsatisfied" and "goes beyond the informational content a learning system needs".
- **A critic would argue**: Variation by type of violated constraint is exactly the credit-assignment information a learning system needs. The example given for the surplus is itself information.
- **Suggested response**: Prior reviews recorded this exchange as answered, so it is not ranked. If the paragraph is touched, the surplus claim should rest on the *felt* quality, not on the variation by type.
