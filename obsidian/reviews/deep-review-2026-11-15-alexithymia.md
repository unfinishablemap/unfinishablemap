## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Result-direction overstatement, Eastabrook et al. 2013.** The article described arousal-discordance as high reported intensity with low physiological arousal "or the reverse", then said Eastabrook et al. "report exactly this pattern". The abstract (retrieved via a library catalogue record; the publisher page returned 403) reports that alexithymic participants' physiological responses were "indistinguishable during arousal and recovery" from non-alexithymic participants', that they experienced and expressed more self-consciousness, and that alexithymic males "experienced and expressed greater self-consciousness compared to their physiological arousal". That is one direction of the discordance, with physiology at parity, not low, and nothing in the reverse direction. **Resolved**: the sentence now says the study reports "one direction of it" and states the finding as the abstract gives it. The 2026-08-05 ledger line certified this cite on metadata only.

### Medium Issues Found

- **Neumann et al. 2004 scope and precision.** The sample was women aged 18–30 and the significant attenuation was in heart-rate and stroke-index reactivity; the "prolonged recovery" of the title is slower diastolic blood-pressure recovery (pre-ejection-period recovery was quicker). **Resolved**: now "in young women ... attenuated heart-rate reactivity" and "slower diastolic blood-pressure recovery".
- **Aaron et al. 2020 mislabelled "general-population"**: resolved as above.
- **"Blunted felt arousal in others"** (constructed-emotion section) asserted attenuated *felt* affect for some cases, where the only cited evidence is autonomic. This leans on one branch of the question the article keeps open. **Resolved**: "blunted autonomic arousal".
- **Residual comparative "a finer instrument than its siblings."** The 2026-11-14 refine removed the sentence that aphantasia and synaesthesia are "roughly all-or-nothing", leaving the comparative without its premise. **Resolved**: "a fine-grained instrument".
