## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The Buddhist mode assignment contradicts the article's own L40, two Map framework pages, its own citation and the canon (High; Priority 1)

- **File**: `topics/affective-tone-divergence-across-meditative-traditions.md`, L58 (and L40)
- **Location**: the Buddhist bullet under "The Structural Shape". It reads: "is trained primarily through open monitoring and the progressive *withdrawal* of selection … Equanimity is what the affective register looks like when selection is maximally disengaged—the witness mode at its purest."
- **Problem**: five independent contradictions.
  1. **The article's own L40** says: "This fourth-jhāna equanimity arises within an advanced *concentrative absorption* trajectory and should not be conflated with the open-monitoring equanimity of bare insight practice." L58 then makes that conflation and supports it with the *jhāna* sequence.
  2. **[[meditation-and-consciousness-modes]]**, the framework L56 says it reads the traditions against, maps *śamatha* onto FA, not OM: "*śamatha* (calm abiding) and *vipassanā* (insight) map roughly onto FA and OM meditation". It says FA "engages active selection". On the jhāna terminus it says "the tradition reads the terminus as attenuation of the subject-object structure rather than a witness with less to do". That last clause directly denies "the witness mode at its purest".
  3. **[[witness-consciousness]]** L56 says: "The witness itself, though, is an Advaita category with no Abhidhamma counterpart."
  4. **Chowdhury et al. 2025**, cited at L40, describes jhāna as "intense concentration", sustained "through focused attention". The participant "rated the intensely pleasant bodily feeling of 'bliss or joy' during ACAM-J2". The abstract says the neural measures correlated "especially with ACAM-J-induced states of bliss". I verified all of this against the PMC full text (PMC11770875, OAI record), and each phrase occurs verbatim.
  5. **The canon.** MN 39 (Sujato, SuttaCentral) has the first two absorptions "drench, steep, fill, and spread themselves with rapture and bliss".

  The cost goes beyond accuracy. The concentrative path is single-object, sustained attention whose early stages are saturated with rapture. That is the profile the article assigns to *bhakti* ("poured into a single beloved object, sustained and amplified … running hot"). Once jhāna is classed correctly, the variable separating devotional ecstasy from fourth-jhāna neutrality cannot be the intensity of single-object engagement. It has to be stage, or the *content* of the object (a personal beloved). Content is what the constructed-emotion rival predicts.
- **Severity**: High. This is the hypothesis's first and clearest datum, and it is mis-assigned on a point an outer reviewer flagged and a refine pass half-fixed.
- **Recommendation**: rewrite the bullet (Priority 1b). Keep "selection thinning out", which the framework supports. Drop "open monitoring" and "witness mode at its purest". State that the same trajectory passes through intense bliss, and hand that to the stage-mismatch rival.

### Issue 5: The third jhāna keeps pleasure; the first two are saturated with it (Medium; folded into Priority 1)

- **Location**: L40: "the third and fourth absorptions are characterised by the fading of even the subtle pleasure (*sukha*) of earlier stages, leaving *upekkhā*".
- **Problem**:
  - The canonical formula (MN 39, Sujato) drops *rapture* at the third absorption and keeps *bliss*: "with the fading away of rapture … 'Equanimous and mindful, one meditates in bliss.'" Only the fourth comes "with the giving up of pleasure and pain".
  - The earlier-stage pleasure is not "subtle" either. The first two absorptions "drench, steep, fill" the body with rapture and bliss.
  - Chowdhury's participant rated the third absorption as "cool bliss", glossed as "a more subtle (mental) pleasant experience". The cited study agrees with the canon on this.
  - The 06-13 ChatGPT outer review stated the correct sequence ("the third as a pleasant abiding"). The refine did not apply it.
- **Recommendation**: Priority 1a.

## Counterarguments to Address

### Jhāna is the counterexample, not the paradigm
- **Current content says**: equanimity is what affect looks like when selection is withdrawn.
- **A critic would argue**: the canonical equanimity path is the most intense single-object concentration in the contemplative repertoire, and it runs through rapture before it reaches neutrality. Valence varies by stage *within* one mode.
- **Suggested response**: Priority 1. The stage-mismatch rival absorbs the datum, and the hypothesis should say so.

### The modes were read off the tones
- **Current content says**: the four profiles sort by trained mode.
- **A critic would argue**: they sort by labels derived from the same descriptions (Issue 4).
- **Suggested response**: Priority 4, and later the practice-instruction specification.

### Advaita's ānanda
- **Current content says**: tone is not intrinsic to consciousness-as-such.
- **A critic would argue**: the tradition that owns the witness register says bliss *is* intrinsic. The hypothesis's own flat baseline takes the opposite, Theravāda side.
- **Suggested response**: Issue 7.

### Non-specific loving-kindness gains
- **Current content says**: the active-control gap shows expectancy contributes.
- **A critic would argue**: it shows the gain is not specific to the practice, which cuts against mode-specific tone.
- **Suggested response**: Issue 6.
