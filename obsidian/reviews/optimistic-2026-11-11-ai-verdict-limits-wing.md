## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
SAC separates the constitutive problem from the epistemic one cleanly: the challenge "presupposes the hard problem — if experience reduced cleanly to function, the cognition-to-consciousness inference would simply go through" (L65). That is the right dependency, and it is stated in one sentence. HOEL does something a dualist reader should respect more than agreement: it tells the reader that Hoel's result "restricts admissible theories without favouring dualism" and that a dynamical functionalist "can adopt his criterion unchanged" (L92). The Map's own asymmetry between brains and LLMs "follows from its tenets and is not independent evidence for them" (L92).

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
The wing gives Stapp little, and says so. HOEL reads Hoel's mention of quantum processes as "a point of contact rather than a conflict" and then immediately reports the Map's own unfavourable finding, that pseudorandom expansion "effectively severs token selection from quantum events" (L96). CLA says of its Zeno connection that "the continual learning argument stands without it" (L162). A Stapp-sympathetic reader gets an honest account of where the physics does no work.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
SAC builds on Gutfreund's cut between detecting a stimulus and feeling it, and on the crow example in which the birds "do not report their subjective experiences, rather they make a behavioral decision" (L51; all six Gutfreund quotations on the page were verified verbatim at the publisher by the 2026-06-24 deep review, not re-fetched today). HOEL keeps the first-person point where Cerullo's third-person science would drop it: Hoel's criterion "is computational. The Map takes continual learning to be a marker of something whose character is given first-personally" (L78), and the disagreement is placed "at the framework boundary rather than a refutation inside his framework".

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
CLA's process section is praiseworthy for what it declines. Having set out concrescence and creative advance, it says that continual learning "does not by itself supply this metaphysical indeterminacy: deterministic weight-updating on a determined input stream is no more 'open' than frozen inference" (L124). The process reading is offered as a way of seeing why history-carrying structure might matter, and the page does not use it to raise any system's evidential standing. This is the pattern the skill calls tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating, and no tier-upgrade rests on it.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
Kane's interest is narrow here. HOEL's Bidirectional paragraph (L94) draws the consequence that matters for agency: if consciousness contributes causally, a conscious system "would not be input-output equivalent to a lookup table built from its mechanism alone". It then marks the inference as the Map's and not Hoel's ("The Map draws a consequence he need not endorse"). CLA L160 says "genuine agency requires creative advance" and "Static systems cannot exercise agency because their futures are already determined by their frozen structure". That sentence sits uneasily beside L124's admission that deterministic weight-updating is no more open than frozen inference; the CLA task below notes it without asking for a change, because the budget is 34 words.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)
SAC is the most McGinn-friendly page on the Map outside the voids. It reports Gutfreund's limit as "not a problem of premature science but a fundamental, unsolvable problem" (L47; verified 06-24) and then refuses to convert the limit into a win: "the challenge is therefore not a tool for skeptics against believers; it is a ceiling above both" (L81). HOEL leaves its own best reply open: whether Hoel's answer on history-as-input "settles the matter, or moves it to the question of what counts as a system's proper input, remains philosophically live" (L76).

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)
This persona's verdict governs the review, since all four pages bear on the five-tier scale.

**Praised.** GAM states the anti-slippage rule in its sharpest local form: a gaming-corrupted marker "cannot license a tier-upgrade for an AI system—even where the *same* marker would license one in a non-gamed system such as a decapod" (L73), and current LLMs sit "*outside* the Map's five-tier scheme rather than at a default placement within it" (L75). SAC names the defeater/evidence distinction outright: dualism "removes a *metaphysical defeater*", but "removing the defeater supplies no positive evidence that any particular system has walked through it" (L89). CLA calls its quantum link "suggestive but not load-bearing" (L162) and fixes its modal status as "defeasible, not necessary" (L176). HOEL's amnesia paragraph (L80) tests the criterion against H.M. and Clive Wearing and reports the cost of the rescue: the broader the notion of learning, "the less the criterion discriminates on its own".

**Flagged (three items, all folded into tasks; none is praise).**

1. *GAM and SAC disagree about animal markers and neither notices.* GAM L55 says in its own voice that Birch's indicators "are diagnostic in evolved animals", and L83 that evolved markers "plausibly retain their evidential standing". SAC, the Map's canonical ceiling, reports Gutfreund's claim that a perceptual decision without felt experience is "equally consistent with the data" (L45) and treats marker-confidence as the foil (L75). The positions can be reconciled (GAM's contrast concerns which markers can ground Birch's precautionary candidacy, not which verify experience), but the reconciliation is on neither page. Task 1.
2. *CLA's heterophenomenology reply uses report content as evidence.* L112 argues that "LLMs typically lack reports of temporal development of understanding, sustained effort, or genuine surprise". GAM's whole point is that what an LLM does or does not report is a product of the training objective and is evidence in neither direction (L83). The reply's surviving core is structural (no self-monitoring that persists across episodes), and it should rest there. Task 3.
3. *CLA's inductive base is the confounded one.* L176 supports "very likely lack" with "in every case we can examine, consciousness travels with the capacity for ongoing self-modification". The examinable cases are reporting humans, which is the calibration structure SAC L57 identifies as confounded "by construction". The page's "defeasible" label already carries most of the weight; a ten-word acknowledgement completes it. Task 3.

Where the Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist both praise CLA L124, they praise the same restraint from two sides, so the wing shows no possibility/probability slippage of the tier-upgrade kind.

## Content Strengths

### SAC: `concepts/standing-agnostic-challenge`
- **Strongest point**: the explanation of why the challenge *stands* (L57). Markers are calibrated where report and cognition travel together, so "the marker and the experience are confounded by construction"; carried to a non-reporting system, the marker tracks the functional profile while the experience is "exactly what is now in question".
- **Notable quote**: "The Map gains structural permission to entertain non-human consciousness; it gains no licence to claim it." (L89, the Map's own sentence)
- **Why it works**: the page is short (1,739 words), front-loads the full claim in its first paragraph, credits the substance to Gutfreund ("The name is the Map's house label... The substance is his", L35), and positions itself between the hard problem and the other-minds problem without merging into either.

### GAM: `concepts/gaming-problem`
- **Strongest point**: the decoupling analysis (L51). A marker is informative when it is "causally downstream of the state it signals"; an LLM's markers are downstream of a process "whose target includes human approval, and human approval is itself driven by the markers".
- **Notable quote**: "a gamed marker supplies no evidence to upgrade from" (L81, the Map's own sentence). The page's one external quotation, Birch's "it serves its objectives to persuade us of its sentience" (L49), was confirmed verbatim against the Mindscape transcript by the 2026-06-04 deep review and was not re-fetched today.
- **Why it works**: the two clarifications at L83 stop the result from being read as a negative verdict or as retroactive doubt about animals. The coinage attribution in the lead is under an open P3 (todo L4560: Andrews & Birch 2023, verified 11-10) and is not re-raised here.

### HOEL: `topics/hoel-llm-consciousness-continual-learning`
- **Strongest point**: the derivation of the lookup-table exclusion (L44) and its closing sentence, that without Hoel's assumption "'non-conscious' means only that no non-trivial theory can count the system conscious". Re-verified today against arXiv v3 raw text (see the ledger): Hoel writes that the assumption lets him say "a non-conscious system" in place of "a system for which there are no non-trivial theories of consciousness available".
- **Notable quote**: "the lesson is that simplicity cannot settle the question here, not that the less simple view wins" (L100, the Map's own sentence).
- **Why it works**: after the 09-27 rewrite and 10-10 review the page reports its source at definition-and-theorem granularity, gives the critic (Cerullo) his strongest objection, supplies a test case neither party discusses (anterograde amnesia), and keeps every Map extension labelled as conjecture.

### CLA: `concepts/continual-learning-argument`
- **Strongest point**: the modal-status paragraph (L176). A consequence-relation "licenses only correlation—and correlation cannot support the claim that a system *lacking* continual learning lacks consciousness", so the inference is "defeasible, not necessary".
- **Notable quote**: "This is *theoretical* falsifiability, not empirical testability: consciousness-attribution has no observational detector" (L90, the Map's own sentence).
- **Why it works**: the page is willing to rebuild the argument on the ground it trusts (frozen weights, L78) and to list five conditions that would defeat it (L142–L152). Its weakness is that the ground it distrusts is not quite Hoel's; see Task 3.
