## Executive Summary

Four pages written two weeks apart share a method no page names. Each takes a term the mind–body debate leans on and splits it in two: "physical" (theory-relative, or the absence of fundamental mentality), "primitive" (ontological or epistemic), "acquaintance" (phenomenal presentation or an information channel) and "content" (heeded information or phenomenal character). Each then shows that the evidence or argument reaches one sense only and declines to count the other as support for dualism. Calibration is strong across the four, and the 10-21 tenet check already passed three of them. The weakness is integration: none of the four links another. Two of them lack a reciprocal from the page they lean on most (`logical-behaviourism` for Hempel, `phenomenological-evidence` for protocol analysis). The Priority List closes three seams and fills one gap in a host's taxonomy.
