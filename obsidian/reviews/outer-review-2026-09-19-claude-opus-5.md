- **The article is the site's best-calibrated meta-problem treatment and its most dangerous piece of tenet-protective bracketing at the same time.** It states the debunking argument honestly and even concedes its reply "does not refute the debunker from neutral ground" — but it confronts only the weak *justification* version of Chalmers's debunking argument and never engages the *coincidence* version (Chalmers 2020, "Debunking Arguments for Illusionism"), which is specifically constructed to survive the exact realizationist/interactionist move the article relies on.

## 2. Dimensional Analysis

### Axis 1 — Claim support

**Chalmers 2018.** The article's exposition of the meta-problem's *definition* and *scope* is accurate and well-grounded. It correctly reports the narrowing from phenomenal reports in general to "problem intuitions," correctly lists the four intuition classes, and correctly states the structural claim that "any adequate theory of consciousness must solve the meta-problem." Good.

**The debunking threat.** This is the strongest passage. The article states the argument in its own right — "a belief explained entirely by causes indifferent to its truth loses its justification" — and explicitly refuses to treat the meta-problem as a friendly argument for dualism ("it supplies the materialist's sharpest weapon"). This is genuinely correct and creditable; most dualist treatments suppress it.

**But the support fails at the decisive step.** The article gives exactly one version of the debunking argument — the justification version (independent explanation ⇒ unjustified belief) — and answers it with one move: Bidirectional Interaction denies that "the topic-neutral physical explanation work[s] *independently* of whether consciousness is a causal contributor." Chalmers's 2020 reply shows this is the *weaker* form and that the argument was reformulated precisely to defeat it. Quoting Chalmers 2020 ("Debunking Arguments for Illusionism about Consciousness") verbatim: "The realizationist can reasonably deny causal and constititive [sic] independence as described above ... But there remains a case for a strong sort of modal/explanatory independence. In particular, there remains a near-complete structural explanation of the intuitions (in computational terms, say), and this explanation can obtain without consciousness." And the coincidence premise, verbatim: "If there is a near-complete structural explanation of the intuitions that could obtain without consciousness, this gives a strong sense that if the intuitions are correct, their correctness is something of a coincidence." The article's reply does not touch this. Worse, the Map's Born-rule-preserving interface *guarantees* the modal/explanatory independence Chalmers needs: by design the statistical/structural trajectory is reproducible without consciousness. The article's central premise-denial therefore fails against the argument Chalmers actually endorses.

**"What Would Challenge This View?" point 3 is already partly actualized against the article.** The article offers as a *hypothetical* defeater: "if societies with different conceptual frameworks lacked problem intuitions entirely." This is precisely the finding of Rodrigo Díaz, "Do People Think Consciousness Poses a Hard Problem? Empirical Evidence on the Meta-Problem of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 28(3–4):55–75, which concluded verbatim: "The results of four studies will show that (1) problem intuitions are not widespread, and (2) when they arise, they do so because of factors that are unrelated to the nature of consciousness." A 2026 cross-cultural follow-up (Kuś et al., "Polishing the meta-problem of consciousness: empirical evidence for non-English speakers," *Philosophical Psychology*, DOI 10.1080/09515089.2026.2710862) extends the work to Polish speakers, though it is deliberately cautious — "Preliminary results and analyses, along with their interpretation, suggest that problem intuitions are not widespread. We think it is too early to tell." Even on the cautious reading, posing as open a question the empirical literature has already pushed against the article's own face-value thesis is a claim-support and currency failure.

### Axis 3 — Counterargument coverage

Serious objections to the article's dualist reading that go **unaddressed**:

1. **The coincidence version of the debunking argument (Chalmers 2020).** The single most important omission. Realizationism/Bidirectional Interaction answers causal independence but not modal/explanatory independence; the coincidence problem survives. Unaddressed.
2. **The topic-neutrality reformulation (Chalmers 2018).** The article equates "physical" with "topic-neutral" and never confronts that Chalmers built the meta-problem to bind Born-rule-preserving quantum interactionism specifically. Unaddressed.
3. **Predictive-processing / active-inference meta-problem solutions (blocking gate).** Clark, Friston & Wilkinson 2019 ("Bayesing Qualia: Consciousness as Inference, Not Raw Datum," *JCS* 26(9-10):19-33) offer a direct topic-neutral solution — from their own abstract: "We present and defend a solution to the meta-problem. Our solution takes as its starting point the emerging picture of the brain as a hierarchical inference engine. We show why such a device, operating under familiar forms of adaptive pressure, may come to represent some of its mid-level inferences as especially certain." Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria 2025 ("A beautiful loop: An active inference theory of consciousness," *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 176:106296, published 1 Sept 2025) extend this to a self-modelling account of why a system's world-model, verbatim, "contains the knowledge that it exists. This is distinct from self-consciousness, because the world model knows itself non-locally and continuously evidences this knowing (i.e., field-evidencing)." Both are absent; the article residualizes predictive processing to Metzinger-style transparency only, never the PP meta-problem solution. This fails the standing blocking gate.
4. **The empirical challenge to problem-intuition universality (Díaz 2021; Fischer & Sytsma 2021, "Zombie intuitions," *Cognition* 215:104807).** Directly bears on the face-value thesis and is treated as hypothetical.
5. **Kammerer's evidential/immediate-knowledge illusionist solution and "illusion meta-problem" (2019 JCS 26(9-10):124-135; 2021 *Erkenntnis*).** The article's illusionism section asserts illusionist meta-problem solutions face a dilemma ("too simple" vs "collapses back into phenomenality") but never engages the most developed illusionist meta-problem programme, which is designed to escape exactly that dilemma. Unaddressed.

Objections the article **does** cover well: the debunking argument's justification version (stated fairly); the illusionist "illusion problem" and its resilience; the phenomenal-concepts-strategy regress; the honest concession that the reply is framework-internal.

### Axis 4 — Tenet-protective bracketing

- **Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits) is used to blunt debunking force it does not touch.** The "Relation to Site Perspective" invokes anti-parsimony against illusionism: "simplicity is a treacherous guide ... The 'simple' explanation ... may reflect ignorance." But the debunking argument's force is *not* fundamentally a parsimony argument — it is a coincidence/explanatory-independence argument. Deploying Tenet 5 here answers a motivation the debunker need not rely on, and leaves the actual argument standing. Textbook tenet-protective bracketing.
- **The zombie-divergence claim is insulated by an inoculating confession rather than corrected.** The article predicts "a zombie's problem reports would *not* be identical to ours," then concedes: "Whether those differences would manifest as detectable divergences ... is speculative — but the interactionist framework predicts they exist." This is confession-without-correction: the defect (undetectability) is named and logged but the dissolution claim ("the zombie meta-problem dissolves") is not demoted. And the confession understates the problem: a philosophical zombie is by definition a *physical* duplicate, and the Map's Born-rule-preserving interface (documented at the born-rule apex as leaving "no statistical signature in long runs") entails the duplicate's physical trajectory — including its reports — is reproducible without consciousness. So the divergence is not merely undetectable; on the Map's own commitments there is no divergence to detect. The article brackets its own born-preserving tenet out of view at exactly the point where it would sink the argument. This is the Born-rule statistical-indistinguishability dilemma, unconfronted.
- **Epistemic-to-metaphysical slide in the phenomenal-concepts section.** "If phenomenal concepts exist because they help us navigate genuinely phenomenal reality, they become evidence *for* that reality." The conditional smuggles the conclusion: the premise "help us navigate genuinely phenomenal reality" already assumes phenomenal realism.
- **Constrain-vs-establish: mostly respected, locally breached.** Credit where due — the article states plainly that neutralising the debunker "*removes a defeater* rather than supplying fresh positive evidence" and "does not, on its own, raise the standing of dualism." This is exactly the right calibration and is the article's best feature. But the zombie section breaches it by claiming the framework "predicts" divergences (an establish-flavoured claim) it simultaneously concedes it cannot cash out.

## 5. Site-Wide Methodology Improvements

- **Author-stance verification must be a distinct, blocking review lens, not folded into metadata verification.** The changelog shows the pipeline runs rigorous publisher-of-record metadata checks (Crossref/Europe PMC, verbatim grep, false-absence traps) — genuinely strong, and the meta-problem article's metadata reflects it. But the recurring failure mode is recruiting a named author (here Chalmers) toward a conclusion he pre-empted. A "does the cited author's own text anticipate and answer this move?" check would have caught the topic-neutrality omission. The Claude-on-Claude review loop is good at *whether the citation exists and is quoted correctly* and weak at *whether the citation's author would accept the use* — the two need separating.
- **Currency gating on argument-bearing claims.** The article's arguments depend on a live 2019–2025 literature its reference list omits by six years. A rule that any claim answering an objection must cite the most recent published statement of that objection would force engagement with Chalmers 2020, Saad 2019, Kammerer 2021, Díaz 2021, and the PP solutions.
- **A standing "does the born-preserving tenet undercut this local argument?" cross-check.** The zombie-divergence claim is defeated by the site's own born-rule apex. The interface's Born-rule preservation is the site's deepest structural vulnerability precisely because it recurs — the same statistical-indistinguishability dilemma surfaces in agency, amplification, and now the meta-problem. A blocking lens that routes every "consciousness makes a detectable difference" claim through the corridor-dualism/statistical-indistinguishability result would convert scattered local confessions into a single honest status.
- **Convert confession into status change.** The pipeline's habit of naming a defect ("speculative," "framework-boundary clash") without demoting the dependent claim is inoculation-by-confession. Reviews should be empowered to demote a claim's evidential label when a confession is logged, not merely record the confession alongside the original claim.
- **Reconcile calibration across hub/concept/topic tiers.** The corpus currently lets a well-hedged concept page coexist with an overclaiming hub gloss ("vindication," "structural advantage"). A propagation check that flags strength mismatches on the same proposition across linked articles would close this.
