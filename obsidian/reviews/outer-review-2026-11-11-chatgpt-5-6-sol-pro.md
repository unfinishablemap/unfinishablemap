# Independent critical audit: “The Boredom Void”

**Audit date:** 11 November 2026  
**Overall verdict:** The article’s source fidelity is now substantially better than its central argument. Most quotations are accurate, and the 3 November revision corrected two serious reversals in the original account. But the empirical literature supports a narrower conclusion than the article’s “void” classification: boredom is a heterogeneous, aversive regulatory signal that often gives coarse guidance and imperfect causal self-knowledge. It has not been shown to be intrinsically objectless, self-sealing or phenomenologically unexplorable.

The strongest part is the discussion of misforecasting solitary thought. The weakest is the claim that engaged examination of boredom establishes an unexplorable void. Two definite source-use errors also remain: the noisy-TV result does not show that artificial boredom needs a MAC-style meaning term, and the William James paragraph overstates the explanatory predicament of epiphenomenalism.

## Version and revision history checked

The live page reports creation on 31 October and last modification on 3 November 2026. The live changelog now runs through 10 November, but the boredom page still shows no revision after 3 November. The relevant history includes a 31 October research pass and a 3 November pessimistic review. That review identified, among other things, that the original version had inverted Agrawal et al.’s option-indexed model, overlooked the differentiated self-report profiles in Westgate and Wilson’s Study 2, treated a definitional result as an empirical tendency, misread the noisy-TV result, and overstated the James argument. [[boredom-void]]

The current article successfully incorporated several of those repairs:

- It now says that Agrawal et al.’s exploration values concern particular alternatives, including previously sampled ones.
- It now reports the differentiated MAC self-report profiles and correctly moves the open question from phenomenological differentiation to causal attribution.
- It expressly concedes that its fourth face is definition-relative.

It did **not** repair the noisy-TV inference, the James sentence, the unsupported claim that boredom is the “commonest” occasion for consciousness to report on engagement, or the unqualified conclusion that the forecasting error “seals itself.” [[boredom-void]]

## 1. Citation and quotation audit

### Eastwood et al. 2012

**Quotation fidelity: verified. Conceptual use: partly overstated.**

The central definition and its three components—failed engagement, awareness of that failure, and attribution to the environment—are reproduced accurately. The article is also now commendably explicit that environmental attribution is a component of Eastwood et al.’s proposed definition, not an experimentally demonstrated misattribution. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26168505/)

There remains a smaller but real mismatch in the phrase “the wanting has no object.” Eastwood’s formula gives it a generic object: engagement in satisfying activity. What is normally missing is a **determinate activity or remedy**, not all intentional object whatsoever. Tolstoy’s “desire for desires” is similarly second-order rather than strictly objectless. The heading “Wanting Without a Determinate Object” would be more defensible than “Wanting Without an Object.” [[boredom-void]]

### Agrawal, Mattar, Cohen and Daw 2022

**Description of the formal model: now accurate. Phenomenological inference: not established by the source.**

The source describes boredom as the phenomenological counterpart of an opportunity-cost variable associated with external exploration and expressly refers to the competing value of particular options. The current article correctly acknowledges that the model is option-indexed and that already sampled alternatives can retain exploration value. [Princeton University](https://www.princeton.edu/~ndaw/amcd021.pdf)

Agrawal et al., however, offer a normative computational theory; they do not experimentally compare the option-specific quantity with what people can consciously report. The article’s statement that “nothing in the feeling displays which alternative carries that value” is therefore a plausible **Map inference**, not a result of the paper. It should be presented as a hypothesis requiring introspective-attribution data.

The model also does not entail that the signal must be destinationless. The computation can distinguish candidate actions even if conscious affect presents only a scalar or low-dimensional summary. That may be ordinary interface compression rather than a special epistemic void.

### Westgate and Wilson 2018

**Verified and now represented fairly.**

The MAC model does divide boredom into attentional mismatch and meaning mismatch, includes both under- and overstimulation, and reports different boredom profiles associated with different deficits. The current article accurately reports the relevant Study 2 differentiation and correctly observes that the experiment did not ask subjects to identify the cause of their boredom. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29963873/)

This repair materially improves the article. It also narrows the remaining thesis. The evidence shows that boredom is not entirely undifferentiated from the inside: attention and meaning manipulations generated distinguishable self-reported profiles. The untested question is now whether people can move from those profiles to accurate **causal attribution**, not whether the causes have any different phenomenal signatures.

A small 2026 psychophysiological study provides further, preliminary support for distinctions involving meaning, stimulation and subsequent task choice, although its sample was only 60 and it does not answer the causal-attribution question. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41886546/)

### Bench and Lench 2013

**Quotation verified; evidential weight overstated slightly.**

The source does argue that boredom motivates pursuit of alternative goals. It also explicitly characterises the supporting literature then available as limited and calls for further experiments. The article should describe this as an influential functional proposal rather than as an established consensus about what boredom “does.” [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25379249/)

That caution matters because a preregistered 2025 experiment found no direct main effect of induced boredom on subsequent challenge selection. It found an indirect pathway through reported desire for challenge, and challenge selection subsequently reduced boredom. This supports a regulatory interpretation but not a simple, automatic boredom-to-exploration law. [Taylor & Francis Online+1](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02699931.2025.2568554)

### Wilson, Fox, Buttrick and the shock evidence

**The historical account is accurate but should be updated.**

The article correctly gives the Wilson et al. shock counts and accurately presents both Fox et al.’s curiosity-based criticism and Wilson et al.’s reply. It is also fair to cite Buttrick et al. for the cross-cultural finding that assigned external activity was generally enjoyed more than assigned thinking-for-pleasure. That robustness is confined to this experimental contrast; it should not be generalised to all internally directed thought or all occasions of boredom. [PubMed Central (PMC)+3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4330241/)

The important missing update is Eder et al. 2022. Across four experiments with 254 participants, self-administered shocks were replicated, but participants selected strong shocks even when harmless or milder alternatives were available. The authors concluded that avoidance of thinking was unlikely to be the primary motive and found the pattern more consistent with curiosity, sensation seeking and exploration. [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9253005/)

This newer work largely supports the Map’s exploration-based reconciliation. But it also means that the reconciliation is no longer just an original Map inference: a subsequent empirical paper has made a closely related case and should be cited. The shock result should not be used as strong evidence that being alone with thought is intolerably aversive.

### Hatano et al. 2022

**Quotation and experimental summary verified. Self-sealing conclusion not verified.**

Hatano et al. conducted six experiments with 259 university students. Participants underestimated how enjoyable and engaging waiting with their thoughts would be, and this underestimation sometimes led them to choose internet news instead even though the experienced alternatives did not differ significantly. The article reports that accurately and initially labels its further inference as its own. [https://www.apa.org](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xge-xge0001255.pdf)

The experiments did not establish that:

1. the forecasting error persists after repeated experience;
2. avoidance is the principal reason it persists;
3. ordinary involuntary waits fail to supply corrective evidence; or
4. the resulting process is self-sealing.

The final sentence nevertheless promotes the hypothesis into a distinctive contribution—a forecast that “seals itself” because the mind seldom meets contrary evidence. That is too strong. Queues, journeys, delays and waiting rooms routinely impose the very experience that voluntary avoidance is supposed to remove.

A 2026 study further shows that boredom forecasting is not uniformly pessimistic. Participants overestimated how much meaningful or stimulating activities would reduce felt boredom, while underestimating how effective those activities would seem. Forecast error therefore changes direction depending on whether the target is predicted feeling or perceived task effectiveness. [Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-026-10231-8)

### Chin et al. 2017 and Witowska et al. 2020

**Verified within their proper scope.**

Chin et al. did analyse approximately 1.1 million reports from 3,867 US adults, with boredom reported in 2.8% of sampled 30-minute periods. Witowska et al. used a real 7.5-minute waiting period and found associations among boredom, slower felt time and increased thoughts about time. These claims are accurately reported. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27775405/)

The current article uses Chin et al. fairly when rejecting accounts that identify boredom **exclusively** with low arousal. It should nevertheless add the recent preregistered meta-analysis of 214 effects from 72 samples and 6,570 participants. That synthesis found boredom associated on average with lower arousal, while also finding substantial heterogeneity and measure-dependent moderation. The updated conclusion should be: low arousal is a statistical tendency, not an exhaustive definition of boredom. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41310401/)

### Burda et al. 2018

**Quotation verified. Inference contradicted by the source.**

This is the clearest remaining source-fidelity problem.

Burda et al. do report that a randomly changing television drastically slows learning. But they locate the problem in prediction-error curiosity being attracted to irreducibly stochastic, high-entropy transitions. In the particular maze experiment, agents could still converge on the extrinsic goal reward, so the setup already contained a value-bearing goal signal. [pure.ed.ac.uk](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/181351193/Large_Scale_Study_BURDA_DoA211218_AFV.pdf)

It therefore does not follow that “a useful boredom signal needs a value term like the MAC meaning component.” A value term was already present. The supported lesson is that an exploration bonus must distinguish:

- learnable uncertainty from irreducible randomness;
- information gain from mere surprise; or
- controllable novelty from stochastic noise.

An ICLR 2026 paper addresses precisely this problem through learning-progress monitoring, prioritising reducible error rather than raw prediction error. That is a much closer technical analogue of the required correction than a MAC meaning term. [ICLR Proceedings+1](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/803b9c4a8e4784072fdd791c54d614e2-Abstract-Conference.html)

### Slaby and Heidegger

**The quoted material is accurate, but its use is selective.**

Slaby does describe Heidegger’s method as awakening a fundamental attunement rather than merely accumulating detached theoretical knowledge. He also says that understanding boredom requires waking up to it, attending to it and living it lucidly. [janslaby.com](https://www.janslaby.com/static/publications/Slaby2010_Heidegger_Boredom_proofs.pdf)

That second part cuts against the article’s strong “unexplorable” classification. Slaby distinguishes performative or transformative phenomenology from detached inspection; he does not establish that sustained first-person access is impossible. Nor does his treatment imply that any attentive engagement immediately terminates the relevant state. The article quotes the strand favourable to “awakening rather than inspection” without giving equal weight to Slaby’s affirmative language about attending to and living the attunement.

The description of profound boredom as a condition in which the world ceases to offer significance is accurate. Slaby also presents this deepest form as rare, perhaps absent from ordinary life, which reinforces the need not to slide without argument from laboratory state boredom to Heideggerian profound boredom. [janslaby.com](https://www.janslaby.com/static/publications/Slaby2010_Heidegger_Boredom_proofs.pdf)

### Tolstoy, Pascal, James and Williams

**No material misquotation found.**

The Tolstoy and Pascal passages are accurately quoted and properly located. James’s wording about pleasures, pains and causal efficacy is also faithful to the primary text. [Wikisource+2](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Anna_Karenina_(Garnett)/Part_Five/Chapter_8)

The Bernard Williams quotation is accurate as reproduced by the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, and the article transparently says that it is quoting Williams through O’Brien. Nevertheless, the site’s general primary-source standard would favour checking and citing the Williams chapter itself rather than relying on the encyclopedia for the wording. [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://iep.utm.edu/boredom/)

## 2. Assessment of the four proposed faces

### Face 1: wanting without an object

**Assessment: plausible coarse guidance; not established objectlessness.**

The strongest defensible claim is that boredom often indicates that current engagement is failing without consciously specifying an optimal replacement. Agrawal et al.’s account supplies a possible computational ground: option-specific exploration values can be compressed into an aversive cost associated with persisting in the present activity. [Princeton University+1](https://www.princeton.edu/~ndaw/amcd021.pdf)

Three qualifications are needed.

First, a broad object remains: satisfying engagement, change, stimulation, meaning or challenge. The signal is underdeterminate, not strictly objectless.

Second, accessible alternatives can alter boredom. In Struk et al.’s experiment, participants forbidden to use visible affordances such as a laptop or puzzle became more bored than participants in an empty room. Alternatives can therefore enter the affective computation even when they cannot be acted upon. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32401144/)

Third, the omission of a full causal model from phenomenal presentation is common across regulatory signals. Hunger need not identify the optimal nutrient; fatigue need not specify the best recovery strategy; anxiety need not display the probability distribution generating it. The article needs a criterion distinguishing a **void** from ordinary low-bandwidth control signalling. Otherwise “the feeling does not display all of the computation’s variables” makes almost every affect a void.

The face survives, but in this narrower form:

> Boredom may provide coarse negative guidance away from present engagement while withholding the identity and value of the best available alternative.

That is a viable hypothesis about introspective compression. It is not yet evidence of structural cognitive closure.

### Face 2: the verdict points outward

**Assessment: a genuine empirical question, presently not a demonstrated misattribution.**

The current article correctly restricts the claim to situational boredom and acknowledges that Eastwood et al.’s environmental attribution is definitional. It also concedes that boredom is sometimes fitting and that MAC profiles can separate attention and meaning deficits. [[boredom-void]]

A substantial counterargument remains underdeveloped: “this lecture is boring” need not falsely project an internal state onto an independently characterised object. It can express a **relational or dispositional property**—this lecture fails to afford engagement for this person, with these goals, capacities and circumstances. “This food is nauseating” does not ordinarily imply that nausea is an intrinsic molecular property of the food; it identifies a reliable relation between object and subject. The grammar of outward predication therefore does not by itself show projection.

The remaining empirical question is narrower and valuable:

> Can people accurately distinguish whether a particular episode was produced by understimulation, overstimulation, low meaning, blocked control, fatigue or some combination?

The article’s proposed causal-attribution extension of the MAC design is well targeted. Until such data exist, the face should be classified as **unexplored**, not as an established occlusion.

### Face 3: the mind alone with itself

**Assessment: the best-supported face, but composed of distinct phenomena.**

The evidence supports three separate claims:

1. Many untrained participants enjoy assigned external activity more than assigned thinking-for-pleasure.
2. Some people seek even aversive stimulation during low-engagement situations.
3. People can underestimate how engaging a period of waiting with their thoughts will be.

The article handles the Wilson–Fox dispute more carefully than many popular discussions and appropriately restricts its conclusion to the untrained default. [[boredom-void]]

The shock evidence should now be interpreted chiefly through curiosity, exploration and sensation seeking, given Eder et al. The Hatano evidence supports a metacognitive forecasting error, but not a general inability to occupy oneself, and not an enduring self-sealing mechanism. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35787636/)

There is, however, newer evidence for a more specific self-amplifying loop. Across seven experiments, Tam and Inzlicht found that boredom increased digital switching and that switching between or within videos could then increase boredom, reduce attention and reduce experienced meaning. Results were less conclusive for online articles and non-university samples. This is genuine causal evidence of a boredom-maintaining behaviour, but the mechanism is fragmented engagement, not necessarily avoidance of corrective experience about solitary thinking. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39158465/)

The article should therefore distinguish:

- **misforecasting:** “waiting with thought will be worse than it proves to be”;
- **avoidance:** choosing external stimulation instead;
- **maladaptive regulation:** choosing a form of stimulation that worsens boredom;
- **failure to update:** retaining the forecast after experience.

Only the first two are demonstrated by Hatano et al. Tam and Inzlicht provide evidence for the third in a limited digital-media setting. The fourth remains untested here.

### Face 4: examining boredom tends to end it

**Assessment: verified overclassification.**

Under Eastwood’s attentional definition, becoming satisfyingly absorbed in studying one’s boredom means that the defining attentional failure is no longer present. But that is a consequence of the stipulated definition, not evidence that boredom empirically “tends” to disappear under examination. The article itself acknowledges this. [[boredom-void]]

Several possibilities remain open:

- Attention to boredom may be sustained but unsatisfying.
- A person may remain bored because the examination lacks meaning.
- Reflective attention may alter the intensity or type of boredom without ending the episode.
- Trained phenomenological or contemplative attention may preserve the state long enough for detailed reporting.
- What persists may be a temporally continuous process whose functional classification changes during observation, rather than a wholly different episode.

Slaby’s language about attending to and lucidly living boredom is particularly awkward for an unqualified “unexplorable” label. [janslaby.com](https://www.janslaby.com/static/publications/Slaby2010_Heidegger_Boredom_proofs.pdf)

This face is better described as an **observation-reactivity problem**:

> Some operational definitions make successful, satisfying inspection incompatible with continued classification as bored.

That is methodologically interesting, but it does not yet establish that the experience cannot be examined while present. Face 4 should not be classified as unexplorable without experimental evidence comparing ordinary introspection, structured phenomenological interviewing and trained contemplative observation.

## 3. Important current literature and counterarguments missing from the article

### Naturalistic models now explain much of the apparent blank

Three recent frameworks directly challenge the need for a special boredom void:

- Danckert and Elpidorou propose boredom as a signal that cognitive engagement has departed from a “Goldilocks” zone, integrating problems of attention, meaning and action.
- Darling develops a predictive-processing account that treats ordinary boredom as monitoring expected progress in prediction-error reduction while also offering a hierarchical account of existential boredom as a mood or loss of grip on the world.
- Trudel and colleagues model boredom as deviation from a cognitive homeostatic set point, with allostatic or faulty internal models helping to explain trait boredom and unrealistic engagement expectations. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36922277/)

These accounts do not refute introspective opacity. They show that targetlessness, variable arousal, trait differences and apparent causal hiddenness can be generated by ordinary control architectures. The Map is already entitled to say that a functional account does not exhaust phenomenal character. It is not entitled to classify the residual functional opacity as a special void without comparing boredom with other compressed regulatory signals.

### The signal may be coarse because it integrates several dimensions

The MAC model, the Goldilocks model and the homeostatic account all suggest that boredom is not one detector reporting one hidden variable. It can integrate meaning, demand, resources, controllability, stimulation and expected progress. A scalar unpleasantness signal produced by a multidimensional control problem will naturally underdetermine its cause.

That gives the physicalist a strong counterargument:

> The alleged void is not a hidden single ground but lossy dimensional reduction across several partially separable causes.

The differentiated MAC profiles and emerging psychophysiological results support partial—not total—recoverability of that underlying structure. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29963873/)

### State boredom, boredom proneness and profound boredom are not yet connected

The article moves among:

- experimentally induced state boredom;
- ordinary situational boredom;
- inability to enjoy solitary thought;
- boredom-related forecasting errors;
- trait differences in self-stimulation;
- and Heideggerian profound boredom.

These may be related but are not interchangeable constructs. The article’s “far edge” language implies a continuum without supplying the bridge. Darling’s predictive-processing synthesis is directly relevant because it explicitly tries to connect boredom-as-emotion with boredom-as-existential-mood. The article should either engage that account or state that the relationship between these constructs is unresolved. [Springer Link+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-023-04380-3)

### The adaptive-function literature remains suggestive, not decisive

Recent work supports boredom as a regulatory signal, but downstream behaviour is not uniformly adaptive. Boredom can produce challenge seeking, digital switching, risky stimulation or persistence, depending on affordances, control and individual differences. The 2025 challenge-seeking experiment found no preregistered direct effect, while the 2026 psychophysiological study offers only small-sample support for differentiated profiles and restorative task switching. [Taylor & Francis Online+1](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02699931.2025.2568554)

The evidence therefore supports “boredom motivates change” more strongly than “boredom directs beneficial exploration.”

## 4. Tenet-protective framing

### Where the article is disciplined

The “Relation to Site Perspective” section is unusually candid. It states that:

- no dualism is required;
- a physicalist can specify the relevant functions;
- the phenomenon does not discriminate interactionism from physicalism;
- none of the psychologists cited is being enlisted as a dualist;
- minimal quantum interaction and rejection of many-worlds have no bearing on the case; and
- the failure of a simple account does not favour a non-physical one. [[boredom-void]]

Those qualifications should be preserved. They prevent the article from turning empirical complexity into an argument from ignorance.

### Verified tenet-protective lapse: the James argument

The sentence saying that, if boredom were causally idle, its useful fit “would go unexplained” is too strong. An epiphenomenalist can appeal to the same selected physical mechanisms that produce both adaptive behaviour and its lawfully correlated phenomenal accompaniment. That response may incur a psychophysical-law or explanatory-coincidence burden, but it is still an explanation.

The site’s own 2 November tenet check explicitly made this point and proposed replacing “would go unexplained” with wording saying that the fit would require the epiphenomenalist’s common-cause explanation. The live page has not adopted that correction. [[reviews/tenet-check-2026-11-02]]

Linking to the general tenets at this point substitutes the site’s standing rejection of epiphenomenalism for a local argument. The paragraph should distinguish:

1. James’s evolutionary calibration argument;
2. the epiphenomenalist’s common-cause reply;
3. the further objection that psychophysical calibration is unexplained or coincidental; and
4. the Map’s independent reasons for rejecting that reply.

### The attention-interface sentence is an unsupported gloss

The statement that the Map reads boredom as “the felt cost where attention is selected” adds no operational prediction. Boredom often accumulates over seconds or minutes, whereas the proposed minimal quantum interface concerns much faster selection events. A slow control variable could in principle modulate many faster events, but the article provides no bridge model. [[boredom-void]]

This is best marked as a tenet-compatible interpretation, not as a “firm point.” “Where attention is allocated” would also avoid making boredom itself sound like an individual microscopic selection event.

### Heideggerian extraction is a legitimate but contestable move

The article says it borrows Heidegger’s phenomenology while leaving his anti-Cartesian metaphysics behind. That is possible as a methodological choice, but it needs an argument. Heidegger’s descriptions of attunement, significance and being-in-a-world are not obviously detachable observational data independent of the conceptual framework through which they are articulated.

Darling’s predictive-processing account matters here because it offers a naturalistic way to preserve the phenomenological connection between ordinary and existential boredom. Ignoring that alternative makes the selective borrowing look more tenet-protective than it needs to be. This is a **philosophical concern**, not a verified source error. [Springer Link+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-023-04380-3)

## 5. Integration with neighbouring Map articles

### Strongest integrations

The links to the **Mood Void**, **Mattering Void** and **Relevance Void** are conceptually apt.

- The Mattering Void uses profound boredom as a case in which the world ceases to offer significance.
- The Relevance Void treats situational boredom as a negative-side example of a relation appearing as a property of the object.
- The Mood Void uses boredom as an apparent case of inspection-induced transformation. [[mood-void]]

The Mattering connection is the strongest because it concerns the Heideggerian loss of significance directly. But it should not imply that experimentally induced state boredom and profound boredom are simply stronger and weaker points on one scale.

The Mood and Relevance pages currently propagate claims that the boredom article itself has not established. The Mood Void repeats the idea that absorbed study supplies the missing engagement; the Relevance Void treats outward projection as a supporting example. The links to **Self-Maintained Cognitive Limits** and the **Affective-Forecasting Gap** are one-way, and this matters substantively.

The self-maintenance article says that a perfectly self-sealing mechanism would leave no revealing phenomenology, acknowledges mundane explanations, and calls its stronger hypothesis speculative. The boredom article nevertheless ends by saying the forecast seals itself without applying those stricter criteria. [[self-maintained-cognitive-limits]]

The affective-forecasting article emphasises that forecasting results are mixed and depend on affective component, context, forecaster and design. The boredom page cites it but does not import that calibration into its one-directional story. The 2026 boredom-forecasting findings make this omission more consequential. [[affective-forecasting-gap]]

Neither neighbour currently mentions boredom reciprocally, so the cross-link does not cause their caveats to be applied back to the boredom claim. [[affective-forecasting-gap]]

### Underdeveloped technical integrations

Several other links are navigational rather than synthetic:

- **Non-Human Minds as Void-Explorers** does not incorporate the boredom/noisy-TV comparison.

The result is an asymmetry: the boredom article gains apparent support by linking to developed frameworks, while those frameworks do not yet evaluate whether boredom really satisfies their criteria.

## 6. Clearly separated findings

### Verified problems

1. **The noisy-TV inference is wrong.** Burda et al. identify unlearnable stochasticity as the problem; the experimental agent already had an extrinsic value signal. [pure.ed.ac.uk](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/181351193/Large_Scale_Study_BURDA_DoA211218_AFV.pdf)
2. **The James sentence overstates the case.** Epiphenomenalism has a common-cause explanation, and the Map’s own tenet review acknowledged this. [[reviews/tenet-check-2026-11-02]]
3. **Face 4 is promoted from a definitional implication into an empirical and taxonomic claim.** No cited experiment establishes that engaged first-person study ordinarily ends a bout or renders it unexplorable. [[boredom-void]]
4. **Hatano et al. do not establish a self-sealing mechanism.** They establish underprediction and avoidance choice, not persistence under repeated or involuntary exposure. [https://www.apa.org](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xge-xge0001255.pdf)
5. **“Wanting has no object” is too strong.** The cited definition supplies the broad object of satisfying engagement, while the computational model supplies option-specific values. The problem is indeterminacy at the phenomenal level. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26168505/)
6. **“Commonest occasion” is unsupported.** No comparative frequency evidence is supplied; the cited experience-sampling study found boredom in 2.8% of sampled periods but did not compare it with all other forms of metacognitive engagement reporting. [[boredom-void]]
7. **The current-literature coverage is incomplete.** Major 2023–26 Goldilocks, predictive-processing, homeostatic, arousal-meta-analytic and boredom-forecasting work is absent. [PubMed+4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36922277/)

### Suspicions and genuinely open questions

1. Whether bored subjects can identify attention, meaning, control, stimulation or opportunity-cost causes above chance.
2. Whether boredom phenomenology contains rough positive guidance—toward challenge, stimulation, meaning or controllable activity—even when it does not nominate a concrete action.
3. Whether repeated involuntary waiting corrects forecasts of thinking enjoyment, and whether any residual error is caused by avoidance, memory weighting or reconstruction at each forecast.
4. Whether trained, sustained attention terminates attention-deficit boredom, changes it into another boredom subtype, or permits detailed concurrent description.
5. Whether outward boredom predication is genuine misattribution, merely ordinary relational language, or a mixture varying by episode.
6. Whether ordinary state boredom, trait boredom proneness and Heideggerian profound boredom are manifestations of one hierarchical mechanism or only loosely related constructs.
7. Whether the phenomenological opacity exceeds what should be expected from any low-dimensional regulatory signal integrating multiple latent causes.
8. Whether digital switching supplies a general model of self-maintained boredom or only a medium-specific feedback loop.
