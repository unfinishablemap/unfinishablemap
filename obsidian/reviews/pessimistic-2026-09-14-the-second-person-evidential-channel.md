## Executive Summary

The article is well hedged sentence by sentence but its *architecture* equivocates on what the "third channel" is. Two of its three evidence bodies (turn-taking corpora, hyperscanning) are third-person measurements of interacting subjects — Schilbach's actual proposal, a paradigm shift *within* the third-person channel — while the thesis it draws ("evidence available only to the one being responded to") is a claim about the participant's own knowing-by-engagement, for which the article supplies no evidence beyond Reddy, whose conclusion it then declines. Downstream of that slide, the Tenet 3 paragraph reads interactionism into the evidence rather than out of it (the same evidence is exactly as available to an epiphenomenalist or a physicalist), and the Relation to Site Perspective converts an epistemic residue into "evidence of ontological difference" after the lead has promised the claim is "epistemic, not metaphysical." Source fidelity: the "dark matter" quotation is a research-note paraphrase promoted to a verbatim quote (the abstract's wording is hedged), the ~200 ms "mode" attributed to Stivers et al. is not in their abstract (which reports a ±250 ms spread around the cross-language *mean*), the hyperscanning and synchrony-artifact claims carry no citation at all, and "protoconversation" is credited to Trevarthen when it is M. C. Bateson's 1975 coinage.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would grant every empirical sentence and deny the inference. Interactive paradigms, dual-EEG, 200-millisecond turn transitions: all of it is neuroscience, and none of it needs "a mind picking up information about another mind." Predictive coupling is two nervous systems modelling each other's motor output — the article says so itself ("each partner modelling and anticipating the other"). The "contingency signal" is a statistical relation between two bodies' behaviour, fully third-personal, fully in the recordings. She would ask what the phrase "evidence *to* you about another's responsiveness" adds beyond "your brain tracks the other body's contingency," and note that the article's answer — that the relevant fact is *relational* — is a fact about the data's shape, not about any second ontological category. Relational quantities (mutual information, cross-correlation) are the bread and butter of physical science. Calling their measurement a distinct "channel" alongside third-person measurement is a category confusion she would expect folk-psychological vocabulary to produce.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett would go straight at the sui generis paragraph and the "contingency signal" gloss. The participant's evidence of being-responded-to arrives *as their experience of the interaction* — felt contingency, felt address. That is either introspectable (then it is first-person report, heterophenomenologically catalogued like any other) or it is behavioural and physiological (then it is third-person, and hyperscanning already has it). The article concedes the reducibility question is "disputed" and holds irreducibility "as a hypothesis rather than a result" — good — but then builds the Relation to Site Perspective as though the hypothesis were established ("none reducible to the others, and their persistent non-convergence is itself the datum"). Dennett's sharpest instrument is the believed-interactivity manipulation in second-person neuroscience's own paradigms: participants who *believe* a gaze-contingent character is a live person respond differently from those told it is pre-recorded, with the same stimulus stream. If the signatures track the belief that one is being responded to rather than the fact, then "the evidence available only to the one being responded to" is equally available to the one who merely takes themselves to be — which makes it a first-person state, not a channel. The article never mentions this class of result.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark has little quantum content to attack here, and would say so pointedly: the article invokes Tenet 3 ("consciousness is legible to another only because it is not causally inert") on evidence that is entirely classical — motor prediction at hundreds of milliseconds, EEG rhythms, corpus statistics. Nothing in the cited timing or synchrony literature is within many orders of magnitude of a regime where a "minimal quantum interaction" could be doing work, and nothing in the article gestures at a mechanism by which a non-physical state pushes "information about a mind into the shared physical arena." He would ask the article to either drop the Tenet 3 paragraph or state plainly that it is a consistency claim, not evidential support.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch would observe that the article discriminates nothing between his picture and the Map's. Reciprocal engagement, predictive coupling and dyad-level observables are exactly what a universal computational account of two interacting agents predicts; an Everettian is as comfortable with Schilbach's programme as a dualist. The only place the Map's distinctive commitments enter is the assertion that "the residue that survives the widening" (you still cannot occupy the other's viewpoint) is evidence for ontological difference — and that residue is the ordinary indexical fact that you are not them, which every physical account also predicts. Reading "I cannot be you" as evidence about the constitution of consciousness is, for him, the same anthropocentric move as reading "I find myself in this branch" as evidence against branching.

### The Empiricist

Popper's ghost would ask for the discriminating test the article owes its own central hypothesis. What observation would show that the second-person channel is *not* sui generis — that it is first-person-plus-third-person recombined? The article names the question but no criterion. Candidates exist inside the field it cites: surrogate-pair (pseudo-dyad) analyses that test whether inter-brain synchrony survives when partners are re-paired with strangers' recordings; yoked-observer designs in which a spectator receives the identical stimulus stream as the interactor; and the believed-interactivity manipulations above. If every "interaction-only" signature is recoverable by one of these, the reducibility side wins. The article's §Synchrony-artifact paragraph asserts that the common-input confound is "open and contested" without citing a single methods paper on it — and the research note that fed the article flagged this exact gap ("sampled but not deeply verified; if the article leans on it, verify at least one primary methods paper"). The article leans on it. He would also note that "in principle" and "literally unavailable" are doing rhetorical work where a design description would do evidential work.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would find the article's most revealing sentence in Where the Map Holds the Line: treating the dyad as bearer "would dissolve the ontological individuality of consciousness the Map maintains." That is offered as a *reason* to decline the constitutive reading, but it is a statement of what the Map wishes to keep, not an argument that it is there to keep. The developmental evidence the article marshals — protoconversation at weeks old, engagement prior to any theory of mind — is on its face evidence that the subject arises *in* relation (dependent co-arising), and the article uses it while refusing its natural reading. He would not ask the article to become enactivist (the deep review rightly marks this as a framework boundary); he would ask it to stop describing a boundary as a finding ("This is a clean case of taking method without ontology") when the method's own developmental data lean the other way.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The "third channel" equivocates between second-person *neuroscience* (third-person study of interacting subjects) and second-person *evidence* (the participant's knowing-by-engagement)
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/the-second-person-evidential-channel.md`
- **Location**: lead ("Second-person neuroscience makes this channel measurable"); §What Only Engagement Reveals, "Dyad-level observables" and "contingency signal" paragraphs (L49–51); §The Third Channel, Bounded (L77)
- **Problem**: Schilbach et al.'s abstract proposes "investigations that allow the study of real-time social encounters in a truly interactive manner" — a paradigm change in what third-person science measures, not a new epistemic route. Hyperscanning is spectating on the dyad from outside; a turn-taking corpus is recorded and coded by observers. Yet the section's synthesising claim is that the coupling "carries information that no third-person recording of either party alone contains" and is "the specific evidence available only to *the one being responded to*." The first half is true only because of the word "alone" (a recording of *both* — hyperscanning — contains it, and the article has just said so); the second half is a claim about the participant's epistemic position, for which the only support is Reddy's developmental work. The closing section then credits the channel as "neurally measurable at the level of the dyad" — but if the participant's evidence is measurable from outside, it collapses into the third-person channel; if it is not, the dyad-level measurements are not evidence for it. The article needs one of these, and currently has both.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Split the two senses explicitly in the lead or at the head of §What Only Engagement Reveals: (a) *second-person neuroscience* — third-person measurement made honest by interactive paradigms, which widens the third-person channel and is where all three evidence bodies live; (b) *second-person evidence* — the participant's own knowing-by-engagement, which is the candidate third channel and rests on Reddy plus the phenomenology of the engaged stance. State that (a) is established and (b) is the hypothesis. Re-scope the "contingency signal" paragraph to say what it can: dyadic contingency is *third-person-measurable* and *only produced under live coupling*; whether the participant has additional, irreducible access to it is the open question of §The sui generis question. Rewrite "neurally measurable at the level of the dyad" in the closing section so that it describes (a), not (b).

### Issue 2: The Tenet 3 paragraph reads interactionism into evidence that is neutral between interactionism, epiphenomenalism and physicalism
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/the-second-person-evidential-channel.md`
- **Location**: §Relation to Site Perspective, "On Tenet 3" (L69): "The second-person channel presupposes that inner states make an outward, detectable difference … That two-way traffic between the phenomenal and the physical is precisely what Tenet 3 commits to … consciousness is legible to another only because it is not causally inert."
- **Problem**: What second-person evidence presupposes is that *states of the other* make an outward difference. A physicalist locates those states in the brain and grants the presupposition trivially; an epiphenomenalist dualist says the neural correlates do the pushing while the phenomenal state rides along, and nothing in reciprocal timing or inter-brain synchrony discriminates "phenomenal state causes expression" from "neural correlate causes expression." The paragraph therefore does not find Tenet 3 in the evidence; it supplies Tenet 3 and then reports finding it. This is the in-framework-argument-that-actually-appeals-to-Map-commitments pattern, applied to tenet support rather than to an opponent. The article's own lead ("The Map endorses the channel while declining the … ontology") and the pupillometry sibling ("consistent with both physicalist and dualist accounts. The Map does not over-claim") show the register the paragraph should be in.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Downgrade from support to consistency, in the pupillometry article's register: second-person evidence is *what Tenet 3 predicts we should find* and is fully consistent with it, but it is equally consistent with epiphenomenalism and physicalism, since what is detected is behaviour and physiology whichever state causes them. If the Map wants more than consistency, the bridge (why the coupling requires the phenomenal state itself to be the cause) lives in the interaction-framework articles and should be linked as the place the argument is made, not restated here as if the second-person data made it.

### Issue 3: Epistemic residue converted to metaphysical evidence after the lead promised the claim was "epistemic, not metaphysical"
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/the-second-person-evidential-channel.md`
- **Location**: L31 ("The claim to be careful about is *epistemic*, not metaphysical") versus L67 ("The residue that survives the widening is what the Map reads as evidence of ontological, not merely methodological, difference") and L73 ("none reducible to the others, and their persistent non-convergence is itself the datum the Map reads dualistically")
- **Problem**: The residue is an *epistemic* fact — even a widened evidence base does not let you occupy the other's point of view. L67 and L73 read that fact as evidence about how consciousness is constituted. This is the interpretive equivocation the evidential-status discipline names (epistemic reading evidenced, metaphysical reading asserted), and the hedge "the Map reads" does not supply the missing bridge. Worse, L73's "none reducible to the others" asserts as a premise the very irreducibility that §The sui generis question, twelve lines earlier, holds "as a hypothesis rather than a result." The datum "persistent non-convergence of three channels" inherits the status of that hypothesis; the article cannot both hold the hypothesis open and rest a dualist reading on the three-way non-convergence. (The prior deep review praised the article for avoiding "possibility/probability slippage"; this is a different slippage — status inherited from a hypothesis is asserted as a datum — and it was not checked.)
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Two edits. (i) Make L73 conditional on the sui generis hypothesis: "*if* the second-person channel is irreducible, three-way non-convergence joins the two-way non-convergence the Map's methodology article already reads dualistically; if it is not, the article's evidential contribution is confined to widening the third-person channel." (ii) At L67, name where the bridge from non-convergence to ontological difference is argued (the methodology article's inference-to-best-explanation) and state that this article inherits, and does not add to, that argument. The deep review's stability note that hedges must not be strengthened into claims is honoured by both edits — they weaken, not strengthen.

### Issue 7: Reddy's "second-person solution" is cited for support and its conclusion silently declined
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/the-second-person-evidential-channel.md`
- **Location**: L45 (Reddy's solution: "we know other minds insofar as we can *respond* to them") versus L77 ("It neither closes the [[problem-of-other-minds|problem of other minds]]")
- **Problem**: Reddy's thesis is that the "problem" is an artefact of the spectatorial stance and that engagement *is* knowing other minds. The article takes her developmental evidence as its strongest datum for the channel's priority and then concludes the channel does not close the problem — without saying why her solution fails. The reason the Map has (engagement yields knowledge of the other *as responder*, not of their phenomenal interior) is a Map commitment Reddy explicitly rejects as the Cartesian picture; so this is a framework boundary that the article treats as if it were uncontested. ## Counterarguments to Address

### The channel is measurable from outside, or it is a channel — not both
- **Current content says**: the third channel is "neurally measurable at the level of the dyad" *and* consists in "evidence available only to the one being responded to."
- **A critic would argue**: whatever hyperscanning measures is by construction available to a non-participant; whatever is available only to the participant is by construction not what hyperscanning measures. The article's evidence is for the first, its thesis is the second.
- **Suggested response**: see Issue 1. Present second-person neuroscience as the widening of the third-person channel it is, and the participant's channel as the hypothesis it is.

### Believed interactivity dissociates "being responded to" from "taking oneself to be"
- **Current content says**: the contingency signal is "the specific evidence available only to *the one being responded to*."
- **A critic would argue**: second-person neuroscience's own gaze-contingent paradigms manipulate whether participants *believe* the partner is live; where the neural and behavioural signatures follow the belief, the "evidence" is a first-person state the participant brings, not information the coupling delivers.
- **Suggested response**: engage the believed-interactivity result directly (it is a Schilbach-group staple): either it shows the channel is partly constituted by the participant's stance — which the Map can accept, since it wants the enabling reading — or it shows the field has not yet separated the two, in which case say so in §The sui generis question.

### "Relational" does not mean "non-third-personal"
- **Current content says**: the coupling carries information "that no first-person introspective report can supply — because the relevant fact is relational."
- **A critic would argue**: cross-correlation, mutual information and phase-locking are relational quantities routinely computed from third-person recordings; relationality is a fact about the data's arity, not about the epistemic mode of access.
- **Suggested response**: concede it; the argument for a third *channel* has to rest on the participant's access, not on the data's shape.
