## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found (all fixed)

1. **Wollheim quotation misdescribed, and copied from the secondary source (L47).** The article said "The sentence runs, in full:" and then quoted a clause. *Art and Its Objects* 2nd ed. (Google Books volume `NBmYGQDjM6sC`, search-within, p. 233; control "physical object" returned 10 hits on the same id) reads: "In doing so Realism acknowledges a well-entrenched principle in aesthetics, which may be called the Acquaintance principle, and which insists that judgments of aesthetic value, unlike judgments of moral knowledge, must be based on first-hand experience of their objects and are not, except within very narrow limits, transmissible from one person to another." So:
   - the quoted span is the final relative clause, not the sentence;
   - the book spells it "judgments", while the article's "judgements" is SEP's British rendering, which the 07-09 research note certified as "VERIFIED VERBATIM" against SEP and the Lexicon only.
   
   **Fix (+7)**: "Wollheim formulated the principle that has anchored the field — "a well-entrenched principle in aesthetics", as he calls it, "which insists that judgments of aesthetic value, unlike judgments of moral knowledge, … another" (1980: 233)." Both fragments are verbatim. The subject of "insists" is the principle, as in the source. Shelley's 2022/2023 abstract independently quotes Wollheim's "a well-entrenched principle in aesthetics". The change also makes Riggle's unquoted "once well entrenched" (L78) visibly an echo of Wollheim. Para 2's "in this sentence" and "Wollheim's sentence" still refer correctly.
2. **Budd's conclusion understated (L72; position strength).** The article said Budd's argument "weakens the principle considerably". Budd's abstract (OpenAlex, DOI 10.1093/bjaesthetics/43.4.386) ends "I try to show that this style of argument fails and that the Acquaintance Principle should be rejected". SEP §2.1 likewise lists Budd 2003 among "those who hold that AP itself is false". This mirrors the 08-02 Livingston error in the other direction: an opponent's case was reported as weaker than it is, which flatters the Map. **Fix (+4)**: "…can suffice, and he concludes that the principle should be rejected."

### Medium Issues Found (all fixed)

3. **Map evaluation inside Riggle's exposition (L78).** "…at the cost of making aesthetic valuing a social practice rather than a distinctive epistemic relation." Riggle's abstract calls the social-practice upshot "surprising", not a cost, and the "rather than a distinctive epistemic relation" contrast does not appear there. The section's own rule (exposition first; RSP "marks clearly where it goes beyond the source debate") puts the cost reading in RSP, which already makes it ("is not an epistemic barrier at all"). **Fix (−2)**: "…; the upshot, which he calls surprising, is that aesthetic valuing is a social practice." The epistemic contrast survives in the same paragraph's "rather than a condition on knowledge" and in RSP.
4. **Encyclopedia gloss quoted as if it were the study's item wording (L82).** "less permissible and legitimate compared to forming such beliefs based on first-hand experience" is verbatim, but it is the SEP experimental-aesthetics entry's description of Andow's design. It sat inside "asked participants whether … is '…'", so it read as the question participants saw. The sentence already says the study is "known here only through" SEP, so the provenance was visible; the quotation marks were the only problem. **Fix (−3)**: de-quoted and paraphrased: "…is less permissible and legitimate than forming them first-hand, or than taking testimony about size or price". The second comparison matches SEP's result sentence ("compared to testimony about non-aesthetic properties").
5. **Shelley called "the most hostile" recent treatment (L100).** Shelley's abstract opens by looking for "something true at which those who have asserted it have been aiming". SEP calls his reading "revisionary". Robson 2022 ("an extreme form of 'optimism'") and Budd ("should be rejected") are more hostile. The sentence's own predicate, "the principle keeps its plausibility only by giving up the epistemic role", fits Shelley and Riggle, so the superlative was a mislabel, not a stand-in for Robson. **Fix (0)**: "The most sympathetic recent treatment and Shelley's revisionary one arrive at the same place".

### Low Issues Found (fixed)
