It admits that the hours-long lability window is *less* temporally localised than other venues (L53). It names the quantum-sensitivity posit that the lability data do not supply (L63). ## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`selective-correction-and-reconstruction-paradox` L91-95 finds a version of the hard problem that is new to the Map. The brain handles signals in three modes: silent repair, faithful pass-through, and alternation under ambiguity. Each mode feels different: seamless, degraded, a sudden switch. The functional account explains which mode runs. It does not explain why there are three distinct *seemings*. The page also answers the illusionist fairly (L95). It grants the heterophenomenological reading and then asks why the functional differentiation exists at all. Chalmers would see the meta-problem put in perceptual terms.

`phenomenology-of-imagination` L116 makes a strong explanatory-gap argument from indeterminacy. An imagined face with no particular eye colour is "present yet unresolved". The physicalist reply ("incomplete encoding") is stated and answered: "An incomplete file has no character at all." That is the gap stated in terms of a single experience.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

`reconsolidation-as-selection-window` is the most careful venue argument in the Map's memory writing. It identifies a physical interval in which a trace is "poised between reconstructions" (L61). It also refuses to treat biochemical openness as quantum openness: "an interval on that scale spans an enormous number of already-decohered microscopic events" (L63). Stapp would welcome the venue. He would also recognise the honest statement of what his own programme still owes, a quantum-sensitive step in reconstruction. The token-authorship claim (L65) is especially clean. Physical machinery produces the *candidates*, and the subject selects *which one* restabilises. That maps directly onto the Process-1 / Process-2 split.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`phenomenology-of-forgetting` L79-89 gives four textures of forgetting: pushing an intrusion away, letting a memory go, a name dissolving, and "the effort that has no object". It then notes that successful forgetting leaves nothing to report. "The reportable phenomenology of forgetting is therefore almost entirely the phenomenology of *attempted* or *incipient* forgetting" (L89). This is first-person method used well: it maps where introspection gives out without pretending to go further. Ricoeur is handled with care. L81 says he "poses, but does not simply affirm" a happy forgetting.

`phenomenology-of-imagination` L70 uses Husserl's neutrality modification as a structural feature: "There is something it is like to *know, while experiencing it, that you are generating the experience*." The Walton passage (L96) makes a sharp point. Quasi-emotion needs a prop and a game, and self-generated imagination has neither.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

`implicit-memory` L151-159 recasts skill automaticity as concrescence dominated by physical prehension. Choking then becomes conceptual prehension intruding where physical prehension was enough (L157). The page does not claim that Whitehead's reading is evidence. It uses it as a way of describing expert action without reducing it to mechanical replay. Whitehead would also like `default-mode-network` L87: the DMN's resting activity is read as *ongoing* constraint rather than a baseline. On this reading, the self is something continually made.

This persona's praise **does not** extend to `implicit-memory` L133 (see Calibration concern 3).

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`reconsolidation-as-selection-window` L79 makes the identity stake precise. If the posited reading holds, the continuity of the self is "partly *authored* by the subject across time rather than merely *suffered* as drift". The bare lability fact gives "a rewriting self without an authoring one". That pair of phrases is the libertarian's self-forming action applied to memory, together with the admission that the data give only the rewriting half. `phenomenology-of-forgetting` L103 adds a clinical point: deficient suppression in PTSD, and suppression training that relieved symptoms (Mamat & Anderson 2023). So the volitional act has consequences for a life, not only for a laboratory score.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`voids/narrative-void` L68-70 is the wing's best new material. Before any story is told, sharp-wave-ripple tagging, recall-gated consolidation and event-boundary flushing have already decided which fragments can be narrated. "The storyteller therefore works on material whose selection it neither authored nor witnessed." The closing sentence limits the claim correctly: these findings "sharpen the gap … without, on their own, settling what consciousness is". L92 adds a second-order point. Knowing that your self-narrative is unreliable "does not give you a reliable one".

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

The wing makes no claims about minimal organisms, so this persona's main test does not apply. Its praise goes to three pieces of restraint:

- **`mental-imagery` L129** (the strongest single passage). The page proposed a test that separates dualism from identity theory: reduced rehearsal benefit despite *intact* neural circuits. It then reports that the controlled study (Dupont et al. 2024) found the circuits themselves impaired, "which is precisely the condition under which identity theory expects reduced benefit … if anything, the newest evidence points the other way". Reporting that a test the page designed has gone against it is exactly the restraint this persona values.
- **`default-mode-network` L104 and L112.** The page updated its own psychedelic argument after Siegel et al. 2024 (cortex-wide desynchronisation, not DMN-specific suppression). On meditation, it cites Kral et al. 2022 (no structural change from MBSR, n=218) against the older cross-sectional morphometry.
- **`reconsolidation-as-selection-window` L98.** "Residual, hard-to-predict variation … proves nothing either way." The page refuses to treat unexplained variance as room for consciousness.

Against this, the same persona flags the older pages' uses of felt effort as evidence (Calibration concerns 1 and 2) and `mental-imagery` L153 (Calibration concern 5).

## Calibration Concerns

**1. `phenomenology-of-imagination` L118: felt imaginative effort "remains the stronger evidence" for mental causation.** Sibling pages reject this move. `phenomenology-of-forgetting` L105 says felt agency "is not first-person-verifiable as a cause (see the agency void on this verification circularity)". `mental-imagery` L117 calls the whole topic an "explanatory standoff". L118 ranks the phenomenology *above* the motor-imagery evidence, and the two sentences before it say that evidence does not adjudicate. Relatedly, L116's closing "The Map's Dualism tenet finds support in the irreducible phenomenal signature" states as a result what the paragraph argued only as a reading. L90's "An AI generates novel text without experiencing the semantic landscape" states as fact something the Map treats as open elsewhere. Fix: clause-level edits. Say the felt effort is what an epiphenomenalist must explain away, not stronger evidence. Link `[[agency-void]]`. Hedge L90. The page has 1239 words of headroom.

**2. `mental-imagery` contradicts its own standoff section in three places.** L44 (lead): "the causal chain runs from conscious imagining to physical performance", asserted before any hedge. L161: the felt effort "is not epiphenomenal accompaniment — it reflects consciousness doing work". L167: phenomenal difference "beyond what functional differences predict supports the claim that consciousness is irreducible". That sentence comes right after its own admission of frontoparietal-visual connectivity differences. It also sits beside L129's report that aphantasics' neural simulation circuits are impaired, which is a *functional* difference. The body (L113-117, L129, L141-149) is well calibrated. The lead and the tenet section have not caught up with it. Last deep review 2026-07-11, the oldest in the wing.

**3. `implicit-memory` L125-133 versus `mental-imagery` L55/L67 and `selective-correction` L61: are priming and blindsight experiential?** Mental-imagery puts priming in the "Unconscious / Involuntary" cell and says it operates through processes "that never reach awareness". Selective-correction L61 calls blindsight "processing without either transmission or correction", where "the subject experiences nothing". Implicit-memory lists both and suggests they may involve anoetic experience. It then concludes (L133): "There may be no purely computational cognitive processing—every operation occurs within some experiential mode." Here the Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist conflict. L133 is hedged ("may"), but it draws a wide conclusion from Tulving's account of *procedural* memory. It also undercuts the contrast cases the other two pages depend on, since blindsight is the Map's standard example of processing without experience. Fix: scope L131-133 to procedural and skill cases, where anoetic character is argued. Say that subliminal priming and blindsight are the cases where the Map's own pages treat experience as absent. This is **tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating**, and the page should say so.

**4. `implicit-memory` L191-193: the quick Many Worlds argument.** "There would be branches where the expert misses every shot … The statistical regularity of expert performance suggests genuine selection." Skilled performance is mostly classical. Branches where every shot misses have negligible Born weight, and an Everettian predicts the observed regularity. The hubs have retired this move: `consciousness-evolution-and-biology` L148 calls it "closer to a restatement of the tenet" and puts the dispute on global exclusion. Yesterday's evolution-wing review (Calibration concern 2) found the same pattern in three other pages. L196's "happens to track performance degradation *perfectly*" also overstates. The page's own L101/L188 concede a common-cause reading. And L41 (lead) still says the felt quality "is precisely what distinguishes it from mere neural noise", which is stronger than the body's conclusion ("not plausibly idle"). The page has 155 words of headroom, so every fix must be length-neutral.

**5. `mental-imagery` L153 files great-ape cognition as "unconscious processing".** "Unconscious processing — even the sophisticated cognition demonstrated by great apes — cannot achieve logical reasoning…". The source page, `baseline-cognition` L53-55, says something narrower: cognition "without *substantial* conscious contribution", with apes lacking *metarepresentational* capacity, not consciousness. The sentence moves ape consciousness *down* the evidential scale. That is the reverse of the usual risk, and just as much a calibration error. Fix: "cognition without metarepresentation". This costs about +0 words.

**6. `narrative-coherence` overclaims, and never meets Strawson.** L35 says narrative coherence "provides evidence that the substantial self is more than a philosophical postulate". L73: the breakdowns "confirm that narrative coherence is neither automatic nor epiphenomenal". Breakdowns show dependence on capacities; they do not show causal efficacy. L81: "Each choice made for narrative reasons … is an instance of consciousness selecting among physical possibilities". This is stated flat. The page treats the absence of coherence only as pathology (depression, dissociation, amnesia, L63-73). It never considers Galen Strawson's "Against Narrativity", in which "episodic" people who lack diachronic narrative feel no loss. The objection is directly relevant, and both `voids/narrative-void` L82-84 and `diachronic-agency-and-personal-narrative` already handle it (`grep -ci strawson concepts/narrative-coherence.md` → 0; positive control: 2 files carry "Against Narrativity"). The page's "someone must be doing the constructing" (L61) also sits uneasily with narrative-void L96-98. There, the construction is done by a physical interpreter module, and consciousness may be an *audience* "systematically misled about its own causal efficacy". The page has 1701 words of headroom, the most in the wing.

**7. `selective-correction-and-reconstruction-paradox`: bistable selection is asserted, not posited.** L45: "consciousness must select among them". L69: consciousness "actively participates in determining which reconstruction becomes experience". L109: "Bistable rivalry provides the cleanest case of conscious influence". L101 reads faster attentional biasing in rivalry as "a more direct form of conscious influence". The page follows the pattern of the reconsolidation page (the brain proposes candidates, consciousness selects) without that page's discipline. Attentional biasing of rivalry is also predicted by attention-as-precision-weighting with no conscious lever. L111's quantum hedge is good, but it arrives only in the fourth tenet paragraph. Separately, and **as a lead to verify at the publisher**: L67 says meditators maintained a percept "sometimes indefinitely" (Carter et al. 2005). My recollection is that only a minority of monks reported complete stability, over a *five-minute* trial. I am not asserting this. Check it before changing the text. **No task is minted for this item.** The next deep-review of the page should take it up.
