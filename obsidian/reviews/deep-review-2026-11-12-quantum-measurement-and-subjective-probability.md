## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Attribution error: von Neumann presented as proposing that consciousness causes collapse.** The page said "Von Neumann (1932) and Wigner (1961) proposed that consciousness does cause collapse—not just belief updating, but real physical change." The live SEP entry "Quantum Approaches to Consciousness" (Atmanspacher; fetched) says von Neumann concluded that the placement of the boundary between observed and observing system makes no difference, calls his stance "fairly cautious", says London and Bauer (1939) "went further and proposed that it is indeed human consciousness which completes the quantum measurement process" in terms of "an update of the observer's knowledge", and that Wigner then "radicalized this proposal" by "suggesting an impact of consciousness on the physical state of the measured system". The Map's own `concepts/von-neumann-wigner-interpretation` L48 and `topics/indexical-identity-quantum-measurement` L79 already state the cautious reading. **Fixed**: the paragraph now gives the three steps in that order and names the SEP entry as its source. The scare-quoted "outside" perspective attributed to London and Bauer was dropped, since it is not their term.
2. **Stale gloss of another Map page.** Further Reading described `quantum-biology-and-neural-consciousness` as "Converging evidence that quantum effects operate in neural systems". The live page's description reads "warm-biology precedent is real; neural quantum relevance remains an open, contested question". **Fixed** to match the live page.
3. **Dropped qualifiers on QBism.** (a) "they describe an agent's knowledge": the SEP entry (Healey; fetched) says a quantum state specifies "the agent's coherent degree of belief (credence)" about "possible future experiences"; QBists distinguish belief from knowledge. **Fixed** to "degrees of belief about future experience". (b) "developed by Christopher Fuchs, Carlton Caves, and Rüdiger Schack": the SEP entry says QBism "began as a collaboration between Caves, Fuchs, & Schack ... although Caves no longer considers himself a QBist". **Fixed** with that qualifier.
4. **Paraphrase that was a near-quotation of a journalist, attributed to Fuchs.** "In Fuchs's view, the wave function does not describe the world—it describes an agent's expectations about future experience." The source sentence is Gefter's, in the Quanta introduction (fetched): "In other words, Fuchs argued, the wave function does not describe the world — it describes the observer." **Fixed**: now quoted verbatim and credited to Gefter's summary. This also gives the Gefter reference its first inline citation.
5. **Calibration, No Many Worlds entry.** "MWI's probability derivations remain circular" was flatter than the linked page, which says each strategy "faces objections that its premises beg the question" and marks Zhang (2026) as an unverified preprint. **Fixed** to "face a standing circularity objection". The entry's last sentence now marks the indexical position as "a commitment rather than a demonstrated result", matching `tenets.md` as it stands. The entry did not infer the tenet from experience, so no further change was needed.
6. **Calibration, Dualism entry.** "The phenomenological gap in subjective interpretations supports dualism." A gap in QBism's account of agents is not evidence for dualism; the next sentence is a conditional. A reviewer who accepts the tenets would still flag it. **Fixed** to "fits dualism without establishing it".
7. **"Perry and Lewis showed"** → "Perry (1979) and Lewis (1979) argued". Contested philosophical conclusions; `concepts/indexical-knowledge-and-identity` itself separates the epistemic from the metaphysical step.
8. **Reference orphans, both directions.** Inline with no entry: London and Bauer 1939, Zeh, Tegmark, Perry, Lewis. Entries with no inline citation: Healey, the SEP consciousness entry, Rovelli, Gefter, Fuchs 2016 (quoted but undated inline). **Fixed**: five entries added, five inline citations added, and the unattributed SEP entry given its author.
