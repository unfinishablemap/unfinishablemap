It rescoped L81 to bare-correlation epiphenomenalism, installed the phenomenal-concept concession at L83, and rewrote the tenet ground at L145 — but left **L85 and L87**, inside the same section, asserting flatly the very claim L83 had just conceded is escapable. ## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

The article's anti-eliminativist move is the bare regress at L52 — if phenomenal consciousness is an illusion, "*that very representation* is either phenomenally conscious … or it is not." The Map's own `concepts/illusionism.md` L93 says in terms that this proves nothing: "he is right that the bare regress proves nothing: a representational system need not instantiate what it represents." The flagship runs, as one horn of a dilemma against all materialism, an argument the dedicated article concedes is worthless, and disposes of Frankish's reply in a subordinate clause ("this appears to redefine rather than explain the phenomenon") where `illusionism.md` L113–115 calls the same programme "alive on this question, not stalled." The hub is a decade behind its own specialist page.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

The Dennett engagement at L65 states its own standard and then fails it. It promises "the non-question-begging reason" and supplies: contemplative training sharpens phenomenal discrimination, therefore phenomenal concepts are not confused. Two problems. First, the claim is empirical and carries **zero citations** — "The reports point the second way" is the load the whole reply rests on. Second, and worse for the article's own discipline, first-person meditative reports are exactly what **heterophenomenology** declines to admit as evidence about phenomenal character; Dennett takes them as third-person data about what subjects believe. The article never mentions heterophenomenology, though `illusionism.md` L61 correctly identifies it as "the methodological foundation." So L71's summary claim — "Each criticism turns on standards the opponent's own programme endorses" — is not earned in the Dennett case. The reply appeals to a species of evidence the opponent's framework rejects, which is engagement across the boundary presented as engagement inside it.

The L137 section makes it explicit: "a functionalist could attribute this to refined internal monitoring, [but] the reports describe qualitative character … that functional descriptions omit." That the descriptions omit it is precisely the point at issue. It is asserted, not argued, and the functionalist reply the sentence raises is never answered.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Your headline number is wrong and has been wrong in 22 articles. You write "seven orders of magnitude longer" (L99). Tegmark 2000 gives 10⁻¹³ s for microtubules; Hagan et al. 2002 recalculate to 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ s. That is eight to nine orders. The likeliest origin of the error is a conflation: 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻²⁰ is a seven-order *span inside Tegmark's own range*, which is not the size of anybody's correction. Getting it wrong in the Map's favour would at least be comprehensible; you have understated your own best rebuttal by one to two orders for months.

More tellingly, L99 presents Hagan as standing unchallenged. Sixteen of the twenty-two corpus loci omit Reimers et al. (2009) and McKemmish et al. (2009), which contest the recalibration and which the Map's own better-calibrated pages (`entanglement-binding-hypothesis` L76, `quantum-holism-and-phenomenal-unity` L128) treat as mandatory. Your `project/outer-reviewer-service-calibration.md` L40 records that Claude Opus 4.7 flagged the "seven orders of magnitude" framing "presented as settled where contested" — the contestedness was patched in six files and the arithmetic in none.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

L147 is the article's most honest sentence and the one that should worry it most: the case against causal closure is conceded to depend on collapse being real. That makes the entire quantum section conditional on Tenet 4, which is a bare commitment, so the argument against materialism inherits its conditionality. The article does not say so. It presents the quantum section as a positive argument *for* room-for-consciousness rather than as an argument available only to those who have already rejected MWI.

### The Empiricist

The article has a Falsifiability section and then repeals it in its last sentence. L133: "materialists need a *positive* explanation of consciousness, while dualists need only that materialism fails. The burden of proof lies with the stronger claim." If dualism needs only that materialism fails, it has no positive commitments, and a position with no positive commitments has no falsifiers — which is what the section three paragraphs above was trying to supply. Worse, the burden allocation is asserted rather than argued, and it is exactly what the opponent reverses: materialists take the posit of a second ontological category to be the stronger claim. L151's "None of this proves dualism" is the honest register; L133 undercuts it.

And the inference is invalid independently of burden. "Materialism fails" does not yield dualism. Panpsychism, neutral monism, Russellian monism, and mysterianism all accept the hard problem and reject dualism. The article never names one of them.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

The Contemplative Perspectives section recruits *vipassanā* as evidence for a persisting phenomenal character that survives the dissolution of conceptual overlay — "what dissolves is the conceptual overlay, not the qualitative character" (L65). That is a substantive and contestable reading of what the practice discloses, offered without a single source, and it runs against the direction the tradition's own analysis takes: sustained observation of *vedanā* is classically a route to *anattā*, not to a stable qualitative substrate. Borrowing the practice's authority while reversing its conclusion is the least defensible move in the article.

### Burden of proof
- **Current content says** (L133): dualists need only that materialism fails; the burden lies with the stronger claim.
- **A critic would argue**: positing a second ontological category *is* the stronger claim, and by your own reasoning the burden is yours. Meanwhile "we need only that the rival fails" is what makes a research programme degenerate.
- **Suggested response**: drop the burden-shift and let the Falsifiability section stand on its own. The three listed disconfirmers are the article's genuinely Popperian content and are undercut by the sentence that follows them.
