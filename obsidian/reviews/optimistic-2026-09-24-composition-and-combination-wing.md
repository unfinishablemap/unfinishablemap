## Executive Summary

**This wing contains some of the Map's most honest rival engagement.** In it the Map repeatedly says "we have not refuted this" and then explains exactly why. `composition-question-rivals` gives deflationism, Bird's compression criterion and Markosian's brutalism a full hearing and concludes that the Map "has neither refuted nor needs to refute" any of them. `consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-composition` takes apart its own convergence argument: van Inwagen reaches life, not consciousness, and McQueen and Tsuchiya explicitly decouple their criterion from consciousness. It then keeps only what survives. `open-individualism-and-the-de-combination-problem` concedes that physicalism also avoids combination, that "locatable is weaker than approachable", and that interface unity is the formulation "that should govern".

**The wing is calibrated in layers, and the older layer has not caught up.** The strongest concessions landed on 2026-07-30 (commits e6a8e47873 and d277aee5b5 on the de-combination bridge). That was after the 07-18 review judged the cluster "resolved honestly" and after the last edits to `panpsychisms-combination-problem` (07-18), `combination-problem` (07-25) and `composition-and-consciousness` (07-11). Those three pages still hold the claims the newer pages retired:

- `composition-and-consciousness` L121 credits "the agreement of Van Inwagen, Merricks, and McQueen and Tsuchiya on consciousness as the composition criterion". Its own L61 says McQueen and Tsuchiya "explicitly decouple" the criterion from consciousness.
- `panpsychisms-combination-problem` L125 prefers the interaction problem as "*empirically approachable*". The bridge article retires exactly that word.
- `combination-problem` L167 says Khan et al. 2024 "support quantum mechanisms for consciousness".

This is the convergence-damping pattern: the older pages look converged because nobody edited them, while the pages they depend on moved. The fixes are length-cheap, because the replacement wording already exists in a sibling page.

A second finding carries more risk. `composition-and-consciousness` L97 quotes William James as writing "we cannot deduce from it what any sum of feelings amounts to". That string does not occur in *Principles of Psychology* Vol. 1 (Gutenberg #57628), where the mind-dust chapter sits. Two positive controls in the same text each hit once: "shut in its own skin" and "hundred-and-first". It also does not occur in *A Pluralistic Universe* (Gutenberg #11984). This is the mind-dust cluster's recurring fabrication vein, on a page that an earlier sweep recorded as "confirmed clean". That verdict applied only to the specific defect strings the sweep searched for.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would recognise his own dialectic treated with care. He is the author of the three-way split into subject, quality and structure combination. `cosmopsychism` L60 applies the same split to decomposition: "folding it into a single 'de-combination problem' hides where a reply has made progress". This lets the page reach four separate verdicts on four cosmopsychist replies (L71–73) instead of one blanket dismissal. `combination-problem` L128 quotes his pessimism accurately and then fences it: "His pessimism is about solving the combination problem, not a concession that panpsychism lacks any advantage over physicalism". A property dualist wants the rival represented at its own author's strength, and this page does that.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp would find the wing's quantum passages mostly well placed. `consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-composition` L133 states that the composition argument "does not by itself support quantum interaction" and that Minimal Quantum Interaction is "a downstream Map hypothesis, not a result this article secures". That is the right placement. `open-individualism-and-the-de-combination-problem` L117 goes further than any other page in the wing. It concedes that a Born-preserving mechanism is "observationally indistinguishable from chance under any unconditioned aggregate test", so only a deviation conditioned on intention would test it.

Stapp would object where the older primer runs ahead of this. `composition-and-consciousness` L125 says the Born rule "describes precisely such an interface: the point where non-compositional consciousness meets compositional physics". L123 says bidirectional causation "follows naturally from non-compositionality". Both reverse the topic page's direction of support. Non-compositionality is compatible with a unified-locus interaction, but it does not deliver one.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Nagel would praise `composition-and-consciousness` L73, the plainest statement on the Map of why perspectives resist addition. One person sees red, another sees blue, and "nothing about placing these two experiences side by side, or even causally connecting the two brains, yields a third experience". He would also praise `composition-question-rivals` L125, which states the first-person datum against the compressionist without claiming to have won: "a viewpoint is not a description of its parts that a shorter description could recover". That page then gives the compressionist the best reply available: a unified subject may be the *paradigm* of efficient compression. It does not treat the datum as decisive.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead appears only briefly (`combination-problem` L62: "drops of experience" compound naturally rather than combining across unbridgeable gaps). The wing's real process-friendly strength is structural. `cosmopsychism` keeps priority monism (Schaffer) apart from existence monism, and keeps grounding apart from constitution (L29, L41). Seeing wholes as prior to parts, and relations as more than summation, is Whiteheadian ground. The page handles it without crude substance talk. **Constraint check:** nowhere in the wing does process-philosophical resonance upgrade anything's evidential status. The Madhyamaka "dependent arising" passage (`panpsychisms-combination-problem` L107) is treated as "a genuine third option" and is then pressed, not adopted. Clean.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

This wing touches agency only through Merricks. `consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-composition` L63 handles it with care: consciousness "may be the only widely-cited candidate for a composite exhibiting powers that genuinely exceed those of its parts—though whether it succeeds even here depends on accepting Merricks' contested claim". Kane would value the conditional. Irreducible causal power is exactly what libertarian agency needs, and the page does not pretend Merricks has already secured it.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`panpsychisms-combination-problem` L125 gives the mysterian option real space: perhaps the combination problem "reveals the limits of human cognition rather than the structure of reality". `composition-and-consciousness` L115 lists it as one of three genuine alternatives. `combination-problem` L126 contains the wing's most McGinn-friendly sentence. It separates the *epistemic* reading ("we cannot conceive or introspect such a merging") from the *metaphysical* one ("no such merging *can* occur"), and calls the latter "a further bet" that owes an account of split-brain and dissociative-identity cases. McGinn would say that separating the two readings is the entire point.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This persona's verdict is the most important one for this wing, and it is split.

**Praise, and plenty of it.** `composition-question-rivals` L35 invokes the evidential-status discipline by name and says the Map "claims to have refuted none of them". L135 rates the composition-based support for dualism as "suggestive at best, and weaker still once the deflationary challenge is granted its due". `consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-composition` L85 grants the selection-effect objection "real force" and closes by saying the convergence "is consistent with a shared truth and equally with a shared blind spot". L97–99 of the same page say that the choice between dualist interaction and monist constitution "is settled—for the Map—by prior commitment, not by the compositional data". That sentence is the model of *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating*: the page had every structural opportunity to upgrade and chose not to. `manyism` L66 adds the mirror discipline: a rival's unmet burden "is a cost the monist owes, not a point scored *for* the Map's ontology". `open-individualism-and-the-de-combination-problem` L109 names a hidden double-count, where one haecceity posit is doing duty in two arguments: "one bet staked twice rather than two mutually supporting rejections". Birch would single out that sentence.

**Flags (calibration concerns, not praise):**

1. **`combination-problem` L163–169**, "Empirical Distinguishability". L167: "Khan et al. 2024 … support quantum mechanisms for consciousness". An epothilone B delay in anaesthetic onset shows at most that microtubules are involved in how the anaesthetic acts. The Map's own `topics/the-strong-emergence-of-consciousness` L151 puts it correctly: "What raises the prior without supporting it". L165 says the nematode "fails behavioral markers of unified consciousness", with no source cited. L169 says interactionism "generates predictions testable by current methods". The bridge article's "locatable is weaker than approachable" (L117) retires this register.
2. **`panpsychisms-combination-problem` L125 and L141.** L125: interaction is "an *empirically approachable* mystery" and therefore "preferable". L141: "the Map's trade is the more promising one". Both `combination-problem` L193 and the bridge's L117 say that this comparison is "a question the Map acknowledges rather than presumes to have settled". This page still presumes it.
3. **`composition-and-consciousness` L43 and L121.** L43 says "Several leading answers converge on consciousness as the criterion" and calls this "one of the strongest indicators". L121 says "the agreement of Van Inwagen, Merricks, and McQueen and Tsuchiya on consciousness as the composition criterion" and "the irreducibility the Map asserts is not merely claimed—it is grounded". Against these: the page's own L61 ("they explicitly decouple that composition criterion from consciousness itself"), the topic page's L53 (van Inwagen's route "reaches *organic unity*", and the step to consciousness is "a Map-internal posit") and `composition-question-rivals` L135 ("suggestive at best"). The primer is less calibrated than the pages it summarises.

## Content Strengths

### concepts/composition-question-rivals.md
- **Strongest point**: The three-way sort at L37–43. Deflationism denies that the question is deep. Compression gives a non-conscious informative answer. Brutalism grants a determinate but unanalysable answer. The page then names deflationism as the most dangerous rival "because it attacks a premise the Map's argument inherits rather than defends".
- **Notable quote**: "In every case the same discipline governs: the Map moves the argument one step and does not end it."
- **Why it works**: Each reply has the same two parts: concede where the disagreement is bedrock, then press the burden that can be pressed without question-begging. Every reply also says the other side may still win ("the pragmatic explanation may well succeed"). This is how a rivals page should be written. It stays over-length under a standing accept-as-survey veto, and nothing here argues for reopening that.

### topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-composition.md
- **Strongest point**: Honest demolition of its own headline argument. L53 takes van Inwagen out of the consciousness camp. L69 does the same for McQueen and Tsuchiya, with their own words ("insufficient for having consciousness, but it is sufficient for existence"). L85 grants the selection effect. The convergence claim that survives is small and defensible.
- **Notable quote**: "it is consistent with a shared truth and equally with a shared blind spot, the present case deciding between neither."
- **Why it works**: The article gives up its easy form in its first paragraph, which is the right order for a reader who stops early. Only 36 words of headroom remain, so any future edit must be length-neutral.

### topics/open-individualism-and-the-de-combination-problem.md
- **Strongest point**: The Relation section (L105–119) holds the Map to a stricter standard than its critics would. Physicalism avoids combination too. IIT's exclusion postulate gives a boundary account where the Map has only an acknowledged void. Illusionism dissolves all three problems at once.
- **Notable quote**: "a stipulated boundary and an admitted void are both unexplained, and the Map owes an argument for preferring the void."
- **Why it works**: It keeps apart two things the rest of the corpus tends to merge: *avoiding* a problem, and *gaining an advantage* from avoiding it. That distinction should now be applied to the older combination pages.

### concepts/cosmopsychism.md
- **Strongest point**: "Which objection bites which variety" (L71). Miller's parthood argument reaches only the constitutive version. The priority reply, filtering and aperspectival dissolution each "step outside its reach and incur different debts".
- **Notable quote**: "that is four separate verdicts rather than one, each reached on different grounds, and Shani's rejection of the mirror-image framing is a live reason to think the Map's reading may be too quick."
- **Why it works**: Coiner care (L33: Shani "put the label into wide circulation", not "its sole coiner") and the identity-versus-grounding split from open individualism (L52) make this a good definition page.

### concepts/manyism.md
- **Strongest point**: The page opens by calling manyism "the most credible monist rival", engages it "as a rival to be taken seriously, not as evidence for dualism", and separates three multiplications: manyism, many-worlds and the many-minds interpretation (L68).
- **Notable quote**: "The Map does not refute manyism, and manyism does not refute the Map."
- **Why it works**: It is short, fair, and has the most headroom in the wing (1877). It is also the page the older combination pages most need to cite and do not (see below).

### concepts/combination-problem.md
- **Strongest point**: "The Unity of the Interface" (L151–157). It asks whether the Map simply stipulates the agent's unity, and answers that constituting a subject out of subjects and relating an already-unified subject to distributed matter "are different burdens". It then locates the leftover difficulty "nearer bedrock than the 'avoids the problem entirely' framing implies".
- **Notable quote**: "The Map escapes *constitutive* combination without thereby dissolving every question of interface unity."
- **Why it works**: The bridge article (L111) names this as the formulation "that should govern". The page is the wing's hub, with 63 inbound links. Its empirical section is the part that needs work (flag 1).

### topics/panpsychisms-combination-problem.md
- **Strongest point**: L123 contrasts turbulence, which is structurally hard, with combination, which is conceptually hard. L139 then admits that treating unity as primitive "is a genuine philosophical cost—it declines to explain something that seems to demand explanation".
- **Notable quote**: "Accepting primitiveness honestly is preferable to concealing it behind a mechanism that does not actually work."
- **Why it works**: Its three-way diagnosis at L139 is memorable and accurate: bonding smuggles in unity, fusionism relabels emergence, elimination dissolves the explanandum. It needs the interface-unity concession next to it, or it claims more than its sibling permits (L133: "There is nothing to combine because consciousness is not composed").

### concepts/composition-and-consciousness.md
- **Strongest point**: L73–75, the red/blue asymmetry, and L103, composition-as-identity as the mereological core of non-compositionality.
- **Notable quote**: "There is no operation on experiences analogous to vector addition or superposition."
- **Why it works**: As a primer the page is clear and quick to read. Its problem is that it summarises the topic page in a stronger form than the topic page itself uses (flag 3, and the James quote).

## Calibration Concerns (flagged, not praised)

1. **The primer outruns its topic page** (`composition-and-consciousness` L43, L121, L123, L125). Details under Birch flag 3 and Stapp above. The page also states the flat impossibility at L85 ("phenomenal parts cannot compose into unified phenomenal wholes") without the split-brain/DID qualification that both `combination-problem` L126 and the topic's L105 carry ("this article inherits that more cautious key").
2. **Probable fabricated James quote** (`composition-and-consciousness` L97): "we cannot deduce from it what any sum of feelings amounts to". The string is absent from *Principles* Vol. 1 and from *A Pluralistic Universe*, with positive controls hitting in the same text. The idea is genuinely James's. The safe disposition is to de-quote it into an attributed paraphrase, or to replace it with the verified "hundred-and-first feeling" passage.
3. **Coleman's dilemma presented as decisive without manyism.** `combination-problem` L56–60 ("Neither option delivers…"), `panpsychisms-combination-problem` L59 ("demonstrably incoherent") and `composition-and-consciousness` L87 all present the dilemma as closing the question. `manyism` L36 records that Roelofs "takes the second horn and embraces it". None of the three pages links `manyism` in the body. `panpsychisms-combination-problem` has it only under Further Reading.
4. **The comparative-advantage register** (`panpsychisms-combination-problem` L125/L141; `combination-problem` L145, L149, L169). Details under Birch flags 1 and 2.
5. **Grain-problem attribution split.** `panpsychisms-combination-problem` L75 says the grain problem was "identified by Grover Maxwell (1979) and Michael Lockwood (1993)". `concepts/sellars-manifest-and-scientific-images` L49 gives it to Sellars. `grain-mismatch` L100 calls Sellars "the pre-Chalmers source". `combination-problem` L80 opens with Nagel. When two Map pages name different originators, one of them usually has the wrong one. The usual account is that Sellars originated the objection and Lockwood named it. **Treat that as a lead to verify, not as a verified fact.**
6. **`cosmopsychism` L79** says the Map's placement "generates no such debt—there is no single cosmic field and nothing to decompose". The bridge's L111 says avoidance "does not thereby settle what makes one non-physical subject numerically distinct from another". A single clause pointing to that would align the two. It is low priority, because L83 already frames the whole thing as a "cost-swap".
