## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. Moon (2019) — wrong given name AND inverted attribution. Both fixed.**

The body read: *"Tim Bayne (2018) argues that exclusion is not genuinely axiomatic, and **Kwangsu Moon** (2019) that the postulate does not follow from the axiom it is derived from."*

Two independent defects in one clause:

- **Wrong given name.** The author of "Exclusion and Underdetermined Qualia" (*Entropy* 21(4), 405) is **Kyumin Moon** (Department of Philosophy, Seoul National University). Confirmed at three independent publisher-side sources: Crossref (`10.3390/e21040405`), MDPI's own listing, and PubMed/PMC (PMC7514894). "Kwangsu" appears nowhere. The References entry read `Moon, K.` and was therefore correct as written — the defect was body-only, which is why an intra-corpus consistency check would never have surfaced it.
- **Attribution inverted — the rival's position stated in the form its author explicitly rejects.** Moon does *not* argue that the exclusion postulate fails to follow from the exclusion axiom. He argues the opposite. What he observes is that IIT's *own presentation* of the link "is not clear," and he then supplies a formal reconstruction (premises 1–7) establishing that it does follow: *"the argument from Premise (3) to (6) is driven by reductio ad absurdum: if the exclusion postulate is incorrect, the exclusion axiom cannot be true...Therefore, the postulate also should be true."* His paper is a **friendly repair of IIT**, not an attack on it — his stated aim is that "IIT can be confirmed with regard to not only its theoretical foundation but also its mathematical modeling." The article had recruited him as a hostile witness for a claim he refutes.

This also broke the sentence that rested on him — *"at a price its critics find unmotivated"* — since one of the two "critics" was a defender.

**Resolution.** The passage was rewritten around what the IIT literature actually shows, which turns out to serve the article's argument better than the false version did. Two costs are now priced: (i) Bayne's dispute over the axiom's standing, stated at full strength (he argues exclusion is *neither* genuinely axiomatic *nor* a substantive constraint — the article previously carried only the first half of a two-pronged conclusion); and (ii) the fact that uniqueness in IIT had to be **engineered rather than read off** — Krohn & Ostwald (2017) showed the formalism could return several tied maxima leaving the quale underdetermined; Moon (2019) added a difference-making criterion to prevent the ties; IIT 4.0 carries explicit tie-breaking rules of its own. Moon's actual role (reconstructing the derivation as valid) is now stated. `Krohn, S., & Ostwald, D. (2017). "Computing integrated information." Neuroscience of Consciousness, 2017(1), nix017` added to References, verified at Crossref.

**Anachronism trap avoided in the fix.** The tie/underdetermination problem was raised against the IIT 3.0-era formalism, while the article quotes **IIT 4.0** for the exclusion axiom. Presenting a 2017 defect as a live cost of the 2023 version would have been a fresh currency-drift error introduced by the correction itself. Verified at the IIT 4.0 paper that 4.0 carries tie-breaking machinery at both the substrate and cause-effect-state levels (the "principle of maximal existence"), and the passage now states the repair sequence chronologically rather than implying an open hole.
