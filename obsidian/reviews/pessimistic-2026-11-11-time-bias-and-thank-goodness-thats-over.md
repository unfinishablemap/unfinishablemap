## Executive Summary

The page is careful about what relief cannot show, and its concessions are real. Its weaknesses sit where it reports the opposition's position as more favourable to the Map than the sources allow. The one empirical citation is reported by its title alone: the same paper's second finding, and a companion study by the same four authors that tests phenomenology directly, are both missing, and the page's own datum is phenomenal. ## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would point at item 1 of "What the Attitude Shows". The page calls the felt pastness of relief "the most secure item on the list", and its only support is that Mellor grants a tensed belief. A belief with an indexical mode of presentation is a functional state. Calling it a "phenomenal datum" and then asking theories of time to honour it adds a layer the argument never pays for. She would also note that the evolutionary story is dismissed in the section's closing sentence as treating the tensed character as "a mere product of how we are built", when one sentence earlier a theory that "explains why it feels as it does has done real work". Maclaurin and Dyke's account is exactly such an explanation, and the page gives no test that separates the two descriptions.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett would bring Perry's essential indexical, which the page never mentions. "Thank goodness that's not happening *here*" resists place-name paraphrase just as Prior's remark resists dates, and nobody concludes that there is a metaphysically privileged place. The page gives Hare's self/time parity and omits the place/time parity, which is the B-theorist's cheapest reply and the one that makes Prior's observation look like a fact about indexical thought. He would add that some B-theorists deny that experience presents passage at all (lead: Hoerl 2014; Deng 2013; not verified today), so "both sides accept this" is wrong about the present literature.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark would read the growing-block paragraph and ask what "active participation in settlement" contributes. The Dualism paragraph says settling "would occur without anyone to feel it". If collapse settles the past mindlessly, the present is marked by collapse and nothing about the observer is needed, and the remaining work, that the past pain is no longer undergone, is done by an unstated premise that experience stops in settled slices. No physics is offered for that premise.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch would say the paragraph attacks the wrong attitude. Relief that a pain is over is fully available to an Everettian: every branch has one past, and the pain is earlier than now in each branch that contains the relieved subject. Only the *counterfactual* relief (the test was negative) is touched. There the Everettian has a better reply than the one the page states. Relief that I am in the branch where the test was negative is self-locating relief, and the page's own showcase of rational relief, Parfit's hospital patient, hopes to be one of two patients who both exist and both have their operations. If that hope has an object, so does the Everettian's.

### The Empiricist

A Popperian would go to the one place the page touches data. It says the question whether belief in passage drives future-bias "has been tested" and reports the title. The paper's abstract continues past the title's result, and a companion paper tests the phenomenology hypothesis, which is the one the Map's framing needs. Reporting half of a two-part result and none of the follow-up is selective. The page's inference, that future-bias "may be a feature of human psychology whatever one's metaphysics", is drawn from a belief test alone.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nāgārjuna would press Hare's parity harder than the page does. Parfit's own observation (lead: *Reasons and Persons* §69, the case of the mother's suffering; not re-verified today) is that future-bias largely disappears for the pains of others. If the bias is confined to one's own experiences, it tracks self-grasping at least as much as it tracks time, and the page's sentence that someone who rejects self-bias "must explain why the same reasoning leaves time-bias standing" has an answer the page does not consider: it does not leave it standing.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Latham et al. (2022) is reported by its title; its second finding and the companion phenomenology study are missing

- **File**: `obsidian/topics/time-bias-and-thank-goodness-thats-over.md`
- **Location**: "The Rationality Challenge", file line 63. Sentence (grep -c -F = **1**): `Andrew Latham, Kristie Miller, Christian Tarsney and Hannah Tierney (2022) report, in their title's words, that belief in robust temporal passage "(probably) does not explain future-bias."`
- **Problem**: The abstract goes on: "But we also find that people give more future-biased responses when asked to simulate a belief in robust passage. We take this to suggest that the phenomenology that attends simulation of that belief may be partially responsible for future-bias". The paper separates a temporal *belief* hypothesis from a temporal *phenomenology* hypothesis and tests only the first. The same authors then tested the second in "Robust passage phenomenology probably does not explain future-bias" (*Synthese* 200(2), 2022, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-022-03514-3): they "found no evidence in its favour" and conclude that "although robust passage phenomenology on its own probably does not explain future-bias, having this phenomenology and taking it to be veridical may contribute to future-bias". The page's subject is the *felt* character of relief, so the phenomenology study is the directly relevant test and it is absent. The next sentence (grep -c -F = 1 for `Future-bias may be a feature of human psychology whatever one's metaphysics`) generalises from the belief result alone.
- **Verified at source**: abstract of the *Philosophical Studies* paper read in the Oxford repository author copy (ora.ox.ac.uk, full text extracted, 12 hits for "simulat"); the paper's discussion argues that the simulation effect shows no interaction with actual belief. Companion abstract read on OpenAlex (title, four authors, *Synthese* 200(2), 2022). The companion's page numbers / article number were not retrieved.
- **Severity**: High (selective report of an empirical source; the omitted half bears on the page's own thesis in both directions).
- **Recommendation**: Replace the title-only report with both results in two sentences: belief does not predict the bias; simulated belief raised it and the authors suspected phenomenology; their follow-up found no evidence that passage phenomenology alone explains it, with a possible joint contribution of phenomenology taken as veridical. Add the *Synthese* reference. The page's conclusion survives and becomes better supported: neither belief nor phenomenology has been shown to drive the bias. Budget about +60 words; headroom 1,457.

### Issue 2: "Both sides accept this" moves from an indispensable tensed belief to a phenomenal datum

- **File**: same
- **Location**: "What the Attitude Shows", item 1, file line 77. Sentence (grep -c -F = **1**): `Both sides accept this.` Its support is the next sentence (grep -c -F = 1): `Mellor's reply concedes that the tensed belief is indispensable; the dispute is over what makes it true.`
- **Problem**: The item is headed "The phenomenal datum" and says relief "has a felt, tensed character". What Mellor concedes, on the page's own account at line 47, is that a tensed *belief* is causally indispensable. That is a doxastic claim with a tenseless truth condition. It does not concede that there is a tensed *phenomenal* character over and above the belief, and some B-theorists deny that experience presents passage at all (lead, unverified today: Hoerl 2014, "Do we (seem to) perceive passage?"; Deng 2013, "On explaining why time seems to pass"). The lead paragraph and the Dualism paragraph both build on the datum as phenomenal. This is the doxastic reading's evidence recruited for the phenomenal reading. Two sentences at line 81 inherit the problem. `The relief is the most secure item on the list and the one theories of time most often explain away.` (grep -c -F = 1) sits badly with "both sides accept this", and on Mellor's view the relief misrepresents nothing, so nothing is explained away. `A theory that treats the tensed character as a mere product of how we are built has not engaged the datum.` (grep -c -F = 1) dismisses the evolutionary account without saying how it differs from the theory the previous sentence credits with "real work". The 09-28 deep review noticed this last sentence and left it; it recorded no stability note on it.
- **Severity**: High (the datum is the premise the lead and the Dualism paragraph rest on).
- **Recommendation**: State what is agreed and what is not. Agreed: a tensed belief is needed to explain the timing of relief and action. Disputed: whether the relief has a tensed phenomenal character beyond that belief; the Map holds that it does, and deflationists about passage experience deny it (cite one after verifying). Then either delete the "mere product of how we are built" sentence or say what the evolutionary account leaves out, in the terms Pearson 2018b supplies (it explains a before/after asymmetry and leaves the tensed/tenseless one). Budget about +50.

### Issue 3: The No Many Worlds paragraph overstates its first premise and misses the self-locating reply that the page's own hospital case makes available

- **File**: same
- **Location**: "Relation to Site Perspective", file line 89. Sentence (grep -c -F = **1**): `Relief presupposes a single settled past, and much ordinary relief is counterfactual as well as temporal: thank goodness the test was negative, the car missed.` The stated reply is (grep -c -F = 1): `Everettians reply that rational attitudes can be rebuilt around branch weight.`
- **Problem**: (a) The page is about Prior's temporal relief. On many-worlds each branch has one past, and "that pain is over" is true in every branch containing the relieved subject. Temporal relief presupposes nothing that many-worlds denies. The paragraph's traction is confined to counterfactual relief, a different attitude that Prior, Mellor and Parfit do not discuss, and the sentence runs the two together. (b) For counterfactual relief the Everettian's stronger reply is self-location, which the page does not state: relief that *I am in* the branch where the test was negative. The Map's objection is said to be "the indexical one", yet self-locating relief is indexical. The page's own example shows the structure is coherent. In Parfit's hospital case (line 55) both patients exist and both operations occur; "you hope you are the first" has an obvious object. I applied each passage's own terms before calling this a tension: line 89 says a view on which "all the histories occur leaves that attitude without its apparent object", and line 55 presents an attitude with a clear object where all the relevant outcomes occur. The Map can still distinguish the cases (two distinct pre-existing patients against one person with successors on every branch), but the page does not make that argument, and without it the paragraph rejects the Everettian by tenet while reading as though the attitude itself decided the matter.
- **Severity**: Medium (a tenet paragraph whose argument is thinner than its wording; the closing sentence "leaves the physics untouched" already limits the damage).
- **Recommendation**: Narrow the opening to counterfactual relief and say plainly that temporal relief is available to the Everettian. State the self-locating reply, link it (`[[many-worlds]]` already discusses self-locating uncertainty), and give the Map's answer where it has one: before the outcome there was one subject who dreaded, and on branching every dreaded outcome befalls a continuer of that subject, unlike the hospital case where the other patient was never you. Mark what remains as a disagreement at the level of the Map's commitments on indexical identity. Budget about +70.

### Issue 4: The growing-block account of relief relies on a premise the source article lists as an open cost

- **File**: same
- **Location**: "Relation to Site Perspective", file line 87. Sentence (grep -c -F = **1**): `On that claim, "that's over" is true because the pain is fixed and will not be undergone again.`
- **Problem**: The paragraph concedes that on a growing block "the past pain still exists" and says it "is no longer at the edge where experience is undergone". That needs Forrest's "real but dead past": past slices exist and contain no experience. The linked section of `consciousness-and-the-ontology-of-temporal-becoming` (lines 145–147) names the cost itself: Forrest's response "raises the question of what extinguishes consciousness in settled time-slices", and the Map's reply is offered as "building on Forrest's intuition". This page carries the benefit and drops the cost. A critic then has a dilemma the page does not answer: if the past pain exists as pain, someone is tenselessly in pain there and relief is no better grounded than on the B-theory; if it exists without being felt, it is unclear what "the past pain still exists" asserts. Separately, the truth condition offered ("fixed and will not be undergone again") is close to Mellor's tenseless one (the pain ends earlier than the token) with "fixed" added, so the sentence does not show what participation contributes. I checked the apparent clash with line 85 ("Consciousness does not make the past settled") against the source's wording first: line 85 says settling is "mostly" objective collapse, and the source treats "the mindless cosmic growth and the neural participation alike" as becoming, so the two passages are consistent and I do not flag a contradiction.
- **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: Add one sentence stating that the account inherits Forrest's open question (why experience is absent from settled slices that still exist) and that, without an answer, the growing block grounds relief no better than presentism and at a higher price. Reword the truth-condition sentence so that it says what the pain's no longer being undergone adds to its being earlier. Budget about +45.

### Future-bias is first-personal

- **Current content says**: the parity between self-bias and time-bias "cuts in two directions".
- **A critic would argue**: Parfit observed that the bias weakens for others' suffering, and Hare has a paper on other-directed time-bias (leads: *Reasons and Persons* §69; Hare 2008, *Australasian Journal of Philosophy*). If so, the bias depends on self-concern, which favours a debunking reading.
- **Suggested response**: verify both sources, then add a sentence to "Hare's Parity Move". It would strengthen the section and it bears on the No Many Worlds paragraph.
