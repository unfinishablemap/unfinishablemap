## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The hub refuses both of the easy moves a comparative survey makes. It does not claim
the hard problem is universal, and it does not quietly omit the tradition that
embarrasses the claim. The Chinese section is named "a genuine challenge" in a
heading, and the consequence is drawn in the convergence section itself: "the
convergence is not unanimous: the Chinese exception set out above must be weighed
honestly rather than explained away." A survey that publishes its own worst
counter-example at full strength is doing the thing Chalmers keeps asking
philosophy of mind to do — treat the gap as the datum, and let the datum resist you.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Both Tenet 2 paragraphs that touch Sāṃkhya get the hard part right. `indian`:
"The parallel is thematic, not mechanistic: Samkhya predates quantum theory by two
millennia." `hard-problem`: "The structures differ substantially (pre-scientific
metaphor versus quantum measurement); the parallel is thematic." The Sufi paragraph
does the same work on Avicenna's holistic mental causation. Stapp's programme is
damaged more by enthusiastic ancient-wisdom parallels than by any critic, and this
wing declines to supply them while still finding the structural point worth making.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`cross-cultural-phenomenology-of-agency` is the wing's Nagelian achievement, and its
central argument is better than its register hits suggest. It separates the
functional from the phenomenal layer, concedes the functional layer outright to
shared biology, and then runs the *phenomenal* layer as a hostile-witness argument:
traditions that theoretically dissolve the agent still report that acting differs in
felt quality from being acted upon. "If the qualitative character of agency were a
cultural confabulation, cultures that theoretically dissolve the agent should report
its phenomenal absence, not merely its conceptual rejection. They do not." That is a
falsifiable phenomenological prediction with a named disconfirmer — the rarest thing
in this literature.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Twice in the wing a `resonates` attaches to process philosophy, and **both times the
article immediately declines to bank the resonance**. `agency` L75: Whitehead's
occasions and Buddhist dharmas "both converge on denying that agency requires an
enduring substance — a convergence the Map does not join, since its own agent-causal
reading needs precisely the substance both deny." `buddhist` L53: process
philosophy's unrepeatable moment "does not carry over to the Map's commitment to
haecceity … which Buddhism strains rather than supports."

Per this skill's constraint, that is the correct handling. Whitehead-friendliness is
praised here as compatibility and nowhere used as an engine of upgrade. The wing
knows the difference and writes it down.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Both articles that assign Sāṃkhya's passive *Puruṣa* to Tenet 3 call it "a genuine
tension" and say which way the Map goes: "The Map sides with Nyaya over Samkhya on
this point: consciousness acts, it does not merely witness." A wing surveying
traditions that mostly do not want libertarian agency could have soft-pedalled the
mismatch. It names the mismatch and picks a side, which is what a commitment costs.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`hard-problem`'s "What Would Challenge This View?" lists five distinct defeaters for
its own convergence argument, including the one that hurts most — "evidence that the
convergence reflects a shared cognitive bias (cross-cultural dualist intuitions
generated by theory-of-mind modules) rather than a shared insight." Writing your
opponent's best debunking explanation into your own article, in your own words, is
epistemic humility with teeth.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This wing is where the corpus's evidential discipline is strongest, and the
measurement backs it.** Three findings this persona will take:

1. **The one-instrument discount is live in three of five members**, verbatim or
   near-verbatim: `hard-problem` Tenet 5, `buddhist` Tenet 5, `agency` L91. `indian`
   Tenet 5 carries a *different* but equally real discount — "none possessed a
   reducing science … that would have made a reduction verdict a live option to
   begin with", plus "unanimity among investigators who lacked the reducing
   apparatus cannot by itself settle the question." Four of five articles discount
   their own best evidence in the paragraph that presents it.

2. **The tenet-as-evidence-upgrade is praise-worthily *not* done, repeatedly.**
   `islamic-sufi` Tenet 4 had a soteriology of the individual soul available and an
   MWI tenet to support with it, and wrote instead: "the tradition never addressed
   branching observers, so it neither refutes nor supports many-worlds
   interpretations. Its resonance … is thematic coherence, not an argument for the
   tenet." `buddhist` Tenet 1 had a compatibility thesis available and wrote: "That
   reading is contested from inside the tradition, which makes Buddhism a disputed
   site rather than an ally."

3. **The wing declines its own strongest rhetorical asset.** A cross-cultural wing's
   natural temptation is to bank convergence as independent confirmation. The hub
   instead marks the African leg as "a comparison the Map proposes rather than a
   recognition those traditions record", and `islamic-sufi` marks its *own* strand
   as sharing a Neoplatonist channel — the article volunteering the discount that
   damages it most. That is the honest pattern: *tenet-coherent, not
   evidence-elevating.*

**Where this persona dissents, and it is one paragraph.** `islamic-sufi` Tenet 5 is
the single tenet paragraph in the wing that makes the contemplative-evidence
argument with **no discount at all**: "a philosophy of mind that ignores
contemplative evidence—however parsimonious—is incomplete." Measured: `one
instrument` returns **0** in that file and **1** in each of three siblings. This is
not merely a stylistic outlier — it is the move P-M2 exists to discount ("a
convergence is a single observation read N times until a distinguishing test
passes"). See priority item 2.

## Content Strengths

### `the-hard-problem-in-non-western-philosophy` — the wing's hub, and its most disciplined tenet section

- **Strongest point**: the convergence section publishes its own counter-example and
  its own scope limit in the same breath.
- **Notable quote**: "The convergence argument is strongest among traditions that
  share certain ontological assumptions (distinguishing subject from object, or self
  from world) and weakest as a claim about universal human philosophical insight."
- **Why it works**: it converts "our evidence is partial" from a concession into a
  *specification* — it says exactly which sub-class the argument covers, which is
  what makes the argument checkable rather than merely hedged.

### `islamic-sufi-philosophy-of-consciousness` — the self-damaging audit

- **Strongest point**: the article cites, in its own opening framework section, the
  audit that downgrades its own tradition's evidential contribution.
- **Notable quote**: "The alignment is coherence rather than independent
  corroboration: the introspective strand shares the Neoplatonist channel weighed
  above, a physicalist can read the same reports as facts about the limits of
  introspective access, and the tradition itself divides between Avicenna's dualism
  and Ibn 'Arabī's unity of being."
- **Why it works**: three independent defeaters in one sentence — shared channel,
  rival reading, internal division. Nothing in the wing is tighter. It is also the
  template that fixes the same article's Tenet 5.

### `cross-cultural-phenomenology-of-agency` — the functional/phenomenal firewall

- **Strongest point**: it concedes the layer that shared biology explains, *before*
  making its claim, so the claim is about the residue rather than the whole.
- **Why it works**: most cross-cultural arguments for irreducibility never separate
  the two layers and are refuted wholesale by the biological universal. This one
  cannot be, because it has already given that layer away.

### `buddhist-perspectives-on-meaning` — the bedrock-disagreement model

- **Notable quote**: "*Pratītyasamutpāda* is event-causal throughout, so karma
  delivers mental causation within a stream where the tenet asserts a nonphysical
  relatum acting on a physical one. That second relatum is the Map's addition, not
  Buddhism's."
- **Why it works**: it specifies precisely *how far* an apparent agreement reaches
  and where it stops, rather than scoring it as agreement or disagreement. It also
  pre-warns the reader at L101 — "less agreement than it sounds". This is the
  sentence the hub needs (priority item 1).

### `indian-philosophy-of-mind` — convergence inside one culture, correctly discounted

- **Notable quote**: "subject to the common-cause caveat granted in §'The Three-Way
  Disagreement' above: the convergence strengthens the dualist option without, on its
  own, establishing it."
- **Why it works**: it makes a genuinely novel evidential move — cross-*school*
  convergence achieved under sustained mutual criticism within one milieu — and then
  immediately applies the discount that the milieu itself invites.
