## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Quote subject splice (Chalmers 2018)**: the page wrote 'weak illusionism "is most naturally seen as a sort of type-B materialism about consciousness"'. Chalmers's subject is "This lower-order variety of weak illusionism". He distinguishes three varieties: strong illusionism (type-A), higher-order weak illusionism ("especially unmotivated", given no letter), and lower-order weak illusionism (type-B). The predicate was verbatim but the subject dropped a qualifier that changes the claim. **Fixed** in §Who Holds Which View, and the matching Common Misroutings bullet now reads "at most the lower-order variety of weak illusionism".
- **Internal contradiction, lead vs routing table**: the lead said the structure-and-dynamics reply reaches "only Type-C", but the table gives it "C and A". **Fixed**: those replies now "miss Type-B; they reach Type-C … and structure and dynamics also reaches Type-A."
- **Internal contradiction, lead vs table**: the lead said Type-B "is reached by a smaller set" of four replies. The table lists six Type-B rows: water/H₂O, primitive identity, two-dimensional, master argument, and two unrun replies. **Fixed**: "a different set", with all six named. The same defect appeared in §Which Map Reply ("One reply has no Map article behind it" when the table marks two rows "not yet run"); **fixed** to "The Tenet-5 reply has no Map article behind it."
- **Wrong example attributed to Chalmers**: "intermediate notions such as information close the gap only by equivocation". Chalmers's §7 example of the intermediate X is *representation* ("This is brought out clearly in the case of representation"), and "information" does not appear in that argument. The research note's "(representation, information)" seeded the error. **Fixed** to "representation".
- **Calibration, Chalmers's own preference (Relation to Site Perspective)**: the page said "Chalmers himself does not prefer D to F; he holds that none of D, E and F 'has obvious fatal flaws'". The 2003 Conclusions say "I give some credence to each of them. I think that in some ways the type-F view is the most appealing", and the fatal-flaws sentence is hedged: "I do not claim to establish … But I do aim to suggest that none of them has obvious fatal flaws". So "does not prefer D to F" was true but misleadingly weak, and "holds" upgraded "aims to suggest". **Fixed**: credence to each, "aims to suggest", and type-F "in some ways" the most appealing, all verbatim. The next sentence based the Map's preference for D on "its commitment that consciousness acts". That does not separate D from F, since Chalmers's type-F also gives phenomenal properties a causal role ("by virtue of constituting the intrinsic nature of the physical") while keeping microphysics closed. **Fixed** to "consciousness selects among outcomes physics leaves open, where type-F keeps microphysics causally closed" (Tenet 2 rationale; Tenet 3 outcome-selection).

### Medium Issues Found

- **Cited-author stance (Yetter-Chappell)**: "Her paper argues, from the physicalist side, that type-B is unstable toward type-A." Her own published programme defends idealism (PhilPeople and personal publications page), and the abstract offers "a new and compelling version of type-A physicalism" as the paper's positive result. "From the physicalist side" implied a stance the paper text could not be fetched to confirm (PhilArchive 403). **Fixed** to "argues that type-B is misguided and offers physicalists a type-A alternative" (abstract: "This paper aims to show why this view is misguided").
- **Source/Map conflation (IEP)**: "The IEP summarises the type-B justification". The IEP entry has 0 occurrences of "type-B" and names the family "weak reductionism". The Map identifies the two because the IEP's cited proponents (Block & Stalnaker, Hill, Loar, Papineau, Perry) overlap Chalmers's type-B footnote. **Fixed** to "naming the type-B family weak reductionism and citing several of Chalmers's type-B authors".
- **Tier phrasing (lead and §Tier)**: "against Type-C it is suggestive relative to Type-C's rivals" read as though the Map's case were suggestive *of* B/D/F. The page also flagged the non-separation for Type-C but not for Type-A, although the widened-explanandum datum favours every view that admits it, Type-B included. That is the same structure. **Fixed**: the lead now says the cases against A and C "favour Type-B as much as dualism, so neither separates dualism from physicalism"; the Type-A bullet adds "Even granted, the datum favours every view that admits it, Type-B included"; the Type-C header is simplified to "suggestive, without separating dualism from physicalism". No tier was raised or lowered.
- **Type-Q gloss**: "rejects the a priori/a posteriori distinctions". Chalmers names three distinctions (conceptual/empirical, a priori/a posteriori, contingent/necessary). **Fixed** to "the distinctions the taxonomy rests on, such as a priori versus a posteriori".
- **"Chalmers's case against Type-B is a cost"** understated his case, which also includes the two-dimensional argument (§6). **Fixed** to "first case".
- **Type-C pushback sourced**: "Type-C sympathisers dispute the structure-and-dynamics premise" was unsourced. Chalmers himself records it ("Some type-C materialists hold we do not yet have a complete physics"; "A type-C materialist might hold that there could be new physical theories that go beyond structure and dynamics"). **Fixed** to "Type-C materialists can dispute …, as Chalmers himself notes".
- **Balog / PCS**: "which Chalmers (2007) treats as the type-B strategy". Chalmers 2007 says "many type-B materialists have turned to" it, and 2018 names an illusionist "type-A" version, which this page itself cites. **Fixed** to "a type-B strategy" (Further Reading line aligned).
- **Common Misroutings intro** ("when a reply that reaches one type is drawn as a verdict on physicalism in general") fit only the first two of the five patterns. **Fixed** to "in which a reply or a view is routed to the wrong type."
