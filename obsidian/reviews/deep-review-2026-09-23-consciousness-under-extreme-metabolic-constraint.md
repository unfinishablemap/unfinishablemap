## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Psychedelic metabolic claim was the reverse of the record.** The article said psychedelics "*decrease* metabolic activity in the default mode network", citing the entropic-brain paper. The DMN decreases are in **cerebral blood flow and BOLD** (Carhart-Harris et al. 2012 PNAS, ASL + BOLD). The one direct FDG-PET measurement found psilocybin **raising** glucose metabolism globally, by about 25% in frontomedial cortex and anterior cingulate (Vollenweider et al. 1997). The article's own falsifier ("psychedelic enhancement without metabolic suppression... the metabolic-suppression pathway would be undercut") is therefore **already partly met**. This also contradicted the sibling pages, which already carry the Vollenweider caveat (`concepts/filter-theory` §The Psychedelic Paradox; `topics/psychedelics-and-the-filter-model` L65). **Fixed**: I rewrote the section as "Psychedelic Enhancement and the Metabolic Record". It now says this line discriminates least, and that it appears here because it is often miscited as metabolic evidence. I rewrote the falsifier as "already partly met". I also updated the filter-model bullet.
2. **Ketamine metabolic claim was false.** The article said "Ketamine produces significant metabolic reduction" and used it for the premise "different consciousness profiles at similar levels of metabolic reduction". Långsjö et al. 2005 (*Anesthesiology* 103(2):258–268, PET) found that anaesthetic S-ketamine has **no effect on whole-brain CMRO2 or glucose metabolism** while raising CBF by 36%. **Fixed**: the paragraph now says unresponsiveness is reached from very different metabolic starting points. I added the Långsjö reference and rewrote the "anaesthetic uniformity" falsifier to match.
3. **Montupil 2023 citation-framing error.** Montupil et al. was cited as documenting "different consciousness profiles at similar levels of metabolic reduction". A full-text grep (PMC10539891) finds no comparison of metabolic levels between agents. The paper supports only the three-state taxonomy and ketamine = disconnected consciousness. **Fixed**: the citation now carries only those two claims.
4. **Unsourced "fingerprint requires metabolism" claim.** "Consciousness-specific neural fingerprints require metabolism to maintain their uniqueness" had no source. The real finding is Luppi et al. 2025 (*Nat Hum Behav* 9(5):987–1004): under propofol and sevoflurane, individual FC fingerprints lose distinctiveness, and this reverses on recovery. The paper makes **no metabolic claim**; "metabol" appears only in its reference list. **Fixed**: the article now states the finding with its citation. Filter and production readings are presented side by side as not decided by the data.
5. **Internal inconsistency: "42% threshold" (L93) vs "44%" (L57).** The threshold is 44%; 42% is the VS mean. **Fixed.**
6. **Calibration: the count of five anomalies no longer held.** After the 08-08 refine conceded that the entropic brain hypothesis predicts the psychedelic finding, L93 and the Tenet 5 paragraph still said production needs auxiliary hypotheses for "all five". L47 had already conceded that network models allow threshold effects. A tenet-accepting reviewer would flag this. **Fixed**: the article now separates the crude metabolic-rate production model from network-level production accounts. The pressure is placed on the intensification cases (NDEs, gamma) and on terminal lucidity. I changed the Dualism paragraph from "evidence that consciousness is not reducible" to "consistent with... not evidence against production as such", noting that the strongest items are also the thinnest.
7. **Calibration: hibernation arousal as "anomalous for simple production models".** This was overstated. Arousals have standard physiological proposals (immune restoration, sleep debt, synaptic regrowth, clearance of by-products). **Fixed**: the article now says the filter gloss has no evidential support over these accounts. Hibernation marks the lower bound and does not discriminate. Also, Cerri 2017's own abstract describes torpor cortex as "a sort of 'slowed wakefulness'" and leaves residual consciousness open. I quoted this verbatim; it was grep-verified in the abstract via OpenAlex.
8. **Calibration: quantum-biology link.** The link was glossed "increasingly supported by experimental evidence". **Fixed** to "still-contested evidence", and the gamma/quantum-interface sentence is now marked "on this speculative reading". This is covered by the stability-note scope rule: an empirical-support claim stays reviewable.
