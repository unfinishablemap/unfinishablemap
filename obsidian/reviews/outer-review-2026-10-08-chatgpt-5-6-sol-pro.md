# External referee report

**Recommendation: Major revision**

## Overall verdict

The article contains a defensible and potentially valuable **weak thesis**: dissociative conditions reveal that autobiographical access, felt ownership, affective salience, identity, memory search and voluntary motor control are componential and selectively dissociable. In many cases, these disturbances occur without a focal destructive lesion capable of straightforwardly explaining the symptom. That makes clinical dissociation useful evidence about functional architecture.

The article does **not**, however, establish its stronger thesis: that the resulting architecture is especially suggestive of an interactionist interface between a non-physical mind and the brain. Its central “intact substrate” argument depends on repeatedly moving between:

1. no gross destructive lesion;
2. no missing tissue;
3. no relevant anatomical abnormality;
4. no physical substrate alteration; and
5. an otherwise fully intact brain whose connection to consciousness has failed.

Only the first, and sometimes the second, are supported. The others are much stronger and are contradicted or at least materially complicated by structural, functional and network-level findings omitted from the article. The page itself initially says “undamaged, not unchanged,” but later asserts “no atrophy,” “without any substrate change,” “nothing being broken” and that “the substrate stays whole.” [[clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence]]

A second central problem is that the article treats very different variables as instances of “connectivity”: regional cerebral blood flow, regional activation, psychophysiological interaction, directed effective influence, predictive precision, retrieval inhibition, autobiographical search policy, metacognitive belief and felt ownership. These are not measurements of one common quantity. The apparent four-condition convergence is therefore partly created by vocabulary rather than discovered in the evidence.

A third problem is model discrimination. A developed physicalist architecture predicts selective failures rather than uniform degradation. The article eventually concedes this, but its positive dualist case still depends on contrasts with “crude identity theory” and “binary production” that are not serious competitors. No cited result has a materially higher likelihood under interactionist dualism than under a suitably developed physicalist account. The interface reading survives as a **compatible interpretation and research heuristic**, not as an empirically favoured explanation. The minimal-quantum-interaction tenet is not tested at all, something the article correctly acknowledges. [[clinical-dissociation-as-systematic-evidence]]

## 3. The central “intact substrate” equivocation

The article’s most important defensible formulation is that “no gross destructive lesion is required.” That formulation should govern the entire argument. Instead, increasingly stronger descriptions accumulate:

- “the substrate is undamaged”;
- “no lesion, no atrophy”;
- partitioning “without anything being broken”;
- ordering “without any substrate change”;
- “intact processing regions”;
- and “the substrate stays whole.”

These expressions are not equivalent. A brain can lack a focal lesion while differing in hippocampal subfield volume, cortical or white-matter morphology, synaptic efficacy, neuromodulation, learned priors, inhibitory control, effective connectivity or state-dependent network recruitment. Those properties are all parts of the physical substrate relevant to contemporary physicalist explanations.

For DID specifically, a structural MRI study of 32 women with DID and 43 matched controls found smaller bilateral global hippocampal volumes and differences in several subfields; dissociative-amnesia scores were uniquely associated with reduced bilateral CA1 volume. A meta-analysis, though based on only three eligible studies and therefore itself tentative, also reported smaller hippocampi in DID than in healthy controls and a smaller left hippocampus than in PTSD controls. These findings do not prove tissue loss causes DID, and “smaller volume” should not casually be called “atrophy.” They do show that “no atrophy,” “nothing broken” and “substrate whole” cannot be used as empirical premises. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34165068/)

A controlled 2022 fMRI study also found that working-memory performance and neural recruitment varied by identity state in genuine DID, with trauma-related states showing altered engagement of frontoparietal regions. This complicates the article’s picture in which the higher autobiographical channel is partitioned while lower cognitive systems simply continue unchanged. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35403592/)

The broader literature is not yet strong enough to replace the article’s dualist interpretation with a settled neurobiological theory. A 2022 systematic review found only thirteen eligible functional-neuroimaging studies across the dissociative disorders—covering just 51 DID, 28 depersonalisation, 24 dissociative-amnesia and six other patients—and emphasized scattered findings and the need for further work. That scarcity should lower confidence in both metaphysical readings. It does not license treating absent gross lesions as evidence of physical intactness. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36143190/)

## 4. Source-by-source clinical and citation audit

### 4.1 Dissociative identity disorder

**Reinders et al. (2003), “One Brain, Two Selves.”**  
The title is accurate. This was a primary PET study of eleven women with DID, using script-driven imagery and comparing identity-state-dependent regional cerebral blood flow. Its core result—that different identity states were associated with different regional blood-flow patterns during autobiographical processing—is represented reasonably. But the article’s description of the result as a “connectivity-pattern signature” is technically wrong. Reinders measured localized rCBF patterns, not functional connectivity between regions. The experiment also did not demonstrate that semantic and procedural channels remained intact, that a single non-physical subject persisted across states, or that tissue-independent access was being gated. It showed state-dependent regional physiology. [Empty Memories+1](https://www.empty-memories.nl/files/science/reinders_onebrain2selves.pdf)

The study should also not bear much contemporary evidential weight by itself: it was small, some participants were medicated, switching was not experimentally induced independently of the patients’ clinical practices, and it lacked the modern simulator and comparison conditions needed to discriminate identity-state physiology from demand characteristics, learned state regulation or other psychiatric processes.

**Marsh et al. (2021).**  
The quotation that some memory representations “may be available for retrieval” is verbatim and fairly transcribed. The article also correctly reports partial cross-identity recognition and the weaker free-recall result. [PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article)

The overreach comes immediately afterward. Marsh et al. did not establish that what was lost was “largely metacognitive access and felt ownership.” They discussed metacognitive belief, failure to initiate a memory search and failure to acknowledge ownership as possible explanations. They explicitly said their tasks could not determine whether participants genuinely lacked access, simulated amnesia or reported amnesia for other reasons. Ownership was therefore an explanatory hypothesis, not a measured dependent variable or settled result. [PLOS+1](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article)

The article also understates the importance of later synthesis. A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis found evidence for some inter-identity memory transfer, but only two of four meta-analyses clearly supported it; the authors emphasized heterogeneous performance, a small cumulative sample, limited research groups and narrow memory domains. That is exactly the kind of result that should replace confident language about a stable autonoetic partition. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39541721/)

**Spanos (1994), Lilienfeld et al. (1999) and Lynn et al. (2022).**  
These works exist and are represented substantially fairly:

- Spanos advanced a sociocognitive account in which multiple-identity enactments are context-bound and shaped by social expectations.
- Lilienfeld and colleagues argued that posttraumatic accounts had significant conceptual and evidential weaknesses and that cultural and iatrogenic factors deserved greater weight.
- Lynn and colleagues argued that neither a simple posttraumatic model nor a simple sociocognitive model was satisfactory, proposing a transtheoretical account involving sleep disturbance, inhibition, hyperassociation, source attribution, reality testing and metacognition. [Brown+2](https://brown.uk.com/brownlibrary/SPAN3.htm)

The problem is not misquotation but dialectical treatment. The article says that it “does not adjudicate the aetiology,” yet preserves the phenomenological partition as an etiologically neutral architectural datum. It is not neutral. If identity states and reported memory barriers are partly learned, socially scaffolded, expectation-sensitive or treatment-shaped, then the relevant architecture may consist of search policies, source-monitoring rules, response criteria and self-modelled roles—not a pre-existing interface partition. The etiology therefore bears directly on the inference the article wishes to retain.

**Omitted DID evidence.**  
By October 2026 the article should have included at least:

- the hippocampal-subfield study and structural meta-analysis already noted;
- the controlled identity-state working-memory study;
- the 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of inter-identity amnesia;
- and work using distributed morphology to distinguish DID groups from controls.

None establishes reductive physicalism. Collectively, however, they make the “one intact substrate, altered access only” presentation obsolete.

### 4.2 Depersonalisation–derealisation disorder

**Sierra and Berrios (1998).**  
This is the clearest citation error. The paper is a theoretical neurobiological review/model. It is not an imaging experiment showing reduced insula or amygdala activation while perceptual cortex functions normally. The article attributes an empirical finding to a source that did not generate that finding.

The likely relevant experiment is Phillips et al. (2001), which compared only six depersonalisation patients, six healthy controls and ten OCD controls during viewing of aversive and neutral images. It found reduced responses in both the insula **and occipito-temporal cortex**, with increased right ventral prefrontal activation. Thus even the likely source does not support the article’s contrast between impaired emotional circuitry and normally functioning perceptual cortex. [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/download/wikipedia-scholarly-sources-corpus/10.1016%252FS0921-4526%252898%252900398-6.zip/10.1016%252FS0925-4927%252801%252900119-6.pdf)

The misattribution matters philosophically. “Normal perceptual processing plus missing felt reality” would provide a relatively clean content-versus-mineness dissociation. The actual early result involved differences in visual association areas as well as emotion-sensitive regions, in an extremely small and partly medicated sample. It is considerably less clean.

**Seth, Suzuki and Critchley (2012).**  
The paper is correctly cited and the article captures its broad proposal: conscious presence is modelled through interoceptive predictive coding, with abnormal interoceptive precision offered as a possible explanation of depersonalisation. But it is explicitly a **Hypothesis and Theory** paper. Its authors described a theoretical model and noted that computationally explicit predictive coding had not yet been formally applied to presence or interoception. The Map’s formulation that presence “just is” successful precision weighting changes a proposed constitutive model into an established identity. [DOI+1](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2011.00395)

**Ciaunica et al. (2022).**  
The citation exists and the article fairly identifies it as an active-inference account of altered self and agency. It is a serious physicalist competitor because it offers a mechanistically articulated relation among precision, self-related priors, sensory attenuation and agency. But “fully specified physicalist rival” is too strong. It is a theoretical synthesis, not a fitted quantitative clinical model that has uniquely predicted DPDR symptoms or treatment response. The proper conclusion is that a motivated mechanistic alternative exists, not that it has been empirically established. [UCL Discovery+1](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10148390/)

**Cambridge Depersonalisation Scale, Sierra and Berrios (2000).**  
This is a scale-development and validation paper. It supports the existence of a reliable self-report instrument for depersonalisation symptoms. It cannot by itself show that autobiographical content is objectively retrievable while only mineness is absent.

**Sierra et al. (2005).**  
“Anomalous Subjective Recall” is the exact name of one factor found in the exploratory factor analysis, so the label is not fabricated. But the inference from a self-report factor to an objectively demonstrated separation between content retrieval and ownership is too strong. Factor analysis shows that questionnaire responses covary in a particular sample; it does not independently test storage, retrieval, source attribution or memory accuracy. Subsequent psychometric work has also reported inconsistent CDS factor structures across samples, which makes the four-factor decomposition an uncertain basis for a metaphysical channel taxonomy. [Cambridge University Press+1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/abs/unpacking-the-depersonalization-syndrome-an-exploratory-factor-analysis-on-the-cambridge-depersonalization-scale/9A4486F31B3A8DC290B61CCE25A0CDAB)

**Stale DPDR evidence.**  
A 2025 study of 47 drug-naïve DPDR patients and 49 controls reported widespread reductions in within- and between-network functional connectivity, with an 80% cross-validated classification result and an association between a CDS self factor and somatosensory–motor network connectivity. This is not definitive and requires replication, but it is far more relevant to the article’s network claims than the uncited 2001 six-patient experiment. It supports a distributed physical-network account at least as readily as an interface-channel account. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39833729/)

### 4.3 Dissociative amnesia

**Mangiulli et al. (2022).**  
The article accurately reports this review’s negative central conclusion: across 128 case studies in sixty articles, evidence for dissociative amnesia was frequently weak and most reports failed to rule out ordinary forgetting, malingering and other alternatives. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/21677026211018194)

The article then largely withdraws that concession. It states that memories “typically return as a body,” that recovery is “reconnection, not reconstruction,” and that the “whole” autobiographical corpus becomes available without tissue change. Those claims are not established by Mangiulli et al. and are not established by the later-cited Staniloiu review. They require evidence that:

1. the material was originally encoded;
2. it remained accurately stored;
3. it was inaccessible rather than ordinarily forgotten;
4. recovery was accurate;
5. recovery was not cue-dependent reconstruction or suggestion;
6. and wholesale recovery was typical rather than selectively reported.

The systematic review’s point is precisely that most published cases do not meet that burden. The article cannot use Mangiulli as a caveat and then continue with the positive case as though the caveat concerned only a minority of reports.

**Staniloiu and Markowitsch (2014).**  
This is a review, not a primary cohort demonstrating typical abrupt, wholesale restoration. It surveys proposed mechanisms, case reports and imaging work, including individual cases with metabolic changes and gradual or substantial recovery. It can support the proposition that reversible cases have been reported. It does not support the frequency claim that memories “typically return as a body,” nor the non-reconstructive conclusion. [The Lancet+2](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366%2814%2970279-2/fulltext)

**Anderson and Hanslmayr.**  
The body text invokes motivated-forgetting and retrieval-suppression work, but no full reference appears in the bibliography. Laboratory retrieval suppression is a legitimate physicalist analogy: top-down control can reduce accessibility without deleting all underlying representations. Its translation to severe clinical autobiographical amnesia is nevertheless uncertain. The article should identify the exact review or experiments intended and distinguish laboratory suppression of trained items from clinically alleged loss of years of autobiographical memory.

**Unsupported organic-amnesia contrast.**  
The “hard drive is damaged” contrast is too crude. Organic amnesia is not one homogeneous process, and physical accounts distinguish encoding, consolidation, storage, cueing, retrieval, executive search and systems-level reorganisation. Even if some organic cases involve irreversible tissue damage, the article cannot infer that reversibility uniquely marks an interface reconnection. Dynamic neural inhibition, state-dependent retrieval and recovery of network function are physical possibilities the article itself recognizes.

### 4.4 Functional neurological disorder

FND is the least suitable of the article’s four cases for a unified dissociation argument. Dissociative symptoms are often comorbid with FND, but FND encompasses heterogeneous motor, sensory and seizure presentations, and current models do not treat it simply as a severed consciousness channel. A 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis found elevated dissociative symptoms but also high heterogeneity and risk of bias, particularly noting the need for more rigorous investigation of their mechanistic relevance. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36451595/)

**Voon et al. (2010).**  
The study is real and the article’s central description is substantially correct. Sixteen patients and sixteen controls completed an emotional-face fMRI paradigm. Patients had positive motor symptoms such as tremor, dystonia and gait disturbance, rather than being a pure conversion-paralysis sample. PPI showed increased right amygdala–right supplementary motor area coupling; exploratory Granger modelling supported directional influence from amygdala to SMA for happy stimuli, with only a trend for fearful stimuli. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article-abstract/133/5/1526/542469)

“Activity predicting subsequent motor-area changes” is therefore too causally confident. Granger directionality in BOLD time series is model-dependent and does not establish that the amygdala physically caused a later symptom-producing motor change. The article should report it as effective-connectivity evidence under the analysis model.

**Hassa et al. (2017).**  
This primary study involved thirteen patients with functional hemiparesis and nineteen controls. It reported left-amygdala hyperactivity during combined emotional stimulation and passive movement, together with increased PPI connectivity between the amygdala, pre-SMA and subthalamic nucleus. [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5429234/)

The statement that Hassa “replicated the direction” is incorrect. PPI detects condition-dependent statistical coupling; it does not determine causal direction. The study replicated a broader pattern of increased limbic–motor interaction in a different task and patient group, but not Voon’s directed amygdala-to-SMA result.

**Edwards et al. (2012).**  
The bibliographic details and title are accurate. Its description of FND in terms of aberrantly precise intermediate-level priors is a fair compact statement of the paper’s Bayesian proposal. But it is a theoretical mechanistic account, not an experimental demonstration that such a prior generated the participants’ symptoms. It should be labelled accordingly.

**Vuilleumier (2005) and Brown (2004).**  
Both exist, but both are reviews or conceptual syntheses rather than new clinical experiments. Vuilleumier reviewed imaging and neuropsychological work on conversion symptoms; Brown proposed an integrative representational account of medically unexplained symptoms. Calling Brown part of an “active-generation family” is reasonable as interpretation, but neither should be counted as independent primary confirmation.

**Anaesthesia and hypnosis.**  
The statement that a paralysed limb “may move normally under anaesthesia or hypnosis” is much too broad. The Map’s own linked conversion-disorder article is more accurate: its main sedation source was an uncontrolled series of eleven patients given propofol, with major improvement or cure in five, and this was sedation rather than general anaesthesia. The hypnosis comparison rested heavily on very small or single-case imaging work. The target article suppresses precisely the evidential qualifications its linked article supplies. [[conversion-disorder-as-consciousness-side-fault]]

## 5. Historical and conceptual references

**Janet (1889).**  
The work is real, and Janet’s account of psychological automatism and narrowing or division of the field of consciousness is relevant historical genealogy. But saying that Janet treated consciousness as an integrative capacity “distinct from the neural substrate” and thereby anticipated the Map’s interface architecture is interpretive and anachronistic. Janet did not provide evidence for a non-physical subject coupled through separable channels. The historical point should be labelled an analogy, not empirical ancestry for interactionist dualism.

**Tulving.**  
The article uses anoetic, noetic and autonoetic consciousness in a recognisable Tulving-derived sense. What is not sourced is the claim that these modes degrade “across most consciousness disruptions” in a fixed autonoetic-first, anoetic-last order. Tulving’s taxonomy distinguishes forms of memory-associated consciousness; it does not establish a universal law of vulnerability. The four clinical literatures reviewed here do not jointly demonstrate such an invariant order.

The phrase “highest-bandwidth, most reconstruction-intensive channel” is also a Map-specific theoretical addition, not a Tulving result. It needs operational definitions and independent evidence rather than being allowed to function simultaneously as description, explanation and prediction.

## 7. Does the dualist interpretation survive the strongest physicalist account?

### The strongest physicalist reconstruction

A serious physicalist need not propose one undifferentiated “brain activity” whose quantity rises and falls with consciousness. The relevant competitor combines several independently motivated ideas:

- representations can persist locally without being globally accessed or reported;
- autobiographical retrieval requires active, self-constrained search rather than passive reading from storage;
- ownership and source attribution are metacognitive constructions that can dissociate from familiarity or content;
- agency depends on comparisons among intention, predicted sensory consequences, proprioception and action;
- interoceptive and exteroceptive precision affect presence, mineness and emotional salience;
- threat learning, attention, neuromodulation and avoidance can stabilize different network states;
- and social expectations or therapeutic practices can influence explicit self-models and retrieval policies.

On this account:

**DID** involves identity-state-dependent executive and autobiographical search policies, learned or trauma-related state regulation, source attribution and metacognitive beliefs. Partial inter-identity transfer is expected because representations may be encoded while explicit search or acknowledgment is state-constrained.

**DPDR** involves altered interoceptive precision, sensory attenuation, emotional overmodulation and an abnormal generative self-model. The broad phenomenological loss of reality need not be produced by gross sensory failure because felt reality is a higher-level property of inference and integration.

**Dissociative amnesia**, where a stringent case can be established, can arise through retrieval inhibition, encoding failure, ordinary forgetting, state-dependent cueing, executive-search disruption or source-monitoring processes. Different cases need not share one mechanism.

**FND** can involve aberrant priors, attention, agency and active symptom generation, with limbic–motor coupling and learned symptom expectations contributing to the resulting motor or sensory state.

This is not merely an ad hoc list designed after seeing the data. The component mechanisms come from broader literatures on memory, predictive processing, executive control, agency, interoception and motor inference. Their clinical deployment remains incompletely validated, but so does the interface model.

### What predictive processing does and does not achieve

The article is correct that “precision weighting” can become an overly elastic label. Retrieval inhibition, interoceptive attenuation and motor priors are not one experimentally demonstrated master mechanism. Predictive-processing explanations can be difficult to falsify when any symptom can be redescribed as excessively high or low precision at a convenient hierarchical level.

But this weakness does not favour dualism automatically. It means both programmes need sharper predictions. Predictive accounts currently have an advantage in being related to measurable neural, autonomic and behavioural variables; the interface account has not yet specified a separate measurable variable representing coupling to a non-physical subject.

### Comparative explanatory burden

The article claims that physicalism must pay a new mechanism cost for every clinical geometry, whereas dualism pays for “the interface” once at tenet level. That comparison is asymmetrical.

The interface model must still explain:

- why these particular channels exist;
- why they correspond to these neural networks;
- why social learning, trauma cues, medication and attention affect them;
- why structural and functional biomarkers accompany symptoms;
- why one channel rather than another disconnects;
- what determines whether DID contains one subject, several subjects or one subject with several self-models;
- how access barriers form and dissolve;
- why partial objective transfer coexists with reported amnesia;
- and how minimal quantum interactions scale into the stable macroscopic patterns observed clinically.

Calling all of this “interface architecture” does not pay those mechanism costs. It redescribes them at a higher level.

### Referee’s answer

The dualist reading survives in three progressively weaker senses:

1. **Logical compatibility:** yes. Nothing in the cited evidence deductively rules out a non-physical mind.
2. **Architectural heuristic:** yes. Channel language may be a useful way to organize dissociable functions, provided it is not treated as already locating those functions between ontological domains.
3. **Positive comparative evidence for interactionist dualism:** no. The article provides no result that is more specifically predicted by dualism than by developed physicalism.

The minimal-quantum-interaction claim survives only as an unconstrained consistency statement. Because no quantum variable is measured and no quantum-interface prediction is stated, the clinical cases supply it with no empirical increment.

## 8. Missed counterarguments

### 8.1 Selectivity is expected, not surprising, under physicalism

The article repeatedly treats selective loss as difficult for production accounts. But any physically implemented system composed of interacting specialized subsystems predicts selective failures. A language model, immune system or motor-control system can suffer highly selective functional impairment without missing a macroscopic component. The relevant question is not whether physicalism permits selectivity but whether the **particular selectivity was prospectively predicted more precisely by one theory**.

The article never establishes that advantage. Its channel labels are generally assigned after the clinical pattern is known.

### 8.2 The “uniform degradation” target is a straw man

The claim that identity theory predicts “more damage, less mind” or uniform impairment is not true of identity theories generally. A token-identity or mechanistic identity theorist can identify different conscious properties with different distributed physical processes. Selective alteration of one process then produces selective phenomenology without any concession to a non-physical interface.

The article eventually restricts its target to “crude identity theory,” but that leaves very little evidential work for the clinical cases. Defeating a position almost no contemporary physicalist needs to hold does not confer support on dualism.

### 8.3 Subject individuation is unresolved

The DID section assumes that a single non-physical mind persists and that identities are interface configurations. That is not inferred from the evidence. It is imported from the dualist framework. A dualist could instead posit multiple subjects, intermittent subject formation, one subject with dissociated self-models or no settled fact about subject number. Without an independent criterion of conscious subject individuation, DID cannot map the non-physical side of the interface.

### 8.4 “No lesion” is not causal evidence

Most psychiatric and functional disorders lack a focal lesion. That does not make them interface failures. The absence of a visible lesion principally excludes some forms of destructive neuropathology; it does not shift probability toward non-physical causation unless the interface theory had independently predicted an effect that neural-state models could not reproduce.

### 8.5 Diagnostic and phenomenological heterogeneity

DID, DPDR, dissociative amnesia and FND are not four standardized perturbations of one variable. They differ in diagnostic status, disputed etiology, symptom heterogeneity, comorbid PTSD, anxiety and depression, medication exposure, treatment history, suggestibility, cultural framing and the evidential reliability of self-report. Treating them as independent replications of one architecture risks multiplying the same selection and interpretation decisions.

A recent cross-diagnostic imaging synthesis did find convergence involving default-mode, salience, basal-ganglia and sensorimotor networks, but that is naturally read as transdiagnostic network involvement and does not uniquely recover the Map’s channel taxonomy. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42667207/)

### 8.6 Recovery is not automatically evidence of retained storage

Abrupt or extensive recovery can follow state change, cue restoration, removal of inhibition, relearning, reconstruction or changed response criteria. To establish retained storage, the article needs prospective evidence of information laid down before the amnesia and objective evidence of its continued presence during the allegedly inaccessible interval. Retrospective clinical narratives are not enough.

### 8.7 A hard problem is not a clinical discriminator

The physicalist still owes an account of why any physical organization is accompanied by experience. That is the general hard problem. Clinical dissociation does not independently strengthen it unless the cases reveal a relation that physical functional architecture could not predict. Otherwise the article risks using the general explanatory gap as though it were a condition-specific result.

## 9. Tenet-protective bracketing

### DID etiology

The article brackets whether DID is trauma-generated, sociocognitively shaped or produced by interacting factors, but retains the partition as robust interface evidence. That is not neutral bracketing: the etiological issue determines whether the partition is a stable internal boundary, a learned retrieval policy, an enacted self-model or some mixture. The bracket removes a challenge while preserving the conclusion that challenge bears on.

### Unity of the non-physical mind

The claim that one non-physical mind remains unified while output fragments is not independently tested. All observed fragmentation is allocated to the interface because the tenet requires the mind to survive intact. This makes the interpretation difficult to falsify: unity is assigned to the unobservable domain and every contrary manifestation is attributed to its connection.

### Trauma as purposive withdrawal

The phrase “the mind protecting itself” introduces teleology. Cases with trauma can be explained as protective withdrawal; cases without a clear trauma are said to make the reading weaker but not false. Unless the account predicts which threat, person or memory will trigger which specific channel change, purposive protection remains a retrospective story.

A physical account can reproduce apparent purposiveness through threat-conditioned attention, reinforcement, avoidance and salience. The dualist version needs evidence for agency on the consciousness side beyond the adaptive shape of the outcome.

### The one-time mechanism-cost claim

Treating the metaphysical interface as a cost paid once protects the theory from being charged for its case-specific mechanisms. Yet an interface without bridge laws predicts almost nothing. Once bridge laws are added—channel definitions, vulnerability rules, transition dynamics and subject-individuation criteria—the dualist model incurs case-specific commitments comparable to those charged to physicalism.

### Quantum interaction

The article is admirably explicit that the quantum connection is only a consistency claim. But placing it within a cumulative “relation to site perspective” section can still create rhetorical support without evidential support. A consistency relation should not contribute to the article’s evidential balance unless a quantum-interface mechanism predicts a result that a classical or network-level model does not.

## 10. Integration with surrounding Map articles

### “Neurological Dissociations as Interface Architecture”

That article opens by saying that if consciousness were identical to neural processing, neurological damage should degrade consciousness uniformly. Later it concedes that sophisticated functionalism predicts exactly the modular dissociation pattern and that dissociation alone cannot adjudicate between functional subsystems and interface channels. The clinical article inherits both positions without resolving the contradiction. [[neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture]]

The opening “uniform degradation” argument should be removed across both pages. The later compatibility-grade formulation is the defensible one.

### “Altered States as Systematic Interface Evidence”

The apex article is better calibrated. It says the six altered-state windows supply one pattern rather than six confirmations and that interface language contributes mutual coherence, not discrimination. The clinical article should adopt this exact evidential grade. Its statement that dissociation is “among the more suggestive empirical evidence” is stronger than the apex methodology warrants. [[altered-states-as-interface-evidence]]

### “Evidential-Status Discipline”

The site’s own rule requires separating a weaker epistemic or access-level reading from a stronger metaphysical reading and supplying a bridging argument before upgrading between them. The clinical evidence establishes facts about access, report, behaviour and neural state. The article then interprets those facts as a bridge between ontological domains without an independent argument for that upgrade. This is almost a textbook instance of the failure mode the methodology page identifies. [[evidential-status-discipline]]

### “Conversion Disorder as the Consciousness-Side-Fault Paradigm”

The linked conversion article explicitly grades the propofol result as a small uncontrolled case series and the hypnosis evidence as exceptionally thin. The target article compresses that into a general statement about normal movement under anaesthesia or hypnosis. The cross-link therefore creates inconsistency rather than qualification. [[conversion-disorder-as-consciousness-side-fault]]

### “Targeted-Lesion Discriminating Tests…”

The dedicated test article says current production and filter readings remain empirically equivalent and that the proposed designs are candidate discriminators. The clinical article instead suggests that selectively perturbing an autonoetic-supporting connectivity pattern while sparing noetic networks would itself separate the theories. It would not: both predict a selective change unless the filter theory specifies a different channel ordering from the network topology. The dedicated test page is more accurate and should control the target article’s wording. [[targeted-lesion-discriminating-tests-between-production-and-filter-readings-of-the-memory-hierarchy]]

### Memory-channel articles

The target imports a fixed autonoetic–noetic–anoetic vulnerability hierarchy, then uses the imported hierarchy as evidence that the clinical cases map a pre-existing interface. This risks circularity:

1. clinical phenomena are grouped into the hierarchy;
2. the hierarchy is redescribed as channel architecture;
3. their fit to the architecture is counted as convergence.

The memory pages need an independent evidential review of whether the ordering is genuinely cross-conditionally stable, which counterexamples exist, and whether “autonoetic” is being used consistently for episodic reliving, autobiographical content, self-relatedness, ownership and pastness.

### Split-brain and fragmentation articles

The clinical article defers its “one non-physical mind, several interface configurations” interpretation to the split-brain article. That merely relocates the unsupported premise. The site needs an explicit subject-individuation framework stating what empirical or phenomenological facts would count for one subject, two subjects or indeterminacy under dualism.

## 11. Final assessment

The article should not be discarded. Its strongest contribution is a comparative catalogue showing that clinical dissociation concerns more than global loss of function and that access, ownership, salience, autobiographical search and motor agency can vary semi-independently.

Its present metaphysical conclusion is nevertheless overstated. The evidence supports:

> **Clinical dissociation reveals selectively dissociable functional and phenomenological organization, often without a focal destructive lesion.**

It does not currently support:

> **Those dissociations are best explained as failures of separable channels between an intact non-physical mind and an intact brain.**

The physicalist and predictive-processing readings survive the audit. Indeed, partial inter-identity transfer, identity-state-dependent cognitive recruitment, distributed DPDR network findings and active-generation accounts of FND fit naturally within them. Those accounts remain incomplete and sometimes overly flexible, but incompleteness is not differential evidence for the interface model.

The correct evidential classification is therefore **architecture-supporting, ontology-underdetermining and quantum-neutral**.
