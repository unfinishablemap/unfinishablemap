## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Pylyshyn's "preattentive" retraction compressed (qualifier and attribution).** The article said: "Since tracking 'may require a great deal of effort and attention', he wrote, 'I now avoid referring to indexing as preattentive'". At source (p. 149) the subject is "the *task* of tracking multiple objects", and the sentence opens "even if the indexing and tracking mechanism is preattentive in this sense". The stated reason for the retraction is terminological: "Since many investigators take susceptibility to disruption and the need for effort as an indication that the process is 'attentive', I now avoid…". The old wording let a reader take Pylyshyn to have conceded that the mechanism is attentional. Fixed: the sentence now names the *task*, gives the investigators' reason, and keeps "continuing to assume that indexing and tracking are 'realized by an automatic and preconceptual mechanism'".
- **Pylyshyn & Storm 1988 credited with proposing the FINST mechanism.** Their abstract discusses the findings "in relation to an earlier, and independently motivated model … called the FINST model". Scholarpedia (Pylyshyn 2007) says MOT "was developed in 1988 in order to test (and illustrate)" the proposed mechanism. Fixed: they "read the result through the earlier FINST model, which posits…".
- **"Pylyshyn wrote as a computationalist, not a dualist" had no citation.** The brief's item 3. The sentence was rescoped to the verified 2001 text: his framing is "causal and naturalistic, not dualist". The items indexes pick out are what the visual system gives us "through a brute causal mechanism", and concepts must be grounded in "a primitive causal connection between thoughts and things" (p. 154). Both strings were grepped in the PDF text layer. Note the subject: at source, "they" in the first quote is the items picked out, not the indexes. The article's paraphrase follows that.
- **Burge stance upgraded.** The article said Burge "rejects" the consciousness condition on perceptual representation. Begby reports only that Burge finds Evans's arguments for it "shot through with problems at practically every turn" (p. 188). Finding an argument defective is weaker than rejecting its thesis. The sentence now quotes that.
- **Weighing item 1 overstated parallel tracking.** "Early vision tracks a few objects in parallel" stated as settled what is the 1988 argument for parallelism; serial-switching accounts dispute it. Now: "Observers can track several identical moving objects at once (Pylyshyn & Storm 1988)". The index hypothesis stays labelled contested.
- **Unscoped absence claim in §Trade-off.** "Though tracking is untested" restated the scoped body claim without its scope, against the writing-style "Absence and Exclusivity Claims" rule. Now: "the Map has found no test of tracking without it".
- **Stale self-quotation.** The lead and Relation to Site Perspective both quoted the host's "plausibly survives", which this pass removed from the host. Both now state the status directly. The condition is contested at the level of selecting the object, and survives, if at all, as an epistemic condition.
- **Mole 2008 orphan and wrong-paper identification.** The inline "(Mole 2026, citing Mole 2008)" had no References entry. The research note had resolved it to the *TiCS* 12(2) letter "Attention in the absence of consciousness?". The SEP bibliography's only Mole 2008 entry is "Attention and Consciousness", *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 15(4): 86–104; a web search confirmed the details and its framing around Kentridge & Heywood 2001. A References entry was added, and the research note's citation line was corrected.
