## Executive Summary

This is the Map's most self-aware historical writing. Every article treats its ancestors as **provenance, never authority**. Sherrington's article says outright that three eminent dissenters are "sociology, not evidence". The occasionalism article admits that "both of occasionalism's sharpest weapons rebound on the Map". The hylomorphism article concedes that if the rival's dissolution works, "it is a cost to the Map, not a win". The wing's best unexploited asset is a result from Leibniz scholarship (Duncan 2012, verified). On that reading the Monadology mill runs on an *intelligibility principle* (no inexplicable connections in nature), and that principle cuts against the brute psychophysical laws the Map itself needs. The mill article currently presents the mill as a pure ally. Adding the cost would make it the wing's sharpest piece, and would link the mill to the "causal intelligibility" horn of the hub's seventeenth-century trilemma.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The mill article separates the **epistemic and metaphysical readings of the absence claim**: "The conclusion in step 4 needs the metaphysical reading, but the mill walk delivers only the epistemic one." Chalmers would recognise this as the exact hinge on which the phenomenal concept strategy turns. The article gets there by close reading of a 1714 text rather than by importing modern machinery. The honest discount on the argument's descendants ("successive sharpenings of one insight … not five independent confirmations") is P-D1 applied in all but name. The article never cites P-D1, and P-D1 never cites the article (grep: zero hits either way).

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The Sherrington article **separates the Beck–Eccles mechanism from Eccles's theology in both directions**: "refuting the theology leaves the mechanism standing—and adopting the mechanism family … commits no one to the theology." The Penfield article adds that Penfield's clinical case is "*independent* of the contested quantum-interface machinery". It is a foothold that does not depend on the quantum bet. A quantum-mind theorist gains from both: the physics proposal can be judged on its physics, and the clinical case adds evidence that does not rest on the physics.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Penfield's **ownership asymmetry** (movement without authorship under the electrode) now has its modern inverse in brain–computer interfaces: authorship without movement (Evans et al. 2015; Serino et al. 2022). The article does not use the inverse case to score for dualism: "the constraint bears on both readings alike, and the dualist inference gains no support from it." It still locates the pressure on the outbound arm, where authorship is fixed. That puts first-person authorship at the centre of the evidence without overselling it.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

The hylomorphism article treats a non-Cartesian metaphysics fairly. It splits the name into three theories (structure, Thomistic, emergent), notes that only some are dualist, and names emergent hylomorphism "the branch a Map reader should watch". The **substance connection** section borrows the persisting subject from Thomistic subsistence while declining the dissolution. That is scoped borrowing, not conscription. The process-friendly point here, that form is an organising principle rather than a second ingredient, is praised for its **conceptual clarity**. No tier upgrade is at stake anywhere in this wing.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

The occasionalism article's reply to **No Necessary Connection** is the wing's strongest argument for creaturely agency. It does not claim the Map's channel meets the necessitarian standard. It rejects the standard, as something "occasionalism stipulates rather than argues for". Real causation includes "lawful, non-necessitating influence". That is the right footing for any libertarian account on which agents bias outcomes without necessitating them.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The hub closes with its candid acknowledgment: "consciousness resolves quantum indeterminacy" may work like Descartes' primitive-notions defence, naming the interface rather than explaining it. "The explanatory gap is narrower, though not closed." Sherrington's 1947 concession ("devoid of a basis for its very beginning") is treated as a debt the lineage inherits, not a problem it has solved. McGinn would count this as the right amount of humility.

## Calibration Concerns

These are flagged, not praised.

1. **The mill article is surer of itself in some places than in others.** Its knowledge-objection reply concedes "a standoff of intuitions … the mill does not by itself break it." The two replies before it assert the disputed thesis as fact: "This response misses Leibniz's point"; computation "*is* fully captured … Nothing is left over. With perception, something is left over". §Historical Lineage says of Chalmers's formulation that the gap "is not temporary ignorance but a structural feature of physical explanation", stated flat. §Relation to Site Perspective says the mill "provides independent support", which the article's own discount paragraph rejects. → **refine-draft task minted.**
2. **The mill article leaves out the scholarship on what Leibniz's argument actually is.** Duncan (2012, *Phil. Quarterly* 62(247): 250–272, doi:10.1111/j.1467-9213.2011.00017.x; Crossref metadata and OpenAlex abstract verified) identifies *two* mill arguments. The first, in the 1702 letter to Bayle, runs from Leibniz's definition of perception as "the expression of a multitude by a simple". The second, in the Preface to the *New Essays* and on Duncan's reading also in *Monadology* §17, is an argument from inexplicability that "depends upon there being no arbitrary and inexplicable connections in nature, because God would not create such things". This matters to the Map in two ways:
   - *Opportunity*: the simplicity argument belongs to the **unity prong**, which P-D1 names as the one genuinely cross-cluster contribution. The article currently files the mill only with the gap/conceivability cluster, where P-D1's discount hits hardest.
   - *Cost*: the inexplicability argument's no-arbitrary-connections principle also counts against the brute but lawful psychophysical connections the Map relies on. It is the same intelligibility demand that the hub's trilemma lists as the horn the Map does not fully keep. The mill article calls the mill an unqualified ally. It is an ally that bills the Map.
   → folded into the same refine-draft task. Lodge (2014, *Ergo* 1, doi:10.3998/ergo.12405314.0001.003, already cited; abstract verified) surveys *four* interpretations, so the article's single reconstruction is one reading among several.
3. **The hub overstates what the quantum proposals share.** "These proposals share a crucial structural feature: consciousness *selects* among possibilities rather than *injecting* force". This fits Stapp and Beck–Eccles. It fits less well Wigner, for whom consciousness causes collapse and does not select an outcome, and Orch OR, where the conscious moment *is* a gravitational self-collapse with no mind choosing among outcomes. → **refine-draft task minted.**
4. **The hub has an orphan reference.** Broad (1925) is entry 29, the last, of its References and is never cited in the body (grep: one hit, in the reference list). → same task.
5. **The wing gives two different ancestries for Tenet 2.** The hub traces the quantum opening from von Neumann through Wigner to Stapp. The Sherrington article calls Beck–Eccles "the direct ancestor of Tenet 2's minimal quantum interaction". The Map's current mechanism is post-decoherence selection (hylomorphism article; P-Q3), which descends from neither in any simple way. "Direct ancestor" should probably read "an ancestor". *Carried, not minted:* the Sherrington article is at `soft_warning` (3026w) and was deep-reviewed 15 days ago. This is a one-word fix for the next pass.
