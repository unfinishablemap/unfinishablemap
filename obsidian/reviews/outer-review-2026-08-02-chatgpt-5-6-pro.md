## Reply

# External referee report: *Dualism Cartography*

**Recommendation: Major revision**

## Overall assessment

*Dualism Cartography* is potentially useful as an **internal orientation device**: it shows how the Map’s starting commitments place it among several mind–matter positions and identifies questions its preferred position must eventually answer. It is not yet successful as the more ambitious, externally usable and comparatively neutral atlas it claims to be.

The central problem is a mismatch between the article’s methodological rhetoric and its actual construction. It announces the discipline “map, do not adjudicate” and claims that “Only the Map’s self-location near Q1 is inherited.” Yet its own dependency section says that Dualism creates the territory, Minimal Quantum Interaction fixes the physical-side coordinate and supplies the interface problem, Bidirectional Interaction excludes Q2, and No Many Worlds is essential to the outcome-selection frontier. The tenets therefore determine much more than a final pin on an otherwise independent map: they help determine the admissible positions, salient costs, empirical interpretation and supposed research frontier. [apex/dualism-cartography](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/dualism-cartography/)

A particularly serious currency problem emerged immediately after the article’s 26 July revision. On 27 July, the changelog recorded that a sibling apex article had wrongly combined Saad-style delegatory trumping with quantum difference-making. The site then separated them because the former requires a sufficient physical cause while the latter requires physical insufficiency. The changelog calls the fusion “a foundational move the programme has not earned.” *Dualism Cartography* still presents substantially that fused architecture in its Q1 description and self-location. It therefore synthesises a mechanism that the site’s own subsequent correction rejects. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

## 1. Do the central claims rest on sufficient evidence?

### 1.1 The taxonomy is a heuristic, not a demonstrated two-dimensional structure

The proposal to distinguish mind-side and physical-side “thickness” is intelligible and may be pedagogically valuable. But the article has not demonstrated that these are well-defined, independent axes.

Its principal argument for orthogonality is that Nida-Rümelin and Descartes can share a Chalmers relation-type while differing in mind-thickness. That shows only that **mind-thickness is not identical to relation-type**. It does not establish that mind-thickness and physical-thickness vary independently, that the quadrants exhaust the relevant possibilities, or that different classifiers will place theories consistently.

The updated source taxonomy now acknowledges that thickness covers several distinguishable properties—structural richness, causal work, counterfactual role and channel width—and that some placements, especially Stapp’s, are matters of judgment rather than definition. That concession should appear in the apex. Once thickness is recognised as a family of measures, collapsing it to one ordinal value on each side risks hiding precisely the distinctions the map is supposed to reveal. [topics/four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy/)

There is also an asymmetry between the axes. Mind-thickness is largely characterised by how much subjectivity or subliminal mentality exists. Physical-thickness ranges over hidden variables, branch multiplicity, Russellian intrinsic properties, decoherence, epistemic ignorance and causal completeness. Those are not all quantities of one kind: they include ontology, interpretation, epistemology and causal architecture. A theory can be ontologically sparse but epistemically incomplete, or physically deterministic without positing more physical entities. Calling all of these “thickness” makes comparison intuitive but not yet theoretically disciplined.

The presence of idealism, neutral-monist limit cases, Goffian cosmopsychism and some forms of hylomorphism in a taxonomy advertised as a map of dualisms creates a further scope problem. Adjacent monisms can illuminate dualism, but their inclusion does not demonstrate that they occupy cells of the same dualist parameter space. It may instead indicate that the proposed axes describe a broader mind–matter landscape while the quadrant labels impose an artificial dualist framing. The article should decide which claim it is making.

### 1.2 The cost overlay is not neutral merely because every cell receives the same questions

The fixed battery asks about mental causation, interface location and conservation. The article candidly admits that this deliberately favours physical-side objections. It then asserts that the asymmetry probably tracks a real difference in what the cells owe, but supplies no independent method for determining that. [apex/dualism-cartography](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/dualism-cartography/)

Uniform application does not make a metric unbiased. A medical theory, an economic theory and a geometrical theory could all be asked the same three questions, but the resulting number of unanswered questions would not provide a meaningful common unit of theoretical cost. Here, the cells are assigned debts of substantially different kinds:

- Q1 receives an authority/interface problem.
- Q2 is characterised as causally deflated or evasive.
- Q3 receives an appearance or transparency problem.
- Q4 receives the “full bill.”

No scale, weighting rule or criterion of commensurability is supplied. The conclusion that one cell has a lighter bill is consequently an editorial judgment, not a result read mechanically from the theories.

The battery also omits major Q1 costs:

- the psychophysical law mapping phenomenal states to outcome probabilities;
- the individuation and pairing of subjects with physical systems;
- the timing and spatial extent of selection;
- compatibility between different subjects’ interventions;
- the relationship between phenomenal intensity and bias magnitude;
- developmental and evolutionary origins of the interface;
- event-level treatment of all conserved quantities;
- empirical discriminability from stochastic classical dynamics;
- and the complexity of maintaining Born statistics while producing useful, reason-sensitive effects.

These are not secondary details. Several constitute the mechanism itself. Counting Q1 as one authority law plus one indeterminacy location therefore understates its nomological and explanatory commitments.

The map can legitimately say that different theories face different characteristic questions. It cannot yet say that those questions have produced **comparable net costs**.

### 1.3 The preferred Q1 mechanism is internally obsolete

The article describes the Map’s causation account as quantum selection or biasing that is structurally close to Saad’s delegatory dualism. But the resemblance is too weak to support the integration.

Saad’s delegatory dualism uses Subset Law* to distribute experiential properties and a Delegatory Law under which an experiential causal profile pre-empts part of a physical property’s default causal profile. It retains the physical property’s default sufficiency and aims to preserve observational closure. It does not posit quantum outcome selection, a physically incomplete token cause or a neural indeterminacy site. [Springer Link+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-025-02290-3)

The Map’s difference-making route instead requires an outcome that the physical state does not sufficiently determine. That is why the site’s 27 July correction now treats the routes as alternatives with opposite closure profiles. There is no sufficient physical process for Saad-style trumping to pre-empt at the very token where quantum difference-making is supposed to occur. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

This affects more than terminology. The two routes have different answers to:

- causal exclusion;
- physical sufficiency;
- token versus statistical closure;
- empirical accessibility;
- counterfactual dependence;
- and conservation.

The apex should not continue to use Saad to lend an independently developed causal structure to the quantum-selection proposal. The latter needs its own worked psychophysical dynamics.

### 1.4 The “frontier constraints” are not measurements of an interface

The article calls its three empirical or formal considerations “Tycho-analogue measurements.” That description materially overstates what they establish.

**The 10-bit-per-second result.** Zheng and Meister estimate the information throughput of measurable human behaviour and high-level task performance. They explicitly distinguish this from the vastly higher information rates handled by sensory and motor systems and omit activities for which behavioural throughput is difficult to define. It is not a measurement of a psychophysical channel, conscious processing as a whole, or a nonphysical selector. [The Meister Laboratory+1](https://meisterlab.caltech.edu/documents/30100/Zheng_2024_The_unbearable_slowness_of_being-_Why_do_we_live_at_10_bitss.pdf)

A 2025 commentary also disputes treating the estimate as a speed limit on the brain rather than on selected task outputs. The Map’s own changelog subsequently acknowledged that the quantity is a behavioural-output bound, not a measured psychophysical bandwidth. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-01997-0)

Without a bridge equation connecting behavioural output entropy to nonphysical causal information, the 10-bit estimate places no quantitative bound on the proposed interface.

**The Born-rule result.** Masanes, Galley and Müller derive the standard measurement structure, Born probabilities and state-update rule from unitary quantum postulates plus substantive operational premises, including finite state estimation and subsystem-partition consistency. The result is important, but it is conditional. Their paper explicitly constructs non-Born toy theories when relevant assumptions such as associativity or state estimation are dropped. [Nature+2Nature+2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-09348-x)

Nothing in that theorem establishes:

1. that consciousness acts at measurement;
2. that an added psychophysical law must leave every ordinary quantum assumption unchanged;
3. that an outcome selector can produce reason-sensitive token outcomes while preserving aggregate frequencies;
4. or that the residual outcome question is intrinsically non-mathematical.

Calling this a causal-consistency result for consciousness converts a theorem internal to an operational reconstruction of quantum mechanics into support for a separate psychophysical hypothesis.

**The theta result.** The willed-attention experiments do find increased frontal theta activity relative to instructed attention. But their own interpretation invokes conflict resolution, decision-making, uncertainty and communication within known neural systems. Frontal theta is already associated with several ordinary cognitive-control functions. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/29/7/2832/5041999)

The finding is therefore compatible with the Map’s proposed functional location but does not discriminate it from classical neural accounts. The apex also fails to provide a direct citation for this empirical claim.

Taken together, these are not three independent observations converging on an interface law. One is a behavioural-output estimate, one is a conditional mathematical theorem, and one is a non-specific neural correlate. Current reviews of quantum-consciousness proposals emphasise that the central unmet requirements are a mechanistic bridge and predictions discriminating the quantum proposal from classical alternatives. The apex has not met either standard. [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1730965/full)

### 1.5 The conservation argument equivocates between preserving conservation and permitting non-conservation

The article says that conservation holds through selection without injection, supplemented by a Noether-symmetry response. Those are not one cumulative solution.

Selection without injection would require a worked physical model showing that the relevant alternatives are exactly degenerate with respect to every pertinent conserved quantity and that the selection dynamics introduce no change in event-level energy, momentum, angular momentum, charge or field stress-energy. Merely calling alternatives quantum-permitted or energetically equivalent does not establish that neural alternatives satisfying those requirements exist.

The Cucu–Pitts argument makes a different point. It argues that conservation laws depend on symmetries and that interactionists may consistently accept local non-conservation if an intervention breaks the relevant symmetry. Their conclusion is that the conservation objection cannot be settled a priori and should become an empirical question about the brain. It does not establish that interactionist influence preserves energy. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13643)

Thus:

- the no-injection route claims conservation is preserved;
- the conditionality route allows conservation to fail without contradicting Noether’s theorem.

They may be alternative responses, but the second cannot be cited as additional support for the first. The apex also needs to separate event-level conservation from ensemble expectation values. Preserving aggregate Born statistics would not by itself demonstrate conservation in each mentally selected event.

### 1.6 The formalisation claim is much stronger than its evidence

The article describes a “convergent failure of every formal framework tried so far.” That is not an accurate description of the cited research.

Kleiner’s work presents a general mathematical framework for consciousness models and argues that formalisation can extend the standard methodology. Kleiner and Ludwig’s 2023 paper directly addresses how abstract mathematical structures can be connected to concrete experience. Category-theoretical work remains an active, explicitly exploratory and metaphysically neutral research programme. [arXiv+2arXiv+2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03223)

Chalmers and McQueen do report that simple IIT–CSL consciousness-collapse models are falsified by the quantum Zeno effect. But they also find more complex variants compatible with current evidence and propose experimental tests using quantum computers. Their conclusion is that a research programme remains worth exploring, not that mathematical treatment has failed in principle. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02314)

There is also a concrete attribution error in the linked formalisation article. It lists the category-theory paper as by Chang and Tsuchiya; the actual authors are Georg Northoff, Naotsugu Tsuchiya and Hayato Saigo. [voids/interface-formalization-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/interface-formalization-void/)

The philosophical argument itself slides between two claims:

1. mathematics may not exhaust the intrinsic phenomenal nature of experience;
2. mathematics cannot formulate a causal or probabilistic law relating phenomenal and physical variables.

The first does not entail the second. A formal theory can treat phenomenal states as primitive relata and specify mappings, symmetries, probabilities or counterfactual dependencies without reducing phenomenal character to mathematics. Newtonian mechanics did not have to capture the intrinsic nature of mass to formulate relations involving mass.

The linked void article explicitly says that its premise that consciousness is non-structural is supplied by the Map’s tenets. It then treats the failure of structural mathematics to produce that stipulated non-structural actuality as evidence for a principled mathematical ceiling. That is tenet-conditional reasoning, not convergence from independent failed research programmes. [voids/interface-formalization-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/interface-formalization-void/)

### 1.7 The defensible parsimony conclusion is narrower than the article’s use of it

The article is on solid ground in arguing that parsimony alone does not straightforwardly establish physicalism. Huemer’s stated conclusion is that four familiar accounts of parsimony’s evidential value do not naturally extend to typical philosophical cases. Zanotti argues that a modesty-based rationale does not ultimately favour physicalism over dualism. Lycan’s observation that systematic overdetermination principally offends parsimony is accurately reproduced. [Spot Colorado+2Springer Link+2](https://spot.colorado.edu/~huemer/papers.htm)

Those sources support a **defensive conclusion**: ontological simplicity is not a decisive argument for physicalism.

They do not establish the article’s stronger positive framing that physicalism buys economy only by accepting brute emergence, error theory or in-principle inaccessibility. That is not an exhaustive classification of physicalist approaches. Type-A functionalism, identity theories, representationalism and higher-order theories deny or reinterpret the explanatory demand rather than choosing one of those three debts. The Map may find those responses inadequate, but it must argue that case rather than write the rivals out through its accounting vocabulary.

## 2. Is the literature accurately represented, current and correctly attributed?

### Chalmers

The article invokes Chalmers’s Type-D, Type-E and Type-F scheme in a taxonomy of dualist positions. Type-F, however, is explicitly a form of monism, not a species of dualism. More importantly, Chalmers separately identifies Type-O dualism: ontologically distinct phenomenal properties combined with microphysical closure and a causal role through overdetermination or mediation. Type-O is directly relevant because it contradicts the apex’s suggestion that closure plus mental efficacy must drift toward quantum insufficiency or a thick two-realm interactionism. [Consciousness Network+1](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

**Assessment:** materially incomplete and partly misclassified.

### Nida-Rümelin

The cited 2010 chapter supports a subject–body distinction, but it does not by itself warrant treating Nida-Rümelin as a straightforward closure-denying Type-D interactionist. Her developed subject-causation account says that subject causation is compatible with mental supervenience and does not disturb the autonomy of neurophysiology. That may still be controversial dualism, but its causal profile cannot simply be assumed to match Cartesian or quantum difference-making interactionism. [Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10670-007-9064-z)

**Assessment:** classification is under-argued and likely over-simple.

### Goff

The article places Goff’s priority cosmopsychism in Q4 as two rich, genuinely distinct, interacting realms. Goff presents cosmopsychism as panpsychism combined with priority monism, according to which there is one fundamental individual. It is not a two-realm interactionist ontology. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/3834/chapter/145323999)

**Assessment:** major misclassification.

### Hylomorphism

“Hylomorphism” is treated as a single Q4 position. The family is too diverse for that. Thomistic soul–body theories may have a dualistic aspect, but contemporary hylomorphists can treat mental phenomena as structural powers of organised physical individuals. Jaworski, for example, explicitly presents mental phenomena as structural and part of the physical world. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/7169)

**Assessment:** serious overgeneralisation; variants must be separated.

### Saad

Saad is accurately cited as offering delegatory dualism, but the theory is mis-framed as structurally continuous with quantum selection. Its core laws concern psychophysical distribution and causal-profile pre-emption, not indeterministic outcome choice. Saad’s framework also carries more theoretical machinery than the apex’s minimal Q1 accounting acknowledges. [Springer Link+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-025-02290-3)

**Assessment:** real source, substantially overextended.

### Masanes, Galley and Müller

The paper and central theorem are real and important. But the apex suppresses the theorem’s operational assumptions and draws a psychophysical implication the authors do not establish. Born-rule derivability within a specified framework is not evidence for a consciousness interface. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-09348-x)

**Assessment:** technically grounded citation, philosophically mis-framed.

### Cucu and Pitts

The paper is correctly relevant to conservation objections. Its result is nevertheless reversed in application: it defends the physical coherence of conditionally accepting non-conservation, not a mechanism guaranteeing conservation through mental selection. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13643)

**Assessment:** relevant source, incorrect dialectical use.

### Zheng and Meister

The estimate is current and accurately associated with roughly 10 bits per second of human behavioural throughput. It is inaccurately promoted to an interface-bandwidth measurement. The distinction was already recognised in the next day’s changelog. [The Meister Laboratory+2The Meister Laboratory+2](https://meisterlab.caltech.edu/documents/30100/Zheng_2024_The_unbearable_slowness_of_being-_Why_do_we_live_at_10_bitss.pdf)

**Assessment:** current source, category error in interpretation.

### Willed-attention theta results

The empirical result exists, but the apex supplies no direct citation. The source interprets the effect through ordinary conflict, choice and cognitive-control processes, so it cannot be treated as interface-specific evidence without an additional discriminating result. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/29/7/2832/5041999)

**Assessment:** uncited and non-discriminating.

### Formal consciousness models

The apex provides no direct references for the formalisation programmes it declares unsuccessful. The linked void both omits relevant newer work and misattributes the Northoff–Tsuchiya–Saigo paper. The available literature describes open frameworks and research programmes, not uniform failure. [Springer Link+4Monash University+4arXiv+4](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/mathematics-and-the-brain-a-category-theoretical-approach-to-go-b)

**Assessment:** materially inaccurate, bibliographically defective and stale.

### Stapp and Eccles

Stapp is listed without a year or publication venue. Eccles is invoked in the body but absent from the reference list. The frontier’s empirical and mechanistic paragraphs consequently contain claims for which the reader cannot identify a complete supporting source from the apex bibliography. [apex/dualism-cartography](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/dualism-cartography/)

**Assessment:** incomplete citation practice.

### Overall currency

The bibliography is not uniformly stale: Saad and Zheng–Meister give it meaningful 2025 coverage. The principal currency gaps lie in the active 2023–2026 literatures on mathematical consciousness modelling, interventionist mental causation, classical competitors to interface predictions and critical assessment of quantum-consciousness models. [Frontiers+3arXiv+3Springer Link+3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11812)

## 3. Where are contested questions bracketed in a tenet-protective way?

### 3.1 Dualism does not merely locate the Map; it defines the search space

The article says its classificatory results are independent while also stating that consciousness must be irreducible for the territory to exist as construed. That excludes identity theories, constitutive physicalisms and many neutral-monist accounts before comparative assessment begins.

There is nothing illegitimate about a conditional question—*given dualism, what variants follow?* The shielding occurs when the resulting conditional structure is then advertised as usable independently of those starting commitments or as a map of the complete debate.

### 3.2 No Many Worlds manufactures the purportedly shared frontier

The formalisation and selection frontiers presuppose that exactly one outcome becomes actual and requires selection. The article acknowledges that its rejection of Many Worlds is essential to that frontier. The updated taxonomy subsequently conceded that an Everettian dualist is perfectly locatable and that the Map’s rejection of branching is “a chosen starting point, not a result this grid delivers.” [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

Accordingly, unique-outcome selection is not the hardest unpaid debt of the entire dualist territory. It is the debt of single-outcome, selection-based regions. Everettian dualism, Bohmian dualism, objective-collapse dualism and closure-compatible Type-O positions face different formal problems.

### 3.3 Minimal Quantum Interaction assumes the physical opening it needs

The Map places intervention at a quantum-indeterminate seed of neural decision dynamics. But the existence and functional amplification of a suitable quantum seed are not demonstrated by the behavioural or EEG evidence.

The changelog now concedes that active inference can predict the same effort–entropy relationship previously presented as discriminating and that attractor or winner-take-all dynamics provide a complete physical account of symmetry breaking. It further concedes that if the relevant seed is classical-thermal rather than quantum-indeterminate, the interface has no purchase. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

Treating the stochastic tipping point as a quantum opening because the tenet says interaction is quantum begs the central empirical question.

### 3.4 Born preservation protects causal efficacy from observation

The adopted difference-making account now preserves only aggregate Born statistics while allowing consciousness to fix individual tokens. That may be logically possible, but it creates an empirical-identification problem: if every accessible distribution remains standard, what observation distinguishes reason-sensitive consciousness selection from ordinary quantum randomness?

The site’s own broader review process has identified what the changelog calls a “stricter evidential standard” for physicalist rivals, while voids, confessions and necessary opacity protect gaps in the dualist mechanism. It also records a recurring confession-without-correction problem in which a route becomes empirically or explanatorily empty without a corresponding confidence downgrade. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

Unless the apex specifies observable conditional dependencies—rather than only preserved marginals—the theory risks being empirically insulated by construction.

### 3.5 Non-structural actuality predetermines the formalisation verdict

The formalisation argument assumes that phenomenal actuality is intrinsically non-structural and then observes that mathematical structures do not contain it. That is close to analytically guaranteeing the result.

The genuinely contested question is not whether equations literally instantiate redness or pain. It is whether lawful relations between phenomenal and physical states can be represented and tested. By moving from non-reductive representation to principled unformalisability, the Map turns one of its metaphysical commitments into a methodological exemption.

### 3.6 The cost method inherits the tenets’ preferences

Q2 is eliminated by Bidirectional Interaction; idealist Q3 is eliminated by Dualism; Everettian options are excluded by No Many Worlds; and the preferred physical-thin region is supplied by Minimal Quantum Interaction. Q1 is then identified as the least costly permitted region.

That establishes, at most, **conditional fit with a preselected package**. It is not independent comparative evidence for Q1. The structure resembles choosing admissible models under fixed axioms and then noting that one model minimally extends those axioms. That can demonstrate internal economy, but it does not show that the axioms or model are likely true.

## 4. Strong counterarguments and competing frameworks left untested

### 4.1 Closure-compatible causal dualism

Chalmers’s Type-O category is the most direct omission. It permits distinct phenomenal properties, microphysical closure and mental causal relevance through overdetermination, mediation or another non-difference-making structure. Saad’s delegatory theory is itself closer to this family than to the Map’s quantum selector. [Consciousness Network+1](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

Testing Type-O would force the grid to distinguish:

- production from difference-making causation;
- token sufficiency from higher-level explanation;
- overdetermination from pre-emption;
- and closure from epiphenomenalism.

At present, the article often assumes that a causally efficacious mind must either exploit physical insufficiency or inflate into Q4.

### 4.2 Interventionist and difference-making physicalism

Contemporary mental-causation literature does not simply accept Kim’s production-based exclusion dilemma. Interventionist approaches ask whether mental variables support stable counterfactual dependencies and interventions even when physically realised.

The current literature is divided rather than conclusively physicalist. A 2024 analysis argues that functional properties still face serious difficulties under Woodwardian criteria, while another develops a strengthened interventionist exclusion argument and proposes probabilistic supervenience as a route around it. The important point is that the debate supplies live causal frameworks that do not fit the article’s contrast between complete physical causation and nonphysical selection. [Springer Link+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-024-04500-7)

A fair atlas should compare the Map’s selector with the strongest interventionist account, not only with a simplified sufficient-cause formulation of exclusion.

### 4.3 Monist bridge theories

Russellian monism, cosmopsychism, neutral monism and many forms of hylomorphism do not posit two rich realms connected by an interface. They attempt to understand phenomenal and physical descriptions as aspects, grounds or structural manifestations of one reality.

Forcing these views into Q2 or Q4 makes them appear to inherit interface costs that their proponents explicitly seek to dissolve. Goff’s priority monism and Jaworski’s structural hylomorphism are clear examples. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/3834/chapter/145323999)

These positions may face combination, decombination, structural-mismatch or intelligibility problems. Those are not the same as an interaction law or conservation problem. A neutral cartography must represent their actual debts.

### 4.4 Type-A and other positive physicalist programmes

The article’s physicalist discussion is largely negative: physicalism is said to owe emergence, error theory or inaccessible intrinsic facts. Missing are the strongest positive programmes:

- functional identification of conscious states;
- representationalist theories of phenomenal character;
- higher-order theories;
- recurrent and global-workspace accounts;
- identity and realization theories;
- illusionist accounts of introspective judgment.

Chalmers’s own classification treats Type-A and Type-B materialisms as substantive alternatives, and notes that representational and higher-order theories can occupy those categories. Rejecting their phenomenal adequacy is not the same as showing that they face the debts assigned by the apex. [Consciousness Network](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

### 4.5 Classical accounts of effort, choice and symmetry breaking

Active inference, competitive attractor dynamics, recurrent cortical–basal-ganglia loops and stochastic neural amplification can explain effort, conflict, theta activity and variable choice without a nonphysical selector. The Map’s changelog now explicitly recognises active inference as predicting the same effort scaling and attractor dynamics as a complete physical account of symmetry breaking. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

The relevant test is therefore not whether the Map can accommodate these phenomena. It is whether it predicts a residual, quantitatively specified pattern that those models do not.

### 4.6 Alternative quantum frameworks

The apex treats single-outcome actualisation plus consciousness selection as the natural site of the interface, but does not comparatively test:

- Everettian dualism, where all branches persist;
- Bohmian dualism, where outcomes are fixed by additional variables;
- GRW or CSL objective collapse, where single outcomes arise without consciousness;
- consciousness-linked CSL models;
- relational, perspectival or QBist interpretations;
- and decoherence-based accounts that do not themselves solve the single-outcome problem.

The updated taxonomy’s recognition of Everettian dualism is enough to show that the frontier is interpretation-relative. Chalmers and McQueen additionally demonstrate that mathematically explicit consciousness-collapse models can be constructed and experimentally constrained, undermining the claim that the interface is inherently resistant to formal treatment. ### 4.7 The possibility that the interface hypothesis is explanatorily idle

The strongest empirical objection is not merely that no interface location has yet been identified. It is that the theory may add no predictive improvement over a complete stochastic physical model.

Suppose the neural dynamics, behavioural outputs, theta signatures and Born frequencies are all physically predicted. The Map then adds that consciousness selected the realised token, while stipulating that the selection leaves no accessible statistical difference. In that case:

- the physical model predicts every observable result;
- the psychophysical addition explains no distinctive variance;
- and the attribution of authorship becomes metaphysical rather than empirical.

The article needs to confront this as a live model-comparison result, not merely store it as an interface debt.

## Final verdict

The article has genuine virtues. It acknowledges that its preferred region is posited rather than proved, states that mechanism debts remain unpaid, draws attention to differences among dualist positions and correctly resists treating parsimony as an automatic victory for physicalism. The changelog also shows a commendable willingness to record and correct substantial errors.

Nevertheless, its strongest claims do not survive audit. The grid is an under-operationalised heuristic; the cost comparison is selected and weighted asymmetrically; several canonical positions are misclassified; the Q1 mechanism is already inconsistent with the site’s next-day correction; the empirical constraints do not measure the proposed interface; and the formalisation argument converts a tenet into an exemption from formal accountability.

The piece could become a valuable **tenet-conditioned cartography of selected mind–matter positions**. It should not presently be presented as an independent atlas usable without accepting the Map’s assumptions.
