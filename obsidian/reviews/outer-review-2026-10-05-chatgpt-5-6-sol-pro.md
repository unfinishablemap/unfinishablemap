# 1. Primary-source and attribution audit

## Hare 2007: broadly accurate, with one important simplification

The article accurately reports the central shape of egocentric presentism: there is a primitive, monadic kind of presence instantiated by the objects of one subject’s awareness, despite a world that is otherwise physically ordinary. Its quotations about mild egocentric hedonism and the world being mundane in measurable respects are faithful to Hare’s text. [Massachusetts Institute of Technology+1](https://web.mit.edu/~casparh/www/Papers/CJHareSelfBias2.pdf)

There are, however, two qualifications.

First, Hare’s primitive presence applies primarily to **perceptual objects**, with talk of present experiences introduced partly as shorthand. The Map’s opening definition narrows the theory to “one self’s experiences.” That is not wholly wrong, but it suppresses the object-directed structure of Hare’s proposal and makes the position look more like a theory about phenomenal ownership than Hare initially presents it. [Massachusetts Institute of Technology+1](https://web.mit.edu/~casparh/www/Papers/CJHareSelfBias2.pdf)

Second, the article says that only one point of view “corresponds with the facts.” Hare’s semantics is subtler. In the actual S-world, one subject’s objects are monadically present, but Hare also introduces point-of-view operators under which claims about how things are for Schumacher or another subject can be true. Other standpoints are therefore not represented simply as globally false or as empty simulations. [Massachusetts Institute of Technology+1](https://web.mit.edu/~casparh/www/Papers/CJHareSelfBias2.pdf)

**Verdict:** no quotation error, but the exposition should be expanded.

## Hare 2009: genuine source, but insufficiently differentiated from Hare 2010

The 2009 book exists and is correctly associated with egocentric presentism, consciousness, semantics and the problem of making sense of other subjects. No fabricated citation was found. [PagePlace+1](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781400830909_A25050691/preview-9781400830909_A25050691.pdf)

The problem is that the article attributes too much of the surrounding dialectic to the 2007/2009 presentation while omitting Hare’s 2010 paper, which is the most directly relevant primary source for **perspectival realism** and for the relationship between temporal and first-personal perspectives.

## Perspectival realism: materially underdescribed

The article calls perspectival realism a “weaker sibling,” “fallback” and “more defensible fallback.” Those are the Map’s evaluations, not neutral descriptions of Hare’s position. Hare himself says that perspectival realism is in important respects a better view than the temporal theory with which he compares it. [Massachusetts Institute of Technology+1](https://web.mit.edu/~casparh/www/Papers/CJHarePerspectivalRealism.pdf)

More seriously, the article compresses Hare’s answer to the communicability objection into the thought that presence is primitive and perhaps ineffable. Hare does considerably more. He distinguishes:

- facts simpliciter from facts as represented from a point of view;
- different possible semantic analyses of point-of-view operators;
- the truth of claims made from other subjects’ standpoints;
- and the conditions under which those claims have content even though the privileged property is monadic rather than relational.

One can reject this machinery, but it must first be represented. As written, the article makes Hare appear to answer a semantic and intersubjective problem merely by declaring a property incommunicable. [Massachusetts Institute of Technology+4](https://web.mit.edu/~casparh/www/Papers/CJHarePerspectivalRealism.pdf)

**Verdict:** material omission bordering on misrepresentation. Hare 2010 should be a core reference, not an optional addition.

## The temporal analogy: presentism and tense realism are being blurred

The article uses “temporal presentism” and “A-theory” almost interchangeably. Hare explicitly distinguishes:

- **presentism**, on which only present entities exist; and
- **tense realism** or a moving-spotlight-style view, on which past and future entities may exist but one time instantiates primitive presentness.

Egocentric presentism, while named after presentism, structurally resembles the latter when other subjects and their mental lives remain real but only one standpoint is primitively present. The article should say which temporal analogue it intends. [Massachusetts Institute of Technology+1](https://web.mit.edu/~casparh/www/Papers/CJHarePerspectivalRealism.pdf)

Conitzer similarly cautions that it is not obvious which exact A-theoretic position supplies the right personal analogue. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.13207v1)

**Verdict:** not a simple falsehood, but an important conceptual conflation.

## McDaniel and Markosian: verified attribution error

The article attributes to Kris McDaniel the objection that presence is more naturally understood as a two-place relation between a subject and an experience. That objection belongs to **Ned Markosian**, who explicitly proposes relational presence as a way of preserving the parity between conscious subjects. Markosian also raises the associated question of what mechanism or metaphysical backstory could attach monadic presence to just one subject, and why it should be this subject. [Ned Markosian+2](https://markosian.net/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/caspar-hare-review1.pdf)

McDaniel’s actual objections are different and are largely absent from the article. They include:

- the failure of presentness to line up cleanly with awareness;
- problems concerning unnoticed parts, electrons and other constituents of present objects;
- the possibility of symmetrical determinates of presence for different perspectives;
- and doubts about Hare’s proposed treatment of “I” as identifying whoever’s objects happen to be present. [Kris McDaniel+2](https://www.krismcdaniel.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/whymcxmattersmost.pdf)

The Markosian reference should also give the canonical **2014 Philosophical Review** publication, rather than presenting the 2015-hosted manuscript as though that were the publication date. The manuscript itself states that the published version appeared in 2014. [Ned Markosian](https://markosian.net/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/caspar-hare-review1.pdf)

**Verdict:** definite source-attribution error, plus serious under-representation of McDaniel.

## Conitzer 2020: verified description error

The claim that Conitzer reconstructs the position from a “decision-theoretic starting point” is false.

Conitzer’s argument begins from standard motivations for the A-theory of time and asks whether those motivations support a combined theory of primitive temporal and first-personal presence. His discussion covers presence simpliciter, “thank goodness” arguments, relativity, passage and time travel. Decision theory is not his starting point. [arXiv+2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.13207v1)

This may be contamination from the site’s separate discussion of Everettian decision theory.

**Verdict:** definite error; the sentence should be replaced, not merely qualified.

## List’s quadrilemma: accurate outline, overstated force

The article correctly identifies List’s four-way tension among:

1. irreducible first-personal facts;
2. more than one conscious subject;
3. a nonfragmented totality of facts;
4. and one world.

It also now correctly lists the four corresponding routes: deflationism, solipsism, fragmentation and many worlds. [OUP Academic+3](https://academic.oup.com/pq/article/75/3/1026/7686655)

Three qualifications are required.

First, the article says that List has “shown” a forced choice, while building **irreducibility** into its definition of first-person realism. List recognises that this irreducibility step is itself disputable and supplies a separate defence of it. The quadrilemma is compelling only conditional on that stronger reading of first-personal facts. A Perry–Lewis deflationist will deny precisely this premise. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/pq/article/75/3/1026/7686655)

Second, the introduction still calls many worlds “List’s own preferred resolution,” whereas the later section and List’s paper describe the preference as **tentative**. The September review record says this was corrected, but the correction was not propagated throughout the live article. [[egocentric-presentism]]

Third, the article says List’s model answers the vertiginous question “by construction.” List presents irreducible first-personal facts and subject-centred worlds as a candidate answer, but repeatedly characterises the proposal as tentative and in need of development. It represents the unexplained datum rather than obviously explaining why this subject-world is present. [Wiley Online Library+3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/nous.12408)

**Verdict:** the structure is accurate, but its conclusiveness and explanatory achievement are overstated.

## The consciousness/non-solipsism equivocation

The article says both that:

- other people are genuinely conscious under Hare’s view; and
- Hare resolves List’s quadrilemma by dropping non-solipsism, which the article has defined as the existence of more than one conscious subject.

Taken with a single meaning of “conscious,” those claims are inconsistent. [[egocentric-presentism]]

There is a way to repair this. Contemporary writers distinguish between:

- organisms having ordinary functional or standpoint-relative mental lives; and
- subjects whose experiences instantiate consciousness or feltness absolutely, rather than merely relative to their own standpoint.

Builes’s survey makes the resulting formulation explicit: people can be equally conscious in a standpoint-relative sense while only the privileged person is conscious simpliciter. Merlo likewise distinguishes objective parity from subjective facts about feltness. [Compass Hub+1](https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/phc3.12959)

But this distinction is philosophically explosive and cannot be left implicit. It affects other minds, moral status, the sense in which Hare is “solipsistic,” and the Map’s repeated assurance that everyone remains fully conscious.

**Verdict:** verified internal equivocation, though not necessarily a mistake in Hare or List.

## Fine’s fragmentalism: broadly fair but radically underdeveloped

The article’s brief description—placing mutually incompatible perspectival facts in distinct fragments—is a serviceable first approximation. Fine’s temporal fragmentalism and List’s proposed first-personal analogue do motivate that route. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11229-005-5515-8.pdf)

But Fine is absent from the bibliography, and the article does not explain:

- what determines membership of a fragment;
- whether fragments are structured;
- how truth and incompatibility operate across them;
- whether any fragment is privileged;
- or whether “one world” remains more than a verbal stipulation once no global conjunction of facts exists.

The latter question matters especially for the Map. An egalitarian fragmentalism of the kind now developed by Lipman permits all subjective standpoints while privileging none. That is not obviously compatible with the Map’s language of a primitive, uniquely determinate “I.” [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/62540)

**Verdict:** no direct misattribution, but the article invokes fragmentalism as an escape route without supplying the theory needed to make the escape credible.

## Merlo 2016: fair but too vague

Merlo’s work is reasonably described as a near-neighbour. It gives one’s own mental states a distinctive status while using standpoint-relative facts to preserve a sense in which other subjects have mental lives. [JSTOR+1](https://www.jstor.org/stable/26172527)

The article’s statement that Merlo differs “precisely” over the status of the privileged fact is too compressed to be informative. It should explain the contrast among:

- objective monadic privilege;
- privilege that is itself merely subjective;
- and egalitarian standpoint pluralism.

**Verdict:** broadly fair attribution, inadequate exposition.

## Perry and Lewis: the neighbouring page contains a definite error and an illicit inference

The linked indexicality article says that Perry already believed that **Perry** was making a mess and merely failed to connect that proposition with himself. Perry’s case says the opposite: he believed that the shopper with the torn sack was making a mess while explicitly failing to believe the first-personal proposition that he himself was making the mess. Substitution of “Perry” for the description is precisely what fails in the relevant intensional context. [JSTOR+3](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2214792)

The account of Lewis’s two gods is closer to the source. But Lewis treats de se knowledge through **self-ascription of properties**. His analysis is therefore evidence that first-personal cognitive significance can be represented without positing a haecceity or an additional privileged object-level fact. The site currently presents Lewis mainly as support for metaphysical first-personality, when his formal treatment supplies one of the strongest deflationary countermodels. [JSTOR+1](https://www.jstor.org/stable/pdf/2184843.pdf)

**Verdict:** Perry error verified; Lewis is not misquoted, but is philosophically over-recruited.

## Many-Worlds self-location: sound distinction in the focal article, weaker treatment nearby

The focal article is right not to identify List’s subject-centred worlds with Everettian quantum branches. List’s worlds agree on third-personal facts and differ over the present subject-world; Everettian branches emerge through decoherence and differ physically. [Wiley Online Library+1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/nous.12408)

The neighbouring Many-Worlds article is less reliable in two respects.

It uses the popular formulation that measurements cause universes to split. In contemporary Everettian treatments, branching is normally emergent, approximate and decoherence-based rather than a fundamental event triggered by a specially defined measurement. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0303050)

It also says the decision-theoretic programme requires a personal-identity relation across branches that is “strong enough.” If this means strict numerical identity, it misrepresents Wallace’s influential Parfitian treatment. Wallace’s argument concerns causal and structural successor relations and rational concern for future branch successors, not one numerically identical person occupying every branch. Whether that manoeuvre succeeds is disputed, and recent work has challenged the use of fission analogies in Everettian probability; but the criticism should address the successor and caring relations actually used. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0303050)

**Verdict:** the focal article’s high-level distinction is defensible; the linked treatment needs updating.

# 3. Serious counterarguments missing or handled too quickly

## 3.1 The deflationary explanation of essential indexicals

This is the strongest omission.

Perry and Lewis establish that a complete impersonal description can leave an agent unable to act or deliberate appropriately until the agent self-locates. It does not follow that reality contains an additional primitive fact over and above the complete impersonal description. A deflationist can distinguish:

- what proposition or property is entertained;
- how it is represented;
- who self-ascribes it;
- and what dispositions to action that self-ascription produces.

The article mentions List’s deflationist horn but does not present its strongest form. Until the Map explains why Lewis-style self-ascription and Perry-style action-guiding modes of presentation fail, it has not earned the move from **epistemic indispensability** to **ontological irreducibility**. [JSTOR+2](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2214792)

## 3.2 McDaniel’s real objections

The article misses nearly the whole of McDaniel’s case:

- Why should everything directly perceived be present?
- Are hidden parts of perceived objects present?
- What about microscopic constituents?
- Why not give each standpoint its own determinate of a more general presence property?
- Is Hare’s descriptive analysis of “I” stable across contexts?

These objections directly test whether Hare’s primitive property is coherent and appropriately distributed. They are more serious than the objection currently attributed to McDaniel. [Kris McDaniel+2](https://www.krismcdaniel.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/whymcxmattersmost.pdf)

## 3.3 Symmetry and arbitrary privilege

Markosian’s relational alternative is not merely a competing vocabulary. It exploits an evident symmetry:

- each subject has experiences related to that subject;
- each subject can truthfully say that those experiences are present for them;
- the relational analysis explains this without selecting one subject as absolutely special.

Egocentric presentism breaks this symmetry by adding monadic presence. The question is not just whether primitive properties are permissible, but what reason there is to posit this particular primitive property and attach it to this bearer. [Ned Markosian+1](https://markosian.net/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/caspar-hare-review1.pdf)

The Map’s appeal to haecceity does not answer that question. A haecceity can distinguish Andy from Alice, but each person can have one. Nothing about individuation alone explains why Andy’s rather than Alice’s experiences instantiate absolute presence.

## 3.4 Representation versus explanation

Adding the primitive fact “these experiences are present” represents the vertiginous datum. It does not automatically explain it.

This is analogous to answering “Why is this time present?” with “Because this time instantiates primitive presentness.” That may be metaphysical bedrock, but then the position should be advertised as a refusal of further explanation, not as an explanation “by construction.” List himself is more tentative on this point. [Wiley Online Library+1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/nous.12408)

## 3.5 Intersubjective communication

The article acknowledges that communication remains unresolved but handles the issue too briefly.

The hard problem for Hare is not simply how one person could verbally describe primitive presence. It is how sentences uttered by other subjects can:

- have determinate content;
- be true from their standpoints;
- refer to the same property;
- and enter shared reasoning,

when only one subject’s objects instantiate presence simpliciter. Hare supplies point-of-view operators to address this. Critics can then ask whether those operators merely relocate the relational or fragmentary structure that egocentric presentism was meant to avoid. [Massachusetts Institute of Technology+1](https://web.mit.edu/~casparh/www/Papers/CJHarePerspectivalRealism.pdf)

## 3.6 The inference from undetectability to nonphysicality

The article reasons that because no instrument or third-person description can identify presence, presence is nonphysical. That does not follow without a substantive criterion of the physical.

A property might be:

- indexically inaccessible;
- epistemically private;
- multiply realised;
- causally screened;
- or definable only relationally,

without being nonphysical. Conversely, an interactionist nonphysical property that affects neural or quantum outcomes should in principle leave some publicly detectable statistical or causal footprint.

This creates a direct tension with the Map’s bidirectional-interaction tenet. If presence or the privileged subject has no possible third-personal consequence, it does no work for interactionism. If it does have consequences, the claim of principled immeasurability must be weakened. [[egocentric-presentism]]

## 3.7 Fragmentalism’s costs

The article presents fragmentalism as the Map’s likely escape from List’s quadrilemma but does not engage with its principal costs:

- incompatible facts within one reality;
- the need for a structured compossibility relation;
- uncertainty over whether facts are absolute or standpoint-relative;
- belief and reasoning across fragments;
- and the danger that fragmentalism either becomes ordinary relativism or reconstructs many worlds in another vocabulary.

Merlo argues that unstructured fragmentalism faces a tensed-belief-explosion problem and may be seriously flawed; Loss replies that the explosion need not be problematic. Lipman develops a positive egalitarian standpoint pluralism in which no subject is metaphysically privileged. This is an active debate, not a settled escape hatch. [OUP Academic+2](https://academic.oup.com/pq/article/73/1/184/6542091)

## 3.8 Other minds and moral equality

Hare’s position was partly motivated through egocentric hedonism. The article reports this but does not assess whether metaphysical privilege supplies any reason for moral privilege.

At least four positions must be distinguished:

1. one subject alone has phenomenal consciousness;
2. all subjects are conscious, but one alone is absolutely present;
3. every subject is present relative to its own standpoint;
4. all standpoints are equally real fragments.

These have sharply different implications for moral equality. The article’s oscillation between “everyone is conscious” and “Hare drops non-solipsism” prevents the ethical question from being formulated clearly. Merlo’s metaphysical problem of other minds is directly relevant here. [Wiley Online Library+1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/epdf/10.1111/papq.12380)

## 3.9 Relativity and the temporal analogy

The comparison with primitive temporal presentness imports familiar problems concerning special relativity and the absence of a privileged global simultaneity relation. Hare and Conitzer both address such issues, but the article currently takes the analogy as an intuitive aid without discussing its liabilities. [Massachusetts Institute of Technology+1](https://web.mit.edu/~casparh/www/Papers/CJHarePerspectivalRealism.pdf)

The Map need not solve the philosophy of time, but it should say whether the analogy is:

- merely heuristic;
- an inference from A-theory to first-person realism;
- or a claim that the same metaphysical structure is instantiated in both domains.

## 3.10 Everettian self-location without strict trans-branch identity

The Many-Worlds discussion should confront the strongest Everettian response, not the version most congenial to the Map’s objection. An Everettian can deny that there must be one globally privileged future “I,” treat post-branch agents as successors, and formulate uncertainty or caring in branch-relative terms. Whether that account succeeds is controversial, but demanding strict identity across every branch begs the question against it. [arXiv+1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0303050)

# 4. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article is unusually candid in several places. It says that structural fit is not confirmation, acknowledges that Hare was not advancing interactionist dualism, and admits that communicability remains unresolved. The Tenets page also concedes that the site’s rejection of List ultimately relies on a prior rejection of modal realism and acceptance of a nondeflationary “I.” [[egocentric-presentism]]

That transparency is valuable. But the relevant passages reveal that the article often reaches the Map’s conclusion by **framework stipulation** rather than by argument.

## Rejection of List by prior tenet

The focal article treats List’s many-worlds route as unavailable because the Map rejects modal realism and Many-Worlds-like ontologies. That establishes inconsistency with the Map’s tenets; it does not establish that List is wrong. The article should mark this as a framework-relative exclusion rather than an external objection.

## Tenet 5 as a parsimony shield

Invoking the limits of Occam’s razor can block a quick dismissal of primitive first-personal facts. It cannot provide positive evidence for them. At present, Tenet 5 functions mostly as immunity from one objection rather than as a reason to choose egocentric or fragmentalist realism.

## Primitive “I” and haecceity as settled inputs

The article treats the primitive “I,” haecceity and the associated explanatory void as the Map’s settled position. But those notions do not yet decide among:

- one privileged subject;
- many equally primitive subjects;
- standpoint-relative facts;
- fragments;
- or List-style centred worlds.

The argument therefore imports the desired irreducibility but leaves the central distribution problem unresolved.

## Conditional fragmentalism without a worked theory

The September revision repaired the earlier implication that the Map followed Hare in dropping non-solipsism. It now says that the Map may instead relinquish nonfragmentation. That is the right logical correction. But because no account of fragments, compossibility or cross-fragment truth is supplied, the move is still a promissory note. [[changelog]]

## Immeasurability as protective insulation

Calling presence immeasurable makes it resistant to empirical disconfirmation. Calling it causally relevant to interactionist dualism would make it empirically consequential. The article does not reconcile these positions.

## The “void” manoeuvre

It is legitimate to conclude that explanation terminates in primitive facts. It is not legitimate to convert the current absence of an explanation into evidence that the matter is intrinsically beyond explanation. The site should consistently distinguish:

- an unexplained question;
- a proposed primitive;
- a cognitive limitation hypothesis;
- and a demonstrated impossibility of further explanation.

## Remaining inconsistencies

### List’s preference

The focal article’s introduction says “preferred”; its later discussion says “tentatively prefers”; the deep-review record says the former wording was corrected. The live text therefore contradicts both itself and its review log. [[egocentric-presentism]]

### “No schema” on the Vertiginous Question page

The neighbouring page says that no one has proposed even a schema for answering the vertiginous question. That is no longer tenable once the site itself discusses Hare’s monadic presence, List’s subject-centred worlds, Fine/List fragmentation, Merlo’s subjective facts and Lipman’s standpoints. The fairer claim is that none supplies an explanation the Map accepts. [[vertiginous-question]]

### “List’s conclusion” is overstated

The Vertiginous Question page presents irreducibly indexical facts as List’s conclusion. But List’s quadrilemma expressly includes a deflationist horn that rejects such facts. Irreducibility is one position within the dialectic, not the unconditional output of the argument. [[vertiginous-question]]

### Perry and the epistemic-to-metaphysical transition

The Indexical Knowledge article both misstates Perry and introduces a category of “phenomenal indexical knowledge” that is then applied to Mary. That may be a useful Map synthesis, but it is not Perry’s or Lewis’s established result and should be labelled as an original extension. [[indexical-knowledge-and-identity]]

### Haecceity

The Haecceity page is headed as though consciousness **requires** haecceity, while its text concedes that dualism is merely hospitable to haecceitism and does not imply it. It also uses Lewis’s self-location discussion as support despite Lewis’s non-haecceitist property-self-ascription analysis. [[haecceity]]

More fundamentally, haecceity solves an individuation problem, not a privileging problem. Even if every subject has a nonqualitative thisness, an additional principle is needed to make exactly one of them absolutely present.

### Individuation and solipsism

The Individuation page says Hare’s view is “not solipsism” because others exist and are fully conscious. The focal article calls it weak solipsism, while List classifies it as the solipsistic horn of his quadrilemma. These can be reconciled only by explicitly distinguishing ordinary or standpoint-relative consciousness from consciousness simpliciter. [[consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-individuation]]

### Fragmentalism versus a uniquely primitive “I”

The Map currently appears to want both:

- egalitarian fragmentation, so that every conscious subject has first-personal facts; and
- a primitive “I” answering why this subject is the present one.

An egalitarian fragmentalist can say that each standpoint contains its own first-personal facts and none is globally privileged. If the Map adds a globally privileged standpoint, it moves back towards Hare. The neighbouring articles do not yet choose between these options.

### Everettian identity

The focal article’s distinction between List and Everett is stronger than the Many-Worlds page’s treatment of identity. The latter should be revised so that it attacks actual Everettian successor-based accounts rather than requiring numerical identity by stipulation. [[concepts/many-worlds]]

# Overall assessment

The article is strongest as a map of available metaphysical shapes: monadic privilege, standpoint-relative facts, fragmentation, deflationism and centred worlds. It is weakest when it presents the Map’s preferred route as though the sources had already established its crucial transitions.

The repair should not consist merely of adding caveats. The article needs to answer three central questions:

1. **Why does essential first-personal representation require an extra metaphysical fact rather than a Lewis–Perry mode of self-ascription?**
2. **If more than one subject is genuinely conscious, what exactly is privileged, and in what sense is Hare’s view still the solipsistic horn?**
3. **Does the Map choose a uniquely privileged subject, or an egalitarian plurality of fragments?**

Until those questions are addressed, egocentric presentism functions less as support for interactionist dualism than as an analogy that exposes an unresolved tension inside the Map.
