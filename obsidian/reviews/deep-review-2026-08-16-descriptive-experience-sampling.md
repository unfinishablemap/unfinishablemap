## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Misstated joint conclusion in the lead; contradicted the article's own body.** The lead asserted that Hurlburt and Schwitzgebel "jointly concluded that momentary sampling beats armchair generalisation." Both parties explicitly refuse that ranking in the very paper where they settle the question (Hurlburt & Schwitzgebel 2011a). Hurlburt calls the ranking reading "backwards": *"There is no tribunal; there are only the genuinely methodologically pluralistic judgments about advantages and disadvantages. DES is a defendant before that methodologically plural tribunal, as should be thought sampling, armchair introspection, Claire Petitmengin's explicitation interview..."* Schwitzgebel, for his part, writes *"I agree with Charles Siewert that Descriptive Experience Sampling (DES) does not deserve a uniquely high position."* The article's own "Rival Readings" section already had it right — "joint responses settled on methodological pluralism rather than a verdict" — so the lead contradicted the body roughly sixty lines below it. This was also a **false shared commitment**: it recruited the method's chief critic into ratifying a superiority claim he declined to grant. **Resolution**: lead rewritten to state the actual joint landing point, quoting Hurlburt's "a defendant before that methodologically plural tribunal" and citing 2011a. The Map's own evaluative judgement ("the most disciplined available way of asking what was present at a moment") is untouched and now stands unambiguously as the Map's, which sharpens the source/Map separation the article otherwise handles well.

## Stability Notes

- The **realist-versus-deflationary reading of DES transcripts** is a bedrock disagreement, not a fixable defect. The article already concedes that nothing in the method's discipline rules out heterophenomenology and that the Map's realist reading is tenet-derived. Future reviews should not re-flag this.
- **Schwitzgebel's introspective pessimism** is likewise bedrock. The article deliberately adopts his scepticism about the report channel without claiming his agreement about consciousness; that asymmetry is intentional and should be preserved, not "resolved."
- The Map's own judgement that DES is "the most disciplined available way of asking what was present at a moment" is an evaluative claim by the Map, and it survives the pluralism finding above — Hurlburt and Schwitzgebel declined to *crown* DES, which is a different claim from denying it is the most disciplined option available. Do not read the corrected lead as licensing a downgrade of that sentence.
