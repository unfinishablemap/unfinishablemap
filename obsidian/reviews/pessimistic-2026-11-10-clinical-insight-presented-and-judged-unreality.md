## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would accept the page's best move, that insight is a classification, and then extend it. On her view "felt reality" is also a classification, made by a sub-personal system with no folk-psychological standing. The page's answer is depersonalisation: belief stands while felt reality goes. That shows felt reality is not the *explicit* belief. It does not show felt reality is anything other than a lower-level estimate. The deep review marked this as bedrock, and it is not re-flagged. The page does, however, owe her the neuroscience she would cite. A model of exactly this dissociation exists (Seth, Suzuki & Critchley 2012), and the page does not mention it (Issue 3).

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would say the page has rediscovered the difference between a perceptual estimate and a belief, and that every hierarchical model predicts the two can come apart. The page grants that "Both registers have physicalist models" (L128). The model it chooses, though, is one it can only call "close to felt reality". It also attributes a metaphysics to Friston that his own stance paper contradicts (Issue 3). Dennett would also press on L130, "Insight is belief-formation on every account". The page's own L51 and L121 concede that Marková and Berrios's "continuum of thinking and feeling" resists a purely cognitive gloss, so "every account" overclaims (folded into Issue 1).

## Medium Issues

### Issue 1: The scope rule equates "judged" with "Factor 2"
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/clinical-insight-presented-and-judged-unreality.md`
- **Location**: §Where the Scope Rule Falls, L112: "Insight is judged, so its presence or loss is a fact about belief evaluation, on the Factor-2 side." Secondary locus: L130, "Insight is belief-formation on every account".
- **Problem**: the page's own definitions show the gap. L110 defines Factor 2 as "the impairment of belief evaluation that turns an anomaly into a fixed delusion". Insight is the *output* of belief evaluation (L47). A change in an output does not show the evaluator is impaired. On one-factor and face-value readings a changed experience, met by a normal evaluator, changes the verdict. The next paragraph (L114) leaves exactly that open for Cotard: "an experience so global that a normal evaluator would endorse it". So L112 states categorically what L114 treats as open. The Map's Cotard page records the rivals by name: Maher's view at L52, Gerrans (2002) "a one-stage model is adequate" and Billon's face-value reading at L56. The defect matters beyond this page, because Capgras L71 links its "reasoning pathology the Map takes no position on" to `#scope-rule`. L130's "on every account" is the same overreach: the page's own L51 and L121 concede a reading of insight that is not purely cognitive.
- **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: keep the scope rule. Separate "a fact about the judgement" from "a Factor-2 impairment" (+~20 words), and change "on every account" to "on the accounts followed here" (+2). The rule ("the Map has no reason to claim insight-loss") survives unchanged, because the Map makes no claim about the judgement whichever factor moved it. → task (P2).

### Issue 2: §limits misdescribes the Fisher sample
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L120, "Seventeen participants in one study and ten in another, all diagnosed under a criterion that requires insight". Secondary locus: L37, "filtered for insight by the diagnosis itself".
- **Problem**: in Fisher et al. 2026 (PMC13180270, raw full text), eligibility is "Community-dwelling adults aged ≥50 years with self-reported eye disease and CBS". Table 3 gives "CBS diagnosis Formal diagnosis 13 Self-diagnosis 4". The authors' limitation reads "Exclusion of differential diagnoses for visual hallucinations was not feasible, but all participants demonstrated insight". Insight was observed in the sample. It was not a recruitment criterion, and four of the seventeen were never diagnosed by anyone. (Suzuki et al.'s ten "reported visual loss resulting in a formal diagnosis of Charles Bonnet Syndrome", with criteria unstated.) The selection effect is real, because people who identify their visions as CBS have already judged them unreal. But the mechanism the page states is false for one of its two key studies.
- **Severity**: Medium. This is source fidelity on the page's own stated limit. The limit survives, so the deep review's "keep the three limits intact" note is honoured.
- **Recommendation**: restate the filter as "diagnosis or self-identification" in both loci (+~19). → task (P3).

### Issue 3: The physicalist rival is mis-attributed and understated
- **File**: same
- **Location**: Relation to Site Perspective, Dualism, L128: "working in active inference, which Friston develops as a physicalist framework, Limanowski and Friston (2018) propose ..."
- **Problem**: (a) The attribution is unsourced, and Friston's own co-authored statement of stance contradicts it. Solms & Friston 2018 (*JCS* 25(5–6), UCL Discovery PDF, raw): "It is in this (dual-aspect monist) sense that we aim to provide a physical theory of what Nagel calls the 'subjective character' of experience". The paper also has a section headed "A Comment on Dual-Aspect Monism". The Limanowski & Friston paper's own physicalist credential is verifiable: it starts from Metzinger's self-model theory, on which "a transparent phenomenal self-model ... itself can in principle be reduced to brain processes" (PMC5945877, raw). (b) Lens 4 asks for the physicalist reading at full strength. The page calls depersonalisation "the clean case for a presented variable" (L96) and uses it as its main reason for two registers. Yet it never cites the physicalist model of that exact case. Seth, Suzuki & Critchley (2012) define presence as "the subjective sense of reality of the world and of the self within the world". They model it as the "successful suppression by top-down predictions of informative interoceptive signals" and read derealisation as "loss of subjective sense of reality of the world" (PMC3254200, raw). The Map's own [[depersonalisation]] page (L67) already calls this "the strongest physicalist rival". At full strength the physicalist does not deny the presented variable. They predict its dissociation from belief, because precision at a lower level and belief at a higher level are separate parameters of one hierarchy. The page's verdict ("Dualism is unaffected") is right, but its case for that verdict is weaker than it should be.
- **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: replace the Friston clause with the paper's own self-model-theory framing, and add Seth et al. 2012 with verified wording (+~70, including the reference). → task (P3).

- **Problem**:

López-Silva et al. 2022 (PMC9424625, raw) report Docherty et al.
