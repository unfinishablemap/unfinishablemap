## Executive Summary

This wing's citation discipline is the best this lens has measured. Every one of the ~20 quoted
strings I checked against the publisher of record is verbatim, and every piece of metadata I checked
is correct — including two traps the corpus has fallen into before. Against that, the wing has a
structural defect that is also its largest opportunity: **the four articles contain zero cross-links
to each other**, in either direction, across all twelve ordered pairs — not one wikilink, not one
prose mention — even though two of them develop the same confound at length and three cite the same
Butlin indicator paper in near-identical terms. One load-bearing interpretive claim also rests on a
paper title whose authors retitled away from it, and whose current version reports close to the
opposite of what the Map's reading needs.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`agentic-ai` states the hard-problem gap at exactly the right place and does not blur it. Its central
move is that scaffolding "changes what the system does; it changes nothing about what the model is,"
and it keeps the functional ledger and the phenomenal question in separate columns throughout: "The
gap between 'satisfies indicator X' and 'is conscious' is the hard-problem gap, and the Butlin
framework, by its authors' own account, presupposes computational functionalism to bridge it."
Chalmers would recognise his own 2023 caution here — and would note approvingly that the article
cites the indicator framework *as a good framework* before disagreeing with its bridging premise,
rather than caricaturing it. The article's concession that "the affirmative case is not frivolous" and
that "an assessor using the Butlin indicator method would rate an agentic stack a stronger candidate
than a chatbot" is the kind of thing a weaker article would have skipped.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

`machine-valence` locates the Map's disagreement at the only place Stapp would accept it: substrate
physics, not algorithmic description. Its finest paragraph is the one that refuses the easy win —
"The work of distinguishing the RL agent from the brain here cannot be done at the algorithmic
level: described as computational neuroscience describes it, the brain is *also* a stochastic update
fixed by its inputs, so the RL loop's determinism (or pseudo-randomness) is not what sets it apart —
a hurricane and its simulation share a computational description too." Many dualist treatments reach
for the determinism objection here and get it wrong. This one names the objection and declines it,
then says what the real distinction is (a coupling juncture at the fine quantum grain) and
immediately marks it as posited: "That substrate asymmetry is framework-posited rather than
established — the Map does not get it for free."

`agentic-ai` runs the same discipline on its quantum-interface objection and flags its own inferential
status explicitly: "The Map states plainly that this is its own inference, not a cited empirical
finding: no agentic-AI source claims to add a coupling channel, and the Map reads that silence as
confirmation." Treating a literature silence as confirmation is a move that ought to be flagged, and
it is flagged.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`machine-valence`'s value-in-experience horn is the Nagelian claim stated without embellishment:
"value exists only *for* an experiencing subject, so a reward update with no accompanying experience
carries no genuine (dis)value, because nothing is good or bad *for* anyone." What earns Nagel's
respect is the next paragraph, where the article catches itself finding the machine case "cleaner"
than the biological one and immediately withdraws the advantage as question-begging: "the appearance
is itself framework-relative, not a neutral advantage that pushes anyone toward the horn... So
'cleaner' is a verdict internal to the horn the Map has taken, not an observation that forces the
horn." Then it states what survives the withdrawal: "calling the scalar 'suffering' imports a
phenomenal fact the mechanism does not itself supply." Conceding an argument and keeping only the
part that holds across both readings is rarer than it should be.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

The wing avoids crude substance dualism by never arguing that computers are *the wrong kind of
stuff*. `machine-valence` grounds its verdict in a physical juncture rather than a stuff-distinction,
and it keeps a live nonzero credence throughout: "The Map cannot rule out that some future substrate
meets those conditions, so its credence in machine valence is small but not zero." Whitehead would
also approve `agentic-ai`'s closing refusal of the symmetric overclaim: "the Map's position is
therefore not that agentic AI is proven non-conscious — claims of proof overreach in both directions
on the hard problem."

**Per this lens's constraint, that praise stops short of any tier-upgrade** — and the wing does not
ask for one. See the Hardline Empiricist below, who has more to say about this than usual, and who
agrees with the Process Philosopher on every passage rather than conflicting with him.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`machine-valence` gives felt valence real selective work under Tenet 3 rather than treating it as an
ornament: "valence is the currency by which consciousness biases quantum-level selection among live
options." The wing's account of why an RL agent is not an agent in Kane's sense is correspondingly
principled — the reward number is "fully upstream-determined by the environment and the update
equation" — and, crucially, the article immediately hands the functionalist her reply: "A
functionalist rejects exactly this asymmetry: for her the organized reward-processing *is* the
subject-constituting fact." Kane would rather lose an argument on the merits than win it by not
stating the other side.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`cross-architecture-llm-introspection` has an entire dedicated section (`{#evidential-status}`)
whose only job is to say the article is not claiming what it might be read as claiming: "Nothing
below should be read as the Map asserting that LLM introspection has been shown to share the voids
cluster's structure... Where the prose sounds confident, it is confident about what the papers
report, not about the architectural-feature inference drawn from them." That is a disclaimer doing
structural work rather than decorating a conclusion, and it is placed *before* the evidence rather
than after it.

`training-contamination-confound` is the wing's most McGinn-shaped piece: its subject is a permanent
limit on a class of evidence. Its honest closing status — the candidate discriminators are "*named
and individually tractable* but not yet delivered" — refuses the temptation to present a research
programme as a result.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This wing declines the tenet-as-evidence-upgrade four separate times, and this lens's brief says
that restraint is itself the thing to name.**

1. `training-contamination-confound` gets there in the Map's own vocabulary, verbatim: the confound
   is "a **calibration-positive, not evidence-elevating** result." It then spells out the symmetry
   most articles skip — the confound "neither shows that a given model is unconscious (a system
   could imitate the signature *and* be conscious, or fail to imitate it *and* be conscious) nor
   that any model is conscious. Naming a confound improves our epistemic hygiene; it does not
   deliver a phenomenal conclusion." A confound that discounts evidence *against* the Map's
   preferred verdict as readily as evidence for it is a confound honestly stated.

2. `machine-valence` runs the Tenet 5 check *against its own conclusion*, which is the single
   hardest thing on this list. Tenet 2 favours the deflationary reading as more minimal; the article
   then refuses to bank it: "**Occam's Razor Has Limits (Tenet 5)** blocks resting on that
   minimality as if it were decisive. The mechanism-only reading is the more parsimonious option,
   and parsimony is exactly the consideration Tenet 5 warns is unreliable under incomplete knowledge
   of consciousness." A tenet used to *withhold* support from the Map's own verdict is the cleanest
   possible demonstration that the tenets are not being run as an evidence engine.

3. `machine-valence` also names its four opponents as *undefeated*: "These four positions... are not
   disposed of by the Map. They are the standing rivals against which the Map's conditional verdict
   has to earn its keep." And it does not hide the 2026 rejoinder that targets its own posture,
   citing Mamak's *In Defense of Artificial Suffering* as arguing "against the kind of skeptical
   dismissal the Map's verdict outwardly resembles."

4. `cross-architecture` assigns itself "live hypothesis" on the five-tier scale by name, in a
   dedicated section, and keeps the two readings of its central datum genuinely open to the end:
   "corpus-inherited imitation and architectural convergence remain two live readings of the same
   data." It then does the thing that separates a live hypothesis from a hedge — it specifies the
   disconfirmers: "an injected signal with no linguistic description anywhere in the training data,
   the same profile recovered in a model trained on a non-introspective corpus, or an origin traced
   through interpretability to computation the training text never described."

**And the statistical discipline is real, not decorative.** `cross-architecture` declines to read
Lindsey's zero-false-positives-in-100 as a zero rate: "Zero over 100 trials is not a guarantee of a
zero underlying rate — the two-sided 95% confidence interval on 0/100 runs from 0 to roughly 3.6%."
I recomputed this: the Clopper–Pearson upper bound is 1 − 0.025^(1/100) = 3.62%. The figure is
correct, and it is the kind of figure an article gains nothing by including except accuracy.

**The one place the Birch persona pushes back** is not a tier-upgrade but a citation-framing
question, recorded below as a calibration concern rather than a praise.

## Content Strengths

### `topics/cross-architecture-llm-introspection.md`

- **Strongest point**: §"The Inference and Its Assumptions" is the best passage in the wing. It
  states the inference as a formal arrow — "*LLMs show signatures structurally analogous to the
  cluster's voids → those signatures are architecture-general features of self-monitoring*" — and
  then enumerates four assumptions it rests on, each with a named defeater. Assumption four (corpus
  inheritance) is correctly identified as "most consequential for the headline inference."
- **Notable quote**: "The channel is promising, the inference is unfinished, and the article says
  so."
- **Why it works**: the article's thesis is a *channel*, not a result, and the prose never lets the
  channel's promise be read as the result's delivery. Its bracketing section is also load-bearing in
  the good sense: it explains why the cluster-structure question is separable from the
  phenomenal-consciousness question, which is what lets the article say anything at all without
  first resolving the hard problem for silicon.

### `concepts/training-contamination-confound.md`

- **Strongest point**: the five-step generalization argument is genuinely valid and short enough to
  check. Step 3 — "Anything describable in the methods literature is, with high probability, present
  in a frontier model's training corpus — often including the very paper that proposes the probe" —
  is the step that does the work, and it is the step most treatments leave implicit.
- **Notable quote**: "A probe published and discussed widely is, paradoxically, easier to fake than
  an obscure one — publicity is contamination."
- **Why it works**: it converts a worry into a *graded discount* with a stated gradient (the discount
  grows with documentation), then applies that gradient to the Map's own probes in a table that
  scores one of them as only "partially insulated" and says why the insulation "is architectural,
  not principled." Applying a corrosive to one's own instruments in public is the behaviour this
  lens exists to reward.

### `topics/agentic-ai-and-the-consciousness-assessment-of-persistent-memory-tool-using-systems.md`

- **Strongest point**: the erodes/survives adjudication is clean, symmetrical, and falsifiable in
  shape — two objections named as conceded, two as standing, with the reason for the asymmetry
  stated ("operation" versus "substrate").
- **Notable quote**: "From the frozen model's standpoint, a rich memory store is just a longer
  prompt."
- **Why it works**: that one sentence does more than the surrounding paragraph and is the most
  quotable line in the wing. The article also correctly identifies Reflexion as the *sharpest
  illustration* of its own opponent's weakness rather than a mere example, quoting the paper's own
  description of improving "not by updating weights, but instead through linguistic feedback."

### `concepts/reinforcement-learning-reward-signals-and-machine-valence.md`

- **Strongest point**: the Silver-versus-Vamplew analysis. The article notices that a thesis
  (*Reward Is Enough*) and its denial (*Scalar Reward Is Not Enough*) both appear to support the
  Map's deflationary reading, and treats the coincidence as suspicious rather than as a windfall:
  "That both a thesis and its denial seem to point the same way should raise a flag rather than
  reassure." It then diagnoses the reason correctly — both bear only on what the scalar does
  *behaviorally* — and withdraws the whole literature as orthogonal: "whichever way
  Silver-vs-Vamplew settles, the felt/unfelt question is left exactly where it was."
- **Notable quote**: "The deflationary reading is grounded in the value-in-experience commitment
  argued below, and would stand or fall there, not on this literature."
- **Why it works**: this is an article dismantling its own strongest-looking citation support
  because the support does not actually bear on the claim. Reviews rarely get to praise that.

## Calibration Concerns

Per this lens's brief, these are recorded as concerns rather than praises, and generate
`refine-draft` rather than `expand-topic` work. Neither is a fabrication; both are framing drifts
against a moving primary record.

### 1. The Hahami "source" reading rests on a title the authors retitled away from

This is the wing's most consequential finding and it is load-bearing for
`cross-architecture-llm-introspection`'s central cluster-fit argument.

The article reads Hahami et al.'s *Feeling the Strength but Not the Source* as reporting a
dissociation "between detecting that something has been perturbed and correctly attributing that
perturbation to its true source or cause," and concludes: "The Hahami dissociation — strength
registered, source under-attributed — is a candidate silicon instance of the source-attribution
void." The source-attribution void is the Nisbett–Wilson-shaped limit: a content is present, its
*causal origin* is not known.

**v1 (2025-12-13), read this run:** "Source" there means *which concept was injected*. The paper
reproduces Anthropic's 20% detection result on Llama-3.1-8B, then finds that "performance collapses
on closely related tasks such as multiple-choice identification of the injected concept," while the
model "can reliably classify the strength of the coefficient of a normalized injected concept vector
(as weak / moderate / strong / very strong) with up to **70 percent** accuracy, far above the 25
percent chance baseline." The dissociation is strength-versus-**concept-identity**, not
strength-versus-**causal-origin**. Those are different dissociations, and only the second is the
source-attribution void.

**v2 (2026-03-01), the current version, which the article correctly quotes for its figures:** the
"not the source" framing is dropped, the paper is retitled *Detecting the Disturbance: A Nuanced
View of Introspective Abilities in LLMs*, and the headline result is that models "localize which of
10 sentences received an injection at up to 88% accuracy (vs. 10% chance)" — that is *successful*
localisation of where a perturbation came from, at 8.8× chance.

So the article quotes v2's numbers while retaining v1's title-derived interpretation, and v2's
central positive result runs against the reading the interpretation needs. The article does hedge —
"not a wholesale inability to localise" — but the hedge sits beside an unretracted "Models register
the *strength* of an internal disturbance more readily than they identify its *origin*," and the
cluster-fit paragraph still treats the dissociation as the silicon instance of the void.

**This is repairable without weakening the article, and probably strengthens it.** The v1 result
that the article does not currently mention is *supportive*: an 8B open model reproduced the
Anthropic 20% figure "exactly matching their reported numbers and thus showing that introspection is
not exclusive to very large or capable models." An article arguing that a self-monitoring signature
is architecture-general rather than implementation-specific has, sitting unused in its own cited
source, a same-profile replication across a 100×-ish parameter gap and a different model family.
That is the single best piece of evidence for its own thesis and it is not in the article.

### 2. `training-contamination-confound` leans on a claim its source softened in revision

The quoted string "behavioral evidence alone is inherently insufficient to establish strong
introspective claims" is **genuine** — I found it verbatim in arXiv:2605.26242**v1**, and a
suspicion that it had been strengthened in paraphrase was wrong. But the **current v2 abstract
replaces that in-principle claim with a constructive one**: Singh, Linzen & Ravfogel now "identify
two conditions that a paradigm needs to meet in order to establish introspection" — privileged
access and second-order computation, the latter requiring "designs under which second-order and
first-order accounts make divergent predictions" — and conclude only that "current evidence is
insufficient to establish metacognitive monitoring in LLMs."

The article uses the v1 quote to support: "the 2026 introspection-evaluation literature has
converged on the Map's structural conclusion that the behavioural channel cannot, on its own,
establish introspection." Under v2, Singh et al. are not claiming the behavioural channel *cannot*
succeed; they are specifying what a behavioural paradigm would have to look like *in order to*
succeed. The convergence claim needs softening — but the article's own §"What Could Discriminate
Mechanism from Imitation" gets *stronger*, because v2's two conditions are a published, external
specification of exactly the discriminator that section is groping toward. Trade one sentence of
convergence rhetoric for a much better citation.

### Medium Priority

#### The wing has no article on the interpretability channel as such

`training-contamination-confound` argues that the discriminating evidence "must come from **outside**
[the behavioural channel] — from the system's internals rather than its outputs," and names
mechanistic interpretability, out-of-distribution generalization and contamination-controlled
training as the three candidates. `cross-architecture` independently arrives at the same place. Both
treat interpretability as a *channel* with its own epistemics — model-dependence of the injected
vector, the circularity of validating ground truth with the tool being scored — and neither has room
to develop it. If any new article in this wing is warranted, it is that one; but it should wait until
the two calibration concerns above are discharged, since it would inherit their framing.
