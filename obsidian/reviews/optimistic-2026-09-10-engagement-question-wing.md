## Executive Summary

This wing is the Map asking its own hardest question — whether a subject exists in a given system — and
the striking result is that it has **three separate machineries for answering it that have never been
introduced to each other**. `coupling-engagement-condition` isolates the question as *engagement* and
gives it a taxonomy. `universal-coupling-response` answers it as *architectural adequacy*.
`synthetic-minimal-agents` faces the question at the built edge and answers it with neither, reaching
for the Tenet-2/3 minimality allocation rule instead. All three are individually excellent and
individually calibrated; the gap between them is the finding.

That gap is measurable rather than impressionistic. **Eighteen live articles mention an engineered case**
(xenobot, anthrobot, organoid, biobot, syn3.0); **fourteen mention the adequacy-or-engagement machinery**
("architectural adequacy", "universal coupling", "coupling-engagement", "adequate architecture"). The
**intersection is zero** — including the apex that synthesises the whole engineered wing,
`apex/competency-without-felt-experience`, which contains no hit for any of the machinery terms. The
Map's scope claim has never been asked of a built system, and its built-system article has never been
run through the scope claim.

On calibration, both personas designed to collide here converge in praise on all three articles. Nothing
in this wing converts tenet-coherence into evidential standing, and two of the three decline the upgrade
in writing at the exact site where it was available.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

`synthetic-minimal-agents` supplies the cleanest statement of the explanatory gap the corpus has produced
for engineered systems, and it earns it by structure rather than assertion: "If consciousness were
constituted by functional organisation, a complete build specification would be a partial answer to the
phenomenal question — the designer would know, from the blueprint, something about whether anyone was
home." That is Chalmers' structure-and-function argument run as an *experiment* rather than a thought
experiment, and the article then does something Chalmers would insist on and most treatments omit: it
marks the limit of its own reach. The argument "bites against accounts that read experience off what a
system can do" and "does not reach Chalmers' principle of organizational invariance, which is indexed to
fine grain". Very few articles anywhere name the one functionalism their argument cannot touch, and name
it as the strongest one.

`coupling-engagement-condition` is Chalmers-adjacent in a different register. Its four-way separation —
engagement, mode, efficacy, scope — is conceptual analysis of the kind Chalmers does for its own sake,
and the payoff is immediate: it dissolves a conflation the corpus was carrying. Engagement and the
interface threshold are both binary, which is why they blur; the article shows the threshold's switch is
on "selection-grade interaction" and that "a system on the wrong side of it still has a subject. Only
engagement's switch decides whether there is anyone home."

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The countable-substrate reading is the most interesting thing in the wing for this persona, and its
virtue is that the article argues *against* the reading it would most like to be true. Tenet 2 puts the
coupling on quantum indeterminacies; whether a system offers any such indeterminacy is a count, and
zero-versus-non-zero is sharp in a way "enough integration" is not. That would hand the Map a physical
ground for a sharp metaphysical fact — exactly the sort of result Stapp looks for. The article then
prices it honestly, on two counts, and the second is the sharper: "a single exploitable indeterminacy is
not obviously rarer in a thermostat than in a nematode, and without an integration requirement the
reading drifts toward the panpsychism the universal coupling response takes trouble to avoid."

`universal-coupling-response` supplies the complementary Stappian move and is the article's best moment:
it derives universality *from* the mechanism rather than asserting it. "If coupling occurs wherever
adequate neural architecture exists ... the mechanism must operate at a level common to all neural
tissue," and quantum indeterminacies are what all neural tissue has. Stapp would note that this makes
the scope claim a prediction of the physics rather than a philosophical preference.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`synthetic-minimal-agents` keeps the what-it's-like question in the position Nagel demands — untouched by
everything else the article establishes. The list of installed competencies is deliberately maximal
(morphogenesis, locomotion, navigation, self-repair, kinematic self-replication, autonomous metabolism,
self-modelling) and the verdict is that "[e]very item on that list is cognitive-grade vocabulary; none of
it was assembled from anything that looks like a phenomenal ingredient." The Nagelian point is made by
exhibiting the gap at its widest rather than by arguing for it.

The marker-method passage is where this persona finds the wing's best single sentence: "A crab's
nociceptive responses inherit credibility from shared ancestry with the calibration case; an anthrobot's
wound-closing inherits none, however similar the behaviour looks." That is Nagel's insistence that the
first-person fact is not read off third-person similarity, converted into a working criterion that
explains *why* the Map accepts markers in one case and refuses them in another.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

This wing gives the persona a great deal and never lets it cash out as a tier-upgrade — which is the
axis-discipline the skill requires.

`universal-coupling-response` is the corpus's most process-congenial page: it predicts that "as empirical
investigation extends downward through the complexity spectrum, evidence of consciousness will follow",
it refuses a threshold drawn among adequate systems, and it treats the erosion of every proposed boundary
(Barron & Klein, Chittka, the New York Declaration) as data rather than as embarrassment. Continuity is
embraced. And yet the page holds the line that separates it from panpsychism: "Universal coupling
requires organisation ... The coupling mechanism needs something to couple *with* ... and unorganised
matter does not provide it." Nothing is elevated; a scope claim is made and its cost is stated.

`synthetic-minimal-agents` is more remarkable still, because the process-friendly rival is given the
floor at full strength and by name. Rouleau and Levin's multiple-realizability argument is stated "in its
own voice before being answered", and the article insists on two things most treatments would suppress:
that Levin co-authors it, so this is not an outsider's position; and that "the argument is not naive: it
grants that sentience is unmeasured and possibly unmeasurable". Then it concedes the half that is true —
"Multiple realizability of *function* is granted without argument" — and denies only the phenomenal half.
This is the Whiteheadian intuition honoured to its exact limit.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Peripheral to this wing and correctly kept so. The one place it bears is `coupling-engagement-condition`'s
insistence that engagement is prior to everything the agency literature cares about: `coupling-modes`
(basis, timing, probability control) all "presuppose that the coupling already holds". Kane would approve
the ordering — there is no question of an agent's ultimate sourcehood until there is an agent — and would
approve more that the article does not smuggle agency into the engagement fact to make it feel less
primitive.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

`coupling-engagement-condition` is a McGinn exhibit of the first order, and the best evidence is its
closing section. "What This Article Does Not Settle" is a four-item ledger of owed debts, and each item
is a real open question rather than a ritual hedge — whether engagement is genuinely non-graded "as
against posited so for framework reasons"; whether a graded trigger is compatible with a sharp ground;
whether the adequacy boundary is conceptual or worldly; whether the countable-substrate reading can
supply an integration requirement without reintroducing the threshold it was meant to avoid.

The trigger problem is where the humility does real work. The article states the objection against itself
in the sorites article's own words, lays out both exits, and then declines both: the second exit "depends
on a distinction it has not yet worked out — between what *grounds* the on/off fact (the coupling
relation) and what *triggers* it (the physical variable) ... The Map should record it as owed." An
article that installs a construct two other articles already lean on, and whose main deliverable is a
precise statement of what the construct does not yet do, is doing the thing McGinn thinks philosophy
mostly ought to be doing.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This persona's verdict is unqualified praise across all three, and the reasons are structural rather
than lexical.**

`coupling-engagement-condition` puts its calibration in the **lead**, not the tail. The second paragraph
of the article — before any argument — states that "the Map does not claim to have *shown* that
engagement is non-graded; it posits a non-graded relation because [Antony's conditional] blocks the
graded alternatives, which is a framework-relative move rather than a demonstration." Truncation
resilience and evidential discipline are usually in tension; here front-loading the epistemic status is
what makes the article safe to read in fragments.

The article then does the rarer thing: it audits its own constructive move for how little it establishes.
"It says the fact has a non-graded ground. It does not say what the relation consists in, and it does not
say under what conditions it obtains." And its Tenet-4 section converts that into a standing obligation
on *other* pages: "This makes the tenet's defence conditional on a posit the Map has argued for but not
secured, and the conditional should be stated wherever the defence is used." A calibration claim with a
propagation instruction attached is a materially stronger artefact than a calibration claim alone.

`synthetic-minimal-agents` declines a tenet-driven upgrade at the site where it was most available, and
declines it twice over.

- The **negative** direction first, which is the direction the Map is structurally tempted by: the
  article names the inference *we assembled the parts, no step introduced an experiencer, therefore no
  experiencer is present*, and kills it with the best datum in the article — JCVI-syn3.0's 149 genes of
  unknown function. "Engineered agency does not entail engineer's transparency." A builder who cannot
  account for a third of what he built cannot certify absence. This is precisely the
  *over-concession-in-reverse* that a dualist article would ordinarily bank without noticing.
- Then the boundary is drawn explicitly: "Withholding attribution on those grounds is not the same as
  establishing absence, and the Map claims only the first."
- And in the Tenets 2/3 paragraph, the withholding is labelled as to its source: "Withholding an
  interface here follows from the allocation rule rather than from any measurement; the Map marks it as
  framework-relative rather than as a finding, and it settles nothing about the bare phenomenal question."

That last sentence is the *"tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating"* pattern executed in the article's own
vocabulary rather than recited, and executed on a **downgrade** rather than an upgrade — which is the
harder and less-practised half of the discipline.

`universal-coupling-response` earns this persona's praise for a falsification condition that is genuinely
falsifying and is stated twice. "A threshold view predicts a zone where adequate-looking architectures
produce no evidence of consciousness ... Universal coupling predicts no such zone. Wherever the functional
signatures of adequate architecture appear, consciousness should be present. If future research
identifies organisms with all the architectural hallmarks but no indicators of consciousness, that would
challenge universal coupling specifically." The article also concedes that its adequacy criteria "are
provisional" and that it "does not require a complete specification of adequacy — only that adequacy is
the right kind of condition to ask about", which is the honest framework-stage calibration this persona
looks for.

**Process Philosopher and Hardline Empiricist converge on all three articles.** Per the skill's own
signal, that means this wing has resolved the tension rather than papered it.

  concedes that *C. elegans* "may fall below the architectural-adequacy condition", so adequacy is a

  rhetorical.

  adequacy**; `coupling-engagement-condition` by **engagement**. These need not conflict, but nobody has
  checked, and the countable-substrate reading pulls hard the other way — if engagement turns on whether
  a system offers *any* exploitable quantum indeterminacy, a ciliated epithelial spheroid of living human
  cells is not obviously on the wrong side of that line.
- **Estimated scope**: Medium (topics, ~2,600-3,000w).
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenets 1, 2 and 5 directly. Tenet 5 is the governing one — the article's job is to

### Medium Priority

`coupling-engagement-condition` records this as **owed** in as many words: whether a graded physical

#### Is the adequacy boundary conceptual or worldly?

The wing's live seam, and the one question whose resolution would settle the most. `coupling-engagement-condition`
sets out both readings with real care and deliberately declines to choose — "Which the Map means has not
been stated anywhere." Because the choice is a genuine metaphysical commitment with downstream effects on
the Tenet-4 defence, it is a **human decision** rather than an automation task, and it should be flagged
as such rather than settled by a refine pass. Recorded here; no task minted.
