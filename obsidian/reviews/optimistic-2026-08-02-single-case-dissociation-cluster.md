## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The cluster's best single move is `interpreter-module`'s **arrives-too-late** argument (L59). The interpreter narrates contents that are, by hypothesis, *already conscious*: the pointing, the seeing of the shovel, and the felt puzzlement must be present as experiences before there is anything to confabulate about. "A mechanism that operates on conscious contents cannot, by that same operation, account for their being conscious in the first place."

This is the explanatory gap deployed as a *scope limit on a rival's evidence* rather than as a standalone intuition pump, and that is the harder and more useful form. Chalmers would recognise it immediately as the structure of his own objection to functional explanations — the machinery explains the reporting, the discrimination, the integration, and leaves the experiencing untouched — but here it is aimed at a specific empirical literature (Gazzaniga/LeDoux, Johansson choice-blindness, Nisbett & Wilson) rather than at physicalism in the abstract. The payoff is a clean, checkable distinction: the confabulation data establish that *narrated authorship* is unreliable, and they do not reach *phenomenal unity of the present field*, because the second is the precondition of the first.

What earns the praise is that the article then refuses the obvious next step. L63: "The Map does not claim the interpreter data prove that phenomenal unity is fundamental." The gap is used to block an inference, not to license one.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Little direct quantum engagement here, and that restraint is itself right — none of these four phenomena has a quantum story that is currently earnable, and none of them reaches for one. What Stapp would value is the cluster's insistence on treating **volition as a hand-off with a mechanism**, not as an unanalysed primitive. `anarchic-hand` L68 frames the fracture as a loss of "write-access by which conscious volition reaches one motor pathway," which is exactly the interventionist grammar a quantum-interface account eventually has to cash out. The article names the efference-copy chain as the mechanism and then asks "what the mechanism is a mechanism *of*" (L64) — the question is posed at the right level, and left open rather than answered with physics the evidence cannot support.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`capgras` is the cluster's phenomenological triumph, and the reason is that it keeps the *felt* datum in the foreground while the mechanism is being described. L38: the patient "does not experience 'my familiarity module is offline' — that machinery is itself invisible to introspection... They experience a person who looks exactly right and feels categorically wrong."

That sentence does what Nagel asks: it refuses to substitute the third-person description for the first-person fact, and it explains *why* the substitution is tempting (the machinery is not introspectible — the point `inventory-blindness` develops). The article then goes further and gives the felt datum an objective trace: the Ellis, Young, Quayle & De Pauw (1997) skin-conductance study with the **medication-matched psychiatric control group**. Naming why that control matters (L48 — it rules out antipsychotic side-effects as the explanation of the absent response) is exactly the kind of methodological care that makes a first-person claim defensible to a third-person audience.

The modality-specificity finding is handled with the same discipline. DS calls his parents impostors on sight but not on the telephone (L46) — "a global belief disorder about who his parents *are* would not switch off over the phone." The article correctly reads this as what converts the disconnection story "from a story fitted after the fact" into a finding.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

The cluster consistently avoids crude substance dualism, and the mechanism is `interface-heterogeneity`: distinct channels fail independently, so what breaks is never *the mind* but always *a hand-off*. `anarchic-hand` L72 states it plainly — "ownership can survive the loss of agency, and the unified witness can survive the loss of a limb's obedience." That is a processual picture, not a ghost-in-a-machine picture: the self is not a thing sitting behind the brain issuing orders, it is what persists while particular relational channels degrade.

`hemispherectomy` L59 gives the same shape at the largest scale: "losing half the interface degrades bandwidth and specific channels... but does not fragment the self." Whitehead-friendly, and — per this skill's constraint — deliberately **not** cashed out as an evidential upgrade. See the Hardline Empiricist below; the two personas converge here rather than conflicting.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

`anarchic-hand` is the cluster's contribution to agency, and its most useful result for a libertarian is negative: **felt authorship is a constructed signal, not a raw datum**. L54: "felt authorship is not a raw datum read directly off the fact that *my* body moved. It is a constructed signal — something the system computes and can fail to compute."

Kane should welcome this rather than fear it. A libertarian who rests on the *phenomenology* of free choice has staked the position on a signal that anarchic hand shows can be withheld; a libertarian who rests on the *metaphysics* of ultimate sourcehood is untouched. The article draws exactly that line — the double dissociation impugns the felt signature of willing without impugning the willer — and it does so while conceding the comparator model is "fully adequate to the *neural* facts" (L64).

The closing paragraph (L92) is a model of what a defensible agency claim looks like: "felt authorship is dissociable from ownership and from execution, that the unified experiencer survives such fractures, and that this pattern is what an interface view predicts — while remaining honestly compatible with the comparator rival."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This persona's verdict is load-bearing here**, because every article in the cluster is doing the thing that most invites tier-slippage: reading a *single clinical population* as architectural evidence about consciousness. Three of four pass cleanly, one has a defect that is structural rather than phrasal.

**What is praise-worthily *not* done.** `anarchic-hand` had the easiest available upgrade in the cluster and declined it explicitly. A hand performing competent goal-directed acts with no felt authorship is a ready-made "competence without experience" exhibit, and the article refuses to bank it (L74): "that a hand can execute competent, goal-directed acts with no felt authorship does not license a bald claim that 'action happens without any experience.'... the framework-relative reading is that a specific channel of felt control is absent, not that competent behaviour proves the general dispensability of experience." That is the `expand-topic-lead-overclaims-phenomenal-absence` hazard caught *in the article itself*, and the substituted claim is named as "narrower and more defensible."

`capgras` does something rarer: it partitions its own explanation and disclaims half of it. L58: "**Only Factor 1 is the Map's territory.**" The two-factor model's Factor 2 (the right-dorsolateral belief-evaluation deficit) is described in full and then explicitly excluded — "named here as the completion of the clinical explanation, not as evidence for interface architecture." Then, having narrowed the claim, the article devotes a whole section to Corlett (2019) attacking *the surviving half*: that the absent skin-conductance may reflect a broad salience deficit rather than a dedicated familiarity channel, and that the ventromedial cases supporting a discrete Factor 1 also carry dorsolateral damage. L66: "The Map engages Corlett as a genuine live challenge, not a footnote to be dismissed." Steelmanning the rival to your own load-bearing premise, in the section immediately after establishing it, is the strongest calibration behaviour in the cluster.

`interpreter-module` handles the Pinto (2017) evidence with matching care. Pinto is cited as *weakening a premise the illusionist relies on* — that the brain obviously contains multiple selves — and the article says so in the same breath (L73): "it weakens a premise the rival relies on, rather than proving the rival false."

**Where sample size is stated, it is stated.** Kliemann 2019 is introduced as "six adults" (L49); Fisher 2022 as "an adult-onset case" (L51); Vining as 58 children, Pulsifer as 71, Curtiss as 43, Ellis 1997 as five patients against two five-person control groups. No n is hidden behind a bare author-year.

**The one calibration concern.** `hemispherectomy` L51 does lean a philosophical conclusion on n=1: Fisher's single adult-onset case is used to "weaken the deflation that 'unity only survives because a child's brain is so plastic'," and the following sentence generalises — "Consciousness stayed unified even when the loss came late, when plasticity is weaker." The n is visible in the preceding clause, but the inference sentence does not carry it. This is minor relative to the structural finding below and is best fixed in the same pass.

### 1. The "cannot even be posed" premise is undercut by the article's own definition (HIGH)

`hemispherectomy` L27 grounds the article's distinctive contribution on substrate *absence*:

> "In hemispherectomy there is no second hemisphere — the question 'one mind or two?' cannot even be posed. Only one candidate substrate remains, and unity is not the survival of one stream over a rival, because there is no rival."

Six lines later, §"What Hemispherectomy Is" (L33) defines the term to cover both subtypes:

> "Hemispherectomy is the surgical removal (anatomical hemispherectomy) or **functional disconnection (functional hemispherectomy / hemispherotomy)** of one cerebral hemisphere."

In a functional hemispherectomy the hemisphere is disconnected but remains in the skull, alive and perfused. The "one mind or two?" question is therefore *exactly* posable — arguably more sharply than in callosotomy, since the disconnection is more complete. And the article's own evidence base is not restricted to the anatomical subtype: **Fisher et al. (2022) is titled "resting-state brain-networks after near-complete hemispherectomy in adulthood"** and the case is a *functional* hemispherectomy following malignant MCA infarction (confirmed in the source research note L88). That case is load-bearing — it is the article's only defence against the "unity survives only because children's brains are plastic" deflation.

There is a named live literature posing precisely this question, and the Map does not cite it anywhere: **Bayne, Seth & Massimini (2020), "Are There Islands of Awareness?", *Trends in Neurosciences* 43(1)** — verified metadata, recorded in `outer-review-synthesis-2026-07-18.md` L104. A 2026-07-18 Gemini outer review raised the isolated-hemisphere challenge against the Map but supported it with a likely-fabricated cite (Gauvry & Rüber, self-contradictory double title), and the synthesis correctly recorded a do-not-chase caution — with the result that **the underlying challenge was flagged, dismissed on citation grounds, and never engaged**.
