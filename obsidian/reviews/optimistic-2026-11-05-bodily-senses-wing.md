## Executive Summary

TAC, THE, VES and INT were written in June 2026 as one series, and they share a calibration template that holds up page after page. Each engages its physicalist rivals as rivals. Each tenet section says the dependence runs from the framework to the data. Each names one register in which its sense presses the explanatory gap: valence arriving with contact (touch), value indexed to need (temperature), a point of view with almost no quale (balance), and owned affect (interoception). DDP was written in March, before the template existed. Its body is well hedged, but its Relation to Site Perspective still says Bidirectional Interaction "finds strong evidence" in mental practice. The Hardline Empiricist flags that as a calibration concern, and it becomes the first task below. The other openings are mostly exports. ## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would single out the thermal grill section. It grants a complete physical model with a confirmed quantitative prediction, then locates the residue exactly where it remains: "the model says which central pattern is produced, not why its production is *like* burning for anyone." [1] (THE L59). TAC holds the same line in evaluative terms: "That is an illustration of the gap, not a demonstration that it cannot close." [1] (TAC L62). In both places the hard problem is posed after the mechanism has been fully granted, which is the strongest position from which to pose it.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp's picture is one where the mind acts by choosing among options the brain has already generated, without overriding them. THE states that relation directly: the predictive framework fits the interface reading because "on the Map's picture conscious participation operates through, not against, the brain's predictive machinery." [1] (THE L83). Stapp would also respect the series' refusal to claim quantum support from sensory data: "No tactile datum secures the quantum-interface mechanism; the dependence runs the other way, from the framework to the reading of the data." [1] (TAC L86). DDP's Minimal Quantum Interaction hook rests on "the distribution of microtubules throughout the nervous system" [1] (DDP L107). Stapp would find that beside the point, since his model does not depend on microtubules. The page hedges the hook itself ("This is speculative"), and six deep reviews have accepted it, so this review leaves it alone.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

VES is the page Nagel would quote. Balance strips away the quale, and what is left is the point of view on its own: the question of "why there is a someone for whom there is an up and a heading." [1] (VES L86). Most of the Map's explanatory-gap arguments ask why a state feels like *this*. VES asks the prior question, why there is a perspective at all. It reaches that question through a sense with almost no phenomenology of its own: "a sense that is almost all frame and almost no object" [1] (VES L100).

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead treats feeling as primary and valuation as present in every occasion. That makes him the series' natural admirer. INT describes interoception's mind side as "the felt ground of selfhood rather than content presented to a subject." [1] (INT L37). THE closes on "a modality whose felt content is value relative to need." [1] (THE L99). TAC's claim that pleasantness arrives with the contact, not as a verdict laid on afterwards, is close to the doctrine of subjective form.

**Constraint applied.** None of this praise licenses a move up the five-tier scale, and none of the pages makes one. State-indexed valence is the series feature most likely to invite an "experience all the way down" extension to simple thermoregulating organisms. THE never makes that move: `grep -c -iE "nematode|bacteri|thermotaxis|C\. elegans|chemotaxis|plant|insect|fish"` returns 0. This is process-friendly and evidence-neutral, which is the right combination.

He would praise DDP's tenet section even more: "**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Bidirectional Interaction]]** finds strong evidence in spatial imagination as mental causation." ## Content Strengths

### `topics/tactile-consciousness-and-the-interface`
- **Strongest point**: it divides each datum into the part mechanism explains and the residue, then sends the residue to the page that owns it.
- **Notable quote**: "The rubber-hand illusion relocates the *boundary* of the felt body; it does not explain, and does not dissolve, the ownership the relocated boundary is felt within." [1] (L72)
- **Why it works**: a reader cannot leave thinking the rubber-hand effect is evidence for an owner, and cannot leave thinking it dissolves one.
- **Under-used**: L58 and L64 between them hold both halves of a mirror pair, which the page presents as two separate points. Pain asymbolia (L64) keeps sensory discrimination while the hurt goes missing. The C-tactile case (L58, "The tell is a dissociation" [1]) runs the other way. Olausson et al. 2002, checked this session at PubMed (PMID 12145636, doi 10.1038/nn896), report: "In a unique patient lacking large myelinated afferents, we found that activation of C tactile (CT) afferents produced a faint sensation of pleasant touch", with insular activation "but not of somatosensory areas S1 and S2". One case shows discrimination without hurt; the other shows a faint pleasant tone with almost no discrimination. The pair spans the contact senses (pain and touch), not a single channel, and the touch side rests on one patient. `concepts/pain-asymbolia` has a "Complementary Dissociations" section (L103) whose only listed inverse, phantom pain, is in its own words a dissociation "between felt location and actual tissue state, not between suffering and all physical causation." [1] That section never mentions TAC (0 hits). See task 4.

## A Seam Between Siblings (VES and INT)

Both pages agree that the evidence does not settle whether the body (or the spatial frame) is had by consciousness or partly constitutive of it. They put the Map's own stance in different registers:

- VES L88: "This parallels the question the interoceptive article marks between a body that is merely *had* by consciousness and a body partly *constitutive* of it." [1] Then: "The Map holds this open at the framework boundary" [1].
- INT L93: "This reading conflicts with the Map's commitment that the body is *had*, not constitutive" [1]. `voids/interoceptive-void` L91 takes the same direction: "because the body is *had* rather than constitutive" [1].

INT records a commitment that the evidence does not decide. VES says the Map holds the question open, and describes INT as marking a question, where INT actually marks a disagreement with a rival. VES's "frame" also has two possible readings. It could mean the vestibular reference, which L80 calls "nearly all brain-side labour" [1]. Or it could mean the subject's "spatial *situatedness*", which L80 names as the mind-side contribution. On the first reading the Damasio-type rival applies, and INT's commitment should carry over. On the second, a frame that partly constitutes the subject is the Map's own picture, and "parallels" misdescribes the relation. Neither page is wrong on the evidence. The opening is to make the two modalities state one stance. See task 2.
