### Critical Issues Found

- **Chomsky quotation spliced, condition dropped (rows 4, 5)**: the quoted sentence does not occur; "in terms of determinacy and randomness" was written into the marks from the preceding sentence, the phrase Chomsky was himself quoting from Descartes was credited to him, and his next clause ("which would not preclude an intelligible theory of such actions") was replaced by "human cognitive architecture may be incapable of grasping certain kinds of truth". The following sentence put Moro's book title in Chomsky's mouth. Resolution: paragraph rewritten around three verbatim fragments (each count 1 at chomsky.info), with the Descartes origin and the "would not preclude" condition restored; the "Not because ... but because" gloss and the Babel sentence cut.
- **Grandin term in quotation marks she does not use (row 6)**: replaced by her words, "three-dimensional visual simulations", with "running" and "in her imagination" outside the marks.
- **Marion attribution unverifiable (row 8)**: no review had sourced it and the research note never mentions Marion. Replaced by the paradox's verifiable classical statement, Augustine, *De Doctrina Christiana* I.6 (New Advent, NPNF translation: "if the unspeakable is what cannot be spoken of, it is not unspeakable if it can be called unspeakable", "unspeakable" count 6), paraphrased without marks. Whether Marion says something equivalent in *God Without Being* was not established either way.
- **Unsourced within-subject claim (2026-09-24 pessimistic Issue 4, never discharged)**: "Japanese speakers categorise objects differently when thinking in Japanese than in English" describes a language-switching result. The known Japanese/English object-classification study (Imai & Gentner 1997, *Cognition*) compares speakers of two languages. Replaced by the study that does report a language-of-operation effect: Athanasopoulos et al. 2015 (Europe PMC abstract: fluent German-English bilinguals "categorize motion events according to the grammatical constraints of the language in which they operate"; effects are "context-bound and transient", count 1). "Substantial empirical support" regraded to "empirical support of a bounded kind"; the lead's "bilinguals think differently" narrowed to "categorise events differently". Reference 16 added (Crossref 10.1177/0956797614567509).
- **Link label did not describe its target**: `[[transit-void|dissolution-on-articulation phenomenon]]` pointed at a page with no treatment of dissolution on articulation (one unrelated "articulates" hit), and this page's own Phenomenology section distinguishes the two. The label was inherited from the 2026-07-06 archival repoint. De-linked; the phrase now refers to this page's own section. The other two transit-void links describe that page correctly and stay.
