## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Henriksen & Parnas 2019 quote attributed to the wrong case** (L72). The article had them "answering Billon's 2013 thought-insertion argument, conclude that the minimal self in such cases is 'affected or impaired but not completely lost'". In the raw PDF (Thaumàzein 7, download/99/90) that phrase is their gloss on Zahn, Talazko and Ebert 2008's patient D.P. ("the authors concluded that the sense of ownership or minimal self was affected or impaired but not completely lost in this patient") — Lane's counter-example, not Billon's. Their own thought-insertion conclusion is "some sense of ownership or for-me-ness is retained even in these psychopathological experiences. If this was not the case ... the experience of thought insertion could simply not articulate itself." Replaced with the grep-verifiable span "ownership or for-me-ness is retained even in these psychopathological experiences". Resolution: fixed.
- **"Mark of mere appearance" cited as Billon's own qualifier without source** (L60). The phrase is absent from the Billon 2016 preprint (0 hits, hyphenation-normalised) and lives in Billon 2023 fn. 5, where Billon reports that the "as if" "is usually interpreted as a 'mark of mere appearance'" and allows an approximation reading (Radovic & Radovic 2002) as an exception. Now "on the usual interpretation Billon adopts, a 'mark of mere appearance' (Billon 2023)". Resolution: fixed.
- **Strength upgrade on Billon 2023's nature claim** (lede L38, L62). Billon's text: "This challenge *suggests* that such reductive theories must fail and that mineness can only be explained in purely neurophysiological ... terms"; the chapter's close says mineness "might be psychologically irreducible". The article had him "conclude" this (lede) and called it "his verdict ... reductive", then said he "rejects the fourth" level (metaphysical irreducibility). Changed to "suggests" (lede), "his verdict is neurophysiological ... the chapter suggests that", and "points away from the fourth". Resolution: fixed. (Documented textual claim; register unchanged.)
