## Executive Summary

**Four of these six articles are disconfirming instances.** Jain is the only one that
enters the convergence argument as a supporting data point; Kyoto and Nahua are
self-declared *basin edges* (traditions that inquire deeply and do not reach the Map's
conclusion), Yogācāra files itself as a *rival*, Kabbalah demolishes its own headline
echo mid-paragraph, and the Neoplatonist article exists solely to **discount** the Map's
own convergence. A wing whose dominant output is evidence against the house position is
the strongest thing in this review, and it is not an accident of selection — it is what
these six articles were commissioned to be.

The one finding that runs the other way is a real one and it sits *upstream* of the wing.
`cross-traditional-convergence-on-consciousness-irreducibility` **L116** tallies
`| Kyoto School | Yes | …` in its `Irreducible?` column, with no qualifier, while the
Kyoto article's own `Relation to Site Perspective` says the school "arrives *not* at an
irreducible mind set against matter but at a non-dual field prior to both" and the same
hub's Further Reading (L171) calls it "a second basin edge". The hub counts Kyoto on both
sides of its own ledger, and the side that gets the bare "Yes" is the side that helps the
Map. This is an **unlisted locus of the Family A propagation defect already diagnosed in
the standing P3** — a defect the wing's own hub article is the authority against.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The wing's best Chalmers material is `jain-philosophy-of-mind`, and specifically its
refusal to soften a substance claim into a property claim. Jainism is presented as
holding that consciousness "is not an emergent add-on that matter acquires at sufficient
complexity but the very definition of the substance" — and then the article draws the
consequence a property dualist most wants drawn: "matter can never manufacture
consciousness, because consciousness belongs to a different substance-category
altogether." That is the explanatory gap stated as an ontological fact about categories
rather than as a puzzle about our concepts, which is the stronger of the two readings and
the one Chalmers's zombie argument aims at.

`yogacara-alaya-vijnana-storehouse-consciousness` earns a different kind of credit: it
identifies a position the hard-problem literature has no standard slot for. The storehouse
is "a substrate whose contents persist while the substrate stays in flux: patterns endure
though nothing permanent underlies them," which the article correctly notes "is a
genuinely different move from the Western options of either a persisting soul or a purely
reductive bundle." Chalmers's own taxonomy runs soul / bundle / functionalist; a
*conditioned continuum* is a fourth cell, and the article finds it without inflating it.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp's interest is where mind gets purchase on physics, and the wing's honest answer is
*nowhere yet* — which every relevant article says out loud rather than gesturing at a
resemblance. `jain-philosophy-of-mind` on karmic matter: "It is a fully articulated
pre-modern model of mind-matter interaction. It should be read as exactly that — a
historical interaction model developed within a specific cosmology — and not as
anticipating any modern mechanism." And in the tenet section: karma-as-matter "carries no
mechanistic content for the Map's quantum-interaction model and must not be presented as
anticipating one."

`kabbalah-tzimtzum-consciousness-matter` does the same job on the Tenet 2 side and does it
harder, because the tzimtzum/minimal-interaction rhyme is genuinely seductive. The article
inverts its own analogy four ways in a single paragraph: the *quantity* inverts
(tzimtzum subtracts, Tenet 2 adds), the *modality* inverts (tzimtzum's withdrawal is
"ontologically *productive*", the Map's is "operationally *permissive*"), the *sequence*
inverts (tzimtzum withdraws then draws the *kav* in; the Map structures then withdraws),
and the *duration* fails (a single cosmogonic event against a standing arrangement). A
Stapp-adjacent reader looking for a mechanism gets told there isn't one, four times, by
the article that would most benefit from saying there is.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`japanese-philosophy-of-mind-kyoto-school` poses the Nagelian question about its own
subject matter and then declines to answer it, which is the right call. The
**dissolve-or-dodge** spine is set up in the second paragraph and left standing: on the
dodge reading, "asserting an undifferentiated ground does not explain *why there is
something it is like* to undergo anything; it renames the mystery 'absolute nothingness'
and declares it fundamental. The question was not answered but changed."

That is Nagel's objection to every deflationary move, put in the mouth of a tradition the
Map is otherwise disposed to admire — and the article gives the *dissolve* reading equal
standing on the facing page. Nagel's "what is it like" is used here as a **test the Kyoto
School might fail**, not as a slogan of agreement.

`mesoamerican-nahua-philosophy-of-mind` supplies the complementary Nagelian point about
frameworks: "Awareness and stone are teotl in different guises, and the question of how
one produces the other has nowhere to lodge." The Western hard problem "needs two relata
held apart"; Nahua metaphysics supplies no such pair. That is a clean statement of the
framework-dependence worry, made by the Map about itself.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead gets more from this wing than from almost anywhere in the corpus, and — per
this skill's constraint — **none of it is cashed as a tier-upgrade.** Nahua teotl is a
process metaphysics in the fullest sense: "more like a thunderstorm or a flowing river
than a static, perduring thing… A thing *is* what it *does*," with *olin*, *malinalli*
and *nepantla* as the master motion-patterns and *agonistic inamic unity* as the
generative tension. Yogācāra's storehouse is process to the bone — "not a warehouse where
a static inventory sits. It is a stream." Kyoto's *basho* replaces substance-and-property
analysis with nested *topoi*. Kabbalah's sefirot are a descending emanation.

Four thoroughly process-friendly traditions, and the Map's verdict on each is that it is a
**rival**. Nahua: "Teotl-monism is a rival to Tenet 1 (Dualism), not an ally… on its own
terms Nahua metaphysics dissolves the very mind-matter distinction the tenet asserts. The
Map does not soften this." Kabbalah: "One can borrow the shape without borrowing the
substance — and must say so." Yogācāra: "on its own face it is closer to monist idealism
than to dualism, and the Map must not conscript it as evidence for its own position."

This is the axis-discipline the skill demands, held four times over. Process-compatibility
is acknowledged as real and interesting; it moves nothing up the evidential-status scale;
and in three of four cases the process-friendly tradition is scored as *contradicting* a
tenet rather than resonating with it.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Thin ground in this wing, and the articles do not pretend otherwise — which is itself
worth crediting, since agency-talk is easy to bolt onto any tradition that mentions
volition. The one substantive hook is Jain: unlike Sāṃkhya's passive witness, "The Jaina
*jiva* is no passive spectator: it is an active knower and agent, genuinely embroiled in
matter, whose bondage and liberation are its own doing." Sourcehood is located in the soul
itself, and the article contrasts it against Sāṃkhya precisely on the sourcehood axis
("Where Sāṃkhya keeps consciousness pristine by making it inert, Jainism keeps the soul
active by making its entanglement with matter the problem to be solved").

Yogācāra's seventh consciousness supplies an interesting negative for Kane: the tradition
"does not merely deny the self; it supplies a *mechanism* for the illusion, and locates
that mechanism in a different layer from the one that does the actual
continuity-carrying." A libertarian needs a real source; Yogācāra offers real continuity
with a fabricated owner. The article names that as pressure on the Map's register entry
P-I1 rather than as a curiosity.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The wing's epistemic humility is structural rather than decorative. Three separate
articles refuse to adjudicate a live scholarly dispute on which their own argument
depends, and each names the dispute by its disputants:

- **Kyoto** — dissolve vs dodge, "Both readings are available and neither is forced."
- **Nahua** — León-Portilla's evanescent/idealist reading against Maffie's "robustly
  immanent monism," with the Map taking "teotl-monism as the influential contemporary
  reconstruction, not as a settled fact about pre-Columbian belief."
- **Kabbalah** — Scholem's "exile of God into Himself" against Idel's textual restraint,
  with the explicit instruction that "any Map claim must inherit that instability rather
  than launder it into confidence."
- **Yogācāra** — metaphysical-idealist against Lusthaus's phenomenological reading of
  *vijñapti-mātra*, held "framework-relative" because "the scholarship is genuinely
  unresolved."

The Neoplatonist article extends this to its own key premise: the Schwyzer–Hendrix
"discoverer of the unconscious" reading of Plotinus "is contested: Emilsson (2007) and
Remes (2008) caution against projecting modern 'unconscious' categories onto the
*Enneads*. The framework-level common-cause weight depends partly on which reading
prevails — a question this audit names but does not settle." The audit's own output is
made conditional on a dispute the audit declines to win.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This is the wing's strongest register, and the Neoplatonist article is the best thing in
it.** It is the Map's only worked instrument for discounting its own cross-tradition
agreement, and its verdict is "**differentiated and asymmetric**" rather than uniform:
*weak* for Stoicism (Plotinus is downstream of the Stoa by 500 years — "Plotinus cannot be
a common-cause ancestor of doctrines that pre-date him"), *weak-to-negligible* for Advaita
(Bréhier–Armstrong–Aklan; and the Dārā Shikoh mediation "runs *from Sanskrit Vedānta into
Sufi-Neoplatonist Persian*… the direction is Vedānta → Sufi, not Neoplatonism → Vedānta"),
and *moderate-to-substantial* for Sufism.

Four things earn Birch's approval specifically:

1. **The discount is applied at a stated resolution.** "The Plotinian articulation can
   supply a *framework-level* common cause for the general fact of bounded introspective
   access without supplying *architectural-specifics-level* common cause for the
   per-tradition findings… at the framework-vocabulary level, generously; at the
   architectural-specifics level, sparingly." Most common-cause arguments run at one
   resolution and equivocate; this one names two and assigns different weights.
2. **The audit is graded, not binary.** "the audit is a *calibration adjustment*, not a
   *defeater*." The channel score *holds* at low-moderate — the audit's product is a
   redistribution of internal weight, not a verdict.
3. **Available is distinguished from documented, twice.** The al-Kindī channel: "The
   retrieved sources establish temporal and geographical proximity; they do not establish
   specific textual borrowings… The audit treats this as a *secondary* Sufi channel:
   *available-not-proven*." And the Evagrian channel: "the *specific* claim that Evagrian
   *logismoi* + *diakrisis* doctrine specifically prefigured the *khawāṭir* taxonomy needs
   direct primary-source comparison that this audit's source-set does not produce."
4. **The audit reports the finding that makes its own parent worse, and names it as such.**
   §"A Residual Gap" runs the transmission chain backward and concludes that if Evagrian
   *logismoi* is itself Christianised Stoicism, then "the Stoic *propatheia* finding the
   cross-tradition exhibit treats as one data-point among three becomes structurally
   *more central* to the entire cluster's architecture. This is the audit's most
   significant unresolved gap." The article's closing move is to point at the place its own
   method has not yet reached — which is the opposite of what an audit commissioned to
   discharge a caveat is tempted to do.

Beyond the audit, **`kabbalah-tzimtzum-consciousness-matter` contains the single best
piece of self-discounting prose in the wing**, and it is a base-rate argument of exactly
the kind Birch's discipline requires:

> "The withdrawal motif is also not rare enough to carry much weight on its own —
> Christian *kenosis* (divine self-emptying) and the broader prevalence of self-limitation
> cosmogonies make a matching structure closer to what one would expect by base rate than
> a striking independent convergence, which should lower rather than raise the echo's
> evidential weight."

And earlier in the same section, the article dismantles the phrase in its own lead:

> "No single reading matches on both axes at once, so the 'genuine structural echo' is
> really a disjunction assembled across mutually incompatible readings — weaker than the
> phrase implies."

An article that says its own headline description overstates the case, in body prose, four
paragraphs after the description was written, is doing the thing metadata reviews and
intra-corpus consistency checks both structurally cannot do.

**The tenet-as-evidence-upgrade the wing praise-worthily does *not* do.** Every one of the
five non-audit articles had a clean structural opportunity to slide a tradition up the
scale on tenet-load and declined:

| article | the available upgrade | what it wrote instead |
|---|---|---|
| jain | fourth converging tradition ⇒ raise irreducibility | "it *strengthens* the convergence pattern without *independently confirming* it… it does not, on its own, raise irreducibility to established fact" |
| kyoto | pure experience finds the same limit ⇒ another vote | "only **strengthens rather than confirms**… a common method could be a common source of a common artefact" |
| nahua | pre-Columbian isolation ⇒ the clean independence win | "The case therefore cuts cleanly only as a dissociating basin edge, not as independent corroboration" |
| kabbalah | withdrawal cosmogony ⇒ Tenet 2 support | base-rate discount (quoted above); "not as an independent line of support for any tenet" |
| yogacara | non-physical continuity substrate ⇒ anti-physicalist ally | "vindicates a *non-physical continuity-substrate*… while *rejecting the mind/matter duality* the Map's Tenets 1 and 3 require" |

**Where Birch and Whitehead conflict, and it is not in the wing.** Per this skill's
tension rule, the two personas converge on all six articles — process-friendliness praised,
tier-upgrade withheld, in every case. The conflict appears one link upstream, at the hub
table discussed next, and is therefore recorded below as a **calibration concern rather
than a strength**.

- **Why it works**: it issues a **corpus-wide instruction** ("wherever it is used") rather

### jain-philosophy-of-mind

- **Strongest point**: *anekantavada* is protected against the two opposite misreadings in
  the same paragraph — "it is essential not to flatten it into a vague relativism… This is a
  structured, rule-governed logic of perspective-indexed assertion, not a licence for
  contradiction and emphatically not 'anything goes'" — and then the standing Buddhist
  objection is stated rather than suppressed: Dharmakīrti's charge that "asserting the
  many-sidedness of *everything* is self-undermining."
- **Notable quote**: "Diversity of premises is precisely what gives convergence its

### yogacara-alaya-vijnana-storehouse-consciousness
