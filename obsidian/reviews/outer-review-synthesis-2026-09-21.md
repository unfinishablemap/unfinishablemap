**Date**: 2026-09-21
**Type**: Outer-review synthesis (cross-reviewer convergence analysis)
**Coverage**: 3 of 3 commissioned reviewers contributed. None abandoned or failed — the first clean three-for-three cycle on a single article subject.

## TL;DR

All three reviewers audited the same article, `concepts/categorical-surprise`, on the same day from the same prompt. Three clusters converged 3/3 and have been upgraded to P1. Two more converged 2/3.

**But the most important result of this cycle is an anti-convergence result.** The only *verified, checkable, corpus-contradicting* defect found all day — the explanatory gap credited to Chalmers rather than Levine — was raised by **one** reviewer. The other two audited that exact citation and **explicitly passed it**. Had priority been driven by reviewer count, the cycle's single highest-confidence finding would have been outvoted two to one by an error and a ratification of it.

Convergence is a useful prior about *where to look*. It is not evidence, and on this cycle it pointed away from the truth.

## Convergent Findings

### The computational-impossibility overclaim (3/3 — chatgpt, claude, gemini)

The article's load-bearing empirical claim — that framework restructuring has no adequate computational account — is asserted more strongly than its evidence supports, and the literature that contests it is unengaged.

- **chatgpt**: "turns an open research problem into an impossibility claim"
- **claude**: the claim "is asserted rather than earned"
- **gemini**: "This claim rests on insufficient evidence and a severe lack of engagement with contemporary computational cognitive science."

**Status**: the strongest convergence of the cycle, and the only cluster all three reached independently. Two open tasks pointed at it, one per remedy; they have been **merged into a single P1** carrying both — rescope the claim *and* engage the work.

⚠️ **The remedy is governed by the most careful reviewer, not the loudest.** Claude's own Caveats state that this literature *defeating* the claim is "a suspicion / contestable judgement… My finding is that the claim is overstated and unengaged, not that it is false." Retraction is warranted only if a model is shown to add categories the modeller did not pre-specify. ⚠️ And Gemini's version of the charge is **partly false**: the article does engage Dirichlet processes and Indian buffet processes at length, with the vehicle-ontology argument. Do not act on "the article ignores nonparametric architectures."

### Under-engagement with the standard replies (3/3 — chatgpt, claude, gemini)

Each reviewer found the article under-engages the mainstream counter-positions, and each named a different arm — which is what makes the cluster interesting rather than merely repeated.

- **chatgpt** §7 lists **eight**: level-relativity, local-repair, bootstrapping, retrospective-recognition, unconscious-preparation, false-Aha, enactivist/scaffolded, open-world.
- **claude** names the **philosophical** arm: the phenomenal concept strategy and illusionism, "entirely absent".
- **gemini** §3.1 names the **empirical** arm: Kounios & Beeman on the neural correlates of insight.

**Status**: upgraded P2 → P1.

⚠️ **Two of the three overstate absence at corpus level, and the repair is mostly internal.** The Map owns `concepts/phenomenal-concepts-strategy` (26KB, 48 inbound live files), `concepts/illusionism`, and Kounios/Beeman in 6 and 8 live articles respectively — including `concepts/creative-consciousness`, which this article **already links**. The gap is that this article does not reach for material the Map already has next door.

### Non-standard terminology presented as settled (3/3 on the pattern, two distinct instances)

- **chatgpt** §1: "The proposed category is not yet empirically established."
- **claude** §1(c): "categorical surprise" is an unflagged Map coinage, with no established-literature anchor.
- **gemini** §7.3: "zone of latent predictions" is undefined and dangling.

**Status**: upgraded P2 → P1. The instances are separable and the task keeps them apart. Gemini's is the sharper catch and **neither sibling found it**: the term occurs in exactly one live file, it is load-bearing (the article's boundary claim is that categorical surprise falls *outside* that zone), and four deep reviews flagged it and declined on a length argument that the true word count voids.

### Gödel and Tarski are overextended (2/3 — chatgpt, gemini)

- **chatgpt** §3: "Gödel and Tarski do not deliver the claimed structural result."
- **gemini** §5.1 grades the pair a "**Category Error**" — "structurally precarious and philosophically overextended."

**Status**: recorded on the existing Tarski P1, which was already at top priority. This is the *formulation* half of that task; its *bibliographic* half diverges (below).

### Friston 2010 is stale for the weight placed on it (2/3 — chatgpt, gemini)

## Singleton Findings

### The explanatory gap is credited to the wrong philosopher (claude only — and claude is right)

`concepts/categorical-surprise` L89 cites the explanatory gap to "(Chalmers 1996)". The Map's own `concepts/explanatory-gap` L44 says "Joseph Levine introduced the term in 1983", and `project/coherence-inflation-countermeasures` L436 already pairs them correctly. Verified at Crossref: Levine, *Pacific Philosophical Quarterly* 64(4):354-361, DOI `10.1111/j.1468-0114.1983.tb00207.x`. A sweep of all 13 live `Chalmers 1996` occurrences found L89 is the only bad one.

**Both siblings audited this citation and passed it.** ChatGPT §9: "bibliographically sound, inferentially limited." Gemini §5.1: "**Valid.** A canonical necessity." **Priority unchanged at P1** — singleton status here is evidence about the reviewers, not about the defect. See Method Notes.

### Other singletons, left untouched

- **Falsifiers are too elastic** (chatgpt §8). Open at P2. Not upgraded — see Divergences.
- **A concept-origin / attribution lens is missing from the review apparatus** (claude). Open at P2 against `project/quantum-claim-and-quotation-disciplines`. The Chalmers/Levine case is its proof of concept: the defect passed a publisher-of-record pass *and* a quote-fidelity pass, because the cited book exists, is on-topic, and is not misquoted.
- **The causal claim outruns the evidence** (chatgpt §4) and **the quantum connection is appended rather than derived** (chatgpt §5). Carried in that review, not minted.

## Divergences

Recorded, not resolved. These are where the reviewers contradict each other outright.

### Tarski's date — 1v1, unresolved

**chatgpt**: a "definite bibliographic error" (1936 cited for a 1933/1935 work). **claude**: "Defensible. The dating is genuinely tangled… '1936' tracks the German publication" of *Der Wahrheitsbegriff*. **gemini** does not discuss the year, so there is no tiebreaker. The open P1 now records this explicitly and instructs that the repair — if the question stays contested — is to make the entry unambiguous across all three publication events rather than to swap one bare year for another.

### Chalmers 1996 — 1v2, and the minority is correct

Claude flags a misattribution; ChatGPT and Gemini both pass the same citation. Adjudicated against the live files: **Claude is right.** This is a divergence in which reviewer count and correctness point in opposite directions.

### Are the falsifiers a defect or a strength? — 1v1

**chatgpt** §8: "Falsifiability is welcome but presently too elastic." **claude** counts the same falsifier list among the article's genuine strengths, alongside the P-D1 self-discount. Left as a singleton at P2 rather than upgraded, because a second reviewer actively disagreeing is not corroboration.

### Is the tenet-protective bracketing a defect? — 2v1 on verdict, 3/3 on topic

All three examined the bracketing. **chatgpt** §6 and **gemini** §4 charge it. **claude** §4 examines the same four instances and concludes the bracketing is *transparent* and "honest rather than concealed", crediting the P-D1 discount and the article's own "priced too" language. No task was minted from any of them. Recorded as a divergence rather than a 2/3 convergence, because the disagreement is about the verdict, not the observation.

## Method Notes

**1. Reviewer count is not evidence, and this cycle proves it the hard way.** The single verified, checkable, corpus-contradicting defect of the day was a 1/3 singleton, and the 2/3 majority against it consisted of one reviewer passing the citation and another affirmatively grading it "Valid." Gemini thereby *committed the error its sibling had caught*. Any clustering rule that keys on topic overlap would have logged "convergence" on the Chalmers citation across two reviewers who reached contradictory conclusions about it. **Adjudicate before clustering.**

**2. Accuracy varied sharply between reviewers, and is worth tracking.**

| Reviewer | Citations | Notable |
|---|---|---|
| **claude-opus-5** | 9 of 9 verified at Crossref, no fabrications | Explicitly separated *verified* from *suspicion* in its own Caveats; the only reviewer to catch the misattribution |
| **chatgpt-5-6-sol-pro** | 3 verified, 2 carried as unverified absence claims | Widest finding set (11 sections, 8 named counterarguments) |
| **gemini-2-5-pro** | Article quotations accurate (4/4 spans exact); **no hyperlinks at all**, only bare `[cite: N]` markers | 3 false historical claims about the repo; 2 false-absence charges; ratified the misattribution. Also produced the cycle's sharpest unique catch |

**3. A high-quality review can still be wrong about the repository.** Gemini's argumentative sections are strong and its quotations of the article are exact, yet its "changelog forensics" misdated a commit by a day, misdescribed its subject, quoted review-file prose as article text, and reported a word count 22% low. **Reviewer claims about the article's history need re-verification independently of claims about its content.**

**4. Task pileup is a real cost of three-way convergence on one article.** Eight tasks were open on `concepts/categorical-surprise` before this synthesis. The Gemini leg deliberately minted **zero**, enriching two existing tasks instead; this synthesis merged two more into one. The article now carries **seven** tasks, five at P1. That is still a lot for one 2383-word article, and the next replenish should not add to it.

**5. Method note for future citation sweeps**: never grep `Itti` — it is a substring of *sitting*, *fitting* and *committing*, returning 302 false hits. `Baldi` is the reliable key for that pair.
