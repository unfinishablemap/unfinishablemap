## Executive Summary

The divided-will page gives the wing a verdict it lacked: fragmentation data are *predicted* by partitioned and distributed architectures and only *accommodated* by a unitary selector, so they tell modestly against an organ-style will and give the Map's selector no support. Today's work carried that calibration into akrasia, control-theoretic-will, valence and mental-effort, and those pages now agree with each other. The verdict has not reached the pages that state the Map's *replies*. The apex still says akrasia "fits the selector model … rather than evidence against it". The canonical luck page, agent-causation and the positions register still present the reasons reply as answering the luck objection, with no akratic exception. The responsibility gradient settles the Tenet 3 quantifier ("as long as the agent experiences anything at all—some control … persists") and claims for dualism a unification a physical controller supplies equally. Four items fix this at a combined +79 words (+96 with one optional sub-edit), most of it in one dated register note.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
The wing never lets the control vocabulary do the hard problem's work. Control-theoretic-will §Analogy, Model, and Ontology (L118–126) keeps the controller/plant split functional and sources the Map's dualism in "the hard problem, the conceivability argument, the limits of physical explanation", and L130 now concedes that "no current prediction separates a non-physical controller from a physical one". Akrasia L110 makes the same separation from the other side: the hard problem and conceivability "support only non-reduction, and epiphenomenalist dualists accept them too". A property dualist gets the explanatory gap taken seriously without having it inflated into a theory of agency.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
Control-theoretic-will's mapping table (L48–56) gives the physics–psychology interface a concrete grammar: the actuator is the quantum selection mechanism, the control signal is attentional selection, and the setpoint is the agent's intention. Mental-effort L118 presents Stapp's Zeno proposal and then states Tegmark's twelve-orders gap with "Stapp's reply restates the desideratum rather than answering it". A Stapp sympathiser gets his model treated seriously and the strongest objection answered in the open, with the Map's post-decoherence alternative named.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
Akrasia keeps the first-person datum, "I chose X while judging Y better", at the centre and then disciplines it. L98 says the evaluative state "must be indexed to the time of action and kept apart from post-choice report", and preference-void L65 supplies the reason: the ordering used to diagnose the lapse "may have been built in the asking". This treats what it is like to choose against one's judgement as data that must be read carefully, and refuses to treat it as an oracle.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
The divided-will page reads Davidson's divisions as functional and interpretive ("The idea of a quasi-autonomous division is not one that demands a little agent in the division", L53), and Korsgaard's unity as practical rather than substantial (L115). A process philosopher will like a picture in which unity is achieved in the settling of conflict and is not a block given in advance. The page keeps this on the right axis: practical unity is adopted for ethics, and "unity of agency then gives the posit no support". The compatibility is praised here and is used nowhere to upgrade anything.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
The wing takes agency seriously enough to state its hardest problem at full strength. The divided-will §luck-objection (L101–109) shows that the reasons reply "fails for exactly that contrast", quotes Levy's "akratic actions are lucky actions" (p. 153), and books two costed replies, accepting the surd or confining agent-causal selection to choices with no settled ranking. Free-will L94 is equally honest about the general case: "a primitive whose modal structure is owed rather than supplied". The responsibility gradient's upper end (L140) keeps the libertarian's reason for caring: on agent causation the clear-headed wrongdoer is "the irreducible author of a choice they had the full resources to make differently". A Kane sympathiser will note that torn decisions have not yet been set beside the settled-ranking case (see Expansion, High 2).

### The Mysterian (McGinn)
The divided-will's "What does a unitary selector predict that its rivals do not? … Nothing remains" (L89) and "supplies a vocabulary and a locus for agent causation, but no prediction" (L99) are the kind of admission a mysterian respects: the selector is held without any pretence that the data have earned it. Control-theoretic-will L130's "the Map owes that test and does not yet have it" and agent-causation L133's "removes a defeater without supplying support" belong with it.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)
This wing is unusually well calibrated, and the Birch persona has most to praise. The divided-will labels its own data: preference reversal under delay "strongly supported, though its functional form is contested", bundling "a live hypothesis resting on two small studies", and Ainslie's organ test "his own scoring backed by two small independent studies". Mental-effort L84 carries the ego-depletion record in both directions (Hagger 2016 d = 0.04; Vohs 2021 d = 0.06; Dang 2025 d = 0.31–0.35) and draws the right conclusion: "The felt-drained sense survives; reading it as a unitary-resource gauge does not." The responsibility gradient's scarcity paragraph (L79) gives Mani et al.'s critics and defenders and then rests on "the weaker and better-supported claim". Akrasia reports Friese et al. at g = 0.30, ".13–.24 after bias correction". The restraint to praise most is Tenet 5's symmetry in the divided-will L125: the tenet "binds the Map equally: a single selector cannot be claimed as the simpler explanation either". That is the pattern "tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating", applied to the Map's own posit. **No minimal-organism tier claim appears in the wing.** Valence L137 defers the boundary to the marginal-organism page, which concludes the boundary "cannot currently be located". **Process Philosopher vs Hardline Empiricist:** the two converge on the divided-will's practical-unity section, which is the honest outcome. The slippages this review flags involve no tier: they are mechanism and unification claims that a physical controller would share (responsibility gradient L134, L152), and a datum read as fitting the selector when the wing has found it to tell against (apex L152).

## Content Strengths

### the-divided-will
- **Strongest point**: the candidate-selection trilemma (L91–99). Horn (i) is tied to the P-Q3 nearness-to-epiphenomenalism concession, so the psychology and the physics registers make the same admission.
- **Notable quote**: "A distributed architecture therefore has its own selection step, specified computationally and assigned to no selector." (L69)
- **Why it works**: it removes the easy contrast (selector versus no selection) and forces the real question, what a non-physical selector adds to a physical arbitration step.

### akrasia-and-weakness-of-will
- **Strongest point**: classification before desert. "Classification cannot be deferred, because the selection reading applies only to weakness: where an act is compulsive, the agent's conscious wish that it not occur is no evidence of bidirectional control." (L112)
- **Why it works**: it blocks the tempting move of reading every felt struggle as selection, and it gives the responsibility gradient the line it now uses (L61).

### control-theoretic-will
- **Strongest point**: the akratic signal mapping (L146) leaves *refusal* (felt pull as a second reference) against *failure* (authority lost against that pull) open, and adds that "A distributed architecture predicts the divergence with no single controller, where a unitary one only accommodates it."
- **Notable quote**: "a physical controller supplies that stability too, so stability does not pay for the posit" (L138).

### responsibility-gradient-from-attentional-capacity
- **Strongest point**: L61's capacity scope: an agent "whose normal capacities of self-control are intact but who lets that recognition lapse shows weakness of will rather than incapacity, and is not thereby excused". This is exactly what Ainslie's organ test requires. A general attentional parameter can excuse incapacity, but a choice-specific lapse cannot be read off a general strength deficit.
- **Also**: the No Many Worlds paragraph (L158): "Desert needs alternatives genuinely rejected, not merely routed to another branch."

### valence-and-conscious-selection
- **Strongest point**: the horn names its own refuter. "The horn therefore predicts, and is refuted by, choice following a commitment while independently measured present affect favours the alternative." (L127)
- **Why it works**: it turns akrasia from an embarrassment into the horn's test, and L113 adds that the horn's whole content is a felt/computed dissociation of which "no such datum yet exists".

### mental-effort
- **Strongest point**: L88 files the Kurzban and Inzlicht process models on the materialist side ("These are functionalist absorption stories, not witnesses for the dualist reading"), and L84 links the depletion record to the organ-model test.

### agent-causation
- **Strongest point**: L65 places the substance-leaning at the agent-causal layer, not the tenet layer: "agent causation needs a persisting subject that exercises causal powers in its own right". This is the posit's ground as tenets L121 states it.

### free-will
- **Strongest point**: L102: the strongest physicalist rival "predicts the same covariation, so the covariation is neutral between it and agent causation". The free-will hub argues the wing's calibration in its own words.
