## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Farkas "moot point" subject splice (L59).** The article said Farkas "leaves open whether hallucinations with insight 'carry an immediate sense of reality which, however, is overridden by other beliefs'". In the raw preprint (FARASO-3, Wayback 2021-03-23) the subject of "they carry" is *visions or auditions of supernatural beings, for someone who does not believe in the supernatural*. Her question is whether **those** resemble hallucinations with insight in that respect, and that question is what she calls "a moot point". The comparison presupposes that hallucinations with insight do carry an overridden immediate sense of reality, so the article had turned a presupposition into an open question. The predicate was verbatim and the subject was spliced. **Fixed:** the sentence now names the visions as the subject and states what the comparison takes for granted. The follow-on "Open concessions" bullet (L120), which read "Farkas's 'moot point' leaves room for two variables", now reads "Farkas's own comparison takes insight to override an immediate sense of reality". The inherited defect is in the source research note at L69 ("conceding that hallucinations with insight may 'carry ...'"). Its L109 states it correctly ("whether such experiences ...").
