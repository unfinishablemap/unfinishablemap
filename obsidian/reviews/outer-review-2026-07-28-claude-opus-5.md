## 2. Dimensional analysis

### Q1 — Is the "functional duplicates can differ phenomenally" claim adequately supported, or is there an epistemic-to-metaphysical slide?

**Not adequately supported as stated; there is a real slide, only partly hedged.** Three specific defects:

**(a) Conflation of behavioural with functional undetectability.** The article's "Core Scenario" and "Why This Challenges Functionalism" sections move from "pass the same colour tests / make the same discriminations / same functional organization" to "qualia aren't functional kinds." But the SEP entry the article itself cites (Byrne, *Inverted Qualia*, §3.1) is explicit that the anti-*functionalist* argument requires a *functionally* undetectable inversion (premise P1b), which is far stronger than the behaviourally undetectable inversion (P1a) the colour-science discussion motivates, and that Block and Fodor (1972) offered only the scenario's "conceptual coherence" to bridge the gap. Byrne then shows the coherence→possibility move is questionable (the gold/atomic-number-79 analogy: conceptually coherent yet necessarily false) and, decisively, that *if* the move were legitimate a simple zombie argument would do the same work more cheaply — so the inversion scenario carries no distinctive weight. The article never confronts the behavioural-vs-functional gap; it treats "same functional organization" as simply given.

**(b) The intrinsicness premise is assumed, not earned.** "The Privacy of Experience" asserts qualia "are intrinsic, private, and not fully captured by their relational properties — what a colour experience is *like* outruns what it does or what it's about." That non-relational intrinsicness is precisely the dualist conclusion the argument is supposed to *deliver*; helping oneself to it in the premises is circular. This is Tenet 1 doing silent work in the modal premise. [[inverted-qualia]]

**(c) Overclaim in the frame.** The article's own metadata description reads: "Functionally identical beings could differ in experience—**proving** function doesn't fix phenomenology." "Proving" is indefensible — the conceivability→possibility inference is contested by every type-B physicalist, a point the *neighbouring* `functionalism` article states plainly ("The load-bearing step is the inference from conceivability to metaphysical possibility, which type-B physicalists contest") but which the inverted-qualia article suppresses in its core sections. The body does hedge at the end ("genuine philosophical intuition rather than settled fact"), and the MQI section is genuinely well-calibrated (it "bites the bullet," concedes the epiphenomenalism tension and the decoherence challenge, and treats strict behavioural-identity-plus-inversion as "physically unrealised even if conceivable"). That calibration makes the triumphalism of the opening, the Dualism subsection, and the "proving" frame worse by contrast, not better. [[inverted-qualia]]

**Empirical over-confidence.** The detectability discussion presents Palmer (1999) as showing "certain inversions—like flipping red-green while leaving blue-yellow fixed—are plausible within color science," then retreats to "the argument is modal, not empirical." This is selective: Palmer's three candidate transformations are contested in the very BBS exchange the article cites (Broackes 2007 argues red-green is detectable via lightness differences; Hardin 1997 doubts the second strategy), and Palmer's overarching frame is a *skeptical* "isomorphism constraint"/"subjectivity barrier," not a licence for the dualist. Nida-Rümelin's pseudonormal-vision case — the closest thing to an *actual* red-green inversion — is not mentioned at all, which is telling, because Nida-Rümelin argues pseudonormals would be behaviourally *detectable*, i.e. the real-world instance cuts against undetectable inversion. ### Q2 — Missed or underweighted counterarguments

**Representationalism / intentionalism (the strongest single rival): reduced to a one-line dismissal.** The "Physicalist Responses → Representationalism" paragraph is a single sentence plus a two-clause "Problem." It does not engage, at framework level, any of: Byrne's "Intentionalism Defended" (*Philosophical Review* 110(2), 2001, 199–240); Tye's teleological/optimal-conditions reply to Inverted Earth (*Consciousness, Color, and Content*, 2000); Speaks's "Spectrum Inversion without a Difference in Representation Is Impossible" (*Philosophical Studies* 156(3), 2011); the transparency-of-experience argument (Harman 1990); or Shoemaker's own appearance-properties accommodation. The article outsources this to the neighbouring `first-order-representationalism` article — which is genuinely good and correctly concludes representationalism is "resisted, not defeated" — but the effect is that the inverted-qualia article is *more triumphalist than its own neighbour* and does not warn the reader that its headline rival is undefeated. [[first-order-representationalism]]

**Quality-space asymmetry / impossibility arguments: absent.** The article treats Hardin's detectability objection as the whole of the structural challenge and then declares it survivable. It never engages the strongest structural rebuttal in the literature: that *every* possible quality space must be asymmetrical, so undetectable inversion is not merely empirically false for humans but impossible in principle for any modality — Hilbert & Kalderon (2000); Rosenthal's quality-space theory ("the properties imagined for undetectable inversion could not be mental qualities … there is no known modality with a symmetrical QS"); and Clark's *Sensory Qualities* (1993). On quality-space theory, undetectable inversion is not even conceivable, "just as it is not conceivable given the nature of water that water is not H₂O" — the exact modal analogy the article deploys *for* the dualist is deployed *against* it by the structuralists, and the article never sees the counter-move coming. [stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qualia-inverted/)

**The phenomenal-concepts strategy: adequate.** The PCS paragraph fairly states the Kripkean conceivability-does-not-entail-possibility reply and links to the strong neighbour article (which does run Chalmers's master argument). This is the article's best-handled rival.

**2020s colour science: entirely missing, and it cuts against the thesis.** The article's empirical base is Hardin (1988) and Palmer (1999) — nothing later. The last twenty years of colour vision science has established a finding that is *prima facie evidence against* strong inversion: very large physiological variation coexisting with stable colour appearance. L:M cone ratios vary from **1.1:1 to 16.5:1 in normal-colour-vision males** — Hofer, Carroll, Neitz, Neitz & Williams, "Organization of the Human Trichromatic Cone Mosaic," *J. Neurosci.* 25(42):9669–9679 (2005): "we found males with normal color vision that varied in the ratio of L to M cones (from 1.1:1 to 16.5:1). We also found a protan carrier with an even more extreme L:M ratio (0.37:1)." Yet unique hues are, in Webster, Miyahara, Malkoc & Raker, "Variations in normal color vision. II. Unique hues," *JOSA A* 17(9):1545–1555 (2000), "largely unconstrained by normal individual differences in the cone-opponent axes… no relationship was found between hue settings and relative sensitivity to the LvsM and SvsLM axes." Bosten's review "Do You See What I See? Diversity in Human Color Perception," *Annu. Rev. Vis. Sci.* 8:101–133 (2022), states: "Variability at all levels of the color vision system creates diversity in color perception… such that each of us lives in a unique perceptual world" — while documenting that compensatory processes keep percepts more stable than spectral-sensitivity variation predicts. Witzel & Gegenfurtner's "Color Perception: Objects, Constancy, and Categories" (*Annual Review of Vision Science*, 2018) reaches the same conclusion. Tregillus, Isherwood, Vanston, Engel, MacLeod, Kuriki & Webster, "Color Compensation in Anomalous Trichromats Assessed with fMRI," *Current Biology* 31(5):936–942.e4 (2021), show chromatic responses reduced in V1 but that "in later areas V2v and V3v, chromatic responses in the two groups were indistinguishable" — cortical compensation for reduced receptor signals. The structuralist reading of all this — that variation *without* corresponding phenomenal/behavioural difference is evidence that appearance is fixed by relational structure, not that qualia float free of function — is exactly the reply the article needed to confront and did not. Tetrachromacy (Jordan & Mollon, "Tetrachromacy: the mysterious case of extra-ordinary color vision," *Curr. Opin. Behav. Sci.*, 2019: ~12% of women are genetic candidates but functional tetrachromats are far rarer — estimated 2–3% — and of 14 obligate carriers only one, cDa1, made discriminating matches) and the World Color Survey universals literature (Regier & Kay; Lindsey & Brown 2009) are likewise absent.

**Dennett: half-engaged.** The article rebuts Dennett's "flip-back" argument (*Consciousness Explained*, 1991) by charging it presupposes eliminativism — a fair but quick move. It does not engage the sharper "Quining Qualia" (1988) intuition pump (the "neurosurgical prank": one cannot tell qualia-inversion from memory-access inversion), which is the more damaging version and which targets the "directly apprehensible" premise the article relies on.

**Relationalism / primitivism (Cohen, Byrne & Hilbert): absent.** No engagement with colour relationalism or primitivism as alternatives that dissolve the inversion intuition.

### Q3 — Tenet leakage, constrain-vs-establish breaches, and co-optation

**Co-optation firewall failure — Shoemaker (primary finding).** The lead states the scenario "survived sophisticated reformulation by Shoemaker (1982)" and lists Shoemaker (1982) as external source, and the body invokes his black-white achromatic case to "preserve the thought experiment's force." This recruits Shoemaker toward a conclusion he explicitly rejects. Shoemaker is a *functionalist* who developed and defended functionalism across "Functionalism and Qualia" (1975) and "The Inverted Spectrum" (*J. Phil.* 79(7), 1982); his move is to show inversion is *compatible with* functionalism (qualia are functionally definable via qualitative-similarity relations) and, crucially, to *deny that absent qualia / zombies are coherent at all* while granting inverted qualia are (see "Absent Qualia Are Impossible—A Reply to Block," *Phil. Review* 90(4), 1981). The SEP entry the article cites flags him verbatim (§3.1) as "a notable dissenter on this point; he holds that while inverted spectrum scenarios are conceptually coherent, zombie scenarios are not. See Shoemaker 1975a, 1981, 1991." Shoemaker's later "appearance properties" apparatus (1994, 2002) is a *defence of representationalism* — per SEP §3.3, both Invert's and Nonvert's experiences "represent the tomato as *red*," and the phenomenal difference is carried by differing *represented* appearance properties (AP_G vs AP_R), so Shoemaker "denies P3" of the anti-representationalist Argument B. So the article enlists, as a builder of its anti-functionalist and anti-representationalist case, the philosopher whose life's work is the functionalist and representationalist accommodation of the very scenario. This is the textbook co-optation firewall failure and the audit's single most serious finding. [[inverted-qualia]][Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/shoemaker-sydney-1931)

**Shared-exhibit / self-contradiction on the zombie parallel.** "Relation to Zombie Arguments" quotes SEP verbatim — "almost uncontroversially: if an inverted spectrum argument against physicalism works at all, then a simple zombie scenario will equally serve the purpose" (quotation accurate to SEP §3.1) — and concludes "the underlying point is the same." But that very SEP passage is the one whose footnote names Shoemaker as the dissenter who denies the parallel. The article thus leans simultaneously on (i) Shoemaker as source and (ii) a parallelism Shoemaker rejects, and it uses the parallelism to double-count: the article says the zombie and knowledge arguments "do similar work," while the `qualia` and `philosophical-zombies` neighbours cite "Mary's Room, inverted qualia, zombies" as "multiple independent arguments [that] converge." That is confession-without-correction: the shared exhibit is disclosed, then still counted as convergent independent support in the Dualism subsection ("contributes to the convergence case").
