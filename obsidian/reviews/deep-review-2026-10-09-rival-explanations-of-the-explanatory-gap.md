## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **C3 over-concedes to Type-B, and the p. 42 quotation drops the clause that shows it** (dropped qualifier plus Map scoring unsupported by the criterion's own source). The C3 paragraph said Type-B "identifies the basis of consciousness with the mechanism that produces judgments about it, so it meets the challenge with no further posit … since that brains produce the reports is settled". Chalmers, who states the criterion, applies it on pp. 36–38 to physicalist theories as well: IIT, workspace, HOT, and biological theories such as Block's, whose silicon "duplicates will share our intuitions" (p. 37), so that biological mechanisms "will not play an essential role in explaining the intuitions". That brains produce reports does not show that a given theory's basis M produces *problem* reports. The article's quotation of p. 42 also ended at "an interactionist picture" and dropped "(or even on a biological or quantum picture)". That is end-truncation inside quotation marks, but it removes exactly the clause that extends the difficulty to physicalist realizers, in a sentence used to score dualism below Type-B. **Resolution:** the Type-B sentence now reads "a brain state, plausibly among the causes of the reports, so it meets the challenge without a further posit … It still owes an account of how its chosen mechanism yields problem reports in particular, which Chalmers presses on physicalist theories too" (with the p. 37 quotation); the p. 42 quotation is restored in full; and a closing clause says why C3 still favours Type-B: "for dualism the question is whether consciousness is a cause at all". The verdict (C3 favours Type-B) and the tier are unchanged. Two dependent glosses were brought into line with it. C1 now reads "Type-B reaches the reports through a brain state" (was "explains the reports at once"), and Relation/Dualism reads "which Type-B reaches without a further posit" (was "explains without remainder").
2. **L61 overstated Kammerer's conclusion** (dropped qualifier). "and he concludes for a physicalism of one of those kinds" was wrong on the source. Kammerer says the two accounts need not "exhaust the logical possibilities" (ms. p. 9), disclaims arguing "for Introspective Illusion Accounts over Lack of Understanding Accounts (or other possible accounts)" (ms. p. 21), and ends by telling physicalists to attend to "these two conceptions, as well as to other potential alternatives" (ms. p. 22). **Resolution:** the sentence now reads "and he points physicalists to those two "as well as to other potential alternatives" (ms. p. 22)". At L73, "which is why Kammerer ends with a physicalist disjunction" became "which is why Kammerer draws a physicalist moral from it".
3. **Pin-cite error, Kammerer "the most typical example".** The phrase is on ms. p. 7, not p. 8 (the research note bundled it into its p. 8 taxonomy line). **Resolution:** corrected to ms. p. 7.
4. **Kammerer's family ordinals were reversed.** Kammerer introduces the two alternatives to the fallacy family in this order: "The first corresponds to … 'Introspective Illusion Accounts'" (ms. p. 8), then "another route … 'Lack of Understanding Accounts'". With Fallacy Accounts as the first family, lack-of-understanding is his third family and introspective illusion his second; the article had them the other way round. **Resolution:** the ordinals are swapped, with no change in word count.
