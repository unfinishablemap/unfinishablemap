## Overall verdict

**Recommendation: retain after major revision.**

The article is strongest where it limits its ambitions. Its explicit statement that akrasia neither requires nor establishes dualism is correct, and its four-part historical scaffold—Socrates, Aristotle, Davidson and Holton—is a useful introduction. It also commendably acknowledges that a physicalist can redescribe its “selection” vocabulary in terms of neural subsystems. It nevertheless falls short of its claim to provide neutral “source exposition.” There is one definite Davidson error, a smaller Davidsonian overstatement, an inaccurate Hare page range, an overcompressed Aristotle, and a much more serious empirical overreach in calling resolution maintenance a distinct, effortful and trainable faculty. ## 1. Citation and attribution audit

### Plato’s *Protagoras*: verified, but interpretively flattened

The citation to *Protagoras* 358b–c exists and supports the article’s central attribution: Socrates denies that someone who knows or believes a better course is available will knowingly continue with the worse course. The quotation reproduced through the SEP is accurate. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

The article’s summary—apparent akrasia is ignorance or miscalculation—is therefore defensible. But “on his intellectualist picture, to judge a course best just is to be most motivated toward it” presents one standard reconstruction as if it were simply the content of the passage. The argument occurs within a hedonistic measurement discussion: apparent defeat by pleasure is redescribed as an evaluative illusion in which proximate and distant pleasures and pains are mismeasured.

Recent scholarship makes the interpretive range wider than the article indicates. Wenjin Liu argues that the relevant ignorance is not merely missing information but a substantive and enduring psychic disorder; Suzanne Obdrzalek interprets the failure as a vividness-driven hedonic illusion rather than the operation of a competing non-rational desire. [Brill](https://brill.com/abstract/journals/phro/67/3/article-p309_2.xml)

**Verdict:** the source is real and the broad attribution is sound. The article should say “on a standard intellectualist reading,” situate the denial within the measuring-art argument, and acknowledge that the nature of Socratic ignorance is disputed.

### Aristotle’s *Nicomachean Ethics* VII: broadly faithful, but too one-dimensional

Aristotle does distinguish having knowledge from actively exercising it, distinguishes universal from particular premises, and compares the akratic state with the impaired access of someone asleep, mad or drunk. The article’s account of knowledge being possessed but not actively deployed is therefore textually grounded. [Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.7.vii.html)

But “his answer runs through the practical syllogism” is too definitive. Aristotle offers several interlocking distinctions rather than one uncontested mechanism. The article suppresses three features that matter directly to its later selector interpretation:

1. Aristotle distinguishes **impetuous akrasia**, in which the person acts before completing deliberation, from **weak akrasia**, in which a deliberative conclusion is reached but not maintained.
2. He acknowledges cases of what might be called **good incontinence**, where abandoning a false or corrupt resolution is praiseworthy.
3. He insists that standing by every judgment or resolution is not automatically good; obstinacy can mimic continence. [Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.7.vii.html)

These omissions matter because the Map later treats akrasia generically as selection against an already formed all-things-considered evaluative state. Aristotle’s impetuous cases may contain no such presently active state, while his Neoptolemus case shows that departing from a prior conclusion can be substantively better.

**Verdict:** substantially accurate but interpretively underqualified. It needs the impetuous/weak distinction, the possibility of justified departure, and at least one modern scholarly source alongside the primary text.

### Davidson: genuine source, one definite error and two needed qualifications

The article correctly gives Davidson’s defining conditions: the action is intentional, the agent believes an alternative remains open, and the agent judges that alternative better all things considered. It also correctly explains the distinction between conditional or relational all-things-considered judgments and all-out conclusions. [topics/akrasia-and-weakness-of-will](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/akrasia-and-weakness-of-will/)

However, the sentence **“Davidson builds irrationality into the definition” is false**. Irrationality is not one of Davidson’s defining conditions. It is derived later from the substantive principle of continence. That distinction is philosophically important: Davidson first establishes that the action is logically possible and non-contradictory, then argues that it is procedurally irrational. [topics/akrasia-and-weakness-of-will](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/akrasia-and-weakness-of-will/)

Two further corrections are needed:

- Saying that the akratic “forms the all-out judgment favouring x” is stronger than the safest Davidsonian formulation. The core conclusion is that the agent does **not** form the all-out judgment favouring the option supported by the all-things-considered judgment. Describing the action as necessarily expressing a contrary all-out judgment is a plausible reconstruction, but should be labelled as such.
- “The agent has no reason” must not be left without qualification. Because the act is intentional, the agent does have a motivating reason for it. What the agent lacks, by their own lights, is an adequate reason to do the worse option **rather than** the option favoured by the total body of considerations. The current SEP explicitly draws this distinction. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

The publication history should also be regularised. The standard first-publication citation is 1970, followed by the 1980 reprint, rather than leaving “1969/1970” unresolved. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

**Verdict:** the central exposition is good, but the definition/irrationality error is substantive and should be corrected immediately.

### Hare: attribution accurate; pagination needs correction

The article accurately represents Hare’s view that fully sincere assent to an ought-judgment has an internal connection to action, subject to the qualification that the action must be physically and psychologically possible. It also correctly notes Hare’s alternatives: actual inability, or use of evaluative language without full prescriptive commitment. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

The Oxford Academic record gives Chapter 5, “Backsliding,” as **pages 67–85**, not 67–86. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12880/chapter/163212119)

The ability qualification should receive more prominence. It is not a minor escape clause: it is exactly where akrasia begins to border on compulsion. Hare can deny a case is genuine akrasia by classifying the agent as psychologically unable, so the article cannot later bracket the freedom/compulsion issue as merely a question for moral responsibility.

**Verdict:** conceptually accurate, with a minor bibliographic correction and an underdeveloped implication.

### Holton: the structural account is accurate; the empirical extrapolation is not

Holton does argue that weakness of will is over-ready revision of a resolution rather than action contrary to present better judgment. His own summary says that temptation typically involves a shift in judgment, that strength of will involves a particular faculty or skill, and that maintaining resolutions is effortful. [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://web.mit.edu/holton/www/pubs/bookpdf/index.html)

The article goes beyond that support when it calls the capacity **“trainable”** and later says this is what “survives.” Neither Holton’s conceptual account nor the cited Hagger replication establishes that there is a unified, domain-general faculty which training reliably strengthens.

The article also underplays the normative element in Holton. Abandonment counts as weakness only when it is over-ready or unreasonable. A broken leg, new evidence, a dangerous resolution or an originally foolish commitment can make revision rational. The current SEP emphasises both the irreducibly normative classification and the possibility that sticking to a resolution may be worse than abandoning it. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

**Verdict:** accurate about Holton’s philosophical proposal, insufficiently supported about trainability, and incomplete about rational revision.

### Hagger et al. 2016: the numerical claim is accurate, but its use is one-sided

The article correctly reports the preregistered 23-laboratory replication: total \(N=2{,}141\), with an estimated ego-depletion effect of approximately \(d=0.04\) and a confidence interval including zero. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/1745691616652873)

What does **not** follow is that a general trainable willpower faculty thereby remains intact while only the resource metaphor falls away. Depletion, effort, trainability, resolution maintenance, inhibitory control, trait self-control and strategic self-regulation are distinct claims and constructs.

The 2016 result is also no longer an adequate standalone summary of the empirical position:

- A 2017 meta-analysis of self-control training found a nominal effect around \(g=0.30\), but bias-corrected estimates were smaller and the authors judged the mechanism insufficiently understood; they could not conclude that repeatedly overriding dominant responses was the operative ingredient. [Universität Wien](https://ucrisportal.univie.ac.at/en/publications/does-self-control-training-improve-self-control-a-meta-analysis/)
- A 2024 review argues that successful self-control often proceeds through habits, early conflict resolution and anticipatory strategies that avoid effortful inhibition. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352250X24000733)
- A 2025 multi-lab study using a much longer and more intense initial task reported effects around \(d=0.31\)–\(0.35\), suggesting that depletion findings may be paradigm- and intensity-dependent rather than simply absent. That result does not restore the original resource model, but it prevents the field from being summarised as closed by the 2016 replication. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/18344909251386084)
- A 2026 meta-analysis connects trait self-control with health behaviour partly through attitudes, perceived control and intentions, again pointing toward heterogeneous cognitive and strategic pathways rather than one isolated faculty. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41801718/)

**Verdict:** the cited result is accurately reported. The article’s positive conclusion about what survives is not adequately supported and is already empirically stale.

### Frankfurt: accurate description, but its consequences are not absorbed

The unwilling addict is indeed moved by a first-order desire rejected by his second-order volition. But Frankfurt describes him as struggling helplessly, being unable to resist, and lacking freedom of the will. [York University](https://www.yorku.ca/dimock/documents/FrankfurtFreedomoftheWillandtheConceptofaPerson.pdf)

The article notes that this is nearer compulsion than ordinary weakness, but then moves on. That should instead force a reconsideration of the opening explanandum—how someone can “freely and knowingly” act against better judgment. Frankfurt’s case shows that the hierarchical conflict can be present while freedom is absent. A divergence among mental levels is therefore not enough to establish free akrasia, still less a freely operating conscious selector.

**Verdict:** accurate citation, under-integrated objection.

### Watson 1975 and 1977: references verified; the second paper is seriously understated

The 1975 article does distinguish the agent’s valuational system from the motivational system and treats their possible divergence as central to unfree action. The Map’s summary of this distinction is broadly correct. [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/pdf/2024703.pdf)

The 1977 article does much more than merely “take up” the weakness/compulsion boundary. Watson challenges the ordinary idea that the weak agent could have resisted whereas the compulsive agent could not. He argues that the usual contrast between a will that is too weak and a desire that is too strong is not independently explanatory, and he seeks a distinction in terms of normal capacities of self-control. [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/pdf/2183785.pdf)

That challenge strikes at three claims in the Map article:

- that strict akrasia can simply be assumed to be free;
- that “selection” identifies an explanatory event rather than redescribing the outcome;
- and that moral responsibility can be deferred without affecting the classification of the phenomenon.

**Verdict:** bibliographically correct, philosophically underrepresented.

### The 2025 SEP: current, but selectively used

The cited SEP article is real and was substantively revised in September 2025. It supports much of the Map article’s canonical exposition. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

But the Map draws on it selectively. The same SEP treatment gives sustained attention to:

- potentially rational akrasia;
- foolish resolutions and rational abandonment;
- Schapiro’s 2021–22 account of weakness as abandoning one’s role as deliberator;
- strategic, habitual and externally scaffolded self-control;
- skill models in which self-control need not be effortful;
- and recent work on compulsion, addiction and attributability. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

These are not peripheral extensions. They directly bear on the article’s “trainable faculty,” “free selection,” and “agent’s evaluative state” claims.

### Internal Map citations: useful for navigation, not independent support

The internal links are real and generally relevant. They cannot, however, supply independent evidence for the selector interpretation. In several places the Map articles cite one another in a loop:

- akrasia is interpreted through the controller model;
- the controller model is illustrated through agency and selection;
- agency phenomenology is then treated as evidence for the controller.

Unless an external discriminator enters the loop, this is mutual elaboration rather than corroboration.

## 2. Claims resting on insufficient evidence

### 2.1 “A distinct, trainable capacity”

This is the article’s least defensible empirical formulation. There may be trainable self-regulatory skills, but the cited evidence does not establish a single, distinct, domain-general faculty for maintaining resolutions. The empirical literature instead distinguishes habits, inhibitory skills, attentional deployment, implementation intentions, environmental structuring, conflict avoidance, motivational valuation and trait-level differences.

A safer formulation would be:

> Holton treats resolution maintenance as involving an executive skill or family of skills. Some self-regulatory capacities appear modifiable, but the extent of domain-general transfer, the role of effort, and the underlying mechanisms remain disputed.

### 2.2 “What survives” the ego-depletion controversy

The phrasing implies that the structural and trainability claims have passed an empirical filter that only the limited-resource account failed. They have not. The 2016 replication bears on one sequential-task depletion paradigm. It neither demonstrates nor directly tests Holton’s resolution-based account, and it does not by itself establish or refute trainability.

The article should separate five questions:

1. Do people form resolutions to insulate later conduct?
2. Can resolutions be abandoned over-readily?
3. Is maintaining them a distinctive skill?
4. Is that skill normally effortful?
5. Can repeated practice produce broad transfer?

The cited sources strongly support the first two as conceptual and ordinary-psychological possibilities. They do not settle the remaining three.

### 2.3 “The post-Davidson consensus says yes”

“Dominant contemporary view” would be safer than “consensus.” Davidson shifted the debate toward explaining rather than denying akrasia, but disputes continue over whether strict synchronic akrasia occurs, whether better judgment was genuinely held at the action time, whether the case is free rather than compelled, and whether “akrasia” and “weakness of will” refer to the same phenomenon. The current SEP itself presents an active debate rather than a closed consensus. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

### 2.4 Freedom is assumed before it is analysed

The opening asks how an agent can “freely and knowingly” act against better judgment. Freedom is then treated as a further matter for the moral-responsibility article. That division does not work.

Desert and blameworthiness can be deferred. **Freedom and control cannot**, because they determine whether the event is strict akrasia, compulsion, addiction, automatic habit, impulsive action or an involuntary symptom. Frankfurt and Watson are not merely later ethical qualifications; they contest the initial description of the phenomenon.

### 2.5 A stable “agent’s evaluative state” is assumed

The controller section treats an all-things-considered evaluation as a determinate state already present, against which the selector acts. Yet several possibilities remain open:

- the judgment shifted before action, as in Holton;
- it was partial or weak rather than all-out;
- it was merely avowed social or moral language, as in Hare;
- it was constructed by the reporting procedure;
- it was retrospectively reconstructed after the act;
- it coexisted with graded or incommensurable evaluations rather than one total ordering.

The Map’s new Preference Void explicitly argues that preferences may be constructed rather than read from a prior ordering and that selection and reception cannot be distinguished from the chooser’s own position. The akrasia article now needs to incorporate that result. [voids/preference-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/preference-void/)

### 2.6 The “transparent read-off” alternative is too weak

The article says that if selection were a transparent read-off of best judgment, Davidson’s gap could not arise. That conditional is true but carries little argumentative weight. No sophisticated physicalist account needs action to be a transparent read-off of one explicit judgment.

A naturalistic architecture can contain:

- multiple valuation systems operating on different timescales;
- habitual and goal-directed control;
- temporal discounting;
- attentional capture;
- conflict monitoring;
- action thresholds;
- metacognitive judgment;
- and later verbal report.

Such an architecture predicts judgment/action divergence without requiring either a nonphysical selector or a single central executive. The article contrasts the Map with a position few serious rivals hold.

### 2.7 “Selection” currently redescribes rather than explains

Calling the outcome a selection does not yet explain why it occurred. The article leaves unanswered:

- What determines the selector’s choice?
- Is the all-things-considered judgment neural, conscious or jointly constituted?
- If the controller is the agent, how can it select against the agent’s own evaluation?
- If evaluation belongs to the brain while selection belongs to consciousness, why call the former the agent’s evaluation?
- If both belong to consciousness, has the article simply recreated the conflict inside the nonphysical self?
- What observable pattern differs from a distributed physical-control system?

Without answers, the selector vocabulary relocates Davidson’s gap but does not close it.

### 2.8 The independent-argument bridge is incomplete

The article says the controller’s nonphysicality rests on the hard problem and conceivability arguments. Even granting those arguments, they would at most support some form of ontological non-reduction or separability. They do not by themselves establish:

1. that nonphysical consciousness is causally efficacious;
2. that it performs action selection;
3. that it acts through quantum indeterminacy;
4. or that akratic actions are among its interventions.

Those are additional bridge premises. The control-theoretic article partly acknowledges that its vocabulary is functionally neutral, but then says it makes dualist interaction “concrete and testable” without supplying a differential akrasia prediction. [concepts/control-theoretic-will](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/control-theoretic-will/)

## 3. Strongest missing or under-represented counterarguments

### 3.1 Akrasia may count against a unitary controller

The article treats judgment/action divergence as something a selector model must accommodate. A stronger objection is that recurrent divergence is prima facie evidence **against** a unitary personal controller.

A distributed-system account can naturally model competition among valuation, habit, affect, planning and action-selection processes. The interactionist model instead posits one conscious agent-controller and must then explain why that controller:

- acts against its own reasons;
- cannot reliably implement its resolutions;
- sometimes appears unaware of the basis of its output;
- and generates a retrospective report disavowing its own selection.

The Map needs to show that a personal selector explains this fragmentation better than a distributed architecture rather than merely being compatible with it.

### 3.2 The weakness/compulsion boundary

Watson’s challenge deserves a full subsection. If an alien desire defeats the agent’s evaluative judgment, what establishes that the agent could have resisted? The fact that similar agents sometimes resist is not enough; nor is the later moral intuition that blame is appropriate.

Recent work also treats compulsion and attributability as graded rather than binary, especially in addiction and neurological conditions. The current SEP highlights Gorman’s 2019 and 2023 treatments of this boundary. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

For the Map this is decisive: an irresistible neural process accompanied by a conscious wish that it not occur is not evidence of bidirectional control. It could instead be evidence of conscious impotence.

### 3.3 Rational akrasia

The article mentions in one sentence that better judgment may itself be defective. That is not enough. Arpaly and McIntyre-style cases challenge the authority the controller interpretation grants to explicit all-things-considered judgment.

An agent may act from emotional sensitivity to reasons that their reflective theory has wrongly discounted. Huck Finn’s affection for Jim and the student who abandons an unsuitable career are standard examples. In such cases, the action may track the better reasons while the stated “better judgment” is morally or epistemically worse. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

This creates a problem for the Map’s valence discussion. An action driven by present affect against a defective reflective judgment may be **more** value-sensitive, not a failure of value-guided selection.

### 3.4 Bad resolutions and rational revision

Holton’s account requires a standard of unreasonable or over-ready revision. Without one, “strength of will” collapses into stubbornness. Aristotle already recognises praiseworthy failure to stand by a bad plan, and the post-Holton literature makes the same point about foolish resolutions. [Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.7.vii.html)

The article therefore needs to distinguish:

- resolution stability;
- rational sensitivity to new evidence;
- resistance to anticipated temptation;
- and inflexible persistence.

A capacity can be strong in the first sense and defective in the others.

### 3.5 Constructed and retrospective judgment

Strict akrasia is usually identified through the subject’s report that they judged another option better at the time. But retrospective reporting is compatible with rationalisation, memory reconstruction and a desire to present oneself as endorsing the socially approved option.

The Preference Void makes this problem especially acute. If the evaluative ordering is partly constructed during elicitation, asking after the action “what did you really think was best?” may generate the very ordering used to classify the act as akratic. [voids/preference-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/preference-void/)

A serious empirical treatment would distinguish contemporaneous judgment, confidence, desire strength, intention, resolution, anticipated valence, choice and post-choice report.

### 3.6 Self-control can succeed by preventing conflict

The article’s effortful-faculty framing underrepresents the strongest contemporary alternative: people with effective self-control may arrange situations, form habits, alter attention and use external scaffolding so that high-intensity conflict never arises. The 2025 SEP and the 2024 effortless-self-control review both emphasise these routes. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

This matters to the Map because phenomenological effort may mark a late-stage control failure rather than the paradigm of successful agency. A system that repeatedly requires heroic conscious intervention could be less well controlled than one whose habits and environment silently keep behaviour aligned with long-term aims.

### 3.7 Schapiro’s deliberator-abandonment account

Tamar Schapiro’s recent account deserves inclusion because it is unusually relevant to an agent-causal site. On her view, weakness is not selecting the inferior conclusion within deliberation but abandoning one’s post as deliberator and letting inclination act without genuinely deciding. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)

That creates an alternative interpretation of the first-person phenomenology:

- Map reading: consciousness selected the worse neural option.
- Schapiro-style reading: the agent ceased exercising the will, so the ensuing behaviour should not be understood as a conscious selection at all.

The article presently assumes the former in its site-perspective section without confronting the latter.

### 3.8 The homunculus and ownership objections

A nonphysical selector does not end the explanatory regress. If it has reasons, desires, judgments and a power to choose among them, the same question reappears: why did it select the option it judged worse?

Nor is “the agent’s evaluative state” sufficiently precise. Watson’s distinction was designed partly to identify which motivational structures express the agent’s standpoint. Frankfurt identifies the agent with a higher-order endorsement; Watson objects that another higher-order desire does not automatically acquire authority. The Map’s persisting subject does not solve this merely by existing: it needs a theory of which of its conflicting states count as its standpoint and why.

## 4. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article’s neutrality disclaimer is, in isolation, intellectually responsible. It correctly states that akrasia is compatible with physicalist and dualist descriptions. The problem is not that it refuses to pretend akrasia proves dualism.

The protection appears in how the article then uses and quarantines the material.

### Semantic uptake with evidential quarantine

The page gains intuitive force by translating the phenomenon into the Map’s preferred vocabulary: an interface, controller and selector choose against the agent’s evaluation. But when that vocabulary is challenged, it is declared merely framework-relative and the evidential burden is transferred to independent hard-problem and conceivability arguments.

That produces an asymmetry:

- the Map receives the explanatory imagery of a conscious controller;
- akrasia is allowed to make that imagery feel natural;
- but akrasia is not permitted to test whether the controller exists or whether it is nonphysical.

This is best described as **semantic uptake with evidential quarantine**. It is not an explicit fallacy, but it permits a tenet to gain rhetorical support without taking evidential risk.

### Tenet 3 is engaged but not exposed to a discriminator

The article says akrasia is relevant to how faithfully the conscious controller tracks reasons, while also conceding that it cannot tell a physical from a nonphysical selector. That means akrasia currently supplies no evidence for bidirectional interaction.

The proper conclusion is stronger than the article states: until the Map derives a differential prediction, akrasia should be catalogued as a **compatibility case only**, not as evidence, support or a manifestation of Tenet 3.

The later Volitional Control article is more candid: it says the interface proposal is hypothesised, lacks a discriminating test, and remains empirically compatible with a physical account of the same inhibitory circuitry. The akrasia article should adopt that exact calibration. [topics/volitional-control](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/volitional-control/)

### Tenet 1 does not entail a controller

The article moves from independent arguments for nonphysical consciousness to the claim that the relevant controller is nonphysical. But dualism alone does not entail interactionism, a central controller, agent causation or quantum selection. Epiphenomenalist dualism is an obvious counterexample.

The Map therefore needs a distinct bridge argument from:

> consciousness is not reducible to physical processes

to:

> consciousness is the entity selecting action outcomes.

At present that bridge is presupposed rather than defended.

### Tenet 2 is silently imported and then omitted

The article speaks of an interface selecting among neural options but does not discuss minimal quantum interaction. Elsewhere, the site makes the selection explicitly quantum-mechanical. [topics/free-will](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/free-will/)

The article should either:

- remain wholly at the functional level and state that Tenets 2 and 4 are not engaged; or
- identify the hypothesised quantum step and admit that akrasia supplies no evidence for it.

The current halfway position lets “interface” carry mechanistic connotations without exposing the mechanism to scrutiny.

### Tenet 5 is used too expansively

The article says that because description underdetermines theory and simplicity is unreliable, all four positions should be held open. That does not follow.

Rejecting simplicity as an infallible truth detector does not eliminate comparative assessment using:

- explanatory scope;
- internal coherence;
- independent motivation;
- predictive accuracy;
- mechanistic specificity;
- prior plausibility;
- and ontological cost.

Tenet 5 can block the inference “simplest, therefore true.” It should not block “simpler, equally predictive, and independently better supported, therefore presently preferable.” Otherwise it becomes a standing device for protecting under-specified theories from normal abductive comparison.

### Moral responsibility cannot be entirely deferred

The article may reasonably defer the final question of praise, blame and desert. It cannot defer control, freedom and knowledge, because its own definition presents the akratic act as free, intentional and knowing. The Moral Responsibility article likewise lists control, sourcehood and knowledge as constitutive conditions. [concepts/moral-responsibility](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/moral-responsibility/)

The target article therefore needs at least a local classification section distinguishing weakness, compulsion, habit, accident and impaired agency.

## 5. Integration with surrounding Map articles

### Control-Theoretic Will

There is broad consistency in saying that control theory is ontologically neutral. The related article explicitly admits that a physicalist can adopt the controller/plant description and that the model does not establish dualism. [concepts/control-theoretic-will](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/control-theoretic-will/)

The unresolved integration issue is the control mapping. In an akratic case:

- What is the reference signal?
- Is the all-things-considered judgment the setpoint?
- Is immediate valence a disturbance or another reference?
- Is the controller the conscious self, or is conscious experience feedback to a controller?
- Why does the controller deliberately drive the plant away from its own setpoint?

Until those correspondences are defined, “control-theoretic” adds vocabulary but no explanatory structure.

The controller article’s claim that the framing makes interaction “testable” also needs either a concrete test or deletion. The akrasia article currently provides none.

### Valence and the Mechanism of Conscious Selection

This article, revised after the target page, says akrasia “demonstrably” shows selection diverging from considered evaluation, while allowing that the relevant currency might instead be present felt pull. [topics/valence-and-conscious-selection](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/valence-and-conscious-selection/)

That language is too strong for three reasons:

1. Strict akrasia is itself descriptively contested.
2. A judgment shift or constructed preference may mean there was no stable considered evaluation at the selection moment.
3. If present felt valence drives the act, the choice may be value-sensitive even while departing from reflective judgment.

The two pages need a common taxonomy separating:

- considered judgment;
- desire;
- intention;
- resolution;
- anticipated phenomenal valence;
- motivational force;
- and action.

Without it, “evaluation” changes meaning from page to page.

### Free Will and Determinism

The free-will article calls phenomenology and neural signatures the “core evidence” for agent-causal consciousness, and says selections reflect the agent’s reasons and values. [topics/free-will](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/free-will/)

Akrasia introduces a direct problem for that formulation. If conscious selection can oppose the agent’s all-things-considered reasons and values, then the site must explain when selection reflects authorship and when it reflects weakness, irrationality or motivational capture.

There is also an evidential inconsistency. The akrasia page says its first-person datum supplies no evidence for the metaphysics; the free-will page treats related first-person phenomena as core evidence. These positions can be reconciled only through an explicit comparative argument showing that the phenomenology is more probable under agent-causal dualism than under its physicalist rivals. “Coherence with” the phenomenology is not enough.

### Phenomenology of Choice and Volition

The 3 October revision says that the arc from deliberation through execution is evidence that consciousness is a genuine selector and controller. It later acknowledges that neural common-cause accounts can accommodate at least some of the same fit. [concepts/phenomenology-of-choice-and-volition](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/phenomenology-of-choice-and-volition/)

This page and the akrasia page need one shared evidential status:

- **phenomenological datum**;
- **constraint on any adequate account**;
- **compatible with conscious selection**;
- but **not presently discriminating between physical and nonphysical selection**.

Calling the same family of experience “neutral” in one place and “evidence that” the Map’s ontology is true in another is an avoidable calibration failure.

### Volitional Control

This article’s opening says the evidence removes the principal empirical case for epiphenomenalism, while its next sentence admits that predictive processing and active inference predict the same evidence. Later it explicitly concedes that the interface hypothesis lacks a discriminating test. [topics/volitional-control](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/volitional-control/)

The later caveat is the defensible one. The opening should be softened, and the akrasia article should link to the no-discriminator admission rather than merely to the general page.

### Mental Effort

Mental Effort is better calibrated than the target article: it says felt effort is real but not a transparent window onto its mechanism, and that effort can dissociate from the operation it appears to report. [concepts/mental-effort](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/mental-effort/)

The akrasia article should import that caution. Its current presentation makes effortfulness, faculty status and trainability sound considerably more settled than the dedicated effort article does.

### Moral Responsibility

The target page says responsibility is only glancingly relevant. That understates the conceptual connection. The Moral Responsibility article makes control, sourcehood and knowledge prerequisites of responsibility; those same conditions help determine whether a case is free akrasia or compulsion. [concepts/moral-responsibility](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/moral-responsibility/)

A reciprocal section should distinguish classification of the act from the further question of deserved blame.

### Preference Void

This is the most important new internal link. The Preference Void states that preference reports may be constructed during elicitation and that the site cannot distinguish conscious selection of an ordering from conscious reception of a physically constructed ordering. It explicitly says neither reading discriminates the Map from physicalism. [voids/preference-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/preference-void/)

The akrasia article should adopt that discipline. Its “agent’s all-things-considered evaluative state” should be made:

- time-indexed;
- confidence-graded;
- sensitive to elicitation procedure;
- and explicitly distinguishable from post-choice report.

## 6. Stale or insufficiently current literature

The classical works and Davidson are not “superseded”; they remain foundational. The problem is that the article gives old canonical sources the appearance of a current survey without adequately representing what has happened around them.

The most significant omissions are:

- **Ancient interpretation:** Liu 2022 and Obdrzalek 2023 on the character of Socratic ignorance and evaluative illusion. [Brill](https://brill.com/abstract/journals/phro/67/3/article-p309_2.xml)
- **Contemporary philosophical accounts:** Schapiro 2021/22 on abandoning the deliberator’s role; Gorman 2019/23 on weakness, compulsion and attributability; Haug 2021 and related work on motivational harmony and indirect self-control. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)
- **Self-control strategy research:** situational strategies, habits, external scaffolding and forms of control that need not be effortful. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)
- **Training evidence:** the 2017 meta-analysis, with its small and bias-sensitive effects and unresolved mechanism. [Universität Wien](https://ucrisportal.univie.ac.at/en/publications/does-self-control-training-improve-self-control-a-meta-analysis/)
- **Post-replication depletion evidence:** the positive 2025 multi-lab result under a more intense protocol. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/18344909251386084)
- **Current trait/mechanism work:** the 2026 meta-analysis linking trait self-control to behaviour through multiple cognitive and intentional pathways. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41801718/)

The article’s empirical paragraph is therefore not simply old; it is **selectively current**. It uses the famous 2016 null result but omits later work that complicates both the negative and positive sides of its conclusion.

# Concrete potential improvements

1. **Correct the Davidson sentence immediately.** Replace “Davidson builds irrationality into the definition” with: “Davidson’s definition identifies the phenomenon; his principle of continence supplies the further argument that it is procedurally irrational.”

2. **Clarify Davidson’s reason claim.** State that the agent has a motivating reason for the intentional act but, by their own assessment, lacks an adequate reason to choose it over the better-supported alternative.

3. **Soften the all-out-judgment reconstruction.** Say that the akratic fails to form the all-out conclusion corresponding to the all-things-considered judgment; label the claim that they form a contrary all-out judgment as an interpretive reconstruction.

4. **Correct Hare’s page range to 67–85** and retain the physical-and-psychological-ability qualification whenever Hare’s denial is summarised.

5. **Name editions and translations for Plato and Aristotle.** “Standard classical text” is inadequate where translated wording is quoted or interpretive weight is placed on terms. Give Stephanus/Bekker locations, translator, edition and stable publisher information.

6. **Expand the Aristotle section.** Add impetuous versus weak akrasia, good incontinence, obstinacy, and the distinction between acting before deliberation and failing to stand by a deliberative result.

7. **Replace “distinct, trainable capacity.”** Use a calibrated formulation describing resolution maintenance as a proposed executive skill or family of skills whose effortfulness, domain generality, mechanism and trainability remain contested.

8. **Rebuild the empirical paragraph around separate constructs.** Distinguish depletion, subjective effort, inhibitory control, resolution maintenance, trait self-control, strategic self-regulation and training transfer.

9. **Update the empirical references.** Add Friese et al. 2017, Gillebaart and Schneider 2024, Dang et al. 2025 and Hagger et al. 2026. Present the depletion literature as method-sensitive and unsettled, not as a simple 2016 defeat followed by survival of everything else.

10. **Change “post-Davidson consensus” to “dominant contemporary view”** and explicitly state what remains contested: simultaneity, sincerity of judgment, freedom, compulsion and the distinction between akrasia and weakness of will.

11. **Change the opening from “freely and knowingly” to “apparently freely and intentionally.”** Then make freedom one of the questions under investigation rather than part of the initial datum.

12. **Add a full weakness-versus-compulsion section.** Engage Watson 1977 and Gorman 2019/23, and specify candidate criteria such as normal capacities, reasons-responsiveness, attributability and the function of the motivating desire.

13. **Expand rational akrasia beyond one sentence.** Include Arpaly/McIntyre cases and explain the difference between substantive rationality—doing what there is actually most reason to do—and procedural rationality—following one’s own stated conclusion.

14. **Add Schapiro’s account.** Present “abandoning one’s post as deliberator” as a direct rival to the claim that consciousness positively selected the worse option.

15. **Add a strongest-physicalist-model subsection.** Describe a distributed architecture containing valuation, habit, temporal discounting, conflict monitoring, executive control and retrospective report. Compare that model with the nonphysical-selector model on explanatory and predictive grounds.

16. **Operationalise the akratic datum.** Distinguish contemporaneous judgment, confidence, desire, intention, resolution, felt valence, action and retrospective report. Explain what evidence would show that the better judgment remained active at the moment of action.

17. **Cross-link the Preference Void in both directions.** The akrasia page should acknowledge preference construction; the Preference Void should list strict akrasia as a case in which retrospective elicitation may create the ordering used to diagnose the phenomenon.

18. **Revise Valence and the Mechanism of Conscious Selection.** Replace “akrasia demonstrably diverges from considered evaluation” with “strict akrasia is reported as such a divergence, if the considered evaluation remained genuinely held at action time.”

19. **Give the site a shared taxonomy of practical states.** Define and consistently distinguish evaluation, all-things-considered judgment, desire, present valence, intention, resolution, motivation, selection and action.

20. **Revise Control-Theoretic Will’s signal mapping.** Identify the reference signal, controlled variable, disturbance, feedback channel and actuator in an akratic episode. Explain why the controller can drive behaviour away from its own purported evaluative setpoint.

21. **Either provide a discriminator for the controller model or remove “testable.”** A useful functional analogy is not yet a testable interaction theory. State what observation would be more likely under a nonphysical selector than under a distributed physical controller.

22. **Harmonise evidential language across Free Will, Phenomenology of Choice, Volitional Control and Akrasia.** Related phenomenology should not be “neutral” in one article and unqualified “evidence that consciousness is a selector” in another.

23. **Use a controlled evidence vocabulary site-wide:**  
   *compatible with* → *constrains* → *weakly supports* → *discriminates* → *establishes*.  
   Require every article to justify movement from one level to the next.

24. **Add a bridge-premise audit to every tenet relation.** For this cluster, audit separately: judgment/action divergence → functional selection → conscious selection → nonphysical selection → quantum-mediated selection. No arrow should be inherited merely because later arrows are site commitments.

25. **Constrain Tenet 5.** State explicitly that limits to Occam’s Razor block simplicity from being decisive, but do not remove parsimony, prior probability or ontological cost from comparative assessment.

26. **Do not defer constitutive questions under the label of moral responsibility.** Desert can be handled elsewhere; freedom, ability, control and compulsion must be addressed where the phenomenon is defined.

27. **Require direct citations for named empirical traditions.** Mischel and Baumeister should not appear only through Holton. Empirical claims need primary studies or current systematic reviews, while Holton should be cited for his philosophical use of them.

28. **Introduce a claim–evidence matrix in the article source.** Tag each important sentence as textual attribution, interpretive reconstruction, empirical claim, Map-specific inference or speculative integration, with exact supporting page or section.

29. **Add an automated cross-article consistency check.** Flag cases where one datum is labelled non-discriminating on one page but evidential on another, or where terms such as “evaluation” and “selection” receive incompatible definitions.

30. **Apply the changelog’s strongest deep-review standard to this article.** The current workflow elsewhere records publisher-of-record verification, exact page pins, metadata corrections and evidence-strength downgrades. The same source-by-source and bridge-by-bridge audit would catch nearly all the problems identified here. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)
