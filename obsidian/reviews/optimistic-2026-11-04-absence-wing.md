## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would recognise AA's tenet paragraph as the right place to locate the hard problem. AA does not claim that physics cannot register an absence. It grants that "the mismatch negativity shows a physical system doing exactly that" (AA L104), and it puts the residue in the absence being experienced *as absence*, "the phenomenal character that makes the room *feel* Pierre-less" (L104; the phrase "the room *feels* Pierre-less" recurs at L90). This is the explanatory gap stated without inflation. Absence offers no new gap, only a vivid case of the old one. That is why `tenet-check-2026-10-15` Warning 14 asks for "supports" at L104 to become "bears on" (Priority 3c). Chalmers would also praise PA's Dualism paragraph. It aligns presence-type voids with the zombie argument while noting that both "inherit the same open branch", and it marks the zombie argument's "further … contested step from conceivability to possibility" (PA L125). The page then says "The category fits that reading without evidencing it." Per the 2026-08-20 Stability Note, AA L88 "Absence is a feature of the field of consciousness, not of the physical field" is bedrock against the totality-facts reply. It is not re-flagged here, and it is not praised either.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The wing has no quantum content, and that is to its credit. A Stapp reader would look first at AA's Bidirectional paragraph, because searching for Pierre is sustained, voluntary attention, the kind his Process 1 account is built to model: "attention to absence is often voluntary and action-guiding" (AA L106). The page does not make that move, and it should not without a mechanism. The repair that Priority 3b carries over from the tenet check keeps the paragraph at the level of fitting the tenet without evidencing it. PA L129 is the other place Stapp would stop. It speculates that presence-type voids "may mark sites where the interface … is particularly dense". It then says at once that "the phenomenology alone does not warrant a locational claim about the interface", and it names two findings that would tilt the evidence. That is how a speculation about the interface should be written.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

AA's five-variety taxonomy (L58–L68) is phenomenology done properly. Each kind of absence gets its own felt character. Temporal absence intensifies: "the absence grows more present with time, not less" (L64). The empty chair "feels like loss, not like a missing object" (L66). The tip-of-the-tongue gap is "not empty; it has the shape of what is missing" (L68). Sartre's café is used for its figure-ground point ("Absence has figure-ground structure just as presence does", L86) and not as rhetoric. AA's own line on auditory absence states the thesis Nagel would underline: "The cessation does not subtract experience; it furnishes a positive experience whose content *is* the absence" (L62). SV is a useful contrast, and the wing does not yet draw it. There the cessation does subtract experience, and nothing is left whose content is the absence (Priority 1c, 3a).

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would value AA's "Consciousness is constitutively temporal" (L96), with retention and protention as conditions of experiencing absence at all, and SV L112's suggestion that discursive consciousness "may be a specific *mode* of something deeper". Both are tenet-coherent. Neither is offered as evidence, and SV L112 is conditional ("If consciousness can take a contentless form"). CvP L69 shows the right discipline in the wing's context: "The tenets remove a defeater", and "it is not evidence-register support". **Calibration concern, not a strength:** SV L68 is a case where tenet-coherence does explanatory work. It says that if consciousness is non-physical, the mind's resistance to quieting "might serve a function: protecting the interface between consciousness and the physical world from disruption". That reads a design function off the tenet, and it passes over the plainer account the pessimistic review gave as F5: a brain whose resting state is spontaneous activity resists being quieted, with no function needed. Priority 1d keeps the reading and labels it as a reading.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane would find SV's Heidegger material a real limit case for agency. *Gelassenheit* "does not belong to the domain of the will" (SV L54), and releasement "cannot be adopted as a method, because effort is precisely what it releases" (L54). That describes an outcome an agent can prepare for but cannot will. It is a sharper test of what "ultimate sourcehood" means than any example of deliberate choice. AA supplies the ordinary counterpart, voluntary search, which does real work as "action-guiding" attention (L106). The opening is a sentence somewhere in the agency cluster on outcomes that can be prepared for but not willed. It is listed under Ideas for Later, because the wing pages are not the right home for it.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

SV opens with the cleanest statement of humility in the wing: "The Unfinishable Map cannot map silence." (L42). Its AI section keeps that humility symmetrical. It says an AI has "probably no stream of consciousness to cease" (L86, calibrated to P-AC1 on 2026-10-26), and it gives the image "An AI can map the coastline of this continent." (L88).
