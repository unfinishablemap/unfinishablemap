# External referee report: “Free Will and Determinism”

**Recommendation: major revision**

## Version and scope

I have audited the repository version modified on **20 September 2026**, rather than relying on the indexed web copy. The repository metadata records that date but also says the last full deep review was on **11 July 2026**.  The accessible web snapshot still reports 9 July, while the indexed changelog stops on 6 August, illustrating exactly the indexing lag mentioned in the request.  (see [[free-will]])

Two recent corrections are material:

- On 7 September the article stopped treating its determinism–randomness–mental-causation trilemma as demonstrably exhaustive. The relevant commit says that the non-exhaustiveness correction had previously failed to propagate across the site.
- On 20 September the William James quotation was repaired to restore the omitted words and proper quotation boundary.

These are genuine improvements. Nevertheless, the article still moves too quickly from evidence for structured volition to evidence for a non-physical, substance-bearing selector.

The defensible conclusion is therefore narrower than the article’s conclusion:

> Human volition has structured phenomenology; self-selected, instructed and automatic behaviour involve partly different neural processes; and Libet-style experiments do not establish that conscious agency is illusory.

That conclusion is compatible with interactionist dualism. It is also compatible with physicalist executive control, higher-order monitoring, predictive processing, event-causal libertarianism, emergent agency and several kinds of compatibilism. The cited evidence does not presently adjudicate among those possibilities.

## 1. Central claims and their evidential support

### 1.1 The argument contains an unacknowledged five-step inference

The article effectively moves through five propositions:

1. Deliberating and choosing have a distinctive phenomenology.
2. Deliberative and automatic processes have different neural signatures.
3. Conscious experience makes a causal difference to action.
4. That causal contribution cannot be wholly physical.
5. A persisting conscious substance selects among physically open outcomes.

The evidence is strongest for propositions 1 and 2. It is consistent with 3, but does not isolate conscious phenomenology from its physical realization as the causal factor. It provides essentially no direct support for 4 or 5.

This matters because much of the article’s rhetoric treats the first two propositions as if they preferentially confirm the fifth. Distinctive phenomenology plus distinctive neural processing establishes a systematic relation. It does not establish the direction, metaphysical category or physical locus of causation.

### 1.2 The revised trilemma is better, but remains dialectically weak

The current article now says that **“three options are usually posed”**, rather than claiming that determinism, randomness and mental causation exhaust logical space.  That correction is important because the three categories are neither mutually exclusive nor collectively exhaustive:

- Mental causation could itself be physically realized and deterministic.
- Event-causal libertarians propose indeterministic but reasons-involving mental causation.
- Christian List proposes real agential alternatives at a higher explanatory level without a dualist quantum selector.
- Non-causal, emergent and hybrid agent/event-causal positions do not fit cleanly into the three labels.
- Robert Kane’s current position expressly argues that one need not choose between agent and event causation: his 2024 account treats the agent as an information-responsive complex dynamical system exercising teleological guidance control over its processes.  ([source](https://academic.oup.com/book/58033/chapter-abstract/477473640))

The revised trilemma can remain as a heuristic describing the Map’s motivation. It should not function as evidence that the Map’s third option is the only route to authorship.

### 1.3 Phenomenology identifies the explanandum, not the metaphysics

The article is right that choosing, merely observing, trying, vetoing and sustaining effort feel different. It is also reasonable to resist accounts that simply deny these distinctions.

But coarse-grained introspective reliability establishes only that these experiences occur and differ. It does not establish that their apparent openness is metaphysically veridical. A deterministic or compatibilist deliberative system can represent several options, compare counterfactual consequences, monitor conflict and experience effort precisely because those processes help determine its eventual action.

The article says that **“If choosing were epiphenomenal decoration”**, its neural correlation would be coincidental.  This sets up the wrong contrast. The principal physicalist alternative is not:

> phenomenal experience exists but floats causally idle beside an otherwise identical decision process.

It is:

> the conscious episode is identical with, realized by, or constituted through a causally active control-and-monitoring process.

On that view, correlations between felt effort, conflict, executive recruitment and behaviour are not coincidences. They are expected. The article repeatedly slides between **physicalism** and **epiphenomenalism**, although most physicalists reject epiphenomenalism.

The same point applies to the agency/ownership dissociation. That dissociation is evidence that the brain maintains separable representations of bodily ownership, action generation and action attribution. It rules against a single undifferentiated agency feeling. It does not distinguish a non-physical causal subject from comparator, predictive or source-monitoring models.

### 1.4 The neural-signature evidence is real but overgeneralized

Rajan et al. found that internally chosen **spatial attention**, compared with externally instructed spatial attention, involved greater frontal theta activity and reciprocal frontoparietal interaction. The study used two implementations of a visuospatial cueing paradigm and interpreted these signals as neural processes involved in frontal decision-making and attentional control.  ([source](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/29/7/2832/5041999))

This supports a distinction between self-selected and instructed attention. It does **not** show:

- that consciousness is non-physical;
- that conscious experience, rather than the executive neural process, is the independent cause;
- that the relevant activity biases quantum outcomes;
- that the finding generalizes without qualification from spatial attention to motor action, reasoning and creativity;
- or that automatic processes lack frontal theta or reciprocal coherence in general.

The free-will article describes these as signatures of “willed actions” across domains, and the motor sections inherit the same language. That is a scope expansion beyond the cited experiment. Rajan et al. is also absent from the article’s reference list despite being central to its positive empirical case.

### 1.5 The Desmurget evidence establishes dissociation, not selection

Desmurget et al. stimulated parietal and premotor regions in seven awake neurosurgical patients. Parietal stimulation elicited intentions or apparent movement without electromyographic movement, while premotor stimulation could elicit movement that patients denied experiencing.  ([source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19423830/))

That is important evidence that intention awareness and motor execution can dissociate. It does not demonstrate that the parietal cortex is a selection site, much less that a non-physical subject acts there. A published methodological comment also noted that direct electrical stimulation can activate or deactivate tissue, limiting the use of these results to validate a detailed cognitive model.  ([source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20203033/))

The article should replace language implying that the study “confirms” a selection/execution architecture with the more limited conclusion that it demonstrates a dissociation between aspects of motor intention, awareness and execution.

### 1.6 Agent causation does not yet answer luck

The central answer to luck is: **“The agent’s exercise of causal power is the explanation.”** 

That may identify the proposed kind of cause, but it does not yet provide a contrastive explanation. Consider a rollback case:

1. The complete world is restored to precisely the same pre-choice state.
2. The agent, character, reasons, phenomenal preferences and brain state are identical.
3. On some repetitions the agent chooses A; on others, B.

Why A on this repetition rather than B?

- If the agent’s reasons or state sufficiently explain A, it is unclear in what relevant sense B remained available under the same total conditions.
- If they do not sufficiently explain A, merely saying that the agent caused A does not explain the token difference between this replay and a B replay.
- If an additional exercise of power differs between replays, the article must explain what makes that exercise A-directed without reintroducing determination or unexplained variation.

This is the rollback or contrastive-luck objection. The article currently stops where the objection begins. Calling agent causation irreducible may legitimately mark a metaphysical primitive, but a primitive still needs a theory of its modal and contrastive structure.

Contemporary libertarian work attempts to supply more structure. Kane’s 2024 hybrid account combines agent and event causation, information responsiveness and teleological control, and explicitly engages the rollback objection.  ([source](https://academic.oup.com/book/58033/chapter-abstract/477473640)) The article should compare its substance-selection account with such views rather than treating primitive substance causation as the settled libertarian answer.

### 1.7 Reasons-guidance currently favours no particular side

The article says reasons influence without determining the selected result. But almost every major theory in the debate claims some kind of reasons-guidance:

- Fischer and Ravizza ground responsibility in action issuing from the agent’s own moderately reasons-responsive mechanism, together with an appropriate history of ownership.  ([source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/responsibility-and-control/responsibility-and-history/CFC73E6DE2541E3AD9E1FA766CB6226A))
- Compatibilists treat reasons-guided causal determination as control rather than its opposite.
- Event-causal libertarians allow reasons to probabilistically cause choices.
- Agent-causal libertarians treat the agent as acting for reasons.

The evidential question is not whether choices correlate with reasons. It is which theory best explains that relation, including why one reason wins when competing reasons remain motivationally live. The article currently counts reasons-guidance as positive evidence for its own account without specifying a prediction that rival accounts lack.

### 1.8 The physical-gap claim is underargued

Indeterminism does not by itself create an incomplete physical cause awaiting a mental supplement. A stochastic physical theory can be causally complete in the relevant sense while assigning probabilities rather than unique outcomes. To establish a usable causal gap, the Map needs to specify:

- the physical candidate outcomes;
- their baseline probabilities;
- the location and temporal scale of selection;
- the information available to consciousness;
- the amount by which consciousness changes outcome probabilities;
- the amplification path from microscopic event to action;
- and the statistical signature distinguishing such bias from ordinary physical noise.

Without that causal-budget specification, consciousness-driven selection remains a metaphysical schema rather than an empirically supported model.

### 1.9 Time symmetry does not yield atemporal agency

The article appropriately calls the transition from time-symmetric microphysics to atemporal agency speculative. But it then continues as though agent causation might operate atemporally independently of mechanism.

Three distinct claims need to be separated:

1. Fundamental equations may be time-reversal invariant.
2. A physical interpretation may allow retrocausal dependencies.
3. A non-physical agent may select an entire temporal history atemporally.

The first does not entail the second, and neither entails the third. Invoking atemporal selection cannot answer Libet’s timing problem until an interpretation and causal model have been specified.

### 1.10 Sartre is being recruited in the wrong direction

The article treats Sartrean self-distance and the phrase about being condemned to freedom as support for a substance-bearing agent. Sartre can indeed motivate non-coincidence with one’s current contents and a strong conception of responsibility. But his early account is explicitly hostile to the ego as an inner substantial source. The Routledge Encyclopedia summarizes his position as denying that the ego is an inner core or source of actions and treating it instead as a construct; the Stanford account likewise presents the ego as constituted in reflection rather than as the subject of pre-reflective consciousness.  ([source](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/sartre-jean-paul-1905-80/v-1/sections/early-philosophy))

Thus:

- Sartre can support the phenomenology of self-transcendence.
- He does not straightforwardly support a persisting mental substance exercising causal powers.
- His anti-egological position may actually intensify the challenge to the Map’s substance requirement.

The article should either remove Sartre from the argument for substance causation or explicitly acknowledge that it is appropriating one aspect of his phenomenology while rejecting his account of the ego.

## 2. Citation and quotation audit

### 2.1 Overall bibliographic integrity

I found no invented reference among the ten bibliography entries. Libet, Schurger, Desmurget, Sjöberg, Byrne, Suddendorf and Corballis, Read, Schwitzgebel, Wegner, and Gallagher and Zahavi all exist in substantially the cited forms.

The principal defects are instead:

- shortened or incomplete titles;
- body claims that go beyond what the cited source tested;
- failure to distinguish experiments, theoretical articles, books and essays;
- and omission from the bibliography of several sources doing major argumentative work.

### 2.2 William James: now accurate, but selectively contextualized

The repaired quotation is accurate. James wrote that the factual free-will issue concerns how much effort of attention or consent can be exerted. But the surrounding passage immediately says that free will is insoluble on strictly psychological grounds and that introspection cannot determine whether more or less effort could have occurred under the same antecedents.  ([source](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin26.htm))

The article uses James as an authoritative compression of its attention-based theory while omitting his epistemic caution. A fair use would add:

- the full bibliographic reference;
- the immediately relevant sentence about psychological insolubility;
- and a note that James presents effort as the location of the question, not as empirical proof of indeterministic agency.

James is still missing from the formal references.

### 2.3 Libet and Schurger: genuine but overcompressed

The Libet reference is real, although the bibliography gives only the opening fragment of its title. More importantly, Libet-style paradigms concern simple self-paced movements and retrospective timing reports, not ordinary reasoned choices.

Schurger et al. genuinely proposed that the average readiness potential can arise from stochastic neural accumulation aligned backwards on movement onset. But this is not equivalent to demonstrating that the readiness potential simply is meaningless noise, nor does it show that consciousness performs the final selection. Contemporary work continues to assign the readiness potential roles involving internal action generation, planning, expected effects and task demands.  ([source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33153362/))

### 2.4 Sjöberg: correctly cited, wrongly weighted

The Sjöberg paper exists with the reported journal, volume and pages. It is, however, a short **Brain essay**, not a newly reported controlled resection study.  ([source](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain%2Fawae180))

The current article’s added caveat—that Sjöberg removes a proposed defeater rather than establishes conscious causation—is welcome. Nevertheless, describing it as clinical evidence pointing in the same direction as Schurger gives it more positive evidential weight than warranted. SMA resection cases challenge a simplistic claim that the readiness potential is the seat of volition. They do not locate volition elsewhere or establish dualism.

### 2.5 Read and comparative cognition: source exists, inference does not follow

Read’s 2008 article argues that limited working memory may constrain recursive thinking in non-human primates. It does not establish that those species have reduced phenomenal consciousness, nor that counterfactual reasoning depends on phenomenal consciousness rather than working memory and representational architecture.  ([source](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/147470490800600413))

The free-will article’s phrase connecting reduced conscious capacity with deficient counterfactual working memory therefore conflates three claims:

- a difference in working-memory capacity;
- a difference in recursive or foresight abilities;
- a difference in consciousness.

Only the first two are even addressed by the cited comparative literature.

### 2.6 Rajan et al.: central but absent from the references

The frontal-theta and bidirectional-coherence claim comes from Rajan et al., yet that paper is not in the formal bibliography. Because it supplies one of the article’s three expressly identified lines of evidence for agent causation, this is not a minor omission. It should be cited locally and listed in full.

### 2.7 Other missing formal references

The article invokes or relies upon Chisholm, Sartre, Kim, Frankfurt, Fischer and Ravizza, Wolf, Deutsch and Wallace, and William James without providing complete bibliography entries. The many cross-links do not substitute for primary-source citation, particularly where the article attributes a specific argument or conceptual distinction.

## 3. Strong counterarguments missing or answered too quickly

### 3.1 The strongest compatibilist rival is not “endorsed desire without coercion”

That description fits an elementary family resemblance, not the best contemporary opposition.

#### Fischer and Ravizza

Their guidance-control theory says responsibility can arise when behaviour issues from the agent’s own moderately reasons-responsive mechanism, where ownership depends partly on the mechanism’s history. This directly predicts:

- reasons-sensitive behaviour;
- deliberative effort;
- differences between compulsive and owned action;
- learning and character development;
- and sensitivity to manipulation history.

It therefore accommodates much of the evidence the article counts for libertarianism without requiring categorical alternative possibilities.  ([source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/responsibility-and-control/responsibility-and-history/CFC73E6DE2541E3AD9E1FA766CB6226A))

#### Vihvelin

Vihvelin argues that experiences of trying, moving and exercising abilities are neutral regarding determinism, and develops a dispositional account on which relevant abilities can survive deterministic laws.  ([source](https://academic.oup.com/book/12362/chapter-abstract/161931717))

This is a direct answer to the article’s phenomenological question. A system can experience deliberation as exploration of alternatives because it possesses a flexible bundle of abilities and because representing alternatives is part of exercising those abilities. The experience need not reveal the modal structure of the complete world.

#### List

Christian List retains intentional agency, causal control and alternative possibilities while locating them at the agential level rather than among fundamental physical variables. This is particularly important because it blocks the article’s implied choice between conditional compatibilism and a dualist quantum interface.  ([source](https://www.harvard.com/book/9780674979581))

The article’s own compatibilism page recognizes these theories much more clearly than the hub article does.  The hub should import their strongest arguments rather than asking readers to follow links after the positive case has already been framed in libertarian terms.

### 3.2 The article does not answer the rollback objection

As discussed above, primitive agent causation does not by itself explain why an identical agent exercises A-directed rather than B-directed power in an identical prior state.

A full answer must distinguish at least:

- **outcome luck:** whether the result depends on chance;
- **present luck:** whether the agent controls which present possibility occurs;
- **contrastive luck:** what explains A rather than B;
- **constitutive luck:** the unchosen character, capacities and values from which the agent acts;
- **rollback luck:** what varies across identical replays;
- **achievement luck:** whether success is appropriately attributable to the agent.

The article mainly answers outcome luck by saying the agent, rather than randomness, causes the act. It does not answer contrastive or rollback luck.

### 3.3 Reasons-influence needs a weighting theory

Saying that reasons influence but do not determine the choice leaves open the crucial issue. Suppose reasons favour A 60:40:

- Does the agent apprehend numerical or ordinal weights?
- Can it choose B while fully recognizing that A is better supported?
- What determines how much influence each reason has?
- Does effort change the weights, select despite them, or constitute another reason?
- How is the selection reliably aligned with values rather than randomly correlated with them?

Without such an account, “reasons-guided but not determined” names the desired middle ground without explaining its control structure.

### 3.4 Manipulation and constitutive luck remain live

Even if a non-physical agent performs the final selection, its reasons, values, habits and attentional tendencies may have been formed by genetics, upbringing, coercion, pathology or covert manipulation. The article links to the manipulation argument but does not explain why an irreducible final selector transforms an engineered motivational profile into ultimate authorship.

This is especially pressing for the article’s claim that immediate, prereflective actions are authored in the same way as deliberative choices. If selection can be wholly spontaneous, the criterion separating authentic agency from implanted, compulsive or scripted selection becomes unclear.

### 3.5 The article targets a weak physicalist null

The article repeatedly contrasts agent causation with random fluctuations, post-hoc fiction, illusion or causal idleness. The strongest physicalist hypothesis is instead:

> Volition is a distributed, recurrent, reasons-sensitive control process whose conscious character is one aspect of the process itself.

Such a model predicts structured phenomenology, neural dissociations, effort gradients, clinical breakdowns, metacognitive opacity and different signals for self-selected versus externally instructed control. The article needs to compare its evidence against that model, not against a passive random-process caricature.

## 4. The post-Libet evidence is more mixed than the article indicates

### 4.1 What the recent literature supports

The crude popular conclusion—neural preparation starts first, therefore the unconscious brain has already decided and free will is disproved—is not warranted.

Several findings weaken it:

- Maoz et al. found that the classical readiness potential was absent or substantially diminished for deliberate, consequential decisions compared with arbitrary button choices.  ([source](https://elifesciences.org/articles/39787))
- A 2021 meta-analysis found only six studies addressing the crucial neural-onset versus reported-intention interval, with considerable uncertainty around the effect.  ([source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34119525/))
- A 2024 primer reviewed 2,220 volition-related publications and presents the field as methodologically and philosophically contested rather than settled by Libet.  ([source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763423004724))

Those results justify rejecting sweeping anti-free-will generalizations from arbitrary motor tasks.

### 4.2 What they do not support

They do not support the inverse inference:

> Libet failed to prove unconscious determination, therefore conscious non-physical selection is positively supported.

Removing evidence for one conclusion does not provide evidence for its metaphysical contrary.

Moreover, the post-Libet literature does not converge on a single noise account:

- Travers and Haggard found evidence relating the readiness potential to the internal source and planning of action rather than simply uncertainty.  ([source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33153362/))
- Gavenas, Schurger and Maoz reported that early readiness-potential buildup did not track reported awareness of preparation and modelled the results through metacognitive access to stochastic accumulation.  ([source](https://direct.mit.edu/imag/article/doi/10.1162/imag_a_00465/127590/Probing-for-intentions-The-early-readiness))
- Gärtner et al. found that readiness-potential amplitude varied with the detailed predictability of action outcomes.  ([source](https://doi.org/10.1162/imag_a_00546))
- Kong et al. found in 2026 that spatial distance, attentional focus and expected outcomes modulated different phases of readiness-potential dynamics and subjective action timing.  ([source](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/psyp.70280))

The emerging picture is of a context-sensitive family of preparatory and action-related signals. That is scientifically interesting, but it offers no known marker that discriminates physical executive control from a dualist selector.

### 4.3 Imperfect prediction is not an evidential opening for consciousness

The related Libet article argues that prediction accuracy around 60% means something other than measured neural activity contributes to the result.  The first half is true; the implied conclusion is not.

Unexplained variance can arise from:

- unmeasured neural variables;
- limited spatial or temporal resolution;
- classifier limitations;
- trial noise;
- participant heterogeneity;
- unstable strategies;
- measurement error;
- or genuinely stochastic physical processes.

A consciousness contribution is one possible explanation among many. It gains support only if it predicts a distinctive pattern of residuals that those alternatives do not.

## 5. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article is admirably explicit that it begins from the Map’s tenets. The problem is not having commitments; it is allowing those commitments to determine how contrary evidence is classified.

### 5.1 Mechanism failure triggers relocation rather than revision

The article says failure of a proposed mechanism **“would require finding an alternative gap”** rather than abandoning libertarianism. 

This creates a one-way evidential structure:

- Successful quantum evidence can support the framework.
- Failed quantum mechanisms do not count against the framework because retrocausality or unknown physics can replace them.
- Failure to observe the interface is explained by structural opacity.
- Difficulty locating causal precedence is answered by atemporal selection.
- Physical closure would have to be proved universally before counting decisively.

A theory may legitimately separate its metaphysics from a provisional mechanism. But failed mechanisms should still reduce confidence in the conjunction that motivated them, and replacements should make new, risky predictions. Otherwise the physical component functions as a succession of protective auxiliaries.

### 5.2 The proposed falsifiers are asymmetrically demanding

The listed challenges include complete neural sufficiency with no residual variance, a materialist solution to consciousness, universal proof of physical closure, and a reversal of effort/neural-cost covariation.

These are not well-balanced empirical tests:

- No finite neural measurement is likely to leave literally no residual variance.
- Physicalism does not predict perfect behavioural classifiers from current measurements.
- Universal causal closure at every scale is not the kind of proposition an experiment can conclusively prove.
- Solving the hard problem is not an operational experimental outcome.
- Effort and neural cost may correlate under both dualism and physicalism.

A better test would identify observations that the Map predicts at a substantially different probability than its best rival—for example, a preregistered statistical deviation in carefully isolated choice probabilities under manipulated conscious attention.

### 5.3 Opacity is repeatedly converted from limitation into fit

The decision void, agency void, self-opacity and causal-interface void correctly emphasize that introspection does not expose the machinery of choice. But the site frequently adds that this is just what minimal interaction predicts.

That risks a reversal:

1. The interface cannot be observed.
2. The theory predicts an unobservable interface.
3. Therefore the non-observation coheres with the theory.

Coherence is not positive confirmation unless rival theories predict observability. Physical and computational theories also predict that a system will lack introspective access to most of its implementation.

### 5.4 The MWI argument is framework-relative

The article defines authorship as requiring one outcome to become actual while alternatives do not, and then objects that branching interpretations do not provide this exclusion.

That shows a conflict between the Map’s conception of authorship and Everettian branching. It does not independently show that Everett is false or that branch-relative agency is incoherent. The article now comes closer to acknowledging this as a framework-boundary disagreement, which is preferable. It should avoid counting the same point both as a definition of agency and as independent evidence against Many Worlds.

### 5.5 The internal review process has hardened some disagreements into exemptions

A May review classified physicalist, compatibilist, Many-Worlds and quantum-sceptical objections as settled “bedrock” disagreements and explicitly instructed future reviews not to reflag several of them, including deeper Wegner engagement, inline Soon evidence and decoherence. 

Delegating detail to child articles can be sensible. The methodological danger is that:

- the hub derives support from claims in the children;
- objections are sent away to those children;
- and later reviews are instructed not to reopen whether the children adequately answer them.

A standing commitment should not become an exemption from renewed evidential comparison.

## 6. Currency relative to the 2020s literature

The bibliography is notably thin for an article last revised in 2026. Apart from Sjöberg 2024, its listed empirical sources end in 2012. That does not make the older sources irrelevant, but it leaves the reader without the research that most directly qualifies the article’s central neuroscience.

At minimum, the neuroscience discussion should add:

- Maoz et al. 2019 on deliberate versus arbitrary decisions;
- Braun, Wessler and Friese 2021’s Libet meta-analysis;
- Schurger, Pak and Roskies 2021 on the interpretation of the readiness potential;
- Travers and Haggard 2021 on internal action source and planning;
- Dominik, Mele, Schurger and Maoz 2024’s comprehensive primer;
- Gavenas, Schurger and Maoz 2025;
- Gärtner et al. 2025;
- and Kong et al. 2026.  ([source](https://elifesciences.org/articles/39787))

The philosophy discussion should add current work representing both sides, particularly:

- Kane’s 2024 agent/event-causal hybrid and explicit treatment of rollback;

# Concrete potential improvements

## To topics/free-will

   - self-selected versus externally instructed;

## To the site’s methodology

25. **Create a claim–source ledger.** For every load-bearing claim record the exact source, source genre, sample or argument, what it directly establishes, and what additional inference the article makes.
26. **Require rival-prediction tables.** Every empirical argument for dualism should state what the strongest physicalist, compatibilist and alternative-libertarian models predict.
27. **Ban residual-to-cause inference.** Unexplained variance, imperfect prediction or an unknown mechanism must not be counted as evidence for consciousness unless a distinctive residual signature is predicted in advance.
28. **Use attainable disconfirmation criteria.** Replace universal closure proofs and perfect neural prediction with preregistered, quantitative tests capable of materially lowering confidence.
29. **Record credence consequences.** Before proposing a mechanism, state how much its failure would reduce confidence in the larger framework. A replacement mechanism should not erase the evidential cost of its predecessor’s failure.
30. **Quantify the low-bandwidth controller.** Specify candidate-set size, information rate, timing, probability bias, amplification path and expected observable signature.
31. **Add automated cross-page consistency checks.** Assertions such as “exhaustive,” “confirms,” “proves,” “selection area” and “resolution” should be checked across the corpus when a central article is corrected.
32. **Run annual literature sweeps by cluster.** The free-will cluster needs a neuroscience and philosophy sweep keyed to primary literature, not just local cross-review.
33. **Make source genre visible.** Label empirical reports, reviews, essays, philosophical arguments and speculative proposals differently in article prose and reference metadata.
34. **Repair publication provenance.** Generate displayed modification dates and changelog entries from the same commit state, and verify that corrections have reached the public build before marking a task complete.

## Final judgment

The article is already a thoughtful and unusually self-aware statement of the Map’s preferred position. Its best contribution is to show why simplistic Libet-based dismissals of agency are inadequate and why phenomenology, reasons and executive-control distinctions deserve explanation.

It is not yet a successful evidential argument for agent-causal dualism. The cited data establish structured volition and differentiated control processes; they do not identify a non-physical persisting subject, a physical causal gap or consciousness-driven outcome selection. The article can become substantially stronger by presenting those latter claims as explicit abductive hypotheses, comparing them with the strongest live rivals, and defining evidence that could genuinely move the comparison in either direction.
