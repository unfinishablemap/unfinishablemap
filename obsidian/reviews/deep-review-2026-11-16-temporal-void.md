### Paraphrase findings

- **Hancock et al. 2019.** The page said the meta-analysis "finds that deep absorption consistently distorts time perception — hours pass in what feels like minutes". The abstract reports 63 articles, 1,094 effect sizes, and moderately positive correlations (r = 0.4, 0.21, 0.17) across affective, consciousness and performance aspects of flow. No "hours as minutes" result. Restated; "shows a trade-off" lowered to "is consistent with a trade-off". Seeded by duration note L133-L136.
- **Addis, Wong & Schacter 2007.** The page said "future simulation produces greater hippocampal activation than memory retrieval". The abstract: left hippocampus common to past and future construction; future construction uniquely engaged the right hippocampus, "possibly as a response to the novelty"; elaboration showed "remarkable overlap"; events "did not differ" on rated detail, emotionality, personal significance or perspective. Restated with the hemisphere, the phase and the matched ratings. "Differs categorically" is now marked as the Map's account. The earlier stability note protects the hedge about later multivariate work, which is kept.
- **Bergson and clocks.** "Clocks don't read themselves" is Evan Thompson's sentence (Aeon, 30 September 2024), glossing Bergson's argument about local simultaneity. The page gave it as Bergson's insistence and listed only Canales. Now "on Evan Thompson's (2024) reading"; Thompson added to the references. Seeded by duration note L73.
- **Husserl and metaphor.** The page placed the breakdown of description at "longitudinal intentionality". In Brough's text the namelessness is said of the absolute time-constituting flow: "For all of this, we have no names" (p. 382, count 1 in Husserl's text). Corrected and quoted.
- **Default Mode Network.** "DMN suppression correlates with time dissolution" has no citation; the note (L136) took it from a news-medical press summary about psychedelics. Scoped to "is reported to accompany time dissolution under psychedelics". **Not verified against a primary study.**
