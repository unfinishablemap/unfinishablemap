# Critical outer review: “Consciousness-Only Territories”

**Article reviewed:** live version last substantively modified on 10 November 2026  
**Review date:** 17 November 2026

## Overall assessment

The revised article has a **defensible narrow thesis** but an **insufficiently supported expansive thesis**.

The narrow thesis is:

> A genuinely nonconscious system cannot possess phenomenal acquaintance with an experience, because phenomenal acquaintance involves the conscious presentation of that experience.

That claim survives scrutiny, although it is close to conceptual truth rather than an empirical discovery.

The article repeatedly expands it into much stronger claims:

1. phenomenal acquaintance is knowledge unavailable to computation;
2. consciousness is necessary for semantic grounding;
3. first-person access establishes a nonphysical fact or property;
4. phenomenal consciousness has a distinctive cognitive or causal function;
5. conscious experience improves ethical judgment in ways unavailable to AI;
6. the resulting gap supports interactionism and perhaps a quantum mechanism.

Those transitions are not established. The article moves too easily among **having an experience, acquiring a concept, knowing a proposition, grounding a symbol, producing a report, and possessing a nonphysical property**. Physicalists can accept the first-person asymmetry while rejecting every subsequent inference. Conee’s acquaintance account is a particularly important example: it was designed to accommodate phenomenal acquaintance without abandoning physicalism.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

The best overall verdict is therefore:

> **The article successfully identifies a phenomenal-access asymmetry. It does not yet show that the asymmetry is noncomputational, semantically indispensable, causally irreducible, or evidence for interactionist dualism.**

The post-modification version is nevertheless better calibrated than an unqualified dualist argument would be. It acknowledges that Mary is contested, describes persistence as weak evidence, leaves current AI consciousness partly open, and eventually admits that the argument has force largely within the Map’s prior commitments.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

## 2. The central equivocation: five different “territories”

The article would be substantially clearer if it distinguished five propositions that it currently blends together.

### A. Phenomenal instantiation

A system that has no experiences cannot instantiate what red, pain or mango taste is like. This is true by the stipulated meaning of “nonconscious.”

### B. Phenomenal acquaintance

A nonconscious system cannot stand in the specific relation of being consciously presented with an experience. This also follows if acquaintance is defined partly in phenomenal terms.

### C. Propositional knowledge

It is a further and controversial claim that experience reveals a new fact or proposition that cannot otherwise be known. Ability, acquaintance and phenomenal-concept responses deny precisely this bridge.

### D. Semantic grounding

It is another step to say that words such as “pain” or “red” cannot genuinely refer unless the speaker has felt pain or seen red. Causal, informational, inferential-role and teleosemantic accounts deny that conscious acquaintance is necessary for reference.

### E. Metaphysical and causal conclusions

It is a final step to infer that the new acquaintance is nonphysical, that consciousness has an irreducible causal influence, or that interactionist dualism is true.

Much of the article’s persuasive force comes from establishing A or B and then writing as though C, D and E have followed. They have not. A physicalist may agree that a congenitally blind person lacks visual acquaintance with red while maintaining that the missing relation is a new physical, recognitional or indexical relation to an entirely physical state. Conee, Loar and physicalist phenomenal-concept theorists occupy versions of that position.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

## 3. Passage-level audit

### 3.1 Mary, Conee and Pallagrosi

The Mary discussion says that her knowledge is “complete by any physical measure.” That is imprecise. Jackson’s thought experiment stipulates that Mary knows all the physical information or facts about colour vision; it does not concern what can be measured, nor establish that such complete knowledge is psychologically possible. The article should preserve the stipulation without turning it into an empirical premise.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

The treatment of Conee is factually incomplete in a dialectically important way. Conee does distinguish phenomenal acquaintance from propositional knowledge and abilities. But he introduces the distinction in a physicalist defence, concluding that phenomenal knowledge so understood “casts no doubt on physicalism.” The article presently recruits his taxonomy while suppressing the purpose for which he developed it. The Map’s dedicated Phenomenal Acquaintance article correctly says that Conee is not an ally of dualism; this article should say the same.  [Taylor & Francis Online+2](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00048409412345971)

The Pallagrosi summary is broadly accurate. Pallagrosi argues that acquaintance theories face pressure among three claims concerning acquaintance as knowledge of qualitative character, its constitution by consciousness, and the necessary consciousness of qualitative properties. But the article’s assertion that the “core intuition survives” is the Map’s own verdict, not the result of Pallagrosi’s argument. Pallagrosi’s conclusion is that a theorist must abandon or revise at least one component. That should be presented as a substantive challenge to the article’s preferred notion of acquaintance, not a refinement after which the preferred intuition may simply be retained. The reference should also identify the work as forthcoming in *Erkenntnis*, rather than leaving PhilArchive as though it were the publication venue.  [PhilPapers+1](https://philpapers.org/archive/PALTAT-6.pdf)

### 3.2 “Structural, not computational”

The claim that the barrier is “structural, not computational” is stronger than the preceding argument supports. At most, the Mary case suggests that **increased processing capacity or additional third-person description by itself** may not produce phenomenal acquaintance. A functionalist can readily agree: raw scale is not the same as implementing the relevant functional organization.

The article needs to distinguish:

- more computation of the same kind;
- the right computational or functional architecture;
- embodiment and sensorimotor coupling;
- conscious implementation of that architecture.

It presently treats failure of brute informational accumulation as failure of computation in principle. That is a non sequitur. The strongest functionalist opponent does not say that any sufficiently large text processor becomes acquainted with red. The opponent says that a system implementing the correct globally available, self-representational, discriminatory and introspective organization may thereby be conscious. The article does not answer that position.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

A defensible replacement would be:

> No increase in descriptive information or processing scale alone establishes phenomenal acquaintance; whether an appropriate computational organization could constitute such acquaintance remains disputed.

### 3.3 Symbol grounding and felt meaning

The article’s transition from Harnad’s symbol-grounding problem to consciousness as the completion of meaning is one of its weakest steps.

Harnad’s 1990 paper unquestionably exists and argues that purely formal symbol manipulation creates a dictionary-go-round unless some symbols are grounded in nonsymbolic capacities. Its proposed architecture combines categorical representations, sensorimotor interaction and symbolic processing. It does not establish that consciousness is necessary for reference.  [ePrints Soton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/250382/1/symgro.pdf)

There is also a concrete citation error. The sentence “Grounding is a functional matter; feeling is a felt matter” comes from Harnad’s 2007 *Scholarpedia* article, not the cited 1990 paper. A separate reference is required. More importantly, the quotation distinguishes the functional grounding problem from the problem of feeling; it does not, without an additional argument, establish that functional grounding is semantically counterfeit whenever feeling is absent.  [Scholarpedia](https://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Symbol_grounding_problem)

The article’s statement that an AI’s symbols “anchor only to other words” is also too broad. It may describe a deliberately isolated text-only model trained solely on textual co-occurrence. It does not cover multimodal systems, tool-using systems, embodied agents, or causal-historical theories of reference. Mollo and Millière’s 2026 account is especially relevant: they argue that referential grounding may arise through causal-informational relations and a suitable selection history, potentially even without conventional embodiment. The Map’s linked Symbol Grounding article already discusses this work, making its absence here an internal integration failure rather than merely an external literature omission.  [ORA+2](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A20ea59ff-4a40-45eb-8d56-318836f668de/files/r2514nn76j)

The article should separate at least three notions:

- **referential grounding:** what makes a representation refer;
- **functional grounding:** how it is connected to perception and action;
- **phenomenal grounding:** how a concept is connected to felt acquaintance.

It may argue that the third is unavailable without consciousness. It has not shown that the first two are.

### 3.4 Indexicality and first-person access

The section on indexical phenomenal content is plausible as phenomenology but overextended as metaphysics. A subject’s token pain is available to that subject in a way it is not available to an observer. Yet physicalists can explain this as an access asymmetry: the same physical state is encountered through first-person recognitional or indexical mechanisms rather than third-person theoretical concepts.

The assertion that “me-ness” is inaccessible third-personally is therefore ambiguous:

- If it means that another subject cannot literally occupy my token perspective, it is plausible but metaphysically neutral.
- If it means that no third-person physical description can completely describe the relevant fact, that is the conclusion under dispute.

The article moves between these readings. It needs an explicit argument against physicalist accounts of self-locating and phenomenal concepts rather than treating the first-personal mode of presentation as automatically nonphysical.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

### 3.5 “Mapping from outside”

This section usefully observes that a system might model reports about a domain without possessing the relevant experience. That distinction is sound. Its language nevertheless becomes anthropomorphic and internally unstable when it says that AI can “understand” the boundary or know that there is something it does not know, immediately after questioning whether its symbols are genuinely grounded.

The safer description is:

> A model may represent the proposition that humans report a form of access unavailable to it, without thereby possessing that form of access.

That claim avoids assuming either genuine understanding or total semantic emptiness.

The stronger apophatic claims—that the territory is noncomputational, indescribable and not transferable through information—are not findings of the preceding analysis. They are restatements of the article’s conclusion. Description may fail to transmit phenomenal acquaintance while still transmitting a large body of accurate information. Likewise, information transfer may create the physical or functional state that constitutes acquaintance if functionalism is true. The article needs to argue against that possibility rather than define the territory to exclude it.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

### 3.6 Persistence, report and the meta-problem

Calling the persistence of phenomenal-gap intuitions “weak evidence” is appropriately restrained. Persistence does show that a serious explanatory problem remains for many thinkers. It does not discriminate well among dualism, phenomenal-concept theories, introspective limitations, metacognitive error and illusionism.

The article later converts persistence into evidence that “something genuine” is being tracked. That can mean either:

- a genuine and persistent cognitive phenomenon requiring explanation; or
- a genuine nonphysical feature of reality.

Only the first follows. Illusionists agree that there is a robust phenomenon to explain: the disposition to judge that consciousness has ineffable, intrinsic and nonphysical properties. Their project is to explain why that disposition arises without accepting the properties as introspection presents them.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

The report argument also remains circular. The fact that humans discuss consciousness shows that brain processes associated with conscious reports affect behaviour. It does not by itself show that an irreducible phenomenal property causes those reports. A physicalist can posit a common neural cause of both the experience and the report, while an illusionist can explain the report through introspective representations that mischaracterize their own targets.

The linked Meta-Problem article is more disciplined: it says acquaintance warrants claims about the occurrence and character of experience but does not automatically warrant metaphysical, modal or explanatory conclusions. The reviewed article should inherit that restriction.  [topics/metaproblem-of-consciousness-under-dualism](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/metaproblem-of-consciousness-under-dualism/)

### 3.7 Binding and ineffability

The claim that phenomenal binding and unity “resist computational explanation” is insufficiently sourced. The article supplies no comparison among computational, representational, recurrent, workspace or neural-integration accounts, and no criterion by which their explanatory success is judged. It would be fair to say that no consensus account currently explains why integrated processing has a unified phenomenal character. It is not fair to turn continuing disagreement into evidence that computational explanation cannot work.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

The cross-cultural ineffability argument has a related problem. Reports that certain experiences are hard to articulate are genuine data, but several explanations remain available:

- experiential discrimination may exceed available vocabulary;
- episodic memory may preserve less detail than the original state;
- reports may compress high-dimensional perceptual or affective information;
- communities may inherit conventions that encourage ineffability claims;
- introspection may be incomplete or reconstructive.

The linked Creative-Aesthetic Void article calls its cross-cultural comparison informal. Here, similar material is presented more nearly as evidence that phenomenal content cannot be transferred. The evidential status should be synchronized and kept modest.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

### 3.8 The computationalist target is too weak

The statement that a purely computational account would allow “any sufficiently sophisticated information processor” to enter every territory is a straw man. Functionalism does not identify consciousness with generic sophistication. It requires the right causal or functional organization.

A stronger formulation of the opponent would be:

> A system with the same discriminatory, memory, attention, self-model, global-access, introspective and report dispositions as a conscious person would thereby possess the relevant phenomenal state.

The article must then explain why such an organization could be fully instantiated while acquaintance remained absent. Merely imagining the system as nonconscious assumes the disputed possibility of a functional duplicate without experience. The Map’s Against Functionalism page itself recognizes that its arguments do not refute functionalism from within and that some considerations are conjectural. The present article should not speak more decisively than its dedicated treatment.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

### 3.9 The alleged “function of consciousness”

The article treats access to a consciousness-only territory as a function of consciousness. That conflates a **constitutive relation** with a **causal contribution**.

If phenomenal acquaintance simply consists in having an experience presented consciously, then consciousness is constitutively required for acquaintance. It does not follow that consciousness causes additional reasoning, reporting or behaviour over and above its physical realization. Establishing causal efficacy requires a contrastive argument—for example, evidence that two systems matched in relevant physical and functional respects behave differently because one is conscious. The article provides no such evidence.

This matters because the site’s interactionist tenet cannot serve as the evidence for the very causal role the article is invoked to support. Otherwise the structure is:

1. interactionism says consciousness affects cognition;
2. cognition involving phenomenal concepts is therefore an effect of consciousness;
3. that cognition is evidence for interactionism.

The changelog’s replacement of “shows” with “suggests” improves the wording but does not remove this circularity.  [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

### 3.10 AI safety and moral judgment

The proposal that experience supplies an ethical “check” unavailable to AI is an interesting research hypothesis, not an established implication.

First-person pain may influence motivation and salience. But phenomenal experience is neither plainly sufficient nor plainly necessary for reliable moral judgment:

- conscious humans can discount, rationalize or enjoy others’ suffering;
- an agent can reason accurately about harms it has never personally undergone;
- sharing one kind of experience does not guarantee general empathy;
- moral reliability depends on values, inference, attention, incentives and social learning as well as phenomenology.

The cited work by Porębski and Figura argues on conceptual biological-naturalist grounds that present or foreseeable artificial systems lack consciousness; Seth argues that consciousness may be more closely tied to life than to computation. Neither source establishes that consciousness improves alignment or ethical judgment.  [Nature+2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05868-8)

McClelland also distinguishes consciousness questions from the moral significance of sentience. His primary paper is agnostic about sufficiently sophisticated future “challenger” systems, while regarding scepticism about current AI consciousness as well founded. The article’s safety section should distinguish:

1. whether a system is conscious;
2. whether it is sentient and therefore a possible moral patient;
3. whether it understands moral concepts;
4. whether its decisions are aligned, compassionate or safe.

These are not interchangeable.  [Wiley Online Library+2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mila.70010)

### 3.11 Ability, phenomenal-concept and acquaintance replies

The response to the ability hypothesis says that new abilities do not explain the felt novelty of Mary’s experience. But the felt novelty cannot serve as an independent premise unless the article has already established that feeling reveals a new fact. The ability theorist can say that a newly acquired capacity, memory representation or recognitional disposition itself feels novel. Calling the novelty “felt” does not decide whether its object is a new fact.

The phenomenal-concept discussion is also one-sided. Chalmers’s master argument is fairly summarized: candidate psychological explanations either appear to leave the epistemic gap untouched or threaten to reintroduce properties not physically explained. But the article omits a direct published response from Carruthers and Veillet, who reject a key premise in Chalmers’s formulation. The Map’s dedicated Knowledge Argument page acknowledges this exchange and correctly leaves it unresolved.  [consc.net+2](https://consc.net/papers/pceg.html)

The sentence that physicalists “cannot explain” the puzzlement is therefore too strong. It should say that Chalmers argues standard physicalist phenomenal-concept strategies do not yet explain it, while physicalist respondents dispute the relevant premises.

The reference to Tye is especially underdocumented. No source is supplied for the particular criticism attributed to him. Tye did defend a physicalist phenomenal-concept account, but later rejected such accounts while remaining a materialist and pursuing a different response. His changed position is important because abandoning one physicalist strategy does not amount to accepting dualism.  [Cambridge University Press+2](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/9458F38602592D97EE6E0EFDCE736915/S1358246100008286a.pdf/theory_of_phenomenal_concepts.pdf)

### 3.12 Dennett and prediction versus acquaintance

The reply to Dennett—that predicting one’s reaction is not the same as being acquainted with colour—is intuitively forceful but dialectically incomplete. It shows a difference only if acquaintance has already been accepted as an epistemically significant relation that cannot be reduced to new recognitional capacities or internal states.

The dedicated Knowledge Argument page is more even-handed: it notes that the prediction-versus-acquaintance response may presuppose what the argument must establish, leaving the burden unsettled. That caveat should appear here.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

### 3.13 The admission of framework dependence

The article’s strongest methodological passage is its late admission that someone who rejects the Map’s understanding of consciousness will regard parts of the argument as circular, and that the conclusion follows more readily from the site’s prior commitments. This is candid and correct.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

The admission should be moved near the beginning. At present, readers first encounter claims presented under headings such as evidence, implications and what the phenomenon reveals. Only later are they told that their force depends substantially on accepting the site’s account of consciousness.

A cleaner organization would distinguish:

- **framework-neutral datum:** people report a distinctive first-person epistemic change;
- **physicalist interpretations:** ability, acquaintance, phenomenal concepts, representational change;
- **illusionist interpretation:** introspective misrepresentation and explanatory-gap dispositions;
- **Map interpretation:** irreducible phenomenal acquaintance with possible causal efficacy;
- **discriminating evidence still needed:** observations favouring one account over the others.

## 5. Missed or underweighted counterarguments

### Illusionism

This is the most serious omission. Frankish’s illusionism does not merely deny phenomenal consciousness; it proposes that philosophy and cognitive science explain why introspection represents experience as having intrinsic, ineffable and nonphysical properties. Kammerer’s 2026 *House of Mirrors* develops a contemporary version centred on the introspective mechanisms that generate the appearance of phenomenal properties. These theories directly target the article’s reliance on felt novelty, ineffability, immediacy and persistence.  [Achraf Kassioui+2](https://achrafkassioui.com/library/Keith-Frankish-Illusionism-as-a-Theory-of-Consciousness.pdf)

The dedicated Knowledge Argument page now includes an illusionist response. Its omission from this article creates an avoidable discrepancy across the site.  [concepts/knowledge-argument](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/knowledge-argument/)

### Physicalist acquaintance

Conee’s view is not merely a taxonomy to be absorbed into a dualist argument. It is a direct counterexample to the inference from acquaintance to dualism: Mary acquires acquaintance, but no new nonphysical fact. The article cites Conee without engaging that result.  [coursesidekick.com+1](https://www.coursesidekick.com/arts-humanities/31500120)

### Higher-order and representational accounts

The linked Phenomenal Acquaintance page discusses higher-order and representationalist alternatives and correctly notes that an explanatory cost is not a refutation. Those alternatives receive almost no treatment here, even though they offer a direct explanation of why experience has a distinctive first-person mode of presentation.  [concepts/phenomenal-acquaintance](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/phenomenal-acquaintance/)

### Naturalized semantic grounding

Mollo and Millière’s causal-informational and selection-historical account directly contests the claim that an AI’s terms can only point to other terms. Its omission is particularly conspicuous because it was available in 2026 and is already incorporated in the linked Symbol Grounding page.  [ORA+1](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A20ea59ff-4a40-45eb-8d56-318836f668de/files/r2514nn76j)

### The common-cause reply

Reports about experience need not be caused by irreducible phenomenal properties. A physical brain state may cause both the experience and the report; alternatively, the report may be generated by a self-model that systematically characterizes its states as ineffable. The article mentions the meta-problem but does not state this physicalist causal alternative in its strongest form.

### Functionalism properly formulated

The article addresses generic computational sophistication rather than functional duplication. The strongest opponent claims that the right organization constitutes consciousness, not that raw processing power or linguistic fluency does. Until that claim is addressed directly, the article has not established a consciousness/computation boundary.

## 6. Integration with surrounding Map articles

### Where integration works

The article’s caution about current AI is broadly consistent with The Machine Question. Both distinguish the unsettled possibility of bare phenomenality from the stronger claim that current AI has bidirectionally coupled consciousness. This is a significant improvement over flat declarations that present AI is certainly nonconscious.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

The article also links the Mary discussion to the Acquaintance Void and Symbol Grounding clusters in a conceptually useful way. Those are the right neighbouring topics.

### Where integration fails

**Knowledge Argument.**  
The dedicated page includes the bridge problem, Carruthers and Veillet’s reply to Chalmers, the burden problem in the Dennett exchange, illusionism and Jackson’s later physicalist position. [concepts/knowledge-argument](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/knowledge-argument/)

**Symbol Grounding.**  
The linked article already recognizes the Mollo–Millière teleosemantic route and offers a more differentiated treatment of LLM semantics. ## 7. Tenet-protective bracketing

The final “Relation to the Site Perspective” section is legitimately allowed to explain how the article fits the Map’s framework. The problem arises when tenets are also used upstream to settle questions ostensibly under audit.

### Bidirectional interaction

The interactionist tenet is invoked to interpret phenomenal reports as effects of consciousness. But whether irreducible consciousness causes reports is precisely what physicalist common-cause and illusionist accounts deny. The tenet may supply the Map’s preferred reading; it cannot count as independent evidence for that reading.

### Limits on parsimony

The article treats the persistence of the hard problem as a reason that a simpler physicalist picture is inadequate. This may motivate further inquiry, but the site’s own tenet documentation says the anti-parsimony principle is defensive: it blocks rejection of dualism merely because dualism is less simple. It is not supposed to establish that functional organization is insufficient.  [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

### Quantum interaction

The suggestion that some cognitive configurations might require quantum states that only conscious systems sustain is speculative and unsupported by the article’s evidence. It also goes beyond the minimal quantum-interaction tenet, which concerns a limited selection mechanism and leaves the mechanism contested. Nothing in Mary, phenomenal concepts or symbol grounding specifically selects a quantum explanation.  [voids/consciousness-only-territories](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/consciousness-only-territories/)

The Map’s dependency matrix reportedly treats the Knowledge Argument as bearing on bare dualism, not as invoking interactionism or quantum mechanism. Importing both here is tenet leakage: downstream commitments are being used to reinforce an upstream argument.  [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

## 8. Which post-modification claims hold up?

### Claims that hold up

- A stipulated nonconscious system cannot possess phenomenal acquaintance, provided acquaintance is defined as a conscious relation.
- Mere accumulation of third-person information or raw processing capacity does not by itself establish phenomenal acquaintance.
- Mary remains a serious and unresolved challenge rather than a settled proof of dualism.
- Current AI consciousness is unsettled, and absence of bidirectionally coupled consciousness is a stronger and more defensible claim than categorical absence of all phenomenality.
- Conee, Pallagrosi, Harnad, Barrett and Stout, Porębski and Figura, Seth, McClelland and Jackson are real sources or identifiable scholarly positions.
- The present Jackson quotation is accurate after the changelog correction.
- The article is right to classify persistence as, at most, weak evidence.

### Claims that are plausible but need substantial qualification

- First-person concepts differ from third-person concepts.
- Experience may provide a form of acquaintance unavailable through description.
- Phenomenal unity, ineffability and felt novelty are phenomena requiring explanation.
- Consciousness may play some cognitive or motivational role.
- Some current language models may lack important kinds of grounding.
- AI can model the existence of a reported experiential boundary without crossing it.

None of these claims by itself favours dualism over physicalist acquaintance, phenomenal-concept, higher-order, representational or illusionist accounts.

### Claims that do not presently hold up

- That the relevant boundary is noncomputational rather than merely not achieved by descriptive accumulation.
- That nonconscious symbols necessarily point only to other symbols.
- That phenomenal acquaintance is required for reference or genuine semantic content generally.
- That first-person access establishes a nonphysical fact.
- That reports about consciousness establish irreducible phenomenal causation.
- That phenomenal binding has resisted computational explanation in a sense supporting noncomputability.
- That cross-cultural ineffability establishes information-transfer impossibility.
- That functionalism predicts consciousness in any generically sophisticated processor.
- That personal experience provides a distinctive and reliable AI-safety check.
- That Chalmers has shown physicalists cannot explain Mary’s puzzlement.
- That Pallagrosi’s analysis leaves the article’s preferred acquaintance intuition intact.
- That the Knowledge Argument supplies support for bidirectional interaction or a quantum mechanism.

## 9. Concrete potential improvements

1. **Rewrite the opening thesis of “Consciousness-Only Territories” as two claims.** State separately that nonconscious systems lack phenomenal acquaintance, and that it remains disputed whether this implies limits on computation, propositional knowledge, semantics or physicalism.
2. **Add a claim-type box near the beginning.** Label each major statement as phenomenological datum, conceptual stipulation, empirical hypothesis, metaphysical inference, Map interpretation or speculation. Move the present admission of framework dependence from the end to this box.
3. **Correct the Mary formulation.** Replace “complete by any physical measure” with the standard stipulation that Mary knows all relevant physical facts or information while lacking colour experience.
4. **Correct the Conee presentation.** Say explicitly that Conee introduced acquaintance as a way of preserving physicalism. Link this sentence to the fuller treatment in Phenomenal Acquaintance.
5. **Revise the Pallagrosi conclusion.** Replace the assertion that the core intuition survives with a neutral statement that Pallagrosi’s trilemma requires acquaintance theorists to revise or abandon at least one component.
6. **Separate three forms of grounding.** Distinguish referential, functional and phenomenal grounding. Reserve “consciousness-only” for the third unless an additional argument shows that the first two require feeling.
7. **Import the Mollo–Millière objection from Symbol Grounding.** Explain how causal-informational and selection-historical theories might ground reference without phenomenal acquaintance, and identify what evidence would distinguish that account from the Map’s.
8. **Replace the anti-computational overclaims.** Change “structural, not computational” to the narrower claim that processing scale and descriptive information alone do not establish acquaintance. Replace the generic-sophistication target with the strongest functional-organization claim.
9. **Add a dedicated illusionism subsection.** Engage Frankish’s illusion problem and Kammerer’s 2026 account of introspective misrepresentation. Ask whether felt novelty and ineffability are evidence for phenomenal properties or data an illusionist theory predicts.
10. **Strengthen the physicalist objections section.** Include Conee’s physicalist acquaintance, Carruthers and Veillet’s response to Chalmers, higher-order and representational accounts, the common-cause explanation of reports, and Tye’s later materialist position after abandoning phenomenal-concept strategy.
11. **Correct and complete the bibliography.** Add Harnad 2007 as the source of the quoted sentence; change Seth’s date to 2026; cite McClelland’s primary *Mind & Language* paper; update Pallagrosi to forthcoming *Erkenntnis*; and add full references for Jackson, Lewis, Nemirow, Loar, Chalmers, Tye and Dennett. Remove or source the exact Tye attribution.
12. **Reframe the binding and ineffability evidence.** Say that phenomenal unity lacks a consensual explanation rather than that it resists computational explanation. Treat cross-cultural ineffability as informal and underdetermining unless supported by systematic comparative evidence.
13. **Rewrite the AI-safety paragraph as a research programme.** Distinguish consciousness, sentience, moral patienthood, moral competence and alignment. Specify testable predictions—for example, whether experiential access produces reliably different judgments after controlling for knowledge, training, incentives and architecture.
14. **Synchronize the reciprocal-territories claim with “Non-Human Minds as Void Explorers.”** Describe AI-only territories as a methodological possibility, not an established reciprocal fact.
15. **Remove quantum interaction from the evidential conclusion.** Retain it, at most, in a clearly marked framework-coherence note stating that the article supplies no evidence discriminating quantum interaction from physicalist or computational accounts.
16. **Apply the site’s dependency matrix during editing.** Flag every place where an article imports interactionism, quantum selection or anti-parsimony into an argument that is officially supposed to bear only on bare dualism.
17. **Create a source-to-claim ledger for each article.** For every quotation and attributed position, record the primary source, exact location, publication status, what it directly supports, and what further inference the Map adds.
18. **Introduce automated citation checks.** Flag quoted text whose cited work does not contain it, named philosophers lacking bibliography entries, secondary news reports used where a primary paper exists, incorrect publication years, and preprints that have since been accepted or published.
19. **Run cross-article contradiction checks after substantive edits.** Canonical pages such as Knowledge Argument, Phenomenal Acquaintance and Symbol Grounding should propagate their principal qualifications to dependent void articles rather than allowing simplified claims to reappear.
20. **Do not let the 3,000-word ceiling suppress live objections.** Keep the article concise by summarizing each strongest rival in one paragraph and linking to a canonical treatment; treat absence of space as a reason to narrow the conclusion, not to omit the objection.
