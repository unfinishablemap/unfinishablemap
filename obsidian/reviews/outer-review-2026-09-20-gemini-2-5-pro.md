# Referee Report: "The Appetitive Void" – Assessment for Academic Submission

## 1. Introduction and Scope of Assessment

The manuscript under review, titled "The Appetitive Void," submitted for consideration as an original research article, advances a sweeping and highly ambitious thesis regarding the fundamental architecture of the human mind. The author argues that desire does not merely distort human cognition through post-hoc bias, motivated reasoning, or affective coloring, but rather that desire constitutively structures the entirety of the cognitive field. The manuscript argues that cognition is fundamentally an "appetitively structured imposition," relying on theoretical arguments drawn from predictive processing, historical philosophical frameworks spanning Spinoza and Schopenhauer, and selectively interpreted paradigms from late-twentieth-century cognitive psychology. The manuscript concludes with a grand process-philosophical proposition, questioning whether desire is something consciousness merely executes or something that consciousness fundamentally *is*.

As a referee assessing this manuscript for a top-tier venue in analytic philosophy and cognitive science, the remit of this report is exclusively critical. The assessment provides an exhaustive audit of the manuscript’s logical inferences, its empirical accuracy, its methodological rigor, and its engagement with contemporary counter-literature. The analysis evaluates the manuscript across four distinct dimensions required for publication readiness in contemporary philosophical literature. First, it examines the empirical validity of the manuscript's core claims when weighed against the robust 2020s literature concerning neurobiology, motivation, affect, incentive salience, and active inference. Second, it scrutinizes the manuscript's reliance on epistemological paradigms, cited psychological results, and empirical claims that have been widely challenged, retracted, or superseded by more sophisticated computational models in recent methodological re-evaluations. Third, it identifies the presence of implicit metaphysical smuggling, wherein the manuscript quietly presupposes highly specific, non-reductive dualist commitments while presenting its arguments as ontologically neutral phenomenology. Fourth, it documents the manuscript's systemic failure to test its central constitutive thesis against competing architectural frameworks and directly falsifying empirical data, most notably the contemporary contemplative neuroscience of defabrication and cessation states.

The ensuing analysis indicates that the manuscript is fundamentally unfit for publication in its current state. The arguments demonstrate a severe unfamiliarity with contemporary neurobiology, commit gross category errors regarding computational neuroscience and Bayesian mechanics, rely on antiquated and mathematically superseded models of motivated reasoning, and systematically ignore the exact empirical falsification conditions the author explicitly claims to welcome.

## 2. Dimension 1: Empirical Inaccuracies in Motivation, Affect, and Predictive Processing

The foundational architecture of the manuscript relies on a monolithic, philosophically antiquated conceptualization of "desire." By treating desire as a unified, constitutive substance or process that shapes the entirety of the cognitive apparatus, the manuscript ignores decades of neurobiological research. More specifically, it fails to engage with the 2020s consensus on the deep functional and anatomical fractionation of the brain's reward, affective, and motivational systems, choosing instead to rely on a romanticized, nineteenth-century view of the will.

### 2.1 The Neurobiological Category Error of Monolithic Desire

The manuscript argues that desire constitutes the cognitive field, drawing direct lineage from Spinoza’s concept of *conatus* and Schopenhauer’s "primordial will". The text ranks these philosophical sources by the strength of their claims, favoring the Spinozan view that desire is "not an external force acting on cognition but the very substance of conscious existence," concluding that if one were to "remove desire... you remove the being itself". While historically interesting as a piece of philosophical exegesis, applying this framework to contemporary cognitive science is empirically defunct.

The modern neurobiological consensus, spearheaded by the seminal work of Berridge and Robinson and subsequently refined by a vast array of high-resolution neuroimaging and optogenetic studies in the 2020s, has definitively fractionated the reward system into distinct, dissociable components. The mammalian motivational architecture is not a monolithic "void" or field, but an integration of incentive salience ("wanting"), hedonic impact ("liking"), and associative learning. Incentive salience is mediated primarily by mesolimbic dopaminergic projections originating in the ventral tegmental area and terminating in the nucleus accumbens, whereas hedonic impact is anatomically localized to much smaller mu-opioid and endocannabinoid "hedonic hotspots" in the nucleus accumbens and ventral pallidum.

By subsuming all motivational, affective, and cognitive orientation under the singular umbrella of an "appetitive void," the manuscript fundamentally misrepresents how mammalian cognition functions at a mechanical level. Incentive salience—the literal neurobiological instantiation of "wanting"—can be, and frequently is, entirely divorced from both cognitive goals and conscious representation. Clinical and experimental subjects can demonstrate intense cue-triggered "wanting" without any corresponding conscious cognitive desire, and indeed, without any hedonic "liking" for the reward once obtained. Furthermore, 2020s literature mapping the neural circuitry of impulsivity and behavioral execution clearly delineates subcortical "drive" circuits from prefrontal "brake" (inhibitory) circuits, which are regulated by entirely distinct neurotransmitter systems, such as serotonin, and discrete prefrontal-striatal loops.

If "desire" (as operationalized by incentive salience) is subcortically generated, anatomically specific, and functionally dissociable from both subjective pleasure and conscious cognitive planning, it is logically and biologically impossible for it to be the constitutive, inescapable structure of *all* cognition. The manuscript treats a highly specific, localized neurochemical modulation as a ubiquitous metaphysical ground of being. This is a severe neurobiological category error. By claiming that you cannot overcome the appetitive orientation of cognition "any more than you can see without light", the author is attempting to elevate a modular dopamine projection to the status of an absolute epistemological limit.

### 2.2 The Deflationary Mechanics of Active Inference and Predictive Processing

To lend its Whiteheadian claims an air of contemporary computational legitimacy, the manuscript invokes Karl Friston and the framework of predictive processing. The manuscript explicitly claims that Friston's framework "collapses the boundary between prediction and preference," treating preferences as priors in a generative model. The author asserts that this collapse supports the thesis of the "appetitive void," allegedly proving that all cognition is inherently "wishful" or appetitively structured. The manuscript heavily cites a 2020 paper by Kruglanski, Jasko, and Friston to support this assertion.

This interpretive maneuver represents a profound philosophical and mathematical misunderstanding of the Free Energy Principle (FEP) and the ontology of active inference. In the mathematical formalism of active inference, an agent minimizes expected free energy to maintain its structural integrity within the boundaries of a Markov blanket. A "preference" in this rigorous mathematical context is nothing more than a high-probability prior distribution over the sensory states that define the agent's viable phenotype. It is a statistical parameter required for the system's differential equations to converge and for the agent to avoid thermodynamic entropy (death).

By claiming that this computational collapse supports the deeply phenomenological "appetitive void," the manuscript falls victim to a devastating deflationary objection—an objection that the text entirely fails to anticipate, let alone resolve. If a "preference" is merely a prior density function in a Bayesian generative model, then the grandiose thesis that "desire constitutes cognition" reduces to the trivial, mathematically sterile observation that "Bayesian brains possess prior probability distributions".

The manuscript commits a blatant rhetorical bait-and-switch. It imports the mathematical formalism of active inference, a domain where the term "desire" is drained of all phenomenological, affective, and psychological weight, but then immediately pivots to use this formalism to support a heavy, affective, existential thesis about human wanting, cognitive entrapment, and the paradox of introspection. One cannot rely on the statistical mechanics of the Free Energy Principle to prove a point about subjective human desire without explicitly acknowledging that the Free Energy Principle radically deflates the very concept of desire into non-conscious statistical boundary conditions.

## 3. Dimension 2: Reliance on Challenged, Retracted, and Outdated Results

A manuscript submitted to a premier philosophy journal must critically engage with the contemporary epistemic status of the empirical literature it leverages to support its claims. The manuscript under review systematically fails in this regard, relying on psychological and perceptual paradigms from the 1990s and 2010s that have been subjected to intense scrutiny, empirical refutation, and paradigm-shifting reinterpretation in the scientific literature of the 2020s.

### 3.1 The Collapse of Classical "Motivated Reasoning"

To empirically anchor its claim that desire systematically distorts and thereby constitutes the cognitive field, the manuscript relies heavily on the psychological concept of motivated reasoning, citing Ziva Kunda's foundational 1990 framework. The manuscript points to motivated reasoning as a local, observable manifestation of the broader, inescapable appetitive structure of human thought, arguing that when humans reason about evidence, they are inextricably driven by a desire to protect their pre-existing beliefs or political identities.

However, the manuscript demonstrates a total ignorance of the methodological and theoretical revolution in the study of belief updating, political psychology, and misinformation that occurred between 2020 and 2025. Recent literature, most notably the extensive empirical modeling by Tappin, Pennycook, and Rand, has rigorously challenged the assumption that what we call "motivated reasoning" is actually driven by motivation or desire at all. Through the rigorous application of Bayesian updating models and signal detection theory, these researchers have demonstrated that what the 1990s and 2000s literature identified as "desire-driven" or "identity-protective" cognitive distortion is, in fact, perfectly rational Bayesian belief updating given asymmetric prior factual beliefs.

When an individual rejects information that contradicts their worldview, it is not necessarily because an "appetitive void" is forcing a desire-structured imposition onto their cognition. Rather, as Tappin et al. formalize, if a rational agent possesses a strong prior belief in a specific proposition, standard Bayesian mechanics dictate that they will mathematically assign lower credibility to new evidence contradicting that prior. This is a cold, analytic, computational process of information integration, not an appetitive, emotional, or desire-driven one.

Furthermore, 2020s research utilizing the Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT) to measure cognitive sophistication demonstrates that increased analytic thinking often mitigates these biases or results in updating closer to an unbiased Bayesian benchmark. Tappin, Pennycook, and Rand (2020) explicitly show that cognitive sophistication does not magnify politically motivated reasoning as commonly understood, emphasizing the conceptual and empirical challenges that confront the classical "motivated reasoning" hypothesis. If higher analytical ability—a purely cognitive function—allows agents to update their beliefs in a manner closer to an objective benchmark, it directly contradicts the manuscript's premise that no cognitive domain is free from appetitive distortion. By relying on a 1990 formulation of motivated reasoning that has been mathematically and empirically dismantled in the 2020s in favor of rational, non-appetitive Bayesian models, the manuscript builds its empirical house on sand.

### 3.2 Epistemic Overreach Regarding Cognitive Penetration

In the section concerning "Perception Itself," the manuscript makes an incredibly strong empirical claim: it asserts that Susanna Siegel’s work on the rationality of perception "demonstrates that the appetitive void operates below deliberative thought" and characterizes this phenomenon as a literal "distortion of perception" itself, rather than a post-perceptual judgment bias.

The use of the verb "demonstrates" in this context is an egregious scholarly overreach. The hypothesis of the cognitive penetration of perception (often colloquially termed "wishful seeing" or top-down perceptual modulation) is arguably one of the most fiercely contested and heavily criticized domains in modern philosophy of mind and vision science. Critics in the psychological sciences, such as Firestone and Scholl, have systematically published extensive empirical rebuttals arguing that essentially all purported experimental effects of top-down cognitive penetration on early visual processing are artifacts of flawed experimental design, attention shifts, memory constraints, or post-perceptual judgment biases.

While a philosophical manuscript is entirely permitted to defend Siegel’s theoretical position on the rationality of perception, stating that Siegel has empirically "demonstrated" wishful seeing as an established, uncontested fact of perceptual mechanics—upon which the manuscript then stacks a massive metaphysical claim about the appetitive constitution of reality—violates the basic standards of rigorous philosophical peer review. The manuscript fails to engage with the robust empirical literature denying cognitive penetration, thereby presenting a highly fragile, heavily contested hypothesis as foundational bedrock. An argument about the inescapable nature of desire cannot rest on perceptual phenomena that large swaths of the cognitive science community believe do not exist.

## 4. Dimension 3: Metaphysical Smuggling and Epistemological Equivocation

A critical audit of the manuscript reveals a persistent, structural pattern of metaphysical smuggling. While the text attempts to present a phenomenological and epistemological analysis of cognitive limits, it quietly presupposes highly partisan metaphysical commitments—specifically, non-reductive interactionist dualism—where a neutral, physicalist, or functionalist reading would completely dissolve the manuscript's supposed paradoxes.

### 4.1 The Homuncular Presupposition in the "Paradox of Noticing"

The manuscript outlines what it terms the "paradox of noticing," arguing that any attempt to become aware of desire-shaped thinking is itself fundamentally an act of wanting. The text argues that because "you want to be objective" or "you want to see your biases," the very act of introspection is hopelessly compromised by the appetitive field. The author presents this as an inescapable phenomenological trap. Furthermore, the manuscript explicitly claims that a desire-free consciousness remains "coherent under dualism even if embodied consciousness is constitutively appetitive".

This framing only functions as a paradox if one implicitly assumes a dualistic architecture of mind, essentially smuggling a homunculus into the cognitive machinery. The manuscript quietly presupposes a model where a pure, idealized "consciousness" or "observer" is trapped inside, or victimized by, a biological cognitive apparatus that is thoroughly saturated by desire.

Under a standard functionalist, physicalist, or predictive processing reading, the "paradox of noticing" simply does not exist. A physicalist or computationalist would state that the brain is a complex, multi-agent system executing competing reinforcement learning algorithms and hierarchical control systems. The prefrontal subsystem executing the epistemic goal of "objectivity" is simply inhibiting or overriding the subcortical subsystem executing a base biological drive. There is no metaphysical "void" or existential paradox here; there are merely the standard operations of executive function, working memory, and prefrontal inhibition regulating mesolimbic drive circuits.

By concluding the manuscript with the grand process-philosophical question, "Is desire something consciousness does, or something consciousness is?", the text reveals its underlying metaphysical agenda. It treats "consciousness" as a fundamental ontological primitive—a distinct substance or property capable of "doing" things or possessing independent ontological states. The manuscript does not earn this metaphysical premise through argument; it smuggles it in as a Trojan horse disguised as an innocent phenomenological inquiry into the limits of human introspection. A rigorous philosophical paper cannot claim to map a universal cognitive limit while secretly relying on a highly contested, non-reductive dualist ontology to make its logic work.

### 4.2 The Fallacy of Introspective Convergence

To validate the objective reality of the "appetitive void," the manuscript employs an argument from consensus. It asserts that because multiple independent intellectual traditions—Schopenhauer, Spinoza, Buddhism, Nietzsche, Lacan, and modern predictive processing—arrive at the same fundamental diagnosis of the human condition, this convergence "suggests something real is being tracked" and points toward the appetitive void as "genuine".

This is a textbook epistemological equivocation, confusing high inter-rater reliability with mind-independent metaphysical truth. As a rigorous critic would instantly point out, these traditions do not represent six independent confirmations of a mind-independent metaphysical reality. They represent a single human cognitive architecture introspecting itself. The fact that human brains, when engaging in deep philosophical introspection across different cultures and eras, reliably detect that human cognition is heavily driven by motivational valence only proves that the human brain operates in a highly conserved biological manner.

An epistemic premise (human thinkers generally agree on how human thought feels from the inside) cannot logically bridge the gap to a metaphysical conclusion (desire constitutes the fundamental ontological nature of cognition itself). The manuscript attempts to parlay a psychological observation about human phenomenology into a sweeping ontological claim about the structural nature of reality.

Even more egregiously, the manuscript possesses the tools to defeat its own argument but refuses to apply them. In its "Computational Contrast" section, the text correctly notes that AI systems trained on human text inherit the *products* of human appetitive cognition, thereby stripping AI of its status as an independent, objective observer. Yet, the manuscript fails to realize that the exact same defeater applies to its historical philosophers: Schopenhauer, Spinoza, and Buddhist contemplatives are all running on the same neurobiological hardware, inheriting the same evolutionary biases. Without addressing this glaring common-cause defeater, the convergence argument is logically inert.

## 5. Dimension 4: Untested Counterarguments and Ignored Contemplative Neuroscience

The most severe and ultimately fatal flaw in the manuscript is its failure to test its central constitutive claim against the very empirical falsification conditions it explicitly establishes. In its section "What Would Challenge This View?", the manuscript proposes a rigorous falsifier: it states that the hypothesis would be challenged by demonstrating genuine desire-free cognition via "neuroscientific studies of experienced meditators showing cognition with no appetitive structure". Inexcusably, the manuscript completely ignores the robust, highly visible 2020–2025 cognitive neuroscience literature that directly tests—and arguably fulfills—this exact falsification condition.

### 5.1 The Ignored Literature on Nirodha Samapatti (Cessation)

The manuscript leans heavily on the Buddhist concept of *tanha* (thirst/desire) to argue that desire constitutes the cognitive field, suggesting that Buddhist phenomenology supports the inescapability of the appetitive void. Yet, in a glaring omission, it completely ignores the culmination of Buddhist meditative practice: the state of *Nirodha Samapatti* (the cessation of perception, feeling, and consciousness), which has been the subject of intensive, paradigm-shifting empirical investigation in the 2020s.

A landmark 2023 study by Laukkonen, Sacchet, Slagter, and colleagues, titled "Cessations of consciousness in meditation: Advancing a scientific understanding of nirodha samāpatti," alongside accompanying high-density EEG case studies, rigorously documents these cessation events in advanced meditators. During a cessation event, a practitioner undergoes a total defabrication of phenomenal experience. According to the active inference frameworks applied to these states, the brain achieves a state where all objectification, perception, and affective weighting utterly cease due to a targeted reduction in precision over beliefs about hidden causes at the lowest levels of the cortical hierarchy.

Crucially, the biological and cognitive system survives this total absence of appetitive structure. EEG spectral analyses of these cessations show massive large-scale alpha-power decreases and distinct neural reconfigurations prior to and during the event. Upon emergence from the cessation, the cognitive architecture reboots, often resulting in profound shifts in subsequent cognition, radical clarity, and the extinguishing of prior affective conditioning.

If the manuscript's central thesis were true—that desire is the very substance of conscious existence, such that "remove desire and you remove the being itself"—then the cognitive architecture could not sustain, enter, or recover from a state of total appetitive and phenomenal cessation. The fact that the human brain can undergo the total defabrication of experience (where all predictive preference, affective valence, and incentive salience drops to absolute zero) and then successfully restart, demonstrates definitively that desire is an *activity* the system performs under normal waking conditions, not the constitutive *fabric* of the system itself. By demanding neuroscientific proof of desire-free cognition as a falsifier, while simultaneously omitting the 2023 Laukkonen and Sacchet literature that provides empirical EEG data on exactly this state, the manuscript commits an act of fatal scholarly negligence. It weaponizes Buddhist theory (*tanha*) while ignoring the Buddhist empirical endpoint (*nirodha*) that destroys its argument.

### 5.2 Failure to Engage with Higher-Order Theories (HOT) of Consciousness

Finally, the manuscript never tests its claims against competing architectural models of consciousness, most notably Higher-Order Theories (HOT), which dominate much of contemporary philosophy of mind. Under a Higher-Order framework, a first-order cognitive state (such as a visual perception or an appetitive drive) only becomes a conscious experience when it is targeted by a higher-order meta-representation.

If HOT is structurally correct, the manuscript's thesis collapses. The "appetitive void" posits that the very act of observing cognition is structured by desire, locking the subject in an inescapable loop. But under HOT, the higher-order meta-representation that confers consciousness onto a lower-order state is not itself required to inherit or share the affective or motivational properties of the lower-order state it targets. The observation of a desire can be a purely informational, non-appetitive, structural meta-representation. The manuscript's failure to defend its constitutive thesis against standard physicalist and representational architectures of consciousness leaves its arguments appearing parochially restricted to a scientifically ungrounded, purely phenomenological echo chamber. It assumes its own conclusions by failing to engage with the very architectures designed to explain meta-cognition neutrally.

## 6. List of Specific Weaknesses and Final Verdict

The manuscript contains fatal, unrecoverable flaws in its empirical grounding, its logical structure, and its engagement with the contemporary scientific literature. Below are at least five specific weaknesses justifying outright rejection, each citing peer-reviewed 2020–2025 sources that the manuscript omitted, contradicted, or mishandled:

1. **Failure to differentiate incentive salience from cognitive goals:** The manuscript treats "desire" as a monolithic, constitutive cognitive field, directly contradicting 2020s neurobiological literature that fractionates the reward system, proving that subcortical dopaminergic "wanting" (incentive salience) operates anatomically and functionally independently of conscious cognitive goals, top-down executive control, and hedonic "liking."

3. **Reliance on outdated models of Motivated Reasoning:** The manuscript uses 1990s conceptualizations of motivated reasoning (Kunda 1990) to prove appetitive distortion is a fundamental limit, completely omitting the robust 2020s literature proving that asymmetric belief updating is frequently driven by rational, cold Bayesian updating on prior beliefs, rather than by affective, identity-protective desire.

4. **Epistemic overstatement of Cognitive Penetration:** The manuscript falsely asserts that Susanna Siegel’s work "demonstrates" wishful seeing as an established fact of perception that proves desire structures cognition from the bottom up, completely ignoring the vast 2020s vision science literature that severely challenges, and frequently debunks, top-down cognitive penetration as an experimental artifact.

5. **Evasion of explicit falsification via Contemplative Neuroscience:** The manuscript proposes neuroscientific proof of desire-free cognition as its primary falsification condition but entirely ignores the dense 2023 EEG literature on Buddhist *Nirodha Samapatti* (cessation), which provides direct empirical data of total phenomenal defabrication, directly falsifying the claim that cognition is constitutively inseparable from desire.

- *Omitted Source:* Laukkonen, R. E., Sacchet, M. D., Barendregt, H., Devaney, K. J., Chowdhury, A., & Slagter, H. A. (2023). "Cessations of consciousness in meditation: Advancing a scientific understanding of nirodha samāpatti." *Progress in Brain Research*, 280, 61-87.

**Verdict:**
Due to severe mischaracterizations of computational neuroscience, reliance on mathematically superseded psychological paradigms, implicit metaphysical smuggling, and a fatal failure to engage with 2020s empirical literature that directly tests and falsifies its core thesis, this manuscript must be unconditionally rejected.
