## Executive Summary

The article's headline scope claim — that **three** arguments are "only supplementary against first-personally centred variants" — is a generalisation the source article does not make and List's own text does not support. `arguments/many-worlds-argument` attaches that qualifier to the **indexical argument alone**, in both its table row and its prose; the apex widened it to all three, in the frontmatter thesis, the lead paragraph and the Synthesis. Worse, per List's own abstract the probability and consciousness arguments are not *weakened* against his view — they are *inapplicable*, because List's centred worlds are "first-personal realizers of a shared third-personal world" and neither argument targets anything but multiplicity of physical outcomes. The overclaim has already been banked outward as **P-I5** in the positions register, where the justification clause names only the indexical argument while the conclusion covers three. Two further defects compound it: the article states Tenet 4's demand two inequivalent ways and uses each where it helps, and its Evidence-and-Dependency audit — the section built to prevent exactly this — omits one of the three arguments it is auditing and mislabels another as tenet-independent.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: A per-argument qualifier generalised to three arguments, then banked in the register

- **File**: `obsidian/apex/one-world-wager.md` (frontmatter `apex_thesis`; lead paragraph; §Synthesis) and `obsidian/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood.md` (P-I5)
- **Severity**: **High**

The apex asserts, three times, that all three arguments are variant-relative:

> "three independent arguments — indexical, probability, consciousness — are strongest against branch-egalitarian Everettianism but only supplementary against first-personally centred variants" (`apex_thesis`, and near-verbatim in the lead)

> "Its full strength covers branch-egalitarian Everettianism only" (§Synthesis)

The source article says something narrower. Its cumulative-case table attaches the qualifier to **one row**:

> | Indexical Problem | ...decisive against branch-egalitarian MWI, supplementary against List's centred worlds |
> | Probability Problem | The Born rule remains unexplained or circularly assumed |
> | Consciousness Unaddressed | The hard problem remains; unity of experience is unexplained |

and its prose sentence explicitly *separates* the treatments:

> "the indexical one decisively for branch-egalitarian variants, and for List's centred variant only alongside the separate rejection of modal realism; **the probability argument challenges its technical adequacy**"

No variant qualifier attaches to the probability or consciousness rows anywhere in the source. The article's own §"The Hardest Target" likewise argues the point for the indexical argument only — "**The indexical argument** was built against branch-egalitarian Everettianism... The argument does not reach List as it reaches Everett" — and then the Synthesis restates the conclusion in the aggregate. The demonstration covers one conjunct; the claim covers three.

This is a synthesis-introduced overclaim of the kind apex articles exist to avoid, and it is not contained: **P-I5** (added 2026-08-21, "drawn whole from [[apex/one-world-wager]]") carries the aggregate form as a live position, and its own text shows the seam:

> "the three arguments are supplementary only, **because the first-person privilege the indexical argument demands is built into the ontology**"

The stated ground licenses the demotion of one argument and is applied to three. Confirming the diagnosis: all three of P-I5's "Would shift if" triggers are indexical-keyed (the indexical argument extended; the centred-worlds ontology shown incoherent; the branch-relative identity programme answering the centred-subject charge). None mentions probability or consciousness. P-I5 is a position about the indexical argument's reach wearing an aggregate title.

- **Recommendation**: Restore the source's per-argument scoping in all four loci (thesis, lead, Synthesis, P-I5). Do **not** edit `arguments/many-worlds-argument.md` — it is already correct, and it is over its hard ceiling and blocked on an open human length decision.

### Issue 2: Against List the two other arguments are not "supplementary" — they are inapplicable, and that is worse

- **File**: `obsidian/apex/one-world-wager.md` §"The Case, Honestly Counted" / §"The Hardest Target"
- **Severity**: **High**

Verified against List's published abstract (Crossref, `10.1111/nous.12408`): the centred worlds are

> "distinct 'first-personal realizers' of a **shared 'third-personal world'**, where the latter is supervenient"

The article itself states this correctly ("a first-personal realiser of one shared third-personal world"), then fails to draw the consequence. The probability argument as the article states it targets "a theory in which all outcomes occur" that "must still recover the Born statistics". The consciousness argument targets "a framework in which consciousness has nothing to select — **because everything happens**". List's third-personal world is *shared and single*: on his view it is not the case that everything happens. Neither argument has a target.

So "supplementary" is the wrong word in the charitable direction. Against List the case is not three arguments diminished — it is **one argument that does not reach, plus a separate framework-level rejection of modal realism**, with the other two silent. That is a materially thinner position than the article registers, and the article's virtue is supposed to be that it counts honestly.

- **Recommendation**: State plainly that against centred variants the probability and consciousness arguments do not engage (List does not multiply outcomes), so the case reduces to the modal-realism rejection at a framework boundary. This *strengthens* the article's credibility — it is a sharper self-applied discount than the one already there — and it makes §"The Hardest Target" say what it has actually shown.

### Issue 3: Tenet 4's demand is stated two inequivalent ways, and each is used where it helps

- **File**: `obsidian/apex/one-world-wager.md` §"What the Tenet Actually Demands" vs §"The Hardest Target"; mirrored in P-I5
- **Severity**: **High**

Two statements, one tenet name:

- **(a) Outcome-uniqueness** — §What the Tenet Actually Demands: "Tenet 4 forbids coexisting equally-real branches and demands that **exactly one outcome is globally actual** — that the alternatives this subject does not experience are nonactual absolutely."
- **(b) World-uniqueness** — §The Hardest Target: "Tenet 4 insists on **global uniqueness — reality consists of one world, not many real ones**."

These come apart precisely at the article's hardest target. List satisfies (a): one shared third-personal world, no alternative outcomes. List violates (b): many centred worlds, all real. And the article relies on each reading in a different place — (a) is what licenses Griffiths's consistent histories as a Tenet-4-satisfying ally and what fixes "what the tenet actually demands"; (b) is what makes List a target at all and what the modal-realism rejection is said to protect. The switch is never noticed, and it is doing the load-bearing work.

The same equivocation is already in the register: P-I5's corollary says "Tenet 4 requires *global single-outcome actuality*", while its **Depends on** line says the case against centred variants rests on "the source of the **global-uniqueness** insistence". Both in one entry.

Note also that §"A Tenet Partly Generated by a Question" identifies Tenet 4 with List's quadrilemma horn 4, whose wording (confirmed in `topics/vertiginous-question` L104 and List's abstract) is "reality consists of one world, not many" — a claim about *worlds*, not outcomes. So the quadrilemma placement commits the Map to (b), the strictly stronger reading, while §"What the Tenet Actually Demands" advertises (a) as the careful statement.

- **Recommendation**: State the demand as a conjunction — single global outcome **and** world-uniqueness — and say which conjunct each downstream use invokes: outcome-uniqueness for the interpretation survey (consistent histories, many-minds), world-uniqueness for the quadrilemma placement and the case against List. If the Map does not want (b), then List is not a Tenet 4 violator and §"The Hardest Target" has no target.

### Issue 4: "It does not presuppose the tenets" is contradicted three times over — inside the section built to catch that

- **File**: `obsidian/apex/one-world-wager.md` §"Evidence and Dependency"
- **Severity**: **High**

> "The indexical argument is independently argued — **it does not presuppose the tenets** — but it does presuppose the metaphysical reading of indexical facts and a non-deflationary subject"

Against three other passages in the same article:

1. §Relation to Site Perspective: "**Dualism** supplies the first-person realism that makes the vertiginous question a datum rather than a confusion."
2. §A Tenet Partly Generated by a Question: "The Map's commitments pick **first-person realism (Tenet 1)**" — first-person realism *is* Tenet 1, explicitly.
3. §Not a Physics Tenet Alone gives the accurate, narrower version: the non-deflationary subject is endorsed "on independent grounds in its agency and subjecthood work, **not from within Tenet 4**."

(3) is right; the Evidence section widened "not from within Tenet 4" to "does not presuppose the tenets", which (1) and (2) refute. The indexical argument needs the vertiginous question to be a datum; Tenet 1 supplies that; so the argument presupposes a tenet.

This matters more than a wording slip because of where it sits. The Evidence-and-Dependency section closes with "Coherence among tenet-derived clauses contributes no independent weight, and this synthesis claims none for it." Mislabelling the Map's *central* objection as tenet-independent is exactly the double-count that sentence promises to prevent. The register is more honest than the apex here: P-I2's discussion calls this an "architectural inversion" and records that Tenet 4's principal argument is supplied by the register rather than the other way round.

- **Recommendation**: Replace with the accurate scoping — "argued without presupposing Tenet 4, but resting on the first-person realism Tenet 1 supplies and on a non-deflationary subject defended in the agency cluster; it therefore contributes no weight independent of Tenet 1." Cross-reference P-I2, which already says this.

### Issue 5: The dependency audit silently omits one of the three arguments it is auditing

- **File**: `obsidian/apex/one-world-wager.md` §"Evidence and Dependency"
- **Severity**: **Medium**

The section grades: the probability disputes (*externally evidenced*), the indexical argument (*independently argued*), the modal-realism rejection and the global-actuality demand (*inherited from Tenet 4*), and the three self-applied discounts. The **consciousness argument** — one of the three the article counts as the tenet's support — is never graded.

The omission flatters the count, because that argument is the most likely to be tenet-inherited. As stated in §"The Case, Honestly Counted" its second clause is "a framework in which consciousness has nothing to select... also faces the standing worry of how consciousness, **causally idle**, reliably reports on physics." Causal idleness is only a defect if consciousness must be causally efficacious — Tenet 3. Against an Everettian who does not hold Tenet 3, that clause is framework-boundary marking, not an in-framework objection. If it grades as *mutually coherent with the framework* rather than *independently argued*, the honest count of independent lines against Everett is closer to two than three — and the article's signature move is its count.

- **Recommendation**: Add the consciousness argument to the audit with an explicit grade, and reconcile the resulting grade with the "three independent arguments" headline. If it grades tenet-inherited, say so and adjust the count; that is the same self-applied discount the article already performs on preferred-basis and extravagance.

## Counterarguments to Address

### Wallace's ill-formedness verdict is granted without being tested

- **Current content says**: "That reply does not refute the programme on its own terms — Wallace can consistently rule the global indexical question ill-formed — so the disagreement here is a framework boundary, and the Map says so."
- **A critic would argue**: The downgrade to boundary-marking is taken one step too early, and the step not taken is available *inside* the Everettian's framework. The article's own lead grants Everettianism its advertised credential — "mathematically conservative" — which is the claim that the ontology is read off the unitary formalism with nothing added. The verdict that the global indexical question is ill-formed is not read off the formalism; it rests on a functionalist account of persons and branch-relative identity that is a substantive addition. The Map can press that the conservativeness covers the *physics* and not the *theory of persons*, and that the Everettian is therefore paying an ontological price the advertisement conceals. (Stated as a test to run, not a verdict: Wallace does argue for the functionalism, and the question is whether that argument is available on Everettian resources alone or imports an independent commitment.)
- **Suggested response**: Run the test explicitly in §"The Case, Honestly Counted". If the addition is genuine, the boundary is narrower than the article concedes; if Wallace earns it, saying so makes the concession stronger rather than merely asserted.

### The provenance of a concession is converted into epistemic standing

- **Current content says**: "it is worth noticing that the discount was self-imposed by the source article's audit, not extracted by a critic"; and in the Synthesis, "every discount in the preceding paragraph was applied by the Map's own audits before any external critic demanded it. A constitutional commitment whose registration includes its own limits is harder to dislodge than one that claims victory — there is less false confidence to strip away."
- **A critic would argue**: Who noticed a weakness has no bearing on the weight of what survives it. "Harder to dislodge" is a rhetorical property of a text, not an evidential property of a position — a well-hedged claim is harder to attack *and no more likely to be true*. The article then cashes this into "**better defended than its critics assume**," an assertion about unnamed critics with nothing behind it. Two passages of self-congratulation for candour is where a Popperian will say the wager stops being costed and starts being sold.
- **Suggested response**: Keep the discounts, delete the credit-claiming. The audits speak for themselves; the sentence claiming they earn the tenet extra standing is the one sentence in the article a critic can quote against it.

### The wager's comparison metric is framework-internal, so it cannot be the comparative bet it claims to be

- **Current content says**: "The Map bets that a single actual outcome undergone by a singular subject is a better place to owe the selection debt than a branching ontology in which nothing is excluded and no subject is singular — better because the debtor still exists as a debtor... It is a comparative bet about where to hold an unpaid bill."
- **A critic would argue**: A comparative bet needs a metric both parties recognise. The metric here is that a framework which keeps the vertiginous question askable is better than one which dissolves it — and the article has already conceded, under Tenet 5, that "even the claim that the indexical question is meaningful is a posited premise, not a neutral datum." If the premise is posited, the comparison is internal, and the "wager" is a statement of preference dressed in decision-theoretic vocabulary. The Everettian's reply is not that the bill is paid but that there is no bill.
- **Suggested response**: Either mark the wager as explicitly internal — a preference conditional on the posit, not a bet adjudicable from outside — or supply the neutral consideration that makes question-preservation a shared virtue. The former is cheaper and matches the article's register elsewhere.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

Everything here stands on the vertiginous question being a datum, and the article concedes it is a posit. Strip the posit and what remains is a framework organised around keeping a folk-psychological question askable — "the tenet protects the explanandum; it does not supply the explanation" is the article's own phrasing, and it is precisely what a research programme optimising for the survival of its vocabulary rather than for truth would say. The concession that choosing one world "does not *answer* the vertiginous question" is offered as honesty; read from outside, it is the admission that the tenet buys nothing but the persistence of a question.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

The indexical argument's datum is "you find yourself in exactly one" — a *report*, and branch-relative identity predicts each successor makes exactly that report. The article knows this: §"What the Tenet Actually Demands" states that "branch-local experience does not discriminate between the readings, since each branch-successor anticipates one outcome and gets one outcome either way." An argument whose central datum is conceded non-discriminating cannot also be "the Map's central objection." One of those two sentences has to give, and the article keeps both.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

Unusually careful — no decoherence-timescale argument is run against Everett, correctly, since einselection is conceded to both sides. But the concession bites harder than admitted. If einselection is granted, the Map's post-decoherence selection programme needs that same machinery to deliver a definite outcome, and the register's P-Q10 records that no worked toy model exists. So the "symmetric debt" is not symmetric in *maturity*: Everett has derivations that are contested (Mandolesi, Dawid–Friederich), the Map has none to contest. The article's own structural asymmetry argument — "collapse readings postulate the Born rule honestly, while Everett must earn it" — cuts back, because postulating is cheaper than deriving and the Map has not shown its postulate is compatible with a selecting consciousness.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

Issue 3 is my opening. If Tenet 4's demand is single-outcome actuality, then Everett is the target and List is not, and the article's hardest section is aimed at a view that satisfies the tenet. If the demand is world-uniqueness, then Tenet 4 has no quantum-mechanical content at all — it is a preference about how to count worlds — and presenting it as the Map's most physics-exposed commitment is a category error. The article cannot have both, and the double-edge section reads like a framework noticing this and calling it a draw.

### The Empiricist (Popper's Ghost)

Check the shift conditions. P-I5's three "Would shift if" triggers are all internal: an argument extended, an ontology shown incoherent, a charge answered from inside. Not one is empirical. The article's "wager" language promises a comparative bet; a bet with no state of the world that settles it is a preference. And the section that grades evidential standing rates only one item as "externally evidenced" — the Everettian probability disputes — which is to say the only external evidence in the registration is a controversy inside the *opponent's* literature about whether the opponent's derivation works.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

The Madhyamaka route is registered as "bedrock disagreement it cannot refute from outside," which is honest but understates the damage. If the I is an aggregate of conditions that already includes the branch, the vertiginous question fails to get a grip — and so does the consolation the whole wager rests on. "Better because the debtor still exists as a debtor" presupposes a debtor. Deny the persisting singular subject and there is no bill to hold, no better place to hold it, and no wager. The article's closing argument presupposes exactly what the deflationary route denies, so it cannot be offered as the neutral ground the "comparative bet" framing needs.

## Strengths (Brief)

Worth preserving through any revision:

- **The citation apparatus is genuinely clean.** Both List DOIs verify at Crossref down to volume, issue and pagination; the quadrilemma's four horns match List's published abstract exactly; the characterisation of List's modal realism and its "only one present per subject" qualifier are faithful to the source.
- **The Hare/List contrast is drawn with real care.** Hare's presence is correctly described as a *monadic* property, which is precisely what distinguishes it from List's subject-relative presence. A less careful article would have collapsed the two.
- **The self-applied discounts are real.** Retiring the preferred-basis objection because einselection is shared with the Map's own selection programme, and refusing to press ontological extravagance because Tenet 5 forbids it, are substantive concessions correctly reasoned. The problem identified in this review is not that the article concedes too little — it is that it concedes slightly less than its own materials require (Issue 2) and then claims credit for conceding (Counterargument 2).
- **The double-edge section is the best thing in the article.** Recognising that the intuition motivating Tenet 4 can be honoured by *multiplying* worlds, and stating it as the tenet's hardest problem rather than burying it, is the kind of move that makes a registration trustworthy.
- **§"Not a Physics Tenet Alone" is correct where §"Evidence and Dependency" is not.** The fix for Issue 4 is already written, in the article, one section earlier — it just needs to govern.
