## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. Wheeler, Stuss & Tulving (1997) quote — dropped word `subjective`. FIXED.**
Article had: "…become aware of their protracted existence across **time**."
Source (*Psychological Bulletin* 121(3):331-354, p. 335, verified in the Rotman Research Institute copy of the published paper): "Autonoetic consciousness is the capacity that allows adult humans to mentally represent and to become aware of their protracted existence across **subjective time**."
No ellipsis marked the omission. The dropped word is the one carrying the philosophical weight — Tulving's contrast between *subjectively experienced* time and physical time (the distinction he draws explicitly in the 2002 K.C. discussion: "He knows… what most other people know about physical time… It is his apprehension of subjectively experienced time… that is grossly impaired"). Fixed, and the article now states the contrast rather than relying on the reader to notice it.
**Corroboration that the correct wording was already known to the corpus:** `obsidian/topics/memory-channel-interface-evidence.md` carried the quote with "subjective time" intact all along.

**2. Klein quote cited to the wrong work. FIXED.**
Article attributed to **Klein (2016)**: "Memory has been designed by natural selection not to relive the past, but rather to anticipate and plan for future contingencies."
The sentence is **verbatim from Klein (2013)**, *The temporal orientation of memory: It's time for a change of direction*, *Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition* 2(4):222-234 (DOI 10.1016/j.jarmac.2013.08.001), where it is the paper's thesis statement: "As I hope to show, memory has been designed by natural selection not to relive the past, but rather to anticipate and plan for future contingencies — a decidedly future-oriented mode of subjective temporality."
It is **not** in the Klein 2016 abstract, and Klein 2016 is a conceptual analysis rather than the evolutionary reframing the article's lead-in described. Re-attributed to Klein (2013); reference entry added. Klein (2016) retained for the claim it actually makes.
**Origin traced:** `obsidian/research/autonoetic-consciousness-2026-02-24.md` files this quote under its "### Klein (2016)" heading. The research note was the source of the misattribution. Annotated in place so the defect cannot re-propagate.

**3. Tulving "blank" quote cited to the wrong work. FIXED.**
Article: `Asked about tomorrow, his mind was "blank" (Tulving, 2002).`
**Verified by direct grep of the primary text** (Tulving 2002, *Annual Review of Psychology* 53:1-25): `blank` occurs **0 times**; `tomorrow` occurs **0 times**. What Tulving 2002 actually says is "when asked, he cannot tell the questioner what he is going to do later on that day, or the day after" — and it cites **(Tulving 1985b)** for the finding. Tulving 2002's own reference list resolves 1985b as "Memory and consciousness. Can. Psychol. 26:1-12" — precisely the Tulving (1985) entry already in this article's references.
Re-cited to Tulving (1985). The other K.C. claims in that bullet *are* Tulving 2002's, so the bullet now carries both citations, each on the claim it supports.
*Restraint noted:* K.C.'s reply is reported in the secondary literature as "Blank, I guess" (Tulving 1985, p. 4). I did **not** extend the quotation to the fuller form, because I could not reach Tulving 1985's primary text and a quotation must not be lengthened on secondary-source authority. The single-word partial quote is safe and is what the article now carries.

**4. Dokic (2014) — polarity inversion plus wrong framework bucket. FIXED.**
Article had: "Dokic's (2014) two-tiered account exemplifies the **epistemic** strand: he distinguishes the episodic content recalled from the metacognitive **'feeling of pastness'** that marks it as remembered, locating autonoesis in the latter."
Both halves contradict the source cited two sentences earlier. Verified in the primary text of Sant'Anna, Michaelian & Andonovski (2024), §4.1.1:
- Dokic (2014a, 2014b) is placed in **§4 METACOGNITIVE ACCOUNTS**, not §5 Epistemic accounts (whose members are Martin 2001, Soteriou 2008, Hoerl 2022).
- Dokic's term is the "**episodic feeling of knowing**" (2014a: 413).
- The paper states outright that Dokic *rejects* the pastness framing: "**Unlike Dokic**, they maintain that autonoesis is best characterized as a feeling of pastness rather than as a feeling of knowing," and "**By Dokic's lights**, the feeling of pastness pertains to the temporal orientation of memory, whereas the episodic feeling of knowing pertains to the relationship between memory and past experience." The "feeling of pastness" belongs to **Perrin and colleagues**.
The article's own sentence was internally incoherent as well — labelling the account "epistemic" while calling its mechanism "metacognitive." Rewritten to the source's actual taxonomy, with Dokic's distinction from the pastness view stated so the inversion cannot recur.
**Regression traced to the coalesce:** the archived predecessor `archive/concepts/autonoetic-consciousness.md` had this **right** ("Metacognitive accounts… Dokic's (2014) 'episodic feeling of knowing'… exemplifies this approach"), and so does the research note ("Dokic (2014) proposes a related 'episodic feeling of knowing'"). The coalesce that produced the current article introduced the error.

**5. Suddendorf & Corballis (2007) — empirical claim mis-stated. FIXED.**
Article: "This capacity emerges **between ages four and six** (Suddendorf & Corballis, 2007)."
Verified by grep of the full 53-page BBS target article: `six` / `age 6` / `six years` occur **0 times**. Corrected to "around age four," and the prose now names S&C as *reviewing* developmental evidence rather than reporting an experiment.
**Where the 4-6 window legitimately comes from:** `obsidian/voids/infant-consciousness.md` attributes it to **Perner & Ruffman (1995)** — a different and plausibly correct source. The figure was right; it had been attached to the wrong paper. ### Medium Issues Found

- **Wixted & Mickes mis-framed as a single-process model. FIXED.** The article described them as modelling "recognition as a single memory-strength variable," which collides with their paper's own title (*A continuous **dual-process** model*) and with their explicit refusal to abandon dual-process theory: the model "does not deny either the validity of dual-process theory or the possibility that remember/know judgments can — when used in the right way — help to distinguish" recollection from familiarity. Direction of the article's use was right; the framing overstated the deflation. Re-framed, not deleted.
