# Hostile Pre-Publication Referee Report: `positions/moral-status/` (P-MS1, P-MS2)

## 1. TL;DR

- **P-MS1 (phenomenal sentientism) — DEMOTE-TO-COHERENCE-ONLY.** The entry's distinctive content is the *phenomenal* qualification, and the entry itself concedes that qualification is "the Map's own, licensed by Tenet 1," carries external-evidence grade D, is framework-internal only, and "buys… not a different verdict in any actual case." A claim with no independent support and no verdict consequence is not a live moderate-credence position; it is a coherence commitment mislabelled. The constrain-vs-establish gate fails: the phenomenal reading is shown consistent with the position, never established against the functionalist/evaluativist rivals its own `valence` page concedes are answerable.
- **P-MS2 (threshold, not scalar) — REVISE-HARD.** The entry names its own fatal objection — the status/stakes separation "relocates Nozick's utility monster instead of blocking it" and may become "practically indistinguishable" from the scalar reading — and then keeps itself live at low credence. That is the site's dominant pathology (confession-without-correction): a conceded collapse into practical gradualism is banked as disclosure rather than triggering a binding status change. It engages none of the actual gradualism literature (Kagan, DeGrazia, Sebo) by name, substituting its own outer-review AIs as "the opposition."
- **Citation posture.** Metadata on the register's thin external roster (Taylor, Attfield, Varner, Feinberg, Nozick) is mostly clean, but two stance problems bite: Varner is recruited as a clean "biocentric rival to beat" when his 1998 view is hierarchical-with-sentience-priority and he later converted to sentientism; and the Feinberg conation reply reads p. 52 while suppressing the broader conative gloss at pp. 49–50. ## 2. Dimensional analysis

### 2.1 Derivation licensing (Q1): the phenomenal qualification is smuggled, and the entry says so

The domain preamble states the derivation direction outright: "the Map derives the status criterion *from* the value premise ([P-VS3]) — normative teleology requires an experiencer for whom the trajectory is good or bad — so P-MS1 sits downstream of P-VS3." P-VS3 ("intrinsic value resides in phenomenal experience") is itself calibrated "no independent evidence · framework-critical" and rests on Tenet 1 (Dualism). The derivation therefore runs Tenet 1 → P-VS3 → P-MS1. This is the constitutional-attractor effect in clean form: a framework-critical tenet reappears three inferential steps downstream wearing the costume of a derived applied-ethics result.

The entry does not hide this. It states: "The phenomenal qualification is the Map's own, licensed by Tenet 1 (Dualism): phenomenal sentientism stands or falls with the tenet, and does not claim independent support from the applied literature that leaves the distinction unmade." It further concedes grade D on the explicit ground that "the encyclopedia literature explicitly declines the phenomenal/functional distinction the position turns on." This is precise self-diagnosis of tenet leakage — and it is exactly the confession-without-correction failure mode, because the precise diagnosis is followed by no status change: the entry remains "live," credence "moderate," rather than being relabelled coherence-only.

**Constrain-vs-establish gate: FAIL.** The entry establishes that a phenomenal reading of valence is *consistent* with value realism. It nowhere establishes it *against* the functionalist and higher-order rivals. Those rivals are named nowhere in the register entry. They are engaged only one layer down, on `concepts/valence`, which pits the hedonic reading against Carruthers's evaluativism (valence as a nonconceptual representation of value) [[valence]] and against the active-inference reading (valence as a precision-weighted signal of expected value) [[valence]] — and there concedes: "That supplies the missing role, and the Map concedes it: the dissociation no longer separates the views… neither side refutes the other from inside its own commitments." [[valence]] So at the layer where the rivals are actually engaged, the site's own verdict is a draw. A register entry that turns entirely on the phenomenal-versus-functional cut cannot stand at moderate credence on a sub-article that concedes the cut is not adjudicated. The strongest rivals the entry must face by name and does not — Frankish's illusionism (which it lists only as a defeater condition, not an interlocutor), Dennett, Barrett's theory of constructed emotion, the Berridge–Kringelbach liking/wanting dissociation, Klein & Barron and Ginsburg & Jablonka on invertebrate valence — are absent from the register entirely.

**The AI verdict is question-begging by the entry's own architecture.** The entry claims the phenomenal reading "buys… not a different verdict in any actual case but a different account of hard-case uncertainty." That concession is fatal to the claim that P-MS1 does independent work: on the site's own statement the phenomenal qualification changes no verdict, including the AI verdict. The AI-moral-status result is carried elsewhere (the consciousness-scope and AI-consciousness-scope domains, e.g. P-AC1's substrate analysis). P-MS1 contributes only the conditional "if phenomenally sentient, then status," which inherits the answer from a prior consciousness attribution. The phenomenal reading generates the wanted AI verdict only by assuming the consciousness question already settled in the scope domain — the question at issue.

### 2.2 Biocentrism reply (Q2): correct on Feinberg's letter in one place, dated in its roster, and silent on the live rivals

The entry's necessity direction rests on "the conation-requirement reply (Feinberg 1974)" against "biocentrism (Taylor 1981/1986, Attfield 1987, Varner 1998)," and honestly rates the reply as one that "locates that disagreement rather than settles it."

**Feinberg fidelity.** Feinberg's interest principle does require a conative life for interests, and he does deny plants interests on the ground that "trees cannot know satisfaction or frustration." But Feinberg's own text at pp. 49–50 first compounds interests out of conations that expressly include "unconscious drives, aims, and goals; or latent tendencies, direction of growth, and natural fulfillments" [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Luke-Roelofs/publication/361954014_Sentientism_Motivation_and_Philosophical_Vulcans/links/62d401dafd347a451bc565dc/Sentientism-Motivation-and-Philosophical-Vulcans.pdf) — a gloss broad enough to admit precisely the biological goal-directedness the biocentrist wants, before he narrows to conscious wants at p. 52. The Map's reading takes the p. 52 narrowing as Feinberg's settled view and suppresses the pp. 49–50 breadth. That is a selective, one-sided rendering of a text that is internally unstable on exactly the point in dispute. Because the register gives no verbatim quotation, this is an attribution-precision problem rather than a misquotation, but it flatters the reply.

**The roster is a pre-2000 strawman relative to the live debate.** Taylor (1986) and Attfield (1987) are correctly dated and are genuine non-sentientist biocentrists, so naming them is fair. But the entry's own strongest shift-condition — "the biocentrist supplied an account of why a subjectless good generates duties that does not covertly appeal to mattering-to-someone" — describes, almost to the word, a live research programme the entry never cites: the welfare-subject / etiological-teleology literature. John Basl's *The Death of the Ethic of Life* (Oxford UP, 28 March 2019; ISBN 9780190923877) grounds welfare "in teleology or goal-directedness, and then… in the workings of natural selection" (ch. 3, "The Etiological Account of Teleological Welfare"), together with Basl & Sandler (2013). Eden Lin's "The Experience Requirement on Well-Being" (*Philosophical Studies* 178(3):867–886, 2021; DOI 10.1007/s11098-020-01463-6) argues directly that no compelling case can be made for an experience requirement on well-being; Gwen Bradford's perfectionism and Kagan (2019) canvass welfare for non-sentient beings. Rebutting Taylor/Varner-vintage biocentrism while ignoring this wave leaves the necessity direction defended against the weaker opponent. The plant-cognition uptake (Calvo, Trewavas, Segundo-Ortin & Calvo) is likewise absent.

A precise caveat sharpens rather than softens the charge: several of these live rivals are *ambivalent* non-experientialists, which the entry could have exploited but instead simply omitted. Basl is in fact a sentientist debunker — he maps the strongest non-sentientist ground and then concludes "the well-being of non-sentient beings, such as plants, species, and ecosystems, is morally significant only to the extent that it matters to sentient beings." Lin, likewise, will claim in his forthcoming *On the Plurality of Goods* that "all of the basic goods and bads are, in virtue of their natures, accessible only to sentient beings." The Map's necessity direction has real contemporary allies here; failing to cite them is not tactically hostile, it is simply under-researched. The single most direct threat — Lin's 2021 argument that the experience requirement is unmotivated — is exactly the paper a phenomenal-sentientist entry most needs to answer and does not name.

**Varner is a hostile witness, not a clean rival.** Cited as "Varner 1998" for the biocentric side, Varner's *In Nature's Interests?* (Oxford, 1998) is in fact a non-sentientist biocentrism carrying a built-in sentience-priority hierarchy, and Varner subsequently abandoned biocentrism for sentientism: per the biographical record on Gary Varner (1957–2023), in *Personhood, Ethics, and Animal Cognition* (Oxford UP, 2012; ISBN 9780199758784) "Varner moved away from his biocentrism, instead endorsing a developed version of Hare's [two-level utilitarian] ethics," distinguishing "persons, near-persons and merely sentient beings." Recruiting him as the "rival to beat" without noting that he ended on the Map's side is a co-optation-adjacent mishandling in reverse — the named opponent is really a convert.

### 2.3 Threshold verdict vs gradualism (Q3): a distinction the entry concedes may make no difference

P-MS2 holds status is a threshold predicate and that enhancement "raises what is at stake in a life without raising its status," routing the upward grading through P-VS4's plural stakes. The disambiguation ("'P suffices for Q' does not license 'more P, more Q'") is logically clean as far as it goes.

It does not go far enough, and the entry knows it. It concedes: "The residue is stated rather than dissolved: the separation **relocates** Nozick's utility monster instead of blocking it, since equal consideration of *equal* interests legitimately weights a subject whose intensity ceiling has been raised, and an unbounded ceiling is an unbounded weight." Its own shift-condition then states the collapse in full: "the status/stakes separation were shown to recommend no act the scalar reading would not also recommend, the utility-monster residue growing until the two are practically indistinguishable." This is precisely the standard objection the task names: if interests can be weightier, and moral status just is the standing for interests to count, then weightier interests plus equal status is practically gradualism under another name. The entry relocates gradualism into the stakes dimension and then admits the relocation may be a distinction without a practical difference. Under the site's own P-M5 ("disclosure is not self-correction"), naming this does not discharge it.

**No engagement with the actual gradualism literature.** The entry's interlocutors are two of the Map's own outer-review AIs (ChatGPT and Claude, 2026-08-23). The published rivals whose entire projects are the scalar/hierarchical alternative appear nowhere: Shelly Kagan's *How to Count Animals, more or less* (Oxford UP, Uehiro Series in Practical Ethics, 22 March 2019; ISBN 9780198829676), defending "limited hierarchy" — "(a) people have higher moral status than animals (and some animals have higher moral status than others), but (b) all people have equal moral status"; David DeGrazia's "Moral Status As a Matter of Degree?" (*The Southern Journal of Philosophy* 46(2):181–198, Summer 2008); Jeff Sebo's *The Moral Circle: Who Matters, What Matters, and Why* (W. W. Norton, 28 Jan 2025; ISBN 9781324064800) with its probability-weighted approach to uncertainty about sentience and moral status; McMahan's time-relative interest account; and Bostrom & Yudkowsky's substrate non-discrimination principle. Citing one's own AI reviewers as the opposition while omitting the field is performative inoculation at the level of the whole entry.

**Marginal cases expose an internal tension.** The entry claims the threshold view protects the comatose: "the Map has already declined to let a comatose human's status fall with their capacity." But it simultaneously asserts threshold crossings "run both directions… removing it takes status with it." A permanently unconscious human has no capacity for valenced experience; strict phenomenal sentientism plus the downward-crossing rule entails they lose status — contradicting the protection claimed. The entry's epistemicist move on the boundary ("ignorance about a real boundary rather than indecision about where to draw one") is a coherent position but is asserted, not defended, and does nothing to fix the comatose verdict or the infant/fetus/insect boundary verdicts.

**Coherence inflation.** P-MS2's stated ground is telling: "Consistency fixes the reading rather than convenience: enhancement is the marginal-cases pressure arriving from above, so a scalar answer here forces one below." The threshold reading is held because the scalar alternative would disturb P-MS1 — coherence with an upstream entry is doing the justificatory work that independent argument should. That is evidential-independence failure.

### 2.5 Calibration honesty (Q5)

Unusually, there is no upward calibration asymmetry: P-MS1 self-rates credence moderate, grade D, framework-internal yes; P-MS2 self-rates credence low, grade D, discriminability none. If anything the numbers are honestly deflationary. The problems are structural rather than inflationary:

- **Epistemic-to-metaphysical slide / coherence inflation** (§2.3): P-MS2's threshold reading is grounded on consistency with P-MS1 rather than on independent argument.
- **Confession-without-correction as a calibration device**: both entries convert precise disclosure of defeaters (tenet leakage in P-MS1; utility-monster collapse in P-MS2) into continued "live" status. The disclosure is banked; the status does not move. The correct calibration response to "grade D + framework-internal + changes no verdict" is coherence-only, not live-moderate.
- **Standing cross-check (Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria 2025; affective active-inference)**: P-MS1 makes a constitutive claim about phenomenal valence as the ground of value and engages none of the predictive-processing/active-inference literature on valence by name. The `valence` dependency engages the generic active-inference reading and concedes a draw, [[valence]] but the named affective-inference programme (Laukkonen–Friston–Chandaria Bayesian binding; Joffily & Coricelli; Hesp et al.; Seth's interoceptive inference; Solms; Barrett & Simmons; Van de Cruys) is absent from the register layer where the constitutive claim is actually registered. Documented site-wide blind spot, present here.

## 3. Bottom-line verdict

- **P-MS1 — DEMOTE-TO-COHERENCE-ONLY.** The bare sentientist criterion (the moral circle) is mainstream and survives; the entry's *distinctive* content is the phenomenal qualification, which by the entry's own admissions (grade D, framework-internal, licensed by Tenet 1, changes no verdict, turns on a phenomenal/functional cut its own sub-article rules a draw) has no independent support and no verdict consequence. That is a coherence commitment, and the register should say so rather than carrying it at live-moderate.
- **P-MS2 — REVISE-HARD.** The threshold/stakes distinction is conceded by the entry to possibly collapse into practical gradualism (utility-monster relocation, "practically indistinguishable"). It must either supply the argument that blocks the collapse or accept practical gradualism, and it must engage the gradualism literature by name. Absent that, the fallback verdict is DEMOTE-TO-COHERENCE-ONLY, since a distinction the author grants may make no practical difference is not a live commitment.

## 5. Site-wide methodology improvements

- **Binding-remediation rule for confession-without-correction.** The corpus's own P-M5 already names the pattern; enforce it. A register entry that names a fatal or potentially-fatal defeater (tenet leakage, a conceded practical collapse) must, in the same commit, trigger a status change — demote-to-coherence-only, flag-as-perpetually-contested, or delete. Disclosure banked without a status move should fail review. Both target entries are current instances.
- **Constitutional-attractor auto-flag.** Any position whose derivation chain terminates in a framework-critical tenet (here Tenet 1 → P-VS3 → P-MS1) should be auto-classified coherence-only unless it can show support independent of that tenet. The AI generation-and-review pipeline should compute this from the `Depends on` graph rather than leaving it to prose self-report, since the prose self-reports it accurately and then does not act on it.
- **Rival-of-record rule.** The pipeline currently lets an entry treat its own outer-review AI passes (ChatGPT/Claude) as the opposition (P-MS2). Require that every "rival to beat" be a named, published author engaged substantively; AI-reviewer objections may seed but not stand in for the literature. This directly attacks the performative-inoculation habit.
- **Literature-currency gate.** Rosters here are frozen at 1974–1998 (biocentrism) and pre-2016 (gradualism). Impose a currency check that any applied-ethics entry engage the live 2018–2025 debate (welfare-subject theory: Basl 2019, Lin 2021, Bradford; moral weight and gradualism: Kagan 2019, Sebo 2025, Rethink Priorities Moral Weight Project) before minting.
- **Predictive-processing named-engagement requirement.** Make the standing cross-check binding at the register layer, not only the concept layer: any article or entry making a constitutive claim about valence, value, phenomenal character, attention, or selection must engage Laukkonen–Friston–Chandaria 2025 and the affective active-inference literature by name with substantive argument. P-MS1 currently discharges this only indirectly and inadequately.
- **Access/audit note.** The two apex calibration benchmarks were not retrievable by direct slug URL during this audit; if the register is to be measured against them as gold-standard templates, the pipeline should ensure those apex pages are reliably reachable and indexed, since an audit that cannot fetch the calibration standard cannot enforce it.
