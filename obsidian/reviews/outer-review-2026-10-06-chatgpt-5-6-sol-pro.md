# External audit: “The Categorical Perception Void”

**Audit date:** 6 October 2026  
**Article state examined:** the live article after its substantive 29 September revision, together with the research note and changelog.

## Overall verdict

**Major revision is needed.**

The article makes one important and substantially correct correction: the strongest textbook version of categorical speech perception—within-category acoustic differences are literally unavailable to listeners—does not survive modern task-sensitive research. Classic identification and discrimination procedures can manufacture a categorical-looking response profile while the underlying representation remains graded. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36586841/)

But after retiring that strong claim, the article does not adequately establish its proposed replacement:

1. **“Default access” is not operationally defined or directly measured.** The cited experiments concern particular reports, decisions, memory comparisons and electrophysiological responses. They do not identify a task-independent default deliverable of consciousness.
2. **The stronger behavioural version is contradicted by ordinary speech processing.** Within-category detail automatically affects lexical activation, eye movements, cue weighting and recovery from misheard words—without subjects being instructed to discriminate points on a continuum. [PubMed+3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12435537/)
3. **The provenance claim is plausible but generic.** Percepts do not normally carry introspectively available causal histories. That is already the thesis of the Map’s Source-Attribution Void; it is not distinctive to categorical perception. The existing article explicitly says mental contents lack native origin tags. (see [[source-attribution-void]])
4. **The colour section is materially stale.** A June 2026 *PNAS* paper, published more than three months before this article, found that the celebrated early EEG category effect was driven principally by chromatic and luminance contrast adaptation, not linguistic colour categories. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42263133/)
5. **There is one unambiguous citation error:** three-month retention of Japanese listeners’ /r/–/l/ training is attributed to Logan, Lively and Pisoni (1991), but that paper was the initial training report. Retention was tested in later studies. [PubMed+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2016438/)

The 29 September repair was nevertheless worthwhile. The changelog shows that the article stopped saying consciousness simply “receives” categories, changed this to what perceivers “report and act on”, acknowledged that categorical phenomenology is untested, and replaced a discovered/installed dichotomy with world-, sensory- and learning-based origins. Those changes remove the worst epistemic/metaphysical equivocation in the original draft. They do not yet supply evidence for the residual “default” claim. (see [[categorical-perception-void]])

## 2. Speech: what survives the task-artifact correction?

### The article is right about the strong claim

Classic forced-choice identification and discrimination tasks do not demonstrate that listeners lose within-category acoustic information. A binary response can look categorical even when the underlying percept is continuous; conversely, a graded response can occur even if an internal stage is categorical. Continuous-response methods, signal-detection analysis and less bias-prone discrimination procedures are therefore necessary. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36586841/)

The article should keep this result prominent. It is one of its strongest passages.

### It then draws the wrong residual conclusion

The replacement claim is that the category is what a perceiver “reports and acts on by default”, while the gradient is available only under special probing. Speech research directly complicates that formulation:

- Within-category VOT changes alter fixation probabilities to competing pictured words during ordinary online word recognition. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12435537/)
- Gradient effects survive changes in lexical versus phoneme tasks, two versus four response alternatives, and natural versus synthetic speech. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19045996/)
- Listeners learn and use fine-grained probability distributions over speech cues. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18582855/)
- Greater individual gradiency predicts better recovery from temporarily misleading lexical interpretations. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33983791/)

These are not demonstrations that a subject can recover a hidden continuum only after being instructed to compare two nearly identical laboratory stimuli. They show that subcategory information automatically enters normal language processing and affects action.

The defensible replacement is therefore:

> **Speech processing maintains graded acoustic evidence while also forming categories; which level dominates an overt response depends on task, context and processing goal.**

That is a dual-representation or multi-level processing thesis. It is not a “default category versus hidden gradient” thesis.

### Toscano is especially damaging to the proposed residue face

The article presents Toscano et al. as revealing an early continuous code followed by a later categorical readout. But the P3 result remained sensitive to within-category VOT. The later phonological/categorical stage did not discard the continuum; it combined category information with graded evidence. [Sage Journals+1](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797610384142)

Consequently, the article’s image of the system “quantising” the continuum but retaining an unquantised copy where the default readout does not look is not what Toscano demonstrated. That is a speculative architecture added by the Map.

### “Default” needs an operational definition

The current article moves among at least four different propositions:

1. people give categorical labels when asked to label;
2. explicit discrimination is poorer within categories;
3. ordinary behaviour is controlled principally by category identity;
4. conscious phenomenology is categorical.

The evidence strongly supports (1) in the relevant paradigms, variably supports (2), contradicts an unrestricted version of (3), and does not directly test (4). The 29 September caveat acknowledges the last point but leaves the headline concept of “default access” doing work that no specified experiment measures. (see [[categorical-perception-void]])

A genuine default-access study would need to preregister what “default” means—for example spontaneous free description, speeded ecological action without category instructions, visual-analogue report, or early untrained eye movements—and distinguish sensitivity from response criterion. There is no neutral, task-free behavioural window onto conscious content.

## 3. Colour: the most serious currency failure

The article arrives at a verdict close to “real but modest” for linguistic colour effects. That is too simple even for the evidence it cites and is no longer current enough for an article revised in late September 2026.

### What the older record supports

Winawer et al. found a language-specific speed advantage that was abolished by concurrent verbal interference. Witzel and Gegenfurtner found category-related response-time effects but not the predicted lateralisation. Martinovic et al. 2020 reproduced a broad blue/green effect while failing to reproduce the Russian dark-/light-blue advantage. [DOI+2](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0701644104)

Even before 2026, well-controlled psychophysics warned against treating this as altered sensory colour coding. When same- and different-category pairs were equated in just-noticeable differences, He et al. found category effects only from roughly 200 ms onwards, in N2/P3 components associated with post-perceptual processing—not in the perceptual colour representation. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24695189/)

Full-colour-space measurements likewise found only a loose relationship between colour categories and discrimination thresholds. Some apparent boundary advantages coincided with known cone-opponent sensory mechanisms, while many linguistic category boundaries did not coincide with sensitivity changes. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23732118/)

### The missing 2026 result

Martinovic and colleagues’ three-experiment 2026 *PNAS* paper closely re-examined the EEG case for early, preattentive category effects. It found:

- the visual mismatch response was primarily driven by chromatic and luminance contrast adaptation;
- categorically different but contrast-equated hues did not reliably generate the expected effect;
- Russian speakers showed no reliable special early green/blue or light-/dark-blue category response.

The authors conclude that evidence for automatic, preattentive linguistic recoding of colour perception has been significantly weakened. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42263133/)

This does **not** show that language never affects colour judgements, attention, memory or decision. It does show that the strongest bridge from category vocabulary to early perceptual encoding is now substantially less secure than the Map reports.

A current verdict should be:

> Linguistic colour categories affect performance in some speeded discrimination, memory and dual-task paradigms. The locus varies, and secure evidence for automatic early sensory recoding is presently weak.

That is importantly different from presenting colour as a straightforward second domain in which perception itself has been warped.

## 4. Faces and music: valid phenomena, underrepresented alternatives

### Facial-expression continua

Etcoff and Magee’s result is genuine, but the subsequent literature supplies several serious alternatives omitted by the article:

- Verbal interference can eliminate cross-category advantages in facial-expression recognition, implicating verbal coding at encoding or storage. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11105523/)
- A category-adjustment model explains errors as memory drifting toward category prototypes after veridical perceptual detail decays. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18062556/)
- Some CP effects arise because particular within-category pairs differ in typicality and are unusually difficult, not because all cross-category pairs receive enhanced perceptual separation. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21327385/)
- Categorical and dimensional processing of expressions can coexist. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21824015/)

This matters because the article treats faces as independent replication of the same category-induced perceptual “warp”. The alternatives instead locate effects in memory, labels, prototype adjustment, stimulus typicality or hybrid representation. The cross-domain repetition may therefore be repetition of a behavioural signature, not of one perceptual mechanism.

### Musical intervals

The music section presents an unusually clear opportunity to distinguish **measured categorisation** from **lost access**. Burns and Ward found that the categorical signature diminished greatly when the adaptive procedure estimated actual discrimination ability. Siegel and Siegel studied six trained musicians under a particular tuning task. Together these studies support expertise-dependent response organisation; they do not establish a phenomenological one-way door. [Ernst Terhardt+1](https://terhardt.userweb.mwn.de/ter/ref/BurnsWard1978a.html)

The claim that “acquiring the category costs the continuum” should therefore be removed. At most:

> Musical training can make interval categories dominate some identification and comparison tasks, while fine-grained sensitivity remains recoverable under other procedures.

That interpretation also fits the article’s own speech deflation much better.

## 5. Perceptual learning: the missing mechanism plurality

The article’s preferred image is compression within categories plus expansion at boundaries. Some learning studies show that pattern, but the perceptual-learning literature does not justify treating it as the single operation behind categorical perception.

Feldman found that improvements in discrimination after category learning were proportional to the mutual information between a feature and category membership. This is adaptive reallocation of precision to informative features, not simply a loss of the within-category continuum. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34283691/)

Dubova and Goldstone argue that conventional categorisation and similarity designs cannot distinguish among competing mechanisms. Their reconstruction task found evidence for several processes at once: adaptation to environmental feature structure, category-based expectations, resource allocation toward categorisation-relevant dimensions and changes that reduce miscategorisation. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34018243/)

Pérez-Gay Juárez et al. likewise found stronger separation than compression and effects only among successful learners. That is a selective learning result, not evidence that every learned category imposes a standard quantisation operation. [PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/doi)

The article should consequently stop using “the warp” as though it denotes one mechanism. Categorical-perception effects are better understood as a **family of task- and domain-dependent changes** that can include:

- attentional weighting of diagnostic features;
- sensory tuning;
- probabilistic cue reweighting;
- criterion placement;
- lexical or verbal coding;
- memory attraction toward prototypes;
- decision-level category use;
- genuine discrimination enhancement near useful boundaries.

Some of these change early encoding; some do not. Some improve fine discrimination rather than suppressing it. Some operate only during explicit categorisation.

## 6. Is the proposed void earned?

### Claim-by-claim assessment

| Proposed component | What the evidence establishes | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cross-boundary discrimination can exceed within-category discrimination** | Yes, in numerous paradigms, though the result is task-, stimulus- and metric-sensitive | **Earned, with qualifications** |
| **Within-category information is absent** | False for speech and unsupported generally | **Correctly retired** |
| **Within-category information is available only with special effort** | False as a general speech claim; graded detail automatically affects lexical processing | **Not earned** |
| **The category controls ordinary action by default** | Categories affect many responses, but gradients also affect ordinary online action | **Overstated** |
| **Conscious phenomenology is categorical by default** | The article now concedes this is untested | **Open, not evidence for a void** |
| **The perceiver cannot introspect the origin of a category boundary** | Generally plausible; perceptual content does not include a causal history | **Plausible but generic** |
| **Category origins divide into world, sensory system or learning** | The three-way correction is better than the old dichotomy, but real boundaries commonly have mixed causal origins | **Still too classificatory** |
| **A distinctive hidden operation transforms continua into categories** | Multiple mechanisms can produce the behavioural signature | **Not established** |

### “Default access” is not a stable scientific construct here

The term allows the article to retreat from “inaccessible” without stating how much remains inaccessible, in what circumstances, or to which cognitive system. Any result can then be accommodated:

- categorical response → evidence of default category access;
- graded response → recovered residual information;
- automatic graded lexical effect → information retained below categorical report;
- no phenomenological evidence → phenomenology remains open.

That architecture is difficult to falsify. The article needs to say what observation would show that there is **no** default-access void—for example, automatic graded effects across untrained ecological tasks, continuous spontaneous reports, or equal within-/cross-category sensitivity after controlling metric and decision factors.

Much of that evidence already exists for speech.

### Provenance is real but not distinctive

A percept normally does not announce whether its organisation arose from:

- receptor and neural nonlinearities;
- environmental statistics;
- developmental exposure;
- explicit training;
- labels and working memory;
- recent task context.

But this is an instance of ordinary causal and source opacity. The Map’s Source-Attribution Void already defines the structural problem as mental contents arriving without native origin tags, and explicitly treats provenance as reconstructed rather than directly read. (see [[source-attribution-void]])

Nor is category provenance necessarily one thing that could have been internally labelled. The /b/–/p/ boundary can jointly reflect auditory sensitivity, speech-production statistics, distributional learning, lexical experience and task demands. Asking whether it was “world-built”, “sense-built” or “installed” may itself impose a false singular-origin model.

The categorical-perception article therefore does not need a second provenance void. It needs a short subsection saying:

> Categorical perception is a particularly clear application of the Source-Attribution Void: subjects experience or report a boundary without direct access to the weighted causal contributions that produced it.

## 7. Tenet-protective bracketing

### The good news: it does not straightforwardly misuse the evidence as proof of dualism

The article explicitly states that the empirical dissociations are compatible with both physicalist and dualist interpretations; that the phenomenology is untested; that the verbal-interference result does not demonstrate non-physical causation; and that Minimal Quantum Interaction and rejection of Many Worlds do no work here. That is good calibration. (see [[categorical-perception-void]])

So this is **not primarily a case of hiding contrary evidence to protect interactionist dualism**.

### It is, however, catalogue-protective

The article was created around a traditional “continuum disappears into categories” picture. Once the research note and pessimistic review acknowledged that this picture was a task artefact, the article preserved a standalone void by moving to:

- undefined “default” access;
- unmeasured categorical phenomenology;
- generic source opacity;
- a hidden “operation” whose implementation can vary across every domain.

The changelog shows this transition explicitly. The 29 September correction removed the strongest unsupported wording while retaining the void classification and framework-independent status. (see [[changelog]])

That is a milder but recognisable form of protective bracketing: the proposition most exposed to evidence is withdrawn, while a less operationalised successor inherits the original article’s title and argumentative role.

### The Occam connection is especially weak

The article treats the history as an illustration of the limits of Occam’s Razor: a simple categorical model and an elegant lateralised-Whorf model turned out to be wrong or incomplete.

But one simple hypothesis being undermined by better task design does not show that parsimony is epistemically unreliable. It shows that:

- the measurements underdetermined the representation;
- response mapping and criterion effects were not controlled;
- perceptual distances were inadequately matched;
- a more complex processing architecture fit the data better.

That is ordinary empirical model correction, not evidence for Tenet 5. The Occam section presently converts an instance of methodological progress into generic support for a pre-existing philosophical commitment.

### The dualist conditional remains unstable

The article says that, **if** default experience is categorical, the phenomenal deliverable is not a transparent readout of the early continuous functional encoding. That conditional is coherent. But since the antecedent is explicitly untested, it cannot yet support the Map’s wider “phenomenal surplus” framing.

A layered physical system also naturally contains different representational formats at sensory, phonological, lexical and decisional stages. The N1/P3 dissociation creates no special pressure towards dualism. Toscano’s finding that both graded and categorical information coexist makes the neat functional/phenomenal contrast less applicable still. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797610384142)

**Bottom line on bracketing:** direct tenet protection is limited; protection of the article’s membership in the void catalogue is substantial.

## 8. Weak integration with neighbouring articles

### Source-Attribution Void

This is the clearest redundancy. Its opening already states that mental content arrives detached from its origin and that introspection supplies reconstruction rather than direct causal readout. Its relation-to-tenets section also correctly says this constrains dualism rather than supporting it. (see [[source-attribution-void]])

The categorical article’s provenance face should be absorbed there as an empirical case study, not presented as an independent void-maker.

### Cognitive Penetration

The neighbouring article correctly distinguishes changes in perceptual experience from changes in attention, judgement and report, and presents Firestone and Scholl’s confound checklist. The categorical article does not apply that same discipline systematically to its own evidence. 

This is a major integration failure. Every domain section should explicitly identify the best-supported locus:

- sensory encoding;
- learned attention;
- perceptual discrimination;
- working memory;
- lexical activation;
- decision;
- verbal report;
- phenomenology.

Without that, “perception” moves between levels.

### Language-Thought Boundary

The Language-Thought Boundary currently uses Greek light-/dark-blue EEG work as evidence that linguistic categories are registered more strongly “pre-attentively”. 

That claim requires immediate qualification in light of the 2026 *PNAS* reanalysis, which found that the early response was explained by contrast adaptation rather than category membership. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42263133/)

### Consciousness and Language Interface

That article moves from categorical response differences to “genuine phenomenal influence”, including the claim that colour-category shifts alter experience rather than merely categorisation or attention. 

The categorical-perception article now explicitly says this phenomenal question is untested. The two pages therefore apply incompatible evidential standards.

### Infant Consciousness Void

The 29 September changelog records a useful local repair: “destroyed” was changed to “largely lost”, with adult training acknowledged. (see [[changelog]])

But the neighbouring article still uses broader rhetoric about demolished cognitive architecture, circuits being pruned away and neural hardware no longer existing. 

That language is not warranted by behavioural narrowing of phoneme discrimination, especially when high-variability training can produce durable adult improvement and critical-period reviews discuss reopening mechanisms. [PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7963022/)

### Expertise Void

The Expertise Void simultaneously claims that the novice regime becomes inaccessible and that earlier representations persist, can re-emerge under pressure, and may be partly re-engaged through deliberate de-skilling. 

The music evidence in the categorical article belongs on the recoverable/task-dependent side of this distinction. It should not be used as an uncomplicated example of expertise permanently closing access.

### Resolution Void

The Resolution Void says that below a just-noticeable difference, distinctions vanish categorically from consciousness and that there is no intermediate “almost seeing” state. 

Psychophysical thresholds are probabilistic estimates over performance, not hard phenomenal cut-offs. Categorical-perception research itself shows graded sensitivity, criterion effects and changes with task. The Resolution Void’s binary language therefore conflicts with the empirical lesson of the article under review.

### Vagueness Void

The reciprocal link added on 29 September is useful. But the proposed contrast—concepts vague at their edges while percepts are sharp—is too broad. Many categorisation functions are probabilistic, context-sensitive and individually variable; perceived boundaries drift with stimulus distributions and task demands. The conjunction is interesting only after replacing “sharp perceptual edge” with “some tasks elicit relatively abrupt categorisation”.

### Changes to site methodology

21. **Require a claim–source–inference ledger for every empirical article.** Each entry should separately record: what the source directly found, what its authors inferred, and what the Map additionally infers.
22. **Tag processing level for every perception claim.** Use a fixed vocabulary: receptor/sensory encoding, learned attention, perceptual discrimination, working memory, lexical activation, decision, report and phenomenology. Do not permit the bare word “perception” where the evidence discriminates among these levels.
23. **Require psychophysical metric controls.** A categorical-perception claim should not pass review merely because physical steps are equal. The site should ask whether stimuli were equated in baseline perceptual distance or JNDs and whether sensitivity was separated from response criterion.
24. **Add a task-dependence field to evidence tables.** Record response format, delay, memory load, adaptive versus fixed comparison, verbal interference, stimulus uncertainty and whether the category labels were demanded by the task.
25. **Record sample sizes beside classic findings.** This would prevent six musicians, four chinchillas or one narrow cultural sample from silently becoming a domain-general law.
26. **Require a latest-literature check against the article’s actual revision date.** The September 2026 article should not have passed publication without the June 2026 *PNAS* colour paper. Searches should include citing-author updates and corrigenda, not merely the original title keywords.
27. **Introduce a “void necessity test”.** Before creating or retaining a standalone void, require answers to three questions: Is the limit empirically demonstrated? Is it non-redundant with existing voids? What evidence would retire or absorb it?
28. **Separate empirical failure from phenomenological absence.** Poor discrimination, categorical reports, absent recall and inaccessible experience are different claims. A behavioural deficit should not automatically be translated into a void in phenomenal access.
29. **Require mechanism plurality before cross-domain synthesis.** Similar behavioural curves across speech, colour, faces and music should not be called one operation until competing sensory, attentional, mnemonic and decisional explanations have been compared.
30. **Create a formal contradiction sweep for neighbouring pages.** When one article changes from “destroyed” to “partially recoverable”, search the corpus for demolition, pruning, permanent closure and phenomenology claims that depended on the stronger version.
31. **Distinguish “source exists” from “source supports this sentence”.** The current article passes the first test almost completely while failing the second at several important inferential transitions.
32. **Track corrections, retractions and publication status in reference metadata.** Mark peer-reviewed article, review, philosophical paper, preprint and corrigendum explicitly; Cacioli should remain visibly provisional.
33. **Add a “strongest deflation” box to every void article.** For this article it should state that traditional categorical signatures can be produced by task and response structure, that graded information remains behaviourally active, and that categorical phenomenology has not been established.
34. **Add a retirement criterion to every void.** For the categorical-perception void: automatic graded use across ecological tasks, continuous spontaneous reporting, and no category-specific provenance deficit beyond generic source opacity should trigger absorption into the Source-Attribution and Cognitive-Penetration articles.
