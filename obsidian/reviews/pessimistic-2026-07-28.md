## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

"Consciousness-quantum interface" is a term with no specification, no measured value, and no failure condition. The section's escape route is to say the relevant grain is finer than any grain yet examined — the exact structure of "the vital force operates below the level chemistry has so far reached." The section is honest enough to call this an open question, but an open question whose closure condition is never stated is not a research programme.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

The Map hands Dennett the argument. § "Bidirectional Interaction and Minimal Quantum Interaction" predicts that real inversions produce "subtle behavioural differences — in aesthetic preferences, emotional valences, reaction-time asymmetries, or the fine structure of introspective reports." Those are heterophenomenological data. If inversion shows up in the reports, the inverts and non-inverts are *functionally different subjects*, and the thought experiment has stopped being about functionally identical beings. The article cannot both predict the signature and claim the scenario is one of strict functional identity. See Critical Issue 2.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Both horns presuppose an interface fine enough to matter and coherent long enough to act. The section says which horn bites "turns on an open question about how fine the interface is." Tegmark's position is that the question is closed and the answer is 10⁻¹³ – 10⁻²⁰ s. The section never gives a number, never cites the decoherence literature, and never says what "fine" means in units. That is not a disagreement the article loses; it is a disagreement the article does not enter.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

No new purchase on tonight's material. The standing objection to L177 ("first-person indexicality supports real collapse") is already scoped in the existing P1 task on this file, with settled corpus wording to inherit. Not re-litigated here.

### The Empiricist

The dilemma as stated is a false test: the Map survives horn 1 by denying detectability and survives horn 2 by denying causal idleness, and nothing in the section says which denial it makes. A dilemma from which both exits are open is not a concession — it is the appearance of one. The sibling article's version *does* close one exit, which is why the sibling's version is the honest one.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

The section's grain talk assumes a determinate fact about *where* the consciousness-relevant boundary of a system lies. Nāgārjuna's objection is that "the system" is a conventional carving; the search for a grain fine enough to fix which experiences belong to which duplicate is a search for an intrinsic nature the analysis was supposed to have dissolved. Not a new objection to the Map, but the section's framing is the place it bites hardest.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The imported dilemma's second horn was weakened in transit, and the weakened form is evadable

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/inverted-qualia.md`
- **Location**: § "The Organizational-Invariance Objection", final paragraph
- **Problem**: `concepts/organizational-invariance.md` L77 states the horn as: *"if it makes **no detectable** difference, Joe's part-silicon stages are behaviourally identical to his all-neural ones while — on the Map's view — his experience has faded, which reinstates the very introspection-versus-experience dissociation the fading argument calls implausible."* The new section restates it as: *"If it makes **no difference at all**, the inversion is causally idle and the Map has rebuilt the [[concepts/epiphenomenalism|epiphenomenalism]] it rejects."*

  Two things changed. The antecedent moved from an *epistemic* condition (undetectable) to a *metaphysical* one (no difference). The consequent moved from Chalmers' actual reductio (introspection/experience dissociation reinstated) to a charge the Map has independent resources against (epiphenomenalism).

  The result is evadable in one step, and the Map's own tenets supply the step: Minimal Quantum Interaction is precisely the claim that consciousness makes a *real* difference that is *statistically invisible* inside the Born-rule envelope. The Map therefore denies "no difference at all" and walks out. But it does not thereby escape the sibling's horn — a real-but-undetectable difference still leaves Joe behaviourally identical while his experience has changed, which is the dissociation Chalmers calls implausible, and which `organizational-invariance.md` correctly logs as conditional on the Map's "sharpest open question", cross-linked to [[ensemble-level-epiphenomenalism]].

  This is the epistemic/metaphysical equivocation pattern: the undetectable/nonexistent distinction is exactly the one the Map's second tenet lives on, and the new section collapses it. The higher-traffic article now carries the softer statement of the dilemma while announcing that it is the dilemma "the Map owns rather than escapes."

  Compounding: the section's closing sentence tells the reader that `organizational-invariance` "works through the reply", implying the sibling ratifies this dilemma. The sibling works through a *different, sharper* one.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Restore the sibling's antecedent ("no **detectable** difference") and its consequent (the introspection-versus-experience dissociation the fading argument calls implausible), and drop or subordinate the epiphenomenalism gloss. Roughly word-neutral. The `[[concepts/epiphenomenalism]]` link can stay in the same sentence as the dissociation point.

### Issue 2: Horn 1 is triggered by the article's own Bidirectional Interaction section, so the "open question" is one the article has already answered

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/inverted-qualia.md`
- **Location**: § "The Organizational-Invariance Objection" final paragraph vs § "Relation to Site Perspective → Bidirectional Interaction and Minimal Quantum Interaction" (L167)
- **Problem**: Horn 1 reads: *"If the interface difference is behaviourally detectable, it is coarse organization that Chalmers' isomorph already reproduces, and the grain dispute collapses."* L167 then commits the Map to exactly the antecedent: *"The Map bites the bullet. Phenomenal character is causally efficacious, so any actual instance of qualia-inversion **should produce subtle behavioural differences** … MQI's bandwidth-limited outbound channel predicts these show up in finer-grained measurements rather than coarse discrimination tasks."*

  The section's escape word is "coarse", but it does not survive the sibling's own definition: `organizational-invariance.md` L39 defines fine-grained functional organization as "the full pattern of causal interactions among a system's parts, specified **at whatever level of detail is needed to fix behaviour**." By that definition *any* behaviourally detectable difference is an organizational difference, whether the measurement detecting it is coarse or fine. The article's fine-grained/coarse-measurement distinction is not the same distinction as coarse/fine *organization*, and horn 1 turns on the latter.

  So on the article's own commitments the Map stands on horn 1 and the grain dispute collapses — yet the section says "which horn is avoidable turns on an open question about how fine the interface is, not on anything settled here." Something *is* settled here, three sections later, and it is settled on the losing side. This is the concession-bolted-onto-contrary-prose pattern: the new paragraph was written without reconciling against the bite-the-bullet paragraph it shares an article with.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: The genuine third option the dilemma has quietly closed off is the **type/token** distinction, and it is the Map's own position elsewhere. A functional isomorph reproduces the *causal pattern* — the type-level dispositional profile, including its statistical envelope. What MQI posits is influence over *which token outcome* is realised inside that envelope. A token-level difference can be (a) causally efficacious, so not epiphenomenal, and (b) not a difference in functional organization, since organization fixes the pattern and not the draw. That is a third position: neither "coarse organization the isomorph reproduces" nor "no difference at all."

  Two ways to fix, both by rewording: state horn 1 as *"if the difference is a difference in the causal pattern itself, Chalmers can simply push his grain finer to include the interface, and invariance is preserved by extension"* — which is the strong form of the horn, and not evaded by pointing at fine-grained measurement — and then say the Map's answer is that the difference is token-level, not pattern-level. Or, if that reply is judged too load-bearing for a section of this size, drop the "open question" framing and say plainly that L167's prediction places the Map on the first horn and that the reply is developed at [[organizational-invariance]]. What should not remain is a dilemma announced as open by an article that has answered it.

### Issue 3: "Illusionist debunking typically targets cognitive processes, not qualitative content" is falsified by the Map's own Schwitzgebel treatment

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/inverted-qualia.md` (L120), against `obsidian/concepts/discrimination-problem.md` (L60, rewritten 07:09 tonight)
- **Location**: § "Introspection and Contemplative Evidence"
- **Problem**: `inverted-qualia.md` L120 says: *"Illusionist debunking arguments typically target introspection's unreliability about cognitive **processes** — confabulation, post-hoc narratives — while the inverted qualia argument concerns qualitative **content**. I may be wrong about *why* I find this red beautiful, but the redness itself is directly given."*

  Tonight's `discrimination-problem.md` edit strengthened Schwitzgebel from "conservatively … untrustworthy … stops short of claiming introspection is wholly unreliable" to *"faulty, untrustworthy, and misleading" — "frequently and massively mistaken, about a great variety of issues" … across domains including visual imagery, emotion, dreams, and peripheral vision.*

  Every one of those domains is qualitative *content*, not cognitive process. The most-cited introspection sceptic in the literature — now quoted at full strength by a sibling article — is a direct counterexample to L120's generalisation. The re-framing was a good citation-fidelity repair in isolation; it left a neighbouring article's dismissal of the debunking literature standing on a premise the corpus now contradicts. `organizational-invariance.md` L61 also cites Schwitzgebel's unreliability line as a live resistance strategy, so the corpus registers his target correctly in two places and mis-registers it in one.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Reword L120 to make the distinction the inversion argument actually needs, which is narrower and survives Schwitzgebel intact: introspection may be massively unreliable about the *fine structure* of phenomenal content — how detailed peripheral vision is, whether imagery is coloured — without being unreliable about *whether there is qualitative content at all*. The inversion argument needs only the latter. Name Schwitzgebel rather than leaving him unmentioned in the one place his position is being characterised. Word-neutral.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The dilemma is exhaustive

- **Current content says**: either the interface difference is behaviourally detectable (→ coarse organization, grain dispute collapses) or it makes no difference at all (→ epiphenomenalism).
- **A critic would argue**: this is a false dichotomy twice over. Between "detectable" and "no difference" sits *real but statistically invisible* — the Map's own MQI position. Between "coarse organization" and "no difference" sits *token-level difference within a reproduced type-level pattern*, which is causally efficacious without being organizational.
- **Suggested response**: see Issue 2. The type/token reply is the Map's, is already implicit in the bandwidth-architecture material the article cites, and converts an artificial dilemma into a stated position with a stated cost.

### The Map's grain reply concedes fine-grained functionalism

- **Current content says**: "If Invert and Nonvert differ at that interface, they were never fine-grained functional duplicates, and the dancing scenario does not arise for a genuine isomorph."
- **A critic would argue**: this concedes that a *genuine* fine-grained isomorph cannot be inverted — which is Chalmers' thesis, and which narrows the article's headline anti-functionalist claim ("if Invert and Nonvert share functional organization yet differ in qualitative character, qualia aren't functional kinds", § Why This Challenges Functionalism) to *coarse-grained* functionalism only. The Core Scenario and § Why This Challenges Functionalism both still state the strong version.
- **Suggested response**: this is not a defect to fix so much as a scope statement to make once. `organizational-invariance.md` L81 already has the right line — the invariance principle "points somewhere he does not follow: substrate can matter *because* the consciousness-relevant grain is finer than any classical functional duplicate reproduces" — i.e. the Map's target is *substrate-independent* functionalism, not functionalism at every grain. One clause in the new section pointing at that would keep the headline claim honest. Not urgent; noted for whoever takes the cluster.
