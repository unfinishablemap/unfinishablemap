## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

The article's own split-brain paragraph concedes my case: "the scope of phenomenal integration is constrained by neural architecture." Corpus callosum severed, integration splits; connectivity restored, it binds. Integration tracks anatomy everywhere we can measure it. What work is the "intrinsic phenomenal unity" doing beyond relabeling the neuroscience? The article never says what would differ observably if phenomenal unity were primitive versus constructed — its honest concession at L147 quietly gives me the explanatory structure and keeps only a metaphysical gloss.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

L159 is my view stated in the article's own voice: "The experienced continuity is constructed, not lived through" — the self as retrospective narrative integration, a center of narrative gravity. Having granted that waking continuity can be constructed, the article cannot then insist (L189) that felt continuity "reflects a genuine metaphysical unity, not an illusion." If construction suffices after every night's sleep, why posit metaphysical unity for the daytime intervals? The article arms the deflationary reading and then ignores it where it bites.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

"These influences accumulate across time" and "persist and compound" (L179–181) smuggle in a classical amplification story no one has modeled: the register's own P-Q10 records that no worked toy model of the coupling exists, and decoherence timescales in warm tissue leave no coherent state for consciousness to "favor patterns" over (L195). The article flags "This is speculative" — good — but then builds "diachronic agency becomes real" on the speculation two sections later. The hedge and the conclusion cannot both stand.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

The fracture argument (L135, L187) attacks a theory nobody holds. Decoherent branches do not interfere; every successor's synchronic binding, working memory, episodic recall, and narrative continuity are exactly as intact as yours. Nothing ever *feels* fragmented in Everett — the theory predicts precisely the unified experience the article cites against it. Your own corpus concedes this: `tenets.md` L183 grants "branch-relative Everettian agency being otherwise available," and `apex/one-world-wager` L40 states the Map "cannot show, using only resources the Everettian accepts, that branch-relative identity is false." The only live objection is indexical — no fact of which successor is *me* — and against first-personally centred variants (List 2023) even that drops to supplementary. This article runs neither concession.

### The Empiricist (Popper's Ghost)

The filter-theory option at L161 — "experience without neural encoding — consciousness continues but cannot later be remembered" — is constructed to be evidentially inert: any absence of memory confirms it, any presence of memory confirms it. Offered as one of two possibilities it is tolerable; note that the article gives no criterion that could ever favor it over the retrospective-construction alternative it sits beside. And "each alter experiences for-me-ness" (L151) asserts a first-person fact of other minds no observation could verify — reports are the data; the experience claim is doctrine.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

The article's best insight — "the self is not a static object but an ongoing achievement" — is the dependent-origination analysis, and it undermines the article's own reification. If selfhood is achieved moment-to-moment by integrative processes, then "haecceity, the property that makes this consciousness mine" is an added metaphysical posit doing no integrative work: the processes run the same with or without it. Claiming both that integration *constitutes* identity (L133) and that a primitive thisness *grounds* it (L131) is clinging to a self twice over, with two incompatible stories. `tenets.md` L121 already concedes the Madhyamaka route as a bedrock disagreement; this article writes as if that concession did not exist.
