## Executive Summary

The page is the corpus's most honest test of the Map's for-me-ness kind-claim, and its quotations are clean. Its weaknesses sit in the section that matters most.

1. **Grain.** The page tests only the claim's *global* falsifier: a whole phenomenal life without for-me-ness, "no position from which the lack is registered". No report could meet it. But the Map also holds the claim *per experience* ("no genuinely anonymous experiences exist"; this page's own L80, "for-me-ness universal"). The per-experience falsifier is one conscious episode not given for-me, registered from the patient's other experiences, and that *can* be reported. Lane's reading says thought insertion is exactly such a case. L92 answers Lane at the global grain.
2. **Equivocation.** "Given to the patient alone" (L94) is true for every party only in its *epistemic* sense: accessible to her alone. That is HPZ's own survival criterion, "The epistemic asymmetry is preserved". The survival verdict needs the *phenomenal* sense. The answer to Billon's §4.1 objection (L98) then rests the claim on the Cartesian principle, which restates the claim.
3. **Lane in the lead.** The lead (L36) and the description (L3) still say that no reading describes a conscious experience without first-personal givenness. By Lane's own account, as the page reports it, that is false of Lane.
4. **Rivals under strength.**

## Critiques by Philosopher

### (a) Billon (2013), on his §4.1 and on the "shared ground"

**Does the one-sentence answer (L98) answer §4.1?** It concedes the inference, which is right, and then says the kind-claim "must rest on the tie between phenomenality and subjectivity that Billon's own solution preserves". That tie is Billon's Cartesian principle, "If a thought is conscious then it is subjective" (L62). In the page's own wording the kind-claim is that for-me-ness belongs to conscious experience in kind, which is the same principle. So the answer rests the claim on itself.

Billon would add that he never argued *for* the principle. He showed that it can be kept, and called his account "consistent with the Cartesian principle" (p. 306). He kept it by adding a hypothesis: thoughts that are reflexively available but not phenomenally conscious, which he grants "would, however, benefit from an empirical confirmation" (p. 308). Resting the kind-claim on "the tie … Billon's own solution preserves" borrows his consistency result and presents it as support.

The answer also collides with L90. The page names HPZ as the kind-claim's "closest published statement", and HPZ's survival criterion (L70) is "The epistemic asymmetry is preserved", which is the inner-location premise §4.1 attacks. Once §4.1 is conceded, HPZ's verdict loses its stated criterion, and the page does not say so. Answer to brief (a): **it restates the claim.**

**Does "shared ground under a disputed name" still smuggle in the conclusion?** Yes, through one word. L94 says "Every party grants that the thought is given to the patient alone." On Billon's reading the thought is given to no one phenomenally; it is reflexively *available*. In the Map's vocabulary, "given" is the root of "first-personal givenness" (L36; [[self-and-self-consciousness]] L106), which is the disputed feature. The sentence is true of Billon only if "given" means "accessible". The bold header then says "It survives" on that ground, and survival needs the phenomenal sense. The header admits that the *name* is disputed but still asserts the *survival*. This is the epistemic/metaphysical equivocation the skill's check describes: evidence for the epistemic reading is used to secure the phenomenal one (Issue 2).

### (b) The ownership-loss theorist (Lane; Metzinger, via Zahavi and Kriegel; Martin and Pacherie)

**Is the reading given at full strength?** No.
- **No arguments.** "Ownership Lost" (L56–58) has three sentences of reported conclusions: two second-hand through ZK and one from an abstract. It gives no reason why anyone holds the view. The agency reading, by contrast, gets Billon's OCD objection with page citations.
- **Absorption.** L76 then recasts the dispute in Guillot's terms as concerning mineness, "though Lane denies more". The reading is placed so that Guillot's split absorbs it.
- **The wrong grain against Lane.** The one author who refuses absorption is answered at the wrong grain, as follows.

Lane (as ZK quote him, L58) holds that phenomenal consciousness is not stamped with a "meish quality" and that "for-me-ness does not play a determining role in its constitution". Applied to thought insertion, his claim concerns *this episode*. The inserted thought is phenomenally conscious and lacks for-me-ness, and the patient registers its alienness from her other experiences, which carry the trivial perspective he allows.

The falsifier the page tests (L96, quoted from [[self-and-self-consciousness]] L148) is global: "phenomenal life continuing without any residue of for-me-ness, no position from which the lack is registered". Its "no position" clause means a single episode's loss can never meet it, because the patient's other experiences always supply the position. L92 then answers Lane with "even Lane leaves the patient a first-person perspective". That is *the patient's* perspective, not the inserted thought's.

The Map does hold the per-episode claim:
- [[self-and-self-consciousness]] L74 says "no genuinely anonymous experiences exist".
- L146 of the same page names "thoughts experienced as alien" as the threat to the constitutive claim, then answers that threat with the global falsifier.
- This page's L80 resolves the collision between mine-ness and for-me-ness with "for-me-ness universal".

So the per-episode form has a falsifier that a report *can* meet, and Lane says it has been met. The Map's only answer to him is that the alien thought is still *in her* mind, which is the inner-location inference that L98 concedes. What remains is HPZ's contextual reading of the reports. The honest verdict for the per-experience form is therefore narrower than "untested": it is contested, and it turns on whether the reports are read at face value or in clinical context (Issue 1). The page's L110 already names that dispute as the one simplicity cannot settle, so the page has the materials; it has not applied them to its own claim.

**Metzinger** is placed in the mineness slot without argument. The page itself says self-model theory "recognises no subject beyond the model" (L86). If so, Metzinger's denial plausibly reaches the thin perspective too, since that perspective is a model product. This is second-hand only and recorded as a lead; it is not ranked.

### (c) The deflationary self-model theorist

The Tenet 1 verdict, *compatible*, holds, and this reviewer agrees with it. The physicalist reading, though, is not presented at full strength.

1. **The mechanism paragraph (L84) cites only failures and concessions.** Its four items are Frith's proposal, Synofzik et al.'s "can neither be extended", Frith 2012's "we still do not understand", and Sterzer et al.'s "no plausible account has been proposed". The research note's own *Physicalist Reading at Full Strength* (note L223) adds:

   - Jack et al. (2019): a "temporally-precise and content-specific corollary discharge".
   - Skipper et al. (2026).

   The note's verdict on that literature (note L219) is: "For thought, a forward-model signal for inner speech now exists, but no model accounts for the alienness of particular thoughts." The page dropped the positive half of that sentence.
2. **Misattributed concession.** L86 reads "Its weakness is a missing mechanism, which its own authors concede." Here "its" is the self-model account, but the concessions the page quotes are Frith's (comparator) and Sterzer et al.'s (predictive coding). Nothing on the page shows Metzinger conceding anything.
3. **One-sided weakness ledger.** The interface reading has no mechanism for why *particular* thoughts lose mineness either. Because that parity is never stated, the mechanism gap becomes the last word on the physicalist reading. The page disclaims the gap argument explicitly, which is good, but the one-sided ledger still does that argument's work. Answer to brief (c): **"no mechanism explains the symptom yet" is formally disclaimed but still tilts the comparison.**
4. **Prediction or accommodation (L104).** "The Map's interface reading and the self-model reading predict the same profile." The page never derives the profile from the interface reading. Self-model theory has a structure that yields it: a thin feature whenever the model runs, plus content-specific tags that can drop out or misfire. The interface reading accommodates the profile. This is the same correction the IBE page received on 2026-10-01.
5. **"The profile all five readings report" (L86).** The five readings report *incompatible* profiles: phenomenal or not, ownership kept or lost. What self-model theory predicts is the core they share: the thought is accessible to the patient alone, and she disowns it.
6. **Recorded, not ranked.** Self-model theory and the kind-claim come apart in one place only: phenomenal content with no self-model running, which is exactly where no report can reach. The concession that the claim cannot be tested is therefore also the location of the only disagreement between the Map and its strongest rival. The Occam paragraph (L110) could say this in a clause.

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

The triad of for-me-ness, me-ness and mineness has been multiplied out of report verbs: three nouns for what may be one neural kind or none. The measured corollary discharge for inner speech is exactly the kind of progress the page leaves out. The retreat of the double dissociation (L78) is the pattern Churchland predicts, folk categories fragmenting under clinical pressure. **Response available:** the triad is offered as vocabulary hygiene for philosophers and is neutral on how it is realised neurally. The page could say so in a clause. Low.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

On the heterophenomenological view the data are the reports, and "the thought is not mine" is a judgement. Whether it reflects missing phenomenal mineness or a misattribution is underdetermined, and Mullins and Spence leave the belief reading open. The kind-claim section reasons about what the patient's experience "keeps" from reports that the page, through HPZ, says should not be taken at face value. Dennett would press further: once the reports are read in context, nothing is left to settle what is phenomenally kept, and the five readings become five theories of how the reports are generated. Issue 5 covers the first step of this.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

The page makes no quantum claim, and its Relation to Site Perspective rightly omits Tenet 2. Tegmark would find nothing to attack. The L106 caution, that felt agency is an unreliable guide to causal origin, is well judged. Nothing to fix.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

L108 reads "Coherence only: 'not mine' presupposes a determinate subject to whom the thought is given." Every branch-relative observer in many-worlds is also a determinate subject, so "not mine" is just as coherent there. The sentence ties the tenet to nothing a many-worlds reader denies. *Neutral* is the honest tier, or the line could go. Low.

### The Empiricist (Popper's Ghost)

**Credit.** "Unrefuted but untested" and "none yet exist" are honest in the way Popper asks for.

**Push.** On the third-person route, any marker of phenomenal presence is calibrated on subjects who report, and who therefore have first-personal access. And because the claim is constitutive, a universalist can always read a presence-without-access dissociation as a failure of the marker. Unless the Map says in advance which marker result it would accept as disconfirming, "none yet exist" names a route the claim can always decline to take. The stability note rightly keeps the route open; the improvement is a precommitment, not a retraction. Recorded.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

Contemplation of not-self applies the formula *this is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self* to every thought, deliberately and without pathology, a non-clinical disowning the page ignores. In that practice "not mine" presupposes no determinate subject, only a conventional designation, so L108's inference is weaker than stated. The contemplative case would also make a second comparison case, alongside OCD intrusions, of disowning without any sense of insertion. Low; recorded.

## Counterarguments to Address

### "Lane's alien thought is a per-episode counterexample"
- **Current content says**: the kind-claim survives because "even Lane leaves the patient a first-person perspective" (L92).
- **A critic would argue**: Lane never denied the patient a perspective. He denies that *this thought* is for-her. The global falsifier cannot register a counterexample confined to one episode, so it was the wrong test.
- **Suggested response**: concede the grain point. State that the per-experience form survives Lane only on the universalists' contextual reading of the reports. Name what clinical finding would count against it (Priority item 1).

### "Accessible to her alone is not for-me-ness"
- **Current content says**: every party grants that the thought is "given to the patient alone" (L94).
- **A critic would argue** (Billon, Lane): what is granted is reflexive availability, or "hosting". Calling it for-me-ness is the conclusion under dispute.
- **Suggested response**: write "accessible", say that this epistemic asymmetry is HPZ's own criterion, and say that the claim survives on this ground only if the asymmetry is phenomenal (Priority item 2).

### "Your answer to §4.1 is the Cartesian principle restated"
- **Current content says**: the kind-claim "must rest on the tie between phenomenality and subjectivity that Billon's own solution preserves" (L98).
- **A critic would argue**: that tie is the claim itself, and Billon only showed it *can* be kept, at a price that awaits "empirical confirmation".
- **Suggested response**: present the claim as a commitment the case does not support, and name Billon's price (Priority item 2(c)).

### "The physicalist has a signal and you have no mechanism"
- **Current content says**: the self-model reading's "weakness is a missing mechanism" (L86).
- **A critic would argue**: inner speech has a measured, content-specific corollary discharge. The interface reading has no mechanism at all for why *particular* thoughts become alien.
- **Suggested response**: add the evidence, state the parity, and write "accommodates" for the interface reading (Priority item 4).
