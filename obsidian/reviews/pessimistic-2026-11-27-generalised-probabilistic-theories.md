## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

The GPT framework is operational through and through: states, effects, probabilities. L78 makes the non-physical substrate a *subsystem* of a GPT composite, "the substrate the purifying environment". Then one of two things holds.

- The substrate has states and effects that compose with the brain's under a tensor rule. By the only criterion the framework has, that makes it one more physical system, and the dualism is nominal.
- Or it has no states and effects, and then it cannot purify anything.

Churchland would say that the article's most interesting move has quietly naturalised its own substrate. The page never says which horn it takes.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

L56 proposes "a coupling that selects outcomes without disturbing statistics". In a framework defined *entirely* by statistics, a coupling that changes no statistic is a difference that makes no difference. Dennett would say the page has proved, in its own preferred formalism, that the selection it posits is invisible to everything the formalism can see. L52 says the framework "makes Tenet 2 ... statable in exact terms". It makes the *statistical constraint* statable. The selection itself lies below the framework's resolution, and the page never says so.

### The Quantum Skeptic

L78 calls the brain's mixedness "the residue of tracing [the substrate] out". Tegmark would answer that the brain's reduced state is mixed because it is entangled with its physical thermal environment, on decoherence timescales of 10⁻¹³–10⁻²⁰ s. Nothing is left over for a substrate to explain. Worse, an environment that *purifies* a system is one that *decoheres* it. On this reading consciousness becomes the agent that destroys coherence, not the one that exploits it.

L88's "least of all inside a brain at interaction-relevant precision" hints that brains are an untested regime. But NMR and MRI run on quantum spin statistics in living brain tissue. The page needs to say *which* statistics are untested, or drop the hint.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

This is the strongest critique, and it uses the Map's own sources. Galley and Masanes write that "the Church of the Larger Hilbert Space is illegitimate" in theories without purification. They add that agents-as-reversible-transformations "is also the starting point of the many-worlds interpretation of quantum theory". They frame their theorem as "an important clue for the problem of deriving the Born rule within the many-worlds interpretation".

Chiribella 2018's closed systems, in which all transformations are invertible and all states are generated from a fixed initial state, describe an Everett universe in operational dress. The page leans on that axiom (§ Purification) and reads the Map's dualism as positing exactly such a closed whole (L78). It then reassures the reader that GPTs "make no appeal to branching" (L98).

Deutsch would grant that the *framework* is neutral. The *axiom the Map picked* is not, by its authors' own account. If brain-plus-substrate is a closed, purified, reversibly evolving whole, the Map owes an account of where an outcome ever happens. Otherwise it has rebuilt the universal state under a dualist label.

### The Empiricist

The page is candid that the framework "does no work *for* the interface reading over its rivals" (L102), and that candour is to its credit. But Popper would press on what follows:

- A Born-rule violation found in brains would, on the page's honest conditional, register as a failure of purification or local tomography. That is a fact about GPT structure, not evidence for consciousness.
- A Born-respecting brain is predicted by the Map as well.
- And because Torres Alegre's theorem covers only rules of the form P = Φ(τ) (Finding 4), a *mind-conditioned* rule escapes it altogether. So "any agent coupling to quantum outcomes is Born-constrained" (L28) is not even a consequence the Map could be held to.

Both outcomes are absorbed, and the constraint does not bind the case of interest.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Chiribella 2018, which the page cites, opens with this: "The choice of subsystems, however, is not defined a priori". It associates each agent with the subsystem its operations carve out, and it defines purity *dynamically*, relative to those operations. Nagarjuna would recognise this as dependent origination in formal dress. No subsystem has a state of its own (*svabhāva*). Every mixedness is the shadow of a larger whole, and the cut between "brain" and "substrate" is a convention.

The page uses this result to *fix* a substrate with a determinate role, "the purifying environment". It reifies one side of a decomposition that its own source calls agent-relative.

### Issue 2: Purification does not forbid selection; CDP's own dilation theorem relocates it

- **File**: same
- **Location**: L72 ("so irreversibility is always apparent rather than fundamental"), L80 ("An interface at which consciousness *selects* an outcome looks like an irreducible process, which purification forbids"), and the dependent L82 ("or is selection an irreducible process within it?"). All three were added in `c0dfc8fa2e` (2026-08-17).
- **Problem**: CDP 2010 (arXiv:0908.1583v5, §IX) proves two separate dilation theorems:
  - **Theorem 15 (Reversible dilation of channels)**: this is the "eventually discarded" realisation the abstract describes.
  - **Theorem 16 (Reversible dilation of tests)**: "For every test {Cᵢ} from system A to system B there exist a system C, a pure state ϕ₀ ..., a reversible channel U ..., and an observation-test {cᵢ} on C".

  An outcome-selecting interface is a *test*. In a theory with purification, every test is a reversible interaction followed by an observation on an ancilla. So selection is not forbidden; it is moved into that observation. The section's "pulls the other way" argument therefore rests on an appearance ("looks like an irreducible process") that the cited paper's own theorem dispels.

  L72 compounds this. The theorem shows that every channel *can be represented* as reversible-plus-discard. The gloss turns that into the claim that irreversibility *is* "always apparent rather than fundamental", and for tests even the representation keeps a final observation. This is **interpretive equivocation** (representability recruited for constitution). The discipline classes it as *critical*, and hedge density will not catch it.
- **Severity**: High. The section's two-directional structure depends on L80. The 08-25 review praised that structure ("genuinely good philosophy and survives intact") after checking the quotations but not Theorem 16.
- **Recommendation**:
  - Rewrite L80 so that selection is a test which purification relocates to an ancilla observation. Keep the Galley–Masanes "irreducible classicality" point as what purification *failure* would add.
  - Scope L72 to channels.
  - Re-aim L82's closing question at *where the selecting observation falls*, which is the real residue and is the measurement problem in operational form.
  - **Pronoun/dependent check**: L82 opens "Neither argument settles the matter ... Both turn on one question". It must still read correctly after L80 changes.
