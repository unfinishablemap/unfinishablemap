## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found (both fixed)

**1. Wrong-work attribution — "Thoughts and things are absolutely homogeneous…" (was L47).** The blockquote sat immediately beneath the paragraph expounding the 1904 essay "Does 'Consciousness' Exist?", presenting it as that essay's words. The 07-12 ledger recorded it as "1904 / *Essays in Radical Empiricism* — verbatim-confirmed (prior ledger)" — an inherited, never-independently-checked attribution.

*Verification*: grepped the complete Gutenberg text of *Essays in Radical Empiricism* (which contains the 1904 essay as Essay I). The phrase "homogeneous as to their material" occurs **exactly once in the entire book**, and not in Essay I. It is in **Essay V, "The Place of Affectional Facts in a World of Pure Experience"** — originally *Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods*, vol. II, No. 11, May 25, **1905** (footnote 75). The primary reads:

> "In opposition to this dualistic philosophy, I tried, in [the first essay] to show that thoughts and things are absolutely homogeneous as to their material, and that their opposition is only one of relation and of function."

So the sentence is genuine James, and it *is about* the 1904 essay — it is James's own retrospective summary of it, written a year later. Right author, right substance, **wrong work**: the textbook trap named in the targeting note. The article had also silently dropped the second "that" ("and their opposition" for "and that their opposition"), converting a reported subordinate clause into a free-standing assertion.

*Fix*: re-attributed (not deleted) with a lead-in pinning it to the 1905 essay and flagging it as James looking back on 1904; restored the dropped "that" and marked the lift with a leading ellipsis. Covered by the existing References entry for *Essays in Radical Empiricism* (1912), so no new reference needed.

**2. Word put in James's mouth — "reducing" (was L174).** The "Neutral monism tensions" section argued: *James's own verbs—"reducing", "permitting", "transmitting"—presuppose something to reduce or permit.* The argument is sound but the evidence was fabricated: **"reducing" is not James's word.**

*Verification*: pulled the full text of *Human Immortality* and counted. `reducing` → **0 occurrences**. `reduce` → 1 occurrence, in an unrelated passage about population ("unreduced in numbers"). James's actual vocabulary for the brain's function is `permissive` (×2), `transmissive` (×5), `permits`, `obstructiveness`, and the prism analogy's "**sifted and limited**": *"In the case of a colored glass, a prism, or a refracting lens, we have transmissive function. The energy of light, no matter how produced, is by the glass sifted and limited in color…"*

"Reducing valve" is **Bergson's and Huxley's**, not James's — a point the article itself makes three sections earlier ("developed by subsequent thinkers (Bergson, Huxley)"), so the article was also **internally inconsistent**. Corpus grep confirms every other file credits the reducing valve to Bergson/Huxley; the 2026-07-17 terminal-lucidity review even de-quoted a Bergson "selective valve" while noting "'reducing valve' is Huxley's." This article was the lone outlier.

*Fix*: replaced with James's real language ("permissive or transmissive", sifting and limiting a light it does not itself produce), adjusted the follow-on to "something to sift or permit", and added a one-clause guard noting the reducing-valve formulation is Bergson's and Huxley's — cheap insurance against reintroduction. The argument is unweakened; it is now actually supported by James's text.

### Claim-fidelity findings (second lens)

- **"mind-dust" objection → *Principles* (1890)** — confirmed. The term appears in Ch. VI "The Mind-Stuff Theory," including the section heading "EVOLUTIONARY PSYCHOLOGY DEMANDS A MIND-DUST." Attribution correct; the "many small feelings / one big feeling" gloss is unquoted paraphrase and faithful.
- **Prism analogy** — James does use "a colored glass, a prism, or a refracting lens." The article's unquoted gloss ("a prism doesn't create colors but reveals them") captures transmissive function correctly. No defect.
- **Filter-theory strength** — correctly hedged throughout ("might be transmissive", "James didn't develop this idea in detail"). James proposed transmission as a *permissible* alternative, not a commitment, and the article does not over-attribute. No defect.
- **Mother-sea reading — strengthened.** The article leans on the mother-sea language as evidence of "an independent reservoir pre-existing any brain." James's **Preface to the Second Edition** bears directly on this and was not previously consulted: answering critics who read transmission as implying survival only in a world-soul, James lets the pre-existing reservoir stand but disowns the monistic reading, writing that he is "anything but a pantheist of the monistic pattern" and had spoken of the mother-sea as a unit only "for simplicity's sake." Added compactly — it sharpens the section's own thesis (the tension is real and unresolved) and matters to the Map, which commits to a mental substance rather than a pantheistic unit. Both quoted fragments verbatim-verified.
