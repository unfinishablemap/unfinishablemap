## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would recognise the shape of `weak-measurement-and-post-selection.md`'s central move as a genuine attempt at the thing he says is hard: giving the explanatory gap a *structure* rather than a slogan. The post-selection framing relocates consciousness from collapse-trigger to boundary condition, and the article states the payoff crisply — "Consciousness's proposed role is selecting the target, not triggering the transition." That is a real distinction doing real work, and it is the kind of proposal Chalmers has spent a career asking physicalists to produce and not receiving.

He would be most pleased by what the article refuses. Section "What Laboratory Post-Selection Actually Involves" concedes that in standard physics post-selection "is not a physical process that could be 'implemented' by anything, including consciousness. It is a method of conditional data analysis." An author defending a thesis does not usually write the sentence that most embarrasses it, in its own voice, under its own heading.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp would find `transactional-interpretation-of-quantum-mechanics.md` the most useful new article on the site. It does the thing his own programme has always needed and rarely got: it identifies *where in the formalism* the mental act could sit, without hand-waving. Kastner's pre-spacetime possibility realm is named as a **venue** for selection rather than a mechanism, and the article is explicit that this is a borrowing: "The pre-spacetime realm is borrowed as a *venue* for selection, not as a self-actualizing mechanism."

He would also appreciate that the Map has not simply absorbed him. `von-neumann-wigner-interpretation.md` L94 registers the Process-1-only relocation as "an alternative the Map could take but declines" — his framework is preserved as "the historically sharpest successor to von Neumann–Wigner and a useful contrast pole, not as the placement the Map favours." Being declined with reasons is better treatment than being co-opted.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Nagel's contribution here is diagnostic rather than celebratory, and the cluster passes his test in an unexpected place. `time-symmetric-physics.md` L114–118 gives Price's argument that resistance to backward causation "reflects cognitive bias—our evolved perspective as forward-moving agents—not physical principle," and then, rather than banking the point, states the objection: mathematical symmetry is not causal symmetry. Nagel would say this is the right way round. The subjective standpoint is where the bias lives, and an article that argues *from* the standpoint had better also argue *about* it.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would read the transactional interpretation as very nearly his own metaphysics in physicists' notation, and he would be right. Cramer's completed transaction is not a substance but an *event* constituted across an interval — the article says so directly: "The completed transaction is better thought of as **atemporal**: it exists across the whole emission-absorption interval rather than at a single moment inside it." Kastner's actualisation of possibility into spacetime event is concrescence under another name, and the article's report that "the network of actualized transactions is what *constitutes* spacetime" is process philosophy arriving from the physics side.

**Held to the evidential-status constraint.** None of this resonance is used to upgrade anything. The article does not argue that because TI is process-friendly, TI is more likely true, or that the Map's selection reading gains support from the fit. It says the opposite: TI "is a physicalist, observer-independent interpretation" that "conflicts with the Map's dualism," and what is borrowed is machinery held "explicitly at arm's length from the physicalism." That is *tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating*, and the article writes it that way without being asked.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane's interest is in whether the Libet objection is *answered* or merely *routed around*, and `forward-in-time-vs-time-symmetric-selection.md` gives him the cleanest statement of the trade in the corpus. The time-symmetric route "absorbs the Libet timing problem rather than answering it" — and the article immediately charges for the absorption: "this dissolution is only as secure as the retrocausal interpretation it rests on, which remains contested rather than established." Kane has watched libertarians buy Libet answers on credit for thirty years. This one comes with the invoice attached.

He would also value the article's refusal to let the ledger settle the metaphysics: L102 says plainly that a reader "who holds presentism or temporal becoming on independent grounds will find the forward route cheaper *for them*." The agency question is left where it belongs — with the reader's own commitments.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

McGinn's praise goes to `weak-measurement-and-post-selection.md` L122, which contains the single best-calibrated sentence in the cluster and possibly the month:

> "Because the theory predicts statistical identity, a confirmed difference between conscious and unconscious measurement contexts would falsify it, while agreement merely shows the constraint is honoured rather than vindicating the boundary-condition reading. The asymmetry matters: the constraint can fail the theory but cannot establish it."

A no-signalling constraint that the theory *passes* is not evidence for the theory. Almost every quantum-consciousness proposal in the literature gets this backwards and treats Born-rule compliance as a virtue earned rather than a bar cleared. The article states the asymmetry, names which direction the inference runs, and then closes the section by conceding that its own value "lies in providing a physically grounded alternative to collapse-based consciousness models, not in immediate testability. This is a genuine limitation, not a virtue."

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

Birch's verdict is **largely favourable, with one over-concession and one under-priced bill** — and both errors run in the *safe* direction, which is why nobody has caught them.

**What is praise-worthily not done.** This cluster had every structural opportunity to slide the time-symmetric route up the evidential scale on tenet-load, and declines at each one. Tenet-3 (bidirectional interaction) makes retrocausality *congenial* — `weak-measurement-and-post-selection.md` L134 says as much, "making [[retrocausality]] a natural feature rather than an awkward addition." A weaker article would have run that congeniality as support. This one instead states at L104 that "neither Aharonov nor other TSVF proponents propose consciousness as the source of post-selection, and the Map should not be read as claiming their authority for this extension," and lands at "This remains a speculative hypothesis." Tenet-coherence removed a defeater; it did not buy a tier. That is the honest pattern, executed without prompting.

**The propagation is clean too.** Vedovato et al. (2017) is the cluster's most upgrade-tempting citation — real, replicated, satellite-scale delayed choice. `time-symmetric-physics.md` L154 holds it at "The results are interpretable without retrocausality, but they're also precisely what retrocausal interpretations predict," and `forward-in-time-vs-time-symmetric-selection.md` L74 *sharpens* rather than softens on inheritance: "They do not confirm the time-symmetric route; they are consistent with it." A citation getting **more** careful as it propagates is the opposite of the usual drift and deserves naming.

**The over-concession.** Ferrie & Combes (2014) is the deflationary reading on which the Map's whole TSVF borrowing turns, and `weak-measurement-and-post-selection.md` treats it with exemplary seriousness — three prose citations plus the entire Falsifiability section. But it presents the paper as an undisputed statement of the statistical position, and closes by saying the question "is one the physics community is actively pursuing rather than treating as closed." The corpus does not record *how* it is being pursued: Ferrie & Combes drew a published Comment in the same journal — Brodutch, *PRL* 114(11), 118901 (2015) — plus arXiv comments from Vaidman (1409.5386), Aharonov & Rohrlich (1410.0381), and Cohen (1409.8555). A corpus-wide grep across `obsidian/` and `archive/` returns **zero** mentions of any of them. Birch's point is not that the Map should stop conceding; it is that the concession is currently *larger than the record supports*, and it has stood since 2026-03-29 for exactly the reason such errors stand — an over-claim running against the house position collects no objections. Running the concession direction as its own pass is what would have caught it.

**The under-priced bill.** See "Calibration Concerns" below: the cost ledger omits the Maudlin objection.
