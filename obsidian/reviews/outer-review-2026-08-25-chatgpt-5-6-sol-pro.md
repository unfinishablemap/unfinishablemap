# External referee report: *Edge-States and the Probing of Voids*

**Recommendation: Major revision**

## Preliminary correction on the revision date

The article was not substantively revised on 18 August 2026. The changelog describes that change as an incidental repair of two links from the archived `mind-space-void` page to its successor, explicitly stating that no prose was added or removed. The last identifiable argumentative revision was on 8 August, when the site corrected the attribution of Metzinger’s “zero-person perspective.” For purposes of judging currency, the substantive text should therefore be dated **8 August 2026**, with 18 August recorded separately as maintenance.

## Overall assessment

The article contains one of the Map’s better statements of evidential discipline. It says that compatibility with dualism is not support for dualism, discounts several allegedly independent observations as consequences of a common underlying pattern, refuses to recruit Metzinger as a dualist ally, and treats near-death experiences more cautiously than many neighbouring pages.

That methodological discipline is not maintained throughout. The article’s opening presents a cross-domain “edge-experience profile” as an established regularity and says the evidence partly discriminates between transcendence and edge-mapping. Later it concedes that the leading metaphysical interpretations remain roughly equally consistent with the evidence and that no comparative likelihood ledger has been supplied. Its strongest methodological paragraph therefore undercuts its lead, several intermediate claims, and much of its conclusion. [voids/edge-states-and-void-probes](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/edge-states-and-void-probes/)

The central reasoning also compresses four distinct propositions:

1. Similar motifs occur in reports from some altered states.
2. Those motifs constitute a single latent phenomenological profile.
3. That profile reveals the structure of a cognitive boundary.
4. The boundary has implications for dualism, filter theory, subjecthood or mental causation.

The cited evidence gives some support to the first proposition. It does not currently establish the second or third, while the article itself ultimately concedes that the fourth does not follow. The article is therefore more defensible as a **research programme for comparing candidate boundary phenomena** than as a completed synthesis of what edge-states show.

## 1. Do the central claims rest on sufficient evidence?

### 1.1 The cross-domain “edge profile” is asserted more strongly than it is demonstrated

The article says that ineffability, noesis, ego dissolution, temporal distortion, epistemic openness and unusual clarity recur across meditation, psychedelics, near-death experiences, aesthetic absorption and philosophical inquiry. But it supplies no systematic review, coding protocol, inclusion criteria, comparison group, prevalence estimates or measure of cross-domain similarity.

The evidential basis differs radically by domain:

- Psychedelic research has questionnaires, dose-response studies and neuroimaging, though its constructs remain disputed.
- Meditation research contains heterogeneous practices, retrospective reports, expert samples and lineage-dependent vocabularies.
- Near-death experience research has severe timing and survivor-selection problems.
- The claims about aesthetic absorption and philosophical inquiry are supported largely by examples and analogies.
- James and Stace are historically important taxonomists, not contemporary demonstrations of measurement invariance across these domains.

Recent psychometric work is directly relevant here. Canby and colleagues found substantial overlap among constructs such as mystical experience, nondual awareness, selflessness, self-transcendence and ego dissolution, but also found that the constructs are imprecisely distinguished and insufficiently integrated with clinically adverse or pathological analogues. That result supports a family of recurrent motifs; it does not validate the article’s singular “edge-experience profile.” [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38346364/)

There is also a selection circularity. Experiences are classified as “edge-states” partly because they display the proposed profile, and recurrence of the profile among those selected experiences is then offered as evidence that edge-states form a genuine class. The article needs an operational definition of an edge-state that does not depend on the very phenomenology whose convergence is being tested. [voids/edge-states-and-void-probes](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/edge-states-and-void-probes/)

The claim that confused processing should produce “idiosyncratic chaos” is especially weak. Shared neural architecture, common pharmacology, cultural transmission, common questionnaires, common training traditions and common human memory limitations can produce systematic errors or recurring report structures. Orderliness does not discriminate veridical access from structured construction.

A defensible formulation would be:

> Several heterogeneous state classes produce partly overlapping report motifs. Whether these motifs reflect one latent boundary structure, several distinct mechanisms, or measurement and cultural common causes remains unresolved.

### 1.2 “Edge-clarity” and aesthetic or philosophical convergence lack an evidential base

“Edge-clarity” is presented as a recurring feature in which conceptual processing fails while experience becomes exceptionally clear. No direct evidence is supplied that this is a stable construct, that it differs from confidence or processing fluency, or that it occurs at comparable rates across the listed domains.

The aesthetic and philosophical sections are thinner still. The article says that profound beauty produces the “full edge-profile” and that inquiry by Cusanus, Wittgenstein and phenomenologists produces similar experiences. These may be plausible phenomenological observations, but they are not presently evidence for cross-domain convergence. They need either:

- systematic phenomenological studies;
- explicit textual analyses with independently coded dimensions; or
- downgrading to illustrative hypotheses.

At present, these domains appear to have been added because they fit the organizing idea, not because comparable evidence was located.

### 1.3 Cessation is described as absence of consciousness without evidence capable of establishing that

The article describes contemplative cessation as a “complete stopping of phenomenal consciousness” that crosses the edge entirely. That wording exceeds both first-person and neurophysiological evidence. A later report cannot distinguish complete absence of experience from absence of memory, metacognitive access or reportable content.

The most directly relevant 2024 neurophenomenological study analysed 37 reported cessation events in one intensively sampled expert practitioner. It provides potentially valuable within-person neural observations, but a single participant’s reports and associated EEG changes cannot establish that phenomenal consciousness itself was absent. The appropriate expression is **reported cessation**, or **a state subsequently described as involving discontinuity of experience**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10548-024-01052-4)

This matters beyond terminological caution. “Cessation without consciousness” and “minimal phenomenal experience without ordinary subject-object structure” are not neighbouring points on a single demonstrated scale. One concerns an asserted absence; the other concerns an unusually sparse but allegedly conscious state. The article currently moves between them too freely.

### 1.4 The dissolving-insight phenomenon has not been shown to be reliable or distinctive

The article says that edge-states “reliably” produce insights that disappear when one attempts to recover them. No incidence data, prospective sampling, comparison with ordinary forgetting, or operational distinction from tip-of-the-tongue states is supplied.

Its own noetic-feelings explanation is stronger than the text admits. A feeling-of-knowing mechanism can explain why certainty remains when retrievable propositional content is absent. On that model there need never have been an insight to lose. The alternative hypothesis—contact with representationally closed territory—requires independent evidence that content existed and was epistemically successful before it became unavailable.

The recent FIBUS framework is particularly important. It argues that psychedelic states may generate both insight-like experiences and false beliefs, while the mechanisms increasing salience and felt significance do not preferentially select truth. Recent work on noetic quality likewise treats it as a metacognitive feeling of epistemic gain rather than evidence that gain occurred. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-024-00120-6)

The current article labels the two explanations “genuinely ambiguous,” but that symmetry is not earned. The noetic-feeling account is supported by established metacognitive mechanisms; representational closure is introduced largely to redescribe the phenomenon. The latter remains possible, but it has not received an evidential update.

### 1.5 The reducing-valve section ultimately refutes its own “partial support” language

The article initially says contemporary neuroscience provides partial support for the reducing-valve hypothesis, principally because intense experience can accompany reduced default-mode coherence or increased network desynchronization. It later correctly observes that predictive-processing production accounts expect much the same pattern and that metabolic findings do not distinguish filtering from production.

The later assessment is the defensible one. A correlation between reduced constraint and increased phenomenal intensity does not tell us whether the brain:

- produces less constrained experience;
- filters an independently existing consciousness less strongly; or
- changes an interface between two ontologically distinct systems.

All three can accommodate the observation by assigning different metaphysical roles to the same neural mechanism. Thus the imaging is **compatible with** filter theory but supplies no evidential preference for it. [voids/edge-states-and-void-probes](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/edge-states-and-void-probes/)

The REBUS characterization is also too strong. REBUS concerns reduced precision weighting of high-level priors relative to lower-level signals, not a demonstrated state in which conceptual priors “suspend entirely.” The latter converts a graded computational proposal into an all-or-nothing phenomenological claim. [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10077110/)

The article commendably includes ALBUS, but uses only part of its significance. ALBUS allows psychedelics to strengthen as well as relax beliefs and highlights the roles of dose, set and setting. Its broader relevance is that the same conditions producing openness may also produce apophenia, paranoia, inaccurate interpretation and false memory. These phenomena bear directly on the epistemic status of noesis and should appear in the edge-mapping discussion, not only in a technical qualification of the valve metaphor. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niae038/8011394)

Finally, the neuroscience synthesis has already moved beyond reliance on an individual 2024 psilocybin study. A 2026 multi-dataset analysis covered five psychedelic drugs and more than 500 scans, identifying a broader pattern involving altered coupling between transmodal and unimodal systems and substantial variation across networks and drugs. Siegel et al. remains relevant, but no longer suffices as the article’s primary summary of the field. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04287-9)

### 1.6 The near-death section is comparatively strong but contains two framing errors

The article is right to reject the inference from a flat or absent scalp EEG to total absence of consciousness-relevant processing. It is also right that the timing of an experience later reported after resuscitation is generally underdetermined.

Its description of AWARE-II needs correction. Among 567 in-hospital cardiac arrests, 53 people survived, 28 completed interviews and 11 reported memories or perceptions suggestive of consciousness. That is an extreme attrition structure, but “self-selected subset” is not the clearest characterization: the subset was primarily created by survival, health and interview availability rather than ordinary volunteer self-selection. The lack of confirmed visual-target identifications is important, but hidden-target perception was one component of a wider physiological and phenomenological investigation, not the study’s sole or unambiguous primary test. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37423492/)

The section’s final evidential ranking—NDEs as the weakest leg—is justified. The neighbouring `near-death-experiences` article should be brought into line with it.

### 1.7 The proposed discriminators are promising headings, not yet tests

The article proposes propositional convergence, practically novel information and intersubjective verification as possible discriminators. These are reasonable starting points, but each requires an adversarial protocol.

Propositional convergence would need independently generated statements, blinded coding, preregistered similarity criteria and controls for common teachings. Practical prediction would require targets genuinely inaccessible through ordinary inference, leakage or latent learning. Intersubjective verification would require precommitted reports and criteria established before participants communicate.

Without those conditions:

- propositional similarity can result from shared traditions;
- novelty can result from recombination or previously implicit learning;
- apparent triangulation can result from mutual influence and flexible interpretation.

The article is correct that a transcendence hypothesis made permanently noncommunicable and nonpredictive receives no evidential upgrade. That rule should be applied with the same severity to “contact with a boundary” and “dissolving insight” claims.

## 3. Counterarguments not adequately engaged

### Shared architecture can produce structured convergence

The article acknowledges common cause late in the discussion, but does not apply it to the construction of the central profile. Similar nervous systems, common memory constraints and common attentional architecture can generate similar phenomenology without access to a common external territory. Shared concepts such as unity, ego dissolution, ineffability and timelessness may also be transmitted through religious teaching, popular psychedelic discourse and questionnaire design.

This possibility is more powerful than the article’s contrast between veridical convergence and “idiosyncratic chaos.” The relevant physicalist alternative is not random confusion; it is **structured construction by a shared system**.

### The relevant constructs may be related without forming a single natural kind

Ego dissolution can involve reduced narrative self-reference, altered body ownership, loss of spatial self-location, altered agency or weakened autobiographical continuity in different combinations. Mystical-type experience, nondual awareness, selflessness and dissociation overlap without being interchangeable. Recent construct work makes this heterogeneity a central problem, whereas the article treats Millière’s multidimensional analysis as though it supported a unified profile. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38346364/)

### Retrospective report may reconstruct rather than preserve edge phenomenology

Many of the article’s most important observations are made after the state has ended. Reports of timelessness, absence of self, total understanding or absence of experience may be reconstructions produced when ordinary memory and conceptual systems attempt to interpret an unusual or poorly encoded state. The article discusses content becoming unavailable but does not examine whether the original characterization itself is generated during recall.

This is particularly important for cessation, NDEs and dissolving insights. A failure to remember content is not evidence that the state contained content that exceeded conceptual capacity, nor is failure to remember experience evidence that experience was absent.

### Loss of the narrative or bodily self does not establish absence of minimal subjectivity

The article moves from altered self-models to the conclusion that “the subject is not essential.” Metzinger’s account makes that possibility intelligible, but it remains contested whether a state could be experienced at all without minimal for-me-ness or pre-reflective subjectivity.

A directly relevant paper published on 13 August 2026—after the article’s actual substantive revision—challenges inferences from psychedelic ego-dissolution reports to complete absence of self-awareness. It argues that changes to represented personal identity need not eliminate minimal subjectivity. This should now be incorporated as a current counterposition. [Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11097-026-10186-8)

### Felt insight can increase vulnerability to error and social influence

The article treats noetic quality mainly as either genuine boundary contact or an internal heuristic misfire. It omits the social dimension: heightened openness combined with certainty, salience or suggestibility may make beliefs unusually responsive to therapists, facilitators, traditions and surrounding narratives. Recent social-epistemological work argues that psychedelic states can produce a distinctive vulnerability to external influence precisely because openness and felt certainty coexist. [Pure](https://pure.au.dk/portal/da/publications/on-the-social-epistemology-of-psychedelic-experience/)

This weakens inferences from cross-person agreement. Agreement may reflect common facilitators, texts or expectations rather than independent contact with the same territory.

### Pragmatic value does not establish metaphysical or propositional truth

Therapeutic improvement, emotional reorganization or reduced confidence in negative self-beliefs can be important without validating the metaphysical content associated with an experience. A false or metaphorical model can sometimes be psychologically useful. The article says practical yield is therapeutic but not clearly epistemically novel, which is correct, but it does not apply that distinction consistently when describing noesis as genuine contact with the structure of limits.

### Ordinary top-down causation does not distinguish interactionist dualism

Meditation, intention and attention can alter behaviour and neural activity under physicalism, functionalism, emergentism and dualism. Demonstrating that a person’s intention predicts neural change is not yet evidence that a nonphysical consciousness supplied an additional cause. The article needs an argument showing why the relevant mental description cannot be realized by or identical with physical control processes.

## 4. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article contains both good and bad examples of handling the Map’s commitments.

### Where it handles the tenets well

The section on the relation to the site perspective correctly says that edge-state convergence is compatible with dualism but does not independently support it. It also recognizes neutral monist, panpsychist and physicalist alternatives, and explicitly notes the absence of a comparative evidential ledger. This is exactly the distinction the Map’s methodology requires. [voids/edge-states-and-void-probes](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/edge-states-and-void-probes/)

The Metzinger section is similarly disciplined: the dualist possibility is kept open but not credited as an inference from the phenomenology.

### Where the commitments shield claims from testing

**Meditation and downward causation.** The article says meditation demonstrates consciousness influencing physical processes. But the Map’s own tenet page describes bidirectional interaction as a metaphysical commitment and acknowledges that first-person awareness does not directly reveal whether conscious agency adds causal power beyond physical mechanisms. Treating ordinary intention-mediated neural change as a demonstration of dualist downward causation therefore presupposes the tenet it is supposed to support. [tenets](https://unfinishablemap.org/tenets/)

**Quantum language.** The suggestion that loosened predictive constraints may permit consciousness to affect “open quantum processes” supplies no specified quantum state, interaction Hamiltonian, measurement operator, coherence timescale or distinctive prediction. It is not presently a mechanism or even an empirically differentiated hypothesis. It should be labelled as a framework-internal possibility, not as a conclusion licensed by edge-state evidence.

**Privileging ordinary consciousness.** The article says that treating waking consciousness as epistemically privileged may amount merely to a simplicity assumption. This ignores the principal grounds for privileging ordinary inquiry: stable memory, repeatability, public comparison, predictive success and organized error correction. Those advantages may not settle metaphysics, but reducing them to an Occamist preference artificially equalizes evidence from ordinary and highly unstable states.

**Boundary contact.** The proposal that noetic feelings reveal the structure of cognitive limits risks becoming self-sealing. Failure to articulate content, failure to remember it and failure to verify it can all be redescribed as signatures of the boundary. Unless the “structure of the limit” is operationally defined independently of those failures, the hypothesis survives precisely because every failed test is absorbed as another manifestation of the explanandum.

The resulting asymmetry is important: physicalist explanations are required to specify mechanisms, while dualist or transcendence possibilities are sometimes retained because the evidence cannot exclude them. Possibility under underdetermination is legitimate, but it must not be presented as positive explanatory standing.

## 5. Currency against the 2024–2026 literature

The bibliography is not generally antiquated. It includes Siegel 2024, Metzinger 2024 and Safron et al. 2025. The problem is **selective currency**: recent works favourable to complexity or openness are included, while equally recent work on construct heterogeneity, false insight, epistemic vulnerability and larger-scale neural synthesis is absent or underused.

The minimum update should include:

- **Canby et al. 2024** on overlap and heterogeneity among mystical, nondual, selfless and ego-dissolution constructs. This directly challenges the article’s unified profile. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38346364/)
- **FIBUS, 2024** on psychedelic false insights and false beliefs. This is the most direct current counterweight to the dissolving-insight and noesis sections. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-024-00120-6)
- **Pedersen and Steglich-Petersen, 2025** on epistemic openness, certainty and vulnerability to social influence. [Pure](https://pure.au.dk/portal/da/publications/on-the-social-epistemology-of-psychedelic-experience/)
- **Seragnoli and colleagues, 2025** on noetic quality as a metacognitive feeling of epistemic gain. [Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10608-025-10588-z)
- **The 2026 multi-dataset psychedelic neuroimaging analysis**, which should replace the presentation of a single 2024 study as the current neural synthesis. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04287-9)
- **Khan 2026** on subjectivity and minimal self-awareness during psychedelic states. This appeared after the real substantive revision date and is therefore a necessary current update rather than a defect attributable to the August 8 editor. [Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11097-026-10186-8)

James, Stace, Katz, Bergson and Huxley remain appropriate historical sources. They cannot, however, establish the empirical prevalence, cross-cultural independence or latent structure of the proposed profile. Brewer 2011 and Vollenweider 1997 may also remain as particular findings, but they should not stand in for current reviews or pooled analyses.

## 6. Integration with neighbouring articles

The article is heavily linked but only partially integrated at the claim level.

### `filter-theory`

The target article says there is no comparative ledger showing that filter theory explains the data better than production accounts. The dedicated `filter-theory` article nevertheless says filter theory better accommodates certain anomalies while also admitting that the models may be empirically equivalent and that no precise independent test has been supplied. Those positions are not jointly stable. “Better accommodates” needs an explicit metric, likelihood comparison or complexity accounting; otherwise both pages should use the target article’s neutral “compatible with” formulation. [concepts/filter-theory](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/filter-theory/)

### `altered-states-of-consciousness`

The corpus-level altered-states article correctly says the cluster is one explanatory pattern rather than six independent confirmations and that the evidence does not adjudicate metaphysics. It nevertheless gives filter theory an organizational advantage over part of the evidence. The relationship among organization, accommodation and evidential support needs to be made explicit on both pages. [apex/altered-states-as-interface-evidence](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/altered-states-as-interface-evidence/)

### `convergent-phenomenological-evidence`

This article argues that the specificity of cross-cultural similarities favours discovery over construction. The target article’s common-cause discount says essentially the opposite: convergence should initially count as one observation until the source of dependence is resolved. The “too specific to be construction” response is not a substitute for a model of what shared human architecture, common transmission and common measurement would predict. [concepts/convergent-phenomenological-evidence](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/convergent-phenomenological-evidence/)

### `minimal-consciousness`

The minimal-consciousness article explicitly treats the analogy between minimal experience and simple organisms as evidentially neutral. The target’s “lower edge” analogy is more suggestive and is followed by conclusions about the subject and conceptual concealment. It should import the neighbouring article’s neutral verdict rather than allowing the analogy to acquire evidential force through placement. [concepts/minimal-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/minimal-consciousness/)

### `noetic-feelings-void`

This is the target article’s strongest internal defeater. It supplies a mature naturalistic account of felt knowing without accessible content. The target links to it but does not fully propagate its implications, and the missing Koriat bibliography entry suggests the integration is lexical rather than citation-level. The two articles should share a single evidence table and conclusion about what noetic quality can and cannot establish. [voids/noetic-feelings-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/noetic-feelings-void/)

### `apophatic-cartography`

The target borrows the idea of expiration conditions from this page, but does not give its central boundary claims explicit expiration criteria. The apophatic article’s asymmetry rule—one cannot call a domain inaccessible while freely asserting what lies beyond the boundary—should govern the claims about wider territory, representational closure and noncommunicable transcendence. [voids/apophatic-cartography](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/apophatic-cartography/)

### `near-death-experiences` and `ineffable-encounter-void`

The target ranks NDEs as the weakest evidence and treats convergence as compatibility rather than support. The dedicated NDE page and the ineffable-encounter page use more dualism-favouring language, including claims that the phenomena increase the cost of materialism or support a consciousness-independent meaning structure. The corpus is therefore assigning different evidential weights to the same families of observations. [concepts/near-death-experiences](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/near-death-experiences/)

### `phenomenology-of-cognitive-limit-types`

That article describes its taxonomy as heuristic and not empirically established. The target nevertheless concludes with four “convergent lessons,” including that conceptual processing hides something and that noetic quality reflects genuine contact with limits. The taxonomy’s provisional status should propagate upward. [topics/phenomenology-of-cognitive-limit-types](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-cognitive-limit-types/)

### Coalesced predecessors

## Referee verdict

The article’s most defensible conclusion is narrower than its present framing:

> Edge-state reports contain recurring but heterogeneous motifs. Those motifs may reveal stable features of human cognitive architecture or reporting limits, but current evidence does not establish a single edge-state natural kind, access to wider reality, absence of minimal subjectivity, filter theory, nonphysical causation or quantum involvement.

That conclusion would still make the article valuable. It would convert it from an intermittently tenet-protective synthesis into a rigorous map of an unresolved empirical and epistemological problem.

4. **Define “edge-state” independently of the proposed profile.** Possible criteria include induction method, perturbation magnitude, breakdown of ordinary reportability or independently measured cognitive transition. ## C. Changes to the site’s methodology

1. **Separate substantive modification dates from maintenance dates.** Link repairs, formatting and metadata changes should not reset the apparent currency of an argument.
2. **Introduce automated bibliographic validation.** Check DOI metadata, year, volume, article number, container title and author list against publishers or Crossref before publication.
3. **Add a body-to-bibliography completeness test.** Any author-year citation used in prose, including Koriat here, should fail validation when no matching reference exists.
4. **Require source-weight labels.** Distinguish primary experiments, pooled analyses, reviews, philosophical arguments, preprints, case studies, conceptual journals and popular works.
5. **Require inline disclosure of decisive design limitations.** Small N, single-participant studies, exploratory analyses and severe attrition should appear where the finding is used, not only in a bibliography or neighbouring page.
6. **Build a canonical claim registry with reverse dependencies.** When a claim such as “zero-person perspective,” “flat EEG,” “REBAS” or “psychological versus representational closure” is corrected, every article that imports it should be identified automatically.
7. **Apply the common-cause discount mechanically.** For every convergence argument, record which observations are independent and which share participants, instruments, traditions, source texts, neural architecture or theoretical assumptions.
8. **Add a construct-validity checkpoint.** Claims that different literatures instantiate one phenomenon should require operational correspondence or measurement-invariance evidence, not merely similar vocabulary.
9. **Add contradiction linting across articles.** Flag combinations such as “compatible with,” “supports,” “better accommodates,” “demonstrates” and “establishes” when applied to the same evidence at different points in the corpus.
10. **Make model comparison explicit.** Every dualism-relevant empirical article should compare at least physicalist production, filter/interface dualism and the strongest relevant monist alternatives using the same observations and complexity assumptions.
11. **Require symmetrical mechanism standards.** A physicalist explanation should not be rejected for lacking detail while a dualist or quantum explanation is retained as a bare possibility.
12. **Create a current-literature gate for phrases such as “current evidence” or “contemporary neuroscience.”** Such language should require a recent review, pooled analysis or documented search covering at least the preceding two years.
13. **Assign human review to load-bearing empirical claims.** For AI-generated pages, a human curator should verify the original source whenever a study is used to support a central inference rather than merely provide background.
14. **Publish correction severity and propagation status.** Each correction should state whether it concerns metadata, characterization, evidential weight or the article’s conclusion, and list all dependent pages checked.
15. **Require operational retraction conditions for void claims.** A claim about inaccessible territory, cognitive closure or noncommunicable transcendence should state what conceivable evidence would count against it; otherwise it should be classified as a metaphysical possibility rather than an evidential hypothesis.
