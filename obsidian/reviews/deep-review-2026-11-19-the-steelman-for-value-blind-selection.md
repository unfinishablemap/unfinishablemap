## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. CRITICAL: the Laukkonen et al. paraphrase dropped a qualifier and blurred which selection the paper means.** (fixed)

The old text said: "makes inferential competition the selector — "Bayesian binding," where the inferences that **most** reduce long-term uncertainty win entry to the world model".

The paper's abstract says: "Only the inferences that **coherently** reduce long-term uncertainty win, evincing **a selection for consciousness** that we call Bayesian binding." The body text agrees. What wins the competition is "partially driven by the contents that best cohere with the existing reality model".

So "most reduce" swapped a coherence criterion for a magnitude criterion. That is a dropped qualifier that changes the meaning (§2.5). "The selector", in turn, hid the fact that the paper's selection is a selection *for* consciousness: it decides what gets into the world model. The Map's selection is consciousness choosing among underdetermined outcomes. The paragraph's closing test ("The first must show its competition *is* the selection at issue") depends on that gap, and the reader could not see it.

*Fix*: the abstract sentence is now quoted verbatim, and the paper's competition is described as deciding "what enters the world model". "Bayesian binding" is confirmed as the authors' own phrase ("that we call Bayesian binding"; in the body, "what we can figuratively call Bayesian binding").

**2. CRITICAL: "no valence term denominating the contest" was a Map gloss the paper does not support.** (fixed)

The paper never says valence is missing from the competition. Valence appears once in the argument (preprint §6.2.2, "Sprays and Fields: Potential Broadcast Channels for Φ"). Neuromodulatory sprays "can shift synaptic gain over hundreds of milliseconds, embedding slow contextual information such as arousal, valence, and uncertainty". These sprays are proposed as one way "to realise the hyper-model's global forecast", and the hyper-model is "a hyper-model for precision-control". On the paper's own account, precision weighting decides which inferences win. So valence may reach the contest through gain, even though it is not part of the stated criterion.

*Fix*: "Valence appears only as one contextual signal neuromodulators may carry into precision control." This puts the paper's one mention of valence in the right place. It also lines up with the article's own concession in Relation to Site Perspective: the rival can let valence inform the channel and still deny that valence selects. The paper turns out to be a published case of that concession. The article could say so in a later pass (see Remaining Items).

**3. CRITICAL: the inline cite had no matching References entry.** (fixed)

"Laukkonen, Friston and Chandaria's "beautiful loop" (2025)" had been cited inline since 2026-09-05 with no References entry (§2.4 step 5). *Fix*: added as reference 9, and the two Map self-cites were renumbered to 10 and 11.
