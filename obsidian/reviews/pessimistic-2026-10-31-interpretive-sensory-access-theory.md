## Executive Summary

The page's citation ledger holds up again: 48 of 48 quotations from sources verify verbatim with their subjects intact. Its weaknesses sit elsewhere, in four places. The most consequential is an access/phenomenal equivocation in the Map's own stance (L106). The page "resists" Carruthers's no-conscious-attitudes corollary by rejecting global workspace and higher-order theories *as theories of phenomenal consciousness*. But Carruthers states the corollary in access terms ("not accessible in working memory, nor are we directly aware of them"), and the Map's own [[global-workspace-theory]] page says GWT "explicitly addresses only *access consciousness*". Two originator defects follow:
- L106 calls Hurlburt the "discoverer" of unsymbolized thinking. Hurlburt & Akhter 2008 §8 say the Würzburg and Cornell introspectionists observed "the same phenomenon that we call unsymbolized thinking" a century earlier.
- L52 credits ISA with moving a line that "earlier confabulation research left in place". The cited SEP sentence itself contrasts Nisbett and Ross 1980, and SEP §4.2.2 pairs Carruthers's attitude-level claim with Bem 1967/1972.

A fourth, smaller defect: the Occam paragraph states Tenet 5 one-sidedly.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would say the page's key distinction does no work it has paid for. "ISA concedes access, not acquaintance, so the agreement is one of line-drawing" (L102). ISA's introspection of sensory states is already the mindreading faculty applying folk-psychological concepts to broadcast contents. That is a trained conceptual response to internal states, which is exactly the move the 2026-10-31 Sellars note traces from EPM §59 through Churchland 1981, p. 70. The Map's "acquaintance" adds a relation with no specified neural or functional signature. Unless the page says how acquaintance issues in a concept-applying *judgement*, she will say it has the shape Sellars called the Given. The Map does have a reply: its layered authority concedes theory-shaped judgement at layers 2–3 and resists only at layer 1. But that reply lives on [[phenomenal-authority-and-first-person-evidence]] and is not signposted here (see U1).

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett's sharpest move uses the page's own L54. The felt immediacy of *attitude* self-knowledge "is explained rather than trusted" ("the illusion of immediacy"). Why, then, is the felt immediacy of *sensory* self-knowledge trusted as acquaintance two sections later? Carruthers does not extend the illusion to sensations, so the pressure does not come from ISA. It comes from Dennett and Frankish, and Frankish is already cited on this page (L79) for a different point. The page never names illusionism. It is honest that ISA "fits without evidencing" Tenet 1. It is silent on why the diagnosis that worked for attitudes stops at sensations. That is a reader's first question, and the answer, a bedrock standoff at layer 1, is one sentence away (U1).

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark has little to attack, and that is to the page's credit: Tenets 2 and 4 are declared "not engaged" (L118). His one point concerns the Bidirectional paragraph. "Its agency case rests on other grounds" (L114) is true, but the other grounds include the quantum-selection programme he disputes. The page is right not to discuss them. It should not be read as implying they are secure, and it does not say they are.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch would note that "acquaintance" (L102) presupposes a determinate subject that is acquainted. That is the indexical self the Map defends under Tenet 4 and Posit One. This is framework boundary, not a page defect, and the page does not lean on it.

### The Empiricist

ISA's case is "mainly predictive" (L69, L116), and the page grants that where a rival predicts what the Map only accommodates, the evidence favours the rival (L108). Good. The Popperian then asks what would decide the Map's own "bet on cognitive phenomenology" (L40). [[positions/thought-and-understanding|P-TU1]] has a stated falsifier ("a clean zero case": genuine understanding with no reportable phenomenal character to the grasping). The page never mentions it, so the bet reads as unfalsifiable on this page when it is not. This is a low-cost improvement, not a defect.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would find ISA closer to *anatta* than the Map is. ISA's subject is an interpreter with no inner witness of its own attitudes, while the Map installs a subject "acquainted" with qualities. The deep review recorded this as bedrock, and I agree. The page claims only weak Revelation and routes the reader to [[phenomenal-acquaintance]], which is the honest posture.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The Map's resistance to the corollary engages only the phenomenal reading, but is stated generally
- **File**: `concepts/interpretive-sensory-access-theory.md`
- **Location**: L106, "The corollary inherits its premises from global workspace and higher-order theories, which the Map does not accept as theories of phenomenal consciousness. That shows the corollary is not forced on the Map; it does not refute it." Also the lead, L40.
- **Problem**: In the source the page cites for the corollary's premises (Ayan 2018), Carruthers defines both premises in *access* terms. GWT: to be conscious a state "must be among the contents of working memory … and thereby be available to other mental functions". HOT/attention-schema: conscious states are "those that you know of, that you are directly aware of in a way that doesn't require you to interpret yourself". His conclusion: decisions and judgments "are not accessible in working memory, nor are we directly aware of them". *The Centered Mind* (2015 abstract) adds an empirical working-memory claim: amodal attitudes "are never among the contents of this stream of conscious reflection". Rejecting these theories *as theories of phenomenal consciousness* does not touch an access-reading corollary. The Map's own GWT page (L70) holds that "GWT explicitly addresses only *access consciousness*". So "not forced on the Map" holds only for the phenomenal reading. The P-TU1 bet in the next sentence is about phenomenal character, so the paragraph's conclusion is right, but its first step equivocates on "conscious".
- **Severity**: Medium-High. The defect is in the Map's own stance section, not a source misreport.
- **Departure from Stability Note 2**: that note forbids re-flagging the P-TU1 bet as under-argued, and I do not. This finding targets the GWT/HOT step that precedes the bet. It rests on raw-source evidence (the interview's access wording) and on the Map's GWT page, which the deep review did not examine.
- **Recommendation**: Split the readings (Priority 1). The split also makes the page's map of positions more accurate. Frankish's mixed position and Shepherd's reply (L96) contest the *access* reading, while the Map contests only the phenomenal one.

### Issue 2: Hurlburt is called the "discoverer" of unsymbolized thinking, and his concession is strengthened
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L106 "since its discoverer allows that it may be sensory"; L90 "real and possibly sensory".
- **Problem**: (a) *Originator.* Hurlburt & Akhter 2008 §8 ("Historical comment: The phenomenon behind imageless thought") say: "The unsymbolized thinking phenomenon appears to be the same phenomenon that led to the imageless thought controversy in classical introspection a century ago". Citing Monson & Hurlburt 1993, they add that "both the Cornell and the Würzburg investigators observed the same phenomenon that we call unsymbolized thinking". Hurlburt names and investigates the phenomenon; by his own account he did not discover it. The Map already records the Würzburg–Titchener precedent ([[cognitive-phenomenology]] L196) and makes it P-TU1's "honesty anchor". (b) *Strength.* Hurlburt 2009 says unsymbolized thinking "may involve (non-symbolic) sensory aspects". The 2008 paper insists it is "not merely, for example, an incompletely formed inner speech or a vague image" and lists it as distinct from sensory awareness. "May be sensory" moves him toward Titchener's re-description. The page's inference survives the correction, since sensory aspects still let a conservative redescribe the case.
- **Severity**: Medium (originator); Low (strength).
- **Recommendation**: Priority 2.

### Issue 3: L52 credits ISA with a move the cited source gives partial precedents for
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L52, "ISA moves a line that earlier confabulation research left in place. Nisbett and Wilson, Schwitzgebel reports, held that attitudes and current sensations "can be known with 'near certainty'" (2024, §2.1.2)."
- **Problem**: The quoted SEP sentence continues: "(1977, 255; though contrast Nisbett and Ross 1980, 200–202, which seems sympathetic to skepticism about special access even to our attitudes)". The page quotes the first half and drops the contrast. SEP §4.2.2, the very sentence the page quotes next, reads "Carruthers (2011; see also Bem 1967, 1972; Rosenthal 2001; Cassam 2014) argues that … people confabulate not just in reporting the causes of their attitudes but also in reporting the attitudes themselves". Two more pieces of evidence point the same way. The page's own L71 cites Carruthers finding the attitude-misreport pattern "in the self-perception and dissonance traditions", which are Bem's. The Map's [[process-content-distinction]] (L49) says the induced-compliance literature, "which Daryl Bem (1972) recast in self-perception terms and which Nisbett and Wilson drew on directly", shows people reporting behaviour-inferred attitudes "as if they had been there all along". So "earlier confabulation research left [the line] in place" is true of Nisbett & Wilson 1977 and no more. ISA's distinct contribution is moving the line *for all attitudes* ("always", 2009) as a theory, not being first to move it.
- **Severity**: Medium. A thesis is credited to its developer while the source credits precedents; a qualifier is dropped; the page is in tension with its own L71 and a sibling page.
- **Recommendation**: Priority 3.

### Issue 4: The Occam paragraph states Tenet 5 one-sidedly
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L116, "The fifth tenet constrains the Map's own simplicity reasoning; it gives no licence to discount that economy, nor to borrow it."
- **Problem**: *Definition applied first.* Tenet 5 ([[tenets#^occams-limits]]) says the tenet "binds the Map's use of parsimony, *not only its critics'*", and that "the discipline is symmetric: parsimony cannot decide for or against a framework when the relevant knowledge is incomplete". The tenet's "Rules out" forbids treating the razor as "anything more than a defeasible heuristic". ISA's economy is a parsimony consideration *for* a framework whose predictions the page itself calls "contested rather than settled". So the tenet does license treating it as non-decisive. "Constrains the Map's own" omits half of the tenet. "No licence to discount" reads as exempting a rival from it, and it sits awkwardly with the next sentence's "predictive, the right currency", which itself discounts the economy. The charitable reading ("no licence to dismiss") is available, which is why this ranks fourth.
- **Severity**: Low-Medium. It misstates a tenet inside the Relation to Site Perspective. The section's existence and structure are not at issue.
- **Recommendation**: Priority 4.
