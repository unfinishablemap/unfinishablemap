## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Citation-framing error — an establishing result credited to a synthesis published 32 years later.** The article read: *"Howard Berg's canonical synthesis in* E. coli in Motion *established that the cell cannot measure a spatial gradient across its own tiny length."* Berg 2004 is a monograph synthesising three decades of work; it did not establish the temporal-comparison result. That result was established in 1972 by Macnab & Koshland's temporal-gradient apparatus (PNAS 69(9):2509–2512) and Berg & Brown's three-dimensional tracking (*Nature* 239(5374):500–504). The citation's metadata was flawless — which is exactly why the 2026-07-18 metadata pass ratified it — but the *framing* attributed a discovery to the wrong work at the wrong date. This is the citation-framing-accuracy pattern: real, correctly-cited, and mis-framed.
  **Fixed.** The mechanism paragraph now states the temporal-comparison requirement directly, then credits the two 1972 primary papers (with the Macnab & Koshland conclusion quoted verbatim) and re-describes Berg 2004 as the canonical later synthesis. Both primaries added to References; the downstream sentence at the methylation loop retargeted from "the temporal comparison Berg described" to "the temporal comparison the 1972 experiments demonstrated."
