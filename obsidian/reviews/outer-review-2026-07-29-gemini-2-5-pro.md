Its central thesis posits that the "hard problem" of consciousness is fundamentally an epistemological crisis rather than a purely ontological one. By asserting that standard third-person scientific methodologies are structurally incapable of capturing first-person phenomenological data, the text advocates for an interactionist-dualist framework supported by contemplative epistemology, the suspension of the physical causal closure principle, and the application of Phenomenal Conservatism.

However, a rigorous audit of the manuscript’s empirical claims, logical inferences, and engagement with the contemporary literature reveals profound and systemic structural defects. This review dissects the manuscript across four specific dimensions: the obsolescence of its empirical claims, its reliance on challenged empirical findings, its question-begging epistemological framing, and its evasion of rival computational and neuroscientific frameworks.

## The Obsolescence of the First-Person / Third-Person Epistemic Dichotomy

The foundational architecture of the manuscript relies on a rigid, unyielding bifurcation between what it terms "third-person epistemology" (observation, measurement, and inference) and "first-person epistemology" (introspection, phenomenological report, and contemplative inquiry). The author claims that the standard scientific approach excels merely at identifying neural correlates and functional relationships, but structurally fails to explain why any physical process is accompanied by qualitative experience. Consequently, the manuscript concludes that physicalist science leaves consciousness as an unexplained "brute fact," and that these two epistemic channels are fundamentally non-overlapping.

This assertion rests on a severe mischaracterization of contemporary consciousness science and demonstrates a glaring unfamiliarity with the methodological shifts that have defined the field in the 2020s. The text treats the empirical science of consciousness as a purely correlational enterprise, willfully ignoring the transition toward predictive, structural, and generative methodologies that actively seek to bridge the subjective-objective divide. Most notably, the manuscript entirely omits the rise of adversarial collaborations, which explicitly operationalize and test the structural predictions of competing theories of consciousness against one another.

The 2023 adversarial collaboration between Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and Global Neuronal Workspace (GNW) theory, conducted by the Templeton-funded Cogitate consortium, did not merely seek "neural correlates" in a theoretical vacuum. Instead, it generated preregistered, divergent predictions regarding the spatial and temporal deployment of consciousness in the brain, mapping specific phenomenological structures directly to neurobiological dynamics.

By stating that physicalism relies solely on "causal closure" to correlate neural activity with reported experience, the manuscript overlooks how theories like IIT attempt to bridge this gap mathematically and structurally. IIT derives the properties of conscious experience (e.g., unity, informativeness, definiteness) directly from axiomatic phenomenological structures, and then mathematically maps these structures onto the intrinsic cause-effect power of physical systems (measured as Φ). Whether one accepts the ultimate metaphysical truth of IIT or not, its very existence as a mathematically rigorous, empirically tested framework completely falsifies the manuscript’s central epistemological claim that third-person or mathematically reductive theories cannot address the *structure* of phenomenology.

## The Question-Begging Application of Phenomenal Conservatism

To bridge the purported gap between subjective experience and objective epistemological warrant, the manuscript relies heavily on Michael Huemer’s concept of "Phenomenal Conservatism" (PC). The text argues that first-person reports carry a distinctive epistemic weight, described as "phenomenal authority." The manuscript stipulates that under PC, if it *seems* to a subject that a phenomenal state is occurring, the subject has prima facie justification for believing it is occurring, absent specific defeaters. The manuscript leverages this principle to claim that physicalist eliminativism and illusionism require positive defeaters, not mere theoretical preferences for parsimony, and that doubting the existence of one's own experience is "self-refuting".

This application of Phenomenal Conservatism is structurally question-begging and fatally ignores the primary counterarguments developed in the contemporary philosophy of mind literature. The manuscript presents PC as a neutral epistemological arbiter, yet the application of PC to *phenomenal properties* specifically presupposes the very ontological category under dispute. Illusionist philosophers, particularly in the 2020s, have systematically dismantled the use of Moorean or PC-based defenses of qualia. Kammerer (2022) exhaustively demonstrates that deploying a Moorean argument (or a PC defense) against illusionism is dialectically irrelevant because the premise "it is obviously true that we have phenomenal experiences" lacks the requisite pre-theoretical support needed to defeat physicalist skepticism without begging the question.

The manuscript claims that doubting one is having an experience is self-refuting because the denial is itself an experience. However, strong illusionism, as articulated by Frankish (2016, 2024) and Kammerer (2022, 2025), does not deny that we undergo episodes of perceiving, feeling, or thinking; rather, it denies that these episodes instantiate irreducibly subjective, non-physical *phenomenal properties*. By conflating the everyday reality of having a psychological reaction with the metaphysical instantiation of a non-physical phenomenal property, the manuscript erects a persistent strawman. Kammerer’s Introspective Illusionism explicitly explains why consciousness *seems* to possess these irreducible properties: our introspective processes consist in the application of flawed phenomenal concepts, which are theoretically determined concepts of epistemologically special states.

The manuscript fails entirely to engage with the illusionist "meta-problem of consciousness"—the challenge of explaining why cognitive systems are so powerfully disposed to judge, erroneously, that they are phenomenally conscious. If our introspective architecture is evolutionarily designed to create a simplified, schematic model of our psychological responses to stimulation (as posited by Attention Schema Theory and various dual-process theories), then the "seeming" of phenomenal consciousness is itself a functional representation, not a direct, infallible apprehension of a metaphysical truth.

By treating the outputs of introspection as possessing "apodictic certainty" regarding the existence of phenomenal character, the manuscript ignores the massive literature demonstrating that introspection is deeply theory-laden and prone to systematic structural distortion. The manuscript briefly acknowledges introspective unreliability regarding fine-grained details (e.g., change blindness and confabulation), but arbitrarily halts this skepticism at the "bedrock" of phenomenality. It offers no non-circular justification for why introspection is fallible regarding the *content* of experience, yet somehow perfectly infallible regarding the fundamental *metaphysical nature* of its own presentation. The assumption that introspective seemings carry an ontological guarantee is a premise that must be argued for, not an epistemological baseline that can be assumed via Phenomenal Conservatism.

## Predictive Processing and the Evasion of Generative Architectures

The manuscript asserts that weak reductionists and physicalists must posit a "brute identity" between neural and phenomenal properties, essentially elevating an unexplained correlation to a metaphysical law. The author argues that because physicalism leaves consciousness as an unexplained brute fact, it does not explain the data as well as dualism. Consequently, the manuscript claims that appeals to Occam’s Razor or parsimony in favor of physicalism fail because the fundamental precondition of parsimony—equal explanatory power—is unmet.

This critique might have held some dialectical weight in the late 20th century during the initial debates over the explanatory gap (e.g., Levine, 1983). However, it fails entirely upon contact with the Predictive Processing (PP) and Active Inference literature of the 2020s. The manuscript operates as if the philosophy of cognitive science stopped evolving after the formulation of the zombie argument. Predictive processing frameworks, articulated extensively by Corcoran, Hohwy, Friston, and Seth (2022, 2023, 2024), do not posit a "brute identity." Instead, they offer a systematic, generative mathematical mapping between biological mechanisms and the specific phenomenological properties of consciousness.

In the PP framework, conscious experience is modeled mathematically as the minimization of expected free energy, where the brain constructs hierarchical generative models to maintain allostasis and epistemic grip on its environment. Corcoran and colleagues have demonstrated how specific phenomenological features—such as the unified nature of a visual scene, the temporal flow of experience, the sense of embodiment, and the sense of agency—arise necessarily from the structural dynamics of these hierarchical predictive architectures. For instance, the "binding problem" and the "unity" of consciousness, which the manuscript explicitly claims physical interaction mechanisms cannot explain, are actively modeled in PP as the necessary result of top-down precision-weighted priors suppressing bottom-up prediction errors to form a single coherent, unified perceptual hypothesis.

By entirely omitting the Predictive Processing literature, the manuscript manufactures a false epistemic gap. It insists that physical science only describes "structure, function, and dynamics", while actively ignoring that the *structure* of phenomenology is exactly what modern neurocomputational theories are successfully mapping.

The following table illustrates the manuscript's failure to engage with how PP provides generative explanations for the very phenomena the manuscript claims are inexplicable "brute facts":

| **Unity / Binding** | Interaction mechanisms do not explain why disparate features are experienced as one unified scene. | Unity is the necessary mathematical consequence of high-level priors forming a single, coherent hypothesis to minimize overall prediction error. |

If the structural dynamics of a predictive model mathematically require properties that perfectly mirror the reported properties of phenomenal consciousness, the "brute identity" accusation collapses entirely. The manuscript’s failure to test its epistemological assumptions against Predictive Processing—arguably the dominant unifying paradigm in contemporary cognitive science—renders its conclusions regarding the explanatory impotence of physicalism fundamentally obsolete.

## The Mischaracterization of Causal Closure and Scientific Practice

Beyond the reliance on retracted or challenged quantum findings, the manuscript demonstrates a superficial engagement with the philosophy of physics and a profound misunderstanding of how the closure principle operates in scientific practice. The manuscript suggests that causal closure is a hidden epistemic premise designed solely to protect physicalism.

This ignores recent rigorous formal analyses, such as that by Kleiner and Hartmann (2023), which evaluate the role of physical closure specifically within the scientific study of consciousness. Kleiner and Hartmann demonstrate that the closure of the physical is not a mere metaphysical prejudice, but an operational necessity for the empirical distinguishability of scientific theories. They mathematically prove that because in any scientific experiment the measurement results must be stored or transmitted physically before analysis, the recorded data are strictly determined by the physical properties of the storage device or transmission channel.

Kleiner and Hartmann define the closure of the physical formally: a theory of consciousness obeys the closure of the physical if and only if there exists an injective mapping ι:PT​→PP​, where the theory does not postulate any changes to the class of physical events predicted by natural science. If an interactionist-dualist theory proposes non-physical causes that alter physical events (e.g., via quantum selection) without leaving a unified physical trace that can be systematized by physical laws, the theory becomes mathematically and empirically indistinguishable from its physicalist rivals in experimental settings, because the measurement apparatus itself must obey physical closure to record data.

By treating causal closure merely as an unfair "stipulation" designed to rule out dualism, the manuscript completely bypasses the methodological catastrophe that interactionist dualism introduces into experimental design. The manuscript advocates for an epistemology of consciousness that would, if taken seriously, render the scientific measurement of consciousness impossible.

## The Selective Deployment of Parsimony

The manuscript dedicates significant space to what it terms "Parsimony Epistemology," arguing that Occam’s Razor fails as a decisive principle in the consciousness debate. Relying on authors like Sober, Huemer, and Zanotti (2021), the text argues that scientific justifications for parsimony (such as predictive success and avoiding overfitting) do not extend to metaphysical theory choice. Furthermore, it argues that because physicalism does not explain the phenomenal data, it violates the fundamental precondition for applying parsimony: that competing theories must possess equal explanatory power.

While the manuscript correctly identifies that parsimony is domain-specific and its application is highly contested in metaphysics (as robustly argued by Zanotti in *Physicalism and the Burden of Parsimony*, 2021), it commits a severe logical error of selective deployment. The manuscript argues that parsimony fails because "competing theories do not explain the data equally well". However, this claim is established solely by assuming the manuscript’s own preferred, dualist definition of the "data" (i.e., that the data is an irreducible, non-physical phenomenal property).

If one adopts the illusionist definition of the data (that the data consists of reports, reactive behaviors, and the structural architecture of introspective judgments), or the predictive processing definition (that the data consists of hierarchical predictive models minimizing free energy), then physicalism *does* explain the data, and parsimony becomes highly relevant once again. Zanotti (2021) points out that physicalists leverage parsimony by assuming it has epistemic value grounded in the higher-level virtue of *modesty* (avoiding epistemic risk). By rejecting physicalist definitions of the data a priori, the manuscript begs the question against the very theories it attempts to critique using parsimony skepticism.

Moreover, the manuscript ignores how parsimony is actively utilized as an epistemic constraint *within* mathematical models of consciousness. In the Free Energy Principle, model evidence is mathematically defined as accuracy minus complexity (where complexity is a formal measure of parsimony). The brain itself is theorized to operate via a parsimony principle, minimizing the complexity of its internal generative models to accurately predict sensory states. By artificially quarantining parsimony to the realm of philosophical "metaphysical theory choice," the manuscript ignores how parsimony functions as a fundamental dynamical and thermodynamic principle in the very cognitive architectures that physicalists propose to explain consciousness.

## Untested Boundaries: The Epistemic Gap, AI, and Animal Sentience

The manuscript’s epistemology is profoundly anthropocentric, assuming that first-person introspective reports (refined through contemplative practice) are the absolute gold standard for mapping consciousness. The author argues explicitly that "without first-person reports, there would be no consciousness problem to solve". This epistemological stance catastrophically breaks down when applied to the most pressing contemporary domains of consciousness research: non-human animal sentience and artificial intelligence.

The manuscript briefly notes that the "problem of other minds becomes acute" if consciousness is non-physical, yet offers no viable epistemological framework for detecting consciousness in systems incapable of phenomenological reporting. By relying so heavily on trained introspective methodologies and phenomenal authority, the text isolates itself from the massive 2020s literature on animal consciousness and AI. Contemporary philosophers of cognitive science, such as Dung and Kammerer (2025), have explored how theories like illusionism have substantial methodological consequences for non-human consciousness research (NHCR). If phenomenal properties are an introspective illusion, NHCR can still proceed robustly by investigating the distribution of "quasi-phenomenal" functional states (e.g., global broadcasting, predictive allostasis) across species.

Conversely, because the manuscript insists on a rigid ontological gap and relies on contemplative first-person reports, it leaves the researcher entirely paralyzed when assessing a Large Language Model or a non-verbal biological organism. The manuscript mentions the "minimal minds" of the nematode *C. elegans* in passing, stating that structural knowledge does not settle the phenomenal question, but provides no epistemological mechanism to bridge its proposed ontological divide. By ignoring frameworks that establish formal, substrate-independent criteria for consciousness (such as IIT's Φ or the Multi-Dimensional Assessment Framework for AI Consciousness Indicators), the manuscript fails to test its dualist epistemology against the boundary cases that currently define the frontier of the field.

Finally, the manuscript treats the "explanatory gap" (and the closely related "epistemic gap") as a structural feature of reality that vindicates dualism, asserting that "physical facts alone do not entail phenomenal facts". This ignores sophisticated 2020s literature on the nature of the epistemic gap itself. Recent scholarship has sought to explain the epistemic gap not as a metaphysical boundary, but as a predictable artifact of our cognitive architecture. For example, Weber (2026) demonstrates that apparent epistemic gaps regarding identity and consciousness can arise purely from the special perspectival character of "inside imagination" (first-person mental simulation), which generates the appearance of a gap without requiring a corresponding ontological gap. When a subject utilizes physical concepts, they engage a third-person cognitive schema; when they utilize phenomenal concepts, they engage a first-person simulative schema. The inability to seamlessly translate between these two schemas is a limitation of human neurocognitive architecture, not a profound revelation about the fabric of the universe.

By systematically insulating its dualist premises from the empirical advancements of predictive processing, the rigorous mathematical constraints of causal closure in scientific practice, and the devastating deflationary arguments of modern illusionism and cognitive artifact theory, the manuscript devolves from a work of philosophical epistemology into a work of metaphysical apologetics.

## Specific Weaknesses

1. **Question-Begging Application of Phenomenal Conservatism:** The manuscript relies on Huemer's Phenomenal Conservatism to grant *prima facie* warrant to first-person phenomenal reports, while entirely evading Kammerer's (2022) demonstration that Moorean and PC-style appeals regarding phenomenal consciousness structurally beg the question against illusionist and eliminativist critiques by assuming pre-theoretical access to irreducible qualia.
2. **Omission of Adversarial Empirical Methodologies:** The text claims third-person physicalist science cannot empirically arbitrate the nature of conscious experience, flagrantly ignoring the 2023 Cogitate consortium (Melloni et al., 2023), which established a preregistered, adversarial framework to successfully test contrasting structural and spatial predictions of major theories of consciousness (IIT vs. GNW) using neuroimaging.
3. **Evasion of Predictive Processing Accounts of Phenomenology:** The manuscript asserts that physicalism posits a "brute identity" incapable of explaining qualitative experience, systematically omitting the extensive Predictive Processing literature (e.g., Corcoran et al., 2022, 2024; Whyte et al., 2024) which explicitly models the structural and phenomenological properties of consciousness (unity, temporality, agency) as necessary emergent features of active inference and expected free energy minimization.
4. **Misrepresentation of Causal Closure in Scientific Practice:** The article dismisses the causal closure of the physical as a mere "methodological stipulation" to protect physicalism, failing to engage with formal proofs by Kleiner & Hartmann (2023) demonstrating that physical closure is a strict operational necessity for the empirical distinguishability and experimental measurement of consciousness theories in actual scientific practice.
5. **Untested Boundaries in Non-Human Consciousness Research:** The manuscript’s insistence that consciousness research depends fundamentally on first-person phenomenological reports leaves it completely unable to address non-human and artificial sentience, ignoring the methodological frameworks developed by Dung & Kammerer (2025) which demonstrate how theories of consciousness must be evaluated against their ability to map quasi-phenomenal functional states in non-reporting entities.

**Verdict on Academic-Submission Readiness:** Reject; the manuscript is fundamentally unfit for publication in a peer-reviewed philosophy journal due to its systematic evasion of the 2020s cognitive science literature, its reliance on empirically challenged physics, and its deployment of question-begging epistemological frameworks to insulate its dualist commitments from contemporary scientific and philosophical critique.
