# Full-site external audit of *The Unfinishable Map*

## Audit basis and scope

I treated the repository state current through **1 September 2026** as authoritative. The changelog records new work and substantive citation corrections through that date, including the new multi-agent Born-preservation article. The public changelog view available during this audit appeared older, so conclusions about current coverage are based on the source repository rather than the indexed public rendering.  [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

This is a **site-wide structural audit**, not a sentence-by-sentence verification of every article. I read the foundational and canonical material, then sampled high-centrality and potentially conflicting pages across the requested topics, concepts, positions, voids, and apex layers. Citation checking was selective and concentrated on claims doing real argumentative work.

## Executive assessment

The Map is now strongest as a **disciplined research programme specifying what an interactionist-dualist theory would have to explain**. It has become unusually explicit about its mechanism debt, evidential grades, dependency problems, subject-pairing problem, and the limited reach of its anti-epiphenomenalist and anti-Everettian arguments. The quantum-interface register expressly says that Born-preserving conscious selection is presently citable only as a framework-internal coherence proposal, not as established mental causation.

It is not yet a physical theory of psychophysical interaction. It has no coupling dynamics, no worked single-agent toy model, no multi-agent composition law, no subject–substrate pairing law, no relativistically covariant localization rule, and no precise mapping from phenomenal reasons to quantum outcomes. The most consequential positive claim—consciousness selects token outcomes while preserving aggregate Born statistics—therefore remains underdefined rather than merely unconfirmed.

The most important corpus-level weakness is **control-plane/content-plane divergence**:

- The tenets and positions register increasingly state the correct qualifications.
- Many older topic, concept, void, and apex pages still spend the confidence that those qualifications withdrew.
- The register describes dependencies but does not yet enforce them in authoring or build validation.
- As a result, a canonical concession can coexist with—or even be followed by—the publication of stronger downstream prose.

The positions documentation itself records several such propagation failures and acknowledges the absence of an automated validator. The changelog shows that the workflow catches many citation and characterisation errors, but usually after the content has been generated.

There is also a deeper methodological hazard. Because the five tenets are described as chosen starting points rather than results, virtually any objection can be recorded as a “debt,” “void,” “boundary pressure,” or “framework cost” without forcing revision of a tenet. Candour is valuable, but **candour without precommitted exit conditions can immunise a programme just as effectively as denial**. The Map now needs rules specifying when accumulated debts lower, conditionalise, split, or retire a foundational commitment.

My overall verdict is therefore:

> **As an exploratory philosophical map:** substantial, increasingly self-critical, and generative.  
> 
> **As an evidential defence of interactionist dualism:** underdetermined.  
> 
> **As a candidate science of mind–matter interaction:** pre-formal and not yet predictively adequate.

# 1. Novel implications not yet developed in the canonical corpus

The following are not restatements of the Born-preserving trilemma, the subject-census problem, or the new multi-agent problem. Those are already present. These are further consequences of the current commitments that I did not find worked through in the central tenets, positions, or apex pages.

## 1.1 A trajectory-level closure theorem is required

The existing formalism distinguishes an unconditioned Born marginal from intention-conditioned outcome distributions:

q(O∣X)=C∑​P(O∣C,X)P(C∣X).

That is enough for a one-shot experiment. It is not enough for an acting organism.

Let Ht​ be the physical and experiential history before event t. Even when every one-time marginal remains Born-exact, mental selection can appear in the **joint distribution of histories**:

P(O1:T​∣do(C1:T​),X1:T​).

This matters because an outcome at t alters the subject’s information, intentions, environment, and future opportunities. A selector may therefore preserve every coarse marginal while creating:

- serial dependence;
- intention–outcome lag correlations;
- context-dependent runs;
- adaptive stopping-time effects;
- policy-conditioned outcome distributions;
- different distributions over complete life histories.

The current apex notices that sequential-dependency tests might matter, but its formal trilemma remains principally one-step. The new multi-agent article similarly extends the problem across agents rather than across closed-loop time.

A fully self-concealing corridor therefore needs a much stronger law:

P(Ot​∣Ht​,do(Ct​),Xt​)=q(Ot​∣Ht​,Xt​)

for every physically realisable history and mental policy—or an explicit restriction on which mental policies and histories are physically possible. The first option eliminates distributional reason-responsiveness at every temporal grain. The second introduces a history-level form of psychophysical superselection.

This yields a sharper dilemma:

1. **Preserve only one-time marginals:** mental action may leak through trajectories.
2. **Preserve every history-conditioned kernel:** consciousness becomes empirically idle at every temporal grain.
3. **Restrict possible sequences of intentions:** add a law coordinating the evolution of conscious reasons with physical histories.

The Map presently owes not just a Born-preserving coupling map but a **Born-preserving stochastic process**.

## 1.2 Energy preservation does not imply absence of usable work

The conservation article now concedes that selected outcomes may differ in energy on individual trials and argues that unchanged Born frequencies preserve ensemble-average energy. That is an improvement. It still leaves a thermodynamic opening.

A conscious policy could select high-work outcomes when they are useful and permit compensating low-work outcomes when they are harmless. Overall outcome frequencies and even overall mean energy could remain unchanged while the agent acquires more **available work** or lowers entropy in a controlled subsystem.

For example:

- outcome A charges a neural or external work reservoir;
- outcome B dissipates equivalent energy in an unexploitable context;
- consciousness favours A when the reservoir is connected and B when it is not;
- global frequencies remain Born-exact.

The physical issue is therefore not simply conservation of mean energy. It is whether an adaptive selector permits a Maxwell-demon-like conversion of fluctuations into work.

The framework needs a condition such as:

- no increase in expected extractable work under any admissible mental policy;
- no violation of relevant fluctuation relations;
- no reduction of entropy without an accounted physical information cost;
- a martingale or no-arbitrage property for free energy under conscious intervention.

Without such a theorem, “selection without injection” does not yet establish thermodynamic innocence.

## 1.3 Mental causation makes the physical state non-Markov-sufficient

The subject-census register correctly observes that once the law contains P(O∣C,X), the subject and phenomenal state become part of the model’s state description.

Suppose two systems have exactly the same complete physical state Xt​, but are paired with different intentions Ct​. If their transition probabilities differ, then:

P(Xt+1​∣Xt​,Ct​)=P(Xt+1​∣Xt​).

The physical microstate alone is no longer Markov-sufficient. Marginalising over the hidden conscious state will ordinarily produce:

- apparent history dependence;
- unexplained heterogeneity between subjects;
- mixtures of transition kernels;
- residual predictive structure recoverable from sufficiently rich longitudinal data.

That creates an empirical route that the current treatment understates. Even when Ct​ cannot be measured directly, latent-variable methods can sometimes infer that an observed process is generated by multiple hidden kernels.

The framework must choose among three possibilities:

1. Different phenomenal states really do give physically identical states different transition laws. Then latent psychophysical structure may be statistically identifiable.
2. The differences always cancel at every observational grain. Then the mental variable does no identifiable causal work.
3. Physically identical states cannot bear different causally relevant phenomenal states. Then the relevant phenomenal state is at least nomologically fixed by the physical state, weakening the intended nonphysical sourcehood.

This is a psychophysical version of the state-sufficiency problem, and it should sit beside the Born-preservation and subject-census debts.

## 1.4 The evolutionary argument faces a bootstrapping problem

The Map uses evolutionary tracking as part of the bridge from the existential result—some consciousness-related reporting is causally grounded—to Tenet 3’s universal claim that consciousness is causally efficacious wherever it occurs. The positions register correctly concedes that the argument itself does not establish that universal conclusion.

But a further problem follows.

Natural selection can favour an interface only after variations in that interface create heritable physical fitness differences. A fully inert proto-interface cannot be selected because of its future conscious efficacy. The Map therefore needs one of the following:

- a continuous gradient from weak to strong psychophysical coupling;
- a physically selected architecture that arose for another function and was later “occupied” by consciousness;
- a threshold transition with a separate explanation for why the pre-threshold machinery evolved;
- conscious efficacy already present in very simple organisms;
- selection acting at a level other than ordinary biological heredity.

Each option has costs. A continuous gradient predicts intermediate forms and raises questions about minimal consciousness. Exaptation reduces the evolutionary support for mental causation because the physical machinery was selected for an independent role. A sharp threshold makes the coincidence between the evolved architecture and the later interface more difficult to explain.

The evolutionary case therefore requires an **interface–fitness curve**, not merely the observation that conscious capacities are adaptive.

## 1.5 Reason-responsive selection requires a semantic decoder and a clock

The Map often moves from high-level phenomenal content—“reach for the glass,” “resist the impulse,” “this result is wrong”—to the selection of low-level quantum or neural outcomes. Information-theoretic bandwidth limits do not specify how that translation occurs.

A complete model needs at least two functions:

D:(Ct​,Xt​)→ΔP(Ot​)

mapping phenomenal content and physical context to an outcome-policy, and

τ:(Ct​,Xt​)→{interface events}

specifying when the mental state is sampled or allowed to intervene.

Without D, “selection for reasons” hides the hardest semantic problem inside the word *selects*. Without τ, it is unclear how a comparatively slow and temporally extended conscious intention controls enormous numbers of rapidly decohering or collapsing events without acting too early, too late, or repeatedly in conflicting ways.

The post-decoherence programme discusses a continuously refreshed pointer-state “menu,” while the subject-census and interface programmes acknowledge missing pairing and boundary rules. None yet provides the decoder or scheduler needed to connect them.

This is more than missing detail. Until the mapping is specified, any observed action can be retroactively described as the outcome consciousness “meant” to select.

## 1.6 Locality, subject pairing, and relativistic covariance form a trilemma

Three current admissions jointly generate a new constraint:

- The quantum register contains no spatial localization rule.
- The subject-census register contains no subject–substrate pairing law.
- The multi-agent article leaves shared and nested quantum systems unresolved.

A viable law must be:

1. **Local enough** not to introduce uncontrolled superluminal influence.
2. **Global enough** to identify a whole subject and distinguish “its brain” from adjacent, prosthetic, duplicated, split, or entangled systems.
3. **Covariant enough** not to depend on an unacknowledged preferred simultaneity slice.

A purely local criterion may not determine which local events belong to one subject. A globally defined subject boundary risks nonlocal coordination. A rule applying “at the same conscious moment” risks requiring a preferred foliation. A retrospective whole-history rule begins to resemble a final-boundary or block-universe theory rather than forward-in-time selection.

No-signalling at the level of outcome probabilities is not by itself a solution: no-signalling does not provide a covariant ontology, a pairing law, or a rule for assigning interventions to subjects.

## 1.7 The subject-census problem produces combinatorial underdetermination

The Map holds that subject boundaries are determinate but not readable from physical or functional organization, while also requiring the subject census to enter the causal law.

For a system with n candidate components, the number of possible partitions grows as the Bell number Bn​. Each partition can in principle yield a different:

- number of subjects;
- pairing assignment;
- set of phenomenal conditionals;
- composition rule where intentions conflict;
- moral-patient count.

Because the physical structure is stipulated not to settle the partition, the theory inherits an enormous family of empirically underidentified models. This is not merely the ordinary “boundary problem.” It is a **model-selection explosion inside the psychophysical dynamics**.

The problem becomes especially severe for:

- split brains;
- brain–computer interfaces;
- gradually replaced neural tissue;
- synchronized AI copies;
- neural organoids;
- nested or distributed cognitive systems;
- fission and fusion cases.

A subject-pairing law must therefore contain a strong symmetry, invariance, or eligibility principle. Merely declaring that there is a determinate fact leaves the causal model incomplete.

## 1.8 Tenet 5 spreads probability across rivals rather than favouring the Map

Tenet 5 says simplicity is not a reliable guide to truth under deep ignorance. That may block a quick dismissal of dualism, but it provides no selective support for this dualism.

Weakening a simplicity prior expands the space of credible theories to include:

- many varieties of substance and property dualism;
- neutral monisms;
- idealisms;
- panpsychisms;
- pluralist or process ontologies;
- many psychophysical coupling laws;
- many collapse and hidden-variable theories;
- many theories of subject individuation.

The Map’s exact conjunction—irreducibility, outcome-selecting quantum interaction, universal bidirectionality, one-world actuality, and a thick subject—is only one member of that enlarged space. Unless independent evidence concentrates probability on this conjunction, limiting Occam’s Razor may actually **decrease** confidence in the precise Map by distributing weight among many complicated alternatives.

The legitimate consequence of Tenet 5 is:

> “Do not reject the Map solely because it is ontologically expensive.”

The illegitimate consequence is:

> “Because simplicity is unreliable, the Map is comparatively supported.”

Several legacy pages still drift toward the latter use.

## 1.9 The whole corpus needs an effective sample-size calculation

The voids methodology now recognises that apparently convergent arguments can share a common cause: the same framework defines the categories and then finds that they fit the framework. It accordingly discounts a catalogue of many voids toward perhaps one or two approximately independent patterns.

The same reasoning applies to the entire site.

Hundreds of pages do not represent hundreds of independent arguments when they are descendants of:

- the same five tenets;
- the same canonical source pages;
- the same prompts and editorial templates;
- the same small set of philosophical intuitions;
- apex syntheses derived from lower-level pages;
- later pages generated from earlier Map research notes.

The Map should construct a source-lineage directed acyclic graph and estimate an **effective independent argument count**. Without this, the sheer density and cross-linking of the corpus can create an illusory impression of consilience. The Map has adopted the relevant principle locally; it has not yet turned it on itself globally.

# 2. Structural weaknesses and logical gaps

## 2.1 The five tenets are not five claims of the same logical type

The foundation presently combines:

- an ontological claim: consciousness is irreducible;
- a mechanism-family commitment: quantum outcome selection;
- a universal causal claim: all consciousness is bidirectionally coupled;
- a quantum-interpretive and personal-identity commitment: one-world actuality;
- a methodological caution: limits on parsimony.

Several unlabelled auxiliary commitments do comparable work:

- a numerically determinate, persisting subject;
- objective baseline collapse before and outside consciousness;
- a real but currently unknown interface-eligibility rule;
- an unknown subject-pairing law;
- a preferred outcome-selection rather than context-selection mechanism;
- exact Born preservation at a still-unclear ensemble level.

The site now acknowledges most of these individually, especially the dependency of Tenet 4 on thick subjecthood and the missing pairing law. But they remain architecturally subordinate even where a tenet’s rationale cannot operate without them.

The result is unstable conditionality. A reader is told that the five tenets are foundational, but some of their principal arguments depend on supposedly downstream positions that cannot be retired without leaving the tenet as a bare stipulation.

A cleaner architecture would separate:

1. **Foundational axioms**
2. **Auxiliary metaphysical hypotheses**
3. **Candidate physical mechanisms**
4. **Empirical conjectures**
5. **Methodological policies**

## 2.2 The central causal predicate is not yet defined

“Consciousness made the difference to this token outcome” is central throughout the site. But there is no formal answer to:

- What is the intervention do(C) on a nonphysical conscious state?
- What remains fixed in the nearest counterfactual world?
- How is the unselected physical trajectory defined?
- What makes one physically permissible outcome the one the subject selected?
- What constitutes the same intention across counterfactual physical histories?
- Is the causal relation interventionist, process-causal, powers-based, or primitive?

The Born-preserving apex distinguishes token counterfactual dependence, distributional change, and metaphysical authorship. That is conceptually useful. But its “trumping” route secures authorship only by adopting a metaphysics under which physical and epiphenomenal models make no different predictions. Its empirical route requires conditional deviations and therefore gives up strict invisibility at some grain.

Until one route is formalised, “selection” remains multiply interpretable:

- an intervention changing probabilities;
- a primitive actualisation relation;
- causal pre-emption;
- retrospective attribution of authorship;
- ordinary physical stochastic resolution plus a dualist label.

The site should not let one meaning inherit support earned by another.

## 2.3 Post-decoherence “selection” imports more physics than it acknowledges

The site is right that decoherence alone does not, without an interpretation or modification, select one unique actual outcome. Schlosshauer’s review treats the implications of decoherence for the measurement problem as controversial rather than claiming decoherence straightforwardly solves it. [APS Journals](https://journals.aps.org/rmp/abstract/10.1103/RevModPhys.76.1267)

But it does not follow that an improper reduced state is a literal menu of available actual outcomes waiting for consciousness. The current tenets page has improved its language, explicitly saying that post-decoherence selection is an additional actualisation postulate rather than a choice from already definite alternatives. The apex programme nevertheless continues to use “menu,” “options,” and environmental preparation in ways that can suggest more ontological definiteness than decoherence supplies.

Three physical debts remain:

1. **Instrument definition:** Which POVM, quantum instrument, or coarse-graining defines the alternatives?
2. **Temporal definition:** At what event does actualisation occur amid continuous decoherence?
3. **Ontology:** What is physically added to unitary quantum mechanics?

Quantum Darwinism explains environmental redundancy and the emergence of objective records; Zurek’s envariance work addresses Born weights through a separate argument. The programme should not present “Quantum Darwinism fixes the menu and probabilities” as one undifferentiated result. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphys1202)

## 2.4 Objective collapse does not create a vacant role for consciousness

The prebiotic solution says objective physical processes supply collapse outside consciousness, while consciousness modulates collapse in suitable neural systems. That avoids the claim that minds were needed for early cosmology, but it changes the dialectical situation.

Once objective collapse already supplies:

- occurrence;
- timing;
- basis or localization;
- and a token result,

conscious modulation is no longer filling an otherwise empty explanatory role. It is an additional law selectively altering how the result is fixed inside certain systems.

The theory then owes comparative evidence for:

objective collapse alone

versus

objective collapse + conscious modulation.

The Map often moves rhetorically from “quantum theory needs an account of outcomes” to “consciousness can supply the outcome.” But its own prebiotic solution supplies a nonconscious outcome mechanism. The consciousness term has to earn its inclusion independently.

## 2.5 Neural competition is not physical indeterminacy

The motor and phenomenology-mechanism pages make productive use of affordance competition, basal-ganglia gating, stochastic accumulation, and threshold crossing. The empirical neuroscience establishes competing action representations and selection dynamics. It does not establish an ontological gap into which a nonphysical selector must enter.

Cisek and Kalaska found that a substantial subset of task-related premotor neurons represented both potential reach directions before the instruction resolved the choice. That supports parallel action preparation, not quantum superposition or nonphysical selection. [ScienceDirect+1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0896627305000590)

Likewise, the recent dopamine result distinguishes fast dopamine transients involved in reward-oriented drive from baseline dopamine required for ordinary movement. It does not reveal an unoccupied “third layer” that consciousness must fill. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08038-z)

The Map repeatedly risks an invalid transition:

the neural description is competitive or stochastic

therefore

the physical causes are insufficient

therefore

consciousness selects.

Neither inference follows. Classical dynamical systems, noisy accumulators, recurrent control systems, and hierarchical policies all implement selection without a central selector.

## 2.6 Phenomenology constrains architecture, not causal direction

Clinical dissociations establish that intention, movement, authorship, ownership, and awareness can come apart. Intentional binding and choking-under-pressure show that agency-related processing has structured behavioural consequences. Cognitive motor dissociation shows that some behaviourally unresponsive patients retain task-dependent command-following activity. Bodien and colleagues found such responses in roughly a quarter of their behaviourally nonresponsive sample. [New England Journal of Medicine+1](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa2400645)

These findings undermine crude accounts in which agency is one undifferentiated post-hoc feeling. They do not discriminate among:

- phenomenal causation;
- physical comparator and control processes that also generate phenomenology;
- higher-order representational accounts;
- predictive-processing accounts;
- physical recurrent-control architectures;
- identity theories on which the conscious state is the causally active physical state.

The bridge sometimes acknowledges this limit, but elsewhere says choking “shows consciousness doing causal work” and treats preserved cognition without behaviour as sitting especially hard on epiphenomenalism. That is too strong. The experiments show that processes associated with conscious agency matter; they do not establish that phenomenal character, rather than its physical basis, supplies an extra cause.

## 2.7 The Stapp mechanism and the Map’s mechanism are not interchangeable

The tenets now draw the crucial distinction correctly:

- Stapp’s Process 1 concerns which question or projection is applied, and when.
- The Map’s adopted Tenet 3 concerns which outcome becomes actual.

Yet the phenomenology and motor-control pages continue to invoke quantum-Zeno stabilization as though it concretely implements the Map’s preferred selection mechanism.

These are different causal proposals. A mechanism that changes measurement context or probing frequency may have physical consequences even while the Born rule governs outcomes. A pure outcome selector within a fixed context faces the bias-without-deviation problem.

The corpus should stop moving between them under the generic label “quantum selection.” Each article should state explicitly whether consciousness controls:

- the measurement basis;
- measurement timing;
- preparation;
- maintenance of coherence;
- collapse occurrence;
- or the resulting outcome.

## 2.8 Several proposed falsifiers are not well formed

The phenomenology-mechanism bridge proposes that conscious selection might produce a **5–15% change in drift rate** between passive and actively willed conditions. The number is not derived from a coupling model, a power calculation, a prior empirical estimate, or the Born-preserving formalism. It is therefore not a prediction; it is an unsupported effect-size placeholder.

Other examples include:

- “near-perfect” neural prediction would close the selection gap;
- empirical confirmation of Many Worlds would falsify the model;
- proving neural coherence impossible at “any relevant timescale” would falsify a mechanism whose preferred form acts after decoherence;
- continued failure of physical theories to solve consciousness supports disclosure.

These conditions are either vague, theory-global, or non-unique to the Map. A severe local test must specify:

1. the intervention;
2. the measured quantity;
3. the expected distribution;
4. an effect size or bound derived from the model;
5. the relevant physicalist and rival-dualist predictions;
6. the result that lowers a named position or tenet.

## 2.9 Thick subjecthood is doing unacknowledged foundational work

The individuation register now frankly concedes that the principal indexical argument against Many Worlds requires a numerically determinate, non-deflationary subject. It also concedes that this subjecthood claim has no independent discriminating evidence and is not readable from physical organization.

This creates a foundational inversion:

- Tenet 4 is presented as a quantum-foundational commitment.
- Its main rationale depends on a contested theory of subjects.
- That theory is registered downstream.
- The theory is partly motivated by the same anti-Everettian intuition it supports.

The site has correctly named the interdependence. It has not resolved it. The clean options are:

- promote determinate subjecthood to an explicit foundation;
- state Tenet 4 conditionally;
- replace the indexical argument with a genuinely subject-neutral case;
- or accept No Many Worlds as a bare starting preference with less argumentative force.

## 2.10 The rejection of branching relocates rather than solves primitive identity

The Map’s fission position says a perspective does not divide: after actual physical fission, neither successor is the original, whereas Everettian branching is denied rather than interpreted as subject division. The position itself now admits that this does not refute an Everettian who accepts branch multiplication.

The remaining asymmetry is:

- physical fission is real, so the original subject ends;
- quantum branching is not real, because the Map rejects it.

That is coherent as a stipulated one-world ontology. It is not an independent argument for it. The primitive fact “which subject is me?” has been relocated into primitive haecceity and global uniqueness. It has not been reduced or explained.

## 2.11 The register risks turning unresolved problems into stable features

The positions system distinguishes credence, evidence, centrality, maturity, discriminability, and framework dependence. This is a real methodological improvement. But many high-centrality claims remain at external-evidence Grade D while continuing to drive large numbers of applied conclusions.

The danger is **debt absorption**:

1. A criticism is accepted.
2. A new position or void records it.
3. The framework becomes more sophisticated.
4. The foundational commitment remains unchanged.
5. Downstream content continues, now with a disclosure paragraph.

This turns criticism into corpus growth. A progressive research programme should instead sometimes become **smaller**: fewer live mechanisms, narrower claims, lower foundational confidence, or retired applications.

## Hidden foundational commitments

At least three propositions deserve foundation-level visibility:

1. **Determinate subjecthood:** there is a real fact about subject identity, boundaries, and persistence.
2. **Baseline objective actualisation:** outcomes become unique independently of consciousness outside interface systems.
3. **Psychophysical eligibility:** only some organized systems can be paired with causally active consciousness.

At present, the first is formally downstream but rationale-bearing, the second appears principally as a solution to prebiotic collapse, and the third is both indispensable and unknown. The current five-tenet presentation therefore understates the framework’s actual commitments.

# 4. Cross-cluster contradictions and unpropagated corrections

## 4.1 `/voids/self-opacity` versus `/positions/voids-as-evidence`

**Self-Opacity** says the convergence of introspection, formal limits, phenomenology, and multiple thinkers provides support for dualism and “points beyond engineering limitation.” It also suggests that hidden mental causation and quantum-scale interaction explain the opacity.

The **voids positions register** says the opposite at the evidential level: the clustering of voids is framework-internal coherence, not independent confirmation, and removing a physicalist defeater does not raise evidence for dualism.

I found no direct citation in Self-Opacity to the corrective positions domain, and the positions domain does not name Self-Opacity. This is a genuine mutually uncited contradiction in evidential status.

**Required resolution:** change “Dualism receives support” to “Dualism provides one interpretation,” and insert P‑V1/P‑V2 as binding dependencies.

## 4.2 `/concepts/physics-as-disclosure` versus `/positions/methodology-and-calibration`

**Physics as Disclosure** treats the measurement problem’s persistence, decoherence’s partial success, and no-go constraints as evidence that physics has reached an ontological boundary at consciousness. It says decoherence’s failure to select one outcome strengthens the disclosure reading.

The methodology register says that:

- framework compatibility is not support;
- removal of a defeater does not establish the positive theory;
- disclosure of a gap is not correction or completion;
- framework-fit must not be confused with independent evidence.

I found no direct link between the concept page and the methodology register. The primary physics source supports the narrower claim that decoherence’s relation to the measurement problem remains controversial, not that the residual problem points to consciousness. [APS Journals](https://journals.aps.org/rmp/abstract/10.1103/RevModPhys.76.1267)

**Required resolution:** recast the page as an interpretive hypothesis and add a rival-explanation matrix covering Bohmian, objective-collapse, Everettian, pragmatist, and instrumentalist treatments.

## 4.3 `/concepts/substrate-independence` versus `/apex/machine-question` and P‑AC1

**Substrate Independence** concludes that consciousness requires something digital computation cannot provide and that purely computational systems cannot be conscious. It also says present digital systems are causally closed in a way brains may not be.

The current **Machine Question** explicitly distinguishes bare phenomenality from report-grounded and bidirectionally coupled consciousness. It leaves bare AI phenomenality open and restricts the negative verdict to bidirectionally coupled consciousness. P‑AC1 likewise says conventional digital AI is low probability, not ruled out, conditional on an interface-eligibility law the Map does not possess.

The apex and register point toward the older concept page, but the concept page does not import their scope correction. This is a one-way corrective relationship rather than a mutually uncited pair, but the practical result is the same: a reader entering through the concept receives a position the canonical layer no longer holds.

**Required resolution:** split the concept’s verdict into:

- substrate independence for bare phenomenality;
- substrate independence for report-grounded experience;
- substrate independence for bidirectional coupling.

Only the third receives the Map’s present negative verdict.

## 4.4 `/apex/phenomenology-mechanism-bridge` versus the quantum-interface register

The bridge claims that phenomenology, neural competitive architecture, quantum indeterminacy, and agent causation form a mutually supporting chain; it says the lower levels empirically anchor agent-causal selection and that the quantum opening fills a neural selection gap.

The quantum-interface register says the causal-selection thesis is Grade D, framework-internal, lacks a toy model, and must not be cited as established mental causation.

The register lists the bridge among downstream argumentative locations, but the bridge does not directly cite the register’s mechanism-debt anchor. Its own internal qualifications do not prevent stronger claims in surrounding prose.

**Required resolution:** the bridge’s executive summary, “chain as evidence” section, and conclusion should inherit P‑Q3/P‑Q10 verbatim or through a machine-enforced claim card.

## 4.5 `/apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme` versus the corrected tenets wording

This pair is linked, so it is not an uncited contradiction, but it shows propagation failure clearly.

The tenets page now says post-decoherence selection is **not** a choice among already definite classical alternatives. The apex continues to speak of the environment fixing a menu and preparing options in language that can easily be read that way.

**Required resolution:** reserve “menu” for a mathematically specified decoherence-selected coarse-graining, and state on every use that none of its elements is thereby an actual outcome.

# 5. Primary-source and citation spot-checks

## 5.1 Decoherence and the outcome problem

The Map’s narrow claim—decoherence does not by itself derive one unique actual outcome—is defensible. The stronger claim—that this residual specifically supports a consciousness-based boundary—is not in the cited physics. Schlosshauer presents the interpretive consequences as contested. [APS Journals](https://journals.aps.org/rmp/abstract/10.1103/RevModPhys.76.1267)

**Verdict:** citation real and broadly fair for the narrow point; philosophical inference overstated.

## 5.2 Quantum Darwinism and Born probabilities

Zurek’s Quantum Darwinism work concerns redundant environmental records and the emergence of objective classical information. His envariance programme supplies a separate route concerning Born weights. The Map compresses these into “the menu and probabilities are fixed,” which hides the distinct assumptions and arguments. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphys1202)

**Verdict:** sources real; characterization needs finer separation.

## 5.3 Cisek and Kalaska on competing reaches

The reported result that many premotor neurons represented both candidate reaches is supported. The experiment concerns parallel motor preparation and competition, not quantum superposition, ontological indeterminacy, or an unfilled causal slot. [ScienceDirect+1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0896627305000590)

**Verdict:** factual report accurate; dualist inference unsupported by the study.

## 5.4 Cai et al. on dopamine dynamics

The current bridge is substantially more careful than earlier formulations. The paper supports a role for fast dopamine dynamics in reward-related engagement or vigour while showing that ordinary movement initiation can survive their removal under the studied manipulation. It does not imply that neural computation and dopamine leave a third, nonphysical selection layer. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08038-z)

**Verdict:** empirical summary mostly fair; tripartite metaphysical conclusion is the Map’s addition.

## 5.5 Bodien et al. on cognitive motor dissociation

The approximately 25% finding is supported: some behaviourally unresponsive patients showed task-dependent fMRI or EEG responses to commands. That is important evidence of preserved covert cognition and command following. It is not direct evidence that phenomenal consciousness is an extra physical cause. [New England Journal of Medicine+1](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa2400645)

**Verdict:** numerical claim accurate; causal use too strong.

## 5.6 Maier et al. on intention and random-number generation

The large sequential Bayesian study involved 12,571 participants and produced evidence favouring the null. The site’s 1 September review correctly removed the claim that this particular study was preregistered; the preregistration language had been imported from a different study. [Frontiers+1](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00379/full)

**Verdict:** current treatment is appropriately negative and better calibrated after correction.

## 5.7 Santander et al. on residual callosal fibres

The finding that a relatively small posterior callosal remnant can preserve broad interhemispheric functional connectivity and avert standard disconnection symptoms is real. It supports the functional importance of strategically placed residual fibres and possibly reorganization. It does not make distributed physical integration puzzling or preferentially support a nonphysical unifier. [ICB+1](https://www.icb.ucsb.edu/publications/5106)

**Verdict:** source real; evidential direction is overstated in the substrate page.

## 5.8 Chalmers and McQueen on consciousness-collapse theories

The quantum register currently characterizes this source responsibly: it is a research programme, some simple versions face quantum-Zeno constraints, more elaborate versions may be empirically testable, and the proposal need not entail substance dualism. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02314)

**Verdict:** good current citation discipline.

## 5.9 The changelog’s own citation record

The 1 September changelog documents several nontrivial corrections:

- a philosophical paper assigned to the wrong journal;
- an unsupported term dropped after full-text checking;
- a result attributed to the wrong paper;
- a finding’s direction reversed in a research note;
- a study incorrectly labelled preregistered.

These repairs are evidence that the verification workflow works, but also that metadata checks and search summaries alone remain insufficient.

**Verdict:** commendably transparent correction practice; still too reactive.

## Additional targeted repairs

### The No-Many-Worlds cluster

The relevant pages should distinguish three claims that are currently too easily combined:

1. Everett does not supply a primitive globally privileged subject.
2. Some centred variants build first-person privilege into their ontology.
3. The Map additionally rejects global branch plurality.

Only the third directly yields one-world actuality. The first is an identity objection; the second narrows it; neither independently proves the third.

### The weak-measurement cluster

Maintain a strict boundary between:

- operational weak-value reconstruction;
- time-symmetric calculational formalisms;
- ontological claims about future boundary conditions.

The existence of successful pre- and post-selection experiments does not independently establish retrocausality or a mind-selected final boundary.

### The altered-state and clinical clusters

Use a standard evidential ladder:

- behavioural or report finding;
- neural correlate;
- inferred conscious state;
- inference about causal efficacy;
- metaphysical conclusion.

Most current evidence is strong at the first two levels, mixed at the third, and weak at the last two.

# 7. Methodological improvements

## 7.1 Make positions machine-binding

Every article should carry claim-level metadata such as:

```
YAMLclaims:
  - id: P-Q1
    relation: depends_on
  - id: P-Q3
    relation: inherits_debt
  - id: P-V1
    relation: evidential_ceiling
  - id: P-AC1
    relation: applies
```

The build should fail when:

- an article makes a high-centrality causal claim without the relevant position;
- a void claims evidential support without P‑V1/P‑V2;
- an AI page collapses bare phenomenality and bidirectional coupling;
- an outcome-selection page invokes a context-selection mechanism without labelling the change;
- a page cites a retired or superseded formulation.

The current register’s prose descriptions are not enough.

## 7.2 Add reverse-dependency propagation

When a canonical claim changes, the system should automatically queue:

- every article that cites it;
- every apex synthesis derived from it;
- every table or dependency matrix containing it;
- every article using a semantically equivalent uncited claim;
- every generated description or search summary that repeats the old wording.

The positions documentation already records a particularly instructive failure in which an incorrect inference was reintroduced shortly after being corrected. Timestamp-based stale-file detection cannot catch that; authoring-time canonical retrieval is required.

## 7.3 Require a canonical read at authoring time

Before generating or substantially revising an article, the workflow should load:

- all cited position entries;
- their latest “would shift if” conditions;
- mechanism-debt anchors;
- current tenet dependency rows;
- any registered contradiction involving the target cluster.

A research note or cached summary should never be allowed to substitute for the current canonical source.

## 7.4 Build a claim-level citation ledger

Each externally checkable claim should record:

- exact supporting passage or data table;
- primary versus secondary source;
- whether the source supports, is compatible with, or opposes the claim;
- peer-review status;
- date last verified;
- whether the statement is quotation, paraphrase, or Map inference;
- which proposition in the article it supports.

This would have prevented several of the errors documented on 1 September.

## 7.5 Track argument lineage and effective independence

Create a graph whose nodes are claims rather than pages. Edges should include:

- derived from;
- paraphrases;
- shares premise with;
- uses same evidence;
- independent replication;
- framework-generated;
- contradicts;
- supersedes.

A cumulative argument should report both:

- nominal number of supporting arguments;
- independence-discounted effective number.

The same common-cause discipline already applied to the voids should govern the entire corpus.

## 7.6 Separate compatibility, constraint, and confirmation in the interface

Every empirical claim should receive one of four labels:

1. **Rival-neutral fact**
2. **Compatible with the Map**
3. **Constrains a Map mechanism**
4. **Discriminating evidence for the Map**

Warm quantum biology, decoherence, action competition, covert command following, and residual callosal connectivity are mostly in categories 1–3. They should not visually accumulate as category 4.

## 7.7 Maintain a formal-model repository

The site’s most important next output should not be another conceptual article. It should be a small executable model repository containing:

- a single-agent Born-preserving selector;
- an epiphenomenal control model;
- a trumping model;
- an interventionally detectable model;
- a sequential adaptive model;
- a two-agent entangled model;
- a thermodynamic work-extraction test;
- a subject-pairing parameterization.

For each, report whether it satisfies:

- marginal Born preservation;
- trajectory-level Born preservation;
- no-signalling;
- covariance;
- conservation;
- no-work extraction;
- reason-responsiveness;
- empirical identifiability.

## 7.8 Replace generic falsifiers with a falsifier registry

Each live empirical position should contain:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Intervention | What is manipulated? |
| Observable | What is measured? |
| Prediction | Exact distribution or bound |
| Comparator | Best rival prediction |
| Grain | Trial, subject, task, history, joint agents |
| Failure rule | Which position changes, and how? |
| Replication rule | What counts as a stable positive or null? |

“Physicalism explains consciousness,” “MWI is confirmed,” or “no quantum effect is found” are not sufficiently local falsifiers.

## 7.9 Add a debt-to-revision policy

A disclosed debt should have consequences. For example:

- Failure to specify a toy model keeps all applied mental-causation conclusions at framework-internal grade.
- Repeated nulls across preregistered conditional grains lower P‑Q1.
- Failure of every reason-responsive model to preserve no-signalling forces revision of Tenet 2 or 3.
- An inability to formulate a covariant pairing law lowers the interface programme, not merely adds another void.
- If determinate subjecthood remains ungrounded, Tenet 4’s indexical argument is formally conditional.

This is the main safeguard against tenet-protective bracketing.

## 7.10 Expose deployment provenance

Every public article should display:

- source commit;
- render commit;
- last deep-review date;
- last primary-source verification date;
- current position dependencies;
- whether the public rendering matches the source version.

That would make source/index lag visible rather than requiring an auditor to infer it from the changelog.

## 7.11 Increase human review where structural centrality is high

The current workflow is excellent at generating breadth. It needs proportionally more human review on the small number of claims that control the whole graph:

- token causal efficacy;
- Born preservation;
- objective actualisation;
- subject pairing;
- thick identity;
- universal causal coupling;
- AI interface eligibility.

A mistaken claim in a peripheral article is local. A mistaken formulation of one of these propagates throughout topics, ethics, AI, agency, and the voids.

# Final judgment

The Map has made genuine progress since its earlier formulations. Its best pages no longer pretend that:

- decoherence positively supports dualism;
- Born preservation automatically solves causal efficacy;
- self-stultification establishes universal interaction;
- subject identity is independent of the anti-Everettian argument;
- AI phenomenality and bidirectional coupling are the same question;
- registering a problem resolves it.

Those corrections matter.

But the central positive thesis remains unearned. The Map has not yet shown that a Born-preserving outcome selector can simultaneously be:

- reason-responsive;
- interventionally meaningful;
- trajectory-invisible;
- thermodynamically harmless;
- no-signalling;
- relativistically covariant;
- correctly paired to subjects;
- compositional across multiple agents;
- and distinct from epiphenomenal redescription.

Its distinctive external evidence remains weak. Decoherence, Quantum Darwinism, neural competition, agency phenomenology, cognitive motor dissociation, quantum biology, and the persistence of the hard problem all constrain the discussion. None currently discriminates the Map’s exact conjunction from its physicalist, neutral-monist, objective-collapse, or alternative-dualist rivals.

The most consequential improvement would therefore be a change of output priority:

> **Stop expanding the conceptual map temporarily and formalise the interface.**

A failed toy model would be more scientifically informative than dozens of additional articles. A successful model would transform the project: it would make the framework criticisable at the level where it presently survives by remaining underspecified. Until then, the most defensible public description is not that The Unfinishable Map has supplied a working account of mental causation, but that it has supplied an increasingly precise catalogue of the conditions any such account would have to satisfy.
