## Reply

# Referee Report: "The Categorical Perception Void" (unfinishablemap.org/voids/categorical-perception-void/)

**Verdict: REVISE-HARD.** The article is right that the strong Haskins-era claim, that listeners cannot hear within-category differences, has collapsed. But it misreports at least three of its own sources, co-opts a colour scientist toward a conclusion he rejects, and opens with a phenomenological claim ("experience arrives already carved") that its own body later admits is untested. Its "void" also reduces to what any Bayesian or predictive-processing perceiver would predict, and the article never builds that rival.

### 3. Empirical claim testing: is "inability to hear within-category differences" a task artefact?

**Where the claim is supported.**

- In speech, discrimination exceeds the identification-predicted ceiling whenever memory load and labelling incentives fall.
- Pisoni's own 1973 analysis puts the categorical/continuous difference down to "the degree to which separate auditory and phonetic memory components are employed in the decision process during discrimination". [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3515632) That frames CP as a memory-mode effect, not a sensory limit.
- An exploratory study of vowel formant thresholds ("Estimating vowel formant discrimination thresholds using a single-interval classification task", PMC2736738) summarises the pattern: moving "from ABX to more sensitive tasks that use less working memory, such as the 4IAX (Pisoni, 1975) or 4I2AFC (Gerrits and Schouten, 2004) tasks, tend[s] to flatten the discrimination function."
- Toscano et al. (2010) show linear VOT encoding at N1. [sagepub](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797610384142)
- Gradient lexical activation (McMurray et al. 2002) shows within-category detail is retained and used. [apa](https://psycnet.apa.org/manuscript/2021-45647-003.pdf)
- On the narrow proposition that within-category differences are *encoded and behaviourally recoverable*, the article is correct.

**Where it overclaims.**

1. **"Artefact" is the wrong word.** A phonetic coding mode that dominates under memory load is a real property of the speech system, not a measurement error. "Task-dependent" is accurate; "artefact" implies the categorical pattern is spurious. The article also inherits McMurray's rhetoric and then contradicts McMurray by keeping "a robust core … warp" in speech.
2. **The boundary peak in stops is partly auditory, not categorical.** The chinchilla data the article cites show this, and so does the natural-auditory-boundary literature. A warp that exists without categories does not show that categories carve experience.
3. **Early categorical neural coding exists alongside graded encoding.** Intracranial recordings from human superior temporal gyrus (Chang et al. 2010, *Nature Neuroscience*; not re-fetched in this pass) show categorically organised responses at perceptual latencies. The accurate statement is that graded and categorical codes coexist at overlapping stages. The article's "underlying representation … appears linear" is true of N1 and false as a generalisation.
4. **The "kept but not delivered" thesis is contradicted by its own literature.** McMurray, Tanenhaus & Aslin (2002), and the follow-up work cited in a *JEP:HPP* manuscript ("Listeners activate speech sound categories in a gradient way and this information is maintained and affects activation of items at higher levels of processing"), show the gradient *is* delivered by default to the processes that act on it: lexical competition, garden-path recovery. [apa](https://psycnet.apa.org/manuscript/2021-45647-003.pdf) What is not delivered by default is *explicit report under a categorisation instruction*. The article's "default readout … withholding the gradient from the process that acts on it" is therefore false for speech.
5. **Colour is worse for the article than it admits.**

- Witzel and Gegenfurtner's programme argues that colour "category effects" largely track uneven discriminability in the stimulus samples or attention rather than perception: their 2014 chapter "Category effects on colour discrimination" concludes that "category effects are not inherent to colour perception… attention to the categorical distinction is at the root of the category effect", although Witzel (2015, *Journal of Vision* 15(8):22) did find categorical facilitation with equally discriminable colours.
- The pre-attentive Greek-blue vMMN result (Thierry et al. 2009, *PNAS*: "greater and faster perceptual discrimination of these colors in native speakers of Greek") is contested by He, Witzel, Forder, Clifford & Franklin (2014). Cognitive Science reviews cite that paper as finding no pre-attentive category effect. [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2657373)[wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cogs.12493)
- Lateralisation is not dead either. Holmes & Regier (2017) "conclude that the non-basic warm–cool distinction yielded lateralized CP", and Zhong et al. (2018, *Cognitive Science*, doi 10.1111/cogs.12493) found "RVF-lateralized color CP corresponding to the green–blue categories" and treat it as a situational effect of language.
- So "did not survive 230 observers" overstates the case in one direction, and "real but modest" overstates it in the other.
6. **Phenomenal access versus encoding.** The body correctly says phenomenal access is "untested". But the first sentence ("experience arrives already carved"), the meta-description ("continua delivered already carved"), and the Japanese /r/–/l/ line ("the two words simply sound alike") all assert the phenomenal claim as fact. [[categorical-perception-void]] This is confession-without-correction: the caveat is printed and the lede is not changed. The Japanese claim is also empirically too strong. Miyawaki et al. (1975, *Perception & Psychophysics* 18:331–340) found that, for isolated F3 transitions, "the nonspeech discrimination functions are virtually identical" for Japanese and American listeners, and Japanese listeners improve with high-variability training, so "sound alike" holds only under speech-mode, categorisation-style conditions.

### 5. Rival engagement: predictive processing and Bayesian accounts

The article never engages the strongest physicalist rival. It residualises it into a one-line "physicalist reading".

- **Feldman, Griffiths & Morgan (2009)**, *Psychological Review* 116(4):752–782, model the perceptual magnet effect as the outcome of "optimally solving the statistical problem of perception in noise", in which "listeners' perception is biased toward phonetic category means". [google](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup) That model jointly predicts:

- graded sensory encoding (the likelihood, corresponding to Toscano's N1);
- a categorical, warped posterior (the default report);
- task dependence (low noise and low memory load let the likelihood dominate, as in the 4I tasks of Gerrits & Schouten);
- boundary drift with stimulus range (prior updating, as in Martinovic et al.).
- **Precision.** Precision-weighting accounts in the Feldman & Friston tradition make the same moves dynamically. Colour has a parallel Bayesian treatment of memory-colour effects (Witzel, Olkkonen & Gegenfurtner 2018, *i-Perception*). [uni-giessen](http://www.allpsych.uni-giessen.de/sfb/personal/mitarbeiter/witzel.html)
- **Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria (2025)**, *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 176:106296, add the consciousness-level piece. ## Site-Wide Methodology Improvements

This is the only section that discusses the site's AI-assisted generation and review pipeline.

1. **Extend the Crossref gate to stance.** The 2026-10-01 changelog shows the pipeline already checks DOIs, volumes and verbatim abstract quotes at Crossref and Europe PMC (for example the Schnakers 2009 and Cruse 2011 corrections).
