## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **"Double dissociation" asserted with only one arm exhibited.** The article twice called the agency/ownership relation a *double* dissociation ("This double dissociation is the structural point"; "agency and ownership *doubly dissociate*") while the body developed only the agency-lost/ownership-preserved arm. The converse arm was introduced in the terminology section (alien hand as partial hemisomatognosia — disowned *limb*) and then never connected back to the argument. Since "double dissociation" is a technical term with a specific requirement, this was a scientific-claim precision defect rather than a thin-section issue. **Resolution**: added a short paragraph making the converse arm explicit — in alien hand what fails is the sense that the limb belongs to the patient, the disowned thing being the limb rather than the act — and re-anchored the following sentence to "That double dissociation ... if authorship and ownership can come apart in either direction." Grounded entirely in the already-verified Marchetti & Della Sala 1998 distinction; no new citation introduced, and the claim is deliberately scoped to what that source asserts (a feeling of non-belonging contrasted with involuntary motor activity), not to an imaging-verified claim about preserved voluntary control.

- **Over-commitment in the new cross-link paragraph.** The installed paragraph read: "The two syndromes bracket the same constructed signal from opposite sides—absent where the act is genuinely one's own, supplied where it is not." The clause "supplied where it is not [one's own]" asserts that the confabulated act in the interpreter case is *not the subject's own*, which (a) prejudges the split-brain subjecthood question this same article declares undecided fourteen lines later ("In split-brain the question is whether divided perceptual report means divided experience"), and (b) diverges from the sibling article's own careful formulation, which says the signal is "*supplied* for a choice the narrating system never made" — a claim about the narrating system, not about ownership of the act. **Resolution**: rewritten to "withheld from a movement the person's own body made, supplied for a choice the narrating system never made," matching the sibling's precision and removing the smuggled metaphysical commitment.

- **"Syndromes" mischaracterises the interpreter.** The interpreter is not a syndrome — the sibling article establishes it as "a standing feature of ordinary minds, not a lesion artifact," on the choice-blindness evidence (Johansson et al. 2005) in neurologically intact adults. **Resolution**: "The two syndromes" → "The two cases".
