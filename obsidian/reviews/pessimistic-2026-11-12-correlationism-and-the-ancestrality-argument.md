## Executive Summary

The quotation work on this page is sound: the 2026-09-29 deep review verified every quoted string, and the strings re-checked here (Meillassoux 2012, Hallward 2008, Zahavi 2016) still match. The weaknesses are in the placement argument. The page classifies the Map with Meillassoux's 2012 vocabulary but stops reading the lecture two pages before he supplies the category that fits (a non-necessitarian postulate he calls *hyperphysics*) and before he states his own objection to dualism. Two of the page's central inferences use the wrong kind of premise: an existence posit is offered as "the exact denial" of a thesis the page itself defines as being about access, and two statistical-concealment positions are said to "commit the Map" to a claim about mind-independence that they do not entail. ### 1. The placement uses Meillassoux's 2012 vocabulary but misses the category he provides for it, and says the literature has not considered a question his lecture answers

- **Sentence A** (count = 1): "What remains in his vocabulary is dogmatic or metaphysical realism of a Cartesian stripe, and the Map should accept the description."
- **Sentence B** (count = 1): "Whether that reading blocks his objection is a question the Map puts to itself, not one the literature has considered."
- **What is wrong**: the page takes its definitions of correlationism, subjectalism, speculative and materialism from the 2012 Berlin lecture (transcript pp. 2 and 4). The same lecture defines the two words in Sentence A at p. 12: "I call 'metaphysical' any speculation that claims to accede to the absolute according to a more or less extended modality of the Principle of Sufficient Reason", and the position of supplying the necessary reason is the one "that I call 'dogmatic' or 'absolutist'". The page's next sentence says the Map asserts no necessary entity and holds Tenet 1 as "a chosen starting point held fallibly". On his definition that is neither metaphysical nor dogmatic, so the label the page tells the Map to accept does not apply by the page's own description of the Map. At p. 13 he names what does apply: "I call 'hyperphysics' every theory that postulates a reality other than that investigated by science, qua heuristic explanation for the supposedly ultimate components of our world, itself recognized as one contingent world among others that are really possible", adding that "all hyperphysics (vitalist, idealist, spiritualist, etc.) become equally legitimate within their order; and speculative materialism has nothing against them." So Sentence B is false as written: he considered the non-necessitarian case and waved it through. The cost he attaches (p. 14) is that such a proposal is "postulative, hypothetical, and without any foundation in necessity", which sits awkwardly beside the page's claim that the Map is "*speculative* in his 2012 sense, claiming an absolute outside the circle of thought". A fallibly held postulate and an attained absolute are different things in his scheme; the page claims both.
- **Also missing from the same pages**: his own treatment of mind. He affirms "a radical ex nihilo emergence of realities (sensations, perception, etc.) that absolutely did not exist before" in "this world of dead matter" (p. 14), says "we need dualisms everywhere" (p. 15), and then states the objection a dualist owes an answer to: that irreducible levels stay coordinated "is the enigma of all dualisms – an enigma that remains, and with it all of its religious overtones – unless we understand that only ex nihilo emergence is at once entirely rational and entirely immanent" (p. 15). This is the nearest point of contact between Meillassoux and the Map, and it is his strongest remark against the Map's position. The page instead cites Harman for "dead matter" and leaves "*Not his materialism*" resting on the one phrase "devoid of all subjectivity".
- **Source check**: transcript at spekulative-poetik.de (Mackay translation), fetched by curl and converted with pdftotext; all strings above grepped in the raw text. Page numbers are the transcript's own footer numbers.
- **Fix**: replace Sentence A's label with hyperphysics and say why (no Principle of Sufficient Reason, no necessary entity); delete or reverse Sentence B; decide between "speculative" and "postulative" for the Map and say which; add one or two sentences reporting his coordination objection and what the Map answers (interaction at the interface is the Map's account of coordination, and it is exactly what he calls an enigma). Estimated +90 to +130 words. Headroom 705.

### 2. Positing two terms is offered as "the exact denial" of a thesis the page defines as a thesis about access

- **Sentence** (count = 1): "Consciousness is not reducible to physical processes, but it is also not constitutive of them: the tenet posits two terms, each existing apart from the other, which is the exact denial of the correlationist thesis and equally of the subjectalist absolutisation of the correlate."
- **What is wrong**: applying the page's own definitions first. Correlationism is defined in the first section as the idea "that we only ever have access to the correlation between thinking and being, and never to either term considered apart from the other". Weak correlationism "holds that the in-itself is thinkable but not knowable". Neither denies that the terms *exist* apart; Kant affirms things in themselves. The page also reports Gabriel's caution that "many correlationist doctrines deny that the world's dependence on thought is causal or ontic", and in the Brassier section it states the distinction outright: "Asserting independence and asserting access are different claims". By those three passages, positing two independently existing terms is compatible with correlationism and cannot be its exact denial. What would deny it is a claim of access to a term apart. The page has that for the physical side (structure, ancestral history). For the mental side the *Not correlationist* paragraph offers only "Tenet 1 makes consciousness a distinct term with its own irreducible existence", an existence posit, under the heading sentence "The Map claims knowledge of both terms apart." (count = 1), while the Brassier section says the Map "asserts transparent access to neither" and that the dualist's self-knowledge is contribution-laden. "Knowledge" and "transparent access" can be reconciled (fallible, mediated knowledge), so this is not a flat contradiction. It is the epistemic/metaphysical equivocation the skill checks for: a metaphysical posit is doing the work an epistemic claim is required for. The 2026-09-29 deep review recorded the nearby worry ("on what basis is the mental term known apart?") as answered by the correlationism-about-the-correlate paragraph; that paragraph answers a different question (whether access is privileged) and leaves this one open.
- **Fix**: in the Dualism paragraph, change "which is the exact denial of the correlationist thesis" to rest the denial on what the Map claims to *know* of the physical term, and say plainly what it claims to know of the mental term apart from the correlation. If the honest answer is "that it exists and is irreducible, by argument, and nothing of its categorical nature", the page should say that the Map is weak-correlationist about the mental term's nature as well as the physical term's. That is consistent with the rest of the page and with the constitutive-exclusion self-refutation paragraph. Roughly net +30 to +50 words.

### 3. P-Q2 and P-Q9 are concealment claims and do not yield the mind-independence claim they are said to commit the Map to

- **Sentence** (count = 1): "Together they commit the Map to the claim Brassier demands and the constitutive-exclusion article implies without stating: contemporaneous physical processes outside the neural interface are as mind-independent as ancestral ones."
- **What is wrong**: "they" is P-Q2 and P-Q9. P-Q2 says outcome frequencies stay Born-exact "in every publicly conditionable context; only conditionals on the conscious state itself are free". P-Q9 says the interface leaves only indirect traces and that "Self-concealment is local to the *unconditioned* aggregate marginal". Both are about what the interface does to *statistics where it operates*. Neither says where it operates, and neither is about independence: a selection that is statistically invisible is still a selection. So the inference has two gaps. First, locality (selection at the neural interface "and nowhere else") comes from Tenet 2 and from `concepts/prebiotic-collapse` ("Where no interface is present, the physical collapse dynamics also fix *which* pointer state actualises"), which the paragraph states in its preceding sentences and then does not credit. Second, concealment is an epistemic property and Brassier's demand is ontological; citing undetectability as the ground for independence runs the two together. A critic can turn it round: if the reason the star is independent is that the statistics are untouched, then neural outcomes, whose statistics are equally untouched, would be independent too, and the fourth rung would have nothing to constitute.
- **Related over-statement in the sentence before it** (count = 1): "P-Q2 holds that the default reading preserves Born statistics exactly, so even where the interface operates the physical world's statistics are untouched; P-Q9 holds that the interface is self-concealing by construction." P-Q2 as now worded leaves conditionals on the conscious state free, and the register's 2026-09-09 update to P-Q9 was made because pages were quoting it as silencing a channel the tenet holds open. "Untouched" without "unconditioned" or "aggregate" repeats that. The tenet paragraph's "leaves aggregate statistics untouched (P-Q2)" has the scope word and is fine.
- **Fix**: credit locality to Tenet 2 and the prebiotic-collapse two-jobs passage; keep P-Q2 and P-Q9 as what they are (the reason the local contribution leaves the public statistical record alone), scoped to the unconditioned marginal; drop "Together they commit". Roughly net 0 to +25 words.

### 4. Hallward is quoted three times as the published reply; the published rejoinder to the quoted sentence is absent, and so are his other three charges

- **Sentence** (count = 1): "The reply literature is largely unpersuaded."
- **What is wrong**, by check (c-vi) of `project/quantum-claim-and-quotation-disciplines`. *Target and version*: Hallward's 2008 review answers the 2008 Continuum translation. A longer version appeared in 2011 as a chapter of *The Speculative Turn* (Bryant, Srnicek and Harman, eds., re.press, open access), whose first note reads "A shorter version of this essay first appeared as a review in Radical Philosophy, no. 152, 2008, pp. 51-7." The page cites only the short version and does not say a longer one exists. *Strongest arguments*: Hallward makes four charges. The page reports the first (epistemology and ontology run together) and uses it again for the facticity argument. The other three are absent: metaphysical necessity confused with natural necessity, pure mathematics confused with applied mathematics, and no account of concrete change. The second and third bear on the Map more than the first does, because the page's own position is physics-first and it notes hyper-chaos "conflicts in spirit" with stable Born statistics. *The reply to the critic*: the chapter that follows Hallward's in the same volume, Nathan Brown's "The Speculative and the Specific: On Hallward and Meillassoux", lists the four charges and answers each. On the sentence the page quotes ("correlationism as Meillassoux defines it is in reality an epistemological theory"), Brown says Hallward's statement "fails, however, to account for the logic of succession inherent in such a thought": the correlationist reaches the ancestral only by "logical retrojection" from the present, so "stricto sensu, the correlationist cannot think ancestral objects as prior to the thought that thinks them", and "Hallward does not engage the problem as it is formulated insofar as he ignores Meillassoux's critique of logical retrojection altogether." So "largely unpersuaded" is a survey claim built on three critics with the one published defence against the first of them left out.
- **Scope of the damage**: limited. The page declares the arche-fossil dispute moot for the Map, and Brown's rejoinder addresses the ancestrality use of Hallward's p. 55, not the "independently of our thinking them so" sentence the page relies on in the facticity sections (that string appears only in Hallward's own chapter in the volume). The finding is about the fairness of the survey, not about the Map's reply.
- **Source check**: *The Speculative Turn* full text from archive.org (djvu text), grepped. Hallward's 2008 PDF fetched from the Radical Philosophy archive.
- **Fix**: one sentence after the Hallward quotation naming Brown's retrojection rejoinder; one clause naming Hallward's other three charges; add Brown (2011) to References and note the 2011 version in the Hallward entry. Roughly +50 to +70 words plus one reference.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would say the page spends 3,300 words locating a position on a ladder built by a philosopher who thinks the question of what exists belongs to the sciences. Meillassoux says so in the lecture the page quotes: "If you want to know or to think what is, you must necessarily... do so by way of a certain regime of experience" (p. 12). The page's own verdict that the Map is "on the deference side" of Zahavi's bind concedes the point and then keeps a non-physical mind that no regime of experience delivered. Item 1 is the textual form of this complaint.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would press item 2. The page says the Map "claims knowledge of both terms apart" and supports the mental half with a tenet. A tenet is a stipulation. If self-knowledge is as contribution-laden as the page admits, then what the Map knows of consciousness is how consciousness seems, which is the correlate and not the term apart.

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark would say the answer to Brassier is bought with physics the Map does not have. The locality of selection, the Born-exact corridor, and objective reduction everywhere else are three unconfirmed commitments, and the register grades two of them D for external evidence. The page presents them as "already registered", which is true of the register and silent about the grade. The 2026-09-29 deep review handled the objective-reduction calibration; the P-Q2 and P-Q9 grades are not mentioned on the page.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch would accept the corrected No Many Worlds paragraph (Everett is realist about the ancestral) and then ask what "one actual truth-maker, fixed then by objective reduction" adds for the arche-fossil argument. Meillassoux's point is about literal sense, and the universal wavefunction gives ancestral statements a literal sense without a collapse. The paragraph states a difference between the views; it does not show the difference matters to this argument.

### The Empiricist
A Popperian would say the page ends with a classification nobody can test and admits it ("no author in the literature surveyed here classifies an interactionist dualism of this kind"). That is honest. Less honest is the unscoped absence claim beside it: see the Bitbol lead under Unsupported Claims.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would reject "both terms apart" as two reifications. The prior review marked this as a tenet-boundary disagreement and it is not re-raised. One thing is new: the strongest living correlationist reply to Meillassoux, Bitbol's, draws on Madhyamaka explicitly (per the Bastien review cited below), and the page's survey does not include it.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The enigma of all dualisms
- **Current content says**: the Map is not Meillassoux's materialism because its absolute contains a non-physical mind.
- **A critic would argue**: Meillassoux already has irreducible mind, by ex nihilo emergence, and says dualism's coordination problem "remains" unless emergence is uncaused. The Map's interaction mechanism is a proposed reason for coordination, which on his view reintroduces sufficient reason.
- **Suggested response**: state it and accept the cost. The Map does offer a reason for coordination and does not accept that reasons for what exists are illegitimate; Tenet 5 is where it parts from him.

### Retrojection
- **Current content says**: the arche-fossil dispute is moot for the Map.
- **A critic would argue**: Brown's retrojection point applies to anyone who holds that what is encountered is partly constituted by the encounter, which is the fourth rung.
- **Suggested response**: the locality answer (item 3, correctly grounded) is the reply: what is constituted is the neural outcome, and the ancestral event is reached by inference from records the Map takes realistically.
