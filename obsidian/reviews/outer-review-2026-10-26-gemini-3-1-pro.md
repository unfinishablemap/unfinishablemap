## 1. Introduction and Overview of Disqualifying Flaws

The manuscript under review, which centres on the concept of the "Thrownness Void"—defined by the author as "the structural inaccessibility of the pre-constitutional moment, the gap between non-existence and existence"—attempts to articulate a novel phenomenological boundary condition for consciousness. By extending the temporal arc of what the manuscript terms the "transit void" backward to the initiation of subjective experience, the text attempts to synthesize Heideggerian *Geworfenheit* (thrownness), Husserlian time-consciousness, and Merleau-Ponty’s paradox of constitution into a unified framework. This framework purportedly demonstrates the hard limits of physicalist explanation and serves as a foundational pillar for a non-reductive, interactionist-dualist view of consciousness.

However, evaluated strictly on its theoretical rigor, engagement with the contemporary philosophy of mind literature, and logical coherence, the manuscript is categorically unfit for publication in a top-tier journal such as *Mind*, *Synthese*, or *Philosophical Studies*. The analysis demonstrates a catastrophic and systemic failure to engage with the 2020–2025 scientific and philosophical literature that directly resolves, dissolves, or radically recontextualizes the very phenomenological "void" it claims to have discovered. Rather than testing its hypotheses against formidable contemporary physicalist, illusionist, and computational counter-frameworks, the manuscript systematically insulates itself through question-begging assumptions. It presupposes interactionist dualism and the inadequacy of Occam's Razor as *a priori* tenets, rather than arguing for them, thereby operating in a closed, self-reinforcing epistemological loop.

The manuscript’s fundamental failure lies not merely in its omission of competing literature, but in its profound misunderstanding of the dialectical obligations required in contemporary consciousness studies. When a theorist posits a "void"—an unbridgeable epistemic or ontological gap—they must demonstrate that this gap cannot be accommodated by the most advanced reductive or functionalist models available. The author fails to do this. The following sections detail the manuscript’s systemic failures across four primary axes: (1) its epistemological gerrymandering and reliance on assumed methodological commitments; (2) its gross misappropriation of the phenomenological tradition; (3) its total evasion of contemporary predictive processing and active inference frameworks; and (4) its failure to address the formidable challenges posed by contemporary illusionism and the meta-problem of consciousness.

## 2. Epistemological Gerrymandering: The Substitution of Assumption for Argument

The most immediate and structurally fatal flaw of the manuscript is its overt reliance on foundational assumptions that predetermine its conclusions, effectively insulating its central claims from empirical or theoretical falsification. The text operates within a broader architectural framework constrained by explicit "tenets," notably a commitment to dualism (the irreducibility of consciousness to physical processes), bidirectional causation (interactionist-dualism), minimal quantum interaction, the rejection of the many-worlds interpretation, and a preemptive rejection of parsimony (epistemic humility regarding Occam's razor).

### 2.1 The Evasion of Dialectical Obligations

In contemporary academic philosophy, asserting a non-reductive theory of consciousness requires demonstrating why reductive, functional, or eliminative theories fail to capture the target phenomenon. This manuscript bypasses this fundamental disciplinary requirement. By taking dualism as an unquestioned methodological starting point, the author effectively assumes away the explanatory obligations of the mind-body problem. The text attempts to justify this by claiming that "Starting with dualism leaves the inquiry open to exploring consciousness, meaning, and reality deeply," whereas assuming physicalism "assumes away the exact phenomenon you are attempting to explain".

This is a profound mischaracterization of the contemporary physicalist project. When the manuscript encounters the "thrownness void"—the cognitive inability to access the conditions under which consciousness became conscious—it immediately categorizes this as an "Unexplorable" or "Occluded" metaphysical gap stemming from the irreducibility of the mind. The reasoning is entirely circular: the manuscript observes a cognitive limitation (a subject cannot remember or access the exact moment they became conscious) and immediately attributes this to a dualistic disconnect between physical initiation and phenomenal onset, because its foundational tenets demand a dualistic interpretation of all cognitive limits.

The manuscript completely ignores Type-B physicalism (which argues that phenomenal concepts refer to physical properties via direct, non-descriptive mechanisms) and local reduction strategies, assuming they have been defeated by decades-old formulations of the explanatory gap (e.g., Chalmers's zombie argument). The manuscript effectively treats the "void" not as a psychological or cognitive phenomenon to be explained, but as a weaponized artifact of its own unproven metaphysical commitments.

### 2.2 The Conflation of Cognitive Limits with Ontological Gaps

The manuscript relies heavily on arguments regarding the "limits of thought" and "cognitive closure," suggesting that the pre-constitutional moment is structurally inaccessible. Yet, it fails to engage with the contemporary literature on epistemic limits. For instance, the manuscript completely ignores the robust 2020–2025 structuralist literature which argues that phenomenal character is fully exhausted by its relational properties in phenomenal space (e.g., Phenomenal Structuralism). If phenomenal character is entirely relational, then what the manuscript perceives as a "void" is merely the boundary of a relational network, not an ontological gap pointing toward a non-physical substance or property.

By framing the limits of human cognition as evidence for dualism, the text commits a basic category error: it conflates the representational limits of a physical system with the metaphysical structure of the universe. The manuscript posits that because we cannot *conceive* of the physical mechanics of our own phenomenal initiation from the first-person perspective, those physical mechanics must be insufficient to explain our initiation. This is a classic violation of the distinction between epistemological limits and ontological reality, a distinction that has been rigorously formalized in the recent literature on phenomenal concepts and which the manuscript simply declines to read.

## 3. The Phenomenological Deficit: Misappropriation of Canonical Texts

The manuscript attempts to ground its concept of the "thrownness void" in the classical phenomenological tradition, heavily citing Heidegger's *Geworfenheit* (thrownness), Husserl's epoché and inner time-consciousness, and Merleau-Ponty's embodied constitution. While the author correctly identifies that these figures dealt with the limits of subjective constitution, the manuscript strips their concepts of their original existential and naturalistic contexts. The text violently mutates these phenomenological structures to serve its pre-ordained dualistic architecture, revealing a superficial and anachronistic reading of the primary sources that ignores the last five years of specialized scholarship.

### 3.1 The Distortion of Heideggerian Facticity

In *Being and Time* (1927), Heidegger introduces the concept of *Geworfenheit* (thrownness) to describe Dasein's facticity—the condition of always already finding oneself situated in a world, characterized by a specific state-of-mind or attunement (*Befindlichkeit*). Thrownness is an existential structure of worldly engagement; it highlights that human beings do not create themselves *ex nihilo* but are delivered over to a world that pre-exists them, laden with historical and cultural meaning.

The manuscript utterly butchers this concept. It redefines thrownness as a temporal "void" marking the transition from non-existence to existence, identifying it as "the structural inaccessibility of the pre-constitutional moment". This is a gross misreading. Heidegger is explicitly concerned with the inescapable embeddedness of human understanding within a hermeneutic circle. Thrownness is not about the inability to remember the nanosecond one became conscious; it is about the ontological weight of one's historical situation.

By reducing thrownness to an inaccessible "pre-constitutional moment" of subjective initiation, the manuscript flattens a rich ontological description of worldly embeddedness into a simplistic, dualistic temporal boundary. The contemporary literature on Heidegger firmly rejects this kind of Cartesian subject-object temporalization. Recent scholarship emphasizes that facticity is "not an abrupt vertical fall into existence" from some non-physical realm, but an ongoing, situated, and inherently relational attunement (*Befindlichkeit*). The manuscript attempts to graft a dualist "initiation problem" onto a philosopher whose entire life's work was dedicated to destroying the dualist framework that makes such a problem intelligible.

### 3.2 The Evasion of Contemporary Time-Consciousness Scholarship

The manuscript’s engagement with Husserlian time-consciousness is similarly anachronistic. The text notes that Husserl analyzed the stream of consciousness through the structures of retention (the holding onto the just-past) and protention (the anticipation of the just-about-to-be). The manuscript leverages this to argue that the past is "inaccessible," that consciousness cannot re-enter its own constitutional origins, and that this points to a fundamental "void". The site's own changelog reveals an internal struggle to correctly attribute these Husserlian concepts, noting a need to fix a misattribution regarding retention-protention appearing across all temporal voids, yet the substantive philosophical error remains uncorrected.

What the manuscript egregiously omits is the extensive 2020–2025 literature bridging Husserlian time-consciousness with cognitive science and spatiotemporal neuroscience. Contemporary phenomenologists like Dan Zahavi have spent recent years operationalizing Husserl’s passive synthesis and inner time-consciousness in ways that directly integrate with empirical, physicalist frameworks.

Recent neurophenomenological studies explicitly model how the brain's intrinsic temporal dynamics align with subjective experience. These studies demonstrate that inner time-consciousness is not a marker of a dualistic "void," but a measurable feature of neural dynamics embedded in the world's larger spatiotemporal structure. For example, the spatiotemporal approach in neuroscience posits that brain activity and subjective experience share common features consisting of space and time, where the brain constantly generates predictions about its internal and external environment. By ignoring the modern naturalization of Husserl, the manuscript treats time-consciousness as a mystical limit rather than a rigorously modeled cognitive dynamic, cutting itself off from the entire discipline of modern neurophenomenology.

### 3.4 Flattening Merleau-Ponty's Paradox

The manuscript briefly cites Merleau-Ponty’s constitution/constituted paradox as a lineage for the thrownness void. Merleau-Ponty argued that the subject is both a constituting consciousness and a constituted object in the world, inextricably intertwined through the lived body. However, in the hands of the author, this paradox is stripped of its embodied, enactive foundation. Merleau-Ponty’s philosophy is deeply anti-dualist; the ambiguity of the body is precisely what dissolves the sharp boundary between mind and matter. To use Merleau-Ponty to support an interactionist-dualist framework that posits consciousness as an irreducible phenomenon requiring a "void" to interface with the physical is a betrayal of the text. As will be discussed in Section 6, the manuscript fails to realize that the 2020–2025 enactivist literature has entirely consumed and updated Merleau-Ponty's paradox without resorting to dualistic voids.

## 4. The Predictive Processing Blind Spot: Computational Phenomenology and Active Inference

Perhaps the most glaring and disqualifying empirical deficit in the manuscript is its complete, inexplicable silence on the dominant framework in the 2020–2025 cognitive neuroscience of consciousness: Predictive Processing (PP) and Active Inference. Pioneered by figures such as Karl Friston, Anil Seth, and Jakob Hohwy, PP models the brain as a hierarchical prediction engine that continuously generates top-down sensory predictions to minimize free energy, or prediction error.

The manuscript’s central claim is that consciousness is plagued by a "thrownness void"—a structural inability to access the moment of its own initiation. The author views this as a profound metaphysical mystery that supports non-reductive dualism. However, under the predictive processing framework, this "inaccessibility" is neither mysterious nor dualistic; it is a mathematical necessity of how generative models operate.

### 4.1 The Computational Necessity of the "Void"

In active inference, conscious experience is the result of a system inferring the causes of its sensory states. A generative model can only infer states that are parameterized within its model space. A cognitive system cannot compute or represent its own pre-existence because, by definition, the generative model did not exist to register prediction errors prior to its own assembly. The "pre-constitutional moment" is inaccessible simply because the biological hardware required to encode the prior probabilities and run the hierarchical message-passing scheme had not yet booted up.

Recent literature, such as the 2023 paper "Time-consciousness in computational phenomenology: a temporal analysis of active inference" in *Neuroscience of Consciousness*, explicitly maps the temporal structure of experience using PP. This literature provides a fully tractable, physicalist explanation for the exact phenomenological boundary the manuscript describes.

To illustrate, predictive coding relies on a message-passing scheme where neuronal representations at higher levels of cortical hierarchies generate predictions of representations at lower levels. The "void" the author feels when looking back at their own initiation is merely the failure of top-down predictions to find a lower-level representation of a time before the hierarchy existed. The fact that the manuscript fails to even mention predictive processing, let alone construct a counterargument against it, indicates that the author is either unaware of the current state of the field or deliberately ignoring falsifying data.

### 4.2 The Markov Blanket and Initiation

Furthermore, the active inference framework relies on the concept of a Markov blanket—a statistical boundary that separates the internal states of an organism from external states, mediating them via sensory and active states. The "initiation" of a conscious subject is the point at which a sufficiently complex Markov blanket begins to actively infer its own continued existence (what Seth refers to as "instrumental interoceptive inference" for allostatic regulation).

The inability of the internal states to directly access the historical physical conditions of the blanket's formation is a trivial feature of Bayesian networks, not an epistemological "void" validating interactionist dualism. The system's boundary is defined by its conditional independence from the external world, given its Markov blanket. It is mathematically impossible for the internal states to peer "outside" or "before" the blanket without using the blanket's own sensory states. By treating a standard mathematical limitation of biological computation as a gateway to the metaphysical beyond, the manuscript commits a profound error of interpretation.

## 5. Illusionism and the Meta-Problem: The Failure to Address Introspective Opacity

The manuscript consistently assumes that the phenomenal "seemings" of consciousness—such as the feeling that there is an unbridgeable gap, a surplus, or an inaccessible pre-constitutional void—are accurate readouts of metaphysical reality. It takes the phenomenology of the "void" at face value. In doing so, it utterly fails to engage with the robust 2020–2025 literature on Illusionism and the Meta-Problem of Consciousness, which directly subverts this assumption.

Illusionism, most notably championed by Keith Frankish and François Kammerer, argues that phenomenal consciousness (in the philosophically loaded, dualistic sense) does not exist; rather, our cognitive systems are hardwired to mistakenly infer its existence due to introspective limitations. This framework provides a devastating counter-narrative to the manuscript’s claims.

### 5.1 Introspective Opacity and the Illusion of the Void

The manuscript asserts that the inaccessibility of the past and the pre-constitutional moment (the thrownness void) points to the irreducibility of consciousness. However, contemporary illusionism fully explains this phenomenon via the concept of *introspective opacity*. As argued extensively in the recent literature (e.g., Kammerer, 2021, *Synthese*), cognitive systems lack access to their own lower-level functional mechanisms.

When a cognitive system introspects, it does not see the neurons firing, the synaptic weight updates, or the generative models processing prediction errors; it simply receives a heavily edited, low-dimensional summary of the data. Because the system is opaque to its own operations, it naturally infers that its mental states are primitive, non-physical, and ungrounded. The "thrownness void" is exactly what we would expect an introspectively opaque functional system to report.

When the system tries to look backward to its own initiation, it hits a representational blank wall. It cannot track the sub-personal physical mechanisms of its own neural ontogeny. Thus, the system falsely concludes it has encountered a metaphysical "void". The manuscript’s failure to recognize the "void" as a potential artifact of introspective opacity is a disqualifying theoretical blind spot. The manuscript simply assumes that the limits of introspection correspond to the limits of reality.

### 5.2 Designed Certainty and the Moorean Fallacy

The manuscript places absolute, unquestioning trust in the first-person phenomenology of the void. Yet, Kammerer’s (2021, 2023) "Designed Certainty" view explicitly warns against this naivety. Kammerer posits that our cognitive systems are evolutionarily designed to hold beliefs about our own phenomenal experiences with absolute certainty. This subjective infallibility is a functional feature designed to ensure rapid decision-making and allostatic regulation; it is not an ontological proof of non-physicality.

When challenged by illusionism, dualists often resort to a Moorean argument: "It is obviously true that I am having a phenomenal experience, therefore physicalism is false." Kammerer (2022) in *Philosophical Studies* systematically dismantles this Moorean defense, demonstrating that subjective obviousness is dialectically irrelevant when the opposing scientific theory (illusionism) *predicts* that very feeling of obviousness.

The manuscript walks directly into this trap. It assumes that because the "thrownness void" feels real, inexplicable, and impenetrable to the subject, it must be real and impenetrable in reality. The author seems entirely unaware that illusionists have mapped this exact cognitive illusion. By ignoring the illusionist critique of phenomenal certainty, the manuscript defaults to a naive, pre-scientific reliance on introspection, rendering its philosophical conclusions wholly unpersuasive to any contemporary scholar of mind.

## 6. Enactivism, Extended Mind, and the Relational Subject

A final, fatal theoretical gap in the manuscript is its hyper-individualistic, neurocentric, and punctiform framing of consciousness's origins. The "thrownness void" is depicted as an isolated, discrete transition where a singular consciousness is instantiated into a body, unable to access the conditions of its insertion. This framing is fundamentally incompatible with the 2020–2025 advances in 4E (Embodied, Embedded, Enacted, Extended) Cognition, particularly the work of Shaun Gallagher on Enactivism and the Socially Extended Mind.

### 6.1 Consciousness as Relational Practice, Not Punctiform Event

In recent literature (e.g., Gallagher 2020, 2024, 2025 in *Synthese* and related journals), enactivism demonstrates that cognition and consciousness are not localized entirely within the individual brain, nor do they "begin" at a discrete pre-constitutional moment in a vacuum. Instead, mind is enacted through dynamic, ongoing coupling with the physical, social, and institutional environment. The conditions that found consciousness are distributed across affordances, cultural practices, and cognitive institutions.

If the mind is socially and environmentally extended—constituted in part by interactions with other agents and institutional structures—then the search for a singular, inaccessible "pre-constitutional moment" of subjective initiation is fundamentally misguided. The manuscript’s search for a temporal "void" relies on an outdated Cartesian assumption that consciousness is a discrete container that is abruptly "turned on."

In enactivist terms, the individual is gradually enculturated and structurally coupled to the environment. The inability to pinpoint a precise moment of subjective initiation is not a "void" of dualistic occlusion; it is the natural consequence of consciousness being a continuous, smeared, relational process rather than a punctual event.

For example, Mastrogiorgio and Gallagher (2025/2026) discuss how "cognitive institutions" provide an external resource where individuals enact mind-extending processes. The subject is not "thrown" across a void into existence; the subject emerges as a node in a pre-existing social and environmental mesh. The manuscript completely fails to test its spatial and temporal assumptions against the contemporary extended mind literature, rendering its core phenomenological claims theoretically localized, fragile, and obsolete.

## 7. Specific Weaknesses and Final Verdict

The manuscript fails to meet the standards of peer review due to its systemic evasion of competing contemporary frameworks, its reliance on circular, unargued assumptions, and its severe misappropriation of foundational phenomenological texts. Specifically, the following five fatal weaknesses demand rejection:

1. **Failure to Address Illusionist Models of Introspective Opacity:** The manuscript asserts that the inaccessibility of the pre-constitutional moment (the "void") is evidence of an irreducible, non-physical boundary pointing toward dualism. It completely ignores contemporary illusionist literature which demonstrates that cognitive architectures structurally lack access to their own lower-level functional mechanisms, thereby naturally generating the false intuition of an inexplicable "gap" or "void" without any dualistic implications.

2. **Uncritical Reliance on the "Designed Certainty" of Phenomenal Intuitions:** The manuscript treats the first-person subjective experience of the "thrownness void" as infallible evidence of a metaphysical reality, refusing to countenance that the mind might deceive itself about its own nature. It fails to test this against the contemporary physicalist counterargument that evolutionary cognitive design enforces absolute, yet factually erroneous, certainty regarding phenomenal experiences (the Moorean fallacy).

- *Citation:* Kammerer, F. (2022). "How can you be so sure? Illusionism and the obviousness of phenomenal consciousness." *Philosophical Studies*, 179(9), 2845–2867.
3. **Evasion of Predictive Processing and Active Inference Mechanisms:** The author claims that the temporal inability of a subject to access its own initiation validates a dualist mystery. This entirely ignores the 2020–2025 predictive processing consensus, which mathematically proves that a generative model can only infer states within its temporal operational window; a system cannot compute its own pre-existence, rendering the "void" a banal necessity of Bayesian inference rather than a metaphysical revelation.

- *Citation:* Hohwy, J., & Seth, A. (2020/2021). "Predictive processing as an empirical theory for consciousness science." *Cognitive Neuroscience*, 12(2), 87-89. (And related PP literature mapping neural correlates).
4. **Ignorance of Computational Phenomenology’s Naturalization of Time-Consciousness:** The manuscript misappropriates Husserlian inner time-consciousness and Heideggerian thrownness to argue for a permanent, structurally occluded limit to cognition. It fails to engage with the specific recent literature that successfully integrates Husserlian temporal dynamics (retention/protention) into physicalist, computational active inference models, dissolving the very phenomenological boundary the manuscript claims is absolute.

- *Citation:* "Time-consciousness in computational phenomenology: a temporal analysis of active inference." (2023). *Neuroscience of Consciousness*, 2023(1), niad004.
5. **Incompatibility with Enactivism and the Socially Extended Mind:** The manuscript frames the "initiation" of consciousness as a discrete, isolated insertion of subjectivity into the world, ignoring the dominant 4E Cognition paradigms. These paradigms show that subjective initiation is a continuous, relational process distributed across environmental and social affordances (cognitive institutions), thereby exposing the manuscript's premise as relying on an obsolete Cartesian container-model of mind.

- *Citation:* Mastrogiorgio, A., & Gallagher, S. (2025/2026). "Norms are relational cognitive institutions: Practices and the ‘where’ question." *Journal of Institutional Economics / Synthese* (related lineage).

**Verdict on Academic-Submission Readiness:**
The manuscript is entirely unready for academic submission and warrants outright rejection due to its catastrophic failure to engage with the 2020–2025 philosophical literature, its reliance on circular, question-begging assumptions regarding dualism, and its persistent tendency to conflate standard cognitive representational limits with metaphysical voids.
