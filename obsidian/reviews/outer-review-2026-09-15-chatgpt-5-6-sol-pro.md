## Reply

# External referee report: *Pragmatism*

**Recommendation: major revision, not rejection.**

The post-modification article is substantially more candid and defensible than its headline suggests. It expressly acknowledges the prior commitment to phenomenal irreducibility, preserves the functionalist and epiphenomenalist replies, treats Jamesian “forcedness” as contestable, distinguishes van Fraassen’s position from the Map’s causal extension, and now applies Peirce’s maxim to the Map’s own quantum mechanism. Those are real strengths.

The central problem is that the page still oscillates between two claims:

1. **Defensible:** from within the Map’s phenomenological commitments, pragmatism exposes explanatory costs for some naturalisms.
2. **Unestablished:** pragmatism itself, reflexively applied on terms acceptable to pragmatists, pressures them toward interactionist dualism.

The second claim depends on precisely what the functionalist, Deweyan naturalist, Rortyan, Brandomian and deflationary pragmatist deny: that normative assessment must derive its authority from irreducible phenomenal consciousness and must be causally efficacious *as phenomenal*. The article acknowledges this dependence locally but continues to advertise the stronger conclusion globally. The current text and metadata confirm that tension.

## The 8 September modification

The modification is a genuine correction. The previous wording treated the proposed quantum influence as indistinguishable from chance under effectively every conceivable test. The revised passage now says that only the **unconditioned aggregate marginal** is constrained to remain Born-distributed, while a deviation conditioned on intention, task or subject remains empirically live. The repository commit explicitly identifies this as the purpose of the change.

That correction should remain. It defeats the easy criticism that the mechanism has been defined as undetectable under every possible empirical design.

It does not completely discharge Peirce’s maxim. Peirce’s criterion concerns conceivable effects on habit and possible practical consequences, including counterfactual circumstances, not merely what current or foreseeable instruments can detect. The article’s remaining answer—that the difference is “per-trial and indexical”—needs to identify what could differ in the selecting subject’s experience, deliberation or conduct. Merely stipulating that a different token outcome was actualised gives a metaphysical difference, but not yet a Peircean practical difference. [Stanford University+1](https://web.stanford.edu/class/history34q/readings/Peirce/Peirce_MakeOurIdeasClear.html)

A useful three-way distinction would be:

- a conditioned statistical signature, giving a public empirical difference;
- a stable subject-indexed difference in experience, deliberation or action;
- no such difference at either level, in which case the proposal remains pragmatically idle even if metaphysically intelligible.

## Attribution audit

### Peirce — **mostly faithful, but overgeneralised**

The description of belief as habit and the pragmatic maxim is broadly accurate. The claim that two purported conceptions lacking any conceivable practical difference are not genuinely different also fits the 1878 essay.

Two qualifications are needed:

- The maxim is not by itself a complete theory of truth, but saying Peirce intended it “not as a theory of truth” is too categorical. The same essay proceeds to discuss truth, reality and the regulative prospect of scientific convergence.
- Peirce cannot support the opening claim that pragmatism simply rejects truth as mirroring an independent reality. His position remains realist in a way the article’s umbrella definition obscures. [Stanford University+1](https://web.stanford.edu/class/history34q/readings/Peirce/Peirce_MakeOurIdeasClear.html)

### James on truth — **major revision required**

The article correctly rejects the crude caricature that James makes truth whatever anyone wants. James requires experiential constraint, continuity with established truths and successful verification.

But the statement that practical fruitfulness marks truth “not correspondence to an unobservable reality” misstates his argumentative form. James opens his truth discussion by saying that pragmatists and intellectualists agree that truth involves agreement with reality; the dispute concerns what “agreement” amounts to. He reconstructs it through verification, successful leading and workable experiential relations, while retaining copying as one genuine form of agreement. [Project Gutenberg+1](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5116/5116-h/5116-h.htm)

This matters to the article’s later argument. Jamesian pragmatic warrant need not derive solely from a felt sense that a theory “illuminates.” Prediction, coordination, inferential integration and resistance from experience can constrain inquiry without already being interpreted as an irreducible phenomenal demand.

### James’s *Human Immortality* — **possibility is inflated into endorsement**

The productive-versus-transmissive distinction is accurately reported. James argues that observed dependence of consciousness on the brain does not logically determine whether the brain produces consciousness or transmits/releases it. He treats both as metaphysical hypotheses and denies that the evidence then available automatically privileges production. [Wikisource+1](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Human_Immortality%3A_Two_Supposed_Objections_to_the_Doctrine)

The article goes further than the lecture supports when it adds that:

- human dignity thereby acquires a non-biological basis;
- consciousness receives a status in the universe denied by materialism;
- the result places interactionist dualism in a pragmatically privileged position.

Those are Map extensions. James establishes underdetermination and continuing possibility, not survival, substance persistence, interactionism or a particular theory of mental causation.

### James’s *The Will to Believe* — **qualified pass**

The revised treatment is unusually careful. It correctly identifies “forced” as the vulnerable condition and says the result may license openness rather than belief.

The remaining conduct-based route to forcedness is nevertheless the Map’s argument, not James’s. James himself gives an abstract theoretical dispute as a paradigm of an avoidable option: judgement may simply be declined. To overcome that, the Map must independently show that treatment of persons or one’s orientation toward death requires acting on a determinate metaphysics rather than on moral precaution under uncertainty. [Wikisource+1](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Will_to_Believe)

### James’s later radical empiricism — **important omitted counterevidence**

In 1904 James explicitly denies that “consciousness” names an entity or a distinctive kind of stuff, while preserving a functional distinction between knowing and what is known within “pure experience.” This does not straightforwardly make him a physicalist, but it sharply complicates the site’s use of the earlier transmissive hypothesis as a trajectory toward interactionist dualism. [Marxists](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/us/james1.htm)

The omission is material because the article invokes James as historical support while not giving his strongest anti-substance and anti-dualistic development comparable weight.

### Dewey — **the decisive source reversal**

This is the article’s most serious attribution problem.

First, the technical term is **“warranted assertibility,”** not “warranted assertion.” Dewey chooses the dispositional formulation deliberately: the warranted status of a conclusion remains embedded in an indefinitely continuing process of inquiry rather than denoting one final token assertion. [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/JohnDeweyLogicTheTheoryOfInquiry/%5BJohn_Dewey%5D_Logic_-_The_Theory_of_Inquiry_djvu.txt)

More importantly, the article says that felt recognition of a problem and felt recognition that a solution works are constitutive of inquiry, and that reducing inquiry to physical mechanism removes the normative standpoint. Dewey explicitly warns that describing a problematic situation as something “felt” is misleading when given a mentalistic interpretation. He treats problematic situations and their resolution as organism–environment transactions rather than as private mental contents. He also regards the interactionism-versus-automatism problem as malformed when it begins by positing something separately mental that must then interact with the biological-cultural person. [Internet Archive+2Internet Archive+2](https://archive.org/stream/JohnDeweyLogicTheTheoryOfInquiry/%5BJohn_Dewey%5D_Logic_-_The_Theory_of_Inquiry_djvu.txt)

The page is entitled to argue that Dewey’s naturalism fails. It is not entitled to present irreducible felt evaluation as an implication Dewey “did not fully pursue.” That reverses his inferential direction. The section should instead present:

1. Dewey’s own naturalistic account;
2. the Map’s objection that it cannot secure normative authority;
3. Dewey’s likely reply that normativity is constituted within inquiry and organism–environment practice rather than conferred by a private phenomenal assessor.

### Rorty — **the alleged internal edge is not internal**

The historical summary is partly fair: Rorty rejects representational foundations, abandons “getting reality right” as philosophy’s master task and evaluates vocabularies through their roles in human purposes and practices.

But the article’s proposed internal criticism—that vocabularies must be “assessable from somewhere”—does not yet expose an inconsistency. Rorty’s answer is that appraisal occurs from within contingent vocabularies, communities and conversations. He does not require a vocabulary-independent standpoint, an intrinsic phenomenal authority or an extra-vocabulary tribunal.

Rorty also explicitly rejects “relativism” when it means that every belief or vocabulary is equally good. Standards remain local, historical and conversational rather than absent. Calling Putnam an alternative to “Rorty’s relativism” without that qualification risks repeating the caricature Rorty expressly denies. [Pitt Sites+4Pitt Sites+4Pitt Sites+4](https://sites.pitt.edu/~rbrandom/Courses/Antirepresentationalism%20%282020%29/Texts/Rorty%20Pragmatism%20Relativism%20and%20Irrationalism.pdf)

The article therefore faces a dilemma:

- If “somewhere” means merely within a practice or vocabulary, Rorty has supplied it.
- If it means within irreducible phenomenal consciousness, the argument has imported the Map’s disputed premise and is no longer internal.

### Putnam — **careful but phenomenally overloaded and historically frozen**

This is one of the better sections. The account of Putnam’s internalist perspective, idealized rational acceptability, conceptual schemes, interests and anti-“magical” account of reference is substantially accurate. The ant-and-Churchill example is also used appropriately. [Internet Archive+4Internet Archive+4Internet Archive+4](https://ia800404.us.archive.org/12/items/HilaryPutnam/PutnamHilary-ReasonTruthAndHistory_text.pdf)

Three revisions are needed:

- The 1981 position should be called **internal realism** or the **internalist perspective**, rather than simply “pragmatic realism.”
- Putnam’s dependence on agents, purposes and rational standards does not entail phenomenal consciousness in the Map’s sense. A functionalist or social pragmatist can accept the agent-relativity without accepting dualism.
- The model-theoretic argument targets metaphysical realism under permissive interpretations and constraints. It should not be compressed into a general proof that physical-causal relations cannot contribute to fixing reference.

The page should also mention Putnam’s later movement toward natural/direct realism rather than presenting the 1981 phase as his settled endpoint.

### van Fraassen — **qualified pass**

The revised concept page makes the right distinction. Van Fraassen’s pragmatics of explanation concerns contrastive why-questions, contextual relevance and the interests of questioners. It does not itself establish phenomenal causation or bidirectional mind–matter interaction. [Carlo Delrazo Canuto+1](https://carlodelrazocanuto.files.wordpress.com/2010/03/bas-c-van-fraassen.pdf)

“Explaining is a speech act” is an acceptable compression, but the 1977 explanation paper should be cited directly. More importantly, the sibling topic article should be brought down to the concept page’s level of calibration: van Fraassen supplies contextual explanation, while causal efficacy is the Map’s additional argument.

### Millikan, Papineau and Dretske — **mostly accurate after correction**

The current distinctions are defensible:

- Millikan grounds proper function in selection history.
- Papineau develops an evolutionary teleosemantic architecture.
- Dretske’s account importantly includes recruitment and learning within the individual lifetime rather than simply phylogenetic function.

The August correction of the earlier Dretske grouping was therefore substantive and correct. The bibliography’s dates are also sound. [Cambridge University Press+4MIT Press+4MIT Press+4](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262631150/language-thought-and-other-biological-categories/)

Two deficiencies remain. Book-level references need chapter or page pinpoints, especially after a previous source-classification error. And teleosemantics should not be called *the* standing naturalist answer to normativity: it is one family of accounts of content and function.

## Missed deflationary and neo-pragmatist counterarguments

The article discusses teleosemantics and generic functionalism, but omits the alternatives most directly targeted at its reflexive move.

**Brandomian normative pragmatics** treats commitments, entitlements, assessment and inferential authority as socially instituted statuses within discursive practice. That gives “ought” and correctness a non-private basis without reducing them to whatever neural event happens or grounding them in irreducible phenomenal feeling.

**Pricean subject naturalism** starts with human linguistic and cognitive practices as activities of natural creatures. Its expressivist and deflationary treatment of representation is specifically designed to resist the inflationary inference from reflective practice to a special mind–world relation or dualist ontology.

**Rortyan conversational normativity** supplies contingent, communal standards without a vocabulary-independent authority.

**Deweyan inquiry naturalism** locates normative distinctions in the transformation of objectively problematic organism–environment situations.

These views may ultimately be inadequate, but together they block the article’s current dichotomy between bare physical mechanism and private phenomenal authority. [Pitt Sites+2Cambridge University Press+2](https://sites.pitt.edu/~rbrandom/Courses/Philosophy%20of%20Language%20%282018%29%20g.html)

A further distinction is essential: **the phenomenology of evaluation is not identical to the authority of evaluation**. A judgement can feel compelling without being warranted, and a norm can bind within a practice without being constituted by a feeling. The page currently moves too quickly from phenomenal character to normative authority and then from normative authority to phenomenal causation.

## Tenet-protective bracketing

### Dualism

The page candidly says that the argument depends on a prior commitment to phenomenal irreducibility. That concession is philosophically decisive. Once the premise is contested, pragmatism does not independently generate dualism; it reveals a cost *conditional on the Map’s framework*.

The article also slides between irreducibility and stronger consequences involving death, post-mortem survival, personal persistence and substance-like agency. Tenet 1 alone does not secure those claims.

### Minimal quantum interaction

The September correction restores the right symmetry. The remaining task is to specify a possible practical difference under the subject-indexed response, rather than treating “which outcome occurs” as automatically sufficient.

### Bidirectional interaction

This is now the strongest tenet section because it separates van Fraassen’s position from the Map’s extension and states the epiphenomenalist/supervenience reply.

The residual gap is the transition from:

> agents ask, assess and choose

to:

> irreducible phenomenal assessment, rather than its functional realization, causes the resulting behaviour.

That extra premise requires an independent defence.

### No Many-Worlds

This tenet is conspicuously absent from the target page’s relation-to-framework section. The sibling article treats branching as dissolving the standpoint from which pragmatic evaluation occurs, but the canonical tenets now acknowledge branch-relative agents, self-locating uncertainty and a live deflationary route. Pragmatism by itself therefore does not exclude Everettian agency. Rejection of Many-Worlds depends on the Map’s additional non-deflationary account of a unique subject. [[pragmatisms-path-to-dualism]]

### Limits of Occam’s razor

The article’s point is conditionally defensible: a parsimonious theory has not really simplified matters if it removes something indispensable to the practice by which simplicity is judged.

But neo-pragmatists deny that naturalism removes the standpoint; they claim to redescribe it functionally, socially or linguistically. The article cannot count “loss of the phenomenal standpoint” as a cost until it has independently established that those redescriptions fail. Otherwise, the conclusion is built into the cost function. The Map’s own tenet presents parsimony as a defeasible heuristic rather than an automatic ontology selector, which supports a more balanced formulation. [[tenets]]

- **Pragmatism’s Path to Dualism:** less careful than the concept page on van Fraassen, Many-Worlds and how much the argument establishes.

The structural problem is therefore not an insufficient number of links. It is that a link sometimes acts as a promissory note to a sibling page that states the inference more strongly than the present page allows.

## Citation hygiene

No central bibliography item appears fabricated, and most dates, titles and publishers are correct. The important problems are instead:

- “warranted assertion” for Dewey’s “warranted assertibility”;
