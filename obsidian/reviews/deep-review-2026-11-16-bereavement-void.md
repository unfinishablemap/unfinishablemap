## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **"At once" outran the evidence (lead and description).** The article opened "the survivor usually believes it at once". Its only quantitative support is Maciejewski et al. (2007), whose cohort was not interviewed within a month of the death, and Fuchs, in the sentence following the one the article quotes, reports "That is not true!" as a frequent first reaction. Resolution: "at once" became "soon" in the lead and the description; Fuchs's first-reaction sentence was added to The Lag with his own reading of it (a struggle between wish and reality).
- **Bounding study stated without its own bounds.** Maciejewski et al. was described as something that "rules out" calling the bereaved unable to believe the death. The full text excludes people who met criteria for complicated grief, covers natural deaths, begins at one month, and the abstract reports that rescaled disbelief "decreased from an initial high at 1 month postloss". Resolution: "rules out" became "counts against", and one sentence states the three limits. The item was also called a "self-report item"; the abstract says "Five rater-administered items". Corrected to "of five interview items".
- **Infurna and Luthar (2016) described as finding more class structure than it did.** The 27% and 36%/64% figures come from three- and two-class solutions that the authors examined "to compare the solutions with those in previously published analyses"; in both relaxed analyses they report that a one-class solution fitted the spousal-loss data best ("there may not be more than one distinct sub-group in the sample"). Resolution: one sentence added saying so; "in its final model the larger group dips and returns" narrowed to "its recovery class dips and returns".
- **Unsourced details from the writer's own knowledge.** "a general practitioner" (Rees) could not be confirmed at the publication record (the PMC page gives no occupation; one secondary page implies it) and was removed. "most of them elderly" for the three prevalence studies could be confirmed for two (Olson et al.: nursing homes; Grimby: early seventies) and not for Rees; narrowed to those two. "The most studied sign of the lag" was an unsourced superlative; now "A much-studied sign".
- **Capitalised mid-sentence quotation (Fuchs p. 49).** The source reads "the deep ambiguity of grief …: the other is lost as a real person". The article began the quotation "The other". Now run in as `In grief "the other is lost…"`.
- **Review type mislabelled.** Kamp et al. (2020) call their paper an "Interdisciplinary and Integrative Review"; "narrative review" was the note's label. Now "an integrative review with no pooled estimate".
- **Olson et al. 54%.** The abstract reads "Nineteen (54%) of the widows had never discussed the experiences with anyone before this study". The article had "in Olson et al.'s sample 54% had never told anyone". Reworded to the abstract's terms. Note for future reviewers: the abstract's own arithmetic is odd (19 of the 28 widows who reported experiences is 68%; 13 of 28 is the stated 46% for a neighbouring item), and the full text was not read. The figure is reported as the abstract gives it.
- **Paraphrase stronger than the source (Marušić).** "The difficulty is sharpest in prospect" — Marušić says the anticipation of grief's end "illuminates my puzzle". Reworded to that.
- **Causal claim about the two prevalence ranges.** "The ranges differ because…" asserted a cause neither review states. Kamp et al. describe Castelnovo et al. as "a systematic review framing SED as hallucinatory experiences" and their own range cites mixed-bereaved samples (Datson and Marwit; Longman et al.) beside widowed ones, so "different experiences and different samples" is supported; the sentence now says the ranges are not directly comparable for that reason.
- **Lead paraphrase of Fuchs.** "resolves by re-integrating the dead person" — Fuchs's abstract speaks of "a re-integration of the conflicting realities", and p. 55 of the relation being "re-established on a new level". Lead now says "re-establishing the relation to the dead person".
- **Tenet-section pointer.** "the felt character of grief resists functional analysis" was credited to `topics/emotion-and-dualism`. That page (L117) says the grief of bereavement "differs qualitatively from a stubbed toe, and that difference itself resists functional analysis", in a paragraph on emotional depth. Now "grief's qualitative depth resists functional analysis".
