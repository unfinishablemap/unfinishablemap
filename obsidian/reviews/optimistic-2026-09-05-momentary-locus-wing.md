## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

The page keeps the subject-side of experience in the explanandum without inflating it. "For-me-ness is the form conscious states take, and by Tenet 1 that form is not captured by physical description" (L91) is a dualist claim stated at the right size — a claim about form, not about a further entity — and the page immediately prices it: "That is a commitment, not a discovery." The honesty that will matter most to this persona is L119's concession that "the reply's structure is available to Strawson's physicalism": the Map borrows a structure and adds a tenet, and says which is which. And the register bites the Vulcan bullet the way this persona's own paper invites — P-MS1's sixth limb concedes that if Chalmers' verdict held up, "felt valence would not be necessary for status", and names the widening that would follow. A register that writes its author's strongest opponent into its shift conditions is one this persona can argue with.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The wing's physics is inherited, not asserted, and the inheritance is stated as debt: P-VS1 "must not read more confident about the metaphysics of causal work than the upstream register does", and the value-sensitive horn "carries more debt than the attention-only horn, not less" (L40, L50). What this persona will applaud is the refusal to let coupling count decide — "the fork turns on comparative evidence, not on coupling count" — because Tenet 2's minimality is "an empirical corridor … not a likelihood ranking". The present-affect relocation (P-VS1's 08-27 note: selection consults "the felt valence of anticipation, an occurrent state at choice") is the reading most congenial to an attention-first interactionism, and the register says so without claiming it as a result.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The reply is a phenomenological finding before it is a metaphysical one: "the badness of a pain-moment is bad *for the locus of that moment* — for whoever is undergoing it, now" (L87). The page's best sentence on the objection — "Momentariness does not remove that locus; it multiplies it" — is the kind of move that only works if one has actually looked at the phenomenon rather than the doctrine. The treatment of Zahavi's minimal self as "identified *with* the experiential dimension rather than posited behind it" (L85) is exact, and the page's handling of Alweiss is fair to a phenomenologist arguing against for-me-ness from inside phenomenology: her line is listed as a live countermodel, not dismissed.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Momentary experiential loci that perish and are succeeded, each with its own for-whom, is Whitehead's ontology in another vocabulary, and the page knows it — [[subjective-aim]] is cited as the place where momentariness poses "a near relative" of the combination problem. This persona will praise the page for building the value chain on occasions rather than on a substance: "A *persisting, substantial* self, which the no-self traditions deny, is not what the value chain draws on." The Buddhist inventory argument — that *vedanā* is one of the five aggregates, so "the analysis that dissolves the person leaves the felt tone of each moment in the inventory of what remains" — is a process-friendly observation made as the Map's own, and labelled as such.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

The cascade isolation is the wing's most under-remarked design decision and the one this persona should care about most. The persisting subject — the "I" that Tenet 4's indexical objection needs and that P-I1 and P-SC3 register — is held "on grounds drawn from agency and from Tenet 4's indexical objection, not from value" (L93), and the value page "deliberately does not draw on those commitments".
