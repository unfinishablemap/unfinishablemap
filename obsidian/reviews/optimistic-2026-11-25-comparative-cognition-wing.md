## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
TM separates access-level from phenomenal-level dependence and then says which one the evidence reaches. L75: each consideration "establishes an *access*-level dependence… which physicalism grants." L129 then concedes that P2 "currently stands closer to a metaphysical commitment than to a tested empirical claim." That is the hard problem respected from the dualist side: the gap is not papered over with comparative data.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
CC L120 keeps the microtubule site as "*one* candidate interface site", calls the decoherence dispute "live rather than settled" with both sides cited, and extends the architecture-independence prediction to "any quantum-interface mechanism that survives the decoherence challenge, including those that do not rely on sustained coherence at all." BC L198 allows that "classical attention-based selection could produce similar effects." Neither page lets the comparative record do physics.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
JH's tip-of-the-tongue section is the wing's best first-person passage: "We experience the *shape* of the missing information even though we cannot access its content" (L103). Its 11-12 repair then refuses the easy inference: tip-of-the-tongue "is defined by verbal report of partial features; a species without language cannot supply that datum, so its absence is a limit on what can be reported, not evidence about what is represented" (L107).

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
JH L153 reads the transition as the move "from occasions that prehend the world to occasions that also prehend their own prehending", and TM L115 "notes the perspective without leaning on it." No tier is raised on process grounds anywhere in the wing: the JH passage says apes "may have" low-grade experience and assigns no evidential label.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
JH L119 gives the cleanest statement of what declarative metacognition adds to agency: an agent "who can ask 'should I?' rather than merely 'can I?'". The claim is conditional ("If metarepresentation requires consciousness") and is kept conditional.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

## Calibration Findings (verified before listing)

**F1. CS L81 reports capuchins backwards.** "Rhesus macaques, capuchins, and dolphins given a perceptual discrimination with an opt-out third response choose the opt-out more on harder trials than on easier ones." The page cites Smith, Couchman & Beran 2014 for this. Read in full text (PMC3929533, 9,768 words, 28 hits for "capuchin"): "Strikingly, capuchin monkeys essentially did not use the UR"; after the error penalty was raised to 90 s, "five of six monkeys did not do so"; the paper calls this "the capuchin-macaque dissociation" and uses it as a main argument against associative accounts, adding that "Le Pelley (2012) also did not apply his associative-learning model to the capuchin monkeys' data patterns." Beran et al. 2009 (PubMed abstract): "capuchins essentially did not use the uncertainty response, but they did use the middle response." The same paper speaks of testing "a dolphin", singular. The Map's own research note had it right: `research/cross-species-channel-…-2026-05-15` L49 says "capuchin status disputed." The five review files for CS (four deep reviews, one pessimistic) have 0 hits for "capuchin"; the species list traces to the suggestion that seeded the article (`reviews/optimistic-2026-05-16` L125: "rhesus monkeys, capuchins, dolphins"). The repair helps the page: the dissociation is the datum Le Pelley's models were not applied to, and it belongs beside the Le Pelley sentence on the same line.

**F2. BC keeps three ape-absence claims its neighbours and its own references contradict.**
- L134: "Great apes show consolation (emotional contagion plus approach) without the understanding-motivated helping that characterises human empathy." JH L99 was repaired on 11-12: consolation is read by Romero, Castellanos and de Waal (2010) "as a possible expression of sympathetic concern, so the third level is not simply absent." Romero abstract (PubMed): consolation patterns suggest "that actors are sensitive to the contact need of victims of aggression." BC L69 itself lists "consolation behaviours, apparent empathy."
- L126: "Evidence for great ape foresight exists but remains limited to immediate futures." BC's own reference, Mulcahy & Call 2006 (PubMed abstract): tools saved "to use them 1 hour later", then "a 14-hour delay", and "seeing the apparatus during tool selection was not necessary."
- L128: "great apes show similar limitations—sophisticated reactive cognition without self-initiated goal pursuit." Unique to BC (1 obsidian, 1 hugo, 0 archive). Applying the page's own definition at L128 ("generating goal-directed behaviour from internal states rather than external triggers"), Mulcahy & Call's apes selecting and carrying tools for an absent apparatus meet it, and BC L70 grants "basic planning."
- Also L82: "Tomasello (2010) characterises ape innovation as occurring within the 'zone of latent solutions'." The coinage is Tennie, Call & Tomasello 2009, already reference 17 on the page; the 07-10 deep review noted this and left it. JH L113 cites it correctly.
- The 07-10 stability notes say *do not re-flag* "cumulative-culture-requires-metarepresentation" and the three-function mapping. Neither is flagged here. The three sentences above are empirical claims about apes, not the framework choice.

**F3. JH L64 states a stasis claim the hypothesis's own paper qualifies.** "Chimpanzees today use essentially the same termite-fishing techniques observed by Jane Goodall in the 1960s." Uncited; "Goodall" has 0 hits in Gruber et al. 2015 full text (PMC4319388, 9,688 words). The paper says wild chimpanzees "show evidence for basic cumulative phenomena (Matsuzawa, 1991; Sanz and Morgan, 2007; Boesch et al., 2009)… In sum, more work is needed to confirm the cumulativeness of animal behavior." The 11-12 deep review recorded this under Remaining Items ("no headroom was spent on it") and no task was minted ("Goodall": 0 hits in `todo.md`). JH has 0 hits for Gunasekaram.

**F4. The 2005 abstract (PubMed 16087417) describes cache protection and pilfering against competitors who had or had not watched caching; no deception of dominants, no allies. The misleading of a dominant is Bugnyar & Kotrschal 2004, *Animal Cognition* 7(2): 69–76 (PubMed 15069605): "a subordinate male… moving to unrewarded clusters… This diversion often led the dominant to approach those unrewarded clusters." "Kotrschal" has 0 hits in live, research and archive. An open P3 task already names this locus (`todo.md` ~L3270). Do not mint a second.
