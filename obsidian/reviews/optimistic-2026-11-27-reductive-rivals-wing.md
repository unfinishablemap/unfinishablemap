## Executive Summary

These four pages are among the best-calibrated in `concepts/`. Each states a rival or an analogy at full strength, then says exactly how far the Map's reply reaches:

- vitalism: "blocks a bad *argument*; it does not by itself establish dualism";
- type-identity: "merely *compatible*";
- biological naturalism: "an unpaid debt";
- type-specificity: "realistic possibility, contested".

The main opportunity is structural. The wing is two pairs that never cite each other: (vitalism, type-specificity) and (type-identity theory, biological naturalism). That holds in all eight cross-pair directions, measured on bodies only. Yet all four pages make one move. A reductive analogy's base cases are fermentation, heredity, heat, water, liquidity, solidity and digestion. In each, the explanandum was a functional role, or the felt quality had been relocated to the observer. Consciousness is the case where neither manoeuvre is available.

Two substantive folds are owed:

1. Vitalism's Relation section never meets the strongest contemporary use of vitalism's defeat. That is Papineau's physiological argument for causal closure, which the Map already sets out elsewhere.
2. Type-identity theory never names Lewis's derivation of the identity from causal roles. The Map's own `concepts/reductionism` presents that derivation as exactly the answer to "asserted, not explained".

## The Wing's Unifying Insight (not yet stated anywhere)

The four pages contain one argument at three grains:

| Page | The rival's analogy | Why it transfers there | Why it fails for consciousness |
|---|---|---|---|
| vitalism | life, fermentation, heredity | the explanandum was a schematic functional role | phenomenal types are determinate (L77) |
| type-identity | heat = molecular motion, water = H2O (L31) | the felt quality was relocated to the *sensation* of heat (L73) | pain has no appearance distinct from itself (L57) |
| biological naturalism | liquidity, solidity, digestion, photosynthesis (L74) | the causal role "just *is* the molecular behavior redescribed" (L96) | no third-person redescription of the first-person feature (L96) |
| type-specificity | (meta-level) | a generic mechanism discharges a schematic demand | a type-specific demand needs a type-by-type delivery |

Searle's examples at `biological-naturalism` L74 are "solidity, digestion, photosynthesis, earthquakes" (1 hit). Digestion and photosynthesis are life-functions of exactly the kind whose mechanisms retired the vital force. Searle runs the vitalism analogy forward: consciousness is biological, like digestion. The vitalism disanalogy is therefore an answer to him too, and no page says so. `biological-naturalism` has 0 hits for "vitalis", and vitalism has 0 hits for "Searle". The cheapest single repair is Priority 3.
