## Executive Summary

This is the strongest wing this reviewer has read for **evidential-status discipline**: six of the eight
articles explicitly separate defeater-removal from evidence, and `valence-as-a-natural-kind` runs the
Occam's-Razor-Has-Limits tenet *against the Map's own preferred reading* — the rarest and most creditable
move on the register. The wing's structural achievement is that three articles independently converge on
the same distinction (gradation of *contents* versus the on/off fact of *presence*) without any of them
citing the others as authority for it. Two genuine uncovered subjects fall out, both flagged by multiple
articles that defer to a page which does not exist: **structuralism about phenomenal character**, and
**the condition under which the mind–brain coupling engages at all**. Expansion in place is largely
unaffordable here — the pivotal article has 61 words to its hard ceiling — so both are framed as
candidate new articles, with the hosts considered named.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would be most pleased by the wing's refusal to let the hard problem be dissolved by
reclassification. `galilean-exclusion` gives the cleanest statement of why the problem is structurally
inevitable rather than merely difficult: "He *decided* to exclude them from physics and found that physics
worked magnificently without them. This tells us that mathematical physics can describe physical structure
while ignoring consciousness. It does not tell us that consciousness is nothing beyond physical structure."
And the sieve analogy earns its keep — "A sieve that filters out sand cannot then be used to study sand —
not because sieves are poorly made, but because filtering out sand is what the sieve *does*."

He would be still more pleased by `ontic-structural-realism`'s closing calibration note, which cites him
*against the Map*: "The Map should not be more confident against a position than the philosopher whose
argument it borrows." Chalmers is on record as generous to structuralist views of physics, and the article
found the quotation, reproduced the hedge, and declined to over-claim on his behalf — including the
scruple that the parenthesis naming Ladyman and Ross is Alter and Pereboom's gloss and not in the lecture.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp's interest is in whether the mental has a determinate place to act, and `phenomenal-sorites-problem`
does the metaphysical groundwork he would want. Its **Fact-Maker Problem** section is the best piece of
argument in the wing: it notices that Williamson *grounds* ordinary sharp boundaries in facts about
speakers' total use of a word, that a phenomenal on/off boundary has no such use-facts to fix it, and that
epistemicism about consciousness therefore "owes a **truth-maker** that Williamson's version got for free
from use." It then lays out three candidate truth-makers, declines Sorensen's truthmaker-gap route with a
reason of its own ("an *ungrounded* phenomenal fact is doubly mysterious, not less") in addition to Jago's,
and takes the third. That is a debt located, priced, and paid rather than waved at.

Stapp would also credit `degrees-of-consciousness` for making gradation a *mechanism* claim with a
prediction: the interface "can be opened or narrowed rather than a generator that can be turned up or
down," and the psychedelic and deep-meditation dissociations — reduced neural organisation with intensified
phenomenal experience — are named as what discriminates the two models.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Nagel is served by the wing's insistence that the first-person datum is not negotiable by third-person
reclassification. `phenomenal-sorites-problem` handles the sharpest threat to that — an unknowable
phenomenal fact sitting badly with self-intimation — by locating the unknowability precisely: it is
"**third-personal**: it concerns whether some system far down the series has any experience at all, not
whether a subject is opaque to its own present experience." The article then declines to bank the reply
in full ("which defuses much of the 'odd unknowable phenomenal fact' worry, though not all of it").

`intrinsic-nature-void` gives Nagel his other favourite point in its strongest form: introspection
"accesses experiences but never the medium of experiencing." And the apophatic-silhouette method — "We
know it must support unity, temporality, intentionality, qualitative character, and causal power" — is a
constructive use of negation rather than a shrug.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead is directly cited and correctly used. `galilean-exclusion` names the bifurcation of nature from
*The Concept of Nature* (1920) and *Science and the Modern World* (1925) and gets the diagnosis right:
"for Whitehead, this bifurcation was not a discovery about nature but an artefact of the Galilean method."
He would also approve `intrinsic-nature`'s refusal of crude substance dualism in favour of bi-aspectual
placement — structure and actuality as two irreducible aspects, with consciousness at the categorical base.

Per this persona's constraint, none of that is permitted to cash out as an evidential-tier upgrade, and
the wing does not attempt one. `degrees-of-consciousness` is where the structural opportunity existed:
it discusses *C. elegans* at 302 neurons and holds a "no lower bound" position under which the question
"at what degree does consciousness begin?" is malformed. It would have been easy to slide a boundary case
up the scale on that load. It does not — the *C. elegans* sentence stays strictly conditional ("If
*C. elegans* with 302 neurons has any phenomenal experience…"), no evidential tier is assigned to any
organism anywhere in the article, and the New York Declaration is reported at the strength its authors
gave it. See the Hardline Empiricist below: on this article the two personas converge.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane's stake here is indirect but real: libertarian agency needs a determinate agent to be the source, and
`phenomenal-sorites-problem` is the article that defends the existence of that determinate subject. Its
framing of the Tenet-4 stake is exactly the discipline Kane's own programme needs and is often accused of
lacking — the motivation is declared *as motivation*: "The Map's stake is worth stating as motivation
rather than as an established result. … The Map is therefore motivated to resist it — but motivation does
not settle metaphysics, and the case for vagueness is live and deserves a fair hearing first."

`valence-as-a-natural-kind` supplies the other half of the agency picture and does it without flinching:
if Scherer's two-level appraisal picture is right, "a currency manufactured at the point of selection
rather than consulted there is the value-blind story in a felt vocabulary." Kane would recognise that as a
real threat honestly stated rather than a rival softened for convenience.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

McGinn is cited in `intrinsic-nature-void` in his own words and used well — cognitive closure as the
possibility that the missing concept is not missing but *unformable*, with the rat-and-calculus analogy.
The wing's genuine mysterian achievement, though, is `voids/intrinsic-nature-void`'s companion move: the
[[conceptual-scheme-void]] distinction between unknown *content* behind known structure and a missing
*shape* any concept of that content would have to take. That is a sharper taxonomy of closure than McGinn's
own, and the compounding claim ("even a successful Russellian reduction would deliver an answer whose
*shape* our minds cannot hold") is a genuine contribution rather than a restatement.

McGinn would also credit `phenomenal-sorites-problem` for accepting the cost of its own preferred view
in plain terms: "Epistemicism is notoriously counterintuitive: it posits a precise fact that no one could
ever know." And for leaving one problem genuinely open rather than closing it rhetorically — see the
Content Strengths entry below.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

**This persona's verdict on this wing is strongly positive, and it is the review's headline finding.**

Six of the eight articles separate defeater-removal from evidence explicitly, and two do it with the named
pattern:

- `intrinsic-nature` §"A discipline note on what the distinction earns": "The intrinsic-nature gap is a
  point about the *limits of physical description*, not positive evidence that consciousness occupies the
  gap. … The gap creates room; it does not furnish the room." That last sentence is the cleanest one-line
  statement of the discipline anywhere in the corpus.
- `ontic-structural-realism` §what-is-lost applies it *to deflate the article's own opening framing*:
  "the remainder argument removes a defeater rather than establishing dualism, so losing it costs a
  defeater-removal rather than a premise — a real cost, and **smaller than the opening framing suggests**."
  An article that measures its own lead and finds it overstated is doing the persona's work unprompted.
- `is-conscious-being-a-natural-kind` states the tenet-coherent-not-evidence-elevating pattern outright:
  "The Map's line is conditional: *if* the Map's dualism, *then* a sharp joint follows. The sharp boundary
  is entailed by the tenets, not offered as independent evidence for them." And it concedes the rival's
  standing rather than caricaturing it: "that is a respectable live position, not a straw man."
- `phenomenal-sorites-problem`: "being drawn to the reading that protects Tenet 4 is a reason to examine
  the epistemic theory hard, not a demonstration that Williamson defeats his rivals; the Map does not
  claim to have refuted ontic vagueness."

**The single most creditable passage in the wing** is `valence-as-a-natural-kind`'s Occam's-Razor section,
because it runs the tenet in the direction that costs the Map something:

> It blocks converting "no neural module" into "no kind," "one measurement scale" into "one felt quantity,"
> and an absent neural bipolarity into an absent felt scalar. **It equally blocks reading a supramodal
> orbitofrontal code as demonstrating that felt valence is one kind**: Chikazoe's result is projectability
> evidence, compatible with the Map's reading rather than confirming it.

Chikazoe 2014 is the article's best supporting result, and the article declines to bank it. It also
declines the verdict its own title invites — "The title question itself stays open: the projectability
evidence is real, and capped below as compatible with the Map's reading rather than confirming it" — and
prices the cluster verdict exactly: "A cluster kind is enough to give the identity claim a relatum and not
enough to give selection a commensurating scalar."

On **minimal-organism restraint** specifically, `degrees-of-consciousness` passes. No tier is asserted for
*C. elegans*, *Hydra*, or any invertebrate; the strongest claim is the New York Declaration's own, reported
as the Declaration's; and the interesting metric — the lower bound — is declared *structurally
inaccessible* rather than estimated ("any attempt to introspect on minimal consciousness enriches it
beyond minimality"). The Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist therefore converge on this
article, which by the skill's own criterion means the tension has been resolved honestly.

One thing this persona would *ask for* rather than flag: the wing nowhere uses the
[[framework-stage-calibration]] vocabulary, though `ontic-structural-realism`'s "the Map's preference is
argued but not secured: both claims doing the work … face standing replies" is that calibration in
substance. This is an observation, not a defect.

- **Strongest point**: the exactness about how far the Map's siding with the quidditist goes. The
  relations-without-relata objection is called out as "tempting but not decisive," because moderate OSR

  against a live rival rather than the rejection of an incoherence."
- **Also notable**: the divergence from Russellian monism is given a *reason from the tenets* rather than

- **Why it works**: Hempel's dilemma is not merely reported but explained — the intrinsic-nature gap is

### `obsidian/voids/intrinsic-nature-void.md`

- **Strongest point**: the two-directions-one-void thesis, and the reason it is structural rather than
  contingent — "science advances by discovering relational descriptions, and introspection accesses
  experiences but never the medium of experiencing. Neither method can, in principle, reveal what underlies
  its domain."
- **Also notable**: the substrate problem given as a *pattern of failures* with three worked instances —
  Descartes' negations, Leibniz's monads specified by what they do, and Chalmers's protophenomenal
  properties "specified only by reference to the building they compose." Then: "The substrate problem may
  be the price of dualism."
- **Why it works**: this is a void article that generously credits the rival it declines. "The Unfinishable
  Map need not endorse [[panpsychism]]. But the intrinsic nature argument reveals its intellectual
  seriousness as a principled response to a genuine problem."

### `obsidian/concepts/degrees-of-consciousness.md`

- **Strongest point**: the article corrects its own title term. Bayne, Hohwy and Owen (2016) argue that
  *levels* talk is theoretically untenable, and rather than burying that, the article concedes it and
  re-scopes: "the present article uses 'degrees' as shorthand for graded *and multidimensional* variation,
  not for a single quantity," and demotes its own governing image — "even though the dimmer metaphor
  remains a useful first approximation."
- **Why it works**: the concession is then made to *pay*. The multidimensional picture fits an interface
  reconfigurable along several axes better than a one-dimensional dimmer does, so the correction
  strengthens the Map's reading instead of merely apologising for the vocabulary.
- **Also notable**: the empirical apparatus is genuinely load-bearing rather than decorative. PCI's graded
  readout under propofol instantiates gradation at the empirical level, and ketamine — PCI at waking levels
  while environmentally disconnected — is used to show that integration measures and access channels
  dissociate. Distinct agents produce distinct gradation profiles, which is what makes the pharmacology
  interface-mapping rather than a list.

### `obsidian/concepts/galilean-exclusion.md`

- **Strongest point**: the circularity diagnosis. "Physics never needed subjective qualities because it was
  *designed* not to need them. The absence of consciousness from physical description reflects a
  methodological choice, not an empirical finding."
- **Why it works**: it is then turned on the Map's own side of the room. "This point cuts against both
  extreme positions. … It tells the mysterian that the difficulty of explaining consciousness scientifically
  may reflect the method's structure rather than any intrinsic ineffability of the phenomenon." An argument
  that only ever embarrasses the opposition is a slogan; this one costs the mysterian something too.
- **Also notable**: the article pre-empts the obvious objection (modern cognitive science has moved past
  Galileo) and answers it structurally — subjective reports enter "as third-person data *about* experience
  — verbal behaviour, button presses, ratings on scales — not as experience itself."
- **New as of 2026-09-05**: the §"A third position" paragraph is the wing's best-executed piece of
  cross-cluster wiring. It concedes that the circularity charge "does not reach a position that denies the
  remainder was ever a datum," notes SEP's *relocation* reading of Galileo (which complicates the
  article's own "decided, not discovered" gloss and is admitted to do so), and lands the contrast:
  "Galileo relocates the residue into the sentient body; ontic structural realism abolishes it."

## The Wing's Structural Achievement (independent convergence)

Three articles arrive independently at the same distinction — gradation of the *contents* of experience
versus the on/off fact of *presence* — and none of them leans on the others as authority:

- `degrees-of-consciousness` (lead): "what is graded throughout is what a subject *has*, while whether a
  subject is present at all is a further question, left open here."
- `phenomenal-sorites-problem` (§ distinguishing three neighbours): "[[degrees-of-consciousness]] concerns
  *gradation* … which already presupposes that there is experience; ontic vagueness targets the prior
  on/off fact, not the dimmer setting."
- `is-conscious-being-a-natural-kind` (§ the axis): "One can hold, without contradiction, that the contents
  of consciousness come in continuous degrees … while the on/off fact of any experience whatsoever is
  perfectly sharp. … Conflating them makes the debate look already settled when it is not."

This is worth naming because it is what lets the wing hold two apparently opposed theses at once —
consciousness is graded (degrees) *and* consciousness is sharp (sorites, natural-kind) — without
contradiction. `valence-as-a-natural-kind` then extends the same move to a fourth site, distinguishing
sharpness about *whether there is any experience* from cluster-structure *within* the evaluative
dimension, and correctly flags that the distinction "the Map draws itself, not one Boyd or the sharpness
literature supplies." Four articles, one distinction, no circular citation. That is a corpus behaving
like a framework.

#### 2. The coupling-engagement condition — what fixes whether the coupling engages at all

`phenomenal-sorites-problem` closes with the Map's own residual problem: "if the coupling's engagement is
itself triggered by a graded physical variable, the sorites re-enters at the trigger's switch-on point."
As of today that sentence carries more weight than when it was written, because `is-conscious-being-a-
natural-kind` was re-grounded onto the same truth-maker — the on/off fact is now fixed in *two* articles by
whether the coupling "engages at all," described as "a simple, non-graded relation that either holds or does
not." That construct is load-bearing for Tenet 4 in both, and **the corpus has no article about it.**

Grep-verified this run: the only live pages that discuss the coupling's *engagement condition* (as opposed
to its *mode* or its *efficacy*) are `concepts/universal-coupling-response`, `concepts/panpsychism`,
`concepts/degrees-of-consciousness` and `phenomenal-sorites-problem` itself. The two obvious hosts are
about something else: `concepts/coupling-modes` is about *how* influence works (basis / timing /
probability control — three matches for "engag" or "adequa" in the whole file), and `concepts/interface-
threshold` is, by both wing articles' own account, an *efficacy* boundary with phenomenal consciousness
present on both sides (one match).

**And the subject is live rather than merely absent.** `concepts/universal-coupling-response` states the
Map's condition for coupling — architectural adequacy — and then says of it:

> "The boundary between 'adequate' and 'inadequate' architecture need not be sharp. … The boundary is vague
> in the way biological boundaries typically are — there is no precise moment when a developing embryo's
> nervous system becomes adequate … The vagueness is in the boundary, not in the coupling."

Neither article cites the other in either direction (grep-verified both ways, both trees, 0 hits). This
review does **not** conclude that the two are inconsistent — the final clause is an attempted distinction
and may well hold, and adjudicating it is a pessimistic-review or deep-review job on a file outside this
wing. What is certain is that the reconciliation has never been written down, and that a construct now
bearing the Tenet-4 defence in two articles deserves a page of its own rather than a clause in each.
