## Executive Summary

The new material is, on the whole, unusually well calibrated — the spoke's refusal to recruit Fanon, Young, Ahmed and Guenther as allies is explicit, repeated, and honoured almost everywhere. But the single load-bearing new claim installed across both articles today — *the anchor's catalogue breaks the Leib/Körper coupling only by lesion; feminist phenomenology supplies the non-lesion case it lacks* — **is false as stated**. The anchor's §Clinical Exhibits contains four bolded exhibits, and the fourth (somatic-awareness training) is explicitly non-lesion, explicitly reversible, and described by the anchor as "the clearest everyday evidence" on that terrain. The spoke enumerates the other three, never mentions the fourth, and asserts an exhaustiveness the anchor's own text refutes three paragraphs above the Further Reading line that repeats the claim. Separately, the paragraph that carries the contrast is unstable in **both** calibration directions at once: it over-claims at L96 (asserting a difference the anatomy does not record — which no physicalist grants) and over-concedes at L80 (asserting the redescription "adds no evidence for or against any theory of mind" one clause before conceding a rival handles it "more readily"). The Guenther citation work is sound where it could be checked and unverified where it could not.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

*Leib* and *Körper* are two nouns for one organism differently attended to, and the article knows it — the anchor says so at L50 ("not two bodies but two modes in which one body is given"). So what is the vocabulary buying? The spoke's new contribution is to observe that social history changes how a body moves and feels. Motor learning already says that, in a vocabulary that predicts and measures. Calling a socialised motor programme a "*Leib*/*Körper* split" adds a nineteenth-century German noun to a twenty-first-century finding. The spoke's own L80 concedes exactly this — "restates a phenomenological finding in a second phenomenological vocabulary" — which is an admirably honest sentence and also, from here, the end of the matter.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

Young's "simultaneous *I can* and *I cannot*" is a report about a report. The spoke treats it as a description of a bodily structure; it is a verbal output of a system that has been trained to produce such outputs. And note the direction the evidence runs in the spoke's *own* account: the socially-trained self-model is exactly what a predictive-processing story predicts, as L80 grants. When your best new exhibit is one the rival framework expects *more readily* than yours, you have found a datum for them, not a constraint on them. The article should say so plainly rather than say "no evidence for or against."

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

The spoke's L78 asserts that Young's thrower, Ahmed's disoriented body and Fanon's passenger "have intact afferents, intact cortical body maps, and an intact motor apparatus." Nothing in the cited sources measures any of that. Worse, the claim is contrary to the standard neuroscience of habituated movement: a comportment sedimented over fifteen years *is* a difference in cortical maps and motor programmes — that is what sedimentation is physically. Either "intact" means "unlesioned," in which case say so and the contrast shrinks, or it means "physically identical," in which case it is asserted without measurement and against the literature.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

Little purchase here; neither article touches branching, indexicality, or measurement. The honest report is that this pair is out of the MWI critic's range, and padding a critique to fill the heading would be worse than saying so.

### The Empiricist (Popper's Ghost)

L96 is the sentence to put to the test: "the same anatomy supports different felt bodies depending on a history the anatomy does not record." What observation would distinguish a history the anatomy does not record from a history the anatomy records in a form we have not yet measured? If none, this is not a constraint on theories of embodiment; it is a stipulation dressed as one — and it is precisely the sentence the article uses as the Map-facing payoff of the whole new section. Note also that the article had already fenced itself against this at L92 ("a feminist phenomenologist could read every description in this article and remain a thoroughgoing physicalist"). L96 is a description in this article that a thoroughgoing physicalist would not grant.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

The spoke does better here than most of the corpus: Fanon's schema, Young's comportment and Ahmed's orientation are all accounts of a bodily self *constituted* by what has been done to it, which is close to emptiness of own-being and is treated as such. The pressure point is the anchor, not the spoke. The anchor's Dualism reading (L76) wants the lived body to be a remainder physical description leaves out — a something that survives the analysis. Fanon's chapter is a report of the self being *dissolved* by another's regard, and the spoke imports it into an article whose anchor is arguing for an irreducible residue. That the spoke declines to draw the inference is to its credit; that the two articles now sit on one edge pulling in opposite directions is worth an author's attention.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The "every exhibit is a lesion" enumeration is not exhaustive — the anchor's fourth exhibit refutes it

- **Files**: `obsidian/concepts/feminist-phenomenology-and-embodied-consciousness.md` (L38, L76, L78, L103); `obsidian/concepts/lived-objectified-body-distinction.md` (L101)
- **Severity**: **High** — this is the load-bearing new claim, it is installed at six string loci across both articles, and it is false as written.

**The claim.** Spoke L76: the anchor "catalogues cases where the two registers come apart: Christina's deafferentation, Sacks's post-surgical leg, the phantom limb, the severed motor channel of locked-in syndrome. Each of those exhibits breaks the coupling by **lesion** — a measurable physical injury at an identifiable site." Spoke L78: "Feminist and critical phenomenology supply the case that catalogue lacks: the coupling bent by **situation**, with no lesion anywhere, and reversibly … a change of situation can change it back, **which is precisely what no lesion case affords**."

**The problem.** The anchor's §Clinical Exhibits contains **four** bolded exhibits, not three-plus-locked-in. Measured (`grep -n '^\*\*'` across the section, lines 60–73):

1. Proprioception loss — Christina *(lesion)*
2. The limb that drops out — Sacks's own leg *(lesion)*
3. Phantom limb *(lesion)*
4. **Somatic-awareness training — the lived body's plasticity** *(**no lesion**)*

…plus locked-in syndrome as "a possible fifth contrast" *(lesion)*.

Exhibit 4 (anchor L70) reads: "The *Leib* is not fixed. Contemplative practice, dance and athletic training, biofeedback, and rehabilitation all reshape the felt body: regions that were dim become vivid, coordinations that were effortful become pre-reflective." There is no lesion anywhere in it, and it is reversible and re-trainable by construction. The anchor calls it "the clearest everyday evidence that the lived body is something consciousness modulates rather than merely registers," and leans on it again at L78 to carry the **Bidirectional Interaction** tenet. It is not a marginal entry; it is the exhibit the anchor's tenet section depends on.

The spoke never mentions it. Measured: `grep -ciF` on the spoke for `somatic-awareness`, `biofeedback`, `contemplative practice`, `plasticity`, `training` returns **0**.

So the spoke's enumeration selects the three lesion exhibits, omits the one non-lesion exhibit, and then generalises over the selection. "Each of those exhibits breaks the coupling by lesion" is true of the three it names and false of the catalogue it claims to be describing. "The case that catalogue lacks" names a case the catalogue has had since May.

**And the anchor now repeats it.** The reciprocal Further Reading line added today (anchor L101) reads: "The coupling bent by *situation* rather than by lesion: no injury anywhere, and reversible." That line sits roughly thirty lines below the anchor's own non-lesion exhibit. An outbound crosslink sentence of this kind is never reviewed by anyone in the normal course, which is how it will survive unless it is fixed now.

**Recommendation — repair, do not delete.** The contrast is worth keeping; it is just drawn at the wrong joint. The anchor's non-lesion case is **self-directed**: training, practice, rehabilitation, *chosen*, and the anchor explicitly frames it as "consciousness modulates." The spoke's cases are **heteronomous**: imposed by others' regard, by a socialisation nobody elected, by deprivation. That is a genuinely new cell in the table and a better one than the lesion/no-lesion cut, because it adds a third route rather than a second:

| Route | Exhibit |
|---|---|
| Lesion | Christina, Sacks's leg, phantom limb, locked-in |
| No lesion, self-directed | Somatic-awareness training |
| **No lesion, imposed** | **Young, Fanon, Ahmed, Guenther** |

The repair costs one clause in the spoke's L76 enumeration (add the fourth exhibit and say what distinguishes it), a re-pointed sentence at L78, a word at L38, and a re-worded Further Reading line on each side. Headroom is ample on both files.

### Issue 2: The paragraph carrying the new contrast is miscalibrated in *both* directions, two sentences apart

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/feminist-phenomenology-and-embodied-consciousness.md`, L80 and L96
- **Severity**: **High** (epistemic/metaphysical equivocation; the article's own calibration promise is at stake)

The spoke makes an unusually explicit calibration promise at L92: "A feminist phenomenologist could read every description in this article and remain a thoroughgoing physicalist. The Map does not claim otherwise." Two sentences in the new material fail that promise in opposite directions.

**Over-claim, L96** (inside §Relation to Site Perspective, presented as "the sharpest form of the constraint" the Map takes from the tradition):

> "the same anatomy supports different felt bodies depending on **a history the anatomy does not record**"

This is not a claim a thoroughgoing physicalist could read and remain one. On any self-model or predictive-processing account, the anatomy is *exactly* where the history is recorded — in synaptic weights, in motor programmes, in the priors of an interoceptive generative model. The sentence asserts a physically-unrecorded difference and then uses it as the Map-facing payoff of the whole new section. This is the equivocation the discipline warns about: the *epistemic* reading ("no lesion has been identified; the history is not legible in gross anatomy") is what the sources support, and the *metaphysical* reading ("the anatomy does not record it") is what the sentence states and the conclusion leans on. It is also heavily hedged elsewhere in the same section, which is precisely why the anchoring audit will not catch it.

The same equivocation appears in the empirical assertion at L78 — "intact afferents, **intact cortical body maps**, and an intact motor apparatus" — which reads as "physically unchanged" and is supported only as "unlesioned."

**Over-concession, L80**, sixteen lines earlier and in the same claim-complex:

> "Naming Young's inhibited intentionality a *Leib*/*Körper* split … **adds no evidence for or against any theory of mind**. The self-model and predictive-processing accounts … redescribe the situation-bent case as readily as they redescribe the lesion cases — **more readily, if anything**, since a socially trained self-model is exactly what those frameworks expect."

These two clauses cannot both be right. If the rival framework accommodates the new case *more readily* than the lesion cases it already accommodates, then the new case is — weakly, but not nullly — evidence *for* the rival. "No evidence for or against" is an absolute of the kind that gets ratified by later passes and is then very hard to undo. The concessive sentence is doing real work, but the absolute overshoots what the paragraph itself goes on to say.

**Recommendation.** One refine pass, two sentences. L96: downgrade to the epistemic reading the sources support — "a history no lesion records," or "a history that leaves no identifiable lesion," which keeps the contrast and drops the metaphysics. L78: replace "intact cortical body maps" with "no identified lesion at any site," or state plainly that habituation is expected to be physically realised and that the absence at issue is the absence of *injury*. L80: replace the absolute with the graded claim the next clause already implies — the redescription supplies no support for the Map's reading and, if anything, sits marginally more comfortably with its rivals.

### Issue 3: Reversibility is asserted for all three cases without support, and is strained for the one case it is asserted hardest about

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/feminist-phenomenology-and-embodied-consciousness.md`, L78 (echoed in anchor L101)
- **Severity**: **Medium-High** — reversibility is the property that makes the three cases "one kind," and it is doing the entire contrastive load against lesion.

L78: "Young's thrower, Ahmed's disoriented body and Fanon's passenger … a change of situation can change it back, which is precisely what no lesion case affords."

Three problems.

**(a) It is unsupported.** None of the three cited sources reports a reversal. Young describes an acquired comportment and does not report un-acquiring it. Ahmed's whole analytic point is that orientations are *inherited* and become "the unremarkable background of perception" — sedimentation is the phenomenon, not its undoing. Fanon's chapter records no restoration whatever.

**(b) It is strained for Fanon specifically.** *Peau noire, masques blancs* chapter five does not end with the schema restored by a change of room; the force of the text is that the epidermal schema is not something the subject can walk out of. Asserting reversibility of the racial epidermal schema in order to make it pair with a trained throwing motion is a claim about Fanon that Fanon's text does not carry. The spoke elsewhere handles Fanon with real care — the Markmann/Seuil translation note at L50 is exemplary — which makes this one unearned assertion stand out.

**(c) The three-way difference is flattened.** Fanon's case is acute, interpersonal and involuntary; Young's is chronic, developmental and habituated; Ahmed's is a background inheritance the subject never notices. Guenther's, which L96 recruits into the same constraint, is deprivation — and her book's subtitle is *and Its Afterlives*, i.e. damage that outlasts release. The article does not claim reversibility for Guenther, correctly, but it does treat all four as instances of one contrast while resting that contrast on a property only one of them plausibly has.

**Recommendation.** Either supply the support (something in the critical-phenomenology literature on the reversibility of sedimented comportment) or drop reversibility from the load-bearing contrast and let *no lesion* + *imposed rather than chosen* carry it — which, per Issue 1, is the better joint anyway. If reversibility is kept for Young alone, say it is kept for Young alone. The anchor's L101 echo must move with it.

### Issue 4: Two of the three Guenther quotations are unverified at any reachable source; the third verified exactly

- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/feminist-phenomenology-and-embodied-consciousness.md`, L70, L115
- **Severity**: **Medium** — this is not a fabrication finding, and the article's quoting is demonstrably accurate where it could be checked.

Guenther is a first corpus treatment (`grep -rlF Guenther` over `obsidian/` returned zero files before today's commit, per the originating task's own measurement). Three quotations are given, with the reference note "(Quotations from p. xii and p. 35.)"

**Verified, exactly.** The third quotation —

> "Without the concrete experience of other embodied egos oriented toward common objects in a shared world, my own experience of the boundaries of those perceptual objects begins to waver."

— matches word for word a raw independent source (Pepper, *Unshared Minds, Decaying Worlds: Towards a Pathology of Chronic Loneliness*, PMC11237889), fetched and grepped as raw text after NFKC normalisation rather than read through a summariser. That source attributes it to **(Guenther, 2013, 35)**, confirming the Map's page assignment. This is a clean verification and the reference-list entry is correct: University of Minnesota Press, 2013, ISBN 9780816679591 (confirmed at the publisher).

**Unverified.** The two quotations attributed to p. xii could not be confirmed against any raw artefact:

- "Who are we, such that we can **become** unhinged from ourselves by being separated from others?" — two independent search passes both rendered this as "who are we, such that we can **be** unhinged from ourselves by being separated from others?" A summariser normally ratifies the phrasing it is asked about, so a departure in the *opposite* direction is the meaningful signal. The publisher-of-record abstract carries a third, clearly distinct formulation ("who are we, such that we can be undone by prolonged isolation from others?"), which is a different sentence and does not settle the question. Archive.org's copy is lending-restricted and Google Books' API is quota-blocked (HTTP 429), so no raw text was reachable.
- "a precise phenomenological description of what happens when the articulated joints of our embodied, interrelational subjectivity are broken apart" — zero web hits under any search; no independent ratification at all.

The pattern is clean and worth stating precisely: the p. 35 quotation verifies perfectly, and both p. xii quotations are unverified. That is consistent with a preface simply being less quoted online, and it is *not* evidence of invention. But a verbatim quotation mark asserts word-for-word fidelity, and one word is currently in doubt.

**Recommendation.** Check both p. xii quotations against the physical book or a preview, and correct "become unhinged" if the text reads "be unhinged." If neither can be checked, convert the two p. xii quotations to reported speech (the sense is not in dispute) and leave the verified p. 35 quotation in quotation marks. Also split the reference note so each quotation carries its own page rather than "p. xii and p. 35" for three quotations.
