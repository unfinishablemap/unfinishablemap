1. **Historical/coiner error in the lead and History section.** The lead said synaesthesia was "identified by Galton's 1880 imagery survey", and History described a "survey of Royal Society fellows". Sachs's 1812 account is the first. Galton popularised the phenomenon, and his informants were not Royal Society fellows. The corpus's own [[aphantasia]] L65 already gives "nineteen of them Fellows". The section heading is now "Sachs (1812) and Galton (1880)".
2. **Wrong-paper attribution** of the consistency figures (1993 data given under the 1987 cite).
3. **Dropped qualifier**: Wager's "externalist" was missing.
4. **Cited-author stance**: Macpherson was cited for the wedge while her chapter rebuts it. A short paragraph now states her objection to Gray's first pattern and her point that some strong functionalisms escape the argument. The "cleanest exemplar" paragraph now says both Gray patterns are contested, the first by Macpherson and the second by predictive processing.
5. **Internal contradiction**: the article said synaesthetes and non-synaesthetes "share the same neural architecture" (L118) and the "same biological substrate" (Relation section), and the lead said "same biological architecture". Its own cited evidence (cross-activation, Tilot's axonogenesis variants and "atypical structural and functional neural connectivity", prior-precision differences) says otherwise. This now reads "species-typical … differing in local wiring" throughout. In the Relation section, the measured phenomenal profiles are now separated from the Map's interface reading of them, which had been presented as "measurably different phenomenal interface configurations".
6. **Empirical-claim fidelity**: Bor's "neurophysiological signatures" are not in the paper; the physiological measure was skin conductance.
