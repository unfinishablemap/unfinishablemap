## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would grant the history and attack the moral. Four centuries of failure to specify a mind-body interface is not a "research programme" — it is what a degenerating paradigm looks like from inside. Every generation, the article concedes, relocated the mystery (pineal gland → primitive notions → quantum indeterminacy); the closing paragraph even admits the parallel. The correct induction from this history is that the interface will keep receding because there is no interface: "soul," like "vapors," is folk vocabulary awaiting elimination. Bedrock disagreement — noted, not a defect.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would target the narrative arc itself: the article's structure (challenge → failed responses → eclipse → "reopening") is a persuasion device that treats the materialist century as an interlude rather than a verdict. He would note that "the arguments for irreducibility have strengthened as neuroscience advances" is asserted, not shown, in an article whose own evidence is a survey percentage. Partially absorbed as bedrock; the survey-sentence over-read is a genuine defect logged as Issue 5.

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark would accept the historical scholarship and reject the payoff section: "The Quantum Opening" names von Neumann, Wigner, Stapp, and Penrose-Hameroff without mentioning that decoherence timescales are the standing objection to the latter two, an objection the Map itself treats as decisive against pre-decoherence mechanisms (P-Q1 prefers post-decoherence selection *because* of it). The article's mechanism paragraph is more confident than the Map's own register — that is Issue 1, and Tegmark gets the credit for it.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch would note the article's frank admission that "the quantum opening depends on collapse being real" and answer: collapse is not real, so this entire four-century narrative terminates in a physics mistake. The article handles this honestly (structural dependence stated, not hidden). Bedrock disagreement.

### The Empiricist
The Popperian would press the asymmetry between the article's two halves: the historical half is dense with checkable claims (and survives checking well); the contemporary half leans on "resolution requires physics that earlier thinkers could not have anticipated" — a promissory note. He would ask what would count as the interaction problem being *closed against* the Map, and note the article defers entirely to sibling articles for falsifiers. Acceptable for a survey, but the Further Reading glosses ("why the conservation objection fails," "why it fails") assert verdicts the sibling articles themselves hedge — Issue 6.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would observe that every position in the seventeenth-century explosion — occasionalism, extended spirit, harmony, monism — reifies the two relata and then struggles with the relation. The article's trilemma ("genuine dualism, genuine creaturely causation, causal intelligibility") is only a trilemma if the substances are taken as intrinsically existent; dependent origination dissolves the question. The article's Sāṃkhya cross-reference shows it knows non-Western dissolutions exist. Bedrock disagreement.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: MQI tenet paragraph reads more confident than the positions register and than the Map's own conservation article (registry-vs-body calibration diff)
- **File**: obsidian/topics/history-of-the-interaction-problem.md
- **Location**: line 133 ("a specific, intelligible mechanism for mind-body causation … requires no transfer of energy or momentum")
- **Problem**: Two seams. (a) "A specific, intelligible mechanism" overstates against the register: [[positions/quantum-interface]] holds the mechanism preference at low-edge moderate with explicit mechanism debt — P-Q10 (no worked toy model), P-Q3 (bias-without-deviation dilemma open), and the 2026-08-13 citation grade making the causal-selection thesis citable downstream "as a framework-internal coherence result only." The mechanism-debt convention states downstream articles "should not read more confident than the register does upstream"; this paragraph does. The article's own closing concession ("naming the interface rather than fully explaining it") contradicts "specific, intelligible mechanism" — but sits at the bottom, so a truncated LLM fetch keeps only the overclaim. (b) The flat "requires no transfer of energy or momentum" states the pre-honesty version of the energy claim. The Map's own [[conservation-laws-and-mental-causation]] (line 98) now says plainly: "on any single realised trial the actual outcome may differ in energy from the counterfactual chance outcome … This per-trial difference should be stated plainly rather than dissolved by stipulation" — only the *ensemble* expectation is preserved. This is the moved-position-stales-downstream pattern: the sibling was calibrated; this article was not re-synchronised.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Refine-draft: demote "a specific, intelligible mechanism" to a candidate-locus formulation consistent with the citation grade; qualify the energy sentence to ensemble-level preservation with the per-trial residual named (one clause + the existing sibling link suffices).

### Issue 2: Cucu & Pitts recruited without disclosing their response *accepts* energy non-conservation — a rival strategy to the no-energy-transfer claim made twelve lines later
- **File**: obsidian/topics/history-of-the-interaction-problem.md
- **Location**: lines 119–121 ("The Conservation Objection Reconsidered") against line 133
- **Problem**: Verified at the raw arXiv abstract (1909.13643): the paper *examines* interactionist attempts "to make it satisfy energy conservation," shows "they all miss their goal," and endorses the conditionality response, "which sees conservation as (bi)conditional upon symmetries and **simply accepts energy non-conservation** as an aspect of interactionist dualism." The article deploys this as if of a piece with the Map's conservation-*satisfying* selection mechanism (line 133). They are alternative strategies — the sibling article states them disjunctively ("either inapplicable … or irrelevant") — and the cited authors' argument cuts against the family the Map's mechanism belongs to unless the selection framework's Born-preservation story is doing the work (which is the sibling's per-trial/ensemble treatment, not cited here). Also "demonstrates" over-claims: Noether conditionality is theorem-grade, but "the argument begs the question" is Cucu & Pitts's *argument*, contested in the replies literature.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Re-frame, don't delete: "Cucu and Pitts argue …"; add one clause noting their response concedes lawful non-conservation, whereas the Map's selection reading aims to avoid the concession at ensemble level — two distinct escape routes, developed in [[conservation-laws-and-mental-causation]].

### Issue 3: Stencil's coined taxonomy presented unattributed at point of use, and bent against his thesis
- **File**: obsidian/topics/history-of-the-interaction-problem.md
- **Location**: line 58 ("Elisabeth's challenge operated at three levels. The **causal interface problem** … The **vapors problem** … And the **principal attribute problem** …")
- **Problem**: Verified at the raw JMP full text (doi 10.32881/jomp.158): all three bolded labels are Stencil's own coinages ("I am calling this problem the causal interface problem to avoid ambiguity"; "I move away, in this paper, from naming any of the problems the 'problem of interaction'"). The article presents the taxonomy as its own analytic frame; the Stencil citation appears only a paragraph later, attached to a different claim (which *is* supported — his real-distinction reading matches line 64). Worse, the framing inverts his thesis: Stencil argues these are three objections to Descartes' account of *the nature of the soul*, of which the causal-interface worry is only one, expressly against reading the interaction problem as the core — the article recasts them as three "levels" of "Elisabeth's challenge," i.e., the interaction-centric reading his labels were coined to resist.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Move "(Stencil 2023)" to point of use and add a clause registering his framing ("three problems about the soul's nature, only the first of which is the interaction worry proper"). The inversion is worth keeping visible: it strengthens, not weakens, the article's Elisabeth-as-philosopher reading.

### Issue 4: Third Elisabeth quote is not from the cited edition
- **File**: obsidian/topics/history-of-the-interaction-problem.md
- **Location**: line 64 ("easier for me to concede matter and extension to the soul, than the capacity of moving a body and of being moved, to an immaterial being")
- **Problem**: The References cite exactly one translation of the correspondence (Shapiro 2007). Quotes 1 and 2 are Shapiro-verbatim (confirmed by grep against Stencil's direct quotations of S 62 and the 1 July letter). Quote 3 is not: Shapiro's 10 June 1643 rendering reads "it would be easier for me to concede matter and extension to the soul than to concede the capacity to move a body and to be moved by it to an **immaterial thing**" (Stencil, passage [3]). The article's variant ("of moving … of being moved, to an immaterial being") is an unidentified older translation; it greps 0 against the cited edition. The 2026-07-11 pass certified it "substance faithful," which is true, but substance-fidelity is not source-traceability: a reader verifying against the only cited translation finds a mismatch. Per the Map's own discipline a quote must be grep-verifiable in its cited source.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Conform the quote to Shapiro's wording (drop-in replacement, no meaning change), or cite the translation actually used.

### Issue 5: "Dualism is no longer marginal" over-reads a non-physicalism statistic
- **File**: obsidian/topics/history-of-the-interaction-problem.md
- **Location**: line 125
- **Problem**: The evidence sentence is accurate (2020 PhilPapers: 51.9% physicalism / 32.1% non-physicalism; re-confirmed 2026-07-11). The concluding sentence converts a *non-physicalism* figure into a *dualism* claim. Non-physicalism about mind is a strict superset of dualism — it includes panpsychist, Russellian-monist, idealist, and neutral-monist respondents, several of which this very article classes among the *rivals* of dualism in its seventeenth-century taxonomy. The survey's dualism-specific figure (the Consciousness question) is substantially lower than 32%. This is an over-claim running in the Map's favour, sitting as the section's final, quotable line.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Either "Non-physicalism is no longer marginal" or add the dualism-specific consciousness-question figure alongside the mind-question figure.

### Issue 6: Internal contradiction on More; Further Reading gloss over-claims its target's conclusion
- **File**: obsidian/topics/history-of-the-interaction-problem.md
- **Location**: line 94 against line 78; Further Reading line for [[conservation-laws-and-mental-causation]]
- **Problem**: (a) Line 78 correctly presents More's spirits as *incorporeal* but extended (extension ≠ materiality is the whole point of his position, marked by penetrability and indiscerpibility). Line 94 then says More "sacrificed the immateriality of spirit" — conflating immateriality with unextendedness, the exact conflation More rejected, and contradicting line 78. (b) The Further Reading gloss "Why the conservation objection fails" asserts a verdict the target article does not reach: its own conclusion is that "conservation arguments cannot resolve the debate — though the dualist position equally presupposes its own conclusion when invoking external influence." Navigation surfaces carry claims no reviewer reads; this one is stronger than the article it points to.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Line 94: "sacrificed the unextendedness of spirit." Gloss: "Why the conservation objection is not decisive" (mirrors the target's own verdict).
