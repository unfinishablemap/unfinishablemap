## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Patricia Churchland)

"Your 'veto' paragraph lists 'Parents sacrifice themselves rather than abandon children' as consciousness 'exercising its causal power against the full weight of biological imperatives' (L77). Parental self-sacrifice is the textbook prediction of kin selection (Hamilton 1964, *J. Theor. Biol.* 7:1–16, verified at Crossref). In that example the biological imperative points *toward* the sacrifice. Loyalty to comrades has its own evolutionary and social-learning accounts. As for 'tunnel vision … not merely a perceptual phenomenon but a volitional one' (L55): stress-induced loss of prefrontal regulation is a mapped catecholamine effect (Arnsten 2009, *Nat. Rev. Neurosci.* 10:410–422, verified at Crossref). You cite the resilience reviews in L81 and then write as if biology had run out. It hasn't. It has a residual, as every biology does."

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Daniel Dennett)

"'Enduring torture does not feel random' (L107). Nothing feels random from the inside, deterministic processes included. That is a fact about self-models, not about the metaphysics of the outcome. 'What the physicalist cannot model, without eliminating it, is the selector' assumes the very selector that is in dispute. And 'Why Not a Physicalist Account?' says that if physical factors fix the outcome, 'the agent's felt experience … would be causally inert' (L105). No physicalist I know holds that. On an identity view the agonising effort *is* the neural process, and it is as causal as anything in the skull. You have argued against epiphenomenalism and called it physicalism."

### The Quantum Skeptic (Max Tegmark)

"'Under duress, the brain's stress response narrows which quantum states remain indeterminate and available for conscious selection' (L113). Cortisol and noradrenaline act through receptor binding over milliseconds to minutes. Decoherence in neural tissue runs femtoseconds to picoseconds. No mechanism lets a stress hormone change which quantum states 'remain indeterminate'. Your own register's preferred family (P-Q1) selects among *already-decohered* outcomes. So the most stress can narrow is the classical neural option set. Say that. Under P-Q2 the physics also fixes the chances of whatever outcomes remain, and the page never mentions it. 'Options that physics leaves open' (L107, L113) is half the story."

### The Many-Worlds Defender (David Deutsch)

"I accept that the 2026-07-28 review marked our disagreement as bedrock, and I won't relitigate it. But read your own paragraph. It grants the Everettian 'a branch-local history of having chosen … answerable for it in this branch', and two sentences later it says one-world actuality 'is what lets moral weight attach to the choice at all' (L115). Answerability is moral weight. What your paragraph argued for is narrower: desert that needs alternatives genuinely rejected. Branch-weighted decision theory (Deutsch 1999, *Proc. R. Soc. A* 455:3129–3137; Wallace 2012, *The Emergent Multiverse*, OUP; both verified at Crossref) gives Everettians a working account of rational and moral choice. Your 'at all' erases it."

### Issue 4: Aristotle on compulsion is misframed
- **File**: same, L47

- **Problem**: Verified against the Ross translation, NE III.1 (classics.mit.edu, grep-confirmed in the raw HTML):

## Counterarguments to Address

### "Felt effort distinguishes selection from luck"
- **Current content says**: Under duress, resistance does not feel random, so the luck objection loses force.
- **A critic would argue**: The objection is about what explains the outcome across replays, not about how the outcome feels. The same felt effort precedes both the endurance and the break.
- **Suggested response**: Concede that felt effort marks authorship and is no contrastive explanation. Then state the Map's terminus as the rollback page does (Issue 1).

### "Physicalism makes the resister and the complier the same"
- **Current content says**: If physical factors fix the outcome, neither agent was "genuinely choosing differently".
- **A critic would argue**: They chose differently because they differ: in training, commitments and the reasons-responsive mechanisms that issued their choices. Fischer–Ravizza ownership secures exactly this.
- **Suggested response**: Grant it, as P-A5 already does. Locate the Map's difference in the narrow residue: who settles the token, and desert that needs real alternatives (Issue 2).

### "The boundary testifies to interaction"
- **Current content says**: That agency can be pushed but not eliminated testifies to mind-body interaction.
- **A critic would argue**: Any graded biological capacity has a boundary. That a boundary exists discriminates nothing. The structured-residual test L83 names would discriminate, and it has not been run.
- **Suggested response**: Use L83's own wording in the conclusion (Issue 3).
