## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. Spliced quotation attributed to Place (fixed).** L39 offered ("a table is a cloud of molecules") as Place's example of the "is" of composition. The string is not in Place 1956. His examples (p. 45) are "his table is an old packing case", "her hat is a bundle of straw tied together with string" and "a cloud is a mass of water droplets or other particles in suspension". The article's string splices the table and cloud examples into a sentence Place never wrote. It was verified against the journal page images (Purdue-hosted scan of *Brit. J. Psychol.* 47(1), pp. 44–50), because the PDF's text layer holds only running heads. Replaced with Place's verbatim cloud example, the one he develops at length (pp. 46–47).

**2. Wrong objection paired with the phenomenological fallacy (fixed).** L39 said Place diagnosed the phenomenological fallacy "against the objection that experience-talk cannot be about brain processes because we know nothing of our brains". In Place, that point is *granted* (p. 45: you can describe your sensations "without knowing anything about your brain processes") and disarmed by the "is" of composition and the cloud analogy (§§II–III). The phenomenological fallacy (§V, pp. 48–49) answers a different objection: the physiologist's (Sherrington's) worry that the subject's reports cannot be accounted for by the known properties of the nervous system, for instance that nothing in the brain is green when the subject reports a green after-image. Both moves are now stated and paired with the right objection. The article's gloss of the fallacy itself was faithful and is kept.

**3. Calibration: the bruteness rebuttal stated as decisive (fixed).** L71 said the modal asymmetry "is exactly what stops bruteness from rescuing *this* identity" and concluded "the demand for explanation is legitimate here", with no mention of the identity theorist's standing answer. Three Map pages set a lower ceiling. The routing page grades the Map's case against Type-B at *compatible*: its replies are "a priori, modal or defeater-removing", and "reaching says nothing about whether the reply succeeds". The Kripke sibling (L47, L67) records Kripke himself calling the argument "no proof that no moves are available" and the Map declining to treat it as a knockdown. The primitive-identities page (L79) treats the phenomenal-concepts reply as live. A tenet-accepting reviewer would flag the L71 wording, so this is a calibration error and not bedrock disagreement. The fix keeps the Kripke point as what defeats *bare* bruteness ("why bruteness alone cannot rescue *this* identity"). It then states the Type-B answer: the phenomenal-concepts strategy grants that pain has no appearance distinct from itself and locates the felt contingency in the cognitive distinctness of the concepts. It adds Chalmers's objection and closes at the routing page's tier ("the Map rates the evidence as merely *compatible* with its dualism, short of favouring it").
   - Wording check: the Type-B reply was first drafted as phenomenal concepts *being* the contingent mode of presentation pain lacks. Chalmers 2003 reports Loar's view as the opposite: phenomenal concepts "are recognitional concepts that lack contingent modes of presentation" and "can corefer with physical concepts that are cognitively distinct". The published wording follows Chalmers's report and [[phenomenal-concepts-strategy]]'s "direct recognitional concepts".

**4. Internal contradiction created by fix 3, and pre-existing (fixed).** L77 said functionalism "inherits the very explanatory gap that sinks the identity theory". After calibration, the page says the exchange against the type-B form is unsettled, so "sinks" contradicted it. It now reads "the very explanatory gap the Map presses against the identity theory".

**5. Lead misdescribed the modern opponent (fixed).** L33 said naming the relation an identity "does not close the explanatory gap it was invoked to dissolve". Type-B identity theorists do not invoke identity to dissolve the explanatory gap. They grant "an unclosable epistemic gap" and deny an ontological one (Chalmers 2003, the routing page's definition), and Levine, who named the gap, is on Chalmers's type-B list. Pressing an unclosed gap against them is the "persistence counted against physicalism" misrouting the routing page lists. The lead now says the gap stays open "and the theory's modern *a posteriori* defenders concede that the gap will stay open, denying only that it marks a gap in reality."

**6. Navigation surface overclaimed (fixed).** The description said the Map "argues Tenet 1 against it". The body (L67) marks Tenet 1 as a commitment it does not argue and presses only the narrower asserted-not-explained charge. The description now says "presses a narrower charge against it".
