# Referee Report: Submission on "Dualism" and the "Unfinishable Map" Framework

## 1. Introduction and Scope of Review

This report provides a formal pre-publication referee assessment of the manuscript "Dualism," currently hosted within the *Unfinishable Map* framework. The submission attempts to mount a comprehensive, cumulative defense of interactionist dualism. To do so, the authors synthesize classical philosophical arguments against physicalism—including the Explanatory Gap, the Knowledge Argument, and the Argument from Reason—with highly speculative interpretations of quantum mechanics. Most notably, the manuscript proposes a "Minimal Quantum Interaction" (MQI) hypothesis and a quantum entanglement solution to the phenomenal binding problem.

The remit of this review is to evaluate the manuscript strictly on the grounds of its empirical claims, its handling of the cited literature, the logical validity of its inferences, and its coverage of competing counterarguments. Tested against the standard of the 2020–2025 cognitive neuroscience, philosophy of mind, and quantum foundations literature, the manuscript is fundamentally flawed. It relies on a systemic pattern of selective citation, elevating debunked or fiercely contested empirical anomalies to the status of foundational premises. Conversely, it systematically evades the most formidable theoretical and empirical achievements of contemporary physicalism, choosing instead to shadowbox with archaic formulations of identity theory and behaviorism.

Ultimately, the manuscript's architectural ambition cannot conceal its epistemic evasions. By explicitly bracketing its core metaphysical commitments from empirical falsification—retreating to "structural arguments" whenever its scientific claims are dismantled—the text ceases to function as a defensible academic thesis. The following sections detail the fatal logical, empirical, and theoretical weaknesses that necessitate the outright rejection of this manuscript.

## 2. Metaphysical and Epistemological Framework Critiques

The manuscript opens by grounding its dualism in a series of classical philosophical arguments, attempting to show that a convergence of epistemological gaps necessarily points to an ontological gap. However, the text systematically ignores the contemporary physicalist literature that successfully bridges or dissolves these gaps.

### 2.1 The Misuse of Kripkean A Posteriori Necessity

The authors deploy Saul Kripke’s modal argument against type-identity theory, arguing that because "pain" and "C-fibre firing" are rigid designators, any identity between them must be necessary. The apparent contingency of their connection, the manuscript claims, cannot be explained away via an appearance/reality gap (the "heat manoeuvre") because pain is identified directly by its phenomenological quality. Therefore, the contingency is real, and the identity is false.

While the manuscript accurately summarizes Kripke’s 1972/1980 framework, it egregiously omits Kripke’s own explicit disclaimer that his argument does not entail Cartesian dualism, as well as the entire 2020s literature on the Phenomenal Concepts Strategy (PCS). The manuscript treats PCS as a mere "standoff," ignoring recent physicalist advancements in conceptual engineering. Contemporary physicalism does not need to deny Kripke's semantic premise; rather, it utilizes frameworks like the self-organizing metarepresentational account (SOMA) to demonstrate that the conceptual isolation of first-person phenomenal concepts is a predictable, physically explicable feature of how a neurocomputational system monitors its own internal states [1].

By treating the epistemic divide between phenomenal and physical concepts as proof of an ontological divide, the manuscript commits a basic fallacy of reification. The authors ignore the eliminativist critiques (e.g., Derek Ball’s legacy extended into the 2020s) demonstrating that the intrinsic, individualistic phenomenal concepts required for Kripke's argument to secure dualism are linguistically and cognitively incoherent.

### 2.2 The Explanatory Gap and the Knowledge Argument

In its treatment of Joseph Levine’s Explanatory Gap and Frank Jackson’s Knowledge Argument (Mary’s Room), the manuscript again relies on a frozen-in-time version of the debate. It acknowledges that Jackson eventually abandoned his own argument in favor of representationalism, yet it completely fails to engage with the modern iterations of representationalism and illusionism that dominate the 2020s literature.

The manuscript dismisses Illusionism—championed by Keith Frankish and Daniel Dennett—by invoking the "illusion problem," claiming that to have an illusion of consciousness, one must be conscious, thus triggering an infinite regress. This is a severe strawmanning of the contemporary Illusionist position. The manuscript completely ignores Attention Schema Theory (AST) as articulated by Michael Graziano in the 2020s [1]. AST does not posit an experiential "illusion" floating in a Cartesian theater; rather, it posits that the brain constructs a low-resolution, schematic data structure to model its own attentional states. Because this data structure lacks the granular physical details of neurotransmitters and synapses, the higher-order cognitive system reports the presence of a "magical," non-physical property.

Under AST and subtle illusionism, there is no infinite regress. The brain is simply a machine that computes a false, simplified proposition about its own mechanics for the sake of processing efficiency. By failing to test the Knowledge Argument against AST—which perfectly explains *why* Mary would feel she is learning something new (updating her internal attention schema) despite learning no new non-physical facts—the manuscript leaves a massive counterargument untested.

### 2.3 The Argument from Reason and Epistemic Self-Stultification

The authors attempt to revive the Lewis-Plantinga "Argument from Reason," asserting that if physicalism is true, beliefs are caused by non-rational physical causes (efficient causes) rather than logical entailment (normative reasons). Therefore, physicalism allegedly provides an undercutting defeater for itself. The site's recent changelog (e.g., `00:36 - deep-review`) indicates the authors have actively refined this section, replacing "luck" framing with "common cause" to make their tenets symmetric.

Despite these internal refinements, the argument rests on a profound category mistake that contemporary computational philosophy has long resolved. The manuscript treats "efficient causes" and "normative relationships" as mutually exclusive ontological forces. However, computational functionalism demonstrates precisely how physical systems can be configured such that their efficient causal transitions perfectly mirror logical entailments. A silicon calculator operates strictly via physical laws (voltage gates and electron flow), yet its physical state transitions reliably track the normative truths of arithmetic.

To claim that a belief lacks rational justification because it is physically instantiated is to confuse the *vehicle* of representation with its *content*. The evolutionary reliabilism that the authors dismiss actually solves this: biological neural networks that failed to isomorphically track the logical and physical regularities of their environment were selected against.

## 3. The Falsification of Gravity-Induced Collapse and the Incoherence of Minimal Quantum Interaction

The submission's foundational mechanism for mind-body interaction is the "Minimal Quantum Interaction" (MQI) hypothesis. Recognizing that classical substance dualism violates the causal closure of the physical and the conservation of energy, the authors attempt to locate a causal opening in quantum indeterminacy. They propose that consciousness "selects" outcomes from quantum superpositions in the brain, thereby exerting causal influence without injecting novel dynamical energy. To ground this physical mechanism, the authors invoke pre-decoherence coherence candidates, most notably the Penrose–Hameroff Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch OR) model and Penrose's gravity-induced collapse framework.

This formulation contains catastrophic empirical and logical failures that the authors either omit or willfully mishandle.

### 3.1 The Empirical Falsification of the Diósi–Penrose Model

The manuscript cites the Diósi–Penrose (DP) model of gravity-induced wave function collapse as a live, plausible mechanism for generating the objective thresholds required for conscious selection. The text states that "gravity itself determines when superpositions become untenable," proposing this as a natural quantum-classical boundary.

This claim ignores a watershed empirical falsification in the recent physics literature. In 2021, Donadi et al. conducted an exhaustive underground test of the gravity-related wave function collapse model at the Gran Sasso laboratory [2]. The DP model, like all continuous spontaneous localization (CSL) models that collapse wave functions in space, necessarily predicts a diffusive motion of the particles involved. For charged particles, this stochastic diffusion inevitably results in the emission of bremsstrahlung radiation [2].

Donadi et al. measured the radiation emission rate of germanium nuclei and found no evidence of the radiation predicted by the DP model, effectively setting a lower bound on the effective size of the mass density of nuclei that is three orders of magnitude larger than previous bounds [4]. This unequivocally rules out the parameter-free version of the Diósi–Penrose model upon which the manuscript's pre-decoherence speculations heavily rely [3]. By continuing to present gravity-induced collapse as a viable physiological mechanism for interactionist dualism without confronting this definitive falsification, the manuscript commits a severe violation of scientific rigor.

### 3.2 The Logical Incoherence of Post-Decoherence "Selection"

Perhaps anticipating the collapse of pre-decoherence models in the warm, wet environment of the brain (a vulnerability the manuscript briefly acknowledges via Max Tegmark's calculations), the authors retreat to what they term "Post-Decoherence Selection." They posit that consciousness selects which outcome becomes actual from the "improper reduced-state mixture left after decoherence has completed."

This betrays a profound misunderstanding of quantum formalism and creates a fatal double-bind. Decoherence explains the diagonalization of the density matrix in a preferred basis, dynamically eliminating interference terms. The resulting state is an improper mixture, mathematically indistinguishable from a classical probability distribution representing subjective ignorance. If a non-physical mind "selects" an outcome from this mixture to influence macroscopic neural firing (e.g., directing attention or motor action), it must systematically bias the probabilities away from those prescribed by the Born rule.

The site's own changelog (`19:08 - check-tenets`) reveals internal anxiety over this exact issue, flagging 6 files for using "biasing probabilities" language. Yet, the dilemma remains unresolved in the text. If the mind *does* alter Born rule probabilities to achieve evolutionary advantageous macroscopic behavior (as the authors require when deploying their evolutionary argument against epiphenomenalism), then MQI is not "minimal" at all. It represents a massive, statistically detectable violation of fundamental physical law, equivalent to macroscopic psychokinesis.

If, conversely, the mind strictly obeys the Born rule and merely "actualizes" outcomes without altering their statistical distribution over time, then consciousness is a statistical slave to quantum randomness. It could not steer the organism away from a predator or toward a mate any better than a random number generator could. The manuscript completely fails to resolve this fatal contradiction, simultaneously claiming that consciousness exerts biologically adaptive causal power and that it leaves no trace-violating signature on the physics.

## 4. The Quantum Binding Hypothesis and Methodological Artefacts

In addressing the unity of consciousness (BP2: the combination problem), the manuscript argues that classical neural architectures can only produce functional segregation (BP1) and can never produce ontological holism. The authors assert that quantum entanglement is the "only known physical mechanism" capable of solving BP2, pointing to non-separability in tensor product spaces.

To support this staggering metaphysical claim, the manuscript leans entirely on a single empirical pillar: Kerskens and López Pérez (2022), who claimed to have detected non-classical entanglement in the brain via heartbeat-evoked zero quantum coherence (ZQC) in MRI [6].

### 4.1 The Evasion of the Warren (2023) Refutation

The manuscript's treatment of the empirical backlash against Kerskens and López Pérez is textbook epistemic bracketing. In 2023, Warren published a comprehensive, physics-based refutation of the Kerskens claim in the *Journal of Physics Communications* [8]. Warren mathematically demonstrated that the signals interpreted by Kerskens as non-classical entanglement were, in fact, standard intermolecular zero-quantum coherence (iZQC) arising from classical dipole-dipole interactions [9].

Warren's calculations show that standard iZQC sequences can detect signals under specific parameters (e.g., TE = 5ms, accounting for T2 relaxation) that the Kerskens protocol simply misinterpreted [12]. The macroscopic magnetization of hundreds of millions of nuclear spins measured by MRI makes the isolation of genuine quantum entanglement within such a noisy, hot, biological system effectively impossible using the reported methodology [11].

How does the manuscript handle this devastating empirical refutation? It briefly acknowledges Warren's critique but immediately retreats to a self-insulating defense: "The core argument is structural rather than empirical" [6]. The authors claim that even if the Kerskens data is an artefact, the *structural* necessity of entanglement for phenomenal unity remains intact.

### 4.2 The Illegitimacy of the "Structural" Retreat

This framing explicitly brackets the site's own commitments from criticism. If the only empirical evidence for macroscopic quantum entanglement in the human brain is admittedly a classical NMR artefact [9], then the "structural argument" is reduced to a mathematically empty metaphor. One cannot solve a neurobiological binding problem with a physical mechanism that demonstrably does not exist in the neurobiology.

Furthermore, the manuscript's structural argument against classical unity relies on a reductive strawman. The authors claim, "All large-scale classical phenomena reduce to coordinated arrangements of parts—meaning there are no genuine physical wholes." This ignores the entire modern discipline of complex systems theory, non-linear dynamics, and integrated information. As Mediano et al. (2022) demonstrate in their extensive work on causal emergence and Information Decomposition, macroscopic classical systems can possess irreducible causal power and structural holism that cannot be decomposed into their constituent micro-parts [13]. By ignoring modern mathematical accounts of classical emergence, the authors force a false dichotomy between archaic reductionism and quantum mysticism.

## 5. Omission of Leading 2020s Neuroscientific Frameworks

The most glaring omission in the manuscript is its failure to test interactionist dualism against the actual theories of consciousness (ToCs) currently driving empirical cognitive neuroscience. The manuscript spends thousands of words critiquing Type-A and Type-B identity theories, epiphenomenalism, and early forms of behaviorism. Yet, it entirely ignores the dominant frameworks mapping the empirical data of the 2020s.

### 5.1 The Evasion of Predictive Processing Theories (PPT)

In their seminal 2022 review in *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, Anil Seth and Tim Bayne outline the four primary empirical theories of consciousness: Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT), Integrated Information Theory (IIT), Higher-Order Theories (HOT), and Predictive Processing Theories (PPT) [14].

The manuscript relies heavily on the "Explanatory Gap," claiming that physicalist explanations cannot account for the "what it is like" of experience, its intentionality, or its unity. However, Predictive Processing models provide exact structural and functional accounts of these phenomena. Under PPT, phenomenal experience is the brain's best generative model of the hidden causes of its sensory input, continually updated via prediction error minimization. Phenomenal unity (which the manuscript desperately outsources to quantum entanglement) is elegantly explained by PPT as a computational imperative: the brain must maintain a unified, multi-modal generative model to effectively minimize global surprise [13].

By failing to engage with PPT, the manuscript leaves the most robust physicalist counterargument untested. The authors claim physicalism treats experience as a "brute fact," completely ignoring that predictive processing deduces the structure of experience from the mathematical first principles of active inference, Bayesian updating, and variational free energy [13].

### 5.2 The Disconnect from the Cogitate Consortium Trials

The manuscript's disconnect from contemporary science is further evidenced by its superficial mention of the Cogitate Consortium trials. The 2025 Cogitate trials represented an unprecedented adversarial collaboration testing the spatial and temporal predictions of GNWT against IIT using highly powered M/EEG and fMRI data across hundreds of subjects [17].

The Cogitate data mapped the precise neural correlates of conscious perception, evaluating whether sustained prefrontal ignition (GNWT) or posterior cortical synchronization (IIT) better predicted subjective awareness [19]. While the manuscript vaguely gestures at these trials in a changelog note (`22:31 - optimistic-review`) to suggest physicalist theories are in conflict, it completely ignores the data's overarching implications. Both IIT and GNWT—despite their differences—locate consciousness strictly within classical, macro-scale neural network dynamics [17].

The wealth of specific, replicable spatial data tying specific conscious states to specific cortico-cortical classical pathways renders the manuscript's hypothesis of a non-local, quantum-thalamic selector utterly disconnected from empirical reality. The authors are practicing armchair metaphysics while ignoring the very neuroimaging data that explicitly maps the causal chain of perception.

## 6. The Mischaracterization of Causal Closure and Parsimony

To justify the injection of dualist causal mechanisms into the brain, the manuscript must undermine the causal closure of the physical—the principle that every physical event has a sufficient physical cause.

### 6.1 The Strawmanning of Causal Closure

The authors dismiss the causal closure of the physical as a mere assumption or metaphysical prejudice, setting up a false narrative that modern physics supports an open, causally porous brain. This ignores the rigorous defenses of closure mounted by philosophers and physicists in the 2020s, such as David Papineau [21].

Causal closure is not a metaphysical dogma; it is an empirical generalization derived from the overwhelming success of physiological research. Over the last century, whenever neuroscience has investigated a muscular contraction, a neural firing, or a synaptic transmission, it has invariably found sufficient biochemical antecedents (e.g., action potentials, neurotransmitter release, ion channel dynamics). There is zero empirical evidence of a "missing physical cause" in the brain that requires a non-physical mind to fill the gap. The manuscript claims that "ignorance of a mechanism is not evidence of non-existence," but when exhaustive neurophysiological tracing consistently demonstrates closed causal chains at the classical level, the postulation of an invisible, quantum-steering ghost becomes scientifically untenable.

### 6.2 The Reversal of Parsimony Epistemology

The submission concludes with meta-theoretical claims regarding parsimony (Occam's Razor). The authors argue that while ontological parsimony favors physicalism, "explanatory parsimony" favors dualism, citing philosophers like Swinburne and Huemer. They claim physicalism requires "complex theoretical machinery" while dualism is "structurally more direct."

This is a breathtaking misrepresentation of theoretical simplicity. Physicalism posits that the known laws of physics, chemistry, and biology apply uniformly to the brain. The manuscript's brand of interactionist dualism, by contrast, explicitly requires:

1. An entirely new, non-physical ontological category (mind/substance).
2. The existence of undiscovered "psychophysical laws" dictating how this non-physical substance interacts with matter.
3. A fundamental revision of quantum mechanics (Outcome Selection that selectively violates the Born rule to achieve biological adaptation).
4. The postulation of macroscopic quantum entanglement in the brain, contrary to known thermodynamic constraints.

To label the addition of these four massive, empirically unsupported ad hoc hypotheses as "explanatory parsimony" stretches the definition of the term beyond recognition, demonstrating a severe theoretical bias.

## 7. At-Least-Five Weaknesses List and Final Verdict

The manuscript's ambition is vast, but its execution represents an alarming departure from the norms of rigorous academic inquiry. The authors routinely elevate speculative analogies to the level of physical mechanisms while simultaneously inoculating their claims against empirical falsification.

### Weaknesses Justifying Rejection:

1. **Empirical Falsification of Proposed Collapse Mechanism:** The manuscript relies on gravity-induced wave function collapse (the Diósi–Penrose model) to ground its pre-decoherence quantum interaction hypothesis, entirely ignoring its definitive empirical falsification in underground radiation tests by **Donadi et al. (2021)** [2].
2. **Mishandling of Rebutting NMR Literature:** The article utilizes Kerskens & López Pérez's MRI findings as proof of non-classical entanglement for the binding problem, but egregiously minimizes and mischaracterizes the fatal physics-based refutation of this exact claim by **Warren (2023)** [8], who demonstrated the signal is a classical intermolecular zero-quantum coherence (iZQC) artefact.
3. **Omission of the Leading Neuroscientific Explanatory Frameworks:** The authors assert that physicalism provides no positive explanation for phenomenal unity or character, selectively ignoring the extensive predictive processing and active inference frameworks detailed by **Seth and Bayne (2022)** [14], which provide precise computational accounts of these phenomena without resorting to dualism.
4. **Evasion of Granular Adversarial Neural Data:** While briefly referencing the **Cogitate Consortium et al. (2025)** [17], the manuscript fails to test its macro-quantum holism hypothesis against the actual classical, spatial, and temporal structural data mapping conscious states verified by these unprecedented adversarial neuroimaging trials.
5. **Strawmanning of Classical Emergence and Integration:** The manuscript falsely claims that classical systems cannot produce genuine structural wholes, relying on a naive reductionism to force a quantum binding solution, completely ignoring contemporary physicalist accounts of causal emergence and information decomposition, such as those formalized by **Mediano et al. (2022)** [13].
6. **Incoherence Regarding the Born Rule and Causal Closure:** The manuscript claims Minimal Quantum Interaction is biologically adaptive (solving the epiphenomenalist evolutionary challenge) while simultaneously claiming it does not violate statistical quantum laws, a mathematical impossibility that contemporary defenders of causal closure, such as **Papineau (2021)** [21], explicitly rule out.

**Verdict:**
Due to its reliance on falsified physical mechanisms, its systematic evasion of leading neuroscientific counter-frameworks, and its use of epistemic double-standards to insulate its metaphysical claims from empirical failure, this manuscript is entirely unready for academic submission and must be rejected.
