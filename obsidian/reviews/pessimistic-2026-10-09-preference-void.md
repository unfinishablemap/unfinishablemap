## Executive Summary

The quotation layer is sound. I checked 15 quoted strings at source and 14 matched verbatim. The one I could not reach is the Braga & Starmer 2005 quote, because no abstract is exposed and the author PDF link is dead. That is unverified, not shown to be wrong. The article's calibration is also better than its research note's: it adds the discovered-preference rival and Cox & Grether's convergence, which the note left out.

The defects are in the argument's architecture.

1. **The seam fails the Map's own test.** L75 asserts that the faces "are severable and their co-occurrence is informative". `apex/conjunction-coalesce` L78 says an article claiming that "must name each face's upstream source and show at least two are independent", and the page does neither. The one severability argument it gives (the frame-logging being) puts the loss on the wrong face.
2. **The mark face recasts an observer's illusion as the chooser's test.** Ariely, Loewenstein & Prelec describe an illusion of order for whoever reads the valuations; their abstract's conclusion is about demand curves. L71 turns that into "the test a mind would apply" to its own preference, without marking the step. L75's "the built answer would feel provisional" does not follow from it.
3. **The front of the page states the strong reading.** The description and lead assert the strong construction reading ("the asking builds the answer"; research "says the presentation misleads"). L77 and L89 retreat from that to the access claim. Under the deflations the asking does not build the answer, and the void survives anyway. The truncation surface therefore gives the void's reason as the one reading the body treats as contested. The skill classes this epistemic/metaphysical equivocation as critical.
4. **The retrospective-face record stops at 2013, and it understates.** Izuma & Murayama's own meta-analysis found that choice-induced preference change "does exist". A 2021 meta-analysis of 43 artifact-free studies found d = 0.40 with no publication bias. L43's "contested measurement debate" and L65's "still argue" are stale. Correcting them strengthens the void.

A fifth finding runs against the void. Butler & Loomes (2007) measured the imprecision surrounding choices and valuations and found it accounts for reversals of both forms. That bears directly on L59 ("no single elicitation contains evidence of its own construction") and L93 ("each has the settledness of a verdict").

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

"You have caught folk psychology in the act and decided the act is a *void*. The literature you cite never needed a stored 'ordering' to explain anything. Tversky, Slovic and Kahneman explain reversals by attribute weighting that depends on response mode. Your own research note cites Stewart, Chater and Brown's decision-by-sampling and Vlaev et al.'s 'Does the brain calculate value?', and both deny a stable internal value scale at any level. The article dropped both. Once they are restored, 'the mind cannot read its ordering' becomes 'there was never an ordering-shaped thing to read'. That is a fact about a bad folk concept, not a limit on access. At L116 you call the gap between preference-as-state and preference-as-choice-pattern 'a datum dualism must explain'. It is a datum folk psychology must give up. The page concedes that it 'confirms nothing', which is right, but then it still files the datum under dualism's explanatory obligations rather than under folk psychology's retirement."

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

"Your phenomenological premise is unsourced. L41 says that an answer 'presents itself as a reading taken from an ordering already in place', and L93 says each answer 'has the settledness of a verdict'. Where is that heterophenomenology? Every study you cite forced a point answer: choose A or B, name one price. A forced point format manufactures the settledness you then report as a feature of the mind. When experimenters let people express imprecision (Butler and Loomes 2007), they could measure it, and it accounted for the reversals. The 'mark face' is then partly an artefact of the instrument. Then L71 moves Ariely's *observer's* illusion (a demand curve that looks as if it came from stable preferences) into the subject's head as a 'test a mind would apply'. Nobody showed that minds run a coherence check on their own preferences. You are inflating an economist's inference problem into an introspective one."

### The Quantum Skeptic

"Reading 1 at L109 has consciousness settle 'which prepared member governs the act' among 'candidate weightings or orderings'. An ordering over options is a distributed valuation state across large neural populations, not a two-way switch. Making one ordering rather than another 'actual' is a macroscopic difference that needs amplification from whatever microscopic event the tenets allow. L116 then calls this 'a small-channel influence'. But Tenet 2's minimality is not channel size: `tenets.md` L69 fixes it as respect for the empirical record (no detectable energy injection, no Born-statistics violation). A choice among few options is not thereby a physically small intervention. There is a further point. Nothing in the reversal data shows several orderings *co-present* within one elicitation. Different orderings appear under *different procedures*. The 'prepared candidates' are a posit that the cited data do not supply."

### The Many-Worlds Defender

"L116: 'whichever procedure ran, one ordering became actual.' Any Everettian agrees: within each branch, one ordering. The sentence has no bearing on Many Worlds, and the 2026-10-05 tenet check said so (Note 8). Procedure-dependent construction is classical cognitive psychology, and it has nothing to say about branching. The honest line is 'not engaged'. Your research note tried an indexical angle ('which of the orderings ... is *its* ordering?') and the article rightly dropped it. Having dropped it, there is nothing left to connect."

### The Empiricist

"Two claims are worded so that nothing could count against them. L59: 'What the void claims survives either outcome.' If a claim survives both outcomes of the experiment that seemed decisive, it has been placed beyond that experiment. That is not a virtue, and the article presents it as one. The Map's reading 1 (L109) is a third-person claim: conscious selection settles which ordering governs. Third-person claims are testable even when first-person access is closed. Which ordering comes back is largely predicted by the procedure (scale compatibility, evaluability: the article's own L57 and L99). If procedure plus pre-choice neural value signals predicted choice to the noise ceiling, reading 1's room would vanish. The page says only that 'a fully physical construction fits the data as well' (L112). It should say what would shrink reading 1, or concede that reading 1 is idle. The single-elicitation claim also has a counterexample. Butler and Loomes's imprecision measure is a within-elicitation signal that tracks where reversals occur, and the article states it as a structural impossibility."

### The Buddhist Philosopher

"At L77 the article prefers the weak readings because they 'leave a fact there, hidden'. That is the clinging. The construction literature reads as dependent origination applied to preference: no ordering has its own nature (*svabhāva*) apart from the conditions of its asking, and the article's own retrospective face shows the asking also conditions what is asked about. Both of your readings at L109–L110 need a consciousness standing outside the construction, to select (reading 1) or to receive (reading 2). But the chooser who 'cannot be reset between askings' (L101) is constructed in the same act, and the same retrospective contamination applies to the self that would do the selecting. Your 'chooser's seat' (L75, L112) is the last unexamined permanent thing on the page."

## Counterarguments to Address

### The procedure does the selecting
- **Current content says** (L109): conscious selection settles which of several prepared orderings "becomes actual in this choice".
- **A critic would argue**: the article's own evidence says which ordering comes back is predicted by the procedure, through scale compatibility (L57) and evaluability (L57, L99). In the cited data, the response mode does the selecting. Whatever room reading 1 has lies in the variance that procedure leaves unexplained, and the page never says so.
- **Suggested response**: a sentence at L109 placing any conscious contribution in what the procedure leaves unpredicted. The imprecision region Butler & Loomes measured (Issue 3) is the natural place, so the Issue 3 fix and this one share a source.
- **Operator item, not priced**: L109 ends "This moves the site from the commitment to the ordering the commitment expresses." `tenets.md` L107 commits the Map to *outcome*-selection and says a relocation that "would weaken outcome-selection to context-setting" is one the Map "could adopt but has not". Selecting which evaluative ordering governs an act is arguably context-setting for that act, unless the ordering is itself counted as an outcome of a valuation process. That reading may be available, but the page should state it. This is a framework-level judgment, so it is recorded rather than priced.

### The common-cause rival
- **Current content says** (L75): the faces are severable, so their co-occurrence is informative.
- **A critic would argue**: on-the-spot, path-dependent construction yields all three faces. Answers are computed relative to whatever was just asked (elicitation) or answered (retrospective and anchoring), so the conjunction counts once.
- **Suggested response**: name the three proximate mechanisms (scale compatibility and evaluability; dissonance-style choice-induced change; anchoring). They are distinct in the literature, which gives the page a real case for independence. Then mark the constructive-processing unification as unrun, as apex L110 requires. See Priority 2.

### Forced point formats manufacture the settledness
- **Current content says** (L93): each answer arrives with "the settledness of a verdict".
- **A critic would argue**: the instruments demanded point answers. Given room to express imprecision, people do, and the imprecision tracks the reversals (Butler & Loomes 2007).
- **Suggested response**: scope the settledness claim to point-answer formats, and treat imprecision as a partial window rather than none. See Priority 3. This weakens "Direct. None." (L97) to "Direct: only the coarse signal of felt imprecision", which the operator may prefer to leave as an open question.
