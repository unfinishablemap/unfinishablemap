## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Internal over-generalisation (lede L39, Site Perspective L121).** The lede said that *each* rival "makes the zombie verdict depend on prior knowledge" of constitutive facts, essences, theory or potentialities. The similarity-empiricism section (L77) and the table say that account *withholds* judgement. L121 repeated the same over-generalisation ("becomes downstream of the disputed metaphysics"). Resolution: the lede now reads "Each either withholds judgement or makes the zombie verdict depend on…". L121 now reads "zombie possibility no longer counts as independent evidence", which is true on every route.
- **Qualifier inflation, "some" to "such" (L75).** The SEP says "*Some* modest theories … are either skeptical or silent about … philosophical zombies". The article said "The SEP counts *such* modest accounts as…", which assigned the verdict to Roca-Royes's account. Resolution: "The SEP calls her approach modest and notes that some modest accounts are…". The SEP's §7 does call it modest ("Roca-Royes and Fischer both take a modest approach"). The application to zombies stays labelled as the Map's inference.
- **SEP paraphrase drift (L45).** The article glossed modal rationalism as "allows a priori access" and modal empiricism as "grounds modal knowledge in the actual world". SEP §2.3 actually says rationalists "prioritize a priori methods" and empiricists "pursue a posteriori accounts", and that empiricists "often … still acknowledge that a priori methods might contribute". The old gloss blurred the distinction the article depends on, because "allows a priori access" is also true of most empiricists. Resolution: rewritten to the SEP's terms, keeping the "often granting a priori methods some role" clause, which supports the article's own L109 point.
- **Attribution (L81).** "The SEP classes it [Vetter 2023] as ambitious" was wrong. The SEP reports that "In her (2023) she emphasizes that her account is ambitious." Resolution: "Vetter stresses that it is ambitious".
- **Dropped qualifier (L114).** Chalmers 2007 says zombie conceivability is "an assumption that type-B materialists *typically* grant". The article had "which Type-B grants". Resolution: "typically grants".

### Medium Issues Found

- **Calibration of the Map's own conceivability report (L51).** "careful reflection has found no contradiction" was stronger than the sibling page [[conceivability-possibility-inference]] L144 ("haven't produced consensus that it does"), and Type-A physicalists claim to have found one. Resolution: "no agreed contradiction".
- **"plausibility rather than on evidence" (L95).** This drifted from the source rating at [[primitive-identities-and-strong-necessities]] L97 ("bears on plausibility rather than tier"). A priori considerations can be evidence; the rating concerns tier. Resolution: "rather than on evidential tier".
- **"leave a priori modal knowledge in place" (L109).** The list that follows includes Williamson's *armchair* knowledge, which he holds is neither strictly a priori nor a posteriori. Resolution: "a priori or armchair", matching the 2026-10-22 repair to [[conceivability-possibility-inference]] (commit 83d137faf7).
- **Hill quote truncated before its key clause (L63).** The quote stopped at "grasped a priori". The later paraphrases at L71 and L123 ("Hill denies that conceiving gives/supplies…") depend on the omitted words "by merely conceiving of them or imagining them". Resolution: the quote is extended to include them (+7 words).
- **Fischer under-attributed (L89).** The article gave only the Map's inference for the theory-based route and called its coverage of consciousness "unresolved". Biggs's review, which was read in full, reports that Fischer's preferred Skeptical-TEM "turn[s] away … rationalists, such as … Chalmers (2002)". It also reports that Fischer argues "we can't justifiably believe or disbelieve many of the modal claims that philosophers advance, such as that expressed by 'I could have been disembodied'". That is the author's own verdict on a Cartesian modal claim parallel to the zombie premise, and the research note had missed it. Resolution: one sentence added with the verbatim Biggs quote, and the mind-independence restriction folded into the following sentence. The table row is unchanged ("Map's inference"), since Fischer's verdict concerns disembodiment, not zombies.
- **Unsupported superlative (L37).** "most fully developed by David Chalmers" → "developed in detail by David Chalmers".
