## Executive Summary

At its best, this wing is where the Map shows it can argue against its own interest. `stochastic-emergence` states its tier in the opening paragraph ("*live hypothesis* … not even strongly supported") and never drifts from it. `decoherence-and-macroscopic-superposition` L109 calls its own position "empirically weightless", because a result that cannot count against the position cannot count for it either. `motor-control-quantum-zeno` L99 points out that Schurger's accumulator is classical and causally closed, so the Libet levelling gives the agent no real indeterminacy to resolve. `stapp-quantum-mind` L64 separates Stapp's Process-1 question-choice from the outcome-selection the Map wants, and L126 concedes the measurement back-action objection by narrowing its claim instead of deflecting.

The weakness is also shared. In three articles the tenet section or the opening says more than the article's own concession allows:
- **Smeared states**: L100 calls definiteness "not an assumption but a discovery", but L124 says the measurement problem "cannot itself be evidence for conscious selection".
- **Motor selection**: L38 and L83 say consciousness "resolves indeterminacy at the selection point", but L99 says the classical models supply none.
- **Stapp**: L66, L106 and L152 have consciousness biasing or determining outcomes, but L64 says Stapp declines to make that move.

One finding is a category error rather than a calibration slip. `sorkin-delta-brain-internal-analogues` L77 treats Stapp's literal Zeno mechanism as something a third-order-interference test could constrain. The Zeno effect is standard quantum mechanics (`quantum-zeno-effect` L43), and the wing's own L47 says standard QM predicts ε = 0 exactly. So Stapp's model predicts no Sorkin residue at any attention rate. The mistake was inherited from `brain-internal-born-rule-testing` L98, and three deep reviews of the Sorkin page passed over it.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would recognise his own caution in how the wing uses the Chalmers–McQueen proposal. `consciousness-in-smeared-quantum-states` quotes their "research program here worth exploring" rather than claiming a result, and the positions register (P-Q3) records that they think consciousness-collapse "need not lead to dualism". He would especially value `decoherence-and-macroscopic-superposition` §"The Deeper Issue: Basis vs. Outcome". It explains that Quantum Darwinism leaves an improper mixture, not an outcome, so the outcome gap survives any decoherence timescale. That is the measurement-problem analogue of the explanatory gap, and the page keeps the gap open without claiming to fill it.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp would find himself treated more accurately here than almost anywhere else. `stapp-quantum-mind` L64 quotes Schwartz, Stapp & Beauregard (2005): nature's reply "conforms to a statistical rule". It then warns that "a reader sympathetic to both should not take Stapp as endorsing the Map's outcome-selection." Stapp would also be well served by the handling of Georgiev's (2015) Monte Carlo critique at L118. The page keeps Georgiev's decoherence-free-subspace escape clause, which turns "impossible in a warm brain" into an open empirical question. Prediction 7 (L180) gives his programme something it has lacked: a way to tell a quantum Zeno selector (a non-linearity as observation rate rises) from a classical Hebbian one (smooth saturation). He might object to how honest L182 is, since it admits that only predictions 4 and 7 test the quantum component. He could not call it unfair.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Nagel would single out `consciousness-in-smeared-quantum-states` §"The Definiteness Problem". It argues that "the very grammar of phenomenal description presupposes a definite experiential character", so there may be no *what it is like* for an indeterminate state. Nagel's question is being used here as a constraint on physics, not an ornament. `motor-control-quantum-zeno` L89 gives the same first-person structure an anatomical form. Desmurget's double dissociation separates felt intention (parietal) from execution (premotor), which matches the ordinary asymmetry that "you choose *what* to do but don't choose the muscle contractions".

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would like how the smeared-states article sets Penrose–Hameroff's proto-experiential moments beside Koch–Neven's superposition-formation hypothesis. Both reject the picture of inert matter plus added mind. He would like `wavefunction-realism-vs-primitive-ontology` even more. Its point that GRW flashes are "sparse, discrete, stochastic localization events" (L65) is the nearest thing in physics to an ontology of actual occasions, and the page offers it as "a ready-made place to act" without claiming it as evidence. **Constraint check**: nowhere in this wing does Whitehead-friendliness push an evidential tier up. The pressure runs the other way, and the Hardline Empiricist's section below deals with it.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Most defences of agent causation that cite Schurger stop at "the readiness potential is noise". ## The Category Error (Sorkin-Δ and Stapp)

`sorkin-delta-brain-internal-analogues` L77 says Stapp's literal Zeno reading "modifies outcome frequencies as a function of attention rate — a *frequency-dependent* deviation, which is exactly what a Δ-analogue conditional on attention rate could target". `brain-internal-born-rule-testing` L98 is the source: "Read literally, this is not Born-compliant at the ensemble level … looks for a Sorkin-style third-order analogue conditional on attention rate."

Three statements already in the corpus are enough to show the error:
- `quantum-zeno-effect` L43: the Zeno effect is "a genuine consequence of the standard formalism".
- `stapp-quantum-mind` L45: the model "requires no new physics: consciousness operates through orthodox quantum mechanics".
- `sorkin-higher-order-interference` L53 and `sorkin-delta` L47: the Born rule's quadratic form forces I₃ = 0, whatever the dynamics.

A schedule of repeated projective measurements does change survival probabilities compared with no measurement. That is a second-order, ordinary statistical effect. Every probability involved is still a Born probability, so the third-order residue stays exactly zero at every attention rate. A brain Sorkin-Δ therefore cannot constrain literal Stapp. Only Orch-OR, which is explicitly non-unitary, and probability-bias channels that are non-quadratic could produce a residue.

The constructive fix already exists in the wing. The discriminator for literal Zeno is `stapp-quantum-mind` prediction 7 (L180): a non-linearity in selection probability as observation rate rises. That is the observable the Sorkin page's Stapp paragraph should point to.

A related but separate problem: `sorkin-delta` L81 uses ε both for Sorkin's third-order parameter (L45) and for the RNG meta-analysis per-event bias. A uniform reweighting of outcome probabilities need not produce any third-order term, so a bound on one does not carry over to the other.

Why this went unnoticed: all three Sorkin deep reviews (2026-06-03, 07-08, 07-19) mention "Stapp literal-Zeno" only under the reasoning-mode check ("characterized neutrally"). The 2026-07-10 deep review of `brain-internal-born-rule-testing` said the Stapp attribution "is faithful". Each review checked whether Stapp was *attributed* correctly, not whether the proposed test *could detect* him.

**Carried, not tasked**: the wider label "Born-bending / minimum-outside-the-corridor" for Stapp in [[born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface]] (cited at `stapp-quantum-mind` L66). The label may be defensible if "Born-bending" means that attention-rate changes the *unmeasured-evolution* baseline rather than the per-measurement rule. The fix above should not widen into that taxonomy without reading the taxonomy article first.
