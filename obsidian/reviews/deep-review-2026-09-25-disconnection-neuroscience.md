## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found
- **Predictive asymmetry favouring the channel reading (possibility/probability slippage).** The old L67 said production theories only *accommodate* disconnection syndromes via an "additional auxiliary hypothesis", while the channel-architecture reading "offers a more direct prediction". This conflicts with the article's own content. (a) The "auxiliary hypothesis" (functions depend on distributed regions whose outputs combine) is the framework's own core claim, which the article grades *established* at L56. (b) L46 records that Wernicke *predicted* conduction aphasia from a neural wiring model, so the production side's connectionist ancestors predicted the pattern and did not merely accommodate it. A tenet-accepting reviewer would still flag the claimed predictive advantage. **Fix:** the paragraph now says production handles the pattern without strain because it rests on the established distributed-function core, and that Wernicke derived conduction aphasia before observing it. The channel reading predicts the same pattern and "holds no predictive advantage here." This makes the underdetermination verdict in the next paragraph consistent with the one before it.
- **Orphan references (Catani & ffytche 2005; Schmahmann & Pandya 2006).** Both were listed in References with no body anchor. The 2026-07-09 review's "no orphan refs" was wrong. **Fix:** both are now cited at the tractography sentence (L54), which is what each work supports: Catani & ffytche's tractography-era revisit of disconnection syndromes, and Schmahmann & Pandya's tract atlas.

### Medium Issues Found
- **Conduction aphasia "speech production intact"**: an overstatement. Output is fluent but marked by phonemic paraphasias. Fixed.
- **"Liepmann's nineteenth-century cases"**: Liepmann's apraxia work dates from 1900 (*Das Krankheitsbild der Apraxie*) and the 1907 callosal case with Maas. Changed to "from 1900 onward". The L38 lineage list ("nineteenth-century associationist line — Wernicke, Liepmann, Dejerine") was kept, since the line is nineteenth-century and Liepmann continues it.
- **Split-brain agency**: the old wording, "Sperry and Gazzaniga's split-brain studies — cutting the corpus callosum", credited the surgery to the researchers. The operations were done by neurosurgeons (Vogel and Bogen), and Sperry and Gazzaniga studied the patients afterwards. Reworded.
- **"A lesion in connective tissue"**: wrong terminology. White matter is neural tissue, and "connective tissue" is a histological term for non-neural tissue. Changed to "the connecting white matter". The same bullet's "independently load-bearing" became "matters independently" (style guide).
- **Dualism paragraph strawman**: the old wording said the framework "constrains the simplest production reading" (one unified system should degrade diffusely). No current production theorist holds that view. The text now says openly that the framework rules out "the crudest production reading — one no current production theorist holds." Also, "channel-specific deficits" in a data-description clause assumed the channel reading, so it became "tract-specific". "Surgically cutting" was dropped because most disconnection lesions are strokes.
