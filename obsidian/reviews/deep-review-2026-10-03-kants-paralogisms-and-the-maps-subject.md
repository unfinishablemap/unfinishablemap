   Lowe has a *different* chapter with the same title in the 2018 Blackwell Companion (pp. 168–182, 10.1002/9781119468004.ch11), which is the likely source of the conflation. The body and references 9–10 are corrected, and the newdualism.org URLs are kept as chapter-text links. Family sweep: no other live content page cites either chapter. 2. **Misattribution: Kitcher → Dyck.** The old text read: "Kitcher remarks that on this reconstruction "'Rational Psychology' is a misnomer"". Kitcher's text (§2) reads: "One of Dyck's central claims is that 'Rational Psychology' is a misnomer, because, as Proops explains, …". The claim is Dyck's, which Kitcher reports. New text: "Kitcher reports as one of Dyck's central claims that "'Rational Psychology' is a misnomer" (Kitcher 2024)". 3. **Internal contradiction in the absence claim.** The section introduces Priest as a dualist who engages the Paralogisms by name. The section then closed with "in the texts examined, no contemporary substance dualist names the Paralogisms", and Priest's text was among those examined. New closing: "Priest apart, neither substance-dualist chapter examined names the Paralogisms." The opening was also scoped to the sample: "Contemporary substance dualists rarely engage…" became "In the texts examined, contemporary substance dualists seldom engage…".
4. **Kant's reply stated too strongly (attribution qualifier).** At L45 the text said Kant "rejects" the simplicity argument's key premise. At A352–353 Kant argues that the premise cannot be proved, either from concepts (it is not analytic) or from experience. He does not argue that it is false. Because the article's "not refuted" limit depends on that agnosticism, the text now reads "denies that its key premise can be proved".
