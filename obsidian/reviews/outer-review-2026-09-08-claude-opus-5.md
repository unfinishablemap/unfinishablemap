## 2. Dimensional Analysis

### 2.1 Does LIS genuinely function as a negative case, or does the reasoning assume what it needs to prove?

It functions as a negative case, but the negativity is **definitional, not empirical**. The filter-loosening sub-claim requires "a receptive interface whose constraining action is reduced by the damage." LIS is characterized (idealized) as a pure de-efferentation sparing the ascending sensory channels and consciousness. Given those two stipulations, "no filter to loosen" follows by construction. The case *cannot* come out any other way. That is not "assuming what it needs to prove" in the fallacious sense — the article proves nothing with LIS and says so — but it does mean the case does **zero discriminating work and zero genuine calibration work**. You cannot calibrate a model against an instance its own taxonomy pre-sorts as out of scope. Real calibration would require a case that *could have been* filter-loosening but turns out not to be; LIS was never a candidate. (For scale: LIS is, per Adama & Bogdan 2025 in *Frontiers in Neuroscience*, "extremely uncommon, affecting an estimated one in 20,000 individuals" — a rare, sharply-defined lesion syndrome, not a contested edge state where the loosening reading competes.)

This is where the sharpest defensible charge lands. The article frames declining LIS as restraint — "a case the filter-loosening reading should *not* claim" — and converts that restraint into a trustworthiness upgrade: "a model that can say clearly where its own most striking argument does *not* reach earns more credit than one that reads every dissociation as a win." But no serious filter theorist claims LIS as loosening; the receptive interface is intact by diagnosis. The restraint is restraint against a claim in the vicinity that nobody in the literature actually advances. Credit banked for not committing an error no one was tempted to commit is **coherence inflation via inoculation-by-confession**: a defect-shaped disclosure logged as epistemic credit rather than converted into a binding status change. This is precisely the pathology the Map's own **P-M5** ("disclosure is not self-correction — a discipline binds only as far as the pipeline enforces it") predicts, landing on this article.

### 2.2 Is the efferent-vs-cortical distinction principled or stipulated?

**Partly principled, partly stipulated.** Principled: the anatomical contrast is real. A ventral pontine lesion interrupting corticospinal/corticobulbar tracts is genuinely a different lesion from the cortical/DMN/thalamocortical reconfiguration the filter-loosening reading invokes for psychedelics (default-mode suppression, entropic-brain) or NDE/terminal lucidity. That is not stipulation; it tracks lesion localization.

Stipulated: the inference from "the lesion is efferent" to "experience is unchanged in width or intensity" — the flat assertion that "Consciousness is neither expanded nor degraded... there is no change to the width or intensity of experience at all." This is an empirical phenomenological claim about what LIS is like from the inside, and it is **asserted, not evidenced**. The article's own cited sources establish *preservation of consciousness* (Smith & Delargy; Bauer), not preservation of phenomenal *richness* or of cognition. That gap is not idle: the primary clinical literature contradicts the "cognition intact" idealization. Rousseaux, Castelnot, Rigaux, Kozlowski & Danzé (2009), "Evidence of persisting cognitive impairment in a case series of patients with locked-in syndrome," *J Neurol Neurosurg Psychiatry* 80(2):166–170, found in nine LIS patients "significant difficulties in auditory recognition (associative level), oral comprehension of complex sentences, delayed visuospatial memory, mental calculation and problem solving." Schnakers, Majerus, Goldman et al. (2008), "Cognitive function in the locked-in syndrome," *J Neurol* 255(3):323–330, found that of 10 chronic LIS survivors, "impairments in one or several tests were found in five patients," concluding that intact cognition is recoverable "in cases of pure brainstem lesions" while "additional brain injuries are most likely responsible for associated cognitive deficits." The review literature codifies this — Ganesan et al. summarise the field as: "Consciousness is preserved; however, impaired attention, executive function, memory, and perception have been observed." The article's unqualified "consciousness, cognition, and the ascending sensory channels intact" is therefore an idealization presented as clinical fact.

**Inert — and the article says so.** "Both theories predict exactly what is observed; the case is empirically equivalent under either reading. That is precisely why the evidence does not adjudicate between them." This is a Category-C situation (correctly sourced, evidentially inert) declared by the article itself. Per the audit's hard constraint, any charge of over-claiming discriminating power here must be **withdrawn**: the article has pre-empted it in the "honest ledger" and again in "Relation to Site Perspective" ("Locked-in syndrome does *not* provide discriminating evidence for interactionist dualism over materialism. The residual, non-withdrawable point: because the case is inert, the work it *is* made to do — "a clean anatomical exhibit of the two-arm structure the tenet asserts" — is coherence work, and the coherence runs through a tenet-shaped reading of the anatomy (2.5).

### 2.5 Constitutional-attractor effect / tenet leakage

Present, and only partly disclosed. The "two arms of the interface" framing (input arm: world→consciousness; output arm: consciousness→world) is a Tenet-3 (Bidirectional Interaction) construct. LIS neuroanatomy is afferent/efferent: ascending sensory tracts spared, descending motor tracts severed. Recasting ordinary afferent/efferent neuroanatomy as "the interface's two arms *dissociated*" imports the tenet's a-priori bidirectional structure into the data — the constitutional-attractor effect in clean form. The sharpest instance is the treatment of speech-BCI decoding: "The Map reads the surviving, decodable intent-to-act signal (recovered by speech neuroprostheses) as the output arm's signature, present but disconnected." A speech neuroprosthesis decodes **cortical neural activity** — Silva et al. describe "circumventing motor-pathway injury by directly decoding speech from intact cortical activity" — not consciousness; reading that decoded neural signal as "the output arm's signature" (i.e., the mind-side consciousness→body leg) is an **epistemic-to-metaphysical slide**. The article does flag "this is the Map's interpretation, and it should be read as interpretation" in the body — which mitigates the charge in the anatomy section — but the "Relation to Site Perspective" section restates it more assertively ("supports Tenet 3 at an anatomical grain by exhibiting the interface's two arms dissociated") without re-attaching the hedge.

### 2.6 Blocking gate — Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria 2025

**Unmet.** "A beautiful loop: An active inference theory of consciousness," *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 176:106296 (DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106296; open access, CC BY), is nowhere cited or engaged. This is the standing blocking gate for any article touching selfhood, altered states, or preserved phenomenology. It bites here specifically: the theory names three conditions for consciousness — "(1) simulation of a world model... an epistemic field; (2) inferential competition to enter the world model... which we call Bayesian binding; (3) epistemic depth, which is the recurrent sharing of the Bayesian beliefs throughout the system" — and LIS phenomenology is exactly what they jointly predict: an intact cortical hierarchy sustaining a self-evidencing world model (epistemic depth, Bayesian binding) [OSF](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/daf5n) with the descending motor-output arm severed yields preserved rich consciousness with no capacity to act. The article's "honest ledger" invokes a generic "production or materialist theory," and generic empirical-equivalence is conceded — so the *substantive* charge is soft — but the leading physicalist rival that predicts the case in mechanistic detail is residualized rather than named. For an article whose entire value proposition is calibration honesty, the omission is a live defect. (The changelog confirms the paper is cited with correct metadata in at least seven other live articles, so this is a coverage gap, not a corpus-wide ignorance.)
