## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would recognise CI's central asymmetry as his own distinction. "**we can refer to what we cannot conceive**" (CI L42) and the three levels at L46–L48 (linguistic reference, logical inference, phenomenal grasping) separate what the conceivability debate calls positive conception from the mere handling of a description. The Map already uses that asymmetry. `concepts/conceivability-possibility-inference` L78 states the confabulation objection with it ("Could zombie conceivability be similar—linguistic reference without genuine conceptual content?"), but it links the 2026-01-23 research note rather than the published article. CI also lists Chalmers (2002) as reference 4 (L177) without citing it anywhere in the body. The opening is to connect them both ways, and to do it honestly. The felt wall marks only overt contradictions, and the conceivability page itself says "many mathematical falsehoods seem conceivable until proven impossible" (L60). So the absence of blockage when imagining a zombie certifies nothing, and the asymmetry explains why the Map needs *ideal* positive conceivability (L29). Priority 1(c) puts this in place of CI's present Dualism paragraph.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

This wing has little physics in it, and Stapp would credit it for not pretending otherwise. QF L115 declines a No Many Worlds connection on the right ground: "decoherence operates at quantum granularity, not at pragmatic-linguistic granularity". CI L141 asks whether the quantum interface might constrain which concepts can stably form, and stops: "This remains speculative—the core point is that logical impossibility produces a distinctive phenomenology regardless of whether physical constraints also contribute." CM L116 makes the move Stapp would most respect. It uses Minimal Quantum Interaction to restrain the dualist reading rather than inflate it: "Since the Map's [[tenets#^minimal-quantum-interaction|minimalism]] assigns schema integration to the physical brain, the claim is modest". The tenet works as a brake here. CI L143 is the one exception (Priority 1(d)).

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

The wing's Nagelian centre is CM L54, which separates five things "going back" runs together: representational persistence, behavioural reactivation, perspective simulation, qualitative reinstatement and numerical recurrence. "Only the fourth is at issue here" (L56). That isolates the what-it-was-like face from everything the experimental record can measure without it, and L56 says so plainly: "no study cited above measured whether they could." CI L52–L69 gives a careful comparative phenomenology. Blockage differs from forgetting ("no content was ever present"), from difficulty ("almost grasping" against "cannot grasp"), from slippage ("Impossibilities never form—only the linguistic shell exists") and from the counterfactual horizon, a "wall" set against thinning. QF L74 draws the line Nagel would insist on: "The four forms below describe the inside of the limit, not signals from past it."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would value CM's treatment of understanding as an event that remakes the one who understands. In the article's sense, metabolism "names a change in the knower's access conditions rather than the destruction of what was known before" (L38), and the article ends: "The mapper changes the map by mapping, and itself in the process" (L122). MV L58 gives Ricoeur's semantic innovation the same creative-advance shape. New metaphors reveal what old ones occlude, and of the void the article says: "This does not escape the void; it navigates within it." QF L54 reads Gadamer's horizon as something that "can fuse with other horizons but never be escaped". These pages concern no organism's tier, so no evidential-status upgrade is at stake. The constraint still applies in one place, though. This persona's praise attaches to the descriptions. It must not carry into MV L96's step from "every mapping … must distort" to "a possible signature of dualism", which is where the Hardline Empiricist objects.

**Calibration concerns (flagged, not praised).** Here the Process Philosopher's and the Hardline Empiricist's readings conflict. Under this skill's contract that means possibility-to-probability slippage, and it generates refine-draft tasks:

1. **MV L96, "a possible signature of dualism".** The argument is that if consciousness is a distinct domain, "every mapping from the physical or spatial world to the phenomenal world must distort", so the metaphor void is "a possible signature of dualism". The page's own L38 removes the premise that would make it a signature. Metaphor-dependence is therefore domain-general and does not single out consciousness. A physicalist predicts the void as readily as a dualist, which is P-V1's non-discrimination point. MV L72 had already drawn the right line for the mapping ("not as standalone evidence for any metaphysical thesis"), and L96 does not keep to it. MV's last deep review (07-19) did not touch L96.
