# Adversarial Audit: "The Ethics of Cognitive Enhancement Under Dualism"

*Peer-review-level adversarial referee report. Target: [topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism/) (last modified 2026-08-13; audited against the site changelog current to 2026-08-21, the positions register, the tenets, and the site's own calibration benchmarks apex #33 and #34).*

## 1. TL;DR Verdict

**REVISE-HARD.** The article is unusually disciplined for enhancement-ethics writing about *flagging* where its metaphysics does and does not do inferential work — the identity and equity sections openly concede that bare dualism under-determines their conclusions. But the central promise of the title and lede — that treating the brain as an interface "transforms the ethical landscape" — is not delivered. Four of the five distinct normative conclusions are reachable by a physicalist on the identical argumentative route; only the quantum-interface "design constraint" is genuinely dualism-entailed, and it rests on the framework's most empirically inert, mechanism-debt-laden premise. The article silently reinvents the Harris "freedom-to-fall" debate as two "positions" it appears to discover for itself, never confronts Levy's ethical-parity principle (a direct refutation of its core interface/substrate distinction), and ignores the entire contemporary moral-status literature while making moral-status-adjacent claims.

- **Does dualism do load-bearing work?** Mostly no. The identity conclusion runs on indexical/closed-individualist identity (P-I1), not dualism; the responsibility and consent conclusions are standard physicalist-available fare; the equity conclusion runs on phenomenal sentientism (the moral-status register), not dualism. Only the quantum-interface constraint is dualism-specific — and it is speculative by the article's own admission.
- **Citations:** metadata clean (four external sources + one Everettian rival, all real and correctly attributed); the Wallace citation is exemplary rival-holding. The failures are by *omission* — no empirical enhancement-efficacy literature, no moral-status literature — plus one mild co-optation risk on Sandel.
- **Biggest single defect:** a live, named, decisive rival to the article's core thesis (Levy's parity principle) is never confronted, and the article's own "interface dependence" section is an uncredited rediscovery of Harris (2011).

## 2. Dimensional Analysis

### FIRST — Entailment audit: does dualism do load-bearing inferential work?

The article draws five distinct normative/evaluative conclusions. Assessed one at a time:

**(1) Identity: enhancement changes "what you can do," not "who you are."** Dualism is **not** load-bearing, and the article says so itself: "Irreducibility alone does not settle it… The claim that the fundamental subject survives rests on a further commitment the Map adopts explicitly: that *which* subject one is—indexical identity—is individuated independently of the interface's configuration." The work is done by **P-I1 (closed individualism / haecceitist indexical identity)** — which the site's own register flags as *failing* the "foundational-dependency test" because it silently supplies an argument Tenet 4 needs. A physicalist animalist (Olson) or a haecceitist physicalist reaches the identical "you survive radical cognitive change" verdict with no substance dualism. Worse, there is an unbridged **metaphysical-to-normative slide**: from "the subject numerically persists" the article infers *reassurance* ("a stronger foundation for personal continuity"), but numerical persistence is not itself an ethical good — the reassurance needs a value premise (that the continued existence of *this* subject is what matters) that is neither stated nor argued here. [topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism/)

**(2) Moral responsibility under enhancement.** Dualism is **decorative**. The "interface neutrality vs. interface dependence" dichotomy — is choosing kindness less admirable when aggression has been pharmacologically removed? — is the textbook debate about the relation between the *difficulty* of virtue and its moral worth. It is fully available to, and was in fact developed by, physicalist compatibilists. Neither agent causation nor the quantum interface adds a premise; the only dualism-specific content is deferred to the quantum section. The claim that agent causation "suggests a nuanced position between these poles" does no discriminating work: both poles survive translation into physicalist vocabulary unchanged.

**(3) The consent problem.** Dualism is **decorative and arguably backwards**. The article concedes "This concern exists under any metaphysics" and then claims "dualism sharpens it," because configuring the interface configures "the parameters of their experiential life." But a physicalist thinks configuring the brain configures the mind *directly and constitutively* — at least as strong a ground for the consent worry, plausibly stronger. The "sharpening" is asserted, not argued; the metaphysics changes the *metaphor* (channel vs. seat), not the normative stakes.

**(4) Equity and access.** Dualism is **not** load-bearing; the article, to its credit, largely admits this. The strong premise ("all conscious subjects possess the same fundamental capacity for experience and agency") is explicitly withdrawn as "stronger than the Map can defend," and the conclusion is conditionalised. But the surviving conditional ("subjects whose fundamental standing may be equal") is carried by an **equal-moral-standing** premise that lives in the moral-status register (phenomenal sentientism), not in dualism. Peter Singer reaches equal consideration of interests across cognitive-capacity differences by the same sentientist route with no dualism whatever. The article even names the load-bearing move honestly ("booking all such variation to the interface, an accounting move that should be made explicitly") — this is the one place it actively resists the constitutional-attractor effect. [topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism/)

**(5) The quantum-interface design constraint.** This is the **only** genuinely dualism-entailed normative conclusion: "enhancement that improves performance while narrowing conscious agency would be… a net loss." It follows from Tenets 2 and 3 *if* the empirical antecedent (consciousness acts at specific, spatially locatable biological sites an implant could bypass) holds. But (a) the article concedes it is "speculative," resting on an unspecified mechanism (the site's own **P-Q10 mechanism debt**); and (b) it commits a further **is–ought / metaphysical-to-normative slide**: from "enhancement narrows the domain of conscious causal action" to "this is a net loss" requires the value premise that conscious authorship is intrinsically valuable — imported here without statement (registered elsewhere as P-VS3 but not cited at point of use). So the one conclusion dualism actually entails is the one standing on the framework's weakest, empirically undetectable leg. [topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism/)

**Entailment verdict:** the dualist framing is decorative in four of five conclusions and load-bearing in the fifth only at the price of resting on an empirically inert premise. The lede's claim that the interface reframing "transforms the ethical landscape" is not established; on the article's own showing it re-describes the landscape without moving any normative furniture except in the speculative quantum section.

- **Tenet 1 (dualism) vs. enhancing a non-physical mind by modifying a physical substrate.** The escape — "enhancement modifies the *interface*, not the mind" — is coherent only on a **filter-theory** reading (consciousness has latent range being throttled that a nootropic can "widen"). Bare Tenet 1 (irreducibility) does **not** entail filter theory; the "production" reading is equally compatible with dualism. The article concedes the altered-states data "does not discriminate this reading from production accounts," yet the entire enhancement reframe presupposes the filter reading. This is **tenet inflation / constitutional-attractor effect**: a further, contested commitment is read into enhancement as though dualism licensed it. ### FOURTH — Unconfronted live positions

The article confronts Sandel, Savulescu, Bostrom–Sandberg, and Wallace. It never confronts the following, several of which are decisive:

- **Levy, ethical parity principle (most damaging).** Levy (2007), *Neuroethics: Challenges for the 21st Century* (Cambridge UP), states the principle: "Unless we can identify ethically relevant differences between internal and external interventions and alterations, we ought to treat them on a par" (p. 62; EPP-weak, p. 61). This is a direct denial of the article's founding move — that whether you modify "the seat" or "the channel" transforms the ethics. *Demand:* show the interface/substrate distinction tracks a morally relevant difference Levy's functionalism misses. *Survives?* No — the article never makes the argument; its thesis presupposes the parity principle is false without engaging it.
- **Harris, "freedom to fall" (uncredited rediscovery).** The article's "interface dependence" position — choosing kindness when aggression is a live temptation is morally different from choosing it when the temptation is pharmacologically removed — *is* Harris's freedom-to-fall objection: Harris, J. (2011), "Moral Enhancement and Freedom," *Bioethics* 25(2):102–111, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8519.2010.01854.x. It is presented as a novel "position" with no citation. *Demand:* engage Harris, the Persson & Savulescu God-Machine reply (2011, "Getting Moral Enhancement Right," *Bioethics*, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8519.2011.01907.x: "effective moral bioenhancement presupposes moral reasoning rather than undermines it… the God Machine would leave us with extensive freedom"), Pugh's parity critique (2019, "Moral Bio-enhancement, Freedom, Value and the Parity Principle," *Topoi*, DOI 10.1007/s11245-017-9482-8), and Douglas on moral enhancement. *Survives?* Only by accident; the article restates one side of a live debate without knowing it is in one.
- **Persson & Savulescu, *Unfit for the Future* / moral bioenhancement.** The article's own example (a drug that "eliminates impulsive aggression") *is* moral bioenhancement, yet P&S's existential-risk "urgent imperative" argument is absent. *Demand:* weigh the interface-dependence worry against the claim that bioenhancement may be a moral imperative. *Survives?* Not addressed.
- **The moral-status literature en masse** — McMahan (the challenge of moral status; the sliding scale), the Singer–Kagan exchange on hierarchy, Jaworska & Tannenbaum (grounds of status), Liao (genetic-basis/rights-based account), Shepherd (consciousness and moral status), Birch (edge of sentience, precautionary framework), DeGrazia (enhancement, authenticity, identity). The equity section asserts equal fundamental standing and the subject matter is status-adjacent, yet none is cited. *Most pointed omission:* the article never asks whether enhancement that increases the capacity for valenced experience could *raise a subject's moral status* — which, under the Map's own phenomenal-sentientism register (`positions/moral-status`), it straightforwardly would. A dualist enhancement ethics that ignores enhancement's effect on moral status has skipped its central question.
- **The conservative / grown-vs-made line:** Habermas (ethics of the species, grown/made), Kass and the President's Council, Buchanan (*Beyond Humanity*, the "personal good" of enhancement), Agar (liberal eugenics; later species-relativism), Parens (gift/creativity framing), Schaefer–Kahane–Savulescu (autonomy), Earp–Sandberg–Savulescu (relationship/"love drug" enhancement), Jotterand (enhancement and moral status) — all absent. Bostrom & Sandberg's own "wisdom of nature" / evolutionary-optimality heuristic (2009) is the naturalistic cousin of the article's "design constraint" caution and is uncited even though the article cites the same authors' other paper.
- **Predictive processing / active inference (documented site-wide blind spot):** Clark, Friston, Seth, Hohwy, and Laukkonen–Friston–Chandaria (2025). Active inference offers a *fully physicalist* account of how modifying the substrate modifies both cognitive capacity and phenomenal character (via precision-weighting) — which would explain the article's own "enhancement predicts phenomenal change" claim (challenge #2) with no interface/filter metaphysics at all. This is a direct rival to the filter reading that the article never names, even though the site has already begun integrating Laukkonen elsewhere (`apex/altered-states-as-interface-evidence`, `apex/contemplative-path`, per the changelog). *Survives?* The filter reframe is left undefended against the strongest naturalistic competitor.
- **Chalmers (virtual/digital minds; the hard problem's ethical implications)** and **Schwitzgebel & Garza (AI moral status)** — absent; less central here, but relevant the moment the article invokes BCIs and "new hardware."

**Calibration against the benchmarks.** Apex #34 ("Born-Preserving Causal Efficacy") earns its causal-efficacy claim only by explicitly conceding it is "citable as a framework-internal coherence result, not as established mental causation," naming the bias-without-deviation dilemma and the mechanism debt in its own voice, and treating Kim and the phenomenal-concept strategy (Loar, Papineau, Frankish) at full strength as rivals rather than recruiting them. The target article meets neither standard: it neither concedes its central interface thesis is coherence-only (it asserts it *transforms* the landscape), nor holds its strongest rivals (Levy, active inference) at strength — it does not name them at all. ## 3. Bottom-Line Verdict

**REVISE-HARD.**

- Not **RETAIN**: the central "transforms the ethical landscape" thesis is oversold and rests on decorative dualism in four of five conclusions, and the literature gaps (Levy, Harris, the entire moral-status corpus) are severe enough to be disqualifying at a peer-reviewed venue.
- Not **DELETE** or **DEMOTE-TO-COHERENCE-ONLY**: the interface-design-constraint argument is a genuine, if speculative, dualism-specific contribution, and the identity and equity sections model exactly the premise-flagging discipline the site prizes — there is a real article here once the decorative claims are cut and the rivals are confronted.
- Not **FLAG AS PERPETUALLY CONTESTED**: the defects are remediable, not deadlocked.

REVISE-HARD is the correct register. **Threshold that would change the verdict downward to DEMOTE-TO-COHERENCE-ONLY:** if, on revision, the author cannot produce a single normative conclusion that (a) dualism entails and (b) a physicalist cannot reach by the same route *and* survives Levy's parity principle, then the article's distinctive claim collapses and it should be demoted to a coherence-only exposition of "how the interface metaphor redescribes enhancement." **Threshold that would raise it toward RETAIN:** confrontation of Levy, Harris/Persson–Savulescu, active inference, and the moral-status question, plus demotion of the lede to the claim the body actually supports.

## 4. Article-Specific Concrete Fixes (keyed to slugs)

**To `topics/ethics-of-cognitive-enhancement-under-dualism`:**

1. **Demote the lede.** "This distinction transforms the ethical landscape" overclaims. Replace with the honest claim the body supports: the interface reading changes the *description* of enhancement and generates *one* distinctive (speculative) constraint; on four of five conclusions it is co-extensive with physicalism. State this in the article's own voice.
2. **Add a Levy section.** Confront the ethical-parity principle head-on; either show the interface/substrate distinction tracks a morally relevant difference parity misses, or concede that on responsibility/consent/equity the article's conclusions are parity-compatible (i.e., not dualism-specific).
3. **Credit and engage Harris.** Rename the "interface dependence" position the freedom-to-fall objection; cite Harris (2011), Persson & Savulescu (God-Machine reply, 2011), Pugh (2019), and Douglas; frame the two "positions" as the existing debate, not a discovery.

6. **Add an active-inference rival paragraph** (Clark/Friston/Seth/Hohwy; Laukkonen–Friston–Chandaria 2025) as the naturalistic competitor to the filter reading, mirroring the treatment already installed at `apex/altered-states-as-interface-evidence`.
7. **Ground the efficacy claims.** Cite Greely et al. (2008) and Roberts et al. (2020); replace "doubles working memory" with an evidence-scaled example and state plainly that current pharmacological effects in healthy adults are small (modafinil SMD ≈ 0.12; MPH ≈ 0.21; d-amphetamine null).
8. **Turn Tenet 5 both ways.** Apply the razor to the article's own added machinery (filter reading, quantum design constraint), as the site's own Occam discipline requires, not only to materialist bioethics.
9. **Repair the Tenet-2 locality import:** either register a "consciousness acts at localizable sites" claim in the quantum-interface positions and grade it, or withdraw the "implant bypasses the sites" argument to what the register actually licenses.

## 5. Site-Wide Methodology Improvements

1. **A parity-principle gate for all "dualism changes the ethics" applied articles.** Any applied piece claiming a metaphysical thesis *transforms* a normative debate should be required to state, per conclusion, whether a physicalist reaches it by the same route — a structured entailment ledger. This article would have failed the gate on four of five conclusions, and the failure would have surfaced pre-publication rather than in review.
2. **A "reinvented literature" check.** The uncredited rediscovery of Harris's freedom-to-fall argument shows the generation/review pipeline is not cross-checking novel-seeming "positions" against the canonical debate in the target subfield. A required "is this argument already named in the literature?" search step, keyed to the article's subject tags (here: enhancement ethics), would catch this class of error.
3. **Author-stance firewall for co-opted arguments, not just co-opted authors.** The Sandel case shows the co-optation risk can attach to an *argument* (giftedness) recruited into a frame its author would reject, even when the author is not miscited. Extend the existing rival-or-support field to flag "argument recruited across metaphysical camps."
4. **Efficacy-evidence floor for enhancement/clinical topics.** Applied articles invoking a technology's effects should cite at least one publisher-of-record efficacy source and scale hypotheticals to it, closing the "confident hypothetical, absent evidence" gap that let "doubles working memory" stand unchallenged.
5. **Cross-cluster blind-spot enforcement.** The predictive-processing / active-inference omission recurs here despite the site's own tune-system flagging it as a documented blind spot; a linter that flags filter/interface articles lacking an active-inference rival paragraph would close it systematically.

**Qualified — the parity principle fits less tightly than the review claims.**
