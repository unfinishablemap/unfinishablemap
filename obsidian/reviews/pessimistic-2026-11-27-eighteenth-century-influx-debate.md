## Executive Summary

All 29 quoted source spans in the page are verbatim at source, and so are the two Sherrington spans added on 11-15. The defects are in paraphrase, chronology and internal consistency. Four stand out:

1. **The closing section misstates Knutzen's concession.** Knutzen said the "very specific mode" of mind-body influx "cannot be distinctly understood". Watkins calls this "a certain degree of skepticism" about "a detailed solution". The page turns this into "the mechanism could not be understood", and in the Relation section into "what Knutzen declared could not be attempted: a specification of how the influence works". The page itself reports Knutzen's positive account of the influence at L69. The page also fails to carry the hub's newer qualifier that the Map "narrows that horn's gap without leaving it".
2. **The page misdescribes Lorini.** It says Lorini treats "the shift" as the organising fact, and it counts him as one of "two specialist historians who take it as given". Lorini's chapter is organised around the *dispute*, dates a *pro-harmony* "turning point" to 1739, and never rules on the outcome. So the stated evidence for the reversal comes down to Watkins alone.
3. **The institutional strand has a chronology error and leaves out part of its own source.** "In the intervening seventeen years Knutzen held his chair". Knutzen was born in 1713 and took the chair in 1735. The Stanford entry, which the page quotes for this strand, also reports that the old king tried to bring Wolff back in 1733 and lifted the teaching ban in 1736. The page omits both facts.
4. **The "Not Empirical" strand contradicts the page.** That strand is the one the Map rates its most confident. It says harmony and influx were "equivalent about every observable event". Yet the page's own L97 says harmony needs "no exception to conservation", and L67 reports Knutzen denying that conservation reaches the soul. A further sentence (L67) also says the inherited conservation argument "assumed the Cartesian quantity of motion". The Map's own [[causal-closure-debate-historical-survey]] says Leibniz's version rested on *vis viva* and conserved direction.

No defect touches the tenets badly. Tenet 3 is graded as compatibility, Tenet 5's symmetric self-binding is honoured, and the page concedes no epiphenomenalism in the Map's own voice.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would grant the page its central concession at L93, that nothing in Halle or Königsberg makes interaction more likely to be true. She would then press harder than the page does. Its own reconstruction makes the 1723–1755 reversal sociology: an edict, a theological worry and a change of terminology. If so, the Tenet 3 paragraph's one evidential residue goes too. That residue is the claim that the episode makes interaction's "obvious non-starter" status "harder to hold". The influxionists were not answering modern energy conservation or causal closure. They were answering an objection that Gottsched could still meet on "the Cartesian quantity of motion". Bedrock beyond that. The page already concedes most of this.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would aim at the four-strand tally. The page lists theology, coercion, redefinition and "not empirical", and its Tenet 5 paragraph names "a theological argument about imputability, a royal edict, and a redefinition". The *arguments* are missing from that list. Knutzen derived influx from the Leibnizian monad's own force (Lorini 2016, citing §28), and Rauscher reports Watkins (2005) presenting Knutzen as arguing that influx "is actually required by Leibniz's own principles". Kant's 1755 principle of succession "explicitly aims to establish physical influx by refuting pre-established harmony" (Watkins 2003, p. 11), and the page quotes that line itself. These are first-order philosophical reasons. A list that leaves them out makes the victory look more extra-rational than the page's own evidence shows. The Tenet 5 conclusion survives, because none of those arguments turned on simplicity. The "redefinition, not refutation" verdict at L73 is weaker than stated (Issue 5).

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark has little to attack. The page refuses Knutzen's inertia reply as "not a reply the Map can use" (L95). His one point of entry is the 11-15 clause at L99. That clause says the einselected menu is factorisation-relative "until the programme says which system/environment split it belongs to". The sibling [[quantum-factorisation-problem]] is more exact: the split-relativity "is shared with every decoherence-based account", and "the Map's own debt is which split is the paired subject's". The page's version makes a general decoherence fact look like the Map's particular stall, and it leaves out the Map-specific debt. Low.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Nothing on the page bears on Tenet 4. N/A.

### The Empiricist
A Popperian would welcome the "Not Empirical" strand and then find it inconsistent. The page says the two theories "were, and remained, equivalent about every observable event", and that this "follows from the structure of the two theories". On Knutzen's reply, though, the conservation law "cannot hold for the soul", so influx predicts non-conserving motion in a body moved by a soul. That is a difference observable in principle, even if nobody measured it. The page's own L97 says harmony needs "no exception to conservation", which implies that influx does need one. The page has slipped from "no observation decided" (a historical-epistemic claim it can support) to "the theories are observationally equivalent" (a structural claim that its own Knutzen material contradicts). That is the equivocation the skill's epistemic/metaphysical check describes, in miniature (Issue 4). The Stanford entry would give the strand a historical source the page says it lacks: Wolff treated each of the three systems as "a mere hypothesis" because "any possible explanation cannot be confirmed or rejected by experience".

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would say that the soul both camps defend is a reification, and that "influx" and "harmony" are two stories about a relation between things that do not exist as relata. The page is a reception history, it does not argue for substance, and this is bedrock. One point lands. The page's L83 Paralogisms sentence reports Kant's own deflation of the "simple, persisting soul" as a coda. It never notes that the *A-edition* Paralogisms chapter also revisits the three commercium systems (A390 onward). That is the influx debate's direct sequel inside the *Critique*. I am going on memory here: Project Gutenberg was down this run, so this is not verified, and it is offered as a lead only.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Knutzen's concession is stated more strongly than the source, and it contradicts L69
- **File**: `topics/eighteenth-century-influx-debate.md`
- **Location**: L35 ("the mechanism he had won the argument for could not be understood"), L87 ("Knutzen, the leading influxionist … conceded in print that the mechanism could not be understood") and L99 ("The quantum-selection proposal attempts what Knutzen declared could not be attempted: a specification of how the influence works").
- **Problem**:
  - **Paraphrase fidelity.** The source sentence (Watkins 2003, p. 10 n. 9, translating Knutzen 1745 §42) says only "the very specific mode" of mind-body influx "cannot be *distinctly* understood". Watkins's body text calls this "a certain degree of skepticism about our ability to understand the specific causal mechanisms that pertain to mind-body interaction in particular and thus about a detailed solution to the heterogeneity problem". The page drops "very specific", "distinctly" and "detailed solution", and promotes "a certain degree of skepticism" to a declaration that the attempt is impossible.
  - **Internal contradiction.** L69 reports Knutzen's positive specification: a simple substance's force to move itself implies a force to move other things, supported by impenetrability (Lorini 2016, citing §§28–29). Watkins p. 10 says the same. So Knutzen did attempt a specification, just not of the very specific mode.
  - **Chronology.** L35's "he had won the argument" is premature for 1745. The page's own L41 has Baumgarten's 1739 counter-attack and Meier's 1743 defence still live.
  - **Tentative claim made flat.** "The leading influxionist" flattens Lorini's hedged "Knutzen and Reusch are maybe the finest holders of physical influx".
  - **Sibling divergence.** [[history-of-the-interaction-problem]] L95 now says that the Map's own attempt "narrows that horn's gap without leaving it", and its closing acknowledgment says the gap "is narrower, though not closed". The influx page frames the Map as having left the horn ("differs … at precisely the point where he stopped").
- **Severity**: High. The closing section's contrast ("cannot" against "not yet") survives the correction, because L99's later sentence already keeps "distinctly". What fails is the framing that sets up that contrast.
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 1.

### Issue 2: The evidence base for the reversal misdescribes Lorini
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L39 ("Lorini (2016) treats the shift as the organising fact of the period"; "two specialist historians who take it as given") and L33 (the "historiography the Map relies on" parenthetical).
- **Problem**:
  - Lorini's abstract and §1 state the chapter's aim as "an overview of the debate in the period between C. Wolff and A.G. Baumgarten" and "to evaluate the different positions emerging in the dispute between pre-established harmony and physical influx".
  - Its §5 opens: "The year 1739 marks a turning point in the debate about the causal theories thanks to the first edition of Baumgarten's Metaphysica. Baumgarten's main goal is to turn back to a Leibniz-oriented version of pre-established harmony."
  - The chapter never says that influx prevailed, or that there was a net shift. Its final remarks are about how Wolff and Baumgarten received harmony.
  - So the page's own two-tier calibration rests its lower tier ("documented shift") on one historian, Watkins. It knows Watkins's position through a title and a review summary, plus his 2003 Kant paper.
  - L33's parenthetical also lists Watkins 1995, which is unread, as part of the historiography on which "the direction of travel is settled".
- **Rider**: L41 gives Meier's 1743 title as *Beweis der vorherbestimmten Harmonie*. Lorini's bibliography, the page's only cited source for Meier, gives *Beweis der vorherbestimmten Übereinstimmung* (Halle: Hemmerde, 1743). German Wikipedia's list of Meier's works gives the same title, for a Halle 1752 edition. No source I reached gives the "Harmonie" form, which seems to have come from the research note.
- **Severity**: High. This is the page's headline claim, and LLM readers take the lead's "settled" at face value.
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 2.

### Issue 3: The institutional strand has a chronology error and is selective about its source
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L59.
- **Problem**:
  - "In the intervening seventeen years Knutzen held his chair in Pietist Königsberg." Knutzen was born in 1713, which Crossref confirms through the title of Kuehn's Bloomsbury dictionary entry, "Knutzen, Martin (1713–51)". He was made extraordinary professor in 1735 (English and German Wikipedia, and the research note L102). So he held the chair for five of the seventeen years, and he was ten years old in 1723.
  - The Stanford entry is the source this paragraph quotes, and it also reports something the page leaves out: "Friedrich Wilhelm I eventually thought better of his precipitous action against Wolff, as he attempted in 1733 to entice him (unsuccessfully) back to Halle and in 1736 lifted a prohibition he had enacted against the teaching of Wolffian texts." Leaving that out makes the coercion look like seventeen unbroken years, which strengthens the strand beyond what its own source says.
  - The influx works of 1733–35 (Gottsched's *Erste Gründe*, Knutzen, Reusch) coincide with the thaw.
  - Harmony's committed defenders published at Halle, in Prussia, in 1739 (Baumgarten, Halle: Hemmerde) and 1743 (Meier, Halle: Hemmerde) (Lorini's bibliography). The edict did not silence them.
  - "Its leading professor" also contradicts L43's "Wolff was its lukewarm patron".
- **Severity**: Medium-high. This is a factual error, and the omission inflates one of the four strands that the Tenet 5 paragraph relies on ("a royal edict").
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 3. The fix drops the false Knutzen sentence, adds the 1733 and 1736 facts, and changes "leading" to "best-known". The Halle-publication point goes to the counterarguments below. It is not added to the prose, to keep the fix cheap.

### Issue 4: The "Not Empirical" strand contradicts the page's own conservation material, and the conservation paragraph contradicts a sibling
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L51 ("equivalent about every observable event") and L67 ("The conservation argument as inherited from the seventeenth century assumed the Cartesian quantity of motion").
- **Problem**:
  - **L51.** See the Empiricist critique above. The claim is false on Knutzen's version (L67), and L97's "no exception to conservation" contradicts it. Wolff himself objected that influx "conflicts with the laws of physics" (SEP, citing RP §§578–9), which is a physics-based discriminator, whether or not anyone measured it.
  - **L67.** [[causal-closure-debate-historical-survey]] L52 says Leibniz argued that "what nature conserves is *vis viva* … not the scalar quantity of motion", and "from the 1690s he added the point that closes the loophole: the total direction of motion … is conserved too (*Monadology* §80)". The hub L91 frames the objection in terms of momentum. So the canonical seventeenth-century conservation argument, the one that motivated harmony, did *not* assume the Cartesian quantity. The Cartesian framing is how the objection reached Gottsched. Lorini says so: "Against the alleged violation of the law of conservation of the quantity of motion implicated by the physical influx …".
- **Omission**: Lorini reports a second step in Gottsched's reply, which is the step that actually meets the corrected law: "Then he adds that the physical influx between soul and body does not contradict even the correct formulation of the law, since the vis viva (motrix) in the world is always the same, whether the action of the soul on the body (or vice versa) is actually expressed or impeded by external actions (cf. 2, § 14-15)". This comes from Gottsched's *Vindiciae systematis influxus physici* (1727–29). The page does not cite that work, and its sequence at L41 starts Gottsched at 1733. Lorini also dates Rüdiger's influxionist *Gegen-Meinung* to 1727. The page's "as far as it can be reconstructed from Lorini" sequence leaves out both of Lorini's earliest influx defences.
- **Severity**: Medium-high for L51, an internal contradiction in the strand the Map rates "most confident". Medium for L67.
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 4 (L51 and L67). The Gottsched second step and the *Vindiciae* date cost about 25 words, so they are listed below the cap.

### Issue 5: "Won by redefinition, not by refutation" leaves out the influxionists' arguments
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L73 and the list in L97.
- **Problem**: See the Physicalist critique. The page itself reports three positive arguments: Knutzen's argument from the Leibnizian monad's force (§28), the reading of Leibniz and Wolff's own grounds that Watkins 1998 reports via Lorini, and Kant's principle of succession. Rauscher's review, a page source, summarises Watkins 2005 as presenting Knutzen arguing that influx "is actually required by Leibniz's own principles". That is stronger than L71's "a permissible interpretation". The German Wikipedia works list gives the 1745 *Systema*'s subtitle as *Ipsis Illustris Leibnitii Principiis Superstructa*. That source is not authoritative, so this is a lead only. An argument from the opponent's own premises is refutation on the opponent's terms. A further counterweight: SEP glosses Wolff's own definition of influx (RP 1734 §§558–60) as already deflated, and Wolff still objected to it on physics grounds (§§578–9). The deflated influx therefore still had an objection aimed at it, so "until the objections no longer had a target" (L63) overstates the case.
- **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: Below the cap, given length. The cheapest repair is to add "and arguments, such as Kant's from the conditions of change, that turned on grounds rather than simplicity" to the L97 list (about +16 words). L73's "On the Map's reconstruction" hedge should then read "chiefly by redefinition".

### Issue 6: The Tenet 5 lesson is labelled framework-conditional but is framework-neutral
- **File**: same
- **Location**: L97: "This is a framework-conditional implication: it follows from Tenet 5's premise that …".
- **Problem**: The lesson is that community convergence is weak evidence of what drove it. That is a historical and inductive claim, and it does not follow from "simplicity is not a reliable guide to truth when knowledge is incomplete". The page's own last sentence shows the lesson is neutral: a reader who trusts parsimony can accept it. Calling it a consequence of Tenet 5 makes it look derived from the tenet it is offered to illustrate. Tenet 5's self-binding ([[tenets]] L145, L147) is honoured everywhere else on the page, including the symmetric clause.
- **Severity**: Low-medium. The error runs toward modesty, but the deep review's stability note ratified it, so it will be re-ratified unless it is flagged.
- **Recommendation**: Below the cap. A draft is in the Language table.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The coercive strand's timing
- **Current content says**: harmony lost partly because "its leading professor" was exiled for seventeen years.
- **A critic would argue**: the exile cannot explain harmony's decline. The old king recanted in part by 1733–36. Harmony's committed defenders published at Halle in 1739 and 1743. Wolff himself was a lukewarm partisan of harmony. The strand explains Wolff's fall better than harmony's.
- **Suggested response**: Keep the strand, but rank it as a contributing pressure on the timing rather than a cause of the outcome. Priority item 3 installs the 1733 and 1736 facts. The rest is a later refine.

### Contemporary witnesses point both ways on dominance
- **Current content says**: dominance is underdetermined by the evidence the page has seen.
- **A critic would argue** (in both directions):
  - The page has, but does not use, a contemporary witness *for* near-triumph. Kant in 1747 says that "All that kept a certain acute author from making the triumph of physical influx over pre-established harmony complete was nothing more than this little confusion of concepts" (Ak. 1:20–21, in Watkins 2003, p. 9; Kuehn reads the "acute author" as Knutzen, n. 7). Watkins's note 4 credits Kant translations to the 1992 and 1998 Cambridge volumes, and neither contains the *True estimation*, so the English is in effect Watkins's.
  - A 1749 title, by Ant. Sparmann, *Gründ- und deutlicher Begriff von der Natürlichen Freyheit* (Frankfurt and Leipzig; Internet Archive item `11761517bsb`, from the BSB scan), speaks of "der nunmehro schon durchgehends herrschenden Lehr-Sätze von denen Monaden, der vorherbestimmten Harmonie", that is, monads and harmony as the doctrines "now already prevailing throughout".
- **Suggested response**: Both are partisan title-page or polemical claims. Together they *support* the page's "underdetermined" verdict and would make it concrete. That would cost about +45 words, which is over budget, so it is listed only as an option. Only the title of the Sparmann work was read, not its text.
