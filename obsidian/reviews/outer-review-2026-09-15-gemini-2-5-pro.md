# Pre-Publication Referee Report: "Pragmatism's Path to Dualism" (Submission Review)

## I. Global Assessment and Methodological Malpractice

The manuscript under review presents a philosophically audacious, structurally elaborate, and ultimately indefensible synthesis. It seeks to enlist the methodological apparatus of classical American pragmatism (specifically the works of William James, Charles Sanders Peirce, and John Dewey), neo-pragmatism (Richard Rorty, Hilary Putnam), and constructive empiricism (Bas van Fraassen) to establish a non-reductive, interactionist dualist account of consciousness. The central thesis asserts that when pragmatist methodology is applied reflexively to its own evaluative criteria, it demonstrates that consciousness must possess an irreducible, non-physical "demand-character" or normative force, thereby arriving inevitably at substance dualism. The manuscript supplements this historical argument with an analysis of pragmatist quantum foundations (QBism, Healey, Brukner-Zeilinger, and Stapp), claiming these interpretations leave an "agent-shaped hole" that only dualism can adequately fill.

This project fails across multiple foundational dimensions, rendering it entirely unsuitable for publication in a top-tier philosophy journal. A forensic philosophical audit reveals a systematic pattern of historical revisionism, epistemological equivocation, and question-begging logic. The manuscript consistently appropriates anti-metaphysical, deflationary methodologies only to aggressively graft them onto a heavily inflated, realist metaphysics of substance dualism. In doing so, the text ignores contemporary scholarship on the cited figures, actively suppresses the reflexive consequences of its own arguments, and entirely omits the most prominent 2020s paradigms in the philosophy of mind that address these exact pragmatic criteria without resorting to dualism.

The manuscript operates not as a genuine work of philosophical inquiry, but as a reverse-engineered apologetic for a pre-determined ontological commitment. The following sections deconstruct the manuscript's empirical claims, its mishandling of citations, its glaring omissions of competing frameworks, and the logical inferences that justify outright rejection.

## II. Dimension A: Accuracy Against the 2020s Literature on Classical and Neo-Pragmatism

The manuscript’s treatment of its core historical figures—James, Peirce, Dewey, and Rorty—relies on highly selective, truncated readings that have been widely discredited by contemporary scholarship. The text isolates early or out-of-context quotations while aggressively ignoring the mature, systematized philosophical architectures these thinkers developed specifically to bypass the Cartesian dualism the manuscript espouses. The manuscript routinely argues that the founders of pragmatism "failed to see the implication of [their] own view," effectively claiming that four of the five founders of the tradition were insufficiently attentive to the logical consequences of their own methodologies.

### The Distortion of William James and the Erasure of Neutral Monism

The manuscript claims that William James established a "pragmatic opening" to dualism by contrasting the brain as a producer versus a transmitter of consciousness in his 1898 Ingersoll Lecture, "Human Immortality". While accurate regarding that specific lecture, the manuscript subsequently attempts to neutralize James's later, highly developed framework of radical empiricism (1904) by claiming it "preserves what the Map requires: the irreducibility of experience" and that the distinction between neutral monism and dualism is merely a "second-order question".

This reading fundamentally misrepresents radical empiricism and actively contradicts the 2020s consensus in Jamesian scholarship. In his seminal 1904 essay "Does 'Consciousness' Exist?", James explicitly rejected consciousness as a separate entity, substance, or non-physical property. He proposed that there is only one primal stuff in the world—"pure experience"—and that the distinction between mind and matter is not ontological, but functional and contextual.

To claim that James's neutral monism is functionally equivalent to substance dualism simply because it refuses to reduce experience to classical mechanics is an egregious category error. Neutral monism is explicitly a monistic metaphysics designed to eliminate the ontological chasm between mind and matter. By defining pure experience as intrinsically neither mental nor physical, James dissolves the very boundary the manuscript's interactionist dualism strictly requires. Recent literature in the philosophy of science continually reaffirms that radical empiricism was formulated specifically to bypass the intractable paradoxes of dualistic interaction, making the manuscript's attempt to draft James into the dualist camp an exercise in historical cherry-picking.

Furthermore, the manuscript relies on James’s "The Will to Believe" (1896) to argue that the choice between materialism and dualism is a "forced, living, and momentous" option, thereby licensing belief based on human need. The application of James’s tripartite condition here is fundamentally flawed, particularly regarding the "forced" condition. In Jamesian terms, a forced option is one where suspending judgment carries the exact same practical cost as choosing wrongly. The ontological status of consciousness is the paradigm case of an unforced pragmatic choice. Suspending judgment on the mind-body problem carries no immediate behavioral cost; the substance dualist, the enactivist, and the strict materialist navigate their environments and raise their arms identically. The failure to recognize that suspension is practically cheap in this domain indicates a profound misunderstanding of Jamesian pragmatism, substituting a psychological desire for metaphysical certainty in place of genuine pragmatic necessity.

### The Truncation of Peircean Synechism

The manuscript's treatment of Charles Sanders Peirce is equally distorted. The text relies on Peirce's doctrine of *tychism* (absolute chance) to argue that Peirce opened an "ontological space for mental causation". The implication is that objective indeterminacy allows a non-physical mind to intervene in the physical world without violating causal closure.

This reading completely isolates *tychism* from the rest of Peirce's architectonic, most notably *synechism* (the principle of continuity) and his objective idealism. Peirce vehemently rejected Cartesian dualism. His evolutionary cosmology posits that matter is simply "effete mind"—mind hidebound with habits. Contemporary Peirce scholarship, particularly the extensive 2020-2025 output by researchers such as Ahti Pietarinen, emphasizes that Peirce’s synechism ensures a psycho-physical monism that leaves absolutely no room for the radical ontological disjunction required by interactionist dualism.

Recent literature demonstrates that Peirce’s recovery of physical laws from psychological laws operates through continuous evolutionary habituation, not through a non-physical agent exploiting quantum gaps. To weaponize tychism while ignoring synechism is to misrepresent Peirce's entire cosmological project. The manuscript treats Peirce as a dualist searching for a causal loophole, when in reality, Peirce was an evolutionary monist explaining how seemingly rigid physical laws emerge from continuous, spontaneous mind.

### Question-Begging Against Dewey and Rorty

When addressing pragmatists who are explicitly hostile to metaphysics, the manuscript abandons historical accuracy in favor of question-begging boundary substitutions. The text acknowledges that John Dewey was a "committed naturalist who would have resisted the dualist conclusion," yet asserts that Dewey's emphasis on "inquiry as active engagement" logically requires a dualist evaluator. This entirely ignores Dewey’s biological matrix of inquiry, wherein evaluation is an organic interaction between organism and environment, not a dualistic intervention.

The treatment of Richard Rorty is even more logically suspect. The manuscript notes Rorty's neo-pragmatist position that phenomenal experience is "just one more vocabulary". The manuscript rejects this by asserting: "the hard problem persists precisely because consciousness is not a description of the world but the condition under which description occurs". The reply’s premise—that phenomenal experience is a fundamental datum constraining any adequate vocabulary—is precisely what Rorty's entire philosophical project denies. Rorty, building on Wilfrid Sellars's critique of the "Myth of the Given," explicitly rejects the idea that there are pre-linguistic data that force themselves upon us and constrain our vocabularies. The manuscript does not offer a pragmatic argument against Rorty's epistemology; it simply restates its own Cartesian assumption that consciousness is an undeniable datum. This is not a philosophical refutation; it is a refusal to engage with the opponent's actual framework.

## III. Dimension B: Readings and Results Challenged, Retracted, or Replicated

A recurring structural vulnerability in the manuscript is its deliberate conflation of epistemology with metaphysics. The text routinely identifies anti-realist, pragmatist frameworks regarding scientific models and forcefully interprets them as providing evidence for a realist, substance-dualist ontology. This equivocation is most prominent in its handling of constructive empiricism and pragmatist quantum foundations.

### The Misappropriation of Constructive Empiricism

The manuscript invokes Bas van Fraassen’s constructive empiricism, specifically his pragmatic theory of explanation from *The Scientific Image* (1980). Van Fraassen argues that scientific explanation is an agent-relative, ternary relation involving the theory, the fact, and the context (the questioner/evaluator). The manuscript seizes upon this to argue that "consciousness is at the foundation" of explanation, using this to bolster its reflexive argument for irreducible phenomenal normativity.

This represents a profound misreading of constructive empiricism. Van Fraassen’s project is entirely epistemological; it is designed to articulate what it means to *accept* a scientific theory as empirically adequate without believing it to be true in a metaphysical sense regarding unobservables. To acknowledge that the act of explanation is necessarily constrained by human context (agent-relativity) is a trivial epistemological truth. The manuscript, however, attempts to transmute this epistemic constraint into a metaphysical claim about the ontological primacy of a non-physical consciousness.

The text admits that "van Fraassen himself does not draw the dualist conclusion" but asserts that the logic of his position licenses it. Constructive empiricism explicitly bars the exact type of metaphysical speculation the manuscript engages in. If one accepts van Fraassen's anti-realist premises regarding the limits of scientific truth, one cannot logically use those premises to posit the existence of a fundamentally unobservable, non-physical mental substance that interacts with matter.

### The Paradox of Pragmatist Quantum Foundations

The manuscript dedicates significant space to pragmatist quantum foundations, organizing QBism (Quantum Bayesianism), Richard Healey's pragmatist interpretation, the Brukner-Zeilinger informational view, and Henry Stapp's Copenhagen pragmatism into a unified family. The text identifies a unified pragmatist move: the refusal to read the quantum formalism as a description of mind-independent reality, reading it instead as guidance for agent expectations. From this, the manuscript deduces an "agent-shaped hole" in quantum foundations, which it promptly fills with its own dualistic model of consciousness.

This maneuver collapses under basic logical scrutiny, fundamentally misunderstanding the nature of Bayesian probability in this context. QBism’s foundational tenet—traceable to Fuchs, Caves, and Schack—is a radical anti-realism about the quantum state. The quantum state does not exist objectively in the world; the Born rule is a normative coherence constraint on a specific agent's personal Bayesian credences regarding future experiences. The manuscript attempts to graft a highly specific, metaphysically realist entity (an interactionist dualist mind) into a framework whose entire purpose is to deny the objective reality of the mechanism (the quantum wave function) with which the mind is supposedly interacting.

Furthermore, the manuscript addresses Richard Healey’s pragmatist interpretation, noting correctly that Healey’s agents "need only be 'physically situated' and capable of acting; they need not be conscious". The manuscript casually dismisses this by accusing Healey of buying parsimony with an "unpaid explanatory bill". Yet, this dismissal ignores vast swaths of contemporary literature demonstrating how physically situated, non-conscious systems can operate as information-processing agents within pragmatic frameworks. By forcing a dualistic consciousness onto QBism and Healey's pragmatism, the manuscript completely invalidates the anti-representationalist logic that makes those interpretations pragmatist in the first place.

## IV. Dimension C: Framings That Quietly Assume Site Commitments

The manuscript’s most critical structural failure lies in its inability to survive the very reflexive methodology it champions. The text heavily weaponizes Charles Sanders Peirce’s pragmatic maxim against materialism but meticulously shields its own dualistic architecture from the exact same deflationary blade. This is not reflexive methodology; it is gerrymandered apologetics that quietly assumes the validity of its own hidden commitments.

### The Self-Defeating Application of the Pragmatic Maxim

The manuscript deploys Peirce’s pragmatic maxim—which states that "if two positions have identical practical consequences, the difference between them is merely verbal"—to validate the mind-body dispute as a genuine, consequential debate. It argues that materialism and dualism yield different practical stakes regarding human dignity and post-mortem survival, therefore escaping the deflationary trap.

However, the internal logic of the manuscript's broader theoretical framework completely destroys this claim. The text acknowledges that its proposed physical mechanism for dualism—termed "Minimal Quantum Interaction"—is structurally selected to hide from unconditioned aggregate measurement. Crucially, the text explicitly concedes that this dualistic interaction is "empirically indistinguishable from chance" and leaves "no statistical trace that any presently conceivable instrument could resolve from the chance baseline".

This creates a fatal, unresolvable internal contradiction. If the proposed dualistic intervention behaves identically to random quantum noise, then the practical, observable, and empirical consequences of this dualism are strictly identical to the consequences of epiphenomenalist materialism or pure physicalist chance. By the manuscript's own load-bearing Peircean logic, if an interactionist dualist universe is empirically indistinguishable from a materialist universe governed by quantum randomness, the difference between this specific dualism and materialism is *merely verbal*.

The manuscript notes this tension in passing, suggesting that the dispute might be resolved by intention-conditioned tests, but it entirely fails to recognize that by rendering its own dualistic mechanism empirically indistinguishable from chance, it has subjected itself to pragmatic elimination. It utilizes the pragmatic maxim to attack the eliminativist, yet refuses to turn the maxim inward upon its own empirically vacant causal mechanism.

### The Circularity of "Demand-Character" and Cognitive Phenomenology

The manuscript's core positive argument asserts that pragmatic evaluation requires genuine theory-choice, which in turn requires "demand-character"—an irreducible, phenomenal, normative "felt sense" of evaluation (cognitive phenomenology). The text claims that any materialist theory that eliminates this demand-character eliminates the very evaluative standpoint required to choose materialism, thereby rendering materialism self-defeating.

This argument is entirely circular. It operates by quietly assuming the central premise it is supposed to prove: that normativity and evaluation *must* be inextricably tied to an immaterial, subjective "feeling" rather than to biological, physical, or structural dynamics. The text asserts that "the felt sense that one framework illuminates while another obscures... is what gives pragmatic assessment its normative force". This presupposes that the felt sense *is* the normative force, rather than merely its phenomenal correlate. By baking the requirement of dualistic phenomenology into the definition of "evaluation," the manuscript ensures that functionalist or materialist accounts will fail by definition. This is a classic example of assuming the site's commitments rather than arguing for them.

## V. Dimension D: Untested Counterarguments and Competing Frameworks

Because the manuscript relies on deeply outmoded Cartesian assumptions regarding normativity, it entirely ignores the most dominant 2020s paradigms in the philosophy of mind and cognitive science that explain cognitive normativity naturalistically. The omission of 4E cognition (enactivism) and illusionism leaves the manuscript's central reflexive argument completely untested against contemporary state-of-the-art counterarguments.

### The Omission of 4E Cognition and Enactivism

Over the past decade, and particularly in the 2020-2025 period, 4E cognition (Embodied, Embedded, Enactive, and Extended cognition) has provided a robust, non-dualistic account of precisely the "normativity" and "evaluation" the manuscript claims requires dualism. The manuscript asserts that functionalism cannot capture normativity without "surrendering the normativity that made evaluation worth trusting". This assertion reveals a profound ignorance of enactivist literature.

Enactivism, as articulated in recent landmark works such as Shaun Gallagher's *Action and Interaction* (2020, Oxford University Press), completely reframes the nature of cognition. Drawing on the biological concept of autopoiesis (self-organization), enactivism demonstrates that meaning, normativity, and evaluation are generated dynamically through an organism's sensorimotor coupling with its environment. For the enactivist, an organism does not passively represent the world and then evaluate it using a non-physical "felt sense." Instead, the organism *enacts* a world of significance.

Evaluation is a biological imperative; it is the embodied process of maintaining viability in a precarious environment. Radical enactivism, advanced heavily in the 2020s by Daniel Hutto and Erik Myin, goes even further. It argues that basic cognition requires no mental representations or distinct cognitive contents whatsoever. Enactivism explains exactly how physical, biological systems generate inherently normative perspectives—the world *matters* to a living organism because the organism's continued existence depends on its active engagement with it. By failing to address enactivism, the manuscript leaves its central argument against physicalist evaluation entirely untested. It attacks an antiquated, passive version of materialism while ignoring the dynamic, normative physicalism that currently dominates the field.

### The Ignored Challenge of Illusionism

Similarly, the manuscript leans entirely on the reality of "cognitive phenomenology"—the "felt sense" of evaluation. It blindly assumes that this introspective feeling is an accurate reflection of an irreducible metaphysical reality. The text ignores the rapidly growing 2020-2025 literature on *Illusionism*, most notably championed by Keith Frankish.

Illusionism argues that phenomenal consciousness (including the "demand-character" of evaluation) is a naturally occurring, adaptive introspective illusion. We are physically wired to model our own cognitive states as having irreducible phenomenal qualities, even though no such non-physical qualities exist. If illusionism is correct, the manuscript's "reflexive methodology" is entirely bypassed. The evaluator (the human brain) is simply a physical system processing information and modeling that processing as a "felt sense." Because the manuscript never addresses illusionism or the unreliability of introspection, it assumes the infallibility of subjective experience—a premise that contemporary philosophy of mind has subjected to devastating critique.

## VI. Final Verdict and Specific Weaknesses

The manuscript’s attempt to fuse classical pragmatism, quantum anti-realism, and phenomenal reflexivity into a defense of substance dualism fails the standards of rigorous philosophical peer review. The text is characterized by severe historical omissions, devastating internal contradictions regarding its own pragmatic criteria, and a complete lack of engagement with contemporary competing frameworks.

The following five specific weaknesses warrant the rejection of the manuscript:

1. **Misappropriation of Jamesian Neutral Monism:** The manuscript falsely equates William James’s "pure experience" with a preservation of dualistic ontology, ignoring consensus scholarship that radical empiricism was formulated explicitly to dissolve the mind-matter ontological gap entirely (see contemporary re-evaluations of neutral monism in 2020-2025 philosophy of science and pragmatism literature).
2. **Truncation of Peircean Metaphysics (Tychism without Synechism):** The author cherry-picks Peirce’s *tychism* (objective chance) to carve out space for mental causation, while entirely ignoring Peirce’s *synechism* (continuity) and objective idealism. This contradicts the findings of prominent contemporary Peirce scholars (e.g., Ahti Pietarinen's 2020-2025 publications on Peircean synechism), which demonstrate that Peirce's system precludes the radical ontological disjunctions required by the manuscript's dualism.
3. **The Enactivist Blind Spot Regarding Normativity:** The manuscript grounds its reflexive argument on the assumption that materialist/functionalist systems cannot generate the "demand-character" or normative evaluation required for theory choice. This entirely omits the paradigm of 4E Cognition and Enactivism (e.g., Shaun Gallagher’s *Action and Interaction*, 2020), which explicitly details how biological autopoiesis and sensorimotor coupling ground genuine normativity without resorting to non-physical dualism.
4. **Epistemological Equivocation on QBism and Constructive Empiricism:** The text forces epistemic, anti-realist frameworks (van Fraassen's pragmatic explanation, QBism's Bayesian credences) to support a metaphysically realist, dualistic ontology. Grafting a realist dualist agent onto QBism directly violates the foundational consensus of the QBist framework, which views the quantum state merely as an agent's personal credence rather than a physical gap for an immaterial mind to manipulate (e.g., DeBrota et al., 2021).
5. **Question-Begging Avoidance of Illusionism:** The manuscript relies heavily on the unassailable reality of "cognitive phenomenology" (the "felt sense" of evaluation) to defeat materialism. It assumes the infallibility of this introspection without addressing contemporary deflationary models like Illusionism (e.g., Keith Frankish's 2020-2025 defenses of illusionism), which provide rigorous naturalistic explanations for why agents falsely intuit their cognitive evaluations as possessing irreducible phenomenal properties.

**Verdict:** The manuscript’s reliance on historical distortion, logical question-begging, internal self-contradiction, and the systematic omission of major contemporary literature renders it fundamentally unsound and unequivocally unready for academic submission.
