## Executive Summary

The page is well sourced. Every direct quotation this review checked is verbatim (45 strings from 17 raw sources), and every writer's guard is met in the text except one: Conroy-Beam and Buss are present but misframed. Its weaknesses are in classification and framing.

1. Two of its *unexplorable* labels rest on conceptual truths. The warrant face's "absence" is a conclusion that no justifying reason exists, and the object face's "residue" is true by the definition of a duplicate. Neither is a limit on knowing in the sense `three-kinds-of-void` defines. The article's own Approaches section proposes an empirical test of the warrant face.
2. The lead asserts that "the lover also runs out of answers". The page itself says no study has tested this.
3. Conroy-Beam and Buss are described as explaining mate choice "through effects belonging to each person". Their central claim is preference-matching, which Joel et al.'s own manuscript classes as relationship-level. So the counter-evidence bears on the very component the void is built on.
4. The lens face runs together two questions: whether the perceived perfections are there, and whether the beloved's value was found or conferred. Stendhal and Murray et al. bear on the first and the appraisal/bestowal dispute on the second. "No external standard" is true only of the second. The conflation also appears in the lead, the description, `voids/voids.md` L228 and the reciprocal sentence in `emotional-epistemology-void`.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would take the forecast face as her evidence. "Desire" splits cleanly into actor, partner and relationship variance. The relationship part is 16–29% predictable once interaction measures exist. So the "void" is a fact about *when* the measurements are taken, not a dark space in anyone's mind. She would then say that the warrant face proves her point instead of marking a limit. If Velleman, Whiting, Han and the bestowal theorists agree that nothing justifies loving this one, the folk demand for a reason was confused, and the correct theory has found that out. Nothing remains hidden. It is a discovery that the folk category "reasons for love" has no extension. The page calls this "close to *unexplorable*", and she would ask what, exactly, cannot be explored. This is Issue 1.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett would focus on the object face. "No qualitative description of the beloved would fail to fit a duplicate" is true by definition of "qualitative duplicate", he would say, so it cannot be a cognitive limit. The page presents a tautology as an "unexplorable residue". He would also note what the rupture cases show. Dementia and the Replika update show that qualitative change can end love. Capgras shows that losing felt familiarity can end it. That makes qualities and familiarity *necessary* for the bond. The non-fungibility intuition concerns *sufficiency*: whether a qualitative twin would do. The rupture cases are silent on that. So the "seam" L69 describes (she thinks she loves the person; the rupture cases show the bond rests on qualities) need not be a conflict. Both conditions can be necessary. This is Issue 6.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Nothing to attack. The page says "Minimal Quantum Interaction has no connection". Its Bidirectional reading is kept "the Map's alone". The discipline is correct.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch would say that "because it was he" has a referent in every branch, just as "here" has one after you cross a room. Indexicals are branch-relative, and nothing goes missing. The page gives this reply (L89) and does not adjudicate, which is right. He would add one point the page omits. The Map's own `concepts/haecceity` L171 grants that "Process haecceity cannot do the work the No-Many-Worlds argument needs". On the process reading, "the pre-branch subject ceases at the measurement and two new ones begin", which is "close to the verdict the branch-relative Everettian already accepts". So the page's "On the Map's reading, branching would leave 'because it was he' with no determinate referent" holds only on the persisting subject the Map *posits* (P-SC3), not on the haecceity account that L89 cites as its route. This is minor. The section is already two-sided.

### The Empiricist

A Popperian would ask, face by face, what could show each one wrong. The **forecast** face is falsifiable: a large enough sample that predicted relationship variance would shrink it, and the page says so. The **warrant** face is also testable, by the page's own design. L81 proposes a contrastive interview "showing lovers a described rival who matches every reason they gave". A face with a specified experiment is *unexplored*, not "close to *unexplorable*". The **lens** face's factual half (are the perceived perfections there?) has external checks, imperfect but real: Murray et al. used the partner's self-report. Only its value half (found or conferred?) lacks a standard. The page's "No external standard settles whether the value was found or conferred" is true only of that half (Issue 4). And the lead's "the lover also runs out of answers" is a prediction the page records as untested (Issue 2).

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would read "because it was he, because it was I" as reification in its purest form: two selves posited at the exact point where every reason has run out. The rupture cases are his evidence. When the moral faculty is injured the caregiver says he is gone, because the "he" was a dependently arisen cluster all along. The felt irreplaceability Badhwar concedes is grasping (*upādāna*) at a permanence the cluster lacks. He would find the lens face's dichotomy (found or conferred) empty from both sides. No value inheres independently in the beloved to be "found", and conferral presupposes a conferring self with standing to bestow. On his view the deflationist reading the page offers at L89 is the closer approach to the middle way, and the haecceity link is the clinging.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Two "unexplorable" labels rest on conceptual truths (interpretive equivocation)

- **File**: `voids/beloved-void.md` L59, L69 and L95; carried by `voids/voids.md` L125.
- **Location**: L59 "This face is close to *unexplorable*, because the absence is a conclusion that theories disagreeing about much else reach from opposite directions." L69 "The face is *occluded*, with an *unexplorable* residue: no qualitative description of the beloved would fail to fit a duplicate." L95 "warrant close to unexplorable".
- **Problem**: `voids/three-kinds-of-void` defines the Unexplorable as "darkness that *cannot* be illuminated". Its anchor is McGinn's cognitive closure: "the properties exist, but the mind's concept-forming procedures cannot extend to grasp them". Both of the page's labels fail that definition:
  - **Warrant.** The page's evidence supports a *normative* reading: on Velleman, Whiting, Han and the bestowal views, no justifying reason exists. The label requires an *epistemic* reading: a reason exists that the lover cannot reach. If the accounts are right, nothing is hidden. The lover who finds no reason has found the truth. The reason given for the label, that rival theories converge, is evidence that the absence is *real*. It is no evidence that anything is *closed*. This is the equivocation the skill's evidential-status check names, run in reverse: evidence for the metaphysical reading is recruited to assert an epistemic limit. The page's own L81 then proposes an experiment (the contrastive interview) on the epistemic reading. That makes the epistemic reading *unexplored*. Montaigne's next sentence supplies the epistemic residue the label needs: "There is, beyond all that I am able to say, I know not what inexplicable and fated power" (verified, Cotton).
  - **Object.** "No qualitative description … would fail to fit a duplicate" is true by the definition of a qualitative duplicate. It marks no limit of any mind.
  - A third, softer instance: L47's "only the third makes it *unexplorable* before the meeting" fits only if the dyad-specific factors exist before the meeting and cannot be measured. If attraction "may emerge" in the interaction (the authors' word), the factors do not yet exist, and the forecast's failure is a fact about the timing of measurement. It is not closure. This is noted, not ranked, because "cannot be anticipated a priori" supports either reading.
- **Navigation surface**: voids.md L125 says the missing warrant "is conceded within the philosophy of love". The page itself records dissent ("Not everyone concedes: Helm quotes LaFollette…", L55), so "several accounts" is what the evidence supports. L95's "a conclusion within the philosophy of value" has the same overreach.
- **Severity**: High. It sets two faces' classification, it appears in the summary line of the tenet section, and the skill rates interpretive equivocation as critical.
- **Recommendation**: Priority 1.

### Issue 2: The lead asserts an untested finding about lovers

- **File**: L41. "Asked the question the models failed, the lover also runs out of answers, and several leading philosophical accounts concede…"
- **Problem**: The only first-person evidence on the page is Montaigne, writing about a friend. L81 says the experiment that would test this claim has not been done: "the research behind this article found no such study". The research note's own phenomenology runs the other way: "The lover is not puzzled about why she loves him: the reasons seem obvious". The lead is the truncation-resilient summary, and it states as fact what the body states as a gap.
- **Severity**: Medium-High. It is in the lead, so it is what a chatbot quoting the page will repeat.
- **Recommendation**: Priority 2 (+7).

### Issue 3: Conroy-Beam and Buss are misdescribed as orthogonal to the pair-specific remainder

- **File**: L51. "That explains who pairs with whom through effects belonging to each person, and leaves the pair-specific remainder untouched. The void is that remainder."
- **Problem**: The C-B&B abstract concludes that "stated mate preferences guide actual mate selections under real mating-market constraints". Its first listed effect is "low-magnitude correlations between stated preferences and the individual traits of chosen partners". It tests models "in which preferences either do or do not drive mate selection". One person's preference matched against another's traits is a **dyadic** effect. Joel et al.'s own manuscript classes it that way. In its section on "efforts to predict relationship-level variance", it gives the example "if Laura reports a preference for extraverted men and Ben reports that he is extraverted" and reports such matching to be negligible (citing Eastwick et al. 2014). The same manuscript cites Campbell, Chin and Stanton (2016), "Initial evidence that individuals form new relationships with partners that more closely match their ideal preferences". Only the mate-value effects in C-B&B (leveraging one's value; assortment on overall value) belong "to each person". So C-B&B bear on the very component the void is built on. What differs is the **outcome**: mate choice in established couples under market constraints, against desire ratings after a four-minute date. The two literatures disagree about preference-matching, and the page's framing hides that disagreement. The research note's own line ("Conroy-Beam and Buss explain *partner selection* by mate value, which is an actor/partner effect") is where the error started.
- **Severity**: Medium-High. The guard asked for this counter-evidence so that the void would be bounded honestly. As written, the counter-evidence is presented as harmless.
- **Recommendation**: Priority 3 (+13).

### Issue 4: The lens face fuses "are the perfections there?" with "was the value found or conferred?"

- **File**: L41 (lead), L77, the page `description`, `voids/voids.md` L228, `voids/emotional-epistemology-void.md` L74.
- **Problem**: Stendhal's crystallisation produces *perceived qualities*: "starting from the broken arm of your friend, you conclude with the absolute conviction of the angelic goodness of your mistress" (verified, Woolf). Murray et al. measure *attributed attributes* against the partner's self-report. Both concern whether the perfections are really there. On the appraisal/bestowal map, that is a question about appraisal going wrong. Bestowal is a different claim. Singer's love "confers importance no matter what the object is worth", and Frankfurt's object "acquires value for us because we love it". Neither says love confers *perfections*. The SEP treats distortion separately, as a question about "the norms … in terms of which such distortions are intelligible" in Jollimore's vision view (verified). So "whether the perfections a lover sees were found or conferred" (L41) mixes the two questions, and L77's "No external standard settles whether the value was found or conferred, because philosophy has not" lets the value question's lack of a standard stand for the whole face. The factual question has an imperfect external check, and the page itself reports it at L75.
- **Reciprocal**: `emotional-epistemology-void` L74 carries both errors. It also says "Stendhal defines a lover's crystallisation as the *découverte*". In the French, crystallisation is "l'opération de l'esprit, qui tire … la découverte": the operation that *yields* the discovery. The definition is slightly misstated. The mattering-void reciprocal speaks of "value conferred … from value found" and is correct.
- **Severity**: Medium. The face's occlusion "from inside" survives the fix. Only the scope of "no external standard" changes.
- **Recommendation**: Priority 4 (+25 on the article, +1 on emotional-epistemology-void).

### Issue 6 (off-list): The object face omits shared history

- **File**: L67 "In practice the beloved is picked out by qualities and by felt familiarity, and each can fail alone."

## Counterarguments to Address

### The rupture cases show necessity, not sufficiency
- **Current content says**: "the rupture cases suggest that qualities and felt familiarity are what sustain the bond" (L69), and the deflationist can read felt non-fungibility as "a useful illusion" (L89).
- **A critic would argue**: rupture shows only that the bond fails *without* qualities or familiarity. Non-fungibility is the claim that a twin with both would not do. Neither rupture case speaks to it, so the "seam" may be two necessary conditions and not a contradiction.
- **Suggested response**: one clause at L69, e.g. "…sustain the bond, though they show what love cannot do without, not that a twin would do" (+15). The tenet section's deflationist sentence can then stay as it is.

### The authors lean towards the unexplorable reading
- **Current content says**: "The authors offer three readings and do not choose between them" (L47).
- **A critic would argue**: the manuscript supports the third reading alone: "This interpretation is supported by research highlighting the role of the situation … as well as by mounting evidence that the qualities that people initially find romantically desirable only weakly match the qualities that they articulate in the abstract". The page understates its own best evidence. (Whether the published discussion keeps this sentence was not checked; cite "manuscript".)
- **Suggested response**: optional, e.g. "and do not choose between them, though the manuscript cites support only for the third" (+10).

### C-B&B and the preference-matching dispute
- See Issue 3. The honest framing is a live dispute between research programmes, separated by outcome measure. The page should not present it as a settled division of labour.
