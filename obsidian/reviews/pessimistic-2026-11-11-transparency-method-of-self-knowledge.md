## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Byrne credited with a concession his text attributes to Moore's remarks
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/transparency-method-of-self-knowledge.md`, L97
- **Location**: "Nor does the method rule out what [[contemplative-epistemology|contemplatives]] report, attending to awareness itself, since Byrne leaves open an inward procedure that is merely "not necessary"."
- **Source check**: the Byrne 2011 manuscript (MIT DSpace bitstream `16daa064…`, pdftotext, §2) reads: "In order to find out that one sees a blue mug, one does not turn one's attention inward to the contents of one's own mind—Moore's remarks suggest either that there is no such procedure or, if there is, it is not necessary. Rather, one turns one's attention outward". The "not necessary" clause is the second disjunct of what *Moore's remarks suggest*. Byrne's own sentence denies that one turns inward. His considered view elsewhere is "there is no inner eye" (2005, p. 100, scan read, scoped to belief), and the 2018 abstract describes self-knowledge as an "outward glance … not … an 'inward glance' at one's own mind".
- **Problem**: the page's L52 reads Byrne correctly ("Byrne reads Moore's remarks as suggesting that an inward procedure either does not exist or 'is not necessary'"). L97 then converts that reading into an allowance by Byrne and uses it to make room for contemplative inward attention. This is a concession attributed to an author who does not make it. The Map's underlying point is sound and has proper support on the same page: Schwitzgebel's "The positive and negative theses are separable: A pluralist might accept the positive thesis without the negative one" (SEP *Introspection* §2.3.4, verified in the raw HTML).
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: re-ground the clause on Schwitzgebel's separability and state Byrne's own stance. See Priority 1 for the exact OLD and NEW text (+11 words).

### Issue 2: Byrne 2005 p. 99 is quoted without its "often pointed out" frame, and his 2005 preference rule is omitted
- **File**: same, L89
- **Location**: "In 2005 Byrne wrote that transparency "covers belief and perception", with desire "the obvious example" of a hard case (p. 99)."
- **Source check**: Byrne 2005, p. 99 (MIT scan, page image read): "It is often pointed out the phenomenon of transparency is quite limited. It covers belief and perception, but what about, to take the obvious example, knowledge of one's *wants* or *desires*?" Byrne goes on to accept it for preference ("As just noted, clearly transparency does not apply here"). On p. 100 he then gives the neutral rule DES ("If ψing is a better option than χing, believe that you prefer to ψ than to χ"), saying "often my eyes are still 'directed outward—upon the world'", but "Unlike BEL, DES is not a transparent rule" and "Unlike BEL, DES is not self-verifying. Slavishly following (or trying to follow) DES will sometimes lead to a *false* belief about one's mental state."
- **Problem**: the quoted words are verbatim but voiced by "It is often pointed out". More importantly, the page's sequence (2005 limit, then 2018 extension) hides that the outward-looking extension to desire began in 2005, and that it began with Byrne conceding that the rule loses self-verification. That concession is the strongest concrete instance of the page's own "each step needs more", and the page leaves it out.
- **Severity**: Medium-low
- **Recommendation**: see Priority 2(i) (+14 words).
