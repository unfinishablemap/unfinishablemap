## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

"Your L153 says 'Qualitative properties *don't* suffice' and backs it with the zombie conditional. My qualitative properties include whatever phenomenal ones you like. Zombies, if possible, show that physical properties leave out the phenomenal. They show nothing about whether *qualitative* properties, phenomenal ones included, fix which subject is which. The second half of your sentence, 'a phenomenal duplicate would leave open which subject is *this* one', is just haecceitism restated as its own premise. Your sibling page `topics/vertiginous-question` L197 at least labels the same move 'on the Map's reading, which is not framework-neutral'. The hub drops the label."

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

"L91 asks '*how do I know* that *I* am this conscious being rather than another?' and answers with a non-qualitative fact. By your own definition (L53), and by your apex, a non-qualitative fact has no qualitative signature: 'No experiment could demonstrate haecceity, because haecceity by definition has no qualitative signature' (`apex/identity-across-transformations` L169). So it cannot be how anyone *knows* anything. You asked an epistemic question and gave a metaphysical answer. Your L77 already concedes that haecceity 'is silent on which of them is the privileged, lived one'. What is left is an intuition with a Latin name."

### The Quantum Skeptic

"L133: 'If consciousness participates in collapse through the quantum Zeno mechanism, then the subject making selections is precisely the haecceitistic "this" that actualizes one possibility rather than another.' On Stapp's own taxonomy, which your `concepts/stapp-quantum-mind` L64 and L106 reproduce, the agent chooses which question is put and nature answers. Attention 'does not weight any single answer'. Under the Zeno mechanism nothing actualizes 'one possibility rather than another'; that is outcome-selection talk the mechanism disowns. The rest is your usual conditional. I grant the 'If' is honest."

### The Many-Worlds Defender

"You concede at L89 that Lewis's self-ascription analysis is non-haecceitist. That analysis is exactly what Vaidman and Sebens–Carroll use. After decoherence and before the observer looks, each copy is in one branch and does not know which, the same structure as Lewis's two gods. Then at L107 you say self-locating uncertainty 'presupposes a haecceitistic fact about which branch contains you', and that 'all branches equally contain your continuers'. That is the pre-branching picture, not the post-branching one where the uncertainty is located. You also concede at L79 that branch-relative treatments 'address the question', then say at L109 that they leave it 'untouched'.

At L83 you still argue 'Yet you find yourself in exactly one'. Your own `concepts/many-worlds` canonical statement (L88) says such claims 'do not by themselves refute MWI—branch-local *de se* attribution already secures them'. Your `topics/indexical-identity-quantum-measurement` L109 says 'the obvious objection does not work … Branch-local phenomenology does not discriminate between the readings'. And `concepts/diverging-worlds-everettianism` L62 says 'The residue is an argument for haecceitism, not a special argument against Everett'. The hub is running the argument your specialists retired."

### The Buddhist Philosopher

"Your L121 reads Nāgasena correctly: 'no determinate fact of strict identity underlies the continuity, which cuts against primitive thisness.'
