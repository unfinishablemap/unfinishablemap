# Independent critical review: “The Cross-State Void”

## Overall assessment

“The Cross-State Void” identifies a genuine and important phenomenon, but gives it a stronger interpretation than the evidence warrants.

The best-supported conclusion is:

> **People’s current visceral and affective states systematically bias their predictions, valuations and explanations of behaviour in other states; ordinary recollection or verbal knowledge often fails to reproduce the motivational influence of the target state.**

The evidence does **not** yet establish that:

1. only description crosses between states;
2. motivational “weight” cannot be represented from outside the state;
3. the limitation is structural rather than a corrigible forecasting difficulty;
4. awareness of the bias cannot materially improve prediction;
5. a distinct phenomenal ingredient, rather than ordinary state-dependent valuation and motivation, explains the effect.

The article is therefore strongest as an account of **cross-state attenuation or projection bias**. It has not yet established a genuine “void” except in the relatively uncontroversial sense that representing an experience is not identical with undergoing it.

### Claim-level verdict

| Article claim | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Current state biases predictions and choices concerning another state | **Moderately well supported** |
| The bias can run in both directions | **Supported, but the article omits its best direct evidence** |
| Familiar experience does not eliminate the bias | **Supported in some domains, but overstated as a general rule** |
| Merely being told about the bias may fail to correct it | **Supported by one narrow experiment** |
| Knowledge of the bias cannot materially correct prediction | **Not established** |
| Only description crosses; action-guiding weight does not | **Contradicted by some of the article’s own evidence unless “weight” means literal reinstantiation** |
| Mental simulation succeeds only by partially entering the state | **An untested, potentially unfalsifiable interpretation** |
| The result has dualist significance | **No evidential support beyond compatibility** |

## 2. The empirical hot–cold literature

### 2.1 Sayette et al. is a fair choice for the principal exhibit

The description of Sayette et al. is accurate. Ninety-eight smokers were assigned to hot, cold and comparison conditions. The cold group predicted its later willingness to accept money to postpone smoking during a high-craving session, while the hot group predicted from an already high-craving session. The cold group subsequently valued smoking more highly than it had predicted; the hot group did not show the corresponding underprediction.  [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02178.x)

This is good evidence for a **cold-to-hot valuation error in a familiar recurring state**. It is much better suited to the article than the Read and Loewenstein pain experiment that previously carried the lede.

It nevertheless measures willingness to accept money for delaying a cigarette, not phenomenal access or the ability to represent craving’s felt character. The explanatory inference remains underdetermined:

- the cold smoker may fail to recreate craving phenomenally;
- may accurately remember craving but misestimate its later incentive salience;
- may know its likely strength but fail to integrate that knowledge into a monetary valuation;
- or may undergo cue- and abstinence-dependent changes in the effective utility of smoking that are not expected to remain motivationally active in a non-craving state.

Sayette supports **state-dependent valuation and forecasting error**. It does not by itself support the article’s sharper distinction between transported descriptive content and non-transportable felt weight.

### 2.2 The broad phenomenon has held up better than the article’s evidence base suggests

The broader projection-bias family is not resting solely on the original small Amsterdam and Carnegie Mellon studies. It has continued to appear in incentivised food valuations, consumer auctions, real and virtual supermarket purchasing, healthcare decisions and other intertemporal-choice settings. A 2026 systematic review treats projection bias as a recurring phenomenon across multiple domains, while also stressing that its mechanisms, real-world generalisability and mitigation remain inadequately understood.  [Geoffrey W. Fisher](https://geoffreywfisher.github.io/symmetry.pdf)

The defensible replication verdict is therefore:

- **The generic effect has held up:** current state often shifts forecasts or valuations concerning a different future state.
- **The exact hot–cold empathy-gap package has not been firmly quantified:** tasks, target states, outcomes and definitions of error vary substantially.
- **The stronger philosophical interpretation has not been replicated:** the studies do not operationalise a phenomenal or motivational “weight” that is shown to be unrepresentable.
- **A dedicated quantitative meta-analysis of the exact paradigm remains absent:** the article’s caveat on that point is reasonable, but it should now cite the 2026 systematic review rather than imply that no current synthesis exists.  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-091924-040158)

### 2.3 The article misses the strongest direct evidence for bidirectionality

The article candidly says that its hot-to-cold case rests on Loewenstein’s 2005 review. That is no longer an adequate literature summary. Fisher and Rangel’s 2014 incentivised experiment was designed precisely to compare the two directions. In 101 participants, hungry subjects overbid by about 20 cents for food they would later consume while satiated, while satiated subjects underbid by about 19 cents for food they would later consume while hungry. The estimated gaps were approximately symmetric and were associated with changes in the evaluation of taste rather than healthiness.  [Geoffrey W. Fisher](https://geoffreywfisher.github.io/symmetry.pdf)

This omission is consequential in two directions.

First, it **strengthens** the article’s claim that projection can operate both ways. The live sentence saying that the hot-side case rests on a review alone is simply stale.  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

Second, Fisher and Rangel favour a deflationary interpretation. Their results concern attribute weighting and valuations under hunger, not an inability to represent phenomenal character. They make the phenomenon look like symmetric state-dependent utility computation rather than evidence of a distinct inaccessible ingredient.

The study is not conclusive: its participants were Caltech students, the manipulation involved moderate hunger, and the authors caution against assuming that symmetry generalises to states such as addiction or sexual arousal. Nevertheless, it is one of the most directly relevant experiments in the literature and should be central rather than absent.  [Geoffrey W. Fisher](https://geoffreywfisher.github.io/symmetry.pdf)

## 3. “Content crosses, weight does not”

### 3.1 Read and Loewenstein partly contradict the article’s formulation

The article now correctly concedes that Read and Loewenstein’s 1999 pain study compares **experience with description**, not straightforwardly a cold state with a hot state. Eighty-two participants experienced a thirty-second cold-pressor pain or received only a description and then decided how many repetitions they would accept for payment.  [Carnegie Mellon University](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/EnduringPain.pdf)

The important result is not merely that description-only participants were more willing to accept pain. Participants who had experienced the pain remained substantially less willing to accept it even when tested a week later and no longer in pain: the delayed experienced groups accepted means of 3.9 and 5.6 offers, compared with 9.9 for description-only controls.  [Carnegie Mellon University](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/EnduringPain.pdf)

That is evidence that **something action-guiding did cross the state boundary**. It may have been:

- a learned utility;
- an episodic or affective trace;
- a conditioned aversion;
- a semantic belief updated by experience;
- or an attenuated simulation.

It was not merely the same sort of description available to the control participants. The experiment therefore creates a dilemma for the article:

- If “weight” means any enduring effect on later valuation or choice, some weight clearly crossed.
- If “weight” means the full phenomenal or motivational state itself, then the claim becomes the relatively trivial proposition that remembering pain is not literally being in pain.

The current article notices that the design is acquaintance-versus-description, but does not draw the revisionary consequence for its lede, which still says that “what crosses” is a description while the pull does not.  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

A better conclusion would be:

> Previous experience carries a durable but attenuated action-guiding trace across states; it does not normally recreate the full concurrent motivational force of the original state.

That formulation is both empirically stronger and philosophically less inflated.

### 3.2 Morley supports non-reinstantiation, not a general void

The Morley quotation is accurate. No participant reported sensory re-experiencing, while 41% could not recall the sensory quality of the remembered pain. The complementary fact matters: **59% apparently could recall some sensory quality without sensory re-experiencing**. Morley concluded that different components of pain-event memory were differentially related, not that only propositional content survived.  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8278211/)

This supports a distinction between:

- remembering that and how one was in pain;
- recalling some qualitative features;
- and actually re-experiencing pain.

It does not show that the remembered representation fails to affect choice, that no non-propositional trace survives, or that the dissociation generalises from pain to hunger, craving, rage and fatigue. The live article now explicitly labels the “difference in kind” conclusion as its own reading rather than Morley’s, which is appropriate.  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

### 3.3 Hume supports attenuation, not categorical closure

The Hume quotations are verbatim and accurately located in Section II. Hume distinguishes present impressions from remembered or imagined ideas by their greater “force and vivacity,” and explicitly describes the difference as one of degree.  [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9662/9662-h/9662-h.htm)

That makes Hume an elegant ancestor of the attenuation thesis. It does not make him evidence for a categorical representational divide. Indeed, Hume’s claim that remembered thought can be a “faithful mirror” with fainter colours is more naturally read as **preserved representation with reduced intensity** than as the article’s “description crosses, weight does not” dichotomy.

## 4. The disclosure and incorrigibility claim

### 4.1 One failed instruction does not establish structural incorrigibility

Nordgren, van der Pligt and van Harreveld reported that fatigued and non-fatigued participants differed in their explanations of past behaviour and that participants did not eliminate the difference when instructed to correct for their current visceral state. The article quotes the abstract accurately.  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16866751/)

But this warrants only the narrow conclusion:

> A particular corrective instruction did not eliminate a particular state-dependent attribution effect in one pre-registration-era study.

It does not establish that:

- knowledge of the bias cannot improve predictions;
- quantitative calibration cannot work;
- feedback across repeated state transitions cannot help;
- external records or individualised models cannot correct estimates;
- structured elicitation cannot outperform intuitive prediction;
- or disclosure fails across pain, addiction, hunger, anger and social suffering.

The article moves too quickly from “this instruction did not remove the measured effect” to “there is nothing to restore it from” and “the limit is structural.”  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

There is also an unresolved tension inside the article. The disclosure section says the limit does not yield to disclosure, while the later workaround section says that knowing the direction of bias can permit a correction factor analogous to correction for a known instrument error. Those can be reconciled only by sharply separating:

1. recreating the target experience;
2. intuitively estimating it;
3. behaviourally correcting for one’s biased estimate.

The article currently slides among all three.  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

### 4.2 Helton and Register do not support the strongest formulation

The page says Helton and Register are known “only through abstract-level summaries.” That is stale: their complete paper is publicly accessible.

More importantly, the full text explicitly allows that higher-order knowledge of hot–cold gaps can be embedded in a broader strategy that improves reliability, perhaps enough to confer knowledge. They give choosing or predicting in an appropriate state as one example. They also argue that understanding relevant to authentic action can sometimes be obtained through testimony from oneself or a close friend rather than first-personal re-entry.  [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/archive/HELHEG.pdf)

Their actual position is therefore:

- unassisted first-person simulation across a state gap is often unreliable;
- it need not be incorrigible;
- and first-person experiential knowledge is not the only route to action-guiding understanding.

That is more congenial to a **correctable epistemic limitation** than to a structural representational void.

### 4.3 Recent healthcare evidence directly tests improved cold-side representation

A particularly important missed study is Ozdemir et al. 2024. It compared pregnant participants’ cold-state predictions of epidural use with their actual subsequent use during labour. A structured discrete-choice experiment achieved approximately 60–71% prediction concordance, increasing to about 76–77% under specifications accounting for earlier ineffective pain-relief choices, while direct elicitation achieved 58%. The sample comprised 248 individuals and actual treatment use was obtained from medical records.  [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0277953624002946)

This does not show that participants phenomenally recreated labour pain. It does show that **a richer cold-state representation can improve behavioural prediction without putting the subject into the target pain state**. It is therefore a direct counterexample to the article’s unqualified claim that successful routes work only by moving the person into the state and that none improves cold representation “as a representation.”

The 2026 systematic review similarly describes information, warnings, simulations, cooling-off periods and attention to basic decision variables as candidate mitigation methods, while correctly noting that the empirical evidence for many of them remains limited. The appropriate verdict is “correction remains under-studied,” not “structurally impossible.”  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-091924-040158)

## 5. Mental simulation and the article’s strongest protective move

Steinmetz, Tausen and Risen found across several studies that imagining cold, heat, hunger or satiation shifted state-relevant current preferences, affected portion choices, and influenced predictions about similar others. The authors explicitly present mental simulation as a weaker substitute for actual experience that may improve anticipation of future preferences.  [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167217741315)

The article recognises this as its strongest counterexample, but then says that simulation succeeds by “installing a partial state,” not by improving representation.  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

This is the clearest instance of tenet-protective or thesis-protective bracketing. Under embodied, enactive and simulationist theories, **partial activation is precisely one way in which representation works**. A representation of hunger may recruit some of the same interoceptive, affective or motivational machinery as hunger without becoming actual metabolic hunger. Calling this “entering the state” rather than “representing the state” does not settle the issue; it stipulates the desired answer.

The move also threatens falsifiability:

- If imagery has no effect, the article says cold representation cannot carry weight.
- If imagery affects preferences, the article says the subject has partially entered the target state.
- If a richer decision model improves forecasting, that can be described as inferential charting rather than access.
- If actual state induction closes the gap, that is re-entry rather than representation.

On these definitions, no successful cold-side technique could count against the thesis. The article needs a prospective criterion distinguishing:

1. a representation whose causal influence increases;
2. partial physiological state induction;
3. full entry into the target state.

Without such a criterion, “every successful representation is partial state entry” is an immunising reinterpretation rather than an empirical conclusion.

## 6. Pain imagination and Noordhof

The presentation of Noordhof is largely fair and unusually well qualified. Noordhof argues only that certain **basic, first-person sensuous imaginings of painfulness** are difficult or impossible, and explains this through the aversive disposition associated with painfulness. He allows non-basic imagining and cases in which involuntary imagery, anxiety, depression or background motivation supplies an aversive disposition.  [White Rose Research Online](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/178347/1/document.pdf)

Three limitations should be made more prominent.

First, this is a philosophical-functional proposal about pain, not evidence for a general law covering hunger, sexual desire, fatigue, anger and addiction.

Second, Noordhof’s explanation is substantially deflationary: the aversive disposition helps constitute or explain painfulness. It does not posit a separately inaccessible phenomenal property.

Third, the exceptions are theoretically important. Involuntary imagery, anxiety and depression can apparently generate pain-like aversive involvement. That suggests variability among representational modes rather than a uniform boundary between representation and state.

The article acknowledges all three points locally but then proceeds to the general conditional—“If it extends to other visceral states”—on which much of the structural argument depends. That extrapolation currently has no independent evidence.

## 7. Social pain, torture and institutional consequences

### Social pain

Nordgren, Banas and MacDonald reported that participants currently experiencing experimentally induced exclusion rated social suffering more severely than non-excluded participants, including when judging their own past suffering. The article accurately reports the authors’ interpretation.  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21219077/)

But the experiment does not independently establish that the hot group was objectively accurate. Possible interpretations include:

- cold participants underestimating social pain;
- excluded participants generalising their current distress;
- mood-congruent amplification;
- demand effects;
- or some combination.

The result supports state-dependent judgement. It does not by itself identify which state has privileged epistemic authority.

### Torture

The interrogation-tactics paper similarly found that people undergoing mild cold or related discomfort classified painful techniques more severely, with its fourth experiment supporting the authors’ interpretation that the cold group underestimated pain.  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21483029/)

The article is too quick to write, “Here the hot state was the accurate one.” Accuracy here is relative to ratings produced under a mild laboratory analogue. The study does not provide an objective threshold for torture, reproduce interrogation conditions, or establish that a current state of discomfort confers correct normative classification. Its policy implication is plausible but should be labelled an extrapolation from low-intensity analogues.

The institutional paragraph also conflates distinct evidential levels:

- state-dependent pain estimation;
- clinical undertreatment of pain;
- evaluation of bullying;
- and legal classification of torture.

Each may involve a hot–cold gap, but the laboratory evidence does not support them with equal directness.

### Kang and Camerer

Kang and Camerer found that participants paid more to avoid eating disliked foods when the choice was real rather than hypothetical, with corresponding differences in insula and amygdala activity.  [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2013.00104/full)

This is not a clean cross-state study. Its principal manipulation is **consequential versus hypothetical choice**, not the same person occupying different visceral states. It is relevant to hypothetical bias and incentive-compatible valuation, but only analogically relevant to the Cross-State Void. The neural results do not identify an otherwise unrepresentable phenomenal “weight”; they show that consequential decisions engage valuation-related processes differently. The paper should be demoted from candidate mechanism to a neighbouring result.

## 9. Missed philosophical counterarguments

### 9.1 Representation need not duplicate what it represents

The article’s central contrast risks imposing an impossible standard. A representation of heat need not be hot; a representation of danger need not itself be dangerous; a representation of a command need not exert the command’s full force. Likewise, representing a craving need not produce the motivational urgency constitutive of craving.

Failure to instantiate the represented state therefore does not entail failure to represent it. To establish a genuine representational deficit, the article would need to show inaccurate discrimination, magnitude estimation, counterfactual reasoning or action prediction—not merely reduced phenomenological and motivational force.

This is the most important missing objection because it threatens to make the claimed void either false or trivial:

- If representation requires duplication, the void exists by definition but says little about cognition.
- If representation requires only sufficiently accurate modelling, several studies show partial success and improvement.

### 9.2 “Description” and “pull” are not exhaustive alternatives

Cross-state cognition can carry information in several forms:

- semantic description;
- episodic imagery;
- learned valuation;
- conditioned bodily response;
- policy or habit;
- statistical self-knowledge;
- external notes and behavioural records;
- simulations;
- commitment devices;
- or an individualised predictive model.

Read and Loewenstein’s durable WTAP effect is already evidence of a nontrivial action-guiding trace. Steinmetz supplies imagery-mediated effects. Ozdemir supplies structured model-based prediction. Treating all of this as “description” unless the full pull is recreated artificially narrows the taxonomy in favour of the conclusion.

### 9.3 Misprediction versus understood preference conflict

A person may understand that a future self will want something different while declining to endorse that preference. A cold smoker may accurately know that the craving self will pay a high price for a cigarette while treating that future valuation as one to resist. Conversely, the craving self may regard the cold self’s plans as motivationally alien.

The self-commitment literature therefore offers a rival explanation: some apparent gaps are not failures to represent a future preference but conflicts among state-dependent preferences. Uhl’s experiment found hot-to-cold but not cold-to-hot “gaps” when participants could commit themselves, while later symmetric commitment research found that both state-selves were similarly inclined to constrain the other.  [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/jrp/jrpwrp/2011-019.html)

This bears directly on the article’s distinction between accuracy and endorsement. The distinction is currently acknowledged but not developed into a serious rival model.

### 9.4 No neutral ground truth in many paradigms

In several studies, one state’s answer is treated as the standard against which the other state is judged:

- actual craving valuation is treated as the criterion for the cold forecast;
- current pain is treated as the criterion for remembered or anticipated pain;
- excluded participants’ ratings are treated as the criterion for social suffering;
- current discomfort is treated as the criterion for torture severity.

That may be reasonable when the question is “What will I choose while in that state?” It is less straightforward when the question is “Which self’s valuation is rational, authentic or normatively authoritative?” The article’s authority section begins to recognise this, but the empirical sections often continue to describe the state difference as straightforward blindness.

### 9.5 Individual and domain differences

The article treats cross-state failure as a general architecture of mind, but the cited work does not establish uniformity. Likely moderators include imagery vividness, interoceptive sensitivity, repeated calibration, expertise, addiction severity, state intensity, elapsed time and whether predictions concern immediate feeling, choice, endurance or later endorsement.

The claim that having occupied the state “helps less than it should” also lacks a benchmark. Read and Loewenstein show a large persistent effect of prior experience; what further level of transport should be expected, and by which theory?

## 10. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article deserves credit for several unusually explicit concessions. It now states that an epiphenomenalist can predict the same choices, that Noordhof reverses the asymbolia explanation, that the ability hypothesis accommodates the Mary analogy, and that Many Worlds and Minimal Quantum Interaction have no bearing. These are genuine instances of evidential discipline rather than superficial caveats.  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

Nevertheless, four protective moves remain.

### 10.1 Counterevidence is redescribed as state entry

The treatment of Steinmetz is the clearest case. Successful simulation is allowed only by redefining it as partial installation of the target state. Unless “partial state” has an independent physiological or functional criterion, the thesis cannot lose.

### 10.2 Compatibility is kept rhetorically adjacent to support

The page calls the void a “negative image” of Bidirectional Interaction even while conceding that a missing physical motivational state predicts the same result. The behavioural data show that present motivational state affects behaviour. They do not distinguish conscious causation from physical motivational processing. The tenet connection should therefore be labelled **illustrative compatibility only**, not a bearing on the tenet.

### 10.3 The Mary analogy adds dualist atmosphere without evidential work

A formerly sighted person unable to recreate seeing red is not epistemically analogous to Mary before her first colour experience. The cold subject possesses autobiographical, recognitional, comparative and practical knowledge that pre-release Mary lacks. Once the article concedes that the ability hypothesis reads the datum equally well, the analogy contributes more rhetorical association than analysis.

### 10.4 The Occam inference overreaches

The article says the cold self’s simpler explanation of its hot behaviour is “systematically wrong and uncorrectable by instruction,” using this as an example for the site’s limits-of-Occam tenet. One fatigue-attribution experiment cannot establish that dispositional explanation is generally the simpler hypothesis, generally wrong, or generally uncorrectable.  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

A narrower lesson is available: simple dispositional explanations can be favoured when relevant state information is inaccessible or underweighted. That is a useful epistemic point, but it is not a substantive argument against Occam’s razor.

## 11. Integration with neighbouring articles

### Direct taxonomy inconsistency

The Cross-State article now describes the phenomenon as “closed to the cold side but open to re-entry,” expressly rejecting the earlier Unexplorable/Occluded classification. “Three Kinds of Void” and the main voids register still retain the old classification, and the changelog explicitly records this propagation debt.  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

This should be treated as more than a wording mismatch. It raises the question whether the phenomenon belongs in the void taxonomy at all. A repeatedly re-enterable, partially correctable and inferentially modellable state gap is unlike McGinn-style cognitive closure. It may need a new category such as **state-indexed access limitation** or **recurrent attenuation gap**.

### Inaccessible Past

“The Inaccessible Past” says, “Only propositional content is reliably preserved.” That is too categorical given:

- Morley’s finding that many participants retained some sensory-quality memory;
- Read and Loewenstein’s durable change in willingness to undergo pain;
- and the Cross-State article’s own acknowledgement of inferentially recoverable behavioural records.  [voids/inaccessible-past](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/inaccessible-past/)

That page should distinguish literal phenomenal recurrence from sensory imagery, affective traces, conditioned responses, action-guiding memory and propositional report.

### Precommitment

The Cross-State sentence groups “Ulysses contracts, advance directives and notes written during craving” as devices transmitting the hot self’s verdict to the cold self. This conflates opposite directions. A standard Ulysses contract carries the cold planner’s verdict forward and binds the later hot self; a note written during craving carries information in the reverse direction. The changelog had already identified this error, and the dedicated precommitment page gives the correct binder/bound structure.  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

The precommitment page should also incorporate the symmetric commitment evidence and link it back to the authority section of the Cross-State article.  [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214804322001410)

### Affective Forecasting Gap

The neighbouring affective-forecasting article is in some respects more careful than the Cross-State page. It distinguishes wanting from liking and acknowledges that anticipatory motivation may diverge from predicted pleasure without either constituting direct access to future feeling.  [concepts/affective-forecasting-gap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/affective-forecasting-gap/)

The two pages should use a shared taxonomy:

- **projection bias:** current state projected into future preferences;
- **impact bias:** error about the intensity or duration of future affect;
- **hot–cold gap:** state difference affecting prediction or valuation;
- **wanting–liking dissociation:** motivational salience differing from hedonic impact;
- **phenomenal non-reinstantiation:** remembering or imagining without undergoing.

At present “Cross-State Void” risks treating these as manifestations of one missing weight.

### Pain, Consciousness, and Causal Power / Valence Void

The Cross-State page correctly states Noordhof’s reverse explanation of pain asymbolia: the missing aversive disposition may explain the missing painfulness, rather than a missing phenomenal painfulness explaining the behaviour. The pain article still repeatedly treats the asymbolia dissociation as isolating an affective phenomenal ingredient and as posing special explanatory trouble for physicalist theories.  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

The Noordhof caveat should be propagated to the pain and valence pages, not confined to the Cross-State article. Otherwise the same evidence is treated neutrally on one page and dualism-favouringly on another.

### Non-human minds

The categorical statement that “a language model has no visceral states” is stronger than the article needs and presupposes a contested conclusion about artificial consciousness. The next sentence already supplies the appropriate qualification by referring to systems with persistent valence-bearing states. The paragraph should instead address **current text-only language models lacking identified persistent valence-bearing control states**, or explicitly mark the claim as the Map’s present position rather than an established result.  [voids/cross-state-void](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/cross-state-void/)

## Final verdict

The article is **empirically respectable at its centre but metaphysically overinterpreted at its boundary**.

Its most defensible contribution is to place several normally separated literatures—visceral projection bias, pain memory, mental simulation, diachronic authority and transformative experience—under a single question: what does prior experience allow one state-self to know and do concerning another?

Its principal error is failure to distinguish four increasingly strong propositions:

1. **Non-identity:** representing a state is not undergoing it.
   This is true but unsurprising.
2. **Attenuation:** representations normally exert less motivational force than the represented state.
   This is plausible and reasonably supported.
3. **Forecasting impairment:** attenuation often creates measurable prediction and valuation errors.
   This is moderately supported, with important domain and method qualifications.
4. **Structural unrepresentability:** no cold-side representational process can recover the relevant weight or correct its behavioural consequences.
   This is not established and is challenged by durable learning, mental simulation, structured preference elicitation and higher-order correction strategies.

The article currently moves from propositions 1–3 to proposition 4 without sufficient evidence. Its dualist significance is correspondingly minimal: the empirical findings are at least as naturally explained by state-dependent utility, incentive salience, interoceptive simulation, memory and motivational circuitry as by an irreducible phenomenal ingredient.

## Concrete potential improvements

### To “The Cross-State Void”

1. **Replace the categorical lede.** Change “what crosses is a description” to a graded claim: prior experience carries semantic, episodic and action-guiding traces, but normally fails to recreate the full concurrent motivational force of the state.
2. **Define “weight” operationally.** Separate phenomenal intensity, motivational urgency, monetary valuation, behavioural choice, remembered intensity and normative endorsement. Do not treat findings in one measure as direct evidence about all the others.
3. **Divide the thesis into four tiers:** non-reinstantiation, attenuation, forecasting error and structural unrepresentability. Give each tier a separate evidential grade.
4. **Add Fisher and Rangel 2014** as the principal evidence for bidirectionality, including its symmetric 20-cent/19-cent result and its limits.
5. **Add Ozdemir et al. 2024** as a counterexample to the claim that cold-side representations cannot be improved without inducing the target state.
6. **Cite the 2026 systematic review** and revise the literature-status sentence to say that no dedicated effect-size meta-analysis was found, while broader systematic synthesis now exists.
7. **Narrow “survives disclosure.”** State that one simple corrective instruction failed in one attribution paradigm; distinguish disclosure, calibration, feedback, structured elicitation and external scaffolding.
8. **Read and cite the full Helton–Register paper.** Remove the abstract-only disclaimer and include their concessions concerning higher-order knowledge, state selection and testimonial understanding.
9. **Treat Read and Loewenstein as evidence of partial transport.** Explicitly acknowledge that prior pain experience produced a durable action-guiding effect a week later.
10. **Make the Steinmetz response falsifiable.** Specify independent criteria for deciding when imagery is representation and when it counts as partial state induction.
11. **Demote Kang and Camerer.** Present it as evidence about real-versus-hypothetical consequential choice, not as direct evidence for a cross-visceral-state mechanism.
12. **Qualify the social-pain and torture inferences.** Distinguish state-dependent judgement from demonstrated accuracy, and laboratory analogues from institutional policy evidence.
13. **Verify Scarry directly.** Cite page 7 and remove the published note that the passage remains unverified.
14. **Correct the Ulysses sentence.** Separate cold-to-hot commitment devices from hot-to-cold records or messages.
15. **Remove or sharply qualify the Mary analogy.** The inability to recreate a familiar state differs importantly from Mary’s lack of first acquaintance.
16. **Replace the categorical language-model claim** with a scoped claim about present architectures lacking identified persistent valence-bearing states.
17. **State prospective falsifiers.** Examples should include reliable cold-state prediction after repeated calibration, structured models that outperform intuitive forecasts, vivid simulation without measurable state induction, or evidence that experiential familiarity asymptotically removes the gap.
18. **Consider renaming the article** “The Cross-State Attenuation Gap” unless the stronger structural thesis can be independently defended.

### To surrounding articles

19. **“Three Kinds of Void” and the main voids register:** propagate the revised state-indexed classification and decide whether a re-enterable, corrigible gap is properly a void.
20. **“The Inaccessible Past”:** replace “Only propositional content is reliably preserved” with a taxonomy covering propositional memory, sensory imagery, affective traces, conditioned responses and phenomenal re-experiencing.
21. **“Precommitment and the Diachronic Agent”:** add the symmetric commitment-device evidence and link it to the Cross-State authority problem.
22. **“The Affective-Forecasting Gap”:** add reciprocal links and a shared terminology table distinguishing projection bias, impact bias, wanting, liking and phenomenal reinstantiation.
23. **“Pain, Consciousness, and Causal Power” and “The Valence Void”:** propagate Noordhof’s reverse causal explanation and mark asymbolia as compatible with, rather than evidence for, the Map’s preferred causal order.
24. **“Mood Void” and “Voids Between Minds”:** add reciprocal integration rather than relying on links only from the Cross-State page.

### To the site’s methodology

25. **Require a claim–evidence matrix** for empirical articles: construct, sample, manipulation, outcome, effect, directness, replication status, strongest alternative and evidential grade.
26. **Do not use abstract-only summaries for load-bearing claims** when a full text is available.
27. **Record literature searches reproducibly:** date, databases, search strings, eligibility criteria and whether the claim is “no study exists” or merely “none was located.”
28. **Create a propagation gate.** A revision should not be marked complete while the changelog records contradictory descriptions on canonical sibling pages.
29. **Require page verification for every quotation** before publication; unresolved quotation leads should remain in research notes rather than live articles.
30. **Separate compatibility from support mechanically.** Every tenet-facing section should identify a rival that would not predict the evidence; where none exists, it should say “illustrative only.”
31. **Require an explicit counterevidence criterion.** An article must state in advance what result would lower confidence in its preferred interpretation and must not redescribe every successful intervention as an instance of the thesis.
32. **Introduce replication-risk labels** for single-lab, modest-sample, pre-registration-era psychology, especially where policy or metaphysical conclusions depend on it.
33. **Relax hard word caps or permit evidence appendices.** Sixteen words of remaining headroom is not compatible with incorporating direct replications, current reviews, construct distinctions and serious rival models.
34. **Schedule literature-refresh triggers** for behavioural-science pages whose main empirical sources are over ten years old, rather than limiting revisions to previously logged wording issues.
