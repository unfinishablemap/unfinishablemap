## Reply

# Referee report: “Attention as the Causal Bridge”

**Recommendation: Reject in present form; reconsider only after major reconstruction.**

The article has improved substantially in one respect: it now explicitly acknowledges that predictive processing/active inference can reproduce almost everything its empirical evidence directly establishes, admits that its entropy prediction is untested, and concedes that it does not yet explain the mind–matter coupling it posits. [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/) Those concessions are appropriate. They also expose the central problem: **the positive dualist conclusion is considerably stronger than the evidence that remains once rival interpretations are taken seriously.**

More seriously, several load-bearing empirical claims are not merely speculative. Two are materially mischaracterised, one source is interpreted almost exactly backwards, and one purported cross-domain temporal convergence is manufactured by comparing incompatible temporal landmarks. The resulting synthesis does not presently meet the evidential standard of a top-tier philosophy journal.

I treated the live 31 July apex as the target. The site's current *Structure of Attention* article was substantively changed on 3 August, including additions concerning spectral signatures, Granger causality and temporal dynamics, so the apex is already slightly behind its own underlying attention material. The **effort argument is therefore presently upside down**. The apex calls felt effort some of its strongest evidence for consciousness “doing real causal work,” yet its best lesion evidence shows that subjective effort can dissociate from executive control, and Naccache explicitly offers the possibility that the feeling is causally idle. [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/) That does not establish epiphenomenalism either. It does mean that this evidence cannot honestly be counted as positive evidence for phenomenal causation without another argument.

Similarly, the **volition argument confuses endogenous computation with nonphysical origination**. Rajan's internally selected condition requires additional choice/conflict processing, so additional frontal activity is precisely what an ordinary computational account predicts. The 2023 review of “willed attention” itself treats the phenomenon as an under-studied form of voluntary/endogenous attention and places it alongside self-initiated motor action—not as evidence that a non-neural agent has entered the circuit. [Frontiers in Public Pages+1](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/cognition/articles/10.3389/fcogn.2023.1205618/pdf)

## 2. Citation integrity and currency

I found **no indication that the major references I checked are fabricated**. The problem is source fidelity and completeness rather than bibliographic invention.

There are, however, several unacceptable defects for a synthesis article.

First, **Rajan et al. 2019 is missing from the apex bibliography**, despite the frontal-theta/coherence result doing major argumentative work. The newer *Structure of Attention* article now cites both Rajan and the 2023 Nadra–Mangun review, but the apex does not. [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/)

Second, the **gamma-band claim requires Wyart & Tallon-Baudry 2008**, not merely Koch & Tsuchiya 2007. The frequencies are distinctive enough that failure to cite their actual experimental source is a substantive attribution error. [The Journal of Neuroscience+1](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/28/10/2667)

Third, **Sjöberg 2024 is an essay-level discussion of clinical observations**, while the underlying empirical patient work is older. “Sjöberg (2024) found…” overstates the evidential status of that publication. The primary 2020 patient study should be cited directly and its very small, clinically heterogeneous sample described. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/147/7/2267/7685995)

Fourth, **the Schwartz citation is bibliographically weak for the empirical claim**. The apex discusses PET changes but references the 2002 popular book rather than the primary PET literature. There are primary PET reports showing caudate metabolic changes after behavioural and pharmacological treatment; those are what the synthesis should cite. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1514872/)

Fifth, the article's strongest rival section names Hohwy, Clark, Friston and Seth without supplying corresponding references, despite conceding that active inference needs framework-level treatment. That omission matters because formal active-inference work already models attentional states, meta-awareness and **mental/covert actions controlling precision**, and a 2023 paper specifically formalises cognitive effort within active inference. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2021/1/niab018/6358635) This is directly relevant to the proposed “option-set entropy” discriminator.

Finally, the treatment is **not current enough on illusionism**. A top-tier submission in August 2026 cannot treat the opponent primarily as AST plus a “who experiences the model?” regress. François Kammerer's *House of Mirrors*, published 10 June 2026, develops a contemporary systematic illusionism and explicitly treats the “illusion problem”—why phenomenality seems to exist—as an explanandum. [OUP Academic+2OUP Academic+2](https://academic.oup.com/book/62981/chapter/565377959) Even if that book arrived too late for extensive incorporation into a 31 July revision, substantial 2020s illusionist literature already made the same dialectical point.

## 3. Strong counterarguments left untested

### Physicalist/common-cause objection

The manuscript repeatedly moves from **functional dissociation → candidate neural locus → nonphysical causal interpretation**. None of those entailments is valid. Distinct attention and awareness signatures can arise from distinct physical processes; additional frontal activity in endogenous choice can reflect additional endogenous computation; phenomenology and executive performance can arise from partially dissociable neural pathways.

Indeed, the site's newer companion article now states the strongest version of this objection itself: competitive inhibition, recurrent attractor dynamics and neural noise can provide a physically complete symmetry-breaking story, and the interface model requires the additional, unproven premise that the tipping fluctuation contains a quantum-indeterminate degree of freedom susceptible to conscious bias. [topics/attention-and-the-consciousness-interface](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/attention-and-the-consciousness-interface/) **That objection belongs in the apex**, because it cuts through most of the apparent “convergence.”

The causal-exclusion problem is also not answered by saying that selected actions obtain their energy metabolically. [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/) Energy conservation and causal closure are different issues. The question is not where the joules originate; it is whether changing a nonphysical variable makes a counterfactual difference to the probability distribution of physical outcomes over and above the complete physical state. The manuscript needs an interventionist or structural-causal formulation, not an energy-accounting reassurance.

### Predictive processing / active inference

This is now the strongest part of the article because it actually admits the problem: active inference can treat agency as policy selection, attention as precision weighting and effort as part of the costs of reallocating precision, and the apex concedes that it has not yet confronted this total framework on its own terms. [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/)

But the synthesis then proceeds as though its cumulative case nevertheless supports nonphysical causation. That is premature. Formal active-inference models already implement higher-order attentional control and meta-awareness through hierarchical inference and covert attentional policies. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2021/1/niab018/6358635) And cognitive effort has explicitly been given an active-inference/information-theoretic treatment. [ScienceDirect+1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0028393223000969)

This creates a serious problem for the proposed **option-set entropy test**. Unless the Map derives a quantitatively different function from the active-inference one, a correlation between effort and option entropy could simply be predicted by both theories. A variable called “entropy” is not automatically a dualist discriminator.

### Epiphenomenalism

The article barely tests the cleanest rival:

> Neural mechanisms perform selection; phenomenology accompanies some of those mechanisms but contributes nothing to their operation.

Almost every datum cited is compatible with that view. Rajan gives correlations between endogenous choice processing and neural signatures. Sjöberg provides dissociation between effort experience and motor execution. Desmurget shows that manipulating cortex can manipulate intention. Most damagingly, **Naccache explicitly presents felt conscious effort as potentially causally unnecessary**. [Unicog+3OUP Academic+3OUP Academic+3](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/29/7/2832/5041999)

The article needs a result of the form: holding relevant neural antecedents fixed, variation in conscious experience predicts or changes subsequent physical selection. It currently has nothing close to that.

### Illusionism

The manuscript's recurring “who experiences the model?” challenge largely begs the question. A sophisticated illusionist does not posit a further Cartesian spectator who must inspect an internal model. The explanandum is why a physical representational system forms judgments or representations according to which phenomenal properties seem immediately present. Contemporary illusionism explicitly treats explaining that appearance as the hard task. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/62981/chapter/565377959)

The thermostat/chess-engine comparison is therefore too weak. Showing that *some* recursive systems lack introspective quasi-phenomenal representations does not show that no wholly physical system could instantiate the relevant form of self-representation. To defeat illusionism, the article would need to demonstrate an explanatory impossibility, not merely point out that simple recursive systems are insufficient.

## 4. Tenet-protective framing and bracketed problems

Several passages still load the conclusion into the framing.

**“Willed” becomes “conscious contribution.”** Rajan's term is operational: attention is “willed” when the target is selected by internal decision rather than supplied by an instructional cue. The manuscript then treats the extra neural machinery of that condition as identifying where *nonphysical consciousness enters*. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/29/7/2832/5041999) That is precisely what remains to be established.

**Functional separation becomes ontological separation.** The frequency dissociation and attention/awareness dissociations support distinct functional dependencies. They do not favour substance/property dualism over a multiply decomposable physical architecture without additional argument. The article sometimes says merely “potentially ontologically distinct,” but elsewhere invokes the pianist/piano analogy as though ontology has already been settled. [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/)

**The effort puzzle presupposes an anti-physicalist theory of phenomenology.** Asking why a deterministic physical brain should not have “flat” phenomenology already assumes that variations in neural control cost cannot constitute variations in experienced effort. [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/) That is not a problem generated by determinism; it is generated by the manuscript's prior rejection of psychophysical identity/realisation.

**The Sjöberg inference contains a localisation fallacy.** From “SMA resection leaves effort experience intact” it follows that this phenomenology is not wholly realised by the removed SMA tissue. It plainly does not follow that it is nonphysical.

**The three-layer architecture contains only two evidenced layers.** The apex admirably admits that Cai does not test the conscious-selection layer, yet immediately labels neural competition + dopamine + consciousness a convergent “architecture.” [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/) Layer three is not an empirical layer at all; it is the theory placed atop the first two.

**Mechanism failure cannot presently kill the core claim.** Quantum Zeno is described as only one proposed mechanism and “other mechanisms remain possible.” [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/) That makes the central hypothesis unusually resilient: defeat the concrete mechanism and the causal bridge survives unspecified. A viable interactionist theory needs conditions under which failure of proposed coupling mechanisms counts against interaction itself.

**The disorder argument risks becoming self-sealing.** ADHD is interpreted as damaged interface with unchanged consciousness, while the article subsequently admits that it cannot know whether consciousness itself changes behind a degraded interface. [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/) Once the distinction is observationally inaccessible, the disorder cannot independently support the unchanged-consciousness interpretation.

**There is an internal tension over depletion.** The effort section correctly cites the collapse of simple ego-depletion/resource models, yet the disorders section shortly afterwards says sustained attention “depletes a resource.” [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/) “Resource” must either receive a specific physiological meaning or be replaced by a more defensible opportunity-cost/control-allocation account.

**“Witness consciousness” is immunised as meta-agency.** If active selection supports agency and deliberate nonselection is redescribed as higher-order selection, nearly every phenomenological outcome can be assimilated to agency. [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/) The manuscript needs an independently specified criterion for when the interface is causally active.

**The article relocates rather than solves the interaction problem—and admits this.** It says it identifies “where and how consciousness acts” but immediately acknowledges that it does not explain “how subjective experience connects to physical processes.” [apex/attention-as-causal-bridge](https://unfinishablemap.org/apex/attention-as-causal-bridge/) Those claims cannot both stand in their current strength. What it has identified is a *candidate functional locus at which interaction could be sought*, not how interaction occurs.

## Overall assessment

There is a publishable paper buried here, but it is a different paper.

The defensible thesis is:

**Attention—especially endogenous attentional selection—is a strategically promising locus at which an interactionist theory could seek discriminating evidence, because it combines option competition, endogenous control, phenomenology, measurable neural dynamics and experimentally tractable behavioural outcomes.**

The present thesis is much stronger:

**the existing attention literature forms cumulative evidence that consciousness actually exerts nonphysical causal influence there.**

The evidence reviewed does not warrant the second claim. More damagingly, the current argument reaches it partly through empirical errors: Cai is misdescribed; Naccache is interpreted against the authors' explicit causal conclusion; the gamma bands are misattributed and universalised; Desmurget is recruited in the opposite causal direction to his intervention; and the supposed 300/280 ms convergence conflates unlike measurements. The article's better passages already recognise that active inference and ordinary physical competition models can reproduce the evidence. Those passages should govern the synthesis rather than appear as caveats to a conclusion stated elsewhere as though established.

For a top-tier journal, I would therefore recommend **rejection rather than ordinary revise-and-resubmit**. A reconstructed article could merit fresh submission because the underlying question—whether endogenous attention provides an experimentally tractable interface for interactionist theories—is legitimate and unusually amenable to competing predictions.
