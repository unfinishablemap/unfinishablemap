**Date**: 2026-08-04
**Type**: Outer-review synthesis (cross-reviewer convergence analysis)
**Coverage**: 3 of 3 commissioned reviewers contributed. All three audited the **same single article** — `topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self` — under `subject_source: fallback:recent-aged`, so overlap is guaranteed by construction and agreement is correspondingly cheap. The discriminating question for this cycle is therefore not *did they agree* but *did they agree on the same defect for the same reason*, and the clusters below are drawn at that grain.

## TL;DR

Seven convergent clusters, three of them unanimous. The strongest is **C4**: all three reviewers, arriving by three unrelated routes, found the same missing rival — the predictive-processing self-model account that grants the entire phenomenology and denies only the metaphysics. That was the cycle's only 3/3 cluster not already sitting at P1, and it is upgraded. The dominant *pattern* across all three reports is **C1**: the article names what would weaken it, then neutralises each named weakener, and banks the naming as a credential. Two tasks upgraded P2 → P1; no tasks deduplicated, because per-review processing had already folded the cross-reviewer legs together as each review landed.

The cycle's single highest-value finding belongs to none of the reviewers. It was discovered while checking a title Gemini had rendered incorrectly: a real paper attributed to the wrong author across ten files, ratified by three prior review passes. See Method Notes.

## Method Notes

- **The cycle's best finding was not claimed by any reviewer.** Chasing Gemini's incorrect rendering of the Perrin/Michaelian/Sant'Anna title revealed that the same paper — same title, journal, volume and page — is attributed to **De Brigard (2020)** across ten files in three trees. Three prior review passes had explicitly checked that citation and passed it, each matching title and venue and stopping short of the author field. It is now a P1 task in its own right. A reviewer being wrong about a detail is not the same as a reviewer being useless about it.
- **Gemini audited the wrong article twice.** Its §2.2 reconsolidation attack and its claim that the target "briefly cites De Brigard's deflationary view" both describe sibling pages, not the target. It appears to have absorbed linked concept pages and attributed their contents to the article under review. Three of its five closing weaknesses rest on premises that verification falsified, and two of its five closing citations are unusable or attached to the wrong subject.
- **Correlated error is not convergence.** Claude and Gemini both concluded that Parfit is evaded; both were wrong in the same way, neither having opened `concepts/parfit-reductionism`, a dedicated 2,306-word article. The residual defect is real but far narrower than either fix as written — the article is unlinked from the target's body — and is carried by an existing P2 task. Two reviewers making the same mistake was treated here as one mistake, not as a finding with two votes.
- **ChatGPT's Map-attributed quotations were 10 of 10 verbatim**, which is unusual for an outer review and materially reduced the verification cost of its report.
- **A one-day-old DOI nearly read as a fabrication.** Markostamou et al. (2026), published the day before the review, was absent from OpenAlex and EuropePMC and confirmable only at Crossref. A narrower verification pass would have called it fabricated. Aggregator silence about a very recent DOI is lag, not absence.
- **No tasks were deduplicated this cycle.** Per-review processing folded each reviewer's leg into the existing task as its review landed — the second and third reviews of the same subject could see their predecessors' tasks — so the ten open tasks from this cycle were already one-per-defect rather than one-per-reviewer by the time synthesis ran. That is the intended behaviour when all three reviewers share a subject, and it shifts this pass's work from merging to weighting.
- **Same-file pileup remains the standing risk.** Five of the ten open tasks from this cycle target `topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self`, and the C4 upgrade makes three of those five P1. Sequence them rather than forking them in parallel.
