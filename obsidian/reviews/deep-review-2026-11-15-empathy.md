## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Tenet misattribution (L109)**: the sentence read "If experiences are ontologically individual, as the Dualism tenet holds". The Dualism tenet (`tenets.md` L51–L53) defines only irreducibility, and it is explicitly neutral between substance and property dualism. On a property-dualist reading, experiences are individuated by their physical bearers. That is exactly the "two token systems" answer this paragraph credits to the physicalist. The further-fact individuation this paragraph needs is Posit One in `tenets/background-commitments` ("Primitive subject individuation"), which that page says "no tenet states outright". A reviewer who accepts the tenets would still flag this, so it is a correctable attribution error, not a bedrock disagreement. **Fixed**: "(a [[tenets/background-commitments|posit the Map adopts]] beside the Dualism tenet)" (+4 words). The following "it" still refers to non-coincidence.
- **Map-internal gloss drift (L99)**: "the Map's [[mine-ness]] analysis, on which every experience presents itself as belonging to a subject". The live mine-ness page treats mine-ness as a *separable* feature. L48 says it can "vary independently". L58 says "two otherwise similar states can differ in whether they carry the mine-ness tag". L88 says "mine-ness can fade while consciousness continues". The universal "every" therefore misstates the Map's own analysis. This was already stale when the page was created: the coalesced mine-ness page with the separability thesis predates 2026-09-26. **Fixed**: "every experience presents itself" became "experience ordinarily presents itself" (0 words).

### Medium Issues Found

- **Source/Map step in Bidirectional (L115)**: the sentence ran "Grief shows in the face as a unity with the grief … If that description is right, experience *shapes* bodily comportment". Stein's and Scheler's description is one of unity or co-givenness (Stein: the distress is "co-given" and the two "form a natural unity", SEP Stein, 1917: 95[77]). It is not a causal claim. Jacob's first horn shows that the unity description can just as well be read constitutively. The causal reading belongs to the Map, and the sentence's next clause already marks it as the Map's. **Fixed**: "shapes" became "is present in" (+2), so the causal step now sits only in "which the Map reads as".
- **Unsupported comparative (L77)**: "Dan Zahavi has done most to bring this tradition into the current debate." Gallagher, whom the article cites at L89 as co-proponent, has an equal claim. **Fixed**: "done most" became "done much" (0).
- **Jacob gloss (L89), considered and kept**: "the perceptual claim either overreaches or collapses into something like fast inference". Jacob's abstract poses the dilemma "either they embrace behaviorism or else they must recognize that one could not understand another's goal or emotion from her behavior alone without making contextual assumptions". The body's second dilemma adds "what is the further principle that enables the phenomenologist to assert that only an agent's goal, not her relevant belief, can be directly perceived?" The article's "overreaches … or collapses into something like fast inference" fairly compresses that second dilemma and the contextual-assumptions horn, but it omits the behaviourism horn. It is hedged ("something like") and was left as it is.
