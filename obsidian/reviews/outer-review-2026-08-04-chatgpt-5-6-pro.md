## 1. Claims resting on insufficient evidence

### 1.1 “Double presence” is presented too nearly as a universal structure

The opening describes remembering as the self encountering itself across time and says this “double presence” constitutes the temporally extended self. It later treats the immediate sense that “that was me” as built into autobiographical recollection. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

That is plausible for many vivid episodic memories, but it should not be written as the invariant form of autobiographical memory. Work on severely deficient autobiographical memory, or SDAM, documents healthy and otherwise cognitively capable people with markedly reduced first-person episodic re-experiencing despite retained autobiographical knowledge and functioning. More recent case work continues to find selective impairment of episodic re-experiencing rather than wholesale loss of selfhood or autobiographical competence. [ScienceDirect+3ScienceDirect+3Memory Time Space Lab+3](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002839321500158X)

SDAM does not prove that autonoesis is unimportant. It does show that the article must distinguish:

1. autobiographical factual knowledge;
2. vivid episodic re-experiencing;
3. present ownership of a representation;
4. subjective continuity; and
5. numerical personal identity.

The current prose repeatedly slides among these.

### 1.2 “Pastness” does not distinguish genuine memory from imagination in the required sense

The article calls pastness a quale “as distinctive as redness” and initially says it distinguishes genuine memory from vivid imagining. Later, however, it concedes that confabulation can reproduce the relevant felt character and that source-monitoring systems may generate the verdict represented by felt pastness. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

These passages leave three different claims insufficiently separated:

- **Phenomenological claim:** an experience currently feels past-directed.
- **Metacognitive claim:** the subject judges that its content originated in personal experience.
- **Causal-veridical claim:** the representation actually derives in an appropriate way from the event represented.

Felt pastness may indicate the first and sometimes track the second. Confabulation and false-memory phenomena show that it is neither sufficient for the third nor an infallible discriminator of genuine causal memory. The article partly recognises this, but its opening and neighbouring pages continue to use pastness as though it authenticated memory.

The claim that source monitoring first reaches a verdict and pastness then “accurately reflects” it is also too simple. Source attribution can be graded, uncertain and partly constructed through the same cues that contribute to the feeling of remembering. The experience need not be a transparent display of a prior computational verdict.

### 1.3 “No analogue in imagination” is too strong

The article says the double temporal positioning of memory has no analogue in perception or imagination. But it also invokes the constructive episodic system shared by remembering and future simulation. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

Episodic future thought, counterfactual simulation and observer-perspective imagination can all involve a present subject representing another temporally located version of itself. What may distinguish autobiographical remembering is not the bare existence of dual self-positioning, but a combination of:

- past-directed temporal orientation;
- a claim or feeling of causal derivation;
- source attribution;
- familiarity;
- ownership; and
- varying degrees of sensory and affective reinstatement.

That narrower thesis is defensible. “No analogue” is not.

### 1.4 The article equivocates between memory revealing a temporally extended self and constituting one

At different points, remembering is said to disclose persistence, constitute the temporally extended self, support narrative integration, and show that “something must persist.” [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

These are distinct positions:

- **Epistemic:** memory gives evidence of continuity.
- **Phenomenological:** memory presents the subject as continuous.
- **Psychological-constitutive:** memory relations partly constitute a temporally extended psychological person.
- **Practical:** memory supports planning and accountability.
- **Metaphysical:** memory reveals a numerically identical substantial subject.

Only the first two receive direct phenomenological support. Psychological and practical constitution require further argument. The metaphysical conclusion requires much more: “something must persist” does not establish that the persisting item is a non-physical substance rather than an organism, a causally continuous psychological process, a policy structure, a socially maintained person-role, or an overlapping series of person-stages.

### 1.5 Diachronic agency is not shown to depend on narrative remembering

The article says narrative integration supports diachronic agency and sometimes writes as though such agency depends on a remembered narrative. That overlooks well-developed alternatives. Bratman’s planning theory explains intertemporal self-governance through policies, intentions and coordination with one’s future self; narrative may enrich this structure without constituting it. Strawson’s anti-narrativism directly rejects both the descriptive claim that human selves are generally experienced narratively and the normative claim that a good life requires such narration. [Cognitive Lab+2Stanford Philosophy+2](https://lchc.ucsd.edu/mca/Paper/against_narrativity.pdf)

Habits, standing values, semantic self-knowledge, prospective intentions, bodily routines and social scaffolding can also maintain agency when vivid episodic recollection is weak. A defensible formulation would be that autobiographical narrative is **one important route** to diachronic coordination, not a necessary condition of it.

### 1.6 “Every remembering is reinterpretation” exceeds the evidence

The article is appropriately cautious when it says retrieval **can** reshape memory, but elsewhere says every act of remembering is reinterpretation. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

Constructive memory does not entail that every retrieval materially revises a memory trace or changes its semantic interpretation. Reconsolidation research identifies boundary conditions involving memory strength, age, retrieval duration and prediction error; retrieval does not invariably destabilise an established memory. [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7167366/)

The stronger formulation should be replaced with something like: retrieval is an active reconstruction that **may** reweight, supplement, omit or update represented content under identifiable conditions.

### 1.7 The semantic-versus-phenomenal contrast is too binary

The article proposes that propositional content may persist reliably while phenomenal content is fragmentary. That captures some clinical cases, but not a general division between stable semantic content and unstable experience. Recent work indicates that internal thoughts and feelings may be especially liable to omission or semantic distortion, while emotionally intense and meaningfully integrated internal experience can also be preferentially retained and contribute strongly to perceived personal importance. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-026-00425-8)

Current models also increasingly treat autobiographical retrieval as multidimensional rather than as a strict episodic–semantic dichotomy. “Episodic narrative personal semantics,” for example, occupies an intermediate region, and recent frameworks distinguish multiple dimensions of content, reliving, temporal specificity and self-relevance. [Springer+2Royal Society Publishing+2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13415-023-01149-6)

This matters because the article itself says that evidence for a smooth functional gradient would weaken its dualist interpretation. The emerging literature already makes that gradient a live empirical possibility.

### 1.8 The claim that felt rightness is anti-correlated with accuracy is unsupported

The article says that felt rightness is “anti-correlated with accuracy in conditions that matter.” [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

The cited discussion supports dissociations between confidence, vividness, consistency and accuracy. It does not establish a general inverse relationship. Emotional autobiographical memories can remain vivid and confidently endorsed while changing, but confidence can also track accuracy under other conditions. Recent reviews emphasise contextual variation rather than a blanket negative correlation. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-024-00312-1)

The article needs either:

- a specific experimental paradigm, population and outcome measure supporting an inverse correlation; or
- the weaker and better-supported claim that felt rightness is not a reliable guarantee of accuracy.

### 1.9 “Emotional continuity is overlooked” misdescribes the field

The article is right that affective resonance can contribute to subjective continuity. It is not right to suggest that this has generally been overlooked by psychological-continuity or autobiographical-memory research. Contemporary reviews explicitly distinguish several forms of self-continuity, examine emotional and motivational dimensions, and also consider circumstances in which experienced discontinuity can be adaptive. Work on emotional autobiographical memory likewise directly investigates the interaction among narrative, affective stability, malleability and identity. [University of Southampton+1](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~crsi/Sedikides%2C%20Hong%2C%20%26%20Wildschut%2C%202023%2C%20ARP.pdf)

The justified criticism is narrower: some **classical philosophical formulations** of psychological continuity underdescribe affective continuity. That should not be generalised to memory science or current narrative-identity research.

### 1.10 The “Proustian recovery or present simulation” question is not wholly unresolvable

The article declares it unresolvable whether apparent recovery of a past experience is genuine phenomenal recovery or present simulation. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

A very strong metaphysical question—whether the present qualitative token is numerically identical to a past token—may indeed resist ordinary empirical resolution. But several relevant questions are tractable:

- how much perceptual and contextual detail is reinstated;
- which internal states are preserved or semantically distorted;
- whether neural and behavioural reinstatement varies with vividness;
- which retrieval conditions improve later detail accuracy; and
- how unique, repeated and general memories differ phenomenologically.

Recent studies already measure some of these dimensions. [Nature+3Nature+3Nature+3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-026-00425-8)

The article should narrow the alleged irresolvability to numerical identity or exact phenomenal-token identity, rather than applying it to degrees and patterns of reinstatement.

## 2. Citation accuracy and source characterisation

### Husserl and Tulving

Both works are real and relevant, but the conceptual bridge is misleadingly compressed. Husserlian retention is the primal holding-on to the just-elapsed phase within ongoing temporal experience. Husserl distinguishes that structure from free recollection or re-presentation of an absent past event. Tulving’s autonoetic episodic remembering is therefore not simply the cognitive form of Husserlian retention. It is closer to Husserlian recollection, although the two traditions can productively illuminate one another. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-030-69521-7_3.pdf)

**Assessment:** genuine sources, broadly relevant, but the article should replace the implied identification with a carefully qualified analogy.

### Klein and Nichols’s patient RB

The source and case are real. The article has improved by conceding that both modular physicalism and dualism can predict a dissociation between remembered content and felt ownership. Nevertheless, it still treats RB as at least demonstrating the separability of mineness from remembered content. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

That interpretation is contested directly in the literature. Rebecca Roache argues that Klein and Nichols’s stronger characterisation of RB is not adequately supported: the case may establish that certain representations did not feel like memories to RB, but not the clean theoretical decomposition that the philosophical argument requires. [REBECCA ROACHE+1](https://rebeccaroache.weebly.com/uploads/2/4/9/0/24901968/memory_and_mineness_in_personal_identity.pdf)

**Assessment:** real and now more fairly framed, but still over-weighted. It should be identified as an interpretively contested single case and treated as hypothesis-generating rather than as a clean dissociation result.

### Tippett and colleagues on aMCI and Alzheimer’s disease

The work is real and the article’s use is directionally accurate: subjective continuity can remain despite serious episodic-memory impairment. But the underlying findings are more differentiated. Participants with amnestic mild cognitive impairment retained considerable autobiographical reasoning and coherence; in Alzheimer’s disease, continuity beliefs could remain while explanations of continuity were less sophisticated. The authors treated causal or narrative scaffolding as an intriguing possibility, not as an established mechanism. [Frontiers+2Frontiers+2](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00094/pdf)

**Assessment:** fair at a high level, but insufficiently granular. The article should distinguish diagnostic groups, continuity beliefs, explanatory sophistication and the tentative causal interpretation.

### Perrin, Fernández and the metacognitive debate

The article’s presentation of Perrin and colleagues’ 2020 metacognitive account, and its contrast with Fernández’s intentionalist view, is broadly fair. The omission is the subsequent development of the dispute. Perrin and Sant’Anna’s 2022 argument directly criticises intentionalism—particularly Fernández’s causal self-reference account—and gives a fuller defence of the metacognitive alternative. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

**Assessment:** the cited work is real and accurately used, but the literature review stops before a significant sequel that bears directly on the article’s central “felt pastness” issue.

### Matthen, Russell, Michaelian and neighbouring philosophy-of-memory sources

The changelog indicates that the 27 July pass corrected earlier framing of Matthen and Russell and rechecked the main load-bearing references. My spot-checking found no fabricated work among these sources and no remaining error comparable to the earlier defects. The positions are generally recognisable from the cited literature. [reviews/deep-review-2026-07-27-phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/reviews/deep-review-2026-07-27-phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

**Assessment:** bibliographic integrity is now good. The residual problem is argumentative rather than bibliographic: competing naturalistic accounts are introduced, but the article often treats their inability to settle the general hard problem as a memory-specific failure.

### Hurlburt and Petitmengin

Both methodological traditions are real. Descriptive Experience Sampling uses random prompts followed by carefully conducted interviews; microphenomenological interviewing aims to elicit fine-grained structures of experience. But these methods do not straightforwardly validate the historical accuracy of a remembered event. Microphenomenological work itself recognises that putting an experience into words is an intervention and that the method remains subject to refinement. [Claire Petitmengin+2Springer+2](https://clairepetitmengin.fr/articles)

The article names Hurlburt and Petitmengin but does not include them in its reference list. Its claim that such methods “restrict the confabulation-eligible space” is an uncited synthesis and should not be allowed to suggest that disciplined reporting validates memory content. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

**Assessment:** genuine methods, incomplete referencing, and an overextension from improved report discipline to possible event veridicality.

### Overall citation verdict

I found no fabricated load-bearing citation in the material checked. The site’s documented 27 July audit likewise reports that existence and metadata were almost entirely clean after correction. But “real citation” and “claim established by citation” should be kept distinct. The remaining weaknesses mostly involve conceptual compression, omitted direct criticism, missing methodological references and stronger conclusions than the cited work supports. [reviews/deep-review-2026-07-27-phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/reviews/deep-review-2026-07-27-phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

## 3. Missed counterarguments

### From memory science

The most important missing challenges are not reductive declarations that experience is unreal. They are accounts that accept the phenomenology while explaining its structure through graded cognitive mechanisms.

**SDAM and individual variation.** The article needs cases in which autobiographical knowledge and practical identity function without normal vivid reliving. These put pressure on claims that double presence, autonoesis or narrative recollection is constitutive of temporal selfhood. [ScienceDirect+2Memory Time Space Lab+2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002839321500158X)

**Episodic–semantic hybrids.** Current research does not force a choice between rich episodic re-experiencing and bare propositional knowledge. Intermediate forms of personal semantic representation undermine the article’s clean phenomenal/propositional contrast. [Springer+2Royal Society Publishing+2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13415-023-01149-6)

**Retrieval-process versus retrieved-product phenomenology.** Recent work distinguishes the experience of searching for a memory from the qualities of the representation eventually retrieved. The article sometimes attributes felt pastness to the memory product when it may partly arise from fluency, effort, familiarity or source-monitoring activity during retrieval. [MDPI+1](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/12/2/21)

**Reconsolidation boundary conditions.** The idea that every retrieval makes memory labile is too strong. A serious reconstruction section should explain when retrieval changes a trace and when it does not. [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7167366/)

**Confidence, vividness and accuracy.** The relevant empirical pattern is not simply that felt rightness is anti-correlated with accuracy. Different subjective qualities can dissociate from accuracy in task- and content-dependent ways. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-024-00312-1)

**Repeated and general memories.** The article’s examples and theory centre on singular episodes. The new 2026 evidence suggests that repeated and general autobiographical events can be highly identity-relevant and phenomenologically similar to unique events. This complicates any model that makes one-off re-entry into a particular past scene the paradigm of self-continuity. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-63043-8.pdf)

### From narrative-identity theory

**Anti-narrativism.** Strawson’s objection is not merely that some stories are inaccurate. It is that many people do not experience themselves narratively and that narrativity is neither a universal psychological fact nor a condition of a good or responsible life. The article does not confront this. [Cognitive Lab](https://lchc.ucsd.edu/mca/Paper/against_narrativity.pdf)

**Planning-based agency.** Bratman provides a developed alternative in which diachronic agency emerges from plans, policies and temporally extended practical commitments. Narrative can interpret this structure without grounding it. [Stanford Philosophy+1](https://philosophy.stanford.edu/sites/philosophy/files/bratman.planningagentselfgovtime.pdf)

**Narrative identity is multidimensional.** A large 2020 study involving 855 participants and 2,565 narratives found separable dimensions involving affective and motivational themes, autobiographical reasoning and structural organisation. Links to well-being were concentrated more strongly in affective and motivational features, and varied with prompts. “Narrative coherence” is therefore not a single explanatory property. [SciSpace+1](https://scispace.com/pdf/the-empirical-structure-of-narrative-identity-the-initial-1u1mzo76ma.pdf)

**Narratives are socially structured.** Contemporary narrative-identity work emphasises cultural scripts, master narratives, power and social recognition. The article’s model is almost entirely intrapsychic: a subject interprets its memories and thereby constitutes itself. It underplays how available identity narratives are socially supplied, constrained and contested. [Experts@Minnesota+1](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/cognitive-scripts-and-narrative-identity-are-shaped-by-structures/)

**Strict narrative identity remains disputed.** Recent debate includes Schroeder’s defence of a genuinely narrative account of numerical identity and Schechtman’s criticism that such an account overemphasises agency and underdescribes embodiment. The article relies largely on Ricoeur and Schechtman’s older work without engaging this current exchange. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/aristoteliansupp/article-abstract/96/1/209/6595527)

**Narrative form may not be easily manipulable.** Experimental attempts to make subjects construct importantly different narratives of the same event have encountered methodological failures. That weakens any casual transition from “narrative structure correlates with identity” to “narrative construction causes a particular form of selfhood.” [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0092656622001313)

**Discontinuity is not always pathological.** Contemporary self-continuity research distinguishes past–present, present–future and global continuity, and recognises that experienced discontinuity can sometimes facilitate change or recovery. The article treats continuity almost exclusively as a benefit and fragmentation as a deficit. [University of Southampton](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~crsi/Sedikides%2C%20Hong%2C%20%26%20Wildschut%2C%202023%2C%20ARP.pdf)

## 4. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article is more transparent about its assumptions than many partisan philosophy pages. The protection occurs not mainly through concealment, but through letting framework assumptions perform evidential work they have not independently earned.

### 4.1 The hard problem is used as a movable fallback

The article initially identifies possible empirical weakeners: a successful computational account of felt pastness, a smooth transition between episodic and semantic states, or a strong functional account of the relevant phenomenology. Later it says that even a strong source-monitoring account would merely relocate the hard problem “one tier up.” [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

Those positions are in tension. Either detailed mechanistic and functional explanation can weaken the dualist inference, or every such success is declared incapable in principle of addressing the phenomenal fact. Under the latter rule, no conceivable memory-science result bears against the view. The argument then reduces to the general hard-problem premise and obtains no distinctive support from memory.

The article should state which findings would genuinely reduce confidence in its interpretation rather than automatically reclassifying each explanation as an account only of access, representation or report.

### 4.2 Haecceity supplies the identity conclusion

The article commendably admits that synchronic mineness is too weak to establish numerical identity and that memory phenomenology underdetermines the stronger conclusion. It then introduces haecceity as the bridge and concedes that the anti–Many-Worlds argument runs through this auxiliary premise. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

That means memory phenomenology does not independently support haecceitistic identity. At most:

> Given haecceitism, hard-problem realism and substantial-subject dualism, memory phenomenology can be interpreted as an expression of the same subject’s persistence.

This is a framework-conditional interpretation, not evidence that selects that framework over rivals.

### 4.3 Introspective authority is allowed to expand beyond its proper scope

Careful introspection can establish, with defeasible authority, that a present experience seems owned, past-directed or familiar. It does not by itself establish:

- that the remembered event occurred as represented;
- that “pastness” is one unified quale rather than a family of cues;
- that the current subject is numerically identical to the represented past subject;
- that the experience is non-physical; or
- that a substantial bearer is required.

The article acknowledges that phenomenological reports alone do not establish dualism, but its later conclusion again treats irreducible first-person givenness as positive support. Its treatment of illusionism as a “bedrock clash” also stops the dialectic just where it should distinguish authority about current seeming from authority about classification, mechanism and ontology. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

### 4.4 Ordinary cognitive causation is redescribed as bidirectional dualist interaction

Remembering can change decisions, emotions and behaviour. That is evidence that memory-related cognitive states participate in causal control. It is predicted by physicalist, functionalist, emergentist and dualist theories alike. The article supplies no discriminating evidence that the causal contribution comes from a non-physical subject or that it enters physical dynamics in the site’s proposed quantum-level manner. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

The inference from “remembered experience affects choice” to the site’s **Bidirectional Interaction** tenet is therefore invalid without an independent causal-exclusion argument and an empirical discriminator.

### 4.5 The Many-Worlds conclusion is imported, not discovered

Nothing in memory phenomenology distinguishes a single-world physical theory from Many-Worlds. The anti-MWI result follows only after adding a particular account of numerical identity and haecceity. The article itself effectively admits this but still lists rejection of Many-Worlds among the implications. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

That material should be labelled “conditional consistency with the site’s framework,” not “support from the phenomenology of memory.”

### 4.6 Naturalistic rivals are sometimes treated as eliminativist by default

The final section says reductive accounts strip away or deny the phenomenon. That is true of some strong illusionist accounts, but not of all functionalist, representationalist, higher-order or type-B physicalist positions. Such theories can accept that remembering genuinely seems owned and past-directed while denying that this requires a non-physical property. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

The article needs to confront the strongest non-eliminative rival: one that accepts the phenomenological datum, explains its cognitive role and denies only the dualist metaphysical inference.

## 5. Currency of the references

The problem is that a 29-item bibliography dominated by pre-2020 sources is used to make empirical claims about a rapidly developing field. Only a very small portion of the list clearly represents 2020s work. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

Priority additions include:

## 6. Integration with surrounding articles

### Personal identity

The target article says remembering constitutes the temporally extended self. The neighbouring personal-identity article says memories and traits are evidence or expressions of the persistence of consciousness rather than the ground of identity itself. [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

Those positions can be reconciled only if “constitutes” in the target means **phenomenologically or narratively constitutes a self-conception**, not metaphysically constitutes numerical identity. The site presently leaves that ambiguity unresolved.

### The minimal, narrative and substantial self

The surrounding synthesis says the substantial self grounds the minimal and narrative layers. The target article instead treats memory and narrative persistence as evidence through which the substantial self “shows itself.” [topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)

This risks circularity:

1. a substantial self is posited to ground narrative continuity;
2. narrative continuity is then treated as evidence for the substantial self.

An independent bridge premise is needed, together with evidence capable of distinguishing the substantial-self model from process, organismic and relational models.

### Narrative coherence and diachronic agency

The narrative-coherence page treats coherence as grounding agency and as evidence for a substantial self. The diachronic-agency page makes the stronger claim that agency across time requires narrative. [concepts/narrative-coherence](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/narrative-coherence/)

These pages amplify the target article’s least secure claim. They need to incorporate anti-narrativism, planning theory, semantic self-knowledge, habit, embodied continuity and social scaffolding. Otherwise the cluster mistakes one important mode of self-organisation for a necessary condition of temporal agency.

### Self and self-consciousness

The self-consciousness article provides the site’s clearest taxonomy by distinguishing minimal, metacognitive and narrative selfhood and by allowing mineness and agency to dissociate. [concepts/self-and-self-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/self-and-self-consciousness/)

That taxonomy should govern the whole cluster. The target article presently moves among mineness, autobiographical self-conception, diachronic agency, subjective continuity and numerical identity without consistently marking the level at issue.

### Episodic memory

The neighbouring episodic-memory article is less cautious than the target. It speaks as though episodic memory universally re-presents experience, pastness marks genuine causal contact, and each retrieval makes a memory labile. It also infers a non-physical purposive contribution from goal-directed retrieval and uses autonoetic immediacy against Parfit-style accounts. [concepts/episodic-memory](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/episodic-memory/)

This produces direct cluster-level inconsistency. The target acknowledges confabulation, underdetermination and physicalist predictions; its sibling page reintroduces stronger authenticity, causation and identity claims. The two articles should be revised together rather than allowing the more cautious qualifications on one page to coexist with unqualified claims on another.

The integration verdict is therefore: **strong linking, weak harmonisation**. The article is embedded in the right cluster, but key terms and standards of inference change from page to page.

# Concrete potential improvements

## To “Phenomenology of Memory and the Self”

1. **Rewrite the opening universals.** Replace claims about what remembering necessarily is with claims about common forms of vivid autobiographical recollection, explicitly acknowledging SDAM, semantic autobiographical memory and observer-perspective variation.
2. **Define three senses of pastness.** Separate phenomenal past-directedness, metacognitive source attribution and genuine causal connection to an event. State explicitly that the first is neither necessary nor sufficient for the third.
3. **Correct the Husserl–Tulving bridge.** Distinguish Husserlian retention from free recollection and present autonoesis as a partial analogue to recollection, not as the cognitive name for retention.
4. **Reframe patient RB.** Label it a contested single case, cite Roache’s direct criticism, and remove language suggesting that it cleanly decomposes remembered content from mineness.
5. **Disaggregate the Tippett findings.** Distinguish aMCI from Alzheimer’s disease, subjective continuity beliefs from explanatory sophistication, and observed associations from the tentative causal-scaffolding proposal.
6. **Remove or operationalise the anti-correlation claim.** Replace “felt rightness is anti-correlated with accuracy” with a claim about context-dependent dissociation unless a specific inverse relationship can be sourced.
7. **Add full references for Hurlburt and Petitmengin.** Explain that these methods may improve the discipline and granularity of present reports but do not independently validate the historical accuracy of remembered content.
8. **Replace the episodic–semantic binary with a multidimensional model.** Include episodic narrative personal semantics, varying degrees of reliving, internal-state reinstatement, temporal specificity and self-relevance.
9. **Narrow “every remembering is reinterpretation.”** Use “retrieval can reconstruct and update memory under identifiable conditions,” with a short account of reconsolidation boundary conditions.
10. **Separate kinds of self and identity in an argument map.** Mark every inference as concerning minimal selfhood, narrative self-conception, subjective continuity, practical agency, numerical identity or substantial identity.
11. **Add a serious non-narrative subsection.** Engage Strawson, Bratman, SDAM, habitual agency, prospective intention and semantic self-knowledge.
12. **Update the narrative-identity literature.** Add the Initial Big Three model, master-narrative and power research, the Schroeder–Schechtman exchange and evidence that narrative manipulations are experimentally difficult.
13. **Recast the metaphysical conclusion.** State that memory phenomenology supplies an explanandum compatible with several ontologies. Present dualism, haecceity and anti-MWI as conditional interpretations rather than findings supported by the memory evidence.
14. **Resolve the falsification inconsistency.** Specify what computational, source-monitoring or gradient evidence would genuinely lower confidence in the dualist account rather than automatically relocating the hard problem.
15. **Narrow the Proustian underdetermination claim.** Reserve “unresolvable” for exact phenomenal-token or numerical identity; recognise that degrees and patterns of experiential reinstatement are empirically investigable.
16. **Add the August 2026 repeated-event evidence at the next revision.** Use it to broaden the analysis beyond unique, scene-like episodes.

## To specific surrounding articles

1. **“Episodic Memory”:** remove the claims that pastness authenticates causal contact and that every retrieval makes a trace labile; add SDAM, source-monitoring uncertainty, reconsolidation boundary conditions and episodic–semantic continua.
2. **“Diachronic Agency and Personal Narrative”:** replace the claim that diachronic agency requires narrative with a plural model incorporating plans, policies, habits, semantic self-knowledge, embodiment and social support.
3. **“Narrative Coherence”:** distinguish structural coherence, autobiographical reasoning and affective-motivational themes; discuss maladaptive coherence, adaptive discontinuity and master narratives.
4. **“Personal Identity”:** distinguish memory as evidence, psychological continuity relation, practical identity and numerical identity; correct any remaining suggestion that every retrieval changes its trace.
5. **“The Self: Minimal, Narrative, and Substantial”:** mark persistence-through-disruption arguments as conditional on the substantial-self tenet and specify what evidence would count against that interpretation.
6. **“Self and Self-Consciousness”:** promote its minimal/metacognitive/narrative taxonomy into a canonical glossary used across the cluster.
7. **“The Inaccessible Past”:** incorporate recent work on internal-state retention and distortion, and separate detail accuracy, phenomenal fidelity and numerical identity of an experience.
8. **“Phenomenal Authority and First-Person Evidence”:** distinguish authority regarding present seeming from authority regarding memory source, cognitive classification, mechanism and metaphysical ontology.

## To the site’s methodology

1. **Introduce age- and risk-based re-verification.** Do not let unchanged text inherit “verified” status indefinitely. The changelog’s own proposal for a review trigger after roughly 60 days should be implemented, with shorter intervals for empirical and medically relevant claims. [reviews/deep-review-2026-07-27-phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self](https://unfinishablemap.org/reviews/deep-review-2026-07-27-phenomenology-of-memory-and-the-self/)
2. **Maintain a claim–source matrix.** For every load-bearing claim, record the exact source passage, evidence type, sample size where relevant, limitations, and the additional inference made by the Map.
3. **Label epistemic status inline.** Distinguish phenomenological report, empirical association, mechanistic model, philosophical inference and tenet-conditional consequence.
4. **Require a current-literature layer.** Each empirical subsection should pair foundational works with at least one current review and one post-2020 primary study where available.
5. **Treat substantive rewriting as a verification event.** Condensing or paraphrasing a previously checked claim can introduce a new mischaracterisation even when the citation remains unchanged.
6. **Adopt a single-case rule.** Any inference based on one neuropsychological patient should state `n = 1`, identify interpretive disputes and prohibit population-level generalisation without converging evidence.
7. **Require operational disconfirmation criteria.** An article should not count a mechanistic explanation as potential contrary evidence in one section and then neutralise it through hard-problem relocation in another.
8. **Run cluster-level consistency audits.** Search for divergent uses of “constitutes,” “mineness,” “pastness,” “continuity,” “narrative self,” “substantial self” and “identity” across linked articles.
9. **Create an evidence ledger with review dates.** Mark each source relationship as established, inherited, newly checked, supportive, challenging or neutral.
10. **Add an adversarial-literature pass.** Every review should explicitly search for direct published criticisms of the central case or interpretation, as the omitted Roache critique illustrates.
11. **Separate changelog categories.** Distinguish metadata repair, source-characterisation repair, empirical-currency update and substantive argument change.
12. **Require every named scholarly method or author in the prose to appear in the references.** This would have caught the Hurlburt and Petitmengin omissions automatically.
13. **Commission independent spot-checks for apex and high-impact pages.** A reviewer should receive the article without the inherited verification ledger and attempt to falsify its load-bearing source claims from scratch.
