## Executive Summary

This is the wing where the Map is most exposed to a specific temptation — information is fundamental, consciousness is where information is *experienced*, therefore consciousness is fundamental — and it declines the temptation four times over, in four different vocabularies. The Wheeler article in particular contains the corpus's cleanest single instance of the pattern the Hardline Empiricist persona exists to praise: it takes a friendly precursor whose framework is congenial to dualism and says in plain terms that the Map is "inspired by Wheeler, not licensed by him." One calibration asymmetry survives, and it is narrow: `adaptive-computational-depth` uses Penrose's Gödelian argument as the *resolver* of its central regress without the hedge the corpus applies to that argument in two other places — and without the hedge the same article applies, one paragraph later, to a *newer and weaker* citation. ## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would find his own view handled with unusual care here, which matters because this wing is where his double-aspect theory of information is most often conscripted.

`consciousness-and-the-physics-of-information` quotes the 1996 formulation *with its scope qualifier intact* and then flags the qualifier as the point: "the qualifier 'or at least some information' acknowledges that whether *all* physically realised information or only *some* has phenomenal character remained unresolved." Most treatments drop that clause, because dropping it makes the double-aspect theory a much bolder ally. Keeping it is citation-framing accuracy of a high order — the article cites Chalmers for what he actually reached, not for the verdict a friendly reader wants him to have reached.

The wing then does the harder thing and turns the qualifier into a *cost*, not a footnote. §"Information Dualism": "If all physically realised information has an experiential aspect, the position collapses into panpsychism—thermostats experience, rocks experience, electrons experience. If only some information has phenomenal character, what distinguishes the conscious from the non-conscious? The answer cannot appeal to physical structure alone, or the position reduces back to physicalism." `metaphysics-of-information-under-dualism` Option 3 states the same dilemma independently and concludes: "The neutral ground becomes less neutral than it appeared."

Chalmers would also endorse the wing's diagnosis of IIT. §"Information Physicalism": "The Map's concern with information physicalism is that it dissolves the hard problem by definitional fiat… We can explain why H₂O behaves as water does. Nobody can explain why Φ-structures are accompanied by experience." That is the explanatory-gap argument applied precisely, to a theory that borrows phenomenological respectability.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp is treated here better than most sympathetic frameworks treat him, and the reason is a paragraph that costs the Map something.

`wheelers-participatory-universe-and-it-from-bit` §"Stapp's Quantum Mind" reports the von Neumann process-1 argument accurately, quotes Stapp on "a central and irreducible role for mind," and then concedes the Tegmark decoherence objection. What follows is the review's single favourite passage in the wing:

> "It would be dishonest to wield Tegmark's result against Stapp alone. The same ~10⁻¹³ s figure targets neural quantum superposition *generally*, and the Map's own thesis — that consciousness modulates collapse locally in neural systems — requires *some* neural quantum process to modulate. The obstacle is not selective to Stapp's Zeno mechanism; it presses on any framework, the Map's included… The Map does not claim immunity."

The Map had every incentive to leave Tegmark pointed at Stapp only. `positions/quantum-interface.md` P-Q4 already demotes the Zeno mechanism relative to post-decoherence selection, so an unreciprocated use of Tegmark would have been locally convenient and externally invisible. Declining it is a discipline that costs and buys nothing but accuracy.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

`metaphysics-of-information-under-dualism` §"The Syntax-Semantics Divide" is the wing's Nagelian core, and its handling of the deflationary reply is what makes it work. The article states the opposing view at full strength — "meaning just *is* sufficiently context-sensitive information processing… a chess engine 'understands' positional advantage in the only sense understanding requires" — and then answers it with a claim about *structure* rather than about evidence:

> "Computational theories of meaning explain the *function* of semantics—how systems respond differentially to context—without explaining why any of that functioning is accompanied by experiential content. The point is structural rather than evidential: functional accounts are complete on their own terms and yet leave the experiential dimension unaddressed."

The final sentence is doing real calibration work. It concedes that the functional account is *complete on its own terms* — no gap of the kind further empirical work would fill — which forecloses the bad version of this argument ("science hasn't explained it yet, so it can't"). Nagel's claim was always modal, not evidential, and the article states it as such.

Nagel would also note the Fuchs quotation in the Wheeler article: "Reality consists of more than can be captured by any putative third-person account of it" — and the article's honest immediate qualification that QBism "is deliberately agnostic about collapse ontology… a significant divergence from the Map's framework."

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Option 4 in `metaphysics-of-information-under-dualism` is the wing's most Whiteheadian move and its most disciplined one. Information is cast as a *relation* between two irreducible domains rather than a substance underlying them:

> "These are not neutral in the way Option 3 proposes (information is not the *substance* underlying both domains) but relational—they are the points where the two domains make contact."

This is exactly the crude-substance-dualism avoidance Whitehead's programme demands, without the panpsychist commitment. The article then anticipates the obvious objection — §"Proximity to neutral monism" — and concedes it is subtle: "the view must continually demonstrate it rather than merely assert it."

The Wheeler article reaches further in the same direction at line 164, speculating that if the information at the base of physics "is not Shannon information (purely syntactic, meaning-free) but something richer," then "the syntax-semantics divide that the Map identifies as the deepest challenge for information ontology may not be a divide at all, but a feature of our current conceptual vocabulary that a deeper understanding would dissolve." It is flagged as speculation and placed after the tenet section, where speculation belongs.

**Constraint check (per this skill's evidential-status discipline): clean.** The place this praise could have cashed out as an upgrade is the panpsychism scope question — "information has a phenomenal aspect" is Whitehead-friendly and would, unrestricted, license widespread consciousness. Both `consciousness-and-the-physics-of-information` §"Information Dualism" and `metaphysics-of-information-under-dualism` Option 3 name that unrestricted reading as the *cost* of the position, not as a result. The Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist converge here rather than conflicting, which by this skill's own criterion means the wing has resolved the tension honestly.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane's requirement is that indeterminacy be genuine and that the agent be the one who settles it. `metaphysics-of-information-under-dualism` §"Information, Causation, and the Exclusion Problem" supplies both halves and separates them carefully:

> "The gaps consciousness fills are not due to ignorance of physical law but are structural features of quantum mechanics itself. The indeterminacy is not epistemic (we don't know the outcome) but ontological (there is no fact about the outcome until measurement). Consciousness fills a genuine ontological vacancy, not an epistemic one."

The Wheeler article then reads the delayed-choice result in exactly the constrained way Kane's programme needs — outcome-selection, not retroactive rewriting: "not influence over a pre-existing past fact, but participation in *which of the open outcomes becomes actual*." The article says so twice, once in §"Wheeler's Core Vision" ("no settled past fact is overturned") and once in the tenet section, and explicitly declines "the dramatic 'backward causation' gloss." Refusing the dramatic reading of one's own best illustration is the mark of an argument that intends to survive.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

The wing's epistemic humility is structural rather than decorative, and appears at three different altitudes.

At the level of a whole debate — Wheeler article §"The Unresolved Debate": "The field remains genuinely divided, in the deeper sense that the question may be ill-posed or may require conceptual resources we do not yet possess."

At the level of the Map's own preferred position — `metaphysics-of-information-under-dualism` §"Status of the View": "The Map treats the interface-medium position as programmatic—a framework that organises inquiry rather than a completed theory. It specifies *what kind of answer* to look for… without yet providing the detailed account. This is honest rather than evasive."

And at the level of a single concept — `adaptive-computational-depth` §"Epistemological Status": "The value here is conceptual rather than predictive… This is a philosophical framework compatible with physics, not a competing physical hypothesis."

McGinn would particularly approve that Option 4's §"Difficulties the View Faces" names three problems the Map has *not* solved (specification, proximity to neutral monism, the direction/circularity problem) and offers no resolution to any of them.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

This persona's verdict is load-bearing for this wing, because the wing's whole subject is a metaphysics that is congenial to the Map's tenets. The temptation is to let congeniality do evidential work. Four separate refusals, in ascending order of how much they cost:

**1. The Map declines to need its friendliest thesis.** Wheeler article, on Barbour's "bit from it" counter-argument: "The Map's core commitments — that consciousness is irreducible and interacts with quantum indeterminacy — do not require 'it from bit.'… What 'it from bit' adds is philosophical motivation — if reality's foundations are already informational, the idea that consciousness participates in determining outcomes becomes less exotic — but the Map's framework survives without it." *Philosophical motivation* is precisely the right register: defeater-removal, not evidence. This is the named pattern "tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating," stated in the article's own words.

**2. The Map denies itself a precursor's authority.** Wheeler article, tenet section, quoting Wheeler ruling out the mental reading directly ("'Consciousness' has nothing whatsoever to do with the quantum process"), then: "the Map's framework is **inspired by Wheeler, not licensed by him**, since his framework is compatible with eliminative materialism (information processing without irreducible mind) and with idealism (reality as observer-created) as readily as with dualism." A framework compatible with one's rivals as readily as with oneself supplies no differential support. The article says this about its own headline precursor.

**3. The Map names its own most exposed commitment.** Wheeler article, "What Would Challenge This View?" item 5 — and note that items 1–4 are all about *Wheeler's* framework, while item 5 turns inward: "The Map's own most exposed commitment is not Wheeler's framework but the 'corridor' reading… By construction, an ensemble experiment cannot tell corridor-selection apart from no selection at all — the distribution is identical either way… Absent such a signature, corridor-selection is currently empirically indistinguishable from chance, and this is a genuine cost the Map states rather than hides."

**4. The praise-worthy thing not done.** The Tegmark passage quoted under Stapp above. A framework that treats a decoherence objection as fatal to a rival and survivable for itself is doing tenet-as-shield work; this wing explicitly refuses: "The honest statement is that the decoherence timescale is a live difficulty for the Map's local-modulation thesis, not merely a club against a rival."

The sibling article `born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface` — read as context, not reviewed — carries this discipline to its limit by stating the *Lakatosian* charge against the Map in the Map's own voice ("an unfalsifiable hard core… ringed by falsifiable auxiliaries the programme disavows in advance… is the textbook profile of a *degenerating* research programme"). The wing reviewed here is consistent with that standard rather than falling short of it.

## Content Strengths

### obsidian/topics/wheelers-participatory-universe-and-it-from-bit.md

- **Strongest point**: The article separates two theses that are almost universally run together — "the participatory universe and 'it from bit' are often conflated, but the two claims are distinct and separable… One can accept either thesis while denying the other; Wheeler held both, but his successors have separated them" — and then uses the separation as the organising spine for the three descendant programmes. Zeilinger/Brukner inherit the information-foundations half; digital physics inherits it and drops participation; QBism inherits participation and stays agnostic on ontology. The taxonomy is doing analytic work, not decorating.
- **Notable quote**: "The Map's framework is inspired by Wheeler, not licensed by him."
- **Why it works**: The article's structural honesty is *load-distributed* — the concessions are not gathered into one penitential section but placed where each argument is made. Zeilinger's programme is credited and then immediately turned into a tension the Map "must confront" (structural randomness derived from finite-information axioms would be violated by biasing). Dembski is named the most compatible descendant and simultaneously the one who denies observer-creation. Stapp is the closest ally and gets the decoherence objection applied reflexively. There is no section where the article relaxes.

### obsidian/concepts/metaphysics-of-information-under-dualism.md

- **Strongest point**: The four-option taxonomy is genuinely exhaustive over the logical space (physical / mental / neutral / relational), each option is stated at its own best, and the Map's preferred Option 4 is defended *negatively* — "The case for preferring Option 4 is that it avoids the specific failures of the other three." An elimination argument makes a much weaker claim than a positive one, and the article knows which it is making.
- **Notable quote**: "This is honest rather than evasive: the metaphysics of information under dualism is genuinely unsettled territory, and the Map's contribution is to argue that the interface framework is more promising than the alternatives, not that it is already worked out."
- **Why it works**: §"What Information Cannot Be Under Dualism" is the article's structural payoff and the wing's clearest instance of a framework generating *prohibitions* rather than accommodations. Three positions are ruled out — strong substrate-independence, information-consciousness identity, information-as-fundamental-substance — and the third rules out a position the Map might have wanted, since informational fundamentality is what makes the wing's opening move attractive. A framework that forbids things it would like to keep is doing work.

### obsidian/topics/consciousness-and-the-physics-of-information.md

- **Strongest point**: The lead front-loads the whole argument in three clauses (Landauer → Wheeler → therefore the hard problem sits at physics' centre, not its periphery), which is textbook LLM-first construction: a chatbot truncating at 200 words still gets the thesis. §"The Informational Turn" then *grades* its own three planks — "Three developments—**of varying epistemic status**"— and delivers on the grading: Landauer is "not an engineering limitation—it is a thermodynamic law"; Wheeler's proposal "was speculative—a working hypothesis, not established physics."
- **Notable quote**: "The Map treats this as a suggestive observation, not a load-bearing argument."
- **Why it works**: That quote closes §"The Black Hole Information Paradox as Diagnostic," which is the article's most seductive section and the one most likely to be over-read. The article builds an attractive line (conscious biasing leaves informational traces; physics says information is conserved; therefore…) and then refuses to bank it, converting the section instead into a *diagnostic for epiphenomenalism*: "Either consciousness participates in the physical information economy, or it stands entirely outside it." Volunteering the weakness of one's own most quotable passage is rare.

### obsidian/concepts/adaptive-computational-depth.md

- **Strongest point**: The article is built around an inversion it announces in its own description — the concept "initially seems to support computationalism but actually implies non-computable mind" — and the §"Non-Computability Turn" states the computationalist objection *first* and at full strength ("Operating systems manage memory, video games run LOD systems as part of their own code, neural networks adjust attention weights. Why can't a computational universe allocate its own depth?") before answering it.
- **Notable quote**: "The difference is between *what to compute* and *what matters*."
- **Why it works**: The Bostrom handling is a small model of citation discipline: "While Bostrom's argument assumes consciousness is computable—a premise The Unfinishable Map rejects—his observation about selective detail allocation is structurally sound regardless of what drives the selection." The article extracts the structural observation and explicitly quarantines the premise it does not accept, rather than either endorsing Bostrom wholesale or discarding him.

## Calibration Concern

*Per this skill's Process-Philosopher/Hardline-Empiricist protocol, a calibration finding is reported here and generates a `refine-draft` task rather than an expansion opportunity.*

### `adaptive-computational-depth` hedges the weaker citation and not the stronger claim

The article's central argument is the allocator regress: whatever allocates computational depth must itself be computed, requiring its own allocator, "unless the allocator is non-computational." The regress is then closed by a single sentence:

> "Roger Penrose's argument that consciousness involves non-computable processes (Penrose 1994) **resolves this regress**."

No hedge, no link to the Map's own treatment. Two paragraphs' worth of context make this an asymmetry rather than a judgement call:

1. **The very next paragraph hedges a newer, weaker citation.** Faizal et al. (2025) — an arXiv preprint — gets "Whether or not Faizal's specific proof withstands scrutiny, the general point reinforces the non-computability turn." The article therefore *has* a hedge channel; it simply does not point it at Penrose-Lucas, which is among the most extensively contested arguments in the philosophy of mind.
2. **The corpus hedges it in two other places.** `obsidian/topics/consciousness-and-mathematics.md:114` states the standard objection directly — "Critics object that the argument assumes human consistency—an unproven claim" — before giving the Map's regress reply. And `obsidian/topics/penrose-gravity-induced-collapse-empirical-prospects.md:86` builds an explicit firewall: "If Penrose-Lucas fails, Penrose's *Gödelian* motivation for OR is undermined and the Map's is untouched."

So the wing's own standard is that Penrose-Lucas is a contested argument the Map's positions are careful not to lean on. `adaptive-computational-depth` is the one place where it is used as a *resolver*, and the word "resolves" is what carries the overclaim — the regress argument would be equally well served by "points toward," which is the verb the article's own abstract and §"Relation to Site Perspective" already use ("actually points toward non-computable consciousness"; "The natural termination point is…"). The internal inconsistency is between the article's body and its own framing elsewhere.

The fix is small: one clause plus a link to the existing treatment. It should **not** become a rewrite — the regress argument itself is sound as stated, §"What Would Challenge This View?" item 4 already names the self-allocation objection as a defeater, and §"Epistemological Status" is exemplary. Only the verb and the missing cross-reference are at issue.

#### Reconcile the Wheeler article's within-trial discriminator against P-Q2 and `born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface`

- **Builds on**: `wheelers-participatory-universe-and-it-from-bit` "What Would Challenge This View?" item 5; `obsidian/positions/quantum-interface.md` P-Q2 and P-Q9; `obsidian/topics/born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface.md` §"What Negative Results Would and Would Not Foreclose."
- **Would address**: Three places in the corpus give different answers to whether the corridor reading is discriminable *in principle*, and none cites the others on this point.
  - The Wheeler article says **yes, in principle**: "What would discriminate the two is a within-trial signature: a detectable correlation between a measured outcome and the conscious state of an observing system at the moment of registration, surviving when ensemble statistics are held fixed (for instance, sequential single-trial outcomes covarying with deliberate attentional state in a way no decoherence-plus-Born model predicts)."
