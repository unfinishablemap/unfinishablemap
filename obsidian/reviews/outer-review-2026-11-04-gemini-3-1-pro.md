# Referee Report: Phenomenology of Agency vs Passivity

## Introduction and Structural Assessment of the Manuscript

The manuscript under review, titled "Phenomenology of Agency vs Passivity," attempts to map the experiential boundary between volitional action and passive undergoing, leveraging this phenomenological contrast to argue for a non-reductive, dualist-leaning architecture of conscious causal efficacy. The text employs a two-pole parallel structure to examine the "well-oiled hinge" of agency, leaning heavily on clinical dissociations—such as schizophrenia, anarchic hand syndrome, and utilization behavior—to establish the necessity of genuine conscious selection. The central theoretical gambit of the manuscript is the assertion of a "causal interface void," an unobservable threshold where consciousness purportedly acts upon neural processing, biasing quantum indeterminacies to collapse into authored actions.

While the manuscript’s ambition to bridge first-person phenomenological data with third-person cognitive neuroscience is evident, a rigorous audit of its empirical claims, citations, counterargument coverage, and logical inferences reveals catastrophic systemic failures. The text operates in an artificially constructed intellectual vacuum, hermetically sealed against the neurocognitive and philosophical consensus of the 2020–2025 literature. The manuscript systematically brackets competing physicalist frameworks to protect its own predetermined metaphysical commitments—specifically, its tenets of "Bidirectional Interaction" and "Minimal Quantum Interaction"—rather than engaging in genuine adversarial testing. It relies on obsolete or radically reinterpreted empirical paradigms, completely ignores the dominant computational models of contemporary cognitive science, and fundamentally mishandles the philosophical counterarguments it claims to defeat.

The analysis below details the specific, irremediable weaknesses across five primary domains of contemporary inquiry. These include the epistemological collapse of the intentional binding paradigm as a proxy for volition; the manuscript's complete evasion of predictive processing and active inference models; the circularity of its anti-illusionist arguments derived from metacognitive artifacts; the severe category errors in its treatment of clinical neuroplasticity; and its indefensible erasure of standard comparator models of motor control.

## The Epistemological Collapse of Intentional Binding as an Agentive Proxy

A foundational pillar of the manuscript’s empirical argument regarding the phenomenology of volition is its reliance on the temporal compression between an action and its outcome. The text assumes the classical, early-2000s interpretation wherein this temporal shift—historically termed "intentional binding"—serves as a transparent, implicit window into genuine, non-physical conscious causation and the sense of agency. The manuscript implies that this temporal binding uniquely indexes the volitional authorship of an action, separating the genuine "doing" of spontaneous or deliberative action from the mere "undergoing" of reflexive or passive movement. This assumption is entirely divorced from the contemporary empirical consensus.

The 2020–2025 literature has systematically dismantled the exclusive link between intentionality, volition, and temporal binding. Extensive empirical replication and theoretical reformulations have demonstrated that the temporal compression effect is not driven by an endogenous "sense of agency" or genuine volitional selection, but rather by domain-general multisensory cue integration and causal inference. The phenomenon is more accurately classified as "causal binding," as it scales with the probability of a causal linkage and the temporal predictability of events rather than the explicit intentionality of the action.

Crucially, the binding effect is reliably observed in contexts entirely devoid of intentional action. Contemporary studies demonstrate that temporal binding occurs during the passive observation of cause-effect sequences, in machine-induced involuntary movements, and in cases where participants are explicitly stripped of any genuine choice or volitional control. For example, when subjects merely observe a mechanical lever depressing a button that subsequently triggers a tone, they report a subjective temporal compression between the lever drop and the tone that is statistically indistinguishable from the compression observed when they press the button themselves, provided the causal predictability remains constant.

Contemporary Bayesian models of perception perfectly account for this phenomenon without invoking a non-reductive volitional interface. In these models, the brain optimally combines prior expectations of temporal intervals with noisy sensory input. When two events (such as a movement and a subsequent tone) are inferred to be causally related, the cognitive system applies a stronger prior for temporal contiguity, resulting in a subjective compression of the interval. The fact that an active, voluntary movement might sometimes produce a slightly stronger binding effect than a passive movement is entirely explained by the fact that endogenous motor commands provide a more reliable, precise sensory cue (efferent copy) than passive proprioception. This increased sensory precision naturally reduces uncertainty in the temporal estimate, leading to a tighter Bayesian integration, rather than serving as evidence for an irreducible agentive force.

By treating intentional binding as a proxy for a dualist "causal interface," the manuscript commits a severe procedural and logical confound. It conflates a ubiquitous feature of statistical multisensory integration with an ontologically unique signature of conscious authorship. The manuscript completely ignores the devastating critique that if binding occurs without intention, it cannot serve as the empirical bedrock for a phenomenology of genuine, non-reductive volitional selection. Consequently, any inferences the manuscript draws regarding a "causal interface void" based on temporal binding paradigms are empirically bankrupt and theoretically obsolete.

## Systematic Evasion of Predictive Processing and Active Inference

The manuscript's core phenomenological claim is that the felt contrast between "doing" and "undergoing"—the agency-passivity axis—requires a non-reductive explanation wherein consciousness acts as an independent selective force. To maintain this framing, the text must artificially bracket and entirely ignore the most prominent, comprehensive, and empirically validated framework in contemporary cognitive neuroscience: predictive processing and its broader corollary, active inference. The total absence of these models from a manuscript claiming to exhaustively map the phenomenology of agency constitutes an academic evasion that immediately disqualifies the text from serious peer review.

In the active inference framework, the brain is modeled as a hierarchical, generative prediction engine that continuously attempts to minimize variational free energy, which serves as an upper bound on sensory prediction error. Action and perception are not dualistic opposites but are unified under this single imperative. Agency, within this model, is not a mysterious, unobservable force injected from a metaphysical void; rather, it is the mathematically precise process of fulfilling proprioceptive predictions through peripheral reflexes.

To execute a voluntary action, the brain issues a high-level prediction of a desired sensory state (e.g., the arm being extended). Because the body is not currently in that state, this generates a massive prediction error at the lower levels of the cortical hierarchy. To resolve this error without simply updating the prediction to match reality (which would result in paralysis), the system must temporarily attenuate the precision (the expected reliability or variance) of the conflicting incoming somatosensory evidence. This precision-weighting mechanism suppresses the sensory consequences of self-generated actions, allowing the predicted motor trajectory to be fulfilled through classical reflex arcs in the spinal cord without being interrupted by the sensory reality of the present moment.

The phenomenological contrast between "doing" and "undergoing" is fully exhausted and seamlessly explained by this precision-weighting dynamic. When an individual "does" something, their sensory cortices preemptively attenuate the predicted sensory feedback; the action is experienced as smooth, authored, and phenomenologically transparent. When an individual "undergoes" something (a passive movement or an unexpected external force), the unpredicted sensory input is weighted with high precision, driving ascending prediction errors that dominate conscious experience and demand immediate cortical updating. Limanowski (2022) has demonstrated in exhaustive detail how precision control entirely dictates the flexibility of body representation, the sense of ownership, and the sense of self, mapping exactly onto the phenomenological spectrum the manuscript attempts to claim as exclusive territory for dualism.

| **Depersonalisation (DP/DR)** | A failure of the conscious "hinge" or a disturbance in primitive *ipseity*. | Hyper-reflexivity disrupts optimal sensory attenuation; self-generated actions fail to be attenuated and thus feel alien/unpredicted. |

Furthermore, the manuscript's integration of clinical dissociations—specifically depersonalization/derealization (DP/DR) and schizophrenia—fails to engage with how active inference elegantly resolves these exact pathologies. The manuscript treats the clinical disruptions of agency as mysteries solvable only by positing a breakdown in a dualist mechanism. Yet, Ciaunica et al. (2022) have provided exhaustive active inference accounts of depersonalization, demonstrating that the loss of the sense of agency and the feeling of "watching oneself act" (the profound disruption of the agency-passivity axis) stems directly from a failure in the optimal precision-weighting of interoceptive and exteroceptive signals.

In DP/DR, hyper-reflexivity (an excess of internal, metacognitive attention) disrupts the automatic attenuation of self-generated sensory feedback. Because the individual fails to attenuate their own sensory signals, their own actions generate ascending prediction errors, causing self-initiated movements to feel unpredicted, externally generated, and therefore unauthored. By completely omitting the mathematically formalized, empirically validated, and entirely physicalist alternative provided by active inference, the manuscript engages in egregious theory-shielding, rendering its central ontological argument invalid by omission.

## The Metacognitive Fallacy: Circularity in the Rebuttal of Illusionism

In its dedicated "Against Illusionism" section, the manuscript attempts to defeat the illusionist theory of consciousness by appealing to the phenomenology of meditative states. Relying on literature from previous decades (e.g., Lutz et al., 2008), the manuscript argues that contemplative practices vastly refine attention, introspection, and meta-awareness. The logical leap follows that because highly trained meditators report an even sharper, more undeniable contrast between agency and passivity, and a clearer, more luminous apprehension of their own phenomenal states, the "illusion" of irreducible consciousness cannot simply be a cognitive error caused by inaccessibility. The text argues that scrutiny sharpens the phenomenal contrast rather than dissolving it, which supposedly contradicts the illusionist deflationary criterion.

This argument is structurally circular and reveals a profound misunderstanding of the contemporary illusionist meta-problem, specifically the sophisticated resolutions formalized in the 2020–2025 philosophical and cognitive literature. The manuscript operates on the naive, historically discredited assumption that subjective introspective certainty—even when refined by thousands of hours of meditation—constitutes objective ontological evidence. Contemporary illusionists, such as Kammerer (2021), have explicitly modeled the exact cognitive mechanisms that generate this infallible intuition, rendering the manuscript's critique moot.

Under the contemporary illusionist framework, the cognitive system necessarily lacks access to its own sub-personal functional and neural mechanisms—a property known in the literature as introspective opacity. Because the brain cannot track the billions of complex physical computations generating a state (e.g., the complex temporal dynamics of a decision to act, or the perception of a color), it models the state in a highly compressed, low-dimensional format. Crucially, the system is evolutionarily designed to trust these compressed introspective outputs absolutely. The subjective feeling that a conscious choice is undeniably real, irreducible, non-physical, and freely authored is not a byproduct waiting to be dissolved by closer attention; it is the highly adaptive, specifically designed functional output of the metacognitive system.

Therefore, applying meditative focus to a phenomenal state does not bypass introspective opacity; it merely concentrates processing power on the compressed, functionally opaque representation itself. If the brain is hardwired to tag its own high-level self-models with an absolute certainty tag (the "infallibility intuition"), then extreme meditative focus will naturally report extreme certainty regarding the non-physical "seeming" of the state. The manuscript attempts to defeat illusionism by pointing to the overwhelming subjective force of the illusion, entirely missing the fact that contemporary physicalist frameworks explicitly predict and functionally explain this exact subjective force.

Furthermore, the manuscript relies heavily on the presumption of introspective accuracy for phenomenal qualia, ignoring a wealth of modern metaperceptual literature demonstrating that metacognitive "superefficiency" is frequently a post-decisional artifact or a methodological illusion. Experimental paradigms utilizing signal detection theory routinely show that what subjects report as "heightened phenomenal clarity" or "pure meta-awareness" often correlates with shifts in response criteria rather than genuine improvements in perceptual or causal sensitivity. By attempting to resolve an ontological dispute utilizing the very a priori introspective intuitions that are the *explanandum* of the dispute, the manuscript engages in an egregious *petitio principii* (begging the question). The failure to engage with Kammerer (2021) and the contemporary formalization of the illusionist meta-problem leaves the manuscript’s primary anti-physicalist defense untested and logically void.

## Category Errors in Clinical Neuroplasticity and Downward Causation

To substantiate its claims regarding "Bidirectional Interaction" and the causal efficacy of consciousness, the manuscript points to clinical neuroplasticity as a trump card. Specifically, it cites Schwartz et al. (1996) regarding changes in cerebral glucose metabolic rates in the caudate nucleus following successful cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD). The manuscript utilizes this dated neuroimaging data to argue for a non-reductive framework: because a purely "mental" intervention (CBT) alters "physical" brain structure (caudate metabolism), the text concludes that consciousness possesses independent, top-down causal powers that cannot be reduced to underlying neural mechanisms.

This inference represents a profound category error and relies on a fundamental misrepresentation of modern biological psychiatry and neurobiology. The assertion that CBT represents an "immaterial" or non-physical intervention acting upon the physical brain is a false dichotomy that no serious neuroscientist has entertained for decades. Psychotherapy is a biophysical, material process. It involves structured auditory and visual sensory inputs, linguistic processing, memory retrieval, affective regulation, and the rigorous, repetitive activation of complex frontostriatal circuits. The fact that repeated engagement of these neural circuits leads to metabolic and structural changes is perfectly explained by standard Hebbian neuroplasticity (e.g., long-term potentiation, synaptic remodeling, and dendritic arborization) and requires zero appeal to dualist downward causation.

The 2020–2025 literature on OCD completely bypasses the manuscript's dualist framing, utilizing high-resolution neuroimaging, machine learning, and mega-analyses to map the precise material etiology of these changes. Mega-analyses by the ENIGMA-OCD consortium (e.g., van den Heuvel, 2026) and dynamic functional connectivity studies (e.g., Norman et al., 2024) have exhaustively documented the structural abnormalities and dysfunctions in the Cortico-Striato-Thalamo-Cortical (CSTC) loops—specifically involving the orbitofrontal cortex, anterior cingulate, and the striatum (including the caudate)—that characterize the obsessive-compulsive phenotype.

Cognitive behavioral therapy, specifically Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP), requires the patient to repeatedly activate prefrontal cognitive control networks to inhibit the prepotent striatal signals driving compulsions. This repeated, effortful inhibition is a material, neurocomputational process. Over time, the continuous top-down (in the anatomical, cortico-fugal sense, not the metaphysical sense) signaling from the prefrontal cortex alters the synaptic weights in the striatum, leading to the observed decrease in caudate hypermetabolism.

The manuscript treats the phenomenon of neuroplasticity as if it were a poltergeist acting upon the brain from an external metaphysical void. By isolating a 1996 PET scan study and aggressively ignoring the subsequent thirty years of network neuroscience and connectomics, the text artificially manufactures a "mystery" of mental causation. The contemporary understanding of dynamic functional connectivity and subcortico-cortical circuit plasticity provides a fully reductive, physicalist explanation for how learning and behavioral therapy remodel the brain. The manuscript’s failure to test its dualist assertions against the modern neurobiological consensus is a disqualifying omission.

## The "Causal Interface Void" and the Erasure of Comparator Models

The manuscript constructs its entire metaphysical architecture around an unobservable "causal interface void," a theoretical space where the mechanism of intention-to-action transition is supposedly phenomenologically invisible and physically irreducible. To empiricize this void, the manuscript heavily relies on classical neurostimulation and lesion data, specifically citing Lhermitte (1983) on frontal lobe damage producing utilization behavior, and Desmurget (2009) on the dissociation between parietal and premotor cortex stimulation.

Desmurget’s findings are central to the manuscript's argument. Desmurget showed that parietal cortex stimulation produces a feeling of intention to move, or even a false belief of having moved, without any actual electromyographic (EMG) muscle activation. By contrast, premotor cortex stimulation produces actual, measurable movement that the patient actively denies having performed. The manuscript leverages this double dissociation to argue for a specific, localized locus where an independent consciousness selects and authors action, interpreting the breakdown of this system as proof of a dualist interface that can be decoupled from physical execution.

This interpretation is fundamentally flawed because it artificially brackets and deliberately ignores the standard computational architecture of motor control that perfectly explains this exact dissociation without any need for an ontological "void" or a dualist selection mechanism: the internal forward model, commonly known as the comparator model.

In the standard comparator model (pioneered by researchers like Frith, Blakemore, and Wolpert, and continuously refined through 2025 in the context of active inference and motor control), voluntary action involves two parallel computational streams. When a motor command is generated in the premotor and primary motor cortices, an "efferent copy" (or corollary discharge) is simultaneously sent to a forward model, which neuroimaging and lesion studies robustly localize to the parietal cortex and cerebellum. This forward model predicts the sensory consequences of the impending movement. The subjective sense of agency and the feeling of conscious intention arise precisely from this predictive signal in the parietal cortex, prior to the arrival of actual sensory feedback.

When Desmurget stimulated the parietal cortex, he artificially injected neural noise into this forward model. The system generated a prediction of movement (mimicking an efferent copy), which the brain interpreted as a conscious intention to move. At higher stimulation levels, this generated the false belief that movement had actually occurred, because the forward prediction was not successfully cancelled by contradictory sensory feedback, overriding the actual state of the body.

Conversely, when the premotor cortex was stimulated, a motor command was sent directly down the corticospinal tract, resulting in actual physical movement. However, because this command was exogenously stimulated by an electrode rather than endogenously generated by the cortical hierarchy, no efferent copy was generated, and no corollary discharge was sent to the parietal forward model. The comparator eventually received proprioceptive feedback of a movement from the periphery, but it had no internal prediction of it to compare against. Consequently, the brain correctly classified the movement as unpredicted, unauthored, and externally generated, leading the patient to legitimately deny having performed it.

The Desmurget data is entirely theory-neutral regarding dualism; it is, in fact, the textbook empirical validation of the purely physicalist comparator model. By systematically excluding any discussion of efferent copies, corollary discharge, and forward models, the manuscript falsely presents the parietal/premotor dissociation as evidence of an inexplicable "interface" where non-physical agency acts upon the brain. This is not a philosophical argument; it is the deliberate suppression of the prevailing scientific explanation to protect a preconceived metaphysical commitment. A paper cannot claim to resolve the phenomenology of agency while completely ignoring the dominant neurocomputational model that explains that exact phenomenology.

## Conclusion and Verdict

The manuscript's attempt to secure a non-reductive, agent-causal framework by analyzing the phenomenology of agency and passivity is fatally undermined by its refusal to engage with contemporary science and philosophy. It relies on the anachronistic interpretation of empirical paradigms, ignores mathematically formalized physicalist models that explain its core phenomenological data, and deploys circular reasoning to dismiss physicalist counterarguments.

**Specific Weaknesses Justifying Rejection:**

1. **Intentional Binding Obsolescence:** The manuscript incorrectly relies on temporal/intentional binding as an exclusive proxy for the genuine phenomenology of volitional authorship, completely ignoring the contemporary literature which demonstrates that binding is a domain-general artifact of multisensory causal inference present even in purely passive or machine-driven contexts (Hoerl et al., 2020; *Cognitive Science*).
2. **Evasion of Active Inference:** The manuscript posits a non-reductive causal interface to explain the phenomenological contrast between "doing" and "undergoing," entirely evading the active inference framework which fully explains this contrast physically via the optimal precision-weighting of sensory prediction errors (Limanowski, 2022; *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*).
3. **Circular Metacognitive Claims:** The manuscript attempts to defeat illusionism by citing the introspective certainty of meditative states, begging the question against contemporary illusionist models which mathematically predict this exact introspective infallibility as a designed, functionally opaque cognitive adaptation rather than an ontological truth (Kammerer, 2021; *Synthese*).
4. **Category Error in Neuroplasticity:** The manuscript isolates 1996 OCD neuroimaging data to claim that CBT efficacy proves dualist "downward causation," aggressively ignoring modern network connectivity meta-analyses that fully account for therapeutic caudate metabolic shifts via standard materialist subcortico-cortical Hebbian plasticity (Norman et al., 2024; *Psychiatry Research*).
5. **Erasure of the Comparator Model:** The manuscript leverages parietal/premotor stimulation dissociations (Desmurget, 2009) to claim an inexplicable "causal interface void," deliberately suppressing the standard forward/comparator models of motor control which seamlessly explain these exact findings through the presence or absence of corollary discharge (Ciaunica et al., 2022; *Consciousness and Cognition*).

**Verdict on Academic-Submission Readiness:** The manuscript is entirely unready for academic submission and warrants outright rejection due to its catastrophic failure to engage with the 2020–2025 empirical and philosophical consensus, substituting systematic theory-shielding and outdated paradigms for rigorous neurocognitive analysis.
