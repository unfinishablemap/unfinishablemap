### Critical Issues Found
- **Factual overstatement (blindsight), Dualism section**: "identical visual discrimination can occur with or without conscious seeing". Blindsight discrimination is not identical. It is largely confined to forced-choice tasks and fails at detection, as the Map's own [[blindsight]] article says. The follow-on argument ("If visual processing were all that mattered, blindsight patients should report seeing") ignored the obvious physicalist reply that the processing is *different*, since the V1 route is lost. **Resolution**: changed to "above-chance visual discrimination"; stated the physicalist reply fairly; moved the dualist conclusion onto the explanatory gap and away from the bare dissociation. Engagement with the physicalist: Mode Two/Three mixed (the reply relocates the question without answering it; the residue is a framework-boundary matter).
- **Unacknowledged challenge to the core evidence (blindsight), opening**: the lead presented blindsight as clean "no qualia" evidence. Type 2 blindsight and Phillips (2021) are not mentioned, although the sibling [[blindsight]] article discusses both. **Resolution**: added one sentence noting that the dissociation is not perfectly clean, citing Phillips (2021), plus a pointer to [[blindsight]]. Also changed "produces no qualia" to "patients report no qualia", which is what the evidence supports.
- **Over-concession / uncited overclaim (PP paragraph, added 07-28)**: "Friston-style generative models *routinely derive* egocentric centring, depth gradients…" and "Spatial behaviour and the discrimination profile *are computationally accounted for*". This conceded, without citation, more than the literature supports (see the over-concession-gets-ratified pattern). **Resolution**: "aim to recover" and "are, on this programme, tractable computational targets". The concession stays honest without overclaiming for the opponent.

### Medium Issues Found
- **V4/achromatopsia bullet** said "V4 is necessary … Lesions cause achromatopsia—complete loss of colour experience". Cerebral achromatopsia follows ventral occipitotemporal (lingual/fusiform, "V4 complex") lesions, and is often partial or hemifield-limited. **Resolution**: bullet re-scoped.
