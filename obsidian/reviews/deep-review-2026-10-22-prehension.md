## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Factual error in the Whitehead exposition (L82, L84).** The article said each occasion prehends its predecessor "more directly than it prehends lateral contemporaries", and set this against "lateral prehension". Whitehead denies that contemporaries prehend each other at all: "It is the definition of contemporary events that they happen in causal independence of each other" (*Adventures of Ideas*, 1933, ch. XII, p. 251; grep-verified in the Macmillan 1933 DLI scan). The real contrast in Whitehead is with the body's inheritances, which "break in upon the dominant line of inheritance" (ch. XI, p. 243; grep-verified). **Fixed**: L82 now uses Whitehead's contrast and says contemporaries "are not prehended directly". I did not write "not at all", because Whitehead allows an indirect immanence through a common past (p. 252). L84 "lateral prehension" became "those other strands of inheritance".
- **Quote fidelity, "personally ordered society" (L82).** The phrase is in quotation marks, but it does not occur in *Process and Reality* (1929 text) or *Adventures of Ideas*: 0 hits for "personally" in either. Whitehead's term is "personal order" (PR: "A nexus enjoys 'personal order' when…", 14 hits). The phrase does occur in Griffin 1998 (UC Press full text): "this mind is a personally ordered society, in which each occasion of experience, besides incorporating feelings from the body, is significantly constituted by its prehension of its own past experiences". **Fixed**: the article now calls it "the process literature's name for what Whitehead calls 'personal order'" and cites Griffin 1998.
- **Quote fidelity, "eliminations" (L68).** The 2026-02-14 review installed this quoted label for negative prehensions. Whitehead's own wording, in Category of Explanation (xii), is that negative prehensions "are said to 'eliminate from feeling'" (PR Part I, ch. II; grep-verified). The noun "eliminations" appears in PR only incidentally, never as his name for negative prehensions. **Fixed**: the article now uses the verbatim phrase and cites the category. I also confirmed that "positive prehensions are termed 'feelings'" is in Part III (Theory of Feelings, §II), so the existing Part III cite stands. "Vacuous actuality" is item (vi) in the PR Preface, confirmed.
- **Source/Map conflation and internal contradiction (L64).** The article said "it means intentionality is not a mysterious emergence… Human aboutness is a sophisticated development of something pervasive". That stated Whitehead's pervasiveness as fact, while L100 says the Map does not commit to prehension being everywhere. **Fixed**: the sentence is now scoped "On Whitehead's view", ends with "a pervasiveness the Map does not adopt", and links forward to Relation to Site Perspective with "explained below".
- **Misstatement of the Map's own position (L100).** The parenthetical "(in beings with sufficiently complex neural organisation)" claimed a neural restriction on where experience exists. `positions/consciousness-scope` P-CS1 holds that the capacity for experience is not reserved for biological systems, P-CS2 grades animal cases by markers, and P-CS5 leaves the prokaryotic floor underdetermined. **Fixed**: now "wherever it exists".
- **Tenet 3 calibration (L106).** "This supports the tenet's claim that consciousness makes a causal difference" treated Whitehead's model being consistent with the tenet as evidence for it. **Fixed**: "This fits, without evidencing, the tenet's claim…: Whitehead's scheme shows what such a contribution could look like, not that one occurs."
- **Modal over-concession (L96).** "No experiment could distinguish a world with prehension from one with ordinary causation" is the "no possible / in principle" tell. Read against L104 and Tenet 3, it gave away testability for the Map's interface claim as well. **Fixed**: the sentence now gives the reason, scoped to the present: Whitehead offers prehension as an analysis of causation, not an addition to it, so "it predicts nothing that ordinary causal description does not". A new sentence says that where the Map ties concrescence to quantum selection, any testability belongs to that interface claim.
- **Four References entries never cited inline (References → inline direction).** Whitehead 1933, Griffin 1998, Rescher 1996 and Stapp 2011 were never cited in the text. The 2026-06-09 and 2026-06-26 reviews recorded "Inline ↔ References cross-reference intact", but that only tested the inline → References direction, and there were no inline year-cites to test. **Fixed**: all four are now cited inline at verified passages (see the ledger). The (surname, year) check now passes in both directions: 5 entries, 5 cited. Whitehead 1929/1978 is cited by title.

### Medium Issues Found

- **Internal tension (L76).** "The occasion inherits superposed possibilities" sat next to "prehension as inheritance from the settled past". In Whitehead, an occasion inherits settled actualities and entertains possibilities. **Fixed**: "inherits a settled past, entertains open possibilities (the analogue of superposed alternatives), and resolves them through subjective aim into definite form."
- **Uncredited idea (L104).** The concrescence–collapse identification was presented only as "the Map speculates". Stapp develops it at length. **Fixed**: "Stapp (2011, ch. 13) builds a 'Whiteheadian quantum ontology' in which each quantum reduction event is identified with a Whiteheadian actual occasion; the Map speculates more cautiously…".
