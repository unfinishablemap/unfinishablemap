## Executive Summary

The routing table is accurate row by row, but it is incomplete in a way that props up the page's thesis. Type-B, "the live opponent", is shown only through Chalmers's case against it. The page never gives Type-B's own answer to the cost argument ("Identities have no explanation"), and it leaves out the argument the same IEP entry says Type-B falls back on, causal closure. That is also the one premise a Map reply reaches head-on (Tenet 2), so the table is missing a Type-B row. The Tenet-5 row says "not yet run", but the IBE page runs that reply against the parsimony in the case for identity, and since 17:13Z philosophy-of-science-under-dualism runs it against the Type-B opponent by name. A table that steers edits should not tell a fixer to build a reply that already exists. On Type-C, "defeating A and B suffices" overlooks Chalmers's own concession. He files the third route under type F, says type F "can be seen as a sort of materialism" from one perspective, and files Stoljar's expanded-physical-base view there. The best Type-C defender therefore keeps the name "physicalist", and the page reports the move as a defeat.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

But your collapse section says type-C 'has no stable position' and then concedes a type-C materialist 'can dispute the structure-and-dynamics premise'. ### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

"'Admitting phenomenal character as a datum is exactly what Type-A rejects.' Most of Chalmers's own type-A list doesn't reject it: Dretske, Harman and Lewis all *explain* phenomenal character, as representation, function or ability. What we reject is a datum *over and above* the functions, and that is Chalmers's own wording." Chalmers 2003 puts the dispute as whether "consciousness (in a nonfunctionally defined sense) exists", and defines type-A as holding that nothing "needs explaining over and above explaining the various functions". The page drops the qualifier in two places (L38, L117), and so files non-eliminativist representationalists as datum-deniers. This is a different objection from the deep review's bedrock note, which was about begging the question. Issue 4.

## Answers to the Driver's Five Questions

1. **Is each routing-table tier earned?** The table carries reach labels, not tiers. Each reach label matches its argument and its host page: IBE L64 (datum, A only, "Type-B physicalist accepts phenomenal character as a datum"); NCC L152 (Type-B "predicts it will persist"); phenomenal-concepts-strategy (master argument, PCS only); arguments-against-materialism and reductionism (structure and dynamics, which B accepts). There are two exceptions: the stale Tenet-5 row (Issue 2) and the missing closure row (Issue 1). The tier section's verdicts (A suggestive and provisional, B compatible, C suggestive and non-separating, nowhere discriminating) match the IBE ladder (IBE L62) and P-M1. Note: P-M1 itself uses the five-tier scale, not compatible/suggestive/discriminating. The page correctly says that ladder is the IBE page's and that P-M1 "governs" it. No row says a reply "reaches" a type whose thesis it misses, with one dependency: row 4 points at [[parsimony-case-for-interactionist-dualism]], whose L75 still calls Type-B emergentist ("strongly emerges"). That is M7, already queued at P2 (todo L1756). Until it lands, the row sends readers to a misdescribed Type-B.
2. **Is the Type-C collapse presented fairly?** Mostly. The four routes match Chalmers's summary ("So the only room left is …") verbatim in substance. The contestability of structure and dynamics is reported and the collapse is kept as Chalmers's argument. What is missing is the best reply, and what is overstated is the payoff (Issue 3). On "a priori entailment from a completed physics": the page's route 3 handles it correctly as Chalmers does (F). The unfairness is in counting F as a loss for the physicalist.
3. **Is Type-B at full strength, and is "no Map article aims the Tenet-5 reply" fair?** No to both (Issues 1 and 2). The IEP entry the page already cites gives both of Type-B's main defences in the same section, and the page quotes only the one that suits its routing.
4. **Is "the Map is type-D by Chalmers's own definition" accurate?** Yes. Chalmers defines closure as "every microphysical state has a microphysical sufficient cause". Tenet 2's rationale rejects "the claim that every physical event has a sufficient physical cause", which is the same formulation. Chalmers's type-D is "causal gaps in microphysical dynamics that are filled by a causal role for distinct phenomenal properties", and his type-D list and quantum discussion include consciousness-collapse (Stapp 1993). The Map's corridor reading does not take it out of D on his definition, because D requires denying sufficient-cause closure, not violating Born statistics. Chalmers even allows that "one could hold that even a random causal role for consciousness is good enough". The honest caveat: his own footnote names the Born-preserving case as type-D's open question, and the Map's register concedes that such a selection "sits genuinely close to epiphenomenalism" ([[positions/quantum-interface#^mechanism-debt|P-Q3]]), that is, near type-E. The page already says "earns no evidence", so this is a hedge, not a correction. Ready text is recorded below, not ranked.
5. **Over-concession and its opposite.** A sweep of the tell words found no "no possible", "cannot ever" or "in principle undetectable", and no unhedged "refutes" or "defeats". "Type-B grants an epistemic gap that will never close" is Chalmers's "unclosable", applied to Type-B and not conceded by the Map. "The first-person warrant cannot be handed to an opponent who declines it" is the bedrock concession, already ratified. The one overclaim is "defeating A and B suffices" (Issue 3). The one under-claim is "which no type addresses" (Issue 1), which concedes too much about the Map's only discriminating channel.
