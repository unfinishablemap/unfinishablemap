## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

The article's evidence base is a catalogue of introspective reports — "gravitational pull," "vertigo," "friction," "cautious pleasure" — offered as data. Churchland's reply is that this is folk-psychological phenomenology treated as a natural kind. Worse, the article supplies its own defeater: §"Framework Dependence and Introspection" concedes introspection is theory-laden and that a dualist "may notice qualitative properties" *because the framework makes them salient*. The dualist's noticing of irreducible phenomenal texture in framework shift is exactly the framework-conditioned report the article has just told us to discount. The article never closes this loop.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

Dennett targets the Irreducibility Argument directly (L138): "If framework inhabitation and change were merely information reorganisation, they would have no phenomenal character. A database can be restructured without anything happening experientially." The inference requires that all information reorganisation resemble a database in the relevant respect — which is precisely what a functionalist denies. As written the argument is: *P has phenomenal character; databases do not; therefore P is not information reorganisation.* Invalid without a suppressed premise the article does not supply and a functionalist will not grant.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

See Critical Issue 2. The MQI paragraph posits "a single selection event across a superposed landscape of possible frameworks." Tegmark's objection lands with unusual force here because the *article's own timescales* refute it: it describes anomaly accumulation across the "late 1890s," framework habits "patterned through years of use," and consolidation as an extended phase. A superposition spanning a landscape of whole conceptual frameworks would have to persist across that interval. The corpus's own figure — Tegmark (2000), 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻²⁰ s, recorded in `tenets.md` — is between twenty and twenty-eight orders of magnitude short.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

The No-MWI paragraph asserts that experienced irreversibility "presupposes that framework change is a real transition, not a branching into parallel versions of oneself." Deutsch's reply is immediate: on MWI every branch-copy experiences the change as singular and irreversible, because each copy has exactly one history. The phenomenology the article cites is what MWI *predicts*, so it cannot discriminate between the two views. The argument does not engage MWI; it restates Tenet 4 in phenomenological vocabulary.

### The Empiricist (Popper's Ghost)

The "What Would Challenge This View?" section is the right instinct undone by the body. Two of its four falsifiers are neutralised by the article's own theses:

- *"Trained introspectors reported dissolution"* — but the article holds introspection is framework-dependent, so phenomenologists reporting dissolution would be exhibiting framework dependence, not refuting it. Either outcome confirms.
- *"AI systems displayed equivalent phenomenology"* — the Map's registered position (`positions/ai-consciousness-scope.md` P-A-series) places LLMs on the low-probability side of a consciousness verdict "not because they fail behavioural tests, but because a substrate analysis... finds no sites." If behavioural tests are not what settles the question, a demonstration of "equivalent phenomenology" in an AI system has no available test procedure. The falsifier names no observation anyone could make.

Falsifiers 1 and 3 survive and are genuine. Two of four are decorative.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

The article reifies "the framework" as a dwelling with walls, gravity, and a maintenance mechanism, and then treats the *inhabitant* as the fixed point that persists through renovation. Nagarjuna's question is why the subject who "can remember thinking the old way but can no longer *think* that way" is a self at all rather than another framework-conditioned appearance. Notably, both archived predecessors engaged this: `phenomenology-of-framework-inhabitation.md` L112 discussed whether contemplative training "genuinely transcend[s] framework dependence or merely install[s] a new framework (the contemplative one)" and flagged that this is "itself a question that framework dependence makes difficult to answer" — a good, honest move. The live article retains only the phrase "beginner's mind" at L111. **The coalesce dropped the corpus's only engagement with the strongest objection to its central metaphor.**

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Chi (1992) is credited with a phenomenal finding she did not report — and a prior review certified this clean

- **File**: `obsidian/topics/phenomenology-of-conceptual-frameworks.md`
- **Location**: L140
- **Severity**: **High** (citation-framing; source-conclusion conflation)

L140 reads:

> Chi (1992) showed that conceptual changes crossing ontological categories — from "matter" to "process" — are qualitatively harder and phenomenally different from within-category changes. The felt difficulty tracks the ontological boundary, not the quantity of information revised.

The predicate **"phenomenally different"** sits inside the scope of "Chi (1992) showed that." Chi's work is instructional cognitive science measuring the *robustness of misconceptions* and their *resistance to remediation*. Her own statement of the programme (Chi 2005, *Journal of the Learning Sciences*, abstract retrieved via OpenAlex) frames the explanandum as "why some concepts of processes are resistant to instructional remediation," concluding that "misconceptions of emergent processes are robust because they misinterpret emergent processes as a kind of commonsense direct processes. To correct such a misconception requires a re-representation or a conceptual shift across ontological kinds." Difficulty and robustness are her measures. Phenomenal character is not among them, and "the felt difficulty tracks the ontological boundary" attributes a felt-difficulty measurement to a study that measured learning outcomes.

Two aggravating facts:

1. **The corpus already had the correct wording and lost it.** `archive/topics/phenomenology-of-conceptual-change.md` L108 reads "why crossing what Chi (1992) **calls** ontological category boundaries **feels** qualitatively different" — label attributed to Chi, phenomenal claim retained in the Map's voice. That is correct discipline. The predecessor was internally inconsistent (its L98 carried the over-attributing form), and the coalesce kept L98 and discarded L108.

2. **A prior review certified the exact absence of this defect.** `obsidian/reviews/deep-review-2026-05-28-phenomenology-of-conceptual-frameworks.md` ran a section headed "Source-conclusion check (not just metadata)" and concluded: *"Notably Carey, Chi, Nersessian, Thagard are cited against their own home discipline's reductive read... this is framed as the Map's argument, not as those authors' conclusions, so no source/Map conflation."* For Chi this is precisely backwards. The 2026-06-25 review then declined to re-check on the strength of that ledger — `changelog-2026-W26.md` L1675: *"the §2.4 web-verify trigger is NOT met — the full 11-cite publisher-of-record ledger from 05-28 stands and is not re-lit."* A metadata ledger was used to license skipping a framing check the ledger never performed.

- **Recommendation**: Restore the predecessor's L108 construction. Attribute the ontological-boundary *taxonomy* and the *difficulty* finding to Chi; keep "feels qualitatively different" and the felt-difficulty claim in the Map's voice as an interpretation of her result. Do not delete the citation — Chi genuinely supports the difficulty asymmetry, which is the load the argument actually needs.

**Metadata, separately, is clean.** Verified this run at Crossref/OpenAlex: McGinn (1989) *Mind* XCVIII(391):349-366 — exact. Chi (1992) chapter title and year — exact. Davidson (1974) APA *Proceedings and Addresses* 47:5-20 — confirmed via the APA Centennial Series reprint (DOI 10.5840/apapa2013236) and the *Inquiries into Truth and Interpretation* reprint. The 05-28 ledger was right about metadata and wrong about framing; the two axes are orthogonal.

### Issue 2: The Minimal Quantum Interaction paragraph asserts a mechanism the Map's own register subordinates

- **File**: `obsidian/topics/phenomenology-of-conceptual-frameworks.md`
- **Location**: L166
- **Severity**: **High** (tenet misalignment; positions-register conflict)

> The gestalt shift's totality — the entire framework reorganising simultaneously — is structurally compatible with quantum selection. If consciousness biases otherwise indeterminate neural states, the holistic character may reflect **a single selection event across a superposed landscape of possible frameworks**, rather than incremental computation.

A "superposed landscape of possible frameworks" is a macroscopic, sustained, brain-wide superposition of entire conceptual systems. This conflicts with the Map's own commitments in two directions at once:

- **Against minimality.** Tenet 2 is "the smallest possible non-physical influence on physical outcomes" (`tenets.md` L62). A superposition spanning whole frameworks is the largest quantum structure posited anywhere in the corpus. The paragraph invokes the minimality tenet while asserting a maximally non-minimal mechanism.
- **Against the register's preferred mechanism.** `positions/quantum-interface.md` P-Q1 holds post-decoherence selection as "the Map's preferred candidate... preferred because it sidesteps the warm-wet decoherence problem," and P-Q4/P-Q5 explicitly *demote* the pre-decoherence-coherence families (Stapp-Zeno, Orch-OR) "on timing grounds." The article's mechanism requires more sustained coherence than either demoted candidate.

The "Speculative, but..." hedge does not repair this. The problem is not confidence level; it is that the paragraph reaches for the wrong mechanism class to cash out its phenomenology, when the Map's endorsed mechanism would serve the argument better and at lower cost. Post-decoherence selection is *selection among already-decohered alternatives* — which is a perfectly good gloss on "the gestalt settles into one organisation rather than another" and carries none of the coherence-timescale exposure.

- **Recommendation**: Re-cash the paragraph in post-decoherence-selection terms, or drop the mechanism speculation and keep only the structural point (holistic outcome-selection is the shape Tenet 2 posits). Link `[[apex/post-decoherence-selection-programme]]`. Do not simply add a hedge.

### Issue 3: The No-Many-Worlds paragraph is boundary-substitution, and `tenets.md` does the honest version

- **File**: `obsidian/topics/phenomenology-of-conceptual-frameworks.md`
- **Location**: L168
- **Severity**: **High** (reasoning-mode failure — boundary-substitution)

> The experienced irreversibility presupposes that framework change is a real transition, not a branching into parallel versions of oneself. The determinacy of "which framework do *I* now inhabit?" reflects real collapse, not the illusion of collapse within one branch.

This presents a tenet-register move as though it refuted MWI inside MWI's framework. It does not: branch-relative determinacy and branch-relative irreversibility are exactly what MWI predicts, so the phenomenology is evidentially inert between the views. The article asserts the conclusion ("reflects real collapse") as the argument.

`tenets.md` handles the same terrain correctly, under "What the indexical objection presupposes (honestly noted)": it concedes the objection "is meaningful only on a *non-deflationary* conception of personal identity," that the Map endorses such an "I" *on independent grounds, not from within this tenet*, and that the deflationary route "is a genuine bedrock disagreement, not an in-framework defect the Map can refute." The authority file marks the boundary honestly; the article dressed the same move as a refutation.

- **Recommendation**: Downgrade to honest framework-boundary marking, inheriting `tenets.md`'s register. The phenomenological observation (framework change is *experienced* as singular and irreversible) is worth keeping — it just cannot carry an anti-MWI verdict, and should be stated as consonant with Tenet 4 rather than as support for it.

### Issue 4: The epiphenomenalism argument is deployed bare, in a form `tenets.md` says the strongest epiphenomenalism survives

- **File**: `obsidian/topics/phenomenology-of-conceptual-frameworks.md`
- **Location**: L144
- **Severity**: **High** (logical gap; contradicts the tenets page)

> If these states were epiphenomenal, framework change should look the same with or without them.

The counterfactual is one epiphenomenalism itself blocks: the phenomenal states are *caused by* the physical states doing the work, so there is no available world where you subtract the phenomenal states and hold the physical base fixed. As stated the premise is not one an epiphenomenalist grants.

Both the archived predecessor and the tenets page are better on this:

- `archive/topics/phenomenology-of-conceptual-change.md` L104 states the same counterfactual but immediately concedes the reply and shifts to an inference to the best explanation: *"An epiphenomenalist can grant that phenomenal states accompany these transitions, but the transitions' distinctive features — their holistic simultaneity, their selectivity, their irreversibility — map onto the phenomenology's contours with a precision that demands explanation."* That is a defensible argument. The coalesce kept the counterfactual and deleted the IBE.
- `tenets.md` (Tenet 3) goes further, treating the phenomenal-concept strategy (Frankish, Papineau, Loar) and concluding that *"the live disagreement belongs [at the mode-of-presentation question] rather than at 'epiphenomenalism is self-undermining,' which the strongest version of the position survives."*

The article therefore deploys, in support of Tenet 3, the very argument the Tenet 3 authority page says fails against strong epiphenomenalism.

- **Recommendation**: Restore the predecessor's IBE form (concede the accompaniment, argue from the precision of the fit), and add a pointer to the tenets page's phenomenal-concept treatment rather than re-litigating it here.

### Issue 5: The anti-functionalist premise contradicts the computational model cited two paragraphs later

- **File**: `obsidian/topics/phenomenology-of-conceptual-frameworks.md`
- **Location**: L140 and L146
- **Severity**: **Medium-High** (internal contradiction)

L140: *"functional reorganisation can proceed piecemeal or holistically, gradually or suddenly, and nothing in functional description alone predicts which pattern a given reorganisation follows."*

L146: *"Computational models — Thagard's (1992) explanatory coherence networks, Nersessian's (2008) model-based reasoning, Bayesian model selection — capture structural aspects..."*

Thagard's ECHO is a connectionist parallel-constraint-satisfaction network. Holistic, all-at-once settling into a globally coherent state is not an unpredicted possibility for it — it is the model's operating principle and its central result. The article's premise says functional description cannot predict the holistic pattern; its own cited model predicts exactly that pattern from functional description alone. The predecessor was again more careful, conceding that *"his framework successfully predicts which framework wins"* — a concession the live article dropped.

- **Recommendation**: Narrow the premise. The defensible claim is not that functional description cannot predict *holism* (ECHO does), but that it does not predict the *phenomenal* accompaniments — totality-as-experienced, irreversibility-as-liberation, retroactive illumination. Restore the Thagard concession so the article is not refuted by its own reference list.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The reflexivity problem is answered with a tu quoque

- **Current content says** (L158): "Acknowledging that the dualist framework shapes what one notices does not make dualism self-refuting, since the same applies equally to materialism."
- **A critic would argue**: Parity is not vindication. The article's §"False Fluency" claims that *"No phenomenal marker distinguishes 'I understand this in its own terms' from 'I have translated this into terms my framework can process'"* — from which it follows that the Map cannot tell, from the inside, whether its own reading of materialism is genuine comprehension or framework-translation. That is not a symmetric embarrassment the Map can shrug off by noting materialists share it; it is a specific defeater for *this article's* central move, which consists in reporting that materialist accounts miss a phenomenal residue. The stronger the article's framework-opacity thesis, the less entitled it is to its own comparative verdict.
- **Suggested response**: Add the argument the tu quoque is standing in for. The most promising line already exists in the corpus's toolkit: the Map's claim is not framework-neutral perception but that the *phenomenal residue survives translation in both directions* — a materialist who takes the residue seriously reports it too, which is why the hard problem is stated in materialist journals. That is an argument; parity is not.

### The "seconds to minutes" claim is presented as an observation

- **Current content says** (L97): "The experience is transient: within seconds to minutes, the framework reasserts its transparency."
- **A critic would argue**: A quantitative duration range is offered with no source, no method, and no indication of who timed it. Nothing in the cited literature (Kuhn, Polanyi, Ratcliffe, Heidegger) supplies it. It reads as a phenomenological finding and is an assertion.
- **Suggested response**: Either mark it as a first-person report of the kind the article elsewhere treats as data, or drop the numbers and keep "transient." The argument that follows — that transience implies transparency is *actively maintained* — does not need the range.
