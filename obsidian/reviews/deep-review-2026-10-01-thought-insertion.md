## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Dropped qualifier on Lane: the concession was truncated before its own denial.** Zahavi and Kriegel (2016, p. 44) write: "Lane actually allows that there is an utterly trivial sense in which the first-person perspective is retained even in pathological cases, **but claims that this has no bearing on the issue of for-me-ness**." Lane, citing Blanke and Metzinger (2009), treats that perspective as a "purely geometrical feature". The page quoted the first half and used it at L75 as evidence that "the live dispute concerns mineness", at L91 ("Lane reportedly grants a trivial first-person perspective") as a reason no reading describes phenomenal experience without first-personal givenness, and at L93 to claim that "Every party grants the thin feature the claim concerns". Lane denies for-me-ness by name ("for-me-ness does not play a determining role in its constitution", Lane 2012, 281, quoted identically by ZK p. 44 and HPZ pp. 2–3). On HPZ's report he also holds that "hosting" a thought does not imply "ownership" and that "perspective does not determine mineness". The page therefore presented the universalists' re-description of Lane as Lane's own concession. This is source/Map conflation plus a dropped qualifier. **Fixed** at L75, L91, L93 and in the lead (L37); see Edits.
2. **Calibration of "It survives on uncontested ground" (L93): overstated.** This is a calibration error inside the framework, not bedrock. A tenet-accepting reviewer would still flag it, because the page's own L97 concedes that "reported from somewhere" proves only a reporting *position* and nothing phenomenal, while L93 asserted that every party grants the phenomenal thin feature. What every party grants is that the thought is given to the patient alone, which Lane calls hosting. Whether that is for-me-ness is the disputed question. The header now reads "It survives on shared ground under a disputed name" and states the dispute. The opposite direction was also checked. The falsifier paragraph (L95) no longer over-concedes: "The falsifier is conceptual" became "Against the clinical record the falsifier is conceptual", because the same paragraph keeps a third-person empirical route open. "none exist" became "none yet exist". No "no possible", "cannot ever" or "in principle undetectable" tell remains. The bold "cannot be met by any report" is exact, since the falsifier itself stipulates "no position from which the lack is registered".
3. **Anachronism (L53): "Gallagher replies … (2007)" to Billon's 2013 objection.** Gallagher 2007 predates Billon (online 2011, print 2013). Billon 2013 pp. 296–297 engages Gallagher 2000's *protention* account of unbidden thoughts, not the 2007 second-order line. Now reads "Gallagher's earlier answer for unbidden thoughts was that …". The Map's inference that follows ("That moves the difference into higher-order attribution …") is faithful to Gallagher 2007 p. 4 of the PDF, where the unbidden thought lacks first-order agency and is "certainly" self-attributed at second order.
4. **Wrong provenance label (References 9).** Graham & Stephens (1994) "Mind and mine" was marked "content via HPZ 2019", but HPZ (p. 2) discuss **Stephens and Graham (1994)**, the *PPP* 1(1) paper, as does Guillot's "standard account" sentence. The full-text secondary for "Mind and mine" is Gallagher 2007, which cites it at pp. 91–109 and describes its "Attributions of subjectivity (ownership)" and "Attributions of agency". Label corrected to "content via Gallagher 2007". The body sentence "Graham and Stephens (1994) introduced the dominant split" stands, since both 1994 works by the pair draw the split.
