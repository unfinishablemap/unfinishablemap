# Independent outer audit: “Four-Quadrant Dualism Taxonomy”

## Review basis and overall verdict

I reviewed the live article as it stood on **12 November 2026**. The page records its last substantive modification as **5 November 2026**. I also checked the site changelog, including entries recording corrections to the treatment of J.P. Saad on other pages while identifying the taxonomy article’s own closure claim as an unresolved, out-of-scope occurrence. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

**Overall verdict:** the article contains a useful exploratory idea, but it does not yet establish an exhaustive or reliably operationalised taxonomy. Its core question—how much structure a theory assigns respectively to the mental and physical sides—is worth retaining. The present implementation, however:

- defines more than two levels on each axis but offers only four quadrants;
- mixes ontological structure with causal efficacy, causal closure, epistemic ignorance and interpretive commitments;
- places several views using criteria different from those stated in the definitions;
- contains important source errors, especially concerning Saad and Chalmers;
- sometimes treats positions that reject the mental–physical two-pole framing as though they occupied extreme points within it;
- and inherits or creates contradictions with linked Map articles.

The best repair is not to abandon the scheme, but to reframe it as a **two-dimensional heuristic supplemented by independent annotations**, rather than as an exhaustive taxonomy of dualism.

### Status labels used below

- **Verified error:** contradicted by a primary source or by an unequivocal statement elsewhere on the site.
- **Source defect:** the claim may be defensible, but the cited source does not establish it.
- **Matter of judgement:** more than one placement is reasonable because the axis or scoring rule is underdetermined.
- **Internal inconsistency:** the article conflicts with another Map article or with the changelog.

# 1. Is the taxonomy exhaustive?

## 1.1 Four quadrants do not follow from the article’s own scale

The article defines mental and physical thickness using at least **thin, medium and thick/maximal** levels. Taken literally, two three-valued axes generate nine combinations, not four. A continuous two-dimensional plane can of course be divided into four quadrants, but that requires a stated origin or threshold separating “low” from “high.” The article supplies neither. It consequently describes views such as the Map’s current preference as “physical-thin plus mind-medium, near Q1,” rather than providing a determinate cell assignment. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

This is not merely terminological. Without thresholds:

- “medium mind” can be grouped with either thin or thick;
- a theory can move quadrant when one redescribes the same commitment as a substance, structure, capacity or law;
- there is no reproducible basis for two reviewers to place the same view;
- and claims about which quadrants are populated or neglected cannot be tested.

The taxonomy is therefore exhaustive only in the weak sense that **four endpoint combinations** are logically possible:

1. low mental / low physical;
2. low mental / high physical;
3. high mental / low physical;
4. high mental / high physical.

It is not exhaustive of the positions actually discussed in the article.

## 1.2 Several theories are not naturally points on these axes

The grid presupposes that a theory supplies two sufficiently separable inventories: a mental pole and a physical pole. That works reasonably well for recognisably dualist theories with two categories or domains. It works much less well for:

- Russellian monism, where the same intrinsic properties may underlie both consciousness and physical structure;
- hylomorphism, where matter and form are co-principles of one substance;
- priority cosmopsychism, where the cosmos is the sole fundamental individual;
- idealism, where the physical pole is derivative;
- neutral monism, where neither pole is fundamental;
- process ontologies, where the basic units are not mental and physical substances.

The article acknowledges that some monist positions are included only as “limit cases,” while the linked substance/property discussion recognises process approaches such as Whitehead’s as a third framing rather than a straightforward member of the substance/property dichotomy. The linked channel discussion similarly treats hylomorphism as lacking the kind of separately located interface assumed by the grid. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

The correct conclusion is therefore:

> **The taxonomy is potentially exhaustive for stipulated high/low combinations within a restricted class of two-pole ontologies. It is not exhaustive of dualism-adjacent philosophy of mind, and it is not presently operationalised well enough to be exhaustive even within that restricted class.**

# 2. Are the axes independent?

## 2.1 Conceptually independent, but not operationally independent

In the abstract, the two intended questions are independent:

- How much internal structure does the theory assign to mind?
- How much internal structure does it assign to physical reality?

It is conceivable to give either answer independently of the other. The four endpoint combinations are therefore logically coherent.

But the article does not consistently measure those two questions. Its mental axis incorporates:

- introspectable versus subliminal contents;
- subject unity and persistence;
- substancehood;
- causal intervention;
- size of causal repertoire;
- cosmic versus individual scope.

Its physical axis incorporates:

- number or type of physical structures;
- hidden variables and extra dimensions;
- causal closure;
- quantum interpretation;
- epistemic ignorance;
- wavefunction realism;
- scientific realism more generally.

The page later says thickness is supposed to track **structural richness only**, rather than causal work or channel width, but its own definitions and placements repeatedly use causal efficacy and closure to determine thickness. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

That creates a construct-validity problem. These are not variants of one quantity:

| Feature | What it actually measures |
| --- | --- |
| Subliminal mental layers | Mental structure |
| Unified persisting subject | Subject metaphysics |
| Mental causal efficacy | Mind–body relation |
| Physical causal closure | Causal sufficiency |
| Hidden variables or extra dimensions | Physical structure |
| Unknown physical facts | Epistemic limitation |
| Universal wavefunction | Interpretation and ontology |
| Branch multiplicity | Ontic interpretation |
| Scientific realism | Metaphilosophical attitude |

An axis can combine these only by stipulation, not by showing that they form a single ordered dimension.

## 2.2 Closure and mental efficacy are especially damaging confounds

The most important entanglement is between **mental causal efficacy** and **physical causal closure**. The article makes causal repertoire part of mental thickness while treating full physical closure as evidence of physical maximality. Yet these are relational properties of the complete theory, not intrinsic amounts of mental or physical structure.

This also builds a tendency towards anti-correlation into supposedly independent axes. If “more mental causation” makes the mind thicker while “more physical causal sufficiency” makes physics thicker, then causal exclusion pressure makes it difficult to score both high without overdetermination, constitution or some distinction between actual and default causation. Chalmers’s Type-O category exists precisely because causal role and ontological distinctness cut across simple pole-by-pole measurements. [Consciousness.net](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

## 2.3 Some views cause double-counting

Russellian views expose another failure of independence. If categorical or quiddistic properties are simultaneously:

- the intrinsic nature of the physical,
- and the basis of phenomenal consciousness,

then counting them once as physical enrichment and again as mental enrichment is not measuring two independent ontologies. It is measuring two descriptions or roles of the same proposed base. Russellian monism is expressly structured around a single underlying property base, though different versions can ultimately be physicalist, idealist, neutral-monist or dualist. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+3](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/)

### Verdict on independence

- **Logical independence:** defensible.
- **Operational independence in the article:** not achieved.
- **Applicability to monist, hylomorphic or constitutive views:** frequently fails.
- **Reproducibility of assignments:** currently low.

# 3. Source and placement audit

## 3.1 J.P. Saad

### Status: **Verified error, high significance**

The article presents Saad as a principal example of closure-compatible causal dualism. It says, in different places, that microphysical closure remains intact, that the physical cause stays sufficient, and that Saad can be integrated with quantum indeterminacy. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

Saad explicitly distinguishes:

- **Closure:** the strong claim that every physical event has a sufficient physical cause;
- **Observational Closure:** the weaker claim that no observable physical deviation from ordinary physical expectations occurs.

His proposed theories preserve the latter while violating the former. Physical states have **default causal profiles**, but an experience can pre-empt that default. In his pain example, the pain rather than the C-fibre state is the actual cause of behaviour, even though the physical state fixes what would ordinarily have occurred in the experience’s absence. Saad also presents his account as avoiding causal overdetermination. [Springer+2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-025-02290-3)

Consequently:

- “microphysical closure remains fully intact” is false if “closure” means causal sufficiency;
- “the physical cause stays sufficient” is false as a description of the actual causal episode;
- treating Saad as involving two concurrently sufficient causes obscures his pre-emption strategy;
- and treating his account as a quantum-indeterminacy mechanism adds a mechanism not supplied by Saad’s published theory.

The changelog makes this particularly clear. It records corrections to the Closure/Observational Closure distinction across six other pages and explicitly lists the corresponding statement in the taxonomy article as an unresolved, out-of-scope occurrence. [[changelog]]

Saad may still be placed near a physically austere, mentally causally active region. What must change is the rationale: his theory preserves **observable physical regularity**, not strong physical causal closure.

## 3.2 Chalmers: Type E, Type F and the missing Type O

### Status: **Verified omission and mischaracterisation, high significance**

The article treats Type-E dualism as physically maximal because physical closure is complete. Chalmers defines Type E by the absence of mental-to-physical causal influence. He says physical closure is a natural accompaniment, but not an essential part of the definition. Type E may also be formulated within either substance or property dualism. It is therefore a relation type, not a measure of physical ontological richness. [Consciousness.net](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

More importantly, the article’s discussion of an underrepresented family combining phenomenal distinctness, causal efficacy and closed microphysics omits Chalmers’s **Type O**. Type O treats phenomenal properties as ontologically distinct while preserving closed microphysical causation, with mental causal relevance supplied through overdetermination or mediation by the physical. That does not settle whether Type O succeeds, but it means the relevant combination was already explicitly named in the taxonomy the article cites. [Consciousness.net](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

Saad is not simply a worked Type-O instance:

- Type O retains closed microphysics.
- Saad denies strong closure while preserving observational closure.
- Type O permits overdetermination or mediation.
- Saad advertises avoidance of overdetermination through pre-emption.

The omission matters because the article presents Saad as the strongest candidate for a family it portrays as difficult even to articulate. Saad is important, but he is not evidence that the conceptual category lacked a prior name.

The article’s “rich versus austere Type-F” language also does not appear to be terminology supplied by Chalmers’s 2003 classification. Chalmers distinguishes phenomenal from protophenomenal variants and discusses questions about fundamental intrinsic properties and high-level instantiation. The article may introduce a useful secondary distinction, but it should mark it as the Map’s extension rather than attribute it to Chalmers. [Consciousness.net+1](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

Finally, Chalmers notes C.D. Broad’s earlier systematic matrix of mind–body possibilities. Broad’s axes are not identical to the Map’s thickness axes, but this is enough to require qualification of claims that a second classificatory dimension has rarely been named. [Consciousness.net](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

## 3.3 Descartes

### Status: **Verified error in the stated rationale; quadrant placement is a matter of judgement**

The article associates Cartesian mental richness with substantial and “extra-introspective” cognitive content. Descartes, however, characterises thought in terms of what is immediately conscious, encompassing understanding, willing, imagining and perceiving. “Extra-introspective cognitive content” is therefore a poor description of what makes the Cartesian mind substantial or rich. [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4391/pg4391.html)

A thick-mind placement can still be defended using other considerations:

- the mind is a distinct substance;
- it is unified;
- it has multiple faculties;
- it persists through changes in particular thoughts;
- and it has causal relations with body.

The physical-side placement is less secure. Descartes characterises corporeal substance through extension and its modes, including figure and motion. That is a comparatively austere account of body, despite body being a substance. “Substantial” and “structurally rich” are not equivalent. [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4391/pg4391.html)

Accordingly, treating Cartesian dualism as Q4 is a legitimate reconstruction only if **substancehood itself** heavily increases both thickness scores. Under a metric of internal structural variety, it could instead be mind-rich and physically thin. The article needs to state which rule it is using.

## 3.4 Stoljar’s epistemic view

### Status: **Verified category mismatch**

The article places Stoljar toward a physically maximal quadrant because reality may contain unknown non-experiential physical facts. But Stoljar’s epistemic view is deliberately framed as ignorance of relevant nonconscious facts, without committing to the distinctive extra metaphysics of Russellianism or McGinn-style cognitive closure. [Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/ignorance-and-imagination-the-epistemic-origin-of-the-problem-of-consciousness/)

An assertion that there are facts we do not know is not by itself an assertion that physical ontology is structurally maximal. The unknown facts might reveal a rich ontology, an austere ontology, or a reconceptualisation of familiar properties.

The appropriate coding is therefore:

- **physical thickness: undetermined**, or
- **range: medium to high, conditional on what the unknown facts are**.

Classifying epistemic ignorance itself as ontological thickness conflicts with the article’s stated commitment to measuring structure rather than our knowledge of it.

## 3.5 Aquinas and hylomorphism

### Status: **Verified mismatch with the Q4 description; exact placement remains contested**

The article places Thomistic hylomorphism in Q4, whose governing image is two rich, distinct realms interacting. Aquinas instead treats the human being as a composite of matter and substantial form. The separated soul is not the complete human person but a part or incomplete principle of that person. [New Advent+1](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1075.htm)

Contemporary interpreters dispute whether hylomorphic accounts should count as substance dualism, property dualism, a sui generis form of dualism, or something that cuts across those classifications. That dispute makes a categorical Q4 placement inappropriate without a stated reconstruction. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dualism/)

The site’s own channel-width article handles the issue more carefully by treating hylomorphism as off-axis: form and matter do not interact across a separately identifiable interface in the way assumed by standard interactionist models. [[dualism-channel-width-axis]]

The target article should therefore either:

1. mark hylomorphism **N/A/off-axis**;
2. introduce a separate “constitutive co-principles” relation type;
3. or explain that it is mapping a modern dualist reconstruction rather than Aquinas’s own two-realm ontology.

## 3.6 Russellian monism and Russellian panpsychism

### Status: **Overgeneralisation and internal inconsistency**

Standard Russellian monism proposes that one underlying set of intrinsic properties constitutes or grounds both physical structure and consciousness. It does not normally posit two independently rich realms communicating through an interface. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/)

There are multiple Russellian variants, including versions that can be interpreted as physicalist, idealist, neutral-monist and, in some formulations, dualist. A specifically dualist Russellian theory could therefore be placed in Q4. But “Russellian panpsychism” without further specification does not identify such a theory. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/)

The target placement also conflicts with the Map’s linked Russellian-monism discussion, which distinguishes the Map’s interactionism from a one-stuff or two-aspect account in which there is no interaction across an ontological interface. [[russellian-monism-versus-bi-aspectual-dualism]]

The repair is to replace the generic entry with one of:

- **standard constitutive Russellian panpsychism: off-axis or monist limit case**;
- **dualist Russellian variant: possible Q4, exact theory to be named**;
- **Russellian interactionism: hypothetical construction, not a standard named view**.

## 3.7 Henry Stapp

### Status: **Verified mechanism error; thickness placement is a matter of judgement**

Stapp’s account distinguishes at least two relevant selections:

1. the human agent selects or poses a Process-1 question, corresponding to a projection or decomposition;
2. nature selects the outcome or answer.

The quantum Zeno effect arises from rapid repetition of similar Process-1 actions, stabilising a selected pattern. It is not the primitive interaction channel by itself. [Physics Division+1](https://www-physics.lbl.gov/~stapp/QID.pdf)

The target article’s compressed characterisation of Stapp as involving “quantum-Zeno biasing only” is therefore inaccurate. A better description is:

> agent-selected question or basis, followed by nature-selected outcome, with repeated Process-1 events allowing a quantum-Zeno stabilisation mechanism.

The linked channel-width page is more accurate in recognising that basis or question selection makes Stapp’s proposed interface layered rather than strictly “outcome selection only.” [[dualism-channel-width-axis]]

Stapp’s physical-thickness placement is less clear. The target article defines ordinary quantum theory plus decoherence as a medium physical ontology, but then appears to count Stapp’s use of the quantum state as physically maximal. That requires an additional argument—perhaps that Stapp treats the state vector as ontically complete or adds a process ontology—not merely the observation that the theory uses the full state vector. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

## 3.8 Martine Nida-Rümelin

### Status: **Source defect rather than demonstrated placement error**

The cited identity work supports a subject–body dualism in which the conscious subject is not identical with or constituted by a material object, and it supports first-person and transtemporal claims about subject identity. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12724/chapter-abstract/162811194)

The article’s additional appeal to causal intervention requires a different source. Nida-Rümelin’s work on doings and subject causation explicitly discusses an active subject continuously causing physical changes and argues for compatibility with much ordinary neurophysiology. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225482890_Doings_and_Subject_Causation)

Thus the broad placement may be defensible, but the bibliography should cite the causal paper for the causal claim rather than asking the identity chapter to support both.

## 3.9 F.W.H. Myers and the Myers–James programme

### Status: **Partly supported; physical-side placement is weakly sourced**

Myers clearly supplies material for a mentally rich classification: the ordinary empirical self is presented as a fragment of a larger self, and the subliminal region contains differentiated strata and capacities. [Project Gutenberg+1](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/38492/pg38492-images.html)

The claim that the corresponding physics is lean or austere is much less secure. Myers also invokes an etherial or metetherial environment, spiritual energy and forms of interaction between material and non-material domains. Those commitments may themselves enlarge the nonmental or intermediary ontology. [Project Gutenberg+1](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/38492/pg38492-images.html)

The current bibliography compounds the problem by directing the reader to a biographical or encyclopaedic entry rather than to the relevant passages in Myers’s primary work. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

The “Myers–James plus Bohm” Q4 case is intelligible as a **synthetic model**, but it should not count as evidence that Q4 is occupied by a historically defended position unless an author actually combined those commitments. Constructed hybrids are useful for demonstrating logical possibility; they should be labelled separately from attributed theories.

## 3.10 Goff and priority cosmopsychism

### Status: **Broad placement defensible, provided it remains a limit case**

Priority cosmopsychism treats the conscious cosmos as the sole or primary fundamental individual. That supports a mentally rich classification, but it is a monist rather than dualist ontology. Treating it as a Q3 boundary or limit case is therefore reasonable, and the article now does this more carefully than earlier versions recorded in the changelog. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/book/3834/chapter-abstract/145323999)

Goff’s work distinguishes several kinds of Russellian and panpsychist position, including constitutive, emergentist, panprotopsychist and panqualityist options. The article’s simpler “rich versus austere panpsychism” distinction may be a useful Map-level overlay, but it should not be presented as Goff’s own taxonomy without a pinpoint source. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/3834/chapter-abstract/145321887)

## 3.11 William Jaworski

### Status: **Matter of judgement, appropriately caveated but not a true quadrant occupant**

Jaworski treats structure as ontologically and explanatorily basic and treats mental phenomena as structural features within the physical world. The article correctly notes that this is not Jaworski’s own dualism. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/7169)

Calling it “near Q2” is therefore an external analogy: it means that the Map interprets structural richness on the physical side as performing work that another taxonomy might assign to mind. That can be illuminating, but it should be visually distinguished from actual quadrant membership.

## 3.12 Everettian quantum mechanics

### Status: **Matter of judgement**

Counting Everett as physically thick because it includes a universal wavefunction and branching structure is defensible. But it is not forced:

- one interpretation counts the multiplicity of branches as ontological proliferation;
- another emphasises a single universal state evolving by a simple law;
- a structuralist reading may deny that branches are additional fundamental objects.

The page appropriately acknowledges that this is a chosen ontological reading rather than a neutral consequence of the formalism. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

This should become the model for other contested placements: state the interpretation, then state how the scoring follows.

## 3.13 Pereboom

### Status: **Probable terminology or attribution error**

I could not verify “qualitative inertness” as Pereboom’s standard label for the position apparently intended. The recognised expression in the relevant work is the **qualitative inaccuracy hypothesis**: introspection represents phenomenal properties as having qualitative features that they do not in fact possess. [OUP Academic+2](https://academic.oup.com/book/7566/chapter-abstract/152559780)

If the article intends a different Pereboom proposal, it needs an exact work and page reference. Otherwise, “qualitative inertness” should be corrected rather than left as though it were established terminology.

## Important missing literature

This is the most consequential omission because it directly anticipates the article’s closure-compatible causal-dualism category. [Consciousness.net](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

### Broad’s earlier matrix

Broad’s classification is not identical to the Map’s, but it provides essential historical context for multidimensional mind–body taxonomies. [Consciousness.net](https://consc.net/papers/nature.html)

### Current dualism and mental-causation surveys

The Stanford Encyclopedia’s “Dualism” entry was substantively revised in October 2025 and treats interaction, hylomorphism and the difficulty of drawing the substance/property boundary. It should be used as a current orientation source, followed by primary literature for placements. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dualism/)

A 2026 Philosophy Compass review by Vaassen is directly relevant because it organises dualist approaches to mental causation partly by whether they reject or accommodate physical self-sufficiency. That cuts across the Map’s thickness axes and would help separate closure strategy from ontological richness. [Compass Hub](https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/epdf/10.1111/phc3.70109)

## Unsupported sociological claims

Claims that Q2 is the reflexive or default position, that Q4 has been neglected, or that materialist intellectual training discourages Q4 exploration are empirical claims about disciplinary behaviour. The article does not provide a survey, bibliometric analysis or systematic review supporting them. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

They should be presented as hypotheses, not findings. A taxonomy may reveal that its author has difficulty finding occupants for a quadrant; it does not by itself show that the quadrant has been culturally suppressed.

# 5. Counterarguments currently omitted or underweighted

## 5.1 The metric objection

No rule states how many commitments, layers, powers or structures move a theory from thin to medium or medium to thick. Nor is there a rule for weighting:

- one additional substance;
- many additional properties;
- a single law with broad causal powers;
- infinitely many branches;
- a cosmic subject;
- a subliminal hierarchy;
- or inaccessible intrinsic properties.

Without a scoring rubric, the classifications remain curated impressions.

## 5.2 The redescription objection

The same theory can move quadrant when redescribed:

- a unified subject can be called one austere entity or a metaphysically substantial addition;
- a universal wavefunction can be called one simple object or an enormously rich state;
- Russellian properties can be counted as physical, mental, neutral or both;
- hylomorphic form can be counted as mental structure, physical organisation or a category outside both.

A robust taxonomy must be invariant under at least ordinary changes of vocabulary, or must identify which theoretical roles determine the score.

## 5.3 The baseline objection

“Physics and no more” is not an interpretation-neutral baseline. Standard quantum mechanics underdetermines questions about:

- wavefunction ontology;
- collapse;
- branching;
- hidden variables;
- the status of decoherence;
- and whether the formal state is representational or ontic.

The article’s thin/medium/max physical rankings sometimes score the formalism, sometimes an interpretation of the formalism, and sometimes a causal thesis such as closure.

## 5.4 The closure-family objection

The article needs to distinguish at least:

- strong causal closure;
- causal completeness;
- observational closure;
- conservation-law compliance;
- counterfactual physical sufficiency;
- nomological predictability;
- actual-cause sufficiency.

Saad is the clearest demonstration of why these cannot be collapsed. Type O, epiphenomenalism and pre-emption theories occupy different positions across these distinctions even when they generate indistinguishable observations. [Springer+2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-025-02290-3)

## 5.5 The relation-versus-inventory objection

A view’s causal relation cannot be inferred from how much ontology it contains. Two equally rich mental and physical theories might endorse:

- interaction;
- parallelism;
- occasionalism;
- pre-established harmony;
- overdetermination;
- constitution;
- grounding;
- identity;
- emergence;
- or epiphenomenalism.

The article recognises channel width as an additional dimension, but relation type is broader than channel width. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

## 5.6 The empirical-discrimination objection

Quadrant placement does not by itself generate a test. A rich theory may have no additional observational consequences, while a thin theory may make precise predictions. Calling Q4 the region with the “strongest resources” risks confusing explanatory vocabulary with evidential success. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

The article needs to ask for each added structure:

- What explanatory burden does it discharge?
- What would be different if it were absent?
- Does it constrain possible mechanisms?
- Does it generate a discriminating prediction?
- Or is it merely permissive?

## 5.7 The synthetic-population objection

A quadrant can always be populated by combining independently selected components—for example, Myers–James plus Bohm. That demonstrates logical consistency only if the components are mutually compatible. It does not show that the resulting theory has been defended, solves the relevant problems, or has a coherent account of cross-component interaction.

Synthetic models should therefore be counted separately from historical and currently advocated positions.

## 5.8 The anachronism objection

Placing Descartes or Aquinas on axes involving quantum closure, scientific realism or present-day property dualism can be useful, but only as reconstruction. The article needs to distinguish:

- the philosopher’s own view;
- a charitable contemporary formalisation;
- and the Map’s adaptation of that view.

Without those labels, historical attribution and modern model construction are easily conflated.

# 6. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article is admirably explicit that the Map’s first three tenets rule out at least one quadrant. Disclosure is preferable to pretending neutrality. But the effect remains protective when the taxonomy is also presented as a general map of the available positions. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

I found five significant forms of protective framing.

## 6.1 Exclusion by tenet is sometimes treated as intellectual diagnosis

It is legitimate to say:

> Given the Map’s commitment to bidirectional interaction, epiphenomenalist positions are outside the project.

It is not legitimate to infer from that commitment that such positions are reflexive, evasive or explanatorily unserious. A framework commitment determines compatibility with the Map; it does not itself refute the excluded position.

## 6.2 The thin physical baseline incorporates the Map’s preferred quantum opening

The thin-physical end is described in a way that leaves room for mental action at quantum indeterminacy. That is not simply “physics and no more”; it is a particular interpretation of what indeterminacy permits. It makes the Map’s preferred minimal-quantum-interaction position appear ontologically inexpensive by locating its proposed opening inside the baseline. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

## 6.3 Closure is treated as physical thickness when this advantages the preferred quadrant

Strong closure does not add entities, properties or levels. Treating it as evidence of a thick or maximal physical ontology makes epiphenomenalism appear ontologically inflated while a theory adding mental causal intervention can remain physically thin. This is inconsistent with the article’s declared structural criterion.

## 6.4 Saad is assimilated to the Map’s quantum programme

The article says the Map can integrate Saad with quantum indeterminacy, although Saad’s published proposal uses default causal profiles and pre-emption rather than a quantum gap. The linked mechanism-cost discussion is more cautious and says the Saad-style route and an indeterminacy-completion route cannot simply be fused, because an undetermined token lacks the already sufficient physical cause that the reassignment strategy requires. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

This is the clearest case where adaptation to the tenets displaces description of the cited theory.

## 6.5 Rhetorical burdens are asymmetric

Q2 is associated with default materialist habits; Q3 is described as tending to “slide” toward idealism; Q4 is credited with the strongest resources and portrayed as neglected. Meanwhile, the Map’s own region remains flexible—“near” a quadrant or occupying a region rather than a cell. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

That asymmetry makes rival views absorb the taxonomic liabilities while the preferred view can move when challenged. Flexibility is appropriate for an exploratory map, but then the same interpretive generosity must be extended to all positions.

### Overall assessment of bracketing

The article does not conceal its commitments, and it repeatedly calls the grid heuristic rather than final. Those are genuine methodological strengths. Nevertheless, its definitions, rhetoric and treatment of Saad give the taxonomy a **moderate-to-strong tenet-protective effect**. The cure is to separate:

1. descriptive placement;
2. compatibility with the five tenets;
3. evidential assessment;
4. and the Map’s preferred reconstruction.

# 8. Balanced assessment

The article’s genuine strengths are:

- It identifies a useful question neglected by simple substance/property labels: how much internal structure each side of a theory receives.
- It recognises that relation type, causal channel and pole structure are not identical.
- It labels many placements as heuristic or judgement-dependent.
- It allows monist theories to be marked as limit cases rather than straightforward dualisms.
- It has already corrected some earlier Goff and Myers-related drift.
- The public changelog makes residual contradictions discoverable rather than concealing them. [[four-quadrant-dualism-taxonomy]]

Those strengths support retaining the project. But the current article should not claim more than it has established. Its best defensible status is:

> **A generative two-axis worksheet for comparing selected mind–body theories, not an exhaustive four-quadrant taxonomy and not yet a reliable historical classification.**

# 9. Concrete potential improvements

## A. Improvements to “Four-Quadrant Dualism Taxonomy”

1. **Retitle or qualify the article.** Use “A Two-Dimensional Thickness Heuristic for Dualist Theories” unless and until the axes and thresholds are operationalised.
2. **Choose one mathematical form.**

- Either use a genuine continuous plane and define the low/high cut points;
- or retain thin/medium/thick and replace four quadrants with a 3×3 matrix.
3. **Define structural richness independently for each pole.** Specify whether the score depends on number of entities, kinds of properties, levels of organisation, independent laws, internal differentiation or modal powers.
4. **Remove causal efficacy from mental thickness.** Record it separately as a relation variable.
5. **Remove causal closure from physical thickness.** Add a separate closure field distinguishing strong Closure, Observational Closure, causal completeness and conservation-law compliance.
6. **Add explicit annotations for:**

- substance/property/process status;
- mind–body relation type;
- channel width;
- strong versus observational closure;
- constitutive versus interactive explanation;
- historical, reconstructed or synthetic status;
- confidence in placement.
7. **Allow “N/A,” “off-axis” and “underdetermined.”** Do not force hylomorphism, neutral monism, Russellian monism, epistemic physicalism or process ontology into quadrants designed for separable poles.
8. **Correct the Saad entries throughout.** State that his account violates strong causal closure while preserving observational closure, uses default causal profiles and pre-emption, and is not itself a quantum-indeterminacy proposal.
9. **Add Chalmers’s Type O.** Contrast it directly with Saad:

- Type O: closed microphysics plus overdetermination or mediation;
- Saad: no strong closure, observational closure through pre-emption.
10. **Revise Type E.** Define it by absence of mental-to-physical influence, not by mandatory maximal physical closure.
11. **Reclassify Stoljar as physically underdetermined.** Epistemic ignorance should not itself raise an ontological score.
12. **Move Thomistic hylomorphism off-axis** or introduce a separate constitutive-co-principles category.
13. **Replace generic Russellian-panpsychism Q4 placement** with clearly differentiated constitutive-monist and specifically dualist Russellian variants.
14. **Correct the Stapp mechanism.** Separate agent-selected question or basis, nature-selected outcome and quantum-Zeno stabilisation.
15. **Revise the Descartes rationale.** Remove “extra-introspective content”; state which features—substancehood, unity, faculties or causal power—are being scored.
16. **Mark Myers–James plus Bohm as a synthetic construction.** Do not count it as a historically occupied quadrant.
17. **Treat Goff and Jaworski as annotated limit cases, not ordinary quadrant occupants.**
18. **Replace “Pereboom’s qualitative inertness”** with the exact sourced proposal or with “qualitative inaccuracy hypothesis.”
19. **Support every named-philosopher placement with a primary-source pinpoint citation.** Include edition, year and page or section.
20. **Separate article terminology from source terminology.** Labels such as “rich Type F” should be introduced as Map terminology.
21. **Downgrade unsupported sociological assertions.** Mark claims about materialist training, default quadrants and neglected regions as hypotheses unless supported by a literature survey.
22. **Add a placement table showing alternative reasonable codings.** Disagreement is useful evidence about axis ambiguity and should not be hidden.
23. **Add a “what would falsify or revise this placement?” field** for each major case.

## B. Improvements to specific neighbouring articles

1. **Linked mechanism-cost article:** replace any surviving description of Saad as involving two simultaneous sufficient causes with the default-profile and pre-emption account; retain its important warning that Saad’s route and quantum-gap completion are not automatically combinable.
2. **“Interventionist and Counterfactual Dualism”:** propagate the Closure/Observational Closure correction recorded in the changelog.

6. **The linked panpsychism article:** propagate the corrected Goff Q3/monist-limit placement and remove any surviving generic Q4 classification.
7. **“Substance Dualism vs Property Dualism”:** cross-link its discussion of hylomorphism and process ontology to the taxonomy’s new off-axis category.
8. **The Map’s parsimony article:** add a cross-reference warning that pole thickness is not an overall measure of theoretical cost.
9. **The Stapp or quantum-Zeno articles:** standardise the vocabulary of question selection, outcome selection and Zeno stabilisation so that “bias,” “selection” and “channel” are not used interchangeably.

## C. Improvements to site methodology

1. **Create a claim-to-source ledger.** For every philosopher placement, record the claim, primary source, pinpoint location, source wording, Map paraphrase and confidence level.
2. **Implement correction propagation.** When the changelog fixes a concept such as Saad’s closure distinction, automatically search all pages citing that concept and create linked remediation tasks.
3. **Designate canonical articles for recurring technical claims.** Other articles should import or summarise the canonical account rather than independently regenerating it.
4. **Distinguish four provenance categories on every taxonomy entry:**

- historically advocated;
- contemporary reconstruction;
- Map-developed extension;
- synthetic possibility model.
5. **Use two independent placement reviewers.** Preserve disagreements rather than forcing consensus; low inter-reviewer agreement would diagnose an axis-definition problem.
6. **Pre-register scoring rules before classifying further philosophers.** Do not modify the meaning of “thick” to accommodate a desired placement.
7. **Use neutral axis labels.** “Low/high structural differentiation” is less evaluatively loaded than “thin/thick,” “deflationary/inflationary” or “resource-rich.”
8. **Separate compatibility from evaluation.** Each article should independently mark:

- compatible with the five tenets;
- supported by evidence;
- explanatorily attractive;
- ontologically costly.
9. **Run periodic literature-freshness audits.** Current survey work, revised reference entries and recent causal-dualism literature should be checked before substantive modification dates are assigned.
10. **Add adversarial edge cases to every taxonomy.** Test the scheme against hylomorphism, neutral monism, Russellian monism, idealism, process ontology, epiphenomenal substance dualism and closure-compatible causal dualism.
11. **Require an empirical-consequence field.** Added structures should be associated with a discriminating consequence, an explanatory role or an explicit admission of empirical equivalence.
12. **Treat changelog “out-of-scope” findings as unresolved defects visible from the affected page.** A reader of the taxonomy should not have to discover in a separate log that one of its central exemplars is already known to be misdescribed.
