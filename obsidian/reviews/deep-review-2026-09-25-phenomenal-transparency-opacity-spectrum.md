## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Source/Map conflation, Pitt (2004).** The sentence "David Pitt argues we can distinguish
   what we are thinking — Paris, not London — but this reveals content, not the
   content-determination process (Pitt, 2004)" put the citation after the Map's gloss, so it
   attributed the gloss to Pitt. Pitt argues that proprietary cognitive phenomenology makes
   thoughts introspectively identifiable, and he takes that phenomenology to individuate content.
   He does not claim the content-determination process is hidden, and his view arguably points
   the other way. Fixed: Pitt's thesis now stands alone, and the gloss is marked "On the Map's
   reading, which Pitt does not himself draw".

### Medium Issues Found

1. **Link anchor pointed at a page that says the reverse.** `[[phenomenal-depth|attend harder]]`
   sat in "You cannot attend harder and see the processing stages". The target argues that
   attending more closely *does* disclose further layers. The two claims agree once content is
   separated from medium, and phenomenal-depth L44 makes that split, but the anchor hid it.
   Fixed by stating the split here too: closer attention can disclose further [[phenomenal-depth|layers of
   content]], but not the medium carrying them.
2. **Term-of-art ambiguity: "cognitively impenetrable".** The new [[cognitive-penetration]]
   article (L74) says this article's "impenetrable" differs from Siegel/Pylyshyn's sense.
   In their sense belief fails to reach perception; here attention fails to reach the
   constructive processing. This article never said so. Fixed with a short clause, and the phrase
   cognitive-penetration quotes at L69 ("cognitively impenetrable to ordinary effort") is kept word
   for word, so that quote still matches.
