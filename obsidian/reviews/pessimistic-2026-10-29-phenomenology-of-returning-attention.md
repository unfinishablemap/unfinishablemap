## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would accept the whole three-phase description and the Hasenkamp sequence and then ask what the article thinks it has added. "Who or what noticed the wandering?" (L57) has a complete answer in the paper the article cites: anterior insula and dorsal ACC, conflict-monitoring, firing between default-mode and executive activity. The "layer of awareness that was operative even during the absence of meta-awareness" is a folk-psychological re-description of a monitoring circuit that never switched off. She would add that "What Returns?" (L111) argues from the *vocabulary* of faculties — focus, meta-awareness, intention, effort, temporal orientation — to a conclusion about consciousness, when those five labels are not five mechanisms.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett's objection lands on the gap. The article says the gap "feels like absence" (L148) and was "a gap where no one was watching" (L39), but by its own Phase One account someone was there the whole time — thinking, imagining, worrying (L49). Nobody *feels* the gap during the gap; what exists is a judgment, formed at the noticing, that the preceding interval lacked monitoring. That is Schooler's temporal dissociation, a fact about re-representation, and heterophenomenology handles it without residue. He would also note that the article cites Schooler 2002 for the dissociation while passing over the same paper's *translation* dissociation, which says the re-representation misrepresents the original experience — exactly the lever that undercuts L128's inference from convergent report to "real phenomenological structure".

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark has little to do here because the article keeps the mechanism in one hedged sentence (L144, "speculative ... decoherence timescales"). He would only point out that the sentence following it — "the neural substrate becomes better trained to maintain intended patterns, requiring less conscious intervention" — concedes that practice effects are substrate effects, which is what Hasenkamp's negative correlations with experience ("performance of well-learned tasks requires less neural activity") already say without any intervention.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch would grant that L146 is honest about MWI's determinism and then deny the inference. "Felt openness" and "the quality of *this* rather than *that*" are experienced by the observer in every branch; a phenomenology that occurs identically whether or not selection is singular cannot "cohere better" with singular selection except by prior preference. The article concedes as much by calling its reading a stance (L79), but L146 does not carry that concession forward.

### The Empiricist
Popper's ghost would say the article is in good order where it names what it cannot do (L79, L134, L136) and in poor order where it says "provides evidence that" (L83). Training-induced change in the phenomenology is predicted by every account on the table; evidence that discriminates nothing is not evidence for the Map. He would ask for the discriminating observation the article never proposes: what report, under training, would the illusionist *not* predict? Gorbachev & Frankish's own "dispellability" dimension supplies the frame for one (see Issue 2).

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nagarjuna would read the softening of the noticing (L87) and the release-like return (L89) as the practice dissolving precisely the authored self the Authorship section wants to preserve. The "bare registering" of advanced practice is noticing without a noticer; the article converts it back into "consciousness re-engages with its own interface" (L75), which reifies a witness the training reports are shedding. He would note that L99's "the meditator could do something else" imports a chooser that the later stages of the very practice described report as absent.

### Issue 1: "What Returns?" contradicts the article's own sequenced phases — High
- **File**: obsidian/topics/phenomenology-of-returning-attention.md
- **Location**: L111 "The bundled character of returning — all these aspects shifting together rather than sequentially — suggests that what returns is not a single faculty but an entire mode of consciousness ... But the bundling extends beyond what a single network predicts. Meta-awareness, phenomenal effort, and temporal reorientation are not all executive-control functions, yet they shift in concert."
- **Problem**: Three of the article's own passages say the opposite. L53-63 place meta-awareness at Phase Two and effort at Phase Three, with the salience interval "preced[ing] the shift in which executive-network regions re-engage" (L55, raw-verified in Hasenkamp: salience "temporally between" default-mode and executive activation). L119-124 give the four-interval cycle in order. So the aspects listed at L105-109 return *in sequence*, which is what the data show, and "rather than sequentially" is false by the article's own account. "Beyond what a single network predicts" is a straw target: the model the article adopts is a three-network sequence (default mode, salience, executive), and a multi-network transition is exactly what every account on the table — including the neural account at L71 — predicts. The conclusion "points to a unified transition in how consciousness relates to the brain" rests on the false premise.
- **Severity**: High (internal contradiction feeding a tenet-level conclusion).
- **Recommendation**: Replace the bundling argument with the sequencing the article already describes: the aspects re-engage in a stereotyped order within roughly a second, and what is notable is the *coupling* (the order is fixed and the set is complete), which a production theorist explains by salience-to-executive hand-off and the Map reads as a mode transition. Drop "rather than sequentially" and "a single network". Word-neutral.

### Issue 2: Gorbachev & Frankish 2025's distortion thesis is described more narrowly than the paper states it — Medium
- **File**: obsidian/topics/phenomenology-of-returning-attention.md
- **Location**: L132 "Frankish's illusionism (Frankish 2016), in the distortion-thesis form examined at [[functional-seeming]] (Gorbachev & Frankish 2025), grants that introspective *access* is real while holding that introspective *interpretation* is systematically flawed"
- **Problem**: The paper (abstract, Springer page) defines the thesis as "the claim that introspective mechanisms systematically misrepresent neural processes in a way that strongly inclines us to judge that they have phenomenal properties", states "there is no consensus among illusionists as to the exact nature of this distortion", maps eight patterns along "positiveness, conceptuality, and dispellability", and says "we do not argue for a specific view". The access-real / interpretation-flawed reading is one value of the *conceptuality* dimension; the paper leaves non-conceptual (perception-like) distortion equally open. The article thus credits the paper with a commitment it explicitly withholds. The sibling [[functional-seeming]] L47 ("The error is theoretical, not experiential") makes the same commitment and is the probable seed; the name "distortion thesis" is the 2025 paper's coinage ("what we call"), so the article's dual citation is right and the sibling's "Frankish develops this through the distortion thesis" is a coiner/populariser slip (report only; the sibling is out of this task's scope).
- **Reasoning-mode note (medium, missed unsupported-move identification)**: the section honestly marks the framework boundary (L134, L136) and names one in-framework pressure (the training gradient), so there is no boundary-substitution and no label leakage. But it never names the standard the illusionist's own camp concedes it has not met: Gorbachev & Frankish say the nature of the distortion is unsettled and call for "empirical research on the nature of introspective distortion". The return cycle is a ready test case for their *dispellability* dimension — a distortion that training dispels predicts that expert reports should converge toward functional description, whereas the article's own L87-89 say trained reports grow *finer in phenomenal character* while shedding evaluative overlay. - **Severity**: Medium.
- **Recommendation**: Reword L132 to describe the thesis as the paper does (introspection misrepresents neural processes as phenomenal; the form of the misrepresentation is left open), move the access/interpretation split to "one of the patterns Gorbachev & Frankish canvass", and add one or two sentences naming the dispellability test. ## Counterarguments to Address

### The recognition quality (L71, L77)
- **Current content says**: the neural account "struggles to explain why noticing carries the distinctive quality of *recognition*, as though something that was already oriented toward the breath detected the deviation"; the threshold account explains *when* but not *what it is like*.
- **A critic would argue**: the maintained intention *is* "something already oriented toward the breath" — a task set held in prefrontal/parietal circuitry — and recognition is what a conflict signal against a maintained task set is like from the inside. The "as though" clause describes the neural account, not a residue it leaves.
- **Suggested response**: keep the hard-problem residue (why any character at all) and drop the implication that *recognition specifically* is unexplained; the article's own L79 already locates the disagreement at phenomenal character as such.

### Who noticed? (L57)
- **Current content says**: noticing "seems to emerge from a layer of awareness that was operative even during the absence of meta-awareness".
- **A critic would argue**: the article has already named that layer: the salience network's conflict monitoring, which the article says fired first (L55). Calling it "a layer of awareness" assumes what is at issue.
- **Suggested response**: "a monitoring function that was operative" preserves the puzzle (why its output is *felt* as recognition) without presupposing that the monitor was aware. The host [[non-temporal-consciousness]] hardens this further ("the witness-layer surfacing ... revealed"); see host sample.
