## Verbatim-Quote Ledger (primary/publisher-grade sources only)

Each quoted string checked against Davidson's own text, not against aggregators and not against any unfinishablemap.org result.

1. **"where there is causality, there must be a law; events related as cause and effect fall under strict deterministic laws"** (attributed Davidson 1970) — **real-wrong-punctuation, CORRECTED.** The worry that this was a splice of two non-contiguous clauses is **unfounded**: it is one contiguous sentence in "Mental Events" §I. But Davidson's own punctuation is a **colon**, not a semicolon: "where there is causality, there must be a law: events related as cause and effect fall under strict deterministic laws." Corrected in place. Verified against the reproduced §I text (philosophy.org.za Classic Text 16, which reproduces Davidson's §I verbatim including the surrounding Bismarck/perception passages) and corroborated by SEP's paraphrase of the cause-law principle.

2. **"there are no strict deterministic laws on the basis of which mental events can be predicted and explained"** (Davidson 1970) — **real-correct, verbatim, no change.** Matches Davidson's third principle word for word.

3. **"have no echo in physical theory"** (attributed Davidson 1970) — **verbatim quote, right author, WRONG WORK. RE-ATTRIBUTED.** The phrase does not occur anywhere in "Mental Events" (grep of the full reproduced text: zero hits). It is Davidson's, but from **"Psychology as Philosophy" (1974)**, Essay 12 of *Essays on Actions and Events* — confirmed by direct text extraction of the Oxford Scholarship Online chapter PDF (DOI 10.1093/0199246270.003.0012, p. 4 of 19). Davidson's actual sentence: *"But in inferring this system from the evidence, we necessarily impose conditions of coherence, rationality, and consistency. These conditions have no echo in physical theory, which is why we can look for no more than rough correlations between psychological and physical phenomena."* Two defects followed from the mis-sourcing and are both fixed: (a) the citation now reads `(Davidson 1974)` and names the companion essay; (b) the article's paraphrase of the constitutive norms as "coherence, consistency, charity" has been replaced with Davidson's own verbatim triple, **"conditions of coherence, rationality, and consistency"** — *charity* is Davidsonian but is not in this sentence and was an interpolation. The quotation was also extended to include the "rough correlations" clause, which is the part that actually does the anti-bridge-law work. New References entry added for Davidson 1974 (S. C. Brown ed., *Philosophy of Psychology*, 41-52, Macmillan 1974; repr. *EAE* ch. 12, 229-244).

4. **"Principle of the Nomological Character of Causally Relevant Properties,"** attributed to Honderich — **real-correct, NO CHANGE. The task's flag on this was a FALSE POSITIVE.** The article does *not* present this as Davidson's principle; it correctly presents it as Honderich's proposed reframing. SEP's *Anomalous Monism* supplement "Mental Properties and Causal Relevance" confirms verbatim: *"Thus, Honderich suggests that the cause-law principle must be articulated slightly differently—as the Principle of the Nomological Character of Causally Relevant Properties. This nomological property principle rules out the causal relevance of anomic mental properties."* The article's gloss ("which explicitly denies causal relevance to anomic mental properties") tracks SEP exactly. Davidson's own 1970 principle is correctly named in the numbered list as the *Principle of the Nomological Character of Causality* — the two are correctly kept distinct in the article.

## Attribution / Example-Provenance Findings

5. **Honderich's illustration — attribution-precision fix (medium).** The article illustrated Honderich's causal-relevance point with an invented example ("the window breaks because the rock is heavy and fast, not because it is grandmother's rock"), positioned immediately after "His point:", where a reader would naturally take it as Honderich's own. Honderich's actual example, confirmed independently at both IEP (*Davidson: Anomalous Monism*) and the SEP supplement, is **green pears on a grocer's scale**: IEP verbatim — *"draws a suggestive analogy between mental properties and the properties possessed by a bunch of green pears sitting on a grocer's scale. These pieces of fruit maybe truly described as green, or as French, but the fact that they possess these properties is clearly not what causes them to make the scale read '1 lb.'"* Replaced with Honderich's real example. Length-neutral, and strictly more faithful.

6. **Sosa's gunshot example — DE-ATTRIBUTED, substance and cite retained (medium).** The article credited the loud-gunshot/loudness example to Sosa 1984 ("with an example: ..."). Six independent searches across SEP (*Mental Causation*, *Anomalous Monism* + both supplements), IEP (*Causation, Mental*; *Davidson: Anomalous Monism*), and PhilPapers failed to attach that example to Sosa at any publisher-grade source; the encyclopedia literature's stock loud-noise case is a *sneeze/virus* example, not Sosa's. Per the disposition rules this is **not** a fabricated citation — Sosa 1984 is real, correctly cited, and SEP's *Mental Causation* §5.2 confirms Sosa's role verbatim (*"the mental properties (or mental types) are causally irrelevant (see, e.g., Stoutland 1980; Honderich 1982; Sosa 1984 ...)"*). So the fix is to keep Sosa's argument and citation and stop attributing the specific illustration to him: "Ernest Sosa pressed the same charge in ... (1984). The shape of the worry shows up in ordinary cases: a gunshot that is both loud and lethal ...". The illustration survives as the article's own.

7. **McLaughlin 1989 — citation-framing correction (critical-adjacent, fixed).** The article said McLaughlin *"argues that anomalous monism collapses into type epiphenomenalism."* That over-commits him and risks the classic framing defect of recruiting a defender as an objector. What is verifiable: (a) the paper's taxonomy of type epiphenomenalism / type dualism / causal priority of the physical is the locus classicus and is what gives the charge its precise form (SEP *Mental Causation* §5.2 cites it as "a review of this literature"); (b) McLaughlin also argues that *being efficacious in virtue of an event's physical properties does not entail being efficacious only in virtue of the physical properties* — a defence of mental causal relevance, not a collapse verdict; and (c) IEP cites his 1993 "On Davidson's Response to the Charge of Epiphenomenalism" (Heil & Mele, 27-41), i.e. he is engaged on both sides. Reframed to "maps the varieties ... and isolates the one that matters here," with an explicit clause noting he is not finally hostile to Davidson. Bibliographic tuple (*Philosophical Perspectives* 3: 109-135, 1989) re-confirmed at IEP's reference list.

8. **Davidson "Thinking Causes" (1993) characterisation** — **real-correct, no change.** SEP *Mental Causation* §5.5 confirms both the article's account of the reply (*"He clearly denies a crucial assumption of his critics, namely, that causes do their causing in virtue of their properties"*) and the "changes the subject" rejoinder with the correct trio of same-volume critics (*"Such a response seems to miss the point (Kim 1993b; McLaughlin 1993; Sosa 1993)"*). The article's "including Kim and Sosa in the same 1993 volume" is accurate.

9. **Supervenience-introduction credit** — **real-correct, no change.** The article's hedge is precisely scoped: "Davidson is often credited with introducing the term *to philosophy of mind* in this very paper." The term predates him (Hare 1952; Moore's 1922 concept), and the standard line is that Davidson brought it into philosophy of mind in 1970. The existing wording is already correct and correctly hedged — a less careful version ("introduced the term to philosophy") would have been wrong. Preserved deliberately.

## Second Lens — Fidelity of the Argument Paraphrase

The reconciliation story checks out against Davidson's §I text. The article's account — principle 2 concerns *events*, causal relations hold however events are described, the covering law need only be framed in *some* (physical) vocabulary, therefore token identity is *derived* rather than assumed — matches Davidson's own statement of the paradox and its dissolution, including his framing of the apparent inconsistency as non-formal ("The inconsistency is not, of course, formal unless more premises are added"). The claim that token identity is a *conclusion* rather than a premise is faithful and is the right thing to emphasise. No strawmanning of Davidson detected: the Map's disagreement is stated against Davidson's actual position (extensional event-causation plus denial of property-causation), not a simplified one.
