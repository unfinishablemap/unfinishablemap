## Executive Summary

The article banks the same datum — dreamless sleep — on both sides of its ledger. Against illusionism and in the Dualism paragraph, "seven hours feel like nothing" is read as *consciousness genuinely wasn't present*; two sections later, sleep-yoga reports of awareness through dreamless sleep are called the filter theory's strongest evidence. That is an epistemic→metaphysical equivocation (no recall → no experience) *and* a live contradiction with `voids/sleep-consciousness-void.md` L97, which the article links in Further Reading and which asserts the opposite reading. Second, the Dualism paragraph's "identical brain patterns can correlate with presence or absence of experience" is false by the article's own evidence (Siclari's finding is precisely that posterior slow-wave activity *differs*), and the git history shows why: the 2026-02-01 condense dropped "gross" from "same gross EEG pattern". Third, the "Why Consciousness Must Withdraw" section reasons from a bound the article itself labels *behavioural* to a conclusion about *phenomenal* withdrawal, and in doing so hands the physicalist a fully functionalist account of consciousness as the serial channel switched off for efficiency. Five deep reviews certified "Self-contradiction: None found"; the contradiction survives because each review re-read the sections in isolation.

Empirical checks: Zheng & Meister 2025, Geva-Sagiv 2023, Demirel 2025 and Lu 2025 all verify at Crossref (DOIs below). But Lu 2025's sleep sample is N1 and N2 only — no N3, no REM — so it cannot ground the article's "dreaming persists in sleep but not under deep anesthesia" inference, and the sibling anaesthesia article (L73) says ketamine anaesthesia produces vivid dreams, which the "anesthesia breaks the hinges" generalisation ignores.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Churchland)

"Read your own second section. Consciousness is 'the serial conscious channel' with 'a strikingly low throughput'; it 'withdraws' because parallel replay 'would overwhelm the conscious bottleneck'. That is a functional-role specification of consciousness, and a good one: the serial, capacity-limited broadcast channel that waking selection needs and consolidation does not. Once you have said what the channel *does* and *why it is shut off*, there is nothing left over for an interface to a non-physical partner to explain. The posterior-hot-zone result is a neural correlate becoming a neural *explanation*: local slow-wave bistability fragments causal structure, experience goes with it. You cite IIT's account approvingly, then call the same data a 'dissociation' from neural activity. Which is it?"

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Dennett)

"Your 'asymmetry of dreamless sleep' argument is heterophenomenology done badly. You have a *report* — 'it felt like nothing' — and you promote it to a fact about what was or was not there. But the report is generated on waking by a system with no access to the interval; 'felt like nothing' and 'forgotten' are indistinguishable from the inside, which is exactly why Siclari's subjects report 'I was experiencing something but can't recall what' on around a third of NREM awakenings. Then you turn around and take the *opposite* report — the sleep-yogi's 'awareness without content' — as evidence that consciousness was there all along. You cannot use reports as transparent windows when they help and as unreliable when they don't. And 'you can train it, so it's a genuine capacity, not a representation' — training a self-model is what MILD *is*. Introspective habits are learnable. Nothing about phenomenal properties follows."

### The Quantum Skeptic (Tegmark)

"'During NREM, this mechanism disengages; REM partially reengages it; lucid REM represents fuller engagement.' What disengages? Attention-mediated quantum Zeno selection requires a measurement-like interaction repeated at a rate faster than decoherence. Sleep changes neuromodulatory tone and cortical bistability on a timescale of seconds. You have not said what physical variable the 'engagement' tracks, what its value is in N3 versus REM, or why a slow-wave down-state would alter a femtosecond-scale process. The paragraph admits the prediction it makes is 'equally predicted by any theory on which metacognition returns during REM'. Then the section makes no prediction at all — it restates the tenet in sleep vocabulary."

### The Many-Worlds Defender (Deutsch)

"'The felt continuity across the sleep gap presupposes a singular you.' Every successor of the sleeper, in every branch, wakes with precisely that feeling of singular continuity; the feeling is what the Schrödinger equation predicts for every branch and therefore discriminates nothing. Worse for you: you say dreamless sleep 'may be a state where no selection occurs'. On your own Tenet 2, consciousness is what selects. So for seven hours either branching proceeds unselected — which is Everett — or selection proceeds without consciousness, in which case consciousness was never needed for it. The paragraph opens a hole in your framework and calls it support."

### The Empiricist (Popper's Ghost)

"Condition 3 says that if sleep-yoga reports proved unreliable, 'the filter theory's strongest evidence would collapse'. Two sections earlier, the same reports are 'difficult to verify'. So your strongest evidence is evidence you cannot currently verify, and the source you cite for it is a 1935 translation. Condition 1 is real science — good. Condition 2, 'consolidation proves to require experience', is unfalsifiable as stated: no measurement distinguishes 'no micro-experience' from 'micro-experience without report'. And the Sleep-versus-Anaesthesia claim is drawn from a dataset (Lu et al. 2025) whose sleep arm contains no deep sleep at all."

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

"You want a witness that persists through dreamless sleep *and* a 'singular you' who wakes as the same person. But the sleep-yoga reports you lean on describe the *dissolution* of the one who could be singular — awareness with no object, no ego, no time. If that testimony is evidence, it is evidence against the substantial self your No-Many-Worlds paragraph requires. You reify the witness into a bearer of haecceity. The traditions you cite would say the gap in sleep shows there was never a bearer, only the arising and ceasing of conditions."

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Dreamless sleep is read as *absence of experience* in two sections and as *presence of experience* in two others — an epistemic/metaphysical equivocation that also contradicts a linked sibling article
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/sleep-and-consciousness.md`
- **Location**: (a) table row "NREM Stage 3 (dreamless) | Nothing—time vanishes"; (b) § The Illusionist Challenge, "**The asymmetry of dreamless sleep**: … seven hours feel like nothing, not like forgotten experience. This differs from waking amnesia and suggests consciousness genuinely *wasn't present*"; versus (c) § Contemplative Evidence: Sleep Yoga, "awareness without content (knowing one remains conscious with nothing to be conscious *of*)"; (d) § What Would Challenge This View? item 3, "the filter theory's strongest evidence".
- **Problem**: The datum in (b) is epistemic — no recall, no felt duration. The conclusion drawn is metaphysical — no experience occurred. The bridging premise (that felt-nothing is introspectively distinguishable from forgotten-experience) is exactly what the "white dream" literature denies: Siclari et al. (2017), which the article already cites, report awakenings where subjects affirm having experienced *something* without any recallable content, and Windt, Nielsen & Thompson (2016, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 20(12): 871–882, "Does consciousness disappear in dreamless sleep?") argue the standard "dreamless = unconscious" assumption is unwarranted. The article then needs the *opposite* metaphysical reading in (c)–(d) to make the sleep-yoga evidence count for filter theory. It cannot bank both: if dreamless sleep is genuinely experience-free, the yoga reports are false or misdescribed and condition 3's "strongest evidence" is gone; if the yoga reports are veridical, the "asymmetry" reply to illusionism is unavailable. `voids/sleep-consciousness-void.md` L97 — linked from Further Reading — already commits the corpus to the second reading ("We forget dreamless sleep not because consciousness was absent…"). Five deep reviews reported "Self-contradiction: None found"; the two halves live four sections apart.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Split the readings explicitly. State the epistemic fact (no recall, no felt duration) and say that its phenomenal status is open, citing Windt et al. 2016 and Siclari's white-dream category. Then choose: either (i) keep the anti-illusionist "asymmetry" point in a weakened form ("if dreamless sleep is experience-free, then…") and demote sleep yoga from "strongest evidence" to "a first-person channel that, if veridical, would…"; or (ii) adopt the void article's reading throughout and drop the asymmetry point. Either way, the table cell "Nothing—time vanishes" should become "No recall; phenomenal status contested".

### Issue 2: "Identical brain patterns can correlate with presence or absence of experience" is false by the article's own evidence, and is a condense regression
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/sleep-and-consciousness.md`
- **Location**: § Relation to Site Perspective › Dualism: "Identical brain patterns can correlate with presence or absence of experience depending on slow-wave distribution. … This double dissociation—same EEG with different experience …—resists identification of consciousness with brain states."
- **Problem**: If the slow-wave distribution differs, the brain patterns are not identical. Siclari et al. (2017)'s result — which the article's own "Posterior Hot Zone" section states correctly — is that dreaming versus non-dreaming NREM is *predicted by* a difference in posterior slow-wave activity. What is "identical" is the stage label (N3), not the neural state. The sentence therefore converts a *success* of the neural-correlate programme (finer-grained correlate found where coarse staging failed) into a *dissociation* from neural activity. `git log -S` shows the pre-condense text (commit `78614cb0f4^`, 2026-02-01) read "The same **gross** EEG pattern produces dreamless sleep in one person and vivid dreaming in another"; the condense dropped "gross" and the 2026-06 and 2026-07 refine passes rewrote around the unqualified form. The second limb ("complex processing with no experience") is the computational/phenomenal binding point, which condition 2 of the article's own falsifiability list admits cannot currently be distinguished from "micro-experience without report".
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Restore "gross" (or "same sleep stage"), and reframe the Dualism paragraph honestly: sleep shows that experience tracks *local* dynamics rather than global state labels — a result every production theory also predicts — so the dualist reading has to be argued from elsewhere (the binding distinction, the interface's independently-engaging components), not from a dissociation the data do not show.

### Issue 3: "Why Consciousness Must Withdraw" argues from a behavioural bound to a phenomenal conclusion, and supplies the physicalist a complete functional account
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/sleep-and-consciousness.md`
- **Location**: § Why Consciousness Must Withdraw (whole section); § The Evolutionary Puzzle, "all apparently require *reduced consciousness* to proceed efficiently"; § The Binding Problem During Sleep, "produces unified phenomenal binding precisely by excluding the parallel processing that consolidation requires".
- **Problem**: Three times the article correctly labels the Zheng & Meister ~10 bits/s figure as a ceiling on *deliberate behaviour*, "not a measured bandwidth of phenomenal experience itself". The section's conclusion — "Consciousness withdraws during sleep not as a side effect but as a functional requirement" — only follows if phenomenal consciousness *is* the bottleneck that parallel replay would overwhelm. If the bound is behavioural, parallel replay would overwhelm deliberate behaviour, which is already suspended in sleep for other reasons; nothing follows about experience needing to withdraw. The Evolutionary Puzzle claim is unsupported for two of its three functions: synaptic downscaling tracks slow-wave activity and glymphatic clearance tracks noradrenergic tone (Xie et al. 2013 showed clearance under anaesthesia as well as sleep) — both are facts about *brain state*, not about the presence or absence of experience. And the section as written is a functional-role theory of consciousness (the serial channel, shut off for efficiency) that Churchland and Dennett would sign; the article never says why the channel's *being experienced* is what has to withdraw rather than the channel's serial *operation*.
- **Severity**: High
- **Recommendation**: Retitle to "Why the Serial Channel Withdraws" and let the section carry only what it earns: the serial *selectional* function is offline during consolidation. Add one sentence acknowledging that the functional story is neutral between production and interface readings, and move the phenomenal claim to the binding section where it is at least argued. Hedge "apparently require reduced consciousness" to "occur in states with reduced or absent report".

### Issue 4: Two of the four replies to illusionism fail inside illusionism's framework, and the "different predictions" sentence misattributes a prediction
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/sleep-and-consciousness.md`
- **Location**: § The Illusionist Challenge: "**Lucid dreaming**: The trainability of lucidity suggests consciousness is a genuine capacity rather than mere representation"; "**The contemplative challenge**: … incoherent if consciousness were merely self-modelling—the model would have nothing to model"; closing paragraph "illusionism predicts extended investigation reveals the 'illusion' as empty".
- **Problem**: Illusionism (Frankish 2016, which the article cites) denies *phenomenal properties*, not capacities. A trainable metacognitive capacity is what an introspective self-model predicts — MILD trains a representational habit ("notice that you are dreaming"), and the fact that it can be trained is silent on whether what it accesses is phenomenal. The "nothing to model" charge fails likewise: a self-model can represent *awareness as such* with no first-order content, and the illusionist reads the report "awareness without content" as exactly that representation's output. Both replies present as in-framework refutations; neither is. The closing sentence attributes to illusionism a prediction it does not make — Frankish's view is that introspection *continues* to represent phenomenality and that the illusion is exposed by theory, not dissolved by contemplative investigation. The 2026-05-14 deep review classified the lucid-dreaming reply as a sound in-framework move ("illusionism cannot easily accommodate … trainable capacities"); this review disagrees and says so.
- **Severity**: High (substitution failure per [[direct-refutation-discipline]])
- **Recommendation**: Keep the asymmetry point (once repaired per Issue 1) and the regress point (already balanced by the Frankish reply). Either drop the lucid-dreaming and contemplative replies or rewrite them as honest boundary-marking: "the Map reads trainable lucidity as an interface parameter; illusionism reads it as a trainable self-model, and the data do not choose." Replace the predictions sentence with what illusionism actually predicts.

## Medium Issues

### Issue 5: The Sleep-versus-Anaesthesia section rests on a dataset with no deep sleep, and overgeneralises across anaesthetic agents
- **Location**: § Sleep Versus Anesthesia; Reference 12.
- **Problem**: Lu et al. (2025), *Communications Biology* 8:1784, DOI 10.1038/s42003-025-09205-2 — verified at Crossref and Europe PMC. Its abstract lists the sleep arm as wakefulness N=31, NREM1 N=24, NREM2 N=19: no N3, no REM. The article infers "This may explain why dreaming persists in sleep but not under deep anesthesia" from light-sleep data. The abstract does say "propofol affected pulvinar-cortical connections", so that half is supported; the paraphrase "natural sleep preserves the brain's integration-segregation balance" was not confirmed from the abstract and needs checking against the full text. Separately, "anesthesia disrupts the interface itself … breaks the hinges" is agent-specific: `topics/anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface.md` L73 says ketamine anaesthesia yields "vivid experiences — elaborate dreams", and L91 (Bonhomme et al. 2019) decomposes anaesthetic experience into separable components. The metaphor is true of propofol and false of ketamine.
- **Recommendation**: Restrict the claim to propofol and to light sleep as measured; cross-reference the ketamine paragraph of the sibling; verify or drop the integration-segregation paraphrase.

## Counterarguments to Address

### Consolidation without experience supports production theories at least as well
- **Current content says**: computational binding without phenomenal binding "sharpens" the hard problem for identity theories.
- **A critic would argue**: Global-workspace and IIT accounts *predict* that offline replay is unexperienced — replay is neither globally broadcast nor integrated in the relevant way. The dissociation is a confirmation of those theories' sufficiency conditions, not an embarrassment.
- **Suggested response**: Concede the parity and relocate the argument to what the production theories do *not* predict — if anything in the sleep data is such. The article should not present a shared prediction as a dualist advantage.

### "Gradation" is a fact about report, not about consciousness
- **Current content says**: "Consciousness varies continuously across sleep states."
- **A critic would argue**: What varies is *recall and reportability* on awakening. The six-row table is discrete, and every row's "typical experience" is a waking report about an interval.
- **Suggested response**: Replace "continuously" with "across a graded series of states", and note once that every entry is report-mediated.
