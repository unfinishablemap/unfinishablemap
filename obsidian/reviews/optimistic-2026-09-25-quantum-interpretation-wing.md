## Executive Summary

**The wing contains the Map's best framework-boundary writing and two pages that have not caught up with it.** The Everett-facing pages (`quantum-immortality`, `many-minds-interpretation`, `consistent-histories-interpretation`) each state the rival's in-framework answer at full strength and then say exactly where the disagreement becomes bedrock. `quantum-immortality` L76–78 concedes that the caring-measure account "defeats the immortality inference from inside MWI—without collapse and without a dualist subject", and demotes the Map's own contribution to "a modest difference of explanatory route". `contextual-selection` does the same for Kochen–Specker: the theorem "does not favour consciousness-collapse over other interpretations" (L49).

The pages that lag are the *probability pair*, `quantum-probability-consciousness` and `quantum-measurement-and-subjective-probability`. They say Born probabilities are "the objective structure of the interface" (QPC L43) and that consciousness fixes which outcome is actual "*for this subject*" (QMSP L128–130, QPC L114/L140). Two of their siblings have already set a narrower scope. `multi-mind-collapse-problem` L88 and `quantum-immortality` L84 both hold that an external detector's outcome "is macroscopically amplified and objectively reduced before any neural processing of it begins". On the Map's own baseline, then, a laboratory spin measurement's Born statistics belong to objective collapse, not to the interface. QPC's own worked example (L124–128) is a laboratory spin measurement. Three further cross-page defects follow the same pattern, where a repaired sibling holds the calibrated wording and the lagging page does not (Birch section): Frauchiger–Renner in QMC L68, PBR in QPC L102, and Stapp's classification in QMC L136.

**Intra-wing wiring is thin.** Of 72 possible directed links among the nine pages, 11 exist. `quantum-immortality` links to none of its eight siblings and receives no links from them. `quantum-probability-consciousness` links out to none of them.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would praise the wing for declining to spend dualism where a physicalist can get the same result. `quantum-immortality` L28 says it outright: the anti-immortality verdict "is available to any single-outcome interpretation, physicalist as much as dualist; what the Map adds is not the conclusion but its tenet-level account of the determinate first-person subject the conclusion presupposes." L78 repeats it: the conclusion "does not require, and so does not establish, the Map's dualism." `many-minds-interpretation` L84 makes the positive dualist point in its proper place. Albert and Loewer, "analytic philosophers with no dualist agenda", found that taking determinate experience seriously in unitary QM forced them to give up mind–brain supervenience. That shows dualism can arise from the physics itself, and the page does not inflate it into more. `consistent-histories` L78 scores CH as "no support, no contradiction" for Tenet 1: "a naturalistic competitor whose success would count against the *need* for dualism, not as evidence for it." A property dualist wants the hard problem to carry the case, and this wing leaves it there.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp would find the most to like in `contextual-selection-in-quantum-foundations`. Its "Attention as context-setting (speculative)" paragraph (L61) reads his Process 1 as contextuality: *which question is asked* determines which answers are available. It then marks the extension as "the Map's conjecture rather than a consequence of contextuality". That is the right reading of Stapp. The agent chooses the question and nature answers by the Born rule.

He would object to `quantum-measurement-and-consciousness` L136, which puts "Stapp's quantum Zeno model and Chalmers-McQueen's CSL-IIT" in "the Born-bending family". That contradicts two repaired pages. `concepts/stapp-quantum-mind` L64 records that Stapp declines probability control ("thoroughly committed to strict adherence to the orthodox statistical rules, in contrast to Eccles"). `born-rule-and-the-consciousness-interface` L163–165 moves his own reading into a separate *question-choice (Born-rule-intact)* row. The 2026-09-24 agency-wing review found the same conflation at `phenomenology-of-agency-vs-passivity` L147, so this is the second dependent page the Stapp repair has not reached. The fix is to separate Stapp's self-description (question-choice) from the Map's use of Zeno holding as a mechanism that could shift neural statistics.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

For Nagel the wing's highlight is `quantum-measurement-and-subjective-probability`'s "Phenomenological Test" (L134–144). The QBist's credences are credences about *what the agent will experience*, so "consciousness appears on both sides of the probability relation—as the subject assigning credences *and* as the content those credences are about." The test is narrow and fair: not "does consciousness exist?" but "can you define Born probabilities without already presupposing a space of conscious experiences?" The thermodynamics analogy is answered in the same careful way (L66). This is the Map's clearest statement of why QBism's silence about agents is a structural gap and not modesty. It is also the page's strongest content, and the probability-pair concern below does not touch it.

`collapse-and-time` L79 makes a smaller Nagelian point well: identifying temporal experience with neural processing "explains the tracking without explaining the phenomenology—why temporal processing *feels* like flow".

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Whitehead would be glad to see his work treated as a reading and not as a proof. `multi-mind-collapse-problem` L119–121 uses concrescence to show why interface locality is natural: each occasion achieves *its own* definiteness, so "there's no moment requiring coordination." `collapse-and-time` L103–105 goes further. It sets out the process-dualist reading of agent identity ("identity as constituted activity rather than enduring substance") and then records it as "an alternative rather than as the framework's answer". Adopting it would require rebuilding the Map's accounts of agency, survival, copying and the indexical objection to Many-Worlds, and the page says so. Whitehead-friendliness is present, and it does not replace the persisting-subject posit (P-I1). Both personas can endorse that.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane would single out `contextual-selection` L73–77, which refuses a move libertarians often make. Contextuality supplies "a structured, non-determined space within which selection occurs", but "structure is not purpose. A roulette wheel constrained to certain numbers is still a roulette wheel." The claim that consciousness fills the space with reasons "requires its own defence, independent of the Kochen-Specker result." Kane spent a career keeping indeterminacy and agency apart, and he would recognise this. `quantum-measurement-and-consciousness` L128 gives the anti-MWI point in its best form: "if every choice branches into all alternatives, preferring one cannot differentially bring it about, since each alternative is equally realised."

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

McGinn would praise the wing's handling of underdetermination. `quantum-measurement-and-consciousness` L84 says the dynamics-preserving interpretations "are empirically equivalent by construction", and that this "cuts symmetrically, against the Map's reading as much as against its rivals". What recommends collapse realism is "programme-level fruitfulness rather than evidential support the rivals lack." L142 concedes that the century-long stalemate "fits the Map's reading rather than selecting it", since empirical equivalence predicts the same stalemate. `consistent-histories` L84 leaves the Dowker–Kent dispute "genuine and unresolved" and states the prior question on which it turns. This is humility with an argument behind it.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

The wing's quality depends on whether its claims about physics are stated at their true strength, so this persona's verdict matters most. The pattern is consistent. **Each defect below has a calibrated counterpart elsewhere on the Map, usually a page repaired after the lagging page was last edited.**

**Praise.** `quantum-immortality` is the model. It grades the measure objection as "decisive only conditional on a premise the survival argument is entitled to dispute" (L54–56). It records Tegmark as still an immortalist about the idealized case (L44). It turns down the Map-specific immortality worry with a stated mechanism: external triggers are objectively reduced before any neural interface reaches them, and modulation is "constrained to stay within Born statistics—minimal by tenet, not a licence to load the dice" (L84). And it ends with "The felt singularity of the first-person subject is evidence consistent with the Map's one-branch reading, not a defeater of the branch-relative reply" (L78). Tenet 4 is *illustrated* here, not *corroborated*, and the page says so at L66. `many-minds-interpretation` L90 declines the parsimony objection it could have used ("The load-bearing objection to MMI is *indexical*, not ontological"). `multi-mind-collapse-problem` L78 makes its whole resolution "conditional on some such mechanism holding" given the GRW/CSL bounds.

**Flags.**

1. **Frauchiger–Renner overstated against the Map's own position** (`quantum-measurement-and-consciousness` L68). The text says FR "showed it generates contradictions for any single-world interpretation". FR is a trilemma: universal applicability (Q), cross-agent consistency (C) and single outcomes (S) cannot all hold. A single-world theory escapes by restricting Q, and collapse theories do exactly that. As written, the sentence says the Map's own single-world view is inconsistent. **Calibrated model**: `multi-agent-born-preservation-problem` L91 ("Keeping a single actual outcome (S) means restricting universal applicability (Q) or cross-agent reasoning (C)") and `self-reference-and-the-limits-of-physical-description` L132 ("The Map takes the first").
2. **PBR stated without its assumption, and completeness without the Bohm exception** (`quantum-probability-consciousness` L102, L106). "The PBR theorem (2012) establishes that the wave function is ontic" leaves out the preparation-independence premise. L106's "quantum mechanics is a complete description of physical structure" leaves out de Broglie–Bohm. **Calibrated model**: `concepts/quantum-completeness` L52 ("given a natural independence assumption … remains debated—notably by Emerson, Serbin, Sutherland, and Veitch (2013)") and L54 ("Determinism is the exception the theorems leave standing"). L106 also says Kochen–Specker values emerge "precisely where consciousness might participate", but the wing's own `contextual-selection` L49 says "Contextuality does not favour consciousness-collapse over other interpretations". That clause should point there.
3. **The scope of the interface reading of Born probability** (QPC L43, L110, L124–130, L140; QMSP L128–130). Discussed under Concerns below.
4. **An unnamed Denton-2024 locus that the sweep missed** (`quantum-measurement-and-subjective-probability` L92): "a 2024 *Nature Communications* study showed it enables cryptochrome magnetoreception." Denton et al. (2024) is a computational spin-dynamics study, not an experiment, and "showed … enables" reads it as a demonstration. The 2026-07-14 residual sweep (`todo.md` ✓ "Sweep residual Denton-2024 'demonstrated' over-claims") searched for "Denton". This sentence does not name the author, and the paper is not in the page's reference list, so the sweep could not find it. **Calibrated model**: `topics/quantum-biology-and-neural-consciousness` ("establishes that warm Zeno-type quantum effects *can* occur in a specific, specialised molecular architecture … The precedent is for the *mechanism category*, not for any neural deployment"). Add the reference: Denton, Smith, Xu, Pugsley, Toghill & Kattnig (2024), *Nat Commun* 15, 10823.
5. **Phenomenology used as evidence against MWI** (`multi-mind-collapse-problem` L173; `collapse-and-time` L117). MMCP says "The phenomenology of being *this* observer … is precisely unitary, supporting real collapse over endless branching". Collapse-and-time says "the future doesn't feel like undiscovered territory; it feels genuinely open". Everett predicts unitary, open-feeling experience in every branch. `indexical-identity-quantum-measurement` L109 already concedes this ("Anticipating *one* future rather than many is preserved under Everett"), and `quantum-immortality` L78 grades it correctly as "consistent with … not a defeater". Both sentences should drop to that grade.

## Calibration Concerns (refine-draft, not expansion)

**1. The probability pair states the interface reading of Born probability without the scope its siblings give it.** QPC's lead says Born probabilities are "the statistical structure of how mind actualises one possibility among many" (L43), and L110 says "Probability isn't a feature of physics independent of consciousness." Its example (L124–128) is a spin measurement read as "a 50% probability that *this consciousness* will experience spin-up." But QPC L140 itself says physical mechanisms "handle the vast majority of 'collapse'", and the siblings go further. `multi-mind-collapse-problem` L84–88 has external systems "collapse physically … Alice and Bob both see the same result because the result was already determined physically". `quantum-immortality` L84 has the external trigger "objectively reduced before any neural processing". On the Map's own objective-reduction baseline, therefore, a laboratory spin measurement's Born statistics are objective-collapse chances. The interface reading applies only to alternatives *within the agent's neural dynamics*. QPC needs one scoping sentence in the lead and a neural-site example, or an explicit note that the spin example is a simplification.

A second, related gap: "which outcome actualises *for this subject*" (QPC L114, L140; QMSP L128–130). Without a qualifier, relativised actuality is the relational/many-minds structure the wing rejects elsewhere (`many-minds-interpretation` L88). The hub has already been repaired: `indexical-identity-quantum-measurement` L165 says one outcome actualizes "with the alternatives *globally* nonactual rather than merely absent from this branch." QMSP L130 adds that the indexical contribution does not require "physical changes detectable from outside". That sits uneasily beside its own L154 ("requires genuine causal connection") and should be reconciled or hedged.

**2. collapse-and-time's Relation section overruns the scope its lead sets.** The lead (L37) and "Atemporal selection" (L83) say "the cosmic arrow itself predates mind" and that consciousness contributes "the felt structure of *experienced* time rather than temporal direction as such." The Relation section then says "physics presupposes time, and time presupposes consciousness" (L131) and "Consciousness influencing which outcomes become actual shapes the fundamental structure of physical reality" (L135). Both should be restricted to *lived* time, in line with L37. A smaller point: L95 says the growing block "preserv[es] past reality (supporting retrocausal influence)". Retrocausal influence runs from future to past, and the growing block denies the future exists. L97's PTI paragraph partly fixes this, but L95's parenthesis still states it the wrong way round.

**3. `multi-mind-collapse-problem` L63: RQM "trades the multi-mind problem for epiphenomenalism."** Denying consciousness any special *interpretive* role does not make the interpretation epiphenomenalist. An RQM physicalist holds that mental states are causally efficacious physical states. The accurate charge is that RQM gives consciousness no *non-physical* role, which conflicts with Tenet 1, not with Tenet 3. This change is one clause.

## Expansion Opportunities

### High Priority

#### Diverging-worlds Everettianism (quantum modal realism) as the strongest rival to the indexical objection
- **Builds on**: `quantum-immortality` L70, the only place on the Map where Wilson (2020) or diverging worlds appears (a corpus search for "quantum modal realism" and "diverging worlds" returns this one file); `concepts/many-worlds`; `indexical-identity-quantum-measurement`; `haecceity`.
- **Would address**: The Map's main objection to MWI is indexical: nothing fixes which successor is *me*. That objection assumes *overlapping* worlds, where one pre-measurement subject fissions. Diverging-worlds views (Saunders 2010; Saunders & Wallace 2008; Wilson 2020) deny fission. Each branch contains a counterpart, so a determinate *I* exists in each world without being strung across branches. `quantum-immortality` L70 grants that this "dissolves the paradox without collapse" and marks the leftover disagreement (counterpart-relative identity) as bedrock. The Map has no page that tests its five-argument case against this variant. It is the variant most likely to be used against the indexical argument.
- **Estimated scope**: Medium concept article (concepts 329/360).
- **Tenet alignment**: Tenet 4. Write it as rival engagement: state where the indexical objection survives (qualitative counterpart similarity versus haecceity) and where it does not.
